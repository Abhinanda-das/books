## Introduction

*om, ramaya ramabhadraya ramacandraya vedhase I*  
*raghunathaya nathaya sitayah pataye namah II*  
*kujantam rama ramoti madhuram madhuraksaram I*  
*aruhya kavitasakham vande valmikikokilam II*

The text *Aditya Hrdayam* is a part of the immortal epic *Srimadramayanam* authored by Adikavi Valmiki. In the Vedic culture, worshipping of *Isvara — isvaropasana* — is very characteristic and unique, and probably slightly different from what we come across today in the Hindu society. Here, *Isvara* is worshipped in a form, which is readily available for our perception as part of the Nature before us. For example, *Isvara* is worshipped as the Fire. The *Rgveda*, the foremost of the Vedas, starts with the invocation:

*agnimide purohitam I*

**I praise the glory of Agni, who is the foremost as a benefactor.**

Here Agni is *Isvara*. Aurobindo explained the word Agni as the Divine Will and Wisdom. In the Vedic culture, fire is universally taken as the symbol of *Isvara*. We relate to, or we worship *Isvara* in the form of fire. Another such altar described in the Vedas is the Sun.

Vedic people regularly practise a particular mode of worship called *sandhya-vandana*. It is the most well-known mode of worship in the Vedic culture even today.

*Vandana* is *upasana* or worship. *Sandhya* means the junction between the night and day when the Sun rises, or between day and night when the Sun sets. Vaidikas worship *Isvara* at that time in the form of the Sun. They offer water (*arghya*) thrice to the rising and setting Sun, and then meditate upon *Isvara* as the presiding deity of the solar orb by reciting the famous Gayatri *mantra*.

Gayatri is the name of a meter. In the Gita (10-35), Bhagavan says:

*gayatri cchandasamaham I*

**I am the meter gayatri among the meters.**

The meter *gayatri* has three lines of eight syllables each. Thus, it has twenty-four syllables in all. Every *mantra* describes the glory of a *devata. Devata* means a particular manifestation of that *para-Brahman* (or *Isvara*). Thus, the recitation of a given *mantra* amounts to the worship of Isvara in the manifestation of that particular *devata*. The *devata* of the Gayatri *mantra* is the Sun.

*tatsaviturvarenyam bhargo devasya dhimahi I*  
*dhiyo yo nah pracodayat II*

**We meditate upon that supreme light (awareness) of the Lord who creates and nourishes this universe. That Lord alone illuminates and inspires our thoughts.**

*Para-Brahman* is essentially without any form or shape, because intrinsically it is not possible for the Supreme Reality to have a given form or shape. If *Brahman* were to have a given form, then It would become yet one more form subject to the limitations of time and space, and hence unreal. If *Isvara* were to have a given form, however exalted that form may be, it would be limited in space and time. Space and time are always together. Whatever is limited in space is necessarily limited in time. *Isvara*, who is the originator of space and time, cannot be really folded into space and time. Therefore, *Isvara* is the attribute less infinite Awareness. *Bhargah* means that *caitanya*, Awareness. It is the origin of the *kriya-sakti* (energy, potential or expressed in the form of action) and *jnana-sakti* (the power of knowledge). This entire universe is nothing but a combination of *kriya-sakti* and *jnana-sakti*. All the matter comes under the category of *kriya-sakti* alone. *Bhargah*, the Awareness is the origin of these two. *Bhargah*, the *caitanya* which is *para-Brahman* is called *tat, tat bhargah*. *Tat* is *sarvanama*, a pronoun. The word *sarvanama* means the name of everything. Everything is a manifestation of *para-Brahman*. Hence, the name of everything also represents the origin of everything.

Worship of the *para-Brahman* in a given altar is called *saguna (sakara) upasana. Saguna* means having attributes; *sakara* means having a particular form. There is a problem in worshipping the *para-Brahman* in Its attributeless formless nature. *Bhagavan* says in the *Gita* ([12.5](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/12.5)):

*kleso'dhikatarastesamavyaktasaktacetasam I*  
*avyakta hi gatirduhkham dehavadbhiravapyate II*

**The affliction is greater still for those whose minds are committed to what cannot be objectified, because an end which cannot be objectified is reached with difficulty by those who are identified with the body.**

For a limited being (*jiva*) rooted to the body, identified with the body, appreciation of formless attributeless *para-Brahman* straightaway is a very hard task, for the mind is accustomed to dwell upon names, attributes, and forms. Therefore, *Bhagavan* advises that the devotees may start with the worship or meditation of the *saguna. Saguna* meditation is also called as *apara-Brahma upasana*, in contrast to *Brahma jnana* (knowledge of *para-Brahman*). So, to make meditation easy by taking to an altar of worship, *saguna sakara* has to be superimposed on the attributeless *para-Brahman*. This idea is summarised in the statement:

*na te rupam na cakaro nayudhani na caspadam I*  
*upasakanam karyartham brahmano rupakalpana II*

**O Lord, You do not have fixed attributes or form. You do not really wield any weapons. There is no location for you. Yet, for the benefit of meditation upon You by the devotees, a particular form is superimposed on the attributeless Brahman.**

Meditation on the *saguna* helps the seeker to purify the mind and thereby gain a higher level of understanding of the *para-Brahman*.

In the Gayatri *mantra*, the Sun god is introduced as *savitur devasya*. This word Savita appears in the *Aditya Hrdayam* also. *Isvara* is called Savita because he creates, protects and sustains the entire life in this universe. For this reason, the Gayatri *mantra* is also called Savitri. With reference to meter it is *gayatri*, and with reference to *devata* it is Savitri. There is one more point of interest in Gayatri in the context of *Srimadramayanam*. If we divide this great epic of 24,000 verses into 24 units of 1000 verses each, then each unit starts with a successive syllable of Gayatri. This incorporation of Gayatri in the verses of *Srimadramayanam* makes the latter particularly auspicious. Quite often people ask me a question: Swamiji, can women recite Gayatri *mantra*? I quote a verse from *Yama-Smrti* as the answer to that question.

*pura kalpe tu narinam maunjibandhanamisyate I*  
*adhyapanam ca vedanam savitrivacanam tatha II*

**In the earlier times, the investiture with the sacred thread, teaching of Vedas and recitation of the Gayatri was allowed (desired) for the women.**

Sitadevi was described by Valmiki as performing the *sandhya* meditation in the Asoka forest, where she was held captive by Ravana. One should relate to *Isvara* every morning at the time of sunrise, irrespective of the situation in which one finds oneself, whether one is in prosperity or in adversity. This daily worship called *sandhyavandana* should not'be dispensed with.

The Cosmic Person (*Isvara*), who brought out this universe out of Himself, is often called in the Vedic literature by the name Hiranyagarbha from the standpoint of the Universal Power and Omniscience. In the Puranic literature, *Isvara* is referred to as Visnu (one who pervades all) or as Siva (the auspicious). That Hiranyagarbha is worshipped in the altar of the Sun, because, in this Solar system where we live, it is the Sun that is the source of all energy, all activities and all life. That *Isvara*, who is manifest in the form of the Sim before us, is also present in us in the form of atman, the Awareness, thereby illuminating our thoughts, and making all varieties of knowing possible. If that awareness were not to manifest, then there would have been no faculty of thinking. The power of thinking (*dhi sakti*) is the basis of human existence. A human being is nothing but his ideas and understanding (*buddhi*). The difference between a person in coma and a normal person is the power of thinking alone. In fact, all the metabolic processes such as regulation of body temperature, heartbeat, etc., go on even for a comatose person. The only difference is that his intellect is not functioning. That is also the reason why he is not moving or doing anything. We think that the *buddhi* is present inside the body. But that is wrong. The body exists in the *buddhi*, and that buddhi exists in atman, the Awareness, which is that *para-Brahman*. That Awareness (*caitanya*) manifesting in this body-mind-sense complex in the form of consciousness, inspiring the *buddhi-vrttis*, is also present before us in the form of the solar orb, Hiranyagarbha. That is the meaning of Gayatri *mantra* in a nutshell.

In *Rudradhyaya* (1-7), Lord Siva is presented as Nilakantha (the one with a blue neck):

*asau yo'vasarpati nilagrivo vilohitah I*

**Lord Siva is blue in the neck, but red in the rest of the body. That Lord is rising before us as the Sun.**

There is a popular story in the Puranas. When the ocean of milk was churned by the gods and the demons, what initially arose was the most virulent poison. On their request, Lord Siva devoured it and held it in his throat without gulping it, imparting blue colour to the neck. But, we may not cite a Puranic story to explain Vedic usage, since it is the Purana that follows the Veda, and not the other way round. The word Nilagriva can be understood differently from the point of view of the Cosmic Person. The words *nila* and *lohita* respectively refer to the blue and red colours of the sky. In fact, Lord Siva is the Cosmic Person Hiranyagarbha, and the Sun is the *adhibhuta* (the gross or the physical) or *adhidaiva* (pertaining to the presiding deity) symbol of the Lord. This *mantra* is describing the rising Sun (*asau yo'vasarpati*). At the time of sunrise and also sunset, the horizon is all red with patches of blue. That horizon is visualised as the neck of the Cosmic Person.

It is normally understood that *Isvara* is known only to a select few called *rsis*. Common people are not supposed to have the *saksatkara* (direct visualisation) of *Isvara*. But then the Veda has the opposite to say:

*utainam gopa adrsannadrsannudaharyah I*  
*utainam visva bhutani sa drsto mrdayati nah II*  
*— Rudradhyaya, Taittinya Samhita, 4-5-1*

**Even the cowherds and the women folk carrying water pitchers see this Lord in the form of the Sun. Indeed, all living beings see Him. He is also seen by us. May He protect us.**

The cowherds and the women carrying water pitchers on their heads represent the illiterate masses of the countryside. Even they see *Isvara*. Not only that; every bird, creature, and animal knows the arrival of the *visvabandhu*, the protector of the entire universe. When the Sun is rising in the east, it is an event recognised by almost every life form, excepting of course, a few people who choose to sleep at that time also!

If there is ever a society in the human history that is very deeply and widely influenced in all aspects of its life by an epic, it is the Hindu society, and that epic is *Srimadramayanam*. Mother India has no existence without Rama. Rama and Ramayam are the weft and warp of Indian nationhood and the heart and soul of Hindu dharma. Valmiki, the very first poet (*adikavi*) to compose verses other than the Vedic hymns, is the composer of the immortal epic. That is the reason why this epic is better known as *adikavya*, the first poem. Rama is the hero of the epic. Innumerable poets over centuries have translated or rewritten this great epic in different languages of India. There are quite a few vernacular versions of *Srimadramayanam* in every language of India. For example, we have the most famous *Tulasi Ramayana* in Hindi, *Bhaskara Ramayanam* and *Molla Ramayanam* in Telugu*, Kamba Ramayanam* in Tamil, etc. Even in Sanskrit, we have a few versions of the S*rimadramayanam* like *Adhyatmaramayanam* and *Anandaramayanam*. There is a fundamental difference between Valmiki's *Srimadramayanam* and other versions written by various authors. In all these versions, Rama is presented even from the very first verse as God, an incarnation of *Isvara*- but not so in Valmiki's *Srimadramayanam* till the very end. Valmiki portrayed his hero as an ideal human being. If we transform Rama into God from the very beginning, the value of Rama as a role model is lost, because we conveniently assume that whatever he has done, only God could have done.

Once, there was a proposal to construct Sivaji Mandir, a temple for the sixteenth-century Hindu Emperor, Sivaji. Some of the *mahatmas* objected to it. Sivaji stands for sacrifice and valour in the face of evil. Their concern is to let Sivaji remain as a role model for the society at least for some more time. If you construct a temple for Sivaji, then Sivaji stops inspiring people. We just do *puja* of 108 or even 1008 names, and there it ends. The message of Valmiki in *Srimadramayanam* is to live like Rama, but not like Ravana. Rama is presented as a human being, facing all the problems associated with human life, and yet standing upright for the sake of *dharma* in the face of the direst calamity. Rama was ready to sacrifice everything for upholding *dharma*.

*ramo vigrahavan dharmassadhussatyaparakramah I*  
*— Srimadramayanam, 3-37-13*

**Rama is the embodiment of virtuousness. He is a thorough gentleman and a warrior of true valour.**

If *dharma* were to take a physical form with a face, hands, legs, etc., it will look like Rama. This statement was made, not by an admirer of Rama, but by Marica, a demon, who was inimical to Rama for a long time. Marica was the most cunning of the demons. Marica is quoted as an example of the most crooked person. It is the greatness of Rama that he was admired even by his enemies.

*Srimadramayanam* is divided into six volumes (*kandas*) called Bala, Ayodhya, Aranya, Kiskindha, Sundara and Yuddha Kandas. This *Aditya Hrdayam* is a small canto (chapter) in Yuddha Kanda. The context was the battle between Rama and Ravana, which was described in glorious detail by the poet Valmiki. It was a very fierce battle, the fiercest of all the battles of all times. We have no parallel to that battle:

*gaganam gaganakaram sagarassagaropamah I*  
*ramaravanayoryuddham ramaravanayoriva II*

**Sky resembles sky; ocean is like the ocean itself. The battle between Rama and Ravana can be compared only with the battle between Rama and Ravana.**

It was the mother of all battles. In that battle, Rama was facing the worst of the enemies in Ravana, who was the most powerful warrior on the face of the earth at that time. Ravana had all the power and implements of war with him. He was fighting the war in his home turf. He had *sthanabala*, the special advantage that a person gets when he is in his own territory. Rama came from a faraway place, and was fighting a war in a distant land. Additionally, Rama was in distress because his dear wife Sita was kidnapped by Ravana. It was the world's first episode of kidnapping, of course, not for a ransom. Ravana initially sent all his war-chiefs, including his ministers, sons, brothers and other followers to fight against Rama. Rama destroyed all of them. They were like so many moths attracted and burnt into ashes by the fire called Rama. Even his invincible brother, Kumbhakarna was slain by Rama in the battlefield. Ravana came into the battlefield at the far end of that historic battle. He faced Rama once in the very beginning of the war, but quickly retreated after tasting the sting of Rama's arrows. Again, he came at the end since no warrior of repute was left in his camp. Even so, he remained invincible.

Rama was the greatest of the warriors (*ramassastrabhrtam varah*). He exterminated a very large number of demons opposing dharma. Even so, Rama was again face to face with a serious problem. His life was full of problems. If everything is comfortable and cozy, following *dharma* is easy. The issue is: when one faces the real test, when adversity stares one in the face, does he follow *dharma* or not? In such a situation, people generally complain: 'O, I have worshipped *Isvara* with devotion; but look what happened to me?' So, Rama is facing the ultimate crisis of his life and a grand test of his valour and his commitment to *dharma*.

Let us now begin the study of the text *Aditya Hrdayam*.

## \[1\]-\[2\]

*tato yuddhaparisrantam samare cintaya sthitam I*  
*ravanamcagrato drstva yuddhaya satnupasthitam II*  
*daivataisca samagamya drastumabhyagato ranam I*  
*upagamyabravidramamagastyo bhagavanrsih II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*tatah*) — afterwards, (*yuddhaparisrantam*) — exhausted in the battle, (*samare*) — in the battle, (*chintaya*) — with worry, (*sthitam*) — standing, (*ramam*) — Rama, (*ca*) — and, (*agratah*) — ahead, (*yuddhaya*) — for the battle, (*samupasthitam*)— standing ready, (*ravatiam*) — Ravana, (*drstva*) — having seen, (*daivataih samagamya*) — having come together with the gods, (*ranam*) — battle, (*drstum*) — to see, (*abhyagatah*) — having arrived, (*bhagavan*) — the revered, (*agastyah*) — Agastya,(*tada*) — then, (*upgamya*) — having approached, (*abravit*) — spoke.

**Перевод:**

**At that time, Rama was very much exhausted in the battle. While standing in the battlefield, he was filled with worry. The sage Agastya saw him. Agastya arrived there together with the gods to witness the battle. Agastya also saw Ravana who arrived ready for the battle. The revered sage then approached Rama and talked to him.**

**Комментарий:**

Ravana, the embodiment of all power, is standing there fully equipped with all the paraphernalia of warfare. Though he was beaten by Rama on an earlier occasion, he has regained some composure and enthusiasm since then. Ravana was indeed a great person except for the *adharma* he had committed. He kidnapped Sita and kept her captive under his control. He did not repent and atone for his misdeed when an opportunity was provided to him. Except for this major sin in his life, he was otherwise a great scholar and devotee of *Isvara*. What is not there in Ravana? His wealth, courage, and military strength have no parallel. When Hanuman saw Ravana for the first time, he was stunned by the brilliance of his countenance and the grandeur of his court. Initially Ravana ordered that Hanuman be put to death, but when Vibhisana told that killing a messenger is an act unworthy of a warrior, he thanked Vibhisana for reminding him about *dharma* and rescinded the order. He is quite a formidable enemy.

Rama is tired and worried. It is like a match point situation in the grand-slam tennis final. If only he wins one more point, he becomes the champion. But by that time, he is extremely tired and he does not have energy even to serve the ball. But then he has to score one more point, and the opponent is ready to grab that point away from him. How desperate he must feel! Rama's feelings are quite similar at this stage. This Ravana, the culprit, is there standing before him ready for the battle. He is a great warrior. How to defeat him? It is yet another major crisis in the life of Rama.

At that time, the gods also came to see this war. And various sages from afar came to see this war. They have the *yoga-sakti* by which they can float in the sky. There are eight *siddhis* (mystic powers) that seekers may achieve by intense practice of yoga. One of the first *siddhis* is called *anima*; it is the ability to become light and float in air. Sage Agastya noticed Rama in distress. Here, Agastya is described as *Bhagavan*. It means a very revered sage. It also means that Agastya is an enlightened person. It is well established in the Upanisads that the knower of *Brahman* is one with *Brahman*.

*sa yo ha vai tatparamam brahma veda brahmavid brahmaiva bhavati*  
*— Mutidakopanisat, 3-2-9*

**The knower of that Supreme** ***Brahman*** **becomes (is) one with** ***Brahman*****.**

Lord Krsna proclaims in the *Gita* (7-18):

*jnani tvatmaiva me matam I*

**It is my final word that the knower of** ***atman*** **is indeed one with me.**

Agastya is a great *jnani*, hence he is called *Bhagavan*. Ramana Maharsi is also called *Bhagavan* because he is a *jnani*.

## \[3\]

*rama rama mahabaho srnu guhyam sanatanam I*  
*yena sarvanartn vatsa samare vijayisyase II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*rama rama*) — O Rama, Rama!, (*mahabaho)* — O mighty armed one!, (*sanatanam*) — eternal, (*guhyam*) — secret mantra (*srnu*) — listen (receive), (*vatsa*) — dear son!, (*yena*) — by which, (*samare*) — in the battle, (*sarvan*) — all (*arin*) — enemies, (*vijayisyase*) — will definitely conquer.

**Перевод:**

**O Rama, Rama, you are a mighty armed warrior. Listen my dear. I will initiate you into an eternal secret** ***mantra*** **(an encomium of the Lord) by which you will conquer all the enemies in the battle.**

**Комментарий:**

There is a lull in the battle, but it is only a calm before the storm. The battle is poised to resume after the rest period. Sage Agastya is waiting for such an opportunity; when he gets that opportunity, he seizes it and comes to Rama. He addresses him, 'O Rama, Rama!' This style of addressing clearly indicates that the blessed sage is anxious to talk to Rama. He wants to give something to Rama before the battle with the indefatigable Ravana resumes. He also addresses him as 'O mighty armed one!' (Mahabaho). Lord Krsna addresses Arjuna similarly in the *Gita* many a time. It originally came from Valmiki. There is remarkable influence of Valmiki in Vyasa's works, especially in the *Mahabharata*. In poetic style, in the narration of the story, in describing dharma and in discussing Upanisadic secrets, Vyasa has emulated Valmiki. Valmiki's contribution to the development of the Sanskrit language and literature is immense and unique. Maha-bahu is one with mighty arms. Sometimes, the sheer physical strength of the hands and legs may not be enough to win in a given situation. 'O Rama, be attentive and listen to me. I want to teach you a *mantra*.'

*Guhya* or *rahasya* (secret) implies a *mantra,* because mantra is considered very valuable to the seeker in his journey to the Reality. The seeker is supposed to hold on to it in his heart for the lifetime. One should not go around telling that he has done the *japa* (recitation) of this or that *mantra*. This *mantra* is *sanatana*, eternal, which means that it has its origin in the Vedas. *Srimadramayanam* very closely resembles the Vedic literature in its content, dictum, and idiom. In fact, it is said:

*vedavedye pare pumsi jate dasarathatmaje I*  
*vedah pracetasadasit saksadramayanatmana II*

**When the Cosmic Person known through the teachings of the Vedas is born as Rama, the son of Dasaratha, the Veda itself took the incarnation of** ***Srimadramayanam*** **through Valmiki.**

This is the reason why the worship of Hiranyagarbha in the solar orb, which is the hallmark of Vedic culture, is presented in its totality in the *Srimadramayanam*. In *Srimadramayanam*, as in the Vedas, Visnu is the Sun. Visnu means the all-pervading *Isvara*. He is in the form of the Sun before us. That is why the Sun is also called Suryanarayana. In fact, *Bhagavan* says in the *Gita* (10-21):

*adityanamaharh visnuh I*

**I am Visnu among the offspring of Aditi.**

We may think that probably the *mantra* could wait for some other occasion! Rama is now in the midst of a battle. Is this the time for a *mantra*? Agastya addresses Rama with all love and affection: 'My dear boy, come near me. I will teach this *mantra* to you with the help of which you will be able to vanquish your enemies.'

## \[4\]

*adityahrdayam punyam sarvasatruvinasanam I*  
*jayavaham japam nityamaksayam paramam sivam*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*adityahrdayam*) — Adityahrdayam, (*japam*) — the mantra or stotra (encomium), (*punyam*) — sacred, , (s*arvasatruvinasanam*) — one which helps to vanquish all the enemies, (*jaya-avaham*) — one which secures victory, (*nityam*) — eternal, (*aksayam*) — unfailing, (*paramam*) — supreme, (*sivam*) — auspicious.

**Перевод:**

**Listen to this sacred and eternal** ***mantra*** **or** ***stotra*** **(encomium) called** ***Aditya-Hrdayam*****. It will help you gain victory by vanquishing all the enemies. It is unfailing and supremely auspicious.**

**Комментарий:**

Sage Agastya says: I will teach you a *stotra* which is called *Aditya- Hrdayam*. The name *Aditya-Hrdayam* is very significant. This *stotra* is an elaborate description of the Lord in the form of Aditya, the Sun. Also, there is no essential difference between Aditya, the presiding deity of the solar orb that is before our eyes, and the *atman*, the awareness obtaining in this body; and that *atman* is visualised in *upasana* in the heart, *hrdayam*. In the Vedic culture, if you ask the question, where the Lord is?, the answer is:

*anoraniyan mahato mahiyanatma guhayam nihito'sya jantoh I*  
*— Mahanarayanopanisat, 10*

**The** ***atman*****, which is smaller than the smallest and bigger than the biggest, abides in this living being in the cave of the heart.**

That is the address of *Isvara*. He abides in the cave of the intellect in all living beings. So, *Isvara* has now got two addresses; one is the Sun, and the other is the Heart. Thus, this *stotra* which elucidates the meaning of Gayatri *mantra*, is named very appropriately as *Aditya- Hrdayam*. This is *punya*; it sanctifies the seeker who recites it. When the Sun rises in the eastern horizon, the entire globe is sanctified because of it. Dirty places remain dirty, if enough sunlight does not come in. The seeker's mind becomes free of impurities like suffering by reciting this *stotra*.

*Sarvasatruvinasanam*. Visualise the glory of *Isvara* in the solar orb, and also contemplate upon that Isvara in your heart. It gives you a wonderful freshness and purity of heart with the help of which you can overcome all the internal enemies. What are these enemies? *Kama* (desire), *krodha* (anger), *lobha* (greed*), moha* (infatuation), *mada* (arrogance), and *matsarya* (jealousy). This group of the six internal enemies is called *arisadvarga*. In life too, it is a Rama-Ravana battle every day, because life is full of conflicts and problems. Nobody has an easy life. By the recitation of this *stotra*, the spirit of dharma is instilled in the heart of the seeker, so that he can face the battles of life with courage and determination without violating *dharma*. That is the significance of teaching the Gita *Aditya-Hrdayam* in the battlefield. It is not a teaching that is done in a cave in Uttara Kasi or some isolated place. It is not a teaching that is exclusively meant for some renunciates. It is for the people who are engaged in the battle of life.

*Jayavaham*: Recitation of this *stotra* gives us victory by destroying these enemies, internal and external. External enemies are in the form of various obstacles in the way of progress. Internal enemies are misplaced emotional values and a wrong value structure. A man is a slave to his own internal value structure. He cannot live an independent life, because he imprisons himself in this value structure. So he needs support to overcome these impediments; the recitation of this *stotra* provides the required support. If memorised, it takes less than ten minutes to recite these verses. One may do so on a daily basis. One can also easily recite it in a journey. Ideally, you may stand before the rising Sun and recite it.

*Aksayam*, immortal and unfailing. *Brahman* is *aksaya*. Therefore the encomium of *Brahman* is also *aksaya*.

*Sivam*, very auspicious. There are some things that are very auspicious. *Tilaka* on the forehead is auspicious. Prostrating before the Lord is an auspicious act. Paying respect to the elders and receiving their blessing is also auspicious. Charity is auspicious. Life should have an auspicious value structure. A good photograph in the house is auspicious. The moment you enter a house and see a picture of Lord Krsna teaching the *Gita*. That is auspicious. Auspiciousness has to be maintained in life. Reciting these verses is very auspicious, because it brings prosperity here and hereafter.

## \[5\]

*sarvamangalamangalyam sarvapapapranasanam I*  
*cintasokaprasamanamayurvardhanamuttamam li*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*sarvamangalamangalyam*) — the most auspicious among the auspicious things, (*sarvapapapranasanam*) — one which completely destroys all the evil propensities, (*cintasokaprasamanam*) — one which mitigates the worry and affliction, (*ayurvardhanam*) — one which enhances the lifespan, (*uttamam*) — the best.

**Перевод:**

**This** ***stotra*** **is the most auspicious among the auspicious. It completely destroys all the evil propensities in the seeker and thus mitigates the worry and affliction. It enhances the lifespan of the seeker. It is the best encomium.**

**Комментарий:**

There are many auspicious things in the world. Which is the best among all? We have a verse that sets this doubt to rest.

*atikalyanarupatvat nityakalyanasamsrayat I*  
*smartrnam varadatvacca brahma tanmangalam param II*

**That** ***Brahman*** **is the embodiment of supreme auspiciousness. It is the eternal source of all auspiciousness. People who dwell upon it are blessed by it. Hence, That Brahman is the auspiciousness supreme.**

Any reference to *Brahman* is auspicious. In the beginning of the *Brahmasutra Bhasya*, Sri Sankara did not compose the customary verse of prayer. Hence, an objection is raised: this work cannot be auspicious, since the prayer is missing. This argument is refuted thus: here, Sri Sankara refers to *atman*, which is non-different from *Brahman*. There cannot be anything more auspicious than that. Every other thing becomes auspicious by association with *Brahman*. That *Brahman* is before us in the solar orb. Standing before the Sun itself is the most auspicious thing. What to speak of reciting these verses?

*Sarvapapapranasanam*. When the devotee contemplates upon the Sun in his heart while reciting these verses, all wrong thoughts disappear and the mind immediately gets purified. In fact, it can take the seeker to the level of knowledge of *Brahman* Itself.

*Cintasokaprasamanam*. Every human being is generally afflicted with two things. One is worry and fear about the future (*cinta*), and therefore we suffer from a sense of insecurity. The other is sorrow about the past (*soka*). We carry the past into the present in the form of sorrow. All good things of the past are readily forgotten; only the bad things are remembered. We have a problem associated with the present also. It is *moha*, the delusion, the absence of discrimination (*viveka*). We take non-*atman* as *atman* and vice versa. This is not a condemnation of human existence. It is just a statement of fact. Human nature is like that. Every human being suffers from them. So condemnation solves nothing. You recognise the fact and seek a solution. Here is the solution. You recite *Aditya-Hrdayam* regularly and contemplate upon *Isvara*; your afflictions and worries are then gone by the grace of *Isvara*.

Ayurvardhanam. Scriptures say that we may desire to gain health by propitiating to the Lord Sun.

*arogyam bhaskaradicchet I*

**One may aspire for good health from the Sun.**

Health is wealth. What would one prefer? Health with a modest bank balance or illness with a fat bank balance? Of course, the former. If one has to visit a hospital, one hundred thousand rupees amount to nothing. Just paying for the diagnostic tests will exhaust that amount. So, good health is equal to a lot of wealth. There can be no better wealth than the health. If we worship the Sun every day in the morning by offering *arghya* (water sanctified by the recitation of Gayatri *mantra*), followed by the chanting of these verses, as already mentioned, the mind becomes purified. When the mind is pure and cheerful, the body becomes healthy automatically. If the seeker persists in his unflinching devotion, mundane benefits like prosperity, good health, etc., will automatically accrue to him. Health comes natural to the person who worships *Isvara* in the form of the Sun both in the morning and evening. Basically, health is in the mind. Suppose one gets up in the morning feeling dull and does not feel like taking bath. So he drinks a cup of coffee and reads newspaper or watches TV. That is the sign of ill-health. On the other hand, suppose one gets up from the bed enthusiastically, takes bath, propitiates the Lord Sun by reciting these verses; that is good health. There is no other health. Of course, there may be some problems of the physical body to be taken care of. But the doctor will also be able to help more effectively if one has this attitude. If the person does not have a proper value system, the doctor's efforts also get defeated many times. Regular exercise is good for health; taking proper medicine at the time of need is good for health; *Aditya-Hrdayam* does not replace them. It is *uttama*, the best support in life. One should have prayer as an integral part of life.

## \[6\]

*rasmimantam samudyantam devasuranamaskrtam I*  
*pujayasva vivasvantam bhaskaram bhuvanesvaram II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*rasmimantam*) — the one with rays, (*samudyantam*) — rising swiftly, (*devasuranamaskrtam*) — the one who is propitiated by gods and demons, (*vivasvantam*) — the one who dispels darkness or the one who is the repository of wealth, (*bhuvanesvaram*) — the overlord of all worlds, (*bhaskaram*) — the Sun, (*pujayasva*) — worship him.

**Перевод:**

**You worship the Sun. As He rises swiftly with his rays, even gods and demons propitiate Him. He dispels the darkness. He is the ultimate source of all wealth. He is the Overlord of all the worlds.**

**Комментарий:**

Sage Agastya advises Rama, who is the greatest of warriors and an incarnation of Visnu thus: *pujayasva vivasvantam*, worship the Sim! What to speak of common people like us! One can imagine how much more applicable this advice is to common people like us.

*Vivasvantam, vivasanavantam*, one who drives away the darkness. Like light darkness is twofold; the outer and the inner (ignorance). By worshipping the Sun, the seeker, in addition to gaining good physical health, can also overcome the dullness of mind. Vivasvan also means, *visesena vasuman*, the one who has immense wealth. The Sun is the ultimate source of all wealth.

Here a few names of the Sun are given. The names of God are indeed the attributes of God. It is not so for us. A person, who did not offer one drop of *soma* in the fire, may be named *somayaji* (one who performed the ritual called *somayaga*), if his grandfather happens to be one. *Namasankirtana*, recitation of the names of the Lord with devotion, is one of the most important ways of worshipping the Lord. But one may preferably know the meaning of the names.

*Rasmimantam samudyantam*. The Sun, as He rises in the east, is the repository of rays and energy. There is a *mantra* with similar description recited in *sandhya-vandanti*:

*udutyam jatavedasam devam vahanti ketavah I*  
*drse visvaya suryam II*  
*— Taittiriya Samhita, 4-43*

**The rays are bringing out the Sun, who is the fire in the outer space (who is the origin of Vedic knowledge) from down below to the higher sky, thereby enabling all living beings to have a glimpse of Him.**

*Devasuranamaskrtam.* Devas are the gods or angels and *asuras* are the demons. The well-known *devas* like Indra, Varuna, Yama, etc., worship the Sun, who is the symbol of the Cosmic Person. Asuras like Virocana also worship the Lord in the solar orb. This is *adhidaiva* meaning. Also, both *devas* and *asuras* live in our midst. *Devas* are the people of wisdom, people who serve the society. Asuras are the bad people, like smugglers, rowdies, etc., who harm the society. These *devas*, namely the sages, the wise and the pious, are all worshipping the Sun as He rises in the east. Even *asuras* worship the Sun in a way. They too seek the grace of the Lord for the success of their evil designs. They are also waiting for the Sun to rise to begin their nefarious activities. This is *adhibhuta* meaning. These devas and asuras are actually within us. *Devas* are the good thoughts and asuras are the opposite. Both kinds of thoughts have their existence in the atman, the Awareness. *Devas* are the sense-organs. When they indulge in carnal pleasures, they are called *asuras* (*asusu ramante iti*). In any case, they derive their energy from the Sun alone. They function in the Awareness that is *atman* alone. This is the *adhyatma* meaning.

*Bhaskaram*, one who gives illumination. The Sun soaks the entire world with his light. The illumination is twofold: the outer, in which we are able to see all objects; the inner, the light of awareness in which all knowledge takes place. The Sun abides in the cave of the heart of the seeker as the awareness that is *atman* and illuminates various mind modifications. He blesses the sense-organs and the organs of actions with their respective functions.

*Bhuvanesvaram*, the overlord of all the worlds. This entire universe is nourished and protected by the Cosmic Person, who abides in the solar orb. All the life forms on earth are under the overall protection and control of the Sun.

## \[7\]

*sarvadevatmako hyesa tejasvi rasmibhavanah I*  
*esa devasuraganan lokan pati gabhastibhih II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*tejasvi*) — brilliant, (*rasmibhavanah*) — the one who creates and protects with his rays, (*esah*) — this Sun, (*sarvadevatmakah hi*) — is indeed the embodiment of all gods, (*esah*) — this Sun, (*devasurganan*) — the groups of gods and demons, (*lokan*) — their worlds, (*gabhastibhih*) — with the rays, (*pati*) — safeguards.

**Перевод:**

**This brilliant Sun alone is in the form of all the gods. He illuminates all the objects of the world with his rays. He protects the groups of gods and demons and their worlds with his rays.**

**Комментарий:**

*Esah*, this Sim. The word *esah* is used when a thing in front is pointed. This verse points out that *Isvara* is in front of us in the form of the brilliant Sun. How can anyone say that *Isvara* is not around? People want to see *Isvara*. A devotee approached a *Mahatma* with a desire to see Isvara. The *Mahatma* wanted to know what kind of Isvara does he desire to see. He wants to see *Isvara* holding conch, discus, mace and sword in his four hands. The *Mahatma* asked the devotee: 'OK, I will show that Isvara to you. What will you do with that Isvara? *Isvara* is there before us as the Sun. You have done nothing about it. You are blind to that Isvara. *Isvara* is there very much before us. But we fail to recognise Him. What a misfortune!

*Sarvadevatmako hyesah*. The Sun stands for Hiranyagarbha, the Cosmic Person. All gods like Indra, Varuna, Agni, etc., are so many limbs of that Cosmic Person, since each god is an expression of the Universal Power.

*Rasmibhavanah*. The Sun creates and sustains life on this planet with his rays. There is creation all around us. The blades of grass sprout from the earth. Plants bear flowers and fruits. All this process of creation is in place because of the Sim's rays.

The Sun protects and nurtures all the gods and demons. The Sun is a *karmayogi*. He does not discriminate between the good and the evil. A *mantra* of *Vajasaneya-Samhita* (36-18) advises the human beings thus:

*mitrasya caksusa sarvani bhutani samiksantam I*

**You may have the vision of the Sun towards all life forms.**

The Sun gives light and warmth to good and bad people alike. A carnivorous animal gets its energy from the Sun. Herbivorous animals like cow also get their energy from the Sun. We should learn from this to avoid judging others. We should recognise the universal *atman* in all people. We should recognise *Isvara* in all life forms.

## \[8\]

*esa brahma ca visnusca sivasskandah prajapatih I*  
*mahendro dhanadah kalo yamassomo hyapam patih II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*esah hi*) — This Sun indeed is, (*brahma*) — the creator, (*visnuh*) — the sustainer, (*ca*) — and, (*sivah*) — the most auspicious, (*skandah*) — the life force or the Lord Kumara, (*prajapatih*) — the overlord of all life forms, (*mahandrah*) — the ultimate source of all strength or Lord Indra, (*dhanadah*) — one who gives wealth, or Lord Kubera, (*kalah*) — the originator of time, or one who is in the form of time, (*yamah*) — the controller, or Lord Yama, (*somah*) — the Lord together with the Universal Power, or Lord Moon, (*apam patih*) — the Lord of waters, or Varuna.

**Перевод:**

**This, the most auspicious Sun, indeed is the creator and sustainer of the universe. He is the life force of this universe. He is Lord Kumara. He is the overlord of all life. He is the ultimate source of all strength as Lord Indra. He is the giver of all wealth as Lord Kubera. As the God of Time, he is the originator of time. He controls all life as Lord Yama. He is the Godhead endowed with the Universal Power. He is Lord Moon. He is the Lord of waters, Varuna.**

**Комментарий:**

People talk of trinity. Descriptions of Brahma, Visnu and Rudra are taken literally by people. They think that there are really three persons identified by those names. These three persons also seem to be engaged in mutual confrontation. But the *Taittiriyopanisat* (3-1) says:

*yato va imani bhutani jayante I yena jatani jwanti I*  
*yatprayantyabhisamvisanti I tadvijijnasasva I*  
*tadbrahmeti II*

**Desire to know That, from which all the things of the Universe have originated, by which they are sustained, and into which they resolve. That is Brahman.**

So, the Godhead is defined as the One who/which creates, sustains and annihilates this universe. The Ultimate Reality is one, not three. Valmlki says that this Sun alone is Brahma, Visnu and Rudra. All life on earth derives its existence from the rays of the Sun. The Sun who sustains life by giving heat and light is Visnu. The Sun causes time, and thus brings death to all life forms. When the Sun becomes cool, all creation comes to an end.

*Skandah*. The particular aspect of *Isvara* in the form of energy is called Skanda. That Skanda, the God of energy, is before us as the Sun.

*Prajapatih*, the progenitor of all life forms in this world. In the Vedic scheme of creation, in the first step, the Cosmic Person manifests by His will as Manu, are carries the creation forward. This Manu is called a Prajapati. Being the offspring of the Cosmic Person, he is one with Him. Accordingly, the Sun is also identified with Prajapati.

*Dhanadah*, the one who gives wealth. An oil tycoon in Texas is a billionaire because of the oil wealth. Where did this oil come from? The Sun god has given all the oil in the world. Agricultural produce is another important source of riches. Once again, the Sun is the source of that wealth. Nowadays, sports and games are a great source of wealth. Players of tennis, soccer, etc., make millions. Their health and vigour of the body is the source of wealth for them. That health and vigour is once again gained from the Lord Sun. In this world, there is only one source of all wealth. And that is *Isvara* in the form of the Sun.

He is *kala*, the death. *Taittiriya Aranyaka* (1-14) describes thus:

*yo'sau tapannudeti I sa sarvesam bhutanam*  
*prananadiyodeti I — asau yo'stameti I sa sarvesam*  
*bhutanam prananadayastameti II*

**This Sun is rising. While doing so, He is taking away the life forces of all living beings. — The Sun is setting. While doing so, He is drawing towards Him the vital energies of all the life forms.**

Here is the Sun rising. What a beautiful sight! Poets write poems on the rising Sim. But, every time the Sun rises, he reduces our lifespan somewhat. Similarly, while setting, the Sun withdraws another small part from the lives of all living beings. The process of ageing and ripening is caused by the rays of the Sun. The fruit ripens only to fall down. Body is ripening, and the cells are ageing. That is all the handiwork of the Sim. The Lord of Death is not hiding anywhere. He is there right in front of us as the Sun. In the Puranas, the Lord of Death is presented as the son of Lord Sun. In Vedanta, we learn to smile at death, because the Lord of death is himself the teacher of this knowledge. Death can teach us much that life can never teach. People consider death as a cataclysmic event, and they are aghast when it occurs. That is not correct. Lord Krsna presents death in its true perspective in the *Gita* (2-27):

*jntasya hi dhruvo mrtyurdhruvam janma mrtasya ca I*

**The one who is born is bound to die, and the one who dies is bound to take birth again.**

We consider death as inauspicious, because we are afraid of death. But, Lord Rudra, the destroyer of the universe, resides in the cremation ground. There is nothing inauspicious about it. All this universe is a manifestation of *Brahman* alone. In this creation, either everything is inauspicious because everything is unreal (*mithya*), or everything is auspicious because everything is *Brahman*. The Sun is called *kala*, the Time, because there is an important oneness between time and the Sun. The Sun is the origin of the concept of time itself. This aspect is described in the *Taittiriya Aranyaka* (1-2) as follows:

*suryo maricimadatte I sarvasmtidbhuvanadadhi I*  
*tasyah pdka-visesena I smrtam kalavisesanam II*

**The Sun spreads his rays over all the world. The rays produce chemical changes, which define the changes in time.**

*Yamah, yamayati iti*, one who controls. The Sun controls everything including time, the seasons, the flow of the rivers, the weather patterns, the oceans, the ocean currents, etc.

*Apam patih*, the overlord of waters. All flora and fauna depend on water. Water is indeed life. Thus, it is possible to recognise *Isvara* in every aspect of life. Such a recognition makes life easy, simple, and auspicious.

## \[9\]

*pitaro vasavassadhya asvinau maruto manuh I*  
*vayurvahnih prajah prana rtukarta prabhakarah II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*pitarah*) — the manes, (*vasavah*) — the Vasus, (*sadhyah*) — the Sadhyas, (*asvinau*) — the Asvinis, (*marutah*) — the Maruts, (*manuh*) — the progenitor Manu, (*vayuh*) — the wind god, (*vahnih*) — the fire god, (*prajah*) — the life forms, (*pratiah*) — the life force, (*rtukarta*) — the maker of seasons, (*prabhakarah*) — the source of all illumination.

**Перевод:**

**The manes, Vasus, Sadhyas, Asvinis, Maruts, Manu, the wind god, the fire god are one with the Sun. The Sun is indeed the Cosmic Power and He alone manifests as all these life forms. The Sim, who illuminates everything, causes the seasons.**

**Комментарий:**

*Pitarah*, our ancestors, who are in majority. The Sun with the name Aryaman represents all the manes. The ancestral rites are generally performed when the Sun is at its full brilliance, namely, at noon.

*Vasavah*, the Vasus, a group of eight gods. Agni (god of fire) is one of them. All gods represent various limbs of Hiranyagarbha, the Sun.

*Sadhyas* are another group of gods. The Vedas describe many such groups of gods. All these gods are indeed several limbs of the Universal Person represented by the Sun. Sadhya also means a yogi, the seeker who practices yoga. All such seekers are identified with the Sun.

*Asvinau* are the smallest of the groups of gods, consisting of only two gods. They are the divine physicians. If Indra gets a stomach pain, he sends for them.

*Marutah*. Maruts constitute another group of forty-nine gods. Together they constitute what is called Wind God. Wind blows ever from one corner of the globe to the other, thus keeping the life forms alive. This huge energy of the wind is once again derived from the Sun alone.

*Manuh*, *manute iti*; one who knows everything. In the *Gita*, the fourth chapter opens as follows:

*sribhagavanuvaca I*  
*imam vivasvate yogam proktavanahamavyayam I*  
*vivasvan manave praha manuriksvakave'bravit II*

**The blessed Lord spoke thus: I gave this knowledge at the time of creation to Lord Sun, who in turn gave it to Manu, and who in turn gave it to Iksvaku.**

This is the reason why the Sun is considered as omniscient. It is said that the sage Yajnavalkya, the seer of *Yajurveda*, gained all that knowledge from the Sun. Also, there is a clan of kings after the Sun god and Lord Rama belongs to that clan. Sage Agastya was advising Rama to propitiate the originator of his own clan, the Sun. Manu is talked about as the son of the Sun as well as of the Cosmic Person, because of the essential identity between the two. Manu is the progenitor of all life forms. The human being is named after him as *manava*, the offspring of Manu. In the *Brhadaranyakopanisat* (1-4-3), it is described that Hiranyagarbha, the creator, created at first Manu and Satarupa by dividing himself into the two of them.

*Vayuh*, the wind god, or the life force pervading this body, is also a manifestation of the Sun alone.

*Vahnih, vahati iti*, One who contains the essence of all life within Himself. Vahni is also the fire. The *Taittiriya Aranyaka* (1-20) gives a very interesting description of Aditi, the Universal Power as follows:

*adityassarvo'gnih prthivyam I vayurantarikse I*  
*suryo divi I candrama diksu I naksatrani svaloke II*

**All fire on earth, the wind in the atmosphere, the Sun in the interstellar space, the moon moving around the earth in the quarters, and the stars in their respective galactical positions are indeed the manifestations of Aditi, the Universal Power.**

*Prajah, prajayante iti*, all forms of life. The entire life on this planet is solely dependent on the heat and light of the Sun*. Encyclopedia of Science and Technology* (vol. 10, p. 184; McGraw-Hill, New York, 1966) gives the following graphic account of how the Sun sustains this globe:

Photosynthesis is one particularly important natural process. It involves the manufacture, in light, of organic compounds (primarily carbohydrates) from inorganic materials with simultaneous liberation of oxygen, by chlorophyll-containing plant cells. — The light energy taken by the green pigment, chlorophyll, is partially converted by photosynthesis into stored chemical energy. Together, the two aspects of photosynthesis — the conversion of inorganic into organic matter and the conversion of light energy into chemical energy — make it the fundamental process of life on earth. It is the unique source of all living matter and of all life on our planet. The elements carbon, oxygen, and hydrogen are exchanged through photosynthesis in an endless cycle between the organic and inorganic worlds. Therefore, life on earth can be maintained only by the constant supply of solar energy and its utilisation through plant photosynthesis.

*Pranah, pranayati iti.* The entire motive power and the power of thinking in the living beings of this world comes from the Sun. That is why the Sun is the best representative of the Universal Person (Hiranyagarbha or *Isvara*) and the eastern horizon is His best temple. This fact was recognised by the ancient Vedic sages. Agastya advises Rama to worship the Sun rising in the eastern skies.

Generally, we take the life in the body as an independent entity. This is not true. The body is alive, because the Universal Power reflects in it. That Universal Power called pram has its origin in the para-Brahman. This point is beautifully explained in a verse in the Kathopanisat (2-2-1).

*na pranena napanena martyo jivati kascana I*  
*itarena tu jivanti yasminnetavupasritau II*

**No mortal person is alive because of inhalation and exhalation. All living beings are alive because of that Universal Power (or the Brahman from which it originates). The inhalation and exhalation are in place because of that Power.**

*Rtukarta*, the maker of the seasons. Different seasons like autumn, spring, etc., obviously owe their origin to the Sim.

*Prabhakarah*, a synonym to Bhaskarah.

## \[10\]

*adityassavita suryah khagah ptisa gabhastiman I*  
*suvarnasadrso bhanurhiranyareta divakarah II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*adityah*) — the offspring of Aditi, (*savita*) — the creator, (*suryah*) — the Sun, (*khagah*) — one who travels in the outer space, (*pusa*) — one who nourishes, (*gabhastiman*) — one who has multitude of rays, (s*uvarnadrsah*) — one who resembles pure gold, (*bhanuh*) — the shining, (*hiranyaretah*) — the energy-seed of the universe, (*divakarah*) — one who causes daybreak.

**Перевод:**

**The Sun, son of Aditi, the creator of all life, pervades all. He is the traveller in the sky, nourisher of all life and the source of all light. He shines golden yellow (early in the morning). He is the energy which was the seed of this universe. He causes the day and night.**

**Комментарий:**

*Adityah*, the son of Aditi. In the Puranas, Aditi is described as the mother of all gods. This Aditi of the Puranas is indeed a symbolic representation of the Universal Power. It is thus described in the *Kathopanisat* (2-1-7):

*ya pranena sambhavatyaditirdevatamayi I*  
*guham pravisya tisthantim ya bhutebhirvyajayata II*

**Aditi (the Universal Power), the embodiment of all gods, has manifested (from** ***para-Brahman*****) as Hiranyagarbha together with the great basic elements (or all life forms). She is established in the cave of the heart of the living beings.**

It is indeed in the fitness of things to describe the Sun as the offspring of that Aditi. According to the Big Bang theory of creation of the modern cosmologists, there was a primordial ylem of infinite energy and density before creation. When that ball exploded, galaxies of stars were created. Aditi corresponds to the energy of that Primordial principle.

Savita, the creator. *Isvara* is described as Savita in Gayatri mantra also.

*Suryah, saratiti*, one who is constantly on the move. The Sun appears to go round the earth. Of course, it is the earth which is going round the Sun. But the Sun is not stationary. The Sim is moving in a straight line within the milky way galaxy. There is nothing stationary in this universe. It is also an expanding universe. The Vedas describe the universe as *brahmanda*, the expanding egg. The expansion of the universe was an unknown concept to the West till the first part of the twentieth century, when Hubble discovered that the universe continues to expand.

*Khagah, khe gacchati iti*, one who moves in the universal space.

*Pusa, pusnati iti*; one who provides food for all living beings. In some of these names, there is some repetition. As the one who gives birth to all life forms, the Sun is Savita. The Sun is Pusa as the one who sustains the life forms after they are born.

*Gabhastiman*, one who has rays of light. The Sun radiates many types of rays,, like visible radiation, ultra-violet rays, gamma rays, x-rays, cosmic rays (high speed protons), etc.

*Suvarnasadrsah*, one who shines with brilliant golden yellow colour, while rising in the east.

*Bhanuh*, one who illuminates everything. In the tropical countries like India, the Sun is very brilliant. We dare not look at the afternoon Sun, particularly in the summer.

*Hiranyaretah.* *Retas* means the seed, the seed of the universe. It entirely consists of *hiranya*, which is energy. In the *Taittiriya-Samhita* (6-1-1Q) the word hiranya is defined as energy:

*tejo vai hiranyam I*

**Hiranyam is indeed the fire of energy.**

Therefore, Hiranyaretas is the same as Hiranyagarbha, the progenitor of the universe, who is the single source of all energy of the universe. The Sun stands for that Cosmic Person. All life forms like plants and trees, insects and creatures, birds and animals, humans and angels, have their origin and existence in that Universal Person.

*Divakarah*, the one who makes the day. The entire phenomenal world is dependent upon the concept of time, and its division into days and nights. This division is caused by the movement of the Sun relative to the earth.

*Haridasvah, haritah asnoti iti*, one who pervades the quarters with his rays.

*Sahasrarcih*, one who has infinite rays, pervading the entire solar system. The word *sahasra* means a thousand as well as infinite. Also, the *Brahman* abiding in the cave of the heart as Awareness, has infinite expressions in the form of thoughts.

*Saptasaptih*, one who has seven horses. The Sun seems to travel a lot of distance in a given day, day after day, forever and without fail. He must have a chariot. If so, there must be horses to draw that chariot. Indeed the Vedas symbolically say that the chariot of the Sun is yoked to seven horses. This is beautifully narrated in the *Taittiriya Aranyaka* (3-11) as follows:

*sapta yunjanti rathamekacakram I eka asvo vahati*  
*saptanama I trinabhi cakramajaramanarvam I*

**Seven horses draw the chariot of one wheel. It is indeed only one horse with seven different names. The wheel has three hubs. Nothing can destroy this immortal wheel. All life in this world exists because of those horses and that wheel.**

The wheel is the cycle of time. The three hubs are the past, present, and the future. The seven horses are the seven rays of the spectrum of the composite visible light. It is interesting to note that the spectrum of visible light is recited in Vedic families by kids that are 8 to 10 years old clad in loin-cloth. The same spectrum was rediscovered later by Sir Isaac Newton in seventeenth century.

*arogo bhrajah patarah patangah I svarnaro jyotisiman*  
*vibhasah I te asmai sarve divamatapanti I urjam duhana*  
*anapa-sphuranta iti II — Ibid., 1-7*

**Red, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, and violet are the seven rays of the Sun that reach this earth from the outer space, providing energy to all life forms, yet not causing any harm to them.**

The composite nature of the sunlight, mentioned above metaphorically as the single horse, is clearly mentioned in the same section (1-3) of the *Taittiriya Aranyaka*.

*sakam janUm saptathamShurekajam I*

**The seven rays originate from a single composite ray.**

*Mariciman, timironmathanah* and *amsuman* are different ways of describing the brilliance of the Sim.

*Sambhuh, sam asmadbhavati iti*. The Sun is the source of all comfort and happiness. It is of common experience that the rising Sun dispels our inertia, fills us with freshness and energy, and generally makes us happy. The rays of the morning Sun are not only soothing and comfortable to the body, but they also generate Vitamin D in the body, and make bones stronger. One *mantra* from the *Taittiriya- Samhita* (1-4-23) is a prayer to the Sun as follows:

*vamamadya savitarvamamu svo dive dive vamamasmabhyam savih I*

**O Sun, bestow on us happiness today, tomorrow, and every-day hereafter.**

*Tvasta*, one who alleviates the suffering of the devotees. Diseases have a way of causing all sorts of problems particularly at night. The moment the day breaks, the pain of illness fades away. This is our normal experience. For example, tooth ache is generally acute in the night, and is on the wane by morning. There seems to be a connection between the pain of illness and the night. That is why, a sick person looks forward to the daybreak. He thinks: 'If I can survive the pain through the night, I will be saved by the daybreak.' The Sun mitigates the pain of diseases through his rays.

*Martandakah, mrtam andam jwayati iti. Isvara* as the Sun breathes life into this universe. There is only one life in this creation and that is *Isvara*. It is well known that all life on earth has its origin in the energy poured out by the Sun in the form of light and heat into the system of planets.

**The Sun is the Cosmic Person. He causes comfort and also discomfort with his rays. He rises boisterously and brings forth the daybreak. He is all fire in the core. The Sun, the offspring of Aditi, is the all-pervading** ***Brahman*** **of infinite bliss. He casts off the cold.**

**Комментарий:**

*Hiranyagarbhah, hiranyam garbhe yasya sah*, one who has the Universal energy in Himself. As already mentioned, this description of the Lord is similar to the modern Big Bang theory in some aspects. The primordial ylem from which the universe was created was a dense ball of infinite energy. Its temperature is estimated to be 10degrees, which is beyond anybody's imagination. The energy in that ball was so immense that there was no difference between energy and mass; they merged into one. The particles like protons, neutrons, electrons did not exist in it. They are all latter products. According to modern theory of creation, even the difference between electro-magnetism and gravitation is obliterated inside that ultimate source of the Universe. The *Taittiriya Samhita* (4-1-8) describes the creator thus:

*hiranyagarbhassamavartatagre bhutasya jstah patireka asit I*

**Hiranyagarbha manifested (from Brahman) prior to the creation of this universe. He is the overlord of this universe, which was created out of Himself. He sustains the earth and other worlds of the universe. We worship that non-dual Godhead by offering oblations.**

The main import of this encomium is that Hiranyagarbha, the Creator of this universe, manifests before us as the Sun.

*Sisirah*, comfortably warm. *Tapanah*, its opposite, meaning, quite hot. These are the two aspects of the Sim in the morning and in the afternoon respectively.

*Ahaskarah*, same as *divakarah*. Yet there is a shade of difference between the two meanings. *Ahas* is a day. Even a cloudy day is a day. But, *diva* is a brilliant sunny day. You can have a great picnic on such a day.

*Ravih, rauti iti*-, one who makes a loud sound. *Rava* is sound. In the *Chandogyopanisat* (1-5-1), it is described that as the Sun moves, the sound of om emerges from that movement.

*omiti hyesa svaranneti I*

**This Sun moves forward while making the sound** ***om*****.**

We are at a very long distance from the Sun; 8 x 60 x 86,000 miles to be precise, and the light from the Sun reaches the earth in eight minutes. Hence, we cannot hear the sound of the Sun's motion. Sun is also the teacher of the Vedas, the *Yajurveda* in particular. It is obvious that teaching of the Vedas involves the sound of speech, since the Vedas are learnt in the oral tradition of teaching.

*Agnigarbhah, agnih garbhe yasya sah*. The core of the Sim is the most powerful source of all heat in this solar system. There, nuclear reactions equivalent to billions of hydrogen bombs are taking place every second. If it is said that an atom bomb serves as a trigger for the hydrogen bomb, the power of the latter can only be a matter of imagination at best. The Solar scientists say that the surface of the Sun is relatively cool when compared with the core of the Sun! If that is cool, then what hot could be!

*Aditeh putrah*, the offspring of Aditi, the Universal Power of *Isvara*. Diti is *dvaita*, which is plurality. If we take the plurality that appears before us as the universe to be real, it is ignorance in the vision of the Vedas; and Diti stands for that ignorance. The offspring of Diti are *daityas*, who are the demons. They take the body to be the *atman*, because they are the offspring of ignorance. Aditi stands for Oneness, which is the Reality. The plural can never be Real. Only Unity is Real. Therefore, the offspring of Aditi (the Sun) stands for all knowledge, which is *Brahman.*

*Sankhah, sam ca kham ca*. *Sam* is Happiness and *kham* is space. *Kham* is also *Brahman*. The *Chandogyopanisat* (4-10-4) presents this aspect of *Brahman* as follows:

*prano brahma kam brahma kham brahmeti I*

**The Universal Power is** ***Brahman*****, the happiness is** ***Brahman*****, and the space (of awareness) is** ***Brahman*****.**

So, *sankha* means the space of awareness, which is all happiness, and which is the Ultimate Reality.

*Sisiranasanah, sisiram nasayati iti*. *Sisira* is cold, or the dullness of the mind. The Sun destroys both. Worship of the Sun gives knowledge to the seeker.

*krtaghnaghnaya devaya jyotisam pataye namah II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*tamoghnaya*) — unto the one who dispels darkness, (*himaghnaya*) — unto the one who removes cold, (*satrughnaya*) — unto the one who destroys the enemies, (*amitatmane*) — unto the one whose essential nature is beyond all limitations, (*krtaghnaghnaya*) — unto the one who punishes the ungrateful, (*devaya*) — unto the Lord, (*jyotisam*) — of the luminous bodies, (*pataye*) — unto the overlord, (*namah*) — salutation.

**Перевод:**

**My salutations unto the Lord, who dispels darkness, who removes the cold, who destroys the enemies, and who punishes the ungrateful. He is essentially beyond all limitations. He is the Lord of all luminous bodies.**

**Комментарий:**

*Satrughnaya*, my salutations unto the Lord who destroys the enemies. The negative emotions, ill health, burden of debt, etc., are all considered as enemies, since they hinder the spiritual progress of the seeker.

*Amitatmane, amitah atma svarupam yasya sah*. Salutation unto the One whose nature is beyond the limitations of time and space. The size of the solar orb is 30,000 times greater than all the nine planets put together. This is an unimaginably huge, yet a finite size. Also, the solar orb has a beginning, as it came into being at the time of creation of the universe. But, the Cosmic Person as the presiding deity of the solar orb is eternal. As the originator of both time and space, He is beyond both time and space.

*Krtaghnaghnaya*, prostrations unto the One who destroys the ungrateful. These are the people who fail to recognise the service rendered to them by others, or even by the Lord. The Lord punishes such people. In Hindu scriptures, the gratitude shown for the help received is considered to be a very important virtue. Valmiki describes Rama as a role model of this virtue.

## \[21\]

*taptacamikarabhaya haraye visvakarmane I*  
*namastamo'bhinighnaya rucaye lokasaksine II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*namah*) — salutation, (*taptacami-karabhaya*) — unto the one shining like pure gold, (*haraye*) — destroyer of suffering, (*visvakarmane*) — unto the creator of the universe, (*tamo'bhinighnaya*) — unto the destroyer of darkness, (*rucaye*) — unto the one who is shining, (*lokasaksine*) — unto the witness of the world.

**Перевод:**

**My salutations unto the Lord Sun, who shines like pure gold. He, the creator and witness of this universe, destroys the darkness and sufferings of the seekers.**

**Комментарий:**

*Haraye, harati iti*. The words *harih, harah, hrm and hrt* are all derived from the same verbal root *hrin*, and they all mean the same, namely, *Isvara*. The Lord is called Hari because he destroys the sins and eliminates the darkness of ignorance from the hearts of the devotees.

*Visvakarmane, visvam karma yasya*. This universe is the handiwork of the Lord. All the activities in the world, big and small — an ant crawling, a person running, a big crane lifting loads, etc. — owe their origin directly or indirectly to the Lord Sun. But for the Sun, nothing would move in this world.

*Lokasaksine*, unto the eternal witness of the world. People commit prohibited acts like theft, and congratulate themselves that nobody has seen them in the act. But the Lord Sun is ever there as a witness. This is a beautiful vision of the Vedic sages. The *Taittiriya Samhita* (3-4-11) describes the Lord Sun as follows:

*hiranyayena savita rathena devo yati bhuvana vipasyan I*

**The Lord in the form of the Sun traverses the skies with a golden chariot (the brilliant rays of light), while witnessing all the activities of the world.**

## \[22\]

*nasayatyesa vai bhutam tameva srjati prabhuh I*  
*payatyesa tapatyesa varsatyesa gabhastibhih II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*esah*) — this, (*prabhuh*) — Lord, (*bhutam*) — the universe, (*vai*) — indeed, - (*nasayati*) — destroys, (*tarn eva*) — that again, (*srjati*) — creates, (*esah*) — this Lord, (*payati*) — protects, (*esah*) — this Lord, (*gabhastibhih*) — with the rays, (*tapati*) — heats up, (*esah*) — this Lord, (*varsati*) — rains.

**Перевод:**

**The Lord, as the Sun, creates this Universe, protects it, and finally annihilates it (into Himself). He heats up the world with his rays and thus causes rain.**

**Комментарий:**

*Isvara*, who shines before us as the Sun, creates the entire universe; He also destroys it. The Sun becomes cold at the time of annihilation, and that will be the end of all life. Between the creation and destruction, He protects the universe.

He blazes in the summer, and He gives plenty of water again in the form of rain later. He accomplishes all this with his rays. In the *Gita* (9-19), *Bhagavan* declares thus:

*tapamyahamaham vrstim nigrhnamyutsrjami ca I*

**I cause suffering due to excessive heat. I cause severe evaporation of water (from the water bodies), and then I also cause the downpour of water (in the form of rain).**

## \[23\]

*esa suptesu jagarti bhutesu parinisthitah I*  
*esa caivagnihotram ca phalam caivagnihotrinam II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*esah*) — This Sun, (*bhutesu*) — as the living beings, (*suptesu*) — while fast asleep, (*parinisthitah*) —abiding as the inner vitality, (*jagarti*) — keeps awake, (*esah eva*) — this Lord alone, (*agnihotram*) —the Vedic ritual, (*ca*) — and, (*agnihotrinam*) —of the performers of the Vedic rituals, (*phalam*) — result.

**Перевод:**

**This Sun, abiding in all the living beings as the inner vitality, keeps awake while all of them are fast asleep. He is in the form of the Vedic rituals performed by the Vaidikas. He is also in the form of the results thereof.**

**Комментарий:**

The Sun, abiding in his own orb as the presiding deity, is shining ever, even while all the life on earth goes to sleep after the apparent sunset. It is obvious that while there is a day and night from the point of view of the person standing on earth, the Sun Himself is utterly devoid of any such distinction. To put it poetically, there is no sleep for Him. He always shines.

Also, the same Lord, who shines in the solar orb, abides in the hearts of the living beings as the inner sentience called *antaryami*. As the *atman*, the ultimate Reality, He is ever awake (shining) in the beings while they are asleep. The *Kathopanisat* (2-2-8) also describes *Isvara* in similar terms:

*esa suptesu jagarti kamam kamam puruso nirmimanah I*  
*tadeva sukram tadbrahma tadevamrtamucyate II*

**This** ***atman*****, abiding in the living beings as the Awareness, is beyond the limitations of time and space. While the organs of action and the sense organs are asleep, this** ***atman*** **keeps awake illuminating the dream projected by the mind — the dream consisting of all the things desired. It is said by the learned that this atman, unsullied by these states of mind, is indeed the immortal** ***Brahman*****.**

Sleep is not void. It is obvious that all the functions of life are active in the sleep state also. *Atman*, the Awareness, is ever present in all three states of experience, namely, waking, dream and sleep. Sleep is an experience of the relative absence of everything that appears in the waking state. That is the reason why everybody asserts that he had slept well without knowing anything around him. That sentience which is ever awake and active is an expression of the Infinite in the finite body. *Bhagavan* says in the *Gita* (18-61):

*Uvarassarvabhutanam hrddese'rjuna tisthati I*

**O Arjuna, the Lord abides in the cave of heart of all living beings.**

*Esa caivilgnihotram ca*. He is the fire altar worshipped by the Vaidikas. Even the ritual of offering oblations in the fire is the Lord Visnu, in the form of the Sun, as declared in the *Taittiriya Samhita* (2-5-5):

*yajho vai visnu I*

**The Vedic ritual is indeed Lord Visnu.**

*Phalam caivdgnihotrinam*. He bestows the results of actions upon the individuals. The Vaidika, who performs the fire ritual, is bestowed upon with the heavens after his death by the Lord. *Brahman* alone manifested as this universe. Therefore, that heaven is not different from *Brahman*. Also, *Bhagavan* gives *moksa* (liberation) to the seekers of Self-knowledge and that *moksa* is not different from Him either. *Bhagavan* declares in the *Gita* (7-18):

*jnani tvatmaiva me matam I*

**It is my considered opinion that the knower of the Self is indeed Me.**

## \[24\]

*devasca kratavascaiva kratunam phalamev ca I*  
*yani krtyani lokesu sarvesu paramaprabhuh II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*devah*) — the gods, (*kratavah*) — Vedic rituals, (*raj)* — indeed, (*kratunam*) — of those Vedic rituals, (*phalameva ca*) — even the result also, (*sarvesu*) — all, (*lokesu*) — worlds, (*yani*) — whichever, (*kratyani*) — activities, WWJ: (*paramaprabhuh*) — omnipotent.

**Перевод:**

**The Sun alone manifests as the gods, as the Vedic rituals, and even as their results. The Omnipotent Lord alone expresses Himself in all the activities of all the life forms of all the worlds.**

**Комментарий:**

All the gods — Indra, Varuna, Agni, Soma, etc. who receive the oblations offered in the Vedic rituals — are so many limbs of that Cosmic Person. Sense-organs are also called *devas*. The power of knowing is bestowed on them by the Lord Himself, who is the *atman*. All *pujas*, all offerings or oblations in the fire, various rituals such as *agnistoma, aptoryama, vajapeya, candiyaga, rudrayaga, atirudrayaga*, etc., are all made possible by the grace of the Lord in the form of the Sim. The results of all these rituals, like prosperity in this world, heaven after death, etc., are bestowed upon the deserving by the Lord Himself.

Also, the motive power of all living beings in this world is due to the grace of the Lord Sun. Even the slightest movement is made possible by the energy given by the Sun. Thus, the Sun is the Supreme Overlord of all life and all activities on the globe. All activity in all the worlds is nothing but the expression of the Infinite Power of the Lord Hiranyagarbha.

## \[25\]

*enamapatsu krcchresu kantaresu bhayesu ca I*  
*kirtayan purusah kascinnavasidati raghava II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*raghava*) — O Rama, (*apatsu*) — in calamities, (*krcchresu*) — in adverse situations, (*kantaresu*) — in difficult paths, (*ca*) — and, (*bhayesu*) — in fears, (*enam*) — this Sun, (*kirtayan*) — one who sings the glory, (*purusah*) — person, (*kascit*) — whosoever, (*na avasidati*) — does not get destroyed.

**Перевод:**

**O Rama, whosoever sings the glory of this Sun (through this encomium) will not come to grief, even if such a person finds himself in calamities, dire straits, difficult paths or such other frightening situations.**

**Комментарий:**

O Raghava, O Rama of the clan of Raghu, a person who finds himself in adversities or in very difficult situations, is advised to recite this encomium. He will be rescued from his difficulties. A person who lost his way in a forest or in the forest of life may recite these verses. People who lose their way in a forest are in deep trouble, because they cannot make out the direction in which they are moving. All directions look alike in a deep forest, deep seas and deep space. Figuratively, human life, with all the conflicting interests, is itself a forest. A person may find himself helpless in the face of the challenges posed by the life. In all such situations, the devotees are advised to recite *Aditya-Hrdayam*. It will attract the grace of *Isvara*, and will show the way out. Also, people suffer from all kinds of worries and fears. When one is alone in the house, even a little creaking sound could be a source of fear. There is then the fear of the adversary, as in the case of Rama. Under all such circumstances, one who recites these verses will not come to grief. His life and his goals will be taken care of by the grace of *Isvara*.

## \[26\]

*pujayasvainamekagro devadevam jagatpatim I*  
*etat trigunitam japtva yuddhesu vijayisyati II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*devadevam*) — the Lord of the lords, (*jagatpatim*) — the Overlord of the universe, (*enam*) — this Sun, (*ekagrah*) — having a single-pointed mind, (*pujayasva*) — worship, (*etat*) — this, (*trigunitam*) — three times, (*japtva*) — having recited, (*yuddhesu*) — in the battles, (*vijayisyati*) — will win.

**Перевод:**

**This Sun is the Lord of the lords and the Overlord of the universe. Worship Him with a single-pointed mind. One will secure victory in the battles by reciting these verses thrice.**

**Комментарий:**

One may recite this *stotra* thrice when one is faced with exceptional difficulties. During the normal course, one may recite it once. But, the important point is that one may not recite it mechanically. We have to recite it by focusing the mind, to the extent possible, on the profound meaning of the verses. That was the advice of Agastya to Rama. This *stotra* will give victory to the devotee in all the righteous pursuits.

## \[27\]

*asmin ksane mahabaho ravanam tvam jahisyasi I*  
*evamuktva tato'gastyo jagama sa yathagatam II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*mahsbaho*) — O mighty armed, (*tvam*) — you, (*asmin*) — in this, (*ksane*) — moment, (*ravanam*) — Ravana, (*jahisyasi*) — shall slay, (*evam*) — thus, (*uktva*) — having spoken, (*tatah*) — then, (*sah*) — that, (*agastyah*) — Agastya, (*yathagatam*) — the way he came, (*jagama*) — went.

**Перевод:**

**O mighty armed Rama, you are going to slay Ravana this very moment. Having spoken thus, that sage Agastya went the way he came.**

**Комментарий:**

*Agastya* assures Rama that Ravana's day of doom is at hand. So, Rama may not worry or hesitate. Victory is certain. Victory would come to Rama immediately after reciting this *stotra*. Similar results are bound to occur to us too. Having done his job, Agastya went his way. He did not wait around to be rewarded or even to be thanked. He is a liberated soul; he has no other agenda. He wanted Rama to win. In fact, the gods requested Agastya to secure victory for Rama. One should not think that this narration reduces the importance of Lord Rama. Valmiki presented Rama as the greatest of the human beings, who stood upright in the face of direst adversity, and who protected dharma and retained his own character. In receiving this *stotra* from a great sage and then following his advice by reciting it, Rama presents a wonderful example before the entire mankind.

## \[28\]

*etacchrutva mahateja nastasoko'bhavattada I*  
*dharayamasa suprito raghavah prayatatmavan II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*tada*) — then, (*mahdtejah*) — very brilliant and powerful, (*raghavah*) — Rama, the scion of Raghu clan, (*etat*) — this, (*srutva*) — having heard, (*nastasokah*) one whose affliction is destroyed, (*abhavat*) — became, (*supritah*) — very much pleased, (*prayatatmavan*) — one who has perfect self-control, (*dharayamasa*) — memorised or held on to.

**Перевод:**

**Then Rama, the scion of the Raghu clan, received the advice of Agastya. His affliction was at once cast off. He was very happy. Being a man of perfect self-control, he memorised it and held on to that encomium.**

**Комментарий:**

Rama is potentially a great warrior. He needed this encouragement from Agastya to manifest that innate potential in the battlefield. Recitation of this *stotra* removed his gloom. He regained all his enthusiasm. He now had no doubt about victory. This encomium is a morale booster. Rama immediately knew it by heart. He could commit anything to memory just by reciting once. That speaks volumes about his self-control, since it is not easy to concentrate the mind on an encomium right in the middle of the battlefield.

## \[29\]

*adityam preksya japtvedam param harsamavaptavan I*  
*triracamya sucirbhutva dhanuradaya vtryavan II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*viryavan*) — Rama, the warrior of prowess, (*trih*) — thrice, (*itcamya*) — having sipped water, (sucih) — pure, (*bhutva*) — having become, (*adityam*) — the Sun, (preksya) — having seen, (idam) — this encomium, (*japtva*) — having recited, (*dhanuh*) — bow, (*adaya*) — having taken, (*param*) — immense, (*harsam*) — joy, (*avaptavan*) — gained.

**Перевод:**

**Rama, the warrior of prowess, sipped water thrice and thus purified himself. Then he looked at the Sun and recited this encomium and became immensely joyous. Then he took the bow into the hand.**

**Комментарий:**

Sipping water three times imparts purity to the body as well as mind. When there is no facility to take bath, this practice could be very useful. Even looking at the Sun with devotion purifies and rejuvenates a person. Nowadays, even cricket players from India first display their devotion to the Sun. That is very nice to see. In Indian villages, every farmer prays to the Sun first thing in the morning, after washing the face.

Recitation of this *stotra* delivers an immediate result. There will be an unseen future result too. One has to wait for it. The mechanical nature of modern life makes the mind very agitated and restless. Practice of recitation certainly helps to overcome the stress. Rama at once became very happy after the recitation. He is yet to kill Ravana and regain his beloved Sita. In spite of it, and in the midst of a crisis, the prayer gave him happiness. He took the bow into the hand. He was ready for the battle. This is the immediate result of reciting the *stotra* with devotion.

Worship the Sun for ten minutes in the morning; then have breakfast and go to the waiting world like a hero!

## \[30\]

*ravanam preksya hrstatma jayartham samupagamat I*  
*sarvayatnena mahata vrtastasya vadhe- 'bhavat II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*ravanam*) — Ravana, (*praksya*) — having seen, (*hrstatma*) — filled in the heart with joy, (*jayartham*) — for the sake of victory, (*samupagamat*) — proceeded forward, (*tasya*) — of him, (*vadhe*) — in the slaying, (*mahata*) — great, (*sarvayatnena*) — with an all-round effort, (*vrtah*) — ready, (*abhavat*) — became.

**Перевод:**

**Rama looked at Ravana, and his heart was filled with joy. He proceeded forward to gain victory. He was ready to make a great all-round effort to slay Ravana.**

**Комментарий:**

Earlier he saw Ravana and became dejected. 'My god! I have to kill this person, a great warrior. Will I be able to do that?'. Those were Rama's thoughts, but that crisis of confidence is no longer there. The whole situation had changed. As he saw Ravana again, he became confident that he can defeat him. He was determined to put in all the necessary effort to secure the victory. Prayer can bring about such a dramatic change in the mind. One has to first win the battle against oneself before fighting the situation outside. Therein lies the secret of *Aditya-Hrdayam*.

## \[31\]

*atha raviravadanniriksya ramam muditamanah paramam prahryamanah I*  
*nisicarapatisanksayam viditva suraganamadhyagato vacastvareti II*

**Пословный перевод:**

(*atha*) — Afterwards, (*suraganamadhyagatah*) — one who is in the midst of the groups of gods, (*ravih*) — the Sim, (*ramam*) — Rama, (*niriksya*) — having seen, (*imuditmanah*) — one whose heart is filled with of the king of demons, (*viditva*) — having known, (*paramam*) — very much, (*prahrsyamanah*) — ecstatic, (*tvara*) — hasten, (*iti*) — thus, (*vacah*) — word, (*avadat*) — spoke.

**Перевод:**

**The Sun was in the midst of the groups of gods. He saw Rama and His heart was at once filled with joy. He could foresee the impending destruction of Ravana, the king of demons. That knowledge made Him extremely happy. He spoke only one word to Rama: 'Hurry up'.**

**Комментарий:**

Suddenly one cloud moved aside and the sunlight fell upon Rama. The Sun has showered His grace on him. In his mind, Rama felt that it was a command from the Sun to hurry up. Rama was very happy. This is how the prayer works. As a result of the prayer, the devotee is blessed with an inspiration, which helps him to overcome the difficulties facing him. The Sun god was also very happy, since *dharma*, in the form of Rama, was going to vanquish the evil. The compound, *nisicarapatisanksaya* (destruction of the king of nocturnals) is very significant. When the grace of the Sun is secured, destruction of the nocturnal powers of evil is certain. The Sun, who is the eternal witness of everyone and everything in the world, responds to the prayers of the devotees without delay.
