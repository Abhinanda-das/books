# Bhagavata-Purana 1.1.1 with 2 commentari

## The Opening Verse of Srimad Bhagavatam

j*anmady asya yato ’nvayad itaratas carthesv abhijnah svarat tene brahma hrda ya adi-kavaye muhyanti yat surayah \|*  
*tejo-vart-mrdam yatha vinimayo yatra tri-sargo ’mrsa dhamna svena sada nirasta-kuhakam satyam param dhlmahi \|\|*

**Prose order**

*arthesu anvayat itaratah ca yatah (paramesvarat) asya (visvasya) janmadi (bhavati, yah ca) abhijnah svarat (ca bhavati), yah (ca) hrda adi-kavaye (tad) brahma tene, yat surayo ’pi muhyanti, yatha tejo-vari-mrdam vinimayah (satya-vat bhasate tatha) yatra (ca) tri-sargah amrsa (bhasate, vayam tam paramesvaram) svena dhamna sada nirasta-kuhakam param satyam dhlmahi.*

**Translation**

We meditate (*dhlmahi*) on Him from whom (*yatah*) the creation, the continua­tion and the destruction (*janmadi*) of the universe (*asya = visvasya*) take place because of His connection (*anvayat*) in things (*arthesu*) and (*ca*) because He is altogether distinct (*itaratah*) from them; who is omniscient (*abhijnah*) and independent (*svarat*); who (*yah*) revealed (*tene*) the Vedas (*brahma*) to Brahma (*adi-kavaye*) through his heart (*hrda*), regarding which Vedas (*yat = yasmin brahmani*) even (*api*) the demigods (*surayah*) are bewildered (*muhyanti*); in whom, in His form as Brahman (*yatra = yatra brahma-svarupe*), the world (*tri-sargah*) appears (*bhasate* is supplied) to be real (*amrsa*) like (*yatha*) the mutual \[illusory\] interactions (*vinimayah*) between fire, water, and earth (*tejo-vari-mrdam*) appear to be real (*satya-vat bhasate* is supplied); by whom the tricks of Maya are always (*sada*) dispelled (*nirasta-kuhakam = nirastam kuhakam yena sah*), by His (*svena*) power (*dhamna*); and who is transcenden­tally (*param*) real (*satyam*).

**Introduction**

Sridhara Svami’s commentary on the first verse of *Bhagavatam* is presented henceforward. The source reference is the edition published by Pandita Rama Teja Pandeya (Caukhamba Samskrt Pratisthan, Delhi, 2011). It is the

most authoritative and is accompanied with Sridhara Svami’s text of *Srimad Bhagavatam*. The meaning of *Bhavartha-dipika*, the name of Sridhara Svami’s commentary, is: “the lamp whose purpose is *bhakti*.”

Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu declared that Sridhara Svami is the authoritative commentator on *Bhagavatam*,

*prabhu hasi’ kahe,“svami na mane yei jana \|*  
*vesyar bhitare tare kariye ganana’” \|\|*  
*eta kahi’ mahaprabhu mauna dharila \|*  
*suniya sabara mane santosa haila \|\|*

“With a wry smile, the Lord said: “Whoever disregards the svami is to be counted among the prostitutes.” Mahaprabhu held silence. Everyone felt contentment by hearing this statement” (*Caitanya-caritamrta* 3.7.115-116).

Lord Caitanya also told Vallabhacarya: *sridhara-svami-prasade ‘bhagavata’ jani, jagad-guru sridhara-svami ‘guru’ kari’ mani. sridhara-upare garve ye kichu likhibe ‘artha-vyasta’ likhana sei, loke na manibe. sridharer anugata ye kare likhana saba loka manya kari’ karibe grahana,* “I understand *Bhagavatam* by the grace of Sridhara Svami. I consider that he, the spiritual master of the universe, is a guru. Whatever you write beyond Sridhara out of pride would be contrary to the real meaning. People will not pay attention to it. One who writes by following Sridhara’s statements will be respected by and accepted by everyone” (*Caitanya-caritamrta* 3.7.133-135).

## The Commentary of Sridhara Svami on the First Verse of Bhagavatam

*sri-ganesaya namah. sri-gopala-krsnaya namah. om namo bhagavate parama-hamsasvadita-carana-kamala-cin-makarandaya bhakta-jana-manasa-nivasaya sri-rama-candraya.*

**Obeisances to Sri Ganesa. Obeisances to Sri Gopala Krsna. Obeisances to Lord Sri Ramacandra. He abides in the hearts of His devotees. The transcendental honey of His lotus feet is relished by** ***paramahamsas*****.**

1  
*vag-isa yasya vadane laksmir yasya ca vaksasi \|*  
*yasyaste hrdaye samvit tam nrsimham aham bhaje \|\|*

**I worship Lord Nrsimha. Sarasvati, the goddess of learning, is in His mouth; Laksmi is on His chest; and the knowledge potency abides in His heart.**

2  
*visva-sarga-visargadi-nava-laksana-laksitam\|*  
*sri-krsnakhyam param dhama jagad-dhama namami tat* \|\|

**I offer obeisances to the highest effulgence, called Sri Krsna, who is indicated by the nine topics of** ***Bhagavatam***  
**beginning from “the creation of the world.” The world is His body.**

3  
*madhavoma-dhavav isau sarva-siddhi-vidhayinau \|*  
*vande parasparatmanau paraspara-nuti-priyau* \|\|

**I extol Madhava and Siva. Those two Lords bestow all perfections. They are the soul of each other. They are fond of praising one another.**

4  
*sampradayanurodhena paurvaparyanusaratah \|*  
*sri-bhagavata-bhavartha-dipikeyam pratanyate* \|\|

**This commentary, named** ***Bhavartha-dipika*****, is presented in consideration of my** ***sampradaya*** **and is in conformity with previous scriptural statements and with subsequent ones also (in this** ***Purana*****).**

5  
*kvaham manda-matih kvedam manthanam ksira-varidheh \|*  
*kim tatra paramamur vai yatra majjati mandarah* \|\|

**Where am I? I am dull-witted. And where is this churning of the ocean of milk? When Mandara Mountain plunged in this ocean, did that mountain not look like a speck in comparison?**

6  
*mukam karoti vacalam pangum lahghayate girim \|*  
*yat-krpa tam aham vande paramananda-madhavam* \|\|

**I praise the supremely blissful Madhava, by whose mercy fools become expert speakers and lame persons are able to cross mountains.**

7  
*srimad-bhagavatabhidhah sura-tarus tarahkurah saj-janih skandhair dvadasabhis tatah pravilasad-bhakty-alavalodayah \|*  
*dva-trimsat tri-satam ca yasya vilasac-chakhah sahasrdny alam parnany astadasesta-do ’tisulabho varvarti sarvopari \|\|*

**The godly tree named** ***Srimad Bhagavatam*****, a sprout of** ***omkara*** **(*****tarankura*****)**, **originates from the Absolute Truth. The tree expanded with twelve boughs (cantos). It rose in the midst of the round watering trench which is superemi-nent** ***bhakti*****. The tree has three hundred and thirty-five resplendent branches (chapters). It has eighteen thousand leaves (verses). It fulfills desires and is easily available. Thus it eminently exists above all other scriptures**.

*atha nana-purana-sastra-prabandhais citta-prasattim alabhamanas tatra tatraparitusyan naradopadesatah srimad-bhagavad-gunanuvarna-na-pradhanam srimad-bhagavata-sastram praripsur veda-vyasas tat-pratyuha-nivrtty-adi-siddhaye tat-pratipadya-para-devatanusmarana-laksanam mahgalam acarati, janmady asyeti.*

**Now, regarding the verse under discussion: Veda-vyasa could not obtain peace of mind with the various** ***Puranas*** **and other scriptures. He was dissatisfied with them. Desiring to begin this scripture called** ***Srimad Bhagavatam*****, in which the main aspect is a continuous description of the Lord’s qualities, on Narada’s advice, with this verse which begins** ***janmady asya*** **Veda-vyasa invokes auspiciousness, characterized by a constant remembrance of the Supreme Lord, who is explained by means of this scripture.**

*param paramesvaram. dhimahiti dhyayater lihi chandasam, dhyayemety arthah. bahu-vacanam sisyabhiprayakam.*

**The word** ***param*** **means** ***paramesvaram*** **(the supreme controller). The verb** ***dhimahi*** **is Vedic. The verb is made from the verbal root** ***dhyai*** **conjugated in the** ***vidhi-lin*** **tense.** ***Dhimahi*** **means** ***dhyayema*** **(let us meditate). The plural suggests that all of us are His disciples**.

*tam eva svаruра-tatastha-lаksаnabhyаm upalaksayati. tatra svarupa-lakumam, satyam iti. satyatve hetuh, yatra yasmin brahmani trayanam maya-guiiainaim. tamo-rajah-sattvanam sargo bhutendriya-devata-rupo ’mrsa satyah. yat-sa-tyataya mithya-sargo ’pi satya-vat pratiyate tam param satyam ity arthah. atra drstantah, tejo-vari-mrdam yatha vinimaya iti. vinimayo vyatyayo ’nyasminn anyavabhasah, sa yathadhisthana-sattaya sad-vat pratiyata ity arthah. tatra tejasi vari-buddhir marici-toye prasiddha, mrdi kacadau vari-buddhir, vainiii ca kacadi-buddhir ity-adi yatha-yatham uhyam. yadva, tasyaiva paramartha-satyatva-pratipadanaya tad-itarasya mithyatvam uktam. yatra mrsaivayam tri-sargo na vastutah sann iti. yatrety anena praptam upadhi-sam-bandham varayati, svenaiva dhamna mahasa nirastam kuhakam kapatam maya-laksanam yasmims tam.*

**Vyasa distinguishes the Lord by means of natural characteristics (*****svarupa-laksana*****) and incidental characteristics (*****tatastha-laksana*****). Of the two, a natural characteristic is expressed with the word** ***satyam*** **(real). The reason He is real is: “In whom, as Brahman, the emanation of the three** ***gunas*****, which takes the shape of the five elements, the senses, and the presiding deities, is real.” This means: “He is transcendentally real. The world, though unreal, appears to be real because He is real.” The words** ***tejo-vari-mrdam yatha vinimayah*** **are an example of that. The term** ***vinimaya*** **(interaction) means** ***vyatyaya*** **(transmutation). In other words, it is the false appearance of being something else in another thing. This means a thing appears to be real because of the reality of the substratum. In that regard, the interactions are inferred in accordance with the suitability. For instance, perceiving water when there is fire means seeing a mirage in the desert (in this case fire is real, relatively speaking, and water is not real). Perceiving water when there is earth means seeing water on the ground when in fact there is only a piece of glass. Perceiving earth whenthere is water means seeing a piece of glass on the ground when in fact there is only water**.

**Another interpretation is as follows (*****tri-sargo mrsa*** **means** ***tri-sargo mrsa*****). To propound the fact that only the Lord is real in the highest sense, it is said that whatever is distinct from Him is unreal: “in whom (as Brahman) the emanation of the three** ***gunas*** **is unreal (*****mrsa*****).” It does not really exist.**

**Next, Vyasa repudiates any connection between the Lord and a limiting characteristic, which was obtained due to the word** ***yatra*** **(in whom) (one might assume that the Lord has a characteristic of Maya since Maya is the correla­tive of the Purusa):** ***dhamna svena nirasta-kuhakam*** **means: “\[We meditate on Him,\] in whom any kind of deceit characterized by Maya is rejected simply by His power.**”

*tatastha-laksanam aha, janmaditi. asya visvasya janma-sthiti-bhanga yato bhavanti tam dhimahi. tatra hetuh, anvayad itaratas ca. arthesv akasadi-karyesu paramesvarasya sad-rйpenanvayad akaryebhyas ca kha-puspadibhyas tad-vyat-irekac ca. yadva, anvaya-sabdenanuvrttih, itara-sabdena vyavrttih. anuvrttatvat sad-rupam brahma karanam mrt-suvamadi-vat. vyavrttatvad visvam karyam ghaia-kuiidalaidi-vad ity arthah. yadva, savayavatvad anvaya-vyatirekabhyam yad asya janmadi tad yato bhavatiti sambandhah. tatha ca srutih, “yato va imani bhutani jayante, yena jatani jivanti, yat prayanty abhisamvisanti” ity-adya. smrtis ca, “yatah sarvam bhutani bhavanty adi-yugagame, yasmims ca pralayam yanti punar eva yuga-ksaye” ity-adya.*

**With the word** ***janmadi*** **and so on, Vyasa mentions the Lord’s incidental charac­teristics: “We meditate on Him because of whom the creation, the continuation and the destruction of the universe (*****asya = visvasya*****) take place.” The explan­atory reason for that is:** ***anvayad itaratas carthesu*****, which means: “because of the connection of the Supreme Lord, as a real form, in things, such as the five elements, and because He is entirely distinct from things that do not exist, like a flower in the sky.”**

**Or else, the concept of “carrying forward” is expressed with the word** ***anvaya*** **(connection), and an exclusion is expressed with the word** ***itara*** **(the other, i.e. the opposite). The sense is this: Brahman is the cause, like clay and like gold, because a cause is present in its effect (by the logical reasoning called Sat-karya-vada); and the world is the effect, like a clay pot and like golden earrings respectively, insofar as an effect is excluded from being the exact same thing as its cause**.

**Alternatively, the syntactical connection is: “The creation, the continuation and the destruction of the universe, which occur by His presence, since He has parts (Time is His potency)**,**and by His exclusion, take place because of Him.” For example, a** ***sruti*** **scripture states:** ***yato va imani bhutani jayante, yena jatani jivanti, yat prayanty abhisamvisanti, tad brahmeti*****, “That from which these beings emerge, by means of which they live, toward which they move, and into which they merge, is Brahman” (*****Taittiriya Upanisad*** **3.1), and a** ***smrti*** **scripture states:** ***yatah sarvani bhutani bhavanty adi-yugagame, yasmims ca pralayam yantipunar eva yuga-ksaye*****, “\[He is VisnuJ from whom all beings originate at the beginning of a** ***yuga*** **and into whom they reach dissolution at the end of that** ***yuga*****” (*****Dana-dharma*****) (cited in** ***Paramatma-sandarbha*** **105).**

*tarhi kim pradhanam jagat-кaranatvad dhyeyam abhipretam, nety aha. abhijno yas tam, “sa iksata lokannu srja iti.” “sa iman lokan asrjata” iti sruteh' “iksater nasabdam” iti nyayac ca. tarhi kim jivo dhyeyah syan nety aha, svarat svenaiva rajate yas tam. svatah-siddha-jhanam ity arthah. tarhi kim brahma dhyeyah “hiranyagarbhah samavartatagre bhutasya jatah patir eka asit” iti sruteh. nety aha, tena iti. adi-kavaye brahmane ’pi brahma vedam yas tene prakasitavan, “yo brahm^am vidadhati purvam yo vai vedams ca prahinoti tasmai, tam ha devam atma-buddhi-prakasam mumuksur vai saranam aham prapadye” iti sruteh. nanu brahmano ’nyato vedadhyayanam aprasiddham, satyam, tat tu hrda manasaiva tene vistrtavan. anena buddhi-vrtti-pravartakatvena gayatry-artho darsitah. vaksyati hi, “pracodita yena pura sarasvati vitanvatajasya satim smrtim hrdi, sva-laksaiidpradurabhut kilasyatah sa me rsinam rsabhah prasidatam” iti. nanu brahma svayam eva supta-pratibuddha-nyayena vedam upalabhatam, nety aha, yad yasmin brahmani surayo ’pi muhyanti. tasmad brahmano ’pi paradhina-jhanatvat svatah-siddha-jhanah paramesvara eva jagat-karanam. ata eva satyo ’satah satta-pradatvac ca paramartha-satyah sarva-jhatvena ca nirasta-kuhakas tam.*

**“Then does it mean that Pradhana (unmanifest material nature) should be meditated upon since it is the cause of the world?” Vyasa refutes this with the adjective** ***abhijnah*** **(fully cognizant), on account of these** ***sruti*** **statements:** ***sa iksata lokan nu srja iti*****, “He glanced, and thought: “I should create the worlds,”” and:** ***sa iman lokan asrjata*****, “He created these worlds” (*****Aitareya Upanisad*** **1.1.1-2), and on account of the logical reasoning in:** ***iksater nasabdam*****, “\[Brahman\] is not inexpressible with words, because of seeing” (*****Vedanta-sutra*** **1.1.5)**.

**“Then should a** ***jiva*** **be meditated upon?” Vyasa says no with the word** ***svarat*****, which means that He shines by Himself. The sense is His consciousness is self-established.**

**“Then should Brahma be meditated upon, since the** ***sruti*** **states:** ***hiranya-garbhah samavartatagre bhutasya jatah patir eka asit*****, “In the beginning, Hiranyagarbha arose. Having appeared, he was the only lord of creation (*****Rg-veda*** **10.121.1)”? Vyasa denies this with the word** ***tene*****. “The Lord revealed (*****tene = prakasitavan*****) the** ***Vedas*** **(*****brahma = vedam*****) to Brahma (*****adi-kavaye = brahmane*****),” in view of the sruti statement:** ***yo brahmanam vidadhati purvam yo vai vedams caprahinoti tasmai, tam ha devam atma-buddhi-prakasam mumuksur vai saranam aham prapadye,*** **“I, who seek liberation, surrender to the Lord. He created Brahma in the beginning and bestowed the** ***Vedas*** **to him. Indeed, the Lord causes the manifestation of the intelligence of the soul” (*****Svetasvatara Upanisad*** **6.18).**

**Someone might argue: “Brahma’s learning the** ***Vedas*** **from another is unheard of.” That is true, yet the** ***Vedas*** **were imparted to him only through his mind (*****hrda = manasa eva*****). By being one who impels the functions of the intelli­gence, the meaning of the third line of the Gayatri mantra is shown, because Vyasa will say:** ***pracodita yena pura sarasvati vitanvatajasya satim smrtim hrdi, sva-laksana pradurabhut kilasyatah sa me rsinam rsabhah prasidatam*****, “May the Lord, the best of those who impart wisdom, be pleased with me. In the beginning, Sarasvati was inspired by Him to revive Brahma’s memory of the past cycle: She, whose characteristics are her own (the six** ***Vedangas*****)**, **appeared (in the form of the** ***Vedas*****) from his mouths” (*****Bhagavatam*** **2.4.22).**

**“Rather, Brahma, like a person awakened from sleep, must have recollected the** ***Vedas*** **by himself.” Vyasadeva refutes this as follows: “In regard to which** ***Vedas*** **(*****yat = yasmin brahmani***) **even the demigods are bewildered.” Therefore, since even Brahma’s knowledge depends on another person, only the Supreme Lord, whose knowledge is self-established, is the cause of the world. Consequently He is real also because He gives existence to what is nonexistent, and He is** ***nirasta-kuhaka*** **(He dispels Maya) because He is real in the highest sense and because He is omniscient.**

*dhimahiti gaiy'anyai p'airam.bbeiia ca gayairy-akhya-brahma-vidya—rilpam etat puranam iti darsitam. yathoktam matsya-purane purana-dana-prastave, “yatradhikrtya gayatnm vamyate dharma-vistarah, vrtrasura-vadhopetam tad bhagavatam isyate,” “likhitva tac ca yo dadyad dhema-simha-samanvitam, prausthapadyam paumamasyam sa yati paramam padam,” “astadasa-sahas-rani puranam tat prakirtitam” iti puranantare ca, “grantho ’stadasa-sahasro dvadasa-skandha-sammitah, hayagriva-brahma-vidya yatra vrtra-vadhas tatha, gayatrya ca samarambhas tad vai bhagavatam viduh” iti. padma-purane ca, ambarisam prati sri-gautama-vacanam, “ambarisa suka-proktam nityam bhagavatam smu, pathasva sva-mukhenapi yadicchasi bhava-ksayam” iti. ata eva bhagavatam naimainyad ity api na sahkaniyam.*

**By the preamble and by a reference to Gayatri with the word** ***dhimahi*****, it is shown that this** ***Рurаnа*** **is a form of spiritual science (knowledge of the Absolute Truth), known as the Gayatri mantra.**

**For instance, in** ***Matsya Рurаnа*****, in the chapter regarding the offering of a** ***Purana*** **as a gift, it is said: (1)** ***yatradhikrtya gayatrim vamyate dharma-vistarah, vrtrasura-vadhopetam tad bhagavatam isyate*****, “That is called** ***Bhagavatam*** **in which, after a description of Gayatri, there is an extensive exposition of** ***dharma*** **and a narration of the killing of the demon named Vrtra,” (2)** ***likhitva tac ca yo dadyad dhema-simha-samanvitam, prausthapadyam paumamasyam sa yati paramam padam*****, “A person who, after writing it, gives it on a golden throne on the full moon day in the month of Bhadra reaches the supreme destination,” and (3)** ***astadasa-sahasrani puranam tat prakirtitam*****, “It is known that the** ***Purana*** **consists of eighteen thousand verses” (cited in** ***Tattva-sandarbha*** **19).**

**In another** ***Purana*****, it is said:** ***grantho ’stadasa-sahasro dvadasa-skandha-sammitah, hayagriva-brahma-vidya yatra vrtra-vadhas tatha, gayatrya ca samarambhas tad vai bhagavatam viduh*****, “That book which consists of eighteen thousand verses and twelve cantos; where spiritual science was told by Dadhici, who had the head of a horse (6.9.52); where there is a narration of the killing of Vrtra; and which begins with the Gayatri, is indeed known as** ***Bhagavatam*****” (cited in** ***Tattva-sandarbha*** **20).**

**There is also this verse in** ***Padma Purana*****, where Gautama Rsi addresses Ambarisa:** ***ambarisa suka-proktam nityam bhagavatam smu, pathasva sva-mukhenapi yadicchasi bhava-ksayam*****, “O Ambarisa, if you want to end your material existence, always listen to** ***Bhagavatam*** **spoken by Suka and recite it yourself” (quoted in** ***Tattva-sandarbha*** **20). Consequently there should be no doubt that another name of this scripture is** ***Bhagavatam*** **(in addition to** ***Srimad Bhagavatam*****, in verse 1.1.2).**

## Visvanatha Cakravarti’s Commentary on the First Verse of Bhagavatam

**Visvanatha Cakravarti’s invocatory verses:**

1  
*krpa-sudha-vrsti-bhrtah sva-bhakti-svar-vaihini-khelita-jiya-padmi \|*  
*sri-krsna-caitanya-ghanah sa-vidyud gauro mano-vyomani nas cakastu \|\|*

“May the Sri-krsna-caitanya cloud who bears the rain of his own nectar-ean mercy, who causes the jiva elephants to play in the celestial Ganges of devotional service to Him, and who is bright with lightning shine in the sky of our hearts.”

2  
*nityanandadvaita-caitanyam ekam tattvam nityalankrta-brahma-sutram \|*  
*nityair bhaktair nityaya bhakti-devya bhatam nityam dhamni nitye bhajamah \|\|*

“We worship Nityananda, Advaita, and Caitanya, who are the same one Absolute truth. They are always adorned with brahmin threads and are shining in the eternal abode along with their eternal devotees and eternal Bhakti-devi.”

As a jab at the Mayavadis, the first half can also mean “We worship the one absolute truth which is always blissful, non-dual, and conscious, and which eternally adorns the *Brahma-sutras* (*Vedanta-sutras*)...”

3  
*rupam nama sanatanam guru-krpam nityan guriams tasya tan srimad-bhagavatat tathaiva viditan justac cirenasrayan \|*  
*drstva vaisnava-tosanim prabhu-matam vijnaya sandarbhatas tikam svamy-anukampito ’sya vidadhe sarartha-sandarsinim \|\|*

“Taking shelter of the Absolute Truth’s eternal form and name, His great mercy, and His eternal qualities, which I have understood from the *Bhagavatam* after serving (studying) it for a long time, I, being favored by the Svami (Sridhara Svami), am compiling *Sarartha-darsini*, a commentary which perfectly shows the essential meaning of this book (the *Bhagavatam*), after first examining *Vaisnava-tosan*i and understanding the opinion of Prabhu (The Lord or Jiva Gosvami) from *Krama-sandarbha*.”

The first half can also mean: “Taking shelter of the persons named Rupa and Sanatana, and talking shelter of the Guru’s mercy and his eternal qualities ...”

4  
*na kacin me vaidusy ahaha su-maha-sahasa iha sva-maudhyam va hetur nirupadhi-krpa va bhagavatah \|*  
*prabhutvam va hine ’py udayati yad adye prahasitam dvitiye tv anandam pratipadam idam dhoksyati satam \|\|*

“Alas I have no intelligence. What a great audacity it is that I am writing a commentary on *Srimad-bhagavatam*. The cause of this is either my own foolish­ness or the unconditional mercy of the Lord. This apparent capability, which is arising even in a wretched person, will, in the first case (of my own foolishness), evoke the ridicule of the devotees, but in the latter case (of the Lord’s uncondi­tional mercy), it will evoke the bliss of the devotees at every step.”

5  
*gopa-rama-jana-pratui-preyase ’tiprabhumave \|*  
*tadiya-priya-dasyaya mam madiyam aham dade \|\|*

“I give myself and my possessions unto the ultimate master, who is dearer than life for the cowherd women, and unto the service of His beloved devotees.”

6

“It is not so wonderful that this *sastra* assumed the qualities of the fruit of a desire tree, of a light, of the sun, and of *brahman*, for it also assumed the quality of Mohiné-mürti in the assembly of devotees and non-devotees to cause \[only the devotees\] to drink the nectar of Hari’s pastimes. Thus we worship this *sastra*”.

**Preamble**

**(by Visvanatha Cakravarti)**

In this world, when the ocean of sweetness and all auspicious qualities, the supreme ruler of grand opulence, the Lord Himself, who is the supreme sun, disappeared at the appropriate time, after exhibiting his pastimes here on earth; and when some of the meanings of the various *sastras* such as the *Puranas* and *Itihasas* which are like guards that protect the mass of people fell asleep in due course of time, by fate, as if because of dullness due to losing their qualities; and when the great wealth of the satisfaction of the mind of everyone, including their authors, was stolen away by some among them who, on the contrary, arose like thieves in the form of contrary meanings which are understood as such from statements like *jugupsitam dharma-krte ’nusasatah sva-bhava-raktasya mahan vyatikra-mah*: \[Narada speaks to Vyasa\] “Because of you, who are teaching abominable activities as *dharma*, the people, who are naturally attached, have committed a great wrong.” (*Bhagavatam* 1.5.15); the crest jewel among the *sastras, Srimad-bhagavatam* descended among the *Vedas* as one that has obtained its justifi­cation from the cause stated in the *Gita*, namely “I manifest Myself whenever there is a decline in religion, O Bharata, and an increase in irreligion in order to protect the saintly and to destroy the wicked.” (*Bhagavad-gita* 4.7-8), just as Maha-mina descended among the aquatics, Yajna-varaha among the beasts, Sri-hamsa among the birds, Sri Krsna, the Lord Himself, among humans, and Upendra among the gods. This is because *Srimad-bhagavatam* is the replica of Sri Krsna, a fact indicated by the words “Now that Krsna has gone to His own abode accompanied by religion, knowledge, and so on, this *Purana* in the form of a sun has arisen for those who have lost their sight in \[the dense darkness of\] the age of Kali.” (*Bhagavatam* 1.3.43). Indeed, because the possibility of something else other than Himself being His equal is ruled out by the statement *mamaham evabhirupah kaivalyat:* “I am my only equal because of my being absolute,” (*Bhagavatam* 5.3.17), \[it is concluded that\] Sri Krsna himself descended. through the agency of Suka and Pariksit. as the sun among the *puranas*, just like the sun among the stars. *Srimad-bhagavatam* is a desiretree full of meanings which are dear to the *mahajanas*. This desire tree contains 12 branches and 18,000 leaves.

**First interpretation of the Bhagavatam verse**

First, with the verse beginning, “*janmady asya*,” the author of *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, Sri Krsna Dvaipayana, the crest jewel among saintly teachers, invokes auspiciousness by meditating on his worshipable Lord.

We meditate upon (*dhimahi = dhyayema*) the supremely (*param*) i.e., preemi­nently (*atisayena*), True (*satyam*), i.e. the Supreme Controller who exists everywhere at all times.

Accepting all the living beings connected to him through disciplic succession in all times and places as his own and directing them to meditation by his instruc­tions, he incorporates them with this use of the plural.

Thus the idea of the *sutra, athato brahma-jijhasa*: “Now, therefore, inquiry into Brahman” (VS 1.1.1) is explained with regard to its result, since this very meditation is the result of inquiry (the desire to know the Truth).

His Supreme Majesty is spoken of: \[We meditate upon\] Him who is the cause (*yatah*) of the creation and so on (*janmadi*), i.e. of the creation, maintenance, and destruction of this (asya) world.

Then do you all meditate on time? No, “because of the presence (anvaya) and the opposite (itara), i.e. because of the presence (anvaya) and the absence (vyatireka), as the presence of clay in a pot and the absence of a pot in clay.” Thus, \[we meditate on\] the material cause (upadana-karana). Such is the meaning. Because of the word ca (and) \[it is indicated that\] He is also the instrumental cause (nimitta-karana), since time is His potency.

Or else: Because of the presence, i.e. because of the universe’s entrance into the Supreme controller during dissolution, and because of the opposite, i.e. the universe’s separation from Him during creation. The meaning is that \[we meditate on Him who is\] the foundational cause \[of the universe\] just as water is the foundational cause of earth and fire is the foundational cause of water.

Or else: \[We meditate on\] Him who is the cause, because of the presence, i.e. because of the entrance made by whom as the cause there is creation of the world; because of the entrance made by whom as the giver of the results of karma there is maintenance; and because of the entrance made by whom as the dissolver in the form of Rudra there is destruction. Here, the cause’s being present in the effect should be understood as entrance into the effect. Denying that His effect, the world, is identical with Him, he clarifies with the word *itaratah* (different), i.e. He is separate from the world, which is to be created, maintained, and destroyed, by means of His personal potency (*svarupa-sakti*). And, because of the use of the word ca, He is not separate from it by means of His illusory potency (*maya-sakti*).

Thus, the two *sutras, janmady asya yatah* (VS 1.1.2) and *tat tu samanvayat* (VS 1.1.4) are described.

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, if the Supreme Lord is the material cause, then His transformation is unavoidable. Therefore, you should say that Prakrti is the material cause and that the Supreme Lord is the instrumental cause.” No. The Supreme Lord alone is the material and instrumental cause of the world because *sruti* statements like *yah sarva-jnah sarva-vit*: “Who is the omniscient, all-knowing” (*Mundaka* 1.1.9), *sa aiksata lokan nu srjai*: “He thought, ‘Let me create the worlds.’” (*Aitareya* 1.1.1), and *tad aiksata bahu syam prajayeya*: “He thought, ‘Let me become many. Let me create progeny.’” (*Chandogya* 6.2.3) explain that the cause of the world is a conscious being” In this regard, He is the material cause since *prakrti* is His potency and there is no difference between the potency and the possessor of the potency. But because He is transcendental to *prakrti* in His own identity, He doesn’t undergo transformation. For instance, the Lord Himself says *prakrtir yasyopadanam adharah purusah parah / sato ’bhivyanjakah kalo brahma tat tritayam tv aham:* “I am in fact the three-fold Brahman: *Prakrti*, the material cause of the real world, the Supreme Person, the foundation, and time, the manifesting agent.” (*Bhagavatam* 11.24.19). Thus *Prakrti’s* independently being the material cause is not supported by *sastra*. Therefore, the omniscient Supreme Lord, not insentient *Prakrti*, is said to be independently the cause of the world. This is said with the words *arthesv abhijnah.* The meaning is, “\[We meditate upon\] Him, who is cognizant (*abhijnah*) of the objects (*arthesu*), i.e of all things created and not created. By this, the meaning of the *sutra, iksater nasabdam*: “not *asabda* (what is without scriptural support), because of seeing.” (VS 1.1.5) is described—“This one is the Brahman, the cause of the world previously described \[in the first *sutra*\].” Why? “Because of seeing (*iksateh = tkwnat*), i.e. because of *sruti* statements about His seeing in the form of a particular kind of deliberation among *sruti* statements teaching about the quality of being the cause of the universe. Therefore, Brahman is not *asabda*, i.e something which has no authority from *sabda*, but rather it is that which has authority from *sabda*. In this regard, there are the following statements of *sruti: tad aiksata bahu syam*: “He thought, ‘May I become many.’” (Chandogya 6.2.3), sad eva saumyedam agra asit: “In the beginning, my dear, there was only this real.” (*Chandogya* 6.2.1), *atma va idam eka evagra asit:* “In the beginning, there was only this one, the Self alone.” (*Aitareya* 1.1.1), *tasmad va etasmad atmana akasah sambhutah:* “From this very one, the Self, Space became manifest.” (*Taittiriya* 2.1), and *yato va imani bhutani jayante:* “That from which these beings are born...” (*Taittiriya* 3.1). There is also the follow­ing *smrti* statement: *yatah sarvam bhutani bhavanty adi-yugagame, yasmims ca pralayam yanti punar eva yuga-ksaye:* “From whom all things come into being at the coming of the beginning of the yuga and into whom they go to dissolution again at the end of the *yuga*.”

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, because at that time the Mahat-tattva and so on had not come into being, His doing the activities of seeing and so on is not possible.” Thus, he says, “*svarat*, i.e. He acts in such ways simply by His own form, for in the *sruti* statement beginning *na tasya karyam karanam ca vidyate:* “He has no duty nor any instrumental organ.”, it says “s*vabhaviki jhana-bala-kriya ca:* “And \[His\] inherent \[potency\] consists of knowledge, strength, and activity.”.

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, Brahma’s independence and sovereignty in the matter of the creation of the world is understood from the *sruti* statement *hiranyagarbhah samavartatagre bhutasya jatah patir eka asit:* “In the beginning, Hiranyagarbha arose. Being born, he was the one lord of creation.” (Rg-Veda 10.121.1). Thus He alone should be meditated upon.” In response to this, he speaks the phrase beginning *tene* —“Who (*yah*) imparted (*tene*), i.e. revealed, *brahma*, i.e. the *Vedas* or the truth about Himself, to the first sage (*adi-kavaye*), i.e. Brahma.” Therefore even Brahma is dependent. \[Someone may argue:\] “Well, Brahma’s learning the Vedas from another is unheard of.” True, the Vedas, however, were imparted to Him through the heart, i.e. through the mind, \[as understood\] from the following passages: *pracodita yena pura sarasvati vitanvatajasya satim smrtim hrdi sva-lakscmai pradurabhut kilasyatah:* “By whom the Vedas (Sarasvati), which describe Him ,were formerly made to appear from the mouth of Brahma, and who imparted, in the heart of Brahma, the remembrance of how to create.” (*Bhagavatam* 2.4.22) and “*kim va sudrstam hrdi me tadaiva*, “Or why \[were you\] easily seen within my heart just then?” (*Bhagavatam* 10.14.15). Moreover, by this quality of causing the intelligence to act, the meaning of the Gayatri mantra is shown. That is stated in Matsya-purana, *yatradhikrtya gayatrim vamyate dharma-vistarah, vrtrasu-ra-vadhopetam tad bhagavatam isyate:* “That is called the Bhagavatam in which, having begun with the Gayatri mantra, a detailed description of dharma is related and which contains the slaying of the demon Vrtra.” and again in another Purana, *grantho ‘stadasa-sahasro dvadasa-skandha-sammitah. hayagriva-brahma-vidya yatra vrtra-vadhas tatha.. gayatrya ca samarambhas tad vai bhagavatam viduh:* “That book with 18,000 stanzas and consisting of 12 cantos, wherein one finds the spiritual meditation taught by Hayagriva (Dadhici) and the slaying of Vrtra and which begins with the Gayatri is indeed known as the Bhagavatam.).”

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, like a person awakened from sleep, Brahma may know the Vedas or the Truth by himself alone.” In response to this, he says, \[ “*muhyanti yat surayah*” \]. “In regard to which Vedas or which truth about Him (*yat=yasmin*) even the demigods (*surayah*) are bewildered (*muhyanti*).” Therefore Brahma has no ability \[to understand\] such things by Himself. By this the meaning of the *sutra, netaro ‘nupapatteh*: “Not the other, because of his not being fit.” (VS 1.1.16) is explained.

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, His having a body is indicated by His being the object of meditation indicated by the word *dhimahi*. Bodies are created from the three *gunas*. And such being the case, His body must be non-eternal.” In response to this, he says, \[ “*tejo-vari-mrdam...*” \]. “Like (yatha) the misappre­hension (*vinimayah = viparyayah*) of light, water, and earth (*tejo-vari-mrdam*), i.e. the seeming appearance of one in another. As the ignorant entertain the notions ‘This is water’ in relation to light, ‘This is the ground’ in relation to water, and ‘This is water’ in relation to earth in forms such as glass, so the conception “This is a threefold creation (*tri-sargah*), i.e. a product of the three guntis. in relation to which (*yatra*) body consisting of full consciousness” is false (*mrsa = mithya*).” Such is the meaning.

The *Gopala-tapani sruti* (1.33) states, *tam ekam govindam sac-cid-ananda-vigraham. vrndavana-sura-bhuruha-talasmam*... ” Him, the One, Govinda, whose body consists of existence, consciousness, and bliss, and who is seated at the base of a spiritual desire tree in Vrndavana.” The Rama-tapani says, *ardha-matratmako ramo brahmanandaika-vigrahah*: “Rama, the single form of the bliss of Brahman, is the *ardha-matra* (the letter m, which is pronounced for the duration of half a *matra*, and which is said to complete the syllable *om*\]).” And in *Nrsimha-tapani*, “*rtam satyam param brahma purusam nr-kesari-vigraham*. (The right, the true, the Supreme Brahman, the Purusa who has the form of a man-lion.)” In the *Dhyana-bindu Upanisad it says, nirdosa-puma-guna-vigraha atma-tantro niscetanatmaka-sarira-gunais ca hinah...ananda-manra-mukha-pada-saro-ruhadi*: “The faultless form, full of virtue, is independent and free of the qualities of bodies possessed of no consciousness. It consists of mouths, lotus-feet, and so on which consist of pure bliss.” The *Brahmanda Purana* (2.36.25) states, *nanda-vraja-jananandi sac-cidananda-vigrahah*: “The giver of bliss to the residents of Nanda’s pastures has a body of existence, conscious­ness and bliss.” The *Maha-varaha* states, *sarve nityah sasvatas ca dehas tasya paratmanah. hanopadana-rahita naiva prakrti-jah kvacit*: “All the bodies of that Supreme Soul are eternal and permanent. They are free from death and origina­tion and are never produced from material nature.” And *Srimad-bhagavatam* states, *sveccha-mayasya na tu bhuta-mayasya*... “Of that which consists of \[His\] personal desire, but does not consist of matter...” (*Bhagavatam* 10,14,2), *babandha prakrtam yatha* “She bound \[Him\] as though He were a product of material nature.” (*Bhagavatam* 10,9,14), *tvayy eva nitya-sukha-bodha-tanau...* “In You whose body consists of eternal happiness and knowledge.” (*Bhagavatam* 10,14,22), *sabdam brahma vapur dadhat* “exhibiting a transcen­dental form which can be understood only through the *Vedas*” (*Bhagavatam* 3,21,8), and *satya-jhananantananda-matraika-rasa-murtayah..*. “Unchanging forms consisting of pure truth, knowledge, and eternal bliss.” (*Bhagavatam* 10,13,54). From these statements the non-illusoriness of His body is understood.

The *Narayaniya* says, *anindriya anahara anispannah sugandhinah. ekantinas te purusah sveta-dvlpa-nivcisinah.*. “Those devoted people who live on Svetad-vipa have no \[material\] senses, eat no food, are not born, and have a pleasant smell.)” The Seventh Canto of *Srimad-bhagavatam* says, *dehendriyasu-hinanam vikwtha-pura-vasinam*... “Of the residents of the city of Vikuntha, who are devoid of \[material\] body, senses, and vital air.” Thus, there is even a negation of having illusory bodies for His devotees, by descriptions such as “without senses” when their having bodies is established by their being residents of Sveta-dvipa or Vikuntha-pura. Therefore, what doubt is there about His body’s being non-illusory?

“Well, some contend even that.” Therefore he speaks the phrase beginning *dhamna*. “\[We meditate upon\] Him, by whom cheaters (*kuhakam*), i.e. those fixed in bad logic, are always (*sada*), i.e. in the three phases of time \[past, present, and future\], driven away (*nirasta*) by His own (svena), unique power (*dhamna*), i.e. by His personal potency, or by the power of the perception of Him had by His devotees, or by His divine form radiant with sweetness and majesty which increase at every moment.” By this, the meaning of the *sutra, tarkapratisthanat* “Because of the foundationlessness of logic” (VS 2.1.11) is described.

Because of the statement *acintyah khalu ye bhava na tams tarkena yojayet. prakrtibhyah param yac ca tad acintyasya laksanam*.. “One should not apply logic to those things which are in fact inconceivable. The characteristic of an inconceivable thing is that it is transcendental to material nature.” (*Mahabharata Bhisma-parva* 5.22), one should not apply fallible logic in relation to Him. Due to the use of the imperative *lin* suffix in the phrase *na yojayet* here, this prohibi­tion is just as strong as the prohibition *para-daran na gacchet*: “one should not have sex with other men’s wives.” In this regard, the word *sva* in the *sruti* text *yam evaisa vmute tena labhyas tasyaisa atma vivmute tanum svam:* “He can be realized only by that person whom He chooses. This \[Super\]soul of his reveals His own body.” (*Mundaka* 3.2.3; Katha 2.23) establishes that His body is His own essential identity. Similarly, the non-illusoriness of His mind, eyes, and so on is understood from *sruti* statements such as, “May I become many,” and “He thought,” \[which refer to events\] prior to the agitation of the material nature, and the inherent quality \[of His potencies\] is clearly spoken of in the *sruti* statement *parasya saktir vividhaiva sruyate svabhaviki jnana-bala-kriya ca*: “The superior potency of this one is inherent and is manifold, consisting of knowledge, strength, and activity.” If, however, in spite of all this, demons and such, wanting to take up arrows of logic with His divine form as the target, will fall down to hell, then may they so fall. Enough of this discussion with them.

**Second interpretation**

Now, in light of the primacy of the tenth topic in this *sastra*, the *asraya* (ultimate shelter) described in the passage *dasamasya visuddhy-artham navanam iha laksanam*: “Herein \[they describe\] the characteristics of the \[first\] nine \[topics\] in order to clarify \[the nature\] of the tenth.” (*Bhagavatam* 2.10.2), and in light of the preeminence of the form of Sri Krsna, this first verse has congruity in praising His unique characteristics. Therefore, there is scope for another interpretation referring to Him alone. That interpretation is as follows: Because of the statement just prior to the birth of Sri Krsna, *satyatmakam tvam saranam prapannah*: “\[We\] have taken shelter of You, whose very self is Truth,” and because of the etymological derivation of Krsna’s names given by Sanjaya in the *Udyama-parva* \[of *Mahabharata*\], *satye pratisthitah krsfiali satyam atra pratisthitam. satyat satyam ca govindas tasmat satyo hi namatah*: “Krsna is situated in truth and truth is situated in Him; Govinda is the truth beyond truth. Therefore He is Truth by name,” \[the meaning is\] “We meditate (*dhimahi*) upon the Truth (*satyam*), i.e. Sri Krsna, who is Supreme (*param*) according to the *Brahmayda-puraya: narakrtiparam brahma* “The Supreme Brahman in the form of a man” and the *Gopala-tapani: tasmat krwa eva paro devas tam dhayet* “Thus, Кгsnа alone is the Supreme God. One should meditate upon Him.”

“\[We meditate upon\] Him, by whom deception (*kuhakam*), i.e. the ignorance of the living entities is always (*sada*) driven off (*nirasta*) by His own *dhama* (*svena dhamna*) named Sri Mathura and by own His divine form (*svena dhamna*) which was, by His mercy, seen everywhere at that time. These two facts are understood respectively from the proof of *Gopala-tapani, mathyate tu jagat sarvam brahma-jnanena yena va / tat-sara-bhutam yad yasyam mathura sa nigadyate*: “That place wherein there is the essence of that spiritual knowledge by which the whole world is churned, is known as Mathura.” and from the statement in the Tenth Canto (10.70.43), *sravanat kirtanad dhyanatpuyante ’nte-vasayinah /tava brahma-mayasyesa kim uteksabhimarsinah*: “O Lord, outcastes are purified through hearing, glorification, and meditation about You who consist of spirit. What to speak of those who see and touch \[You\].” In this regard, the Sanskrit dictionary *Amara-kosa* says, *grha-deha-tvit-prabhdvd dhamani* “Abode, body, splendor, and power \[are all meanings of the word\] *dhaman*.”

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, because His body is seen by worldly people, it must be non-eternal in accordance with the logic that whatever is seen is non-eternal, as in the case of a pot.” In response to this, he says. “In relation to whom (*yatra*) a creation by the three (*tri-sargah*), i.e. \[the concept of having\] a body created by the three *gunas*, which is just like (*yatha*) a combination (*vinimayah = paraspara-milanam*) of the three visible elements—fire, water, and earth (*tejo-vari-mrdam*) is in fact false (*mrsa*). “ The idea here is that since His body is not said to be created from these three, it is not said to be false. The vision of Him which is had by worldly demons, even though He is beyond the worldly, is in fact \[caused\] by His own will which accomplishes His various pastimes and is of an incomprehensible nature. This vision, however, is devoid of the experience of His sweetness, like the tasting of condensed sugar-cane juice by a person whose tongue is afflicted by \[excessive\] bile. But the vision of Him had by others \[i.e. by devotees\] is indeed coupled with experience of His sweetness by the power of His incomprehensible grace. As it is said, *anye ca tan-mukha-sarojam udara-hasa-snigdheksanam nrpa papur drsibhir nr-naryah.. tebhyah sva-viksana-vinasta-tamisra-drgbbyah ksemam tri-loka-gurur artha-drsam ca yacchan*: “...and other men and women, O king, drank with their eyes His lotus face, filled with a munificent smile and affectionate glances. Bestowing satisfaction and vision of the \[supreme\] truth to those the blindness of whose eyes was destroyed by His own glances.” (*Bhagavatam* 10.86.20-21). Therefore one should understand that His becoming visibile, even though He is actually not visible, indicates the great power of His mercy. Therefore, \[we find\] this statement of the *Narayanadhyatma* cited in *Laghu-Bhagavatamrta* (1.5.422), *nityavyakto’pi bhagavan iksyate nija-saktitah. tam rteparamanandam kah pasyetam itam prabhum*, “Although eternally unmanifest, the Lord is seen by His own potency. Without that \[potency\] who can see this supremely blissful Lord.” And the *karika* there says *tatah svayam prakasatva-saktya sveccha-prakasaya / so’bhivyakto bhavet netre na netra-visayatvatah*: “Therefore, He becomes manifest within the eye by Himself, by \[His\] potency of illumination, which is a manifestation of His own will, not because of being a perceivable object of the eye.” From the *sruti* statement *evam eva tasam madhye saksat brahma gopala-puri hi*: “Thus indeed, amongst them, the city of Gopala is verily directly brahman,” the visibility of His abode and so on, although they consist of brahman \[is established\]. Therefore those who know the Bhagavata infer, “Whatever is visible, apart from the spiritual, is non-eternal, as in the case of a pot.”

Thus, having described mercy as the root cause of His descent, His activities are described: “And (*ca*) he went (*ayat = agacchat*) to (*anu*) another place (*itaratah = itaratra*), i.e. the house of Nanda, than the place i.e. the house of Vasudeva, where (*yato = yatra*), His (*asya*) birth and so on (*janmadi*), i.e. birth, manifesta­tion of majesty, narration of previous activities, and so on \[took place\].”

Why did he go? “Cognizant (*abhijnah*) of purposes (*arthesu*), i.e. the desires of Kamsa and so on or purposes in the form of causing the appearance of the parental love (*prema*) and other kinds of love connected with Vraja.” But He is not dependent on another. Therefore, he says, “He rules over Himself.” Or else the meaning is “\[He goes\] for the sake of becoming beautifully situated with His own father and so on, namely Sri Nanda and others.”

Moreover, despite His being characterized by such pastimes due to His being controlled by the love of various devotees in various places, one should not conclude that He is naively ignorant. Thus, he says, “He showed (*tene = prakasayam asa*) spirit (*brahma*), i.e. spiritual calves and boys to the first sage (*adi-kavaye*), i.e. even to Brahma. And that \[was done\] by the heart, i.e. simply by His will.” \[The rest means\] “in relation to which (*yatra*), i.e. the power of His *yoga-maya*, even sages (*surayah*) such as Bhava (Siva) and Narada are bewildered (*muhyanti*).” Or else, “He imparted (*tene*), i.e. manifested through the words of His self-same part, Matsya-deva, *brahma*, i.e. His own unqualified form, unto He who is both first and wise (*adi-kavaye*), i.e. unto Satyavrata-manu who is the first male in His family and who is a wise one, i.e. a knower. The words of Matsya-deva in this regard are *madiyam mahimanam ca param brahmeti sabditam. vetsyasy anugrhitam me samprasnair vivrtam hrdi*: “Being favored by me, you will know My greatness, designated as the supreme Brahman, which will be revealed within your heart because of Your questions.” (*Bhagavatam* 8.24.38). This passage is explained by Sridhara Svami as follows: *me maya anugrhitam prasadi-krtam brahma aparoksi-krtam vetsyasi*: “You will know Brahman, which has been made visible, being favored by me, i.e. being made my mercy.)” Brahman’s being made the mercy of Him is explained in the beginning of the prayers of the *Vedas* \[10.87.2\].

**Third interpretation**

Now, in spite of His being characterized by an abundance of *rasas* like *santa* and *dasya*, there is again scope here for yet another meaning showing \[Krsna’s\] excellence by the superiority of His *rasa* due to the arising of supreme sweetness by association with the goddesses of Vraja, as understood from statements like *tatratisusubhe tabhir bhagavan devaki-sutah*: ’’There, with those ladies, the Lord, the son of Devaki, was excessively beautiful.” (*Bhagavatam* 10.33.6). That \[meaning\] is as follows. “We meditate (*dhimahi*) upon Him, from whom (*yatah*) \[comes\] the birth (*janma*) of the original (*adyasya*), i.e. *srngara-rasa*.” The sense is that, although this *rasa* existed previously, it became unmanifest, because it was censured by good people who were seers of the Absolute Truth. “Because of connection (*anvayat*), i.e. union, and the other (*itaratah*), i.e. separation.’ The sense is, “s*rngara-rasa*, together with its accompaniments, only comes about by union and separation.’ Just as Bhimasena is called Bhima, the *adya-rasa* is also indicated by the word *adya*. Or else the *adya-rasa* is indicated by the word *adya* because the word *rasasya* is present here as the noun modified by the adjective *adyasya* due to the close proximity of the idea of *rasa* since this *sastra* itself has the form of rasa as understood from the statement *pibata bhagavatam rasam*... “Drink this rasa which is *Bhagavatam*,” (*Bhagavatam* 1.1.3). Or else, the production of this original thing (*adya*) by union and separation necessarily brings to mind the only possible correlative noun of *adya*, namely *rasa*. Thus, the lack of the word *rasa* is not a reason to doubt its application in the text. On the contrary, by such absence, the confidentiality of the *adi-rasa* is illustrated.

In that regard, His superiority over others in terms of being the *alambana-vibhava* (foundational cause of the *adi-rasa*) is stated. “\[We meditate upon Him who is\] cognizant (*abhijnah*), i.e skillful (*vidagdha*) in the things (*arthesu*), i.e. in all things helpful for \[bringing about\] rasa such as the sixty-four arts.’

Nor is He affected by time and karma like Nala and other mundane *nayakas* (heroes). This is stated with the word *svarat* (independent).

Furthermore, *rasa* can never result in any other person. Thus, he says, “\[We meditate upon Him\], who (*yah*) alone imparted (*tene*) the truth (*brahma*), i.e. the truth about *adi-rasa*, unto the sage of the original (*adi-kavaye*), i.e. Bharata, the sage of the *adi-rasa*, through the heart (*hrda*), i.e. just through his (Bharata’s) mind.” The meaning is, “\[He did this\] just to show that *rasa* is related only to Him.” The Sanskrit dictionary *Amara-kosa* says *vedas tattvam tapo brahma*: “The Vedas, the truth, and austerity \[are all meanings of the word\] *brahma*.”

“Nevertheless, \[it is that\] truth about which (*yat = yatra*) the poets (*surayah = kavayah*) are bewildered (*muhyanti*), due to it‘s being described in relation to mundane heroes such as Nala.

The example \[given\] in this regard is *tejo-vari-mrdam yatha vinimayah*. “Thinking rasa, which is related only to the Lord, to be related to ordinary people is like thinking light (*tejah*) and so on to be water (*vari*) and so on.” The meaning is that the poets describe *rasa* in relation to mundane heroes who at the end are reduced to worms, feces, and ashes, who are utterly transitory, and in relation to whom rasa does not come to be, but rather, upon inverstigation, only the opposite, namely tastelessness (*vairasyam*) consisting of disgust, arises because of the perversion of the *vibhava*.

Furthermore, “\[We meditate upon Him\] in relation to whom (yatra) a creation (*sargah*), i.e. a literary production of the three (*tri*), i.e. the three kinds of meaning, namely literal (*vacya*), figurative (*laksya*), and suggestive (vyangya) or the three, namely suggestion (*dhvani*), poetic quality (*guna*) and embellish­ment (*alankara*), being not false (*amrsa*), i.e. true indeed, can be astonishing, by its otherwordliness. Poetic inspiration consisting merely of the arrogant words of a poet in relation to some other mundane hero is certainly false.

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, some do not esteem *rasa*.” In response to this he says, “\[We meditate upon\] Him by whom cheaters (*kuhakam*), i.e. old Mimamsakas, are driven away by His own (*svena*) unique power (*dhamna*), i.e. by the special power of astonishment arising from direct perception of the taste of \[His\] sweetness.”

**Fourth interpretation**

Now, even among those \[Vraja-devis\], it is by the association of the divine queen of Vrndavana, who is the highest chief \[*gopi*\] in accordance with statements like *kasyah padani caitani yataya nanda-sununai... anayaradhito nilnum bhagavan harir isvarah*: “To which fortunate lady walking along with the son of Nanda do these footprints belong?... Certainly the Lord and Controller, Hari, was worshiped \[*aradhitah*\] by Her.” (*Bhagavatam* 10.30.27-28), that the ultimate supreme excellence of sweetness comes to be manifest. Thus a meaning which also indicates Her should be sought after in this first verse. That meaning is as follows. “\[We meditate upon them\] from whom (*yatah*), i.e. from which two (*yabhyam*) indeed \[there comes to be\] the birth (*janma*), i.e. the manifestation of the original (*adyasya*) *rasa*. \[That is to say, we meditate upon\] the two who indeed are the supreme reservoir of the science of the *adi-rasa*.” Such is the meaning. Out of those two, \[we meditate upon Him\] who is knowledgeable (*abhijnah*) about the objects (*arthesu*), i.e. about the primary *rasas*, which are full of components like the *dhira-lalita* \[personality-type\] which are conducive to rasa, because of His following (*anvayat*) after abandoning (*parityajya*) the other (*itara*) beloveds, i.e. because of the obedience seen in such statements as, *atravaropita kanta puspa-hetor mahatmana.. atra praslnavacayah priyarthe preyasa krtah*: “Here the beloved was put down by the great soul, for the purpose \[of collecting\] flowers; here flowers were gathered by the lover for the sake of His beloved...” (*Bhagavatam* 10.30.31-32). The fifth case \[ablative\] is used in *itaratah* due to the elision of the *lyap*-ending word *parityajya*.

And \[we meditate upon\] Her who is *svarat*, that is, who for this very reason rules over (*rajate*) Her own (*svena*) beloved, i.e. one whose lover is under her control (*svadhina-kanta*).

And \[we meditate upon\] Him, who (*yah*), for the sake of revealing these various \[qualities\], imparted (*tene*) through the heart (*hrda*) the scripture (*brahma*), i.e. the *Bhagavatam*, which contains the five chapters about the *rasa-lila* which are full of the original *rasa*, unto the *adi-kavi* (*adi-kavaye*), the one who was a sage (*kavi*), i.e a knower of the truth, from the very beginning (*adi*), i.e. beginning from birth, that is Sri Sukadeva. \[*Bhagavatam* is *brahma*\] in accordance with statements like *idam bhagavatam nama puranam brahma-sammitam*: “The *Purana* named *Bhagavata* is equal to *brahman*” (*Bhagavatam* 1.3.40), and \[Sukadeva is the *adi-kavi*) in accordance with statements like “Filled with nectarean juice \[it fell\] from the mouth of Suka” (*Bhagavatam* 1.1.3) and “The moon \[arising from\] the ocean of nectar in the form of the words of Suka.”(*Sri-Krsna-namastottara-sata-namamrta in Brihinamda Purana*)

“... Because of which (*yat*) *Bhagavata*, or during which (*yat*) *rasa-lila*, the sages (surayah) are bewildered (muhyanti), i.e. reach a state of blissful, swooning stupefaction arising from the tasting of *rasa*.” Or else, “The sages (*surayah*), i.e. devotees, of which two (*yat = yayoh*).” Or else “because of which two (*yat = yabhyam*), who are the object of their hearing, seeing, and so on, the sages (*surayah*), i.e. the devotees who are their entourage, are bewildered (*muhyanti*). The meaning is that, becoming fools, although \[actually\] great wise ones, they attain the opposite \[of their natural\] properties. In this regard, with the example (*tejo-vari-mrdam yatha vinimayah*), he includes others also: “... As in the case of the transmutation of light, water, and earth, i.e. the transmutation of their own properties.” In this regard, “as \[there is\] transmutation of the moon’s and other lights’ own property of moving \[through the night sky\] by paralysis caused by vision of His *rasa-lila*; as \[there is\] water’s \[taking on\] the property of earth by paralysis \[brought about\] by the sound of His flute; and as rocks and other \[forms of\] earth \[take on\] the property of water by melting.”

“\[We meditate on those two\] in whom (*yatra=yayoh*), the creation \[*sargah*\] of the three (*tri*), i.e. Sri, Bhu, and Lila, or the cowherd maidens, queens, and goddesses of fortune, or the internal, external, and marginal potencies, is not false (*amrsa*), i.e. true indeed by Their own (*svena*) power (*dhamna = prabhavena*).” “Moreover, because all these threes are energies of those two and because those two are the foundational cause, a fact established by the use of the word *yatra* here, Sri and so on, who are created by those two, always (*sada*) exist by their own (*svena*) greatness (*dhamna*).” Such is the meaning.

“We meditate (*dhimahi*) on those two in a way that is devoid of cheating (*nirasta-kuhakam*), i.e. non-duplicitously, in a way that is true (*satyam*), i.e. truly, and in a way that is best (*param*), i.e. in the best of all ways, upon those two, ” Thus, the subject-matter (*visaya*) of this *sastra* has been revealed—\[the pair\] which, because of their eternal relationship \[have all these characteristics between them\].

**Fifth interpretation**

Now, the only means by which the *asraya-tattva* (supreme shelter) whose qualities were previously described \[in the second interpretation\], can be attained is *bhakti-yoga*, the injunction (*abhidheya*) of this *sastra*. Moreover, the same *bhakti-yoga*, having reached its supreme culmination called *prema* (pure love), such being the attractor of the Lord, is also the goal (*prayojana*). That *bhakti­-yoga* is undeniably to be esteemed by this verse. Therefore, another meaning is properly included here. That meaning is as follows: “We meditate (*dhimahi*) upon *bhakti-yoga*, which is true (*satyam*) as understood from the statement of the Twelfth Canto of *Srimad-bhagavatam* (12.12.49-50), *satyam tad eva satyam tad u haiva mahgalam ... yad uttama-sloka-yaso ’nugiyate*: “That \[process of *bhakti-yoga* \] in which the fame of Uttama-sloka is sung is true, that alone is true, that alone is verily auspicious,” and which is moreover supreme (*param*), i.e. the greatest, i.e the highest, i.e beyond the three *gunas* because of being a real thing. Likewise, that *bhakti-yoga* is true (*satyam*), i.e. suitable for the saintly, and supreme (*param*), i.e. full of supremely auspicious qualities.” This is stated \[in *Srimad-bhagavatam* 3.29.12\], *laksanam bhakti-yogasya nirgunasya hy udahrtam* “\[Thus\] the symptoms of *bhakti-yoga* free from the *gunas* is illustrated...” and \[in *Srimad-bhagavatam* 11.29.20\], *na hy ahgopakrame dhvamso mad-dharmasyoddhavanv api. maya vyavasitah samyah nirguiiaivaid anasisah* “My dear Uddhava, there is not even the slightest destruction in the performance of dharma that is free from desire and aimed at Me, because such dharma is free from the *gunas*, \[being\] perfectly established by Me.” The *sruti* text of the *Gopala-tapani Upanisad* also says this, “*vijnana-ghana ananda-ghanah sac-cidanandaika-rase bhakti-yoge tisthati*: “\[Krsna, who is\] full of knowledge and full of bliss, is present in bhakti-yoga, which has the single \[unchanging\] essence of reality, sentience, and joy.”

Its power is described: “\[We meditate upon that\] from which (*yatah*) alone there comes to be the birth (*janma*), i.e. the appearance, of the original (*adyasya*), i.e. the Supreme Controller as the Lord (*bhagavan*) within worshippers and (*ca*) likewise because of the connection (*anvayat*), i.e. because of the combination with which in other things (*itaratah arthesu*, here *itaratah = itaresu*), namely *niskama-karma-yoga* and *jnana-yoga*, there comes to be the birth (*janma*), i.e. the direct perception, of the Supreme Controller as the Supersoul and as Brahman within worshippers.” Such is the syntactical connection.

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, direct perception of *brahman* can be achieved by pure *jnana* alone.” In response to this, he says, “\[We meditate upon that\] which is *abhijnah*, i.e. that from which there comes full (*abhi = sarvato-bhavena*) knowledge (*jna = jnanam*). Because *jnana* is a product of the mode of goodness, without combining with *bhakti* which is beyond the gunas, there certainly cannot be knowledge of the Supersoul and Brahman.” Such is the meaning, as understood from statements like *naiskarmyam apy acyuta-bhava-varjitam na sobhate jnanam*: “Even freedom from karma \[does not manifest\] without a notion of Acyuta (the infallible Lord)” (*Bhagavatam* 1.5.12); and *bhaktya mam abhijanati yavan yas casmi tattvatah* “One knows Me by *bhakti*, who I actually am and how great I am.” (*Bhagavad-gita* 18.55).

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, as *jnana-yoga* done for the sake of direct percep­tion of Brahman is dependent on *bhakti*, similarly, for the sake of direct percep­tion of Bhagavan, *bhakti-yoga* is dependent on *jnana*. ” In answer to this, he says, “\[We meditate upon that which is\] *svarat*, i.e. which rules over itself, i.e which is independent like an emperor, not under the control of anyone.” Such is the meaning, as understood from the injuction *akamah sarva-kamo va moksa-kama udara-dhih / tivrena bhakti-yogena yajeta purusam param*: “A person who has broader intelligence, whether he be full of all material desire, without any material desire, or desiring liberation, should by all means worship the Supreme Person with intense *bhakti-yoga*.” (*Bhagavatam* 2.3.10). The meaning of this word *tivrena* (intense),” is “unmixed with *jnana* and so on, like a pure sunbeam, which has not come into contact with clouds and so on.” It is likewise understood from statements such as *yat karmabhir yat tapasa jnana-vairagyatas ca yat ... sarvam mad-bhakti-yogena mad-bhakto labhate ’njasa*: “My devotee quickly attains, by my *bhakti-yoga*, everything that is \[attained\] by ritual activities (*karmas*), by austerity, and by knowledge and detachment.” (*Bhagavatam* 11.20.32,33). And, on the other hand, it is understood from the statement prohibiting connection with them, *tasman mad-bhakti-yuktasya yogino vai mad-atmanah / na jnanam na ca vairagyam prayah sreyo bhaved iha*: “Therefore, for a *yogi* engaged in My *bhakti*, with mind absorbed in Me, indeed, knowledge (*jnanam*) and renunciation are generally not beneficial in this regard.” (*Bhagavatam* 11.20.31).

Furthermore, such *bhakti-yoga* is not attained without the mercy of a devotee. This he says with the phrase beginning tene. “which (*yah*) was imparted (*tene*), i.e. mercifully revealed, to the first sage (*adi-kavaye*), i.e. Vyasa, by *brahma-hrd*, i.e. by him in whose heart there is Brahman, i.e by Narada.”

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, how are we to understand that even the omniscient Vyasa’s knowledge of *bhakti-yoga* is dependent on another?” In response to this, he speaks the phrase beginning *muhyanti*. “\[That\] about which (*yat = yasmin*) even sages (*surayah*) such as Vasistha are bewildered. Because of the inability of the inner faculties such as the intelligence, which are born from the modes, to enter by themselves into *bhakti-yoga*, which is beyond the modes, they achieve only bewilderment, i.e. ignorance.” Such is the meaning, as understood from the statement of the *Hamsa-guhya* prayers, *yac-chaktayo vadatam vadinam vai vivada-samvada-bhuvo bhavanti / kurvanti caisam muhur atma-moham tasmai namo ’nanta-gunaya bhumne* “Obeisances to that all-per­vading one with unlimited virtues, whose potencies cause the disagreements and agreements of philosophers propagating \[various views\], and constantly create self-bewilderment for them.” (*Bhagavatam* 6.4.31).

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, *bhakti-yoga* is not absolutely beyond the modes alone, for it is seen in the Third Book of *Srimad-bhagavatam* that even it consists of the three *gunas*." In reponse to this, he says, “*yatra tri-sargo mrsa*.” “\[We meditate upon that\] in relation to which (*yatra*) the creation from the three (*tri-sargah*), i.e. being created by the three *gunas*. is false (*mrsa*), i.e. irrational.” Such is the meaning.

“Just like (*yatha*) the mixing (*vinimayah = melanam*) of fire, water, and earth (*tejo-vari-mrdam*). As milk, although free of fire, free of water, and free of dirt particles, becomes “scorched,” “watered-down” and “polluted” because of mixing with these various elements, similarly *bhakti-yoga*, which is beyond the three *gunas*, is called *sattvika*, *rajasa*, and *tamasa* because of contact with *sattva* and the other *gunas* present within a person.”

\[Someone may argue:\] “Well, many dispute *bhakti-yoga’s* being beyond the three *gunas*." In response to this, he speaks the phrase beginning *dhamna svena*. “\[We meditate upon\] that, by which cheaters (*kuhakam*), i.e. false logicians, are cast out (*nirasta*), by its own \[*svena*\] essential form (\[*dhamna*\] = *svarupena*), which is full of otherworldly sweetness and which actually comes within the range of the experience of devotees.” The sense is, “There is no need of \[extraneous\] evidence for an object that is directly experienced.”

**Visvanatha Cakravarti’s elaboration on his last invocatory verse**

In this regard, *Sri-Bhagavatam’s* being a light is seen from the two statements *adhyatma-dipam atititirsatam tamo ‘ndham*: “The spiritual light for those wishing to cross over the dense darkness \[of material existence\].” (*Bhagavatam* 1.2.3) and *kasmai yena vibhasito ‘yam atulo jhana-pradipah*: “\[We meditate upon that Supreme Truth\] by which this unparalleled light of knowledge was revealed to Ka (Brahma).”(*Bhagavatam* 12.13.19), its being a sun is seen from the statement puranarko ’*dhunoditah*: “\[This\] *Purana*-sun has now arisen.” (*Bhagavatam* 1.3.43) , its being a fruit consisting of rasa is seen from the statement *nigama-kalpa-taror galitam phalam rasam*: “The *rasa* fruit fallen from the desire tree of the *Vedas*,” (*Bhagavatam* 1.1.3), and its being Mohini is seen from the statement, *hari-lila-katha-vratamrtanandita-sat-suram*: “That by which the demigods in the form of the saintly devotees are made blissful by means of the nectar of many stories of the pastimes of Hari” (*Bhagavatam* 12.13.11).

In this regard, in this verse \[the *Bhagavatam’s*\] being a light is understood through the first interpretation, its being a sun is understood through the second interpretation and its being a fruit full of *rasa* is understood through the third, fourth, and fifth interpretations. Furthermore, because these five meanings are nectar since they are utterly delectable and extremely difficult to attain, and because this *sastra*, which expresses these various meanings, is \[like\] the Lord by the fact that only the devotees are recipients of this nectar, it is understood that this *sastra* is also Mohini, as the server of this nectar. Furthermore, even if the qualities of being a fruit consisting of *rasa*, being a sun, being a light, and so on belong to this entire *sastra* with its twelve books, still, by the logic that “things are named according to their prominent features”, this *sastra* is called a light because, in \[the narrations of\] creation (*sarga*), destruction (*nirodha*), and some prayers about such, it is the illuminator of only the *adhyatma* (the Lord’s feature as the Supreme Being). It is called a sun because, in \[the narrations of\] secondary creation (*visarga*), maintenance (*sthana*), protection (*posana*), and so on, it is the illuminator of *dharma, artha, kama, moksa* and all varieties of other results of active and inactive practices, both ordained and prohibited, It is called a fruit consisting of *rasa* when the subject matter being discussed is the birth, activities, and so on of the Lord, who is the *asraya-tattva* (supreme shelter), or when the subject matter being discussed is the devotion, love (*prema*), and so on of His devotees. And it is called Mohini because in the very same narrations it acts for the bliss of His devotees by giving meanings favorable to bhakti and for the bewilderment of the demons by giving meanings opposed to bhakti.

Nor should it be said that it is inappropiate for this *sastra*, which directly consists of *bhakti-rasa*, to describe such opposing meanings, because, as a revealer of the characteristics of all potencies, it is appropriate for it to describe various meanings according to the hearts of the various *adhikaris* of this *sastra*, just as the Lord Himself, who is full of all potencies, revealed Himself as the univer­sal form to the unintelligent as seen in the phrase *virad avidusam* in the verse beginning *mallanam asanih* (*Bhagavatam* 10.43.17). Thus all is sound and proper.
