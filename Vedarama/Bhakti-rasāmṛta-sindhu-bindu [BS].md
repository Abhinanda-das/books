# Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu-bindu \[BS\]

## Chapter 1

Śrī Rūpa begins the book by describing the characteristics of *uttama-bhakti*. Just as a verb is understood only through the sense of its verbal root (*dhātu*), so the word *anuśīlanam* must be understood only through the sense of itsverbal root.

The active sense of the root *śīl* (*pravṛttyātmaka*) is active cultivation involving the body, mind (such as smaraṇa and dhyāna) and words. *Nivṛttyātmaka* means that which is different from active*.* The inactive sense of the root *śīl* takes the form of cultivation which uses emotions of joy and sorrow in the mind (*prīti-viṣādātmaka*). It also takes the form of the *sthāyi-bhāva* on the levels of *bhāva* and *prema*, which will be explained later. What is indicated by the words *sevā-nāmāparādhānām udbhavābhāva kāritā* (1.2.81) is a negative form of cultivation (*abhāva-rūpa*), avoidance of offense to the name and the deity (which are obstacles to the favorable cultivation).

With this consideration (of *ceṣṭā-rūpa* and *bhāva-rūpa-bhakti*), *anuśīlanam* means service to persons or things related to Kṛṣṇa or service to Kṛṣṇa directly. That is the meaning of *kṛṣṇānuśīlanam.* Because this definition also includes persons related to Kṛṣṇa as well as Kṛṣṇa Himself, it avoids the fault of being too narrow. Thus, *aṅgas* of *bhakti* such as taking shelter of *guru’s* lotus feet may be included in *bhakti*, even in *bhāva-rūpa-anuśīlanam*. At the stage of *bhāva*, *sthāyi-bhāvas* and *vyabhicāri-bhāvas* may be expressed in relation to *guru* within this definition of *bhakti.*

This service or *anuśīlanam*, obtainable only by the mercy of Kṛṣṇa and his devotees, is a transformation of the Lord’s *svarūpa-śakti,* and thus is not material but spiritual*.* However, one should understand that though it is purely spiritual, it makes its appearance (and is not created since it is eternal) in the body, mind and words by taking over the functions of the body, mind and words. This will be clarified later on. In this verse, the word “Kṛṣṇa” also indicates *bhakti* for other forms of God, because Kṛṣṇa is Svayam Bhagavān and includes all other forms of God (such as Nṛsiṁha). Gradation of these forms will be considered later.

The word *ānukūlyena* (with favorableness or friendliness) is the descriptive element of *anuśīlanam* or service. It is necessary in order to limit the definition of bhakti. The definition of bhakti cannot be established with a disagreeable attitude towards Kṛṣṇa (*pratikūlya*). The word *ānukūlyena* in conjunction with *kṛṣṇānuśīlanam* means “behavior pleasing to Kṛṣṇa.” *Pratikūlya* means the opposite: behavior disagreeable to Kṛṣṇa.

The definition of *ānukūlya* as “pleasing to Kṛṣṇa” is both too extensive and too limiting. For instance, the action (*anuśīlanam*) of a demon attacking Kṛṣṇa is the rasa of fighting (*yuddha-vīra-rasa*) derived from utsāha-rati from Kṛṣṇa’s perspective. It is pleasing for Him, since He takes pleasure in fighting with them. Yuddha-vīra-rasa is expressed in the following verse:

*patiṁ prayāntaṁ subalasya putrī*  
*pati-vratā cānujagāma sādhvī*  
*himālayaṁ nyasta-daṇḍa-praharṣaṁ*  
*manasvinām iva sat samprahāraḥ*

The gentle and chaste Gāndhārī, who was the daughter of King Subala of Kandahar \[or Gāndhāra\], followed her husband, seeing that he was going to the Himalaya Mountains, which are the delight of those who have accepted the staff of the renounced order like fighters who have accepted a good lashing from the enemy. SB 1.13.30

Again, an illustration of the definition being too limited (if *ānukūlya* means “pleasing to Kṛṣṇa”) is given. When Yaśodā left Kṛṣṇa and went to take the milk off the fire, Kṛṣṇa was not pleased. (Thus Yaśodā would be excluded from the definition of bhakti.) The following example is given:

*sañjāta-kopaḥ sphuritāruṇādharaṁ*  
*sandaśya dadbhir dadhi-mantha-bhājanam*  
*bhittvā mṛṣāśrur dṛṣad-aśmanā raho*  
*jaghāsa haiyaṅgavam antaraṁ gataḥ*

Being very angry and biting His reddish lips with His teeth, Kṛṣṇa, with false tears in His eyes, broke the container of yogurt with a piece of stone. Then He entered a room and began to eat the freshly churned butter in a solitary place. SB 10.9.6

In order to prevent the definition from being too extensive (the first case) and too limiting (the second case), the definition of *ānukūlya* should be explained as “devoid of hostility on the part of the performer”. This will exclude the demons, because they have hostility, in the form of hatred for Kṛṣṇa. This definition will also not exclude Yaśodā, because she is devoid of hostility to Kṛṣṇa.

“Therefore, let bhakti be defined in terms of *ānukūlya,* a friendly attitude directed to Kṛṣṇa*.* The word *anuśīlanam* which is modified by *ānukūlya* is thus unnecessary, because *bhakti* can be defined merely as a favorable attitude to Kṛṣṇa.” This objection is rejected, since even a pot is devoid of hostility to Kṛṣṇa, and is, therefore, pleasing to Him, but cannot be described as having bhakti. Therefore, the word *anuśīlanam* is also necessary.

In order to define the highest bhakti (*uttama-bhakti*), two further descriptive phrases are added. What type of *anuśīlanam* is this? It should be devoid of desires for obtaining results other than bhakti.

*smarantaḥ smārayantaś ca mitho ’ghaugha-haraṁ harim*  
*bhaktyā sañjātayā bhaktyā bibhraty utpulakāṁ tanum*

The devotee, who remembers, and induces others to remember, the Lord, who takes away heaps of sin, develops symptoms like hairs standing on end by bhakti, which has sprung from bhakti. SB 11.3.31

Bhakti which aims at achieving bhakti is proper, and bhakti which aims at achieving other goals is improper. In the verse, the phrase *anyābhilāṣa-śūnyam* has not been used. Instead, the phrase *anyābhilāṣitā-sūnyam* has been chosen. This means “devoid of other desires which are as deep-rooted as one’s nature.” In death threatening situations, a devotee may say, “O Lord! Please save me, Your devotee, from danger at this time.” Because it is a sudden, temporary desire, it is not harmful to his bhakti. That is because this desire arises beyond his control, opposite of his devotional nature. That desire is not his nature.

How is *anuśīlanam* further described? It is not covered by jñāna or karma or other items. *Jñāna* means inquiry about the undifferentiated Brahman, not inquiry about the worshipable Lord, for knowledge of the Lord is necessary for bhakti. *Karma* refers to the daily and periodic duties enjoined by the smṛti scriptures, not to the services offered to the Lord, for devotional activities are *anuśīlanam*. The word *ādi* indicates engaging in sacrifices, renunciation, yoga, sāṅkhya and other such items. These items should not cover *anuśīlanam*. The word *śūnya* (devoid) is not used in the second descriptive phrase, as it was in the first, namely, *anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam*. The intention of using *anāvṛtam* instead of *śūnya* is to forbid only those types of jñāna and karma, which conceal bhakti. Performing nitya-karmas (daily duties) with faith, out of fear of sin if one does not perform them, under the authority of rules, is a covering on bhakti. As well, performing them with faith that they will produce bhakti is a covering on bhakti. However, a great devotee is not excluded from pure bhakti by performing śrāddha ceremonies to the Pitṛs,if it is done without faith in their absolute nature, just to teach the common people.

Someone may propose that since the definition mentions *kṛṣṇānuśīlanam,* bhakti also should be defined by using the word *kṛṣṇa-bhakti* instead of just *bhakti*. In response to this, it may be said that the definition uses the word *bhakti* rather than the word *kṛṣṇa-bhakti* because in the devotional scriptures the word *bhakti* alone is commonly used to indicate devotion to Kṛṣṇa.

## Chapter 2

There are three types of bhakti: sādhana, bhāva and prema. Sādhana-bhakti has two divisions: vaidhi and rāgānuga.

“Let there be only two types of bhakti: sādhana and sādya. Bhāva should be included in sādhya-bhakti. Why is it necessary to have three types?” The answer is as follows. Later it will be said:

*utpanna-ratayaḥ samyaṅ nairvighnyam anupāgatāḥ*  
*kṛṣṇa-sākṣāt-kṛtau yogyāḥ sādhakāḥ parikīrtitāḥ*

Those are called practitioners (sādhaka) who have developed rati for Kṛṣṇa and are qualified to see Kṛṣṇa directly, but who have not completely extinguished the anarthas. BRS 2.1.276

In spite of the fact that bhāva or rati has appeared (*utpanna-ratayaḥ*), the obstacles have not been completely dissipated. Such persons are therefore classed as sādhakas. “Not having extinguished all obstacles (*samyaṅ-nairvighnyam anupāgatāḥ*)” means that there still remains a trace of some serious aparādha, because the effects of aparādha are very strong and long-lasting. In this condition, with even a trace of aparādha that generates suffering*,* sādhya-bhakti(prema) cannot appear. The qualities of the siddha-bhakta, who has sādhya-bhakti,are described as follows in the same section:

*avijñātākhila-kleśāḥ sadā kṛṣṇāśrita-kriyāḥ*  
*siddhāḥ syuḥ santata-prema-saukhyāsvāda-parāyaṇāḥ*

Those who experience no suffering at all, who perform all actions while taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa and who always taste the happiness of continuous prema, are known as the perfected devotees. BRS 2.1.280

Therefore, bhāva-bhakti cannot be included in sādhya-bhakti. However, by no means can it be included in sādhana-bhakti, because in this section, which defines sādhana-bhakti, the goal of sādhana-bhakti is described as bhāva-bhakti. Therefore, it cannot be included in sādhana-bhakti, since the goal of bhāva-bhakti is not to produce bhāva. Thus, it should be considered proper to classify bhakti into three types, not two.

## Chapter 3

*ādau śraddhā tataḥ sādhusaṅgo ’tha bhajanakriyā*  
*tato ’narthanivṛttiḥ syāt tato niṣṭhā rucis tataḥ (15)*

*tathāsaktis tato bhāvas tataḥ premābhyudañcati*  
*sādhakānām ayaṁ premṇaḥ prādurbhāvaḥ bhavet kramaḥ (16)*

First, there is association with devotees, by which one gains faith. Then, one associates with the devotees to learn the scriptures, and then practices bhakti. The anarthasare then destroyed. Steady *bhakti* without confusion follows. Desire for the Lord (*ruci*) appears. This is followed by spontaneous desire for the Lord (*āsakti*). This becomes bhāva and then prema*.* This is the progression for manifesting prema for those performing sādhana-bhakti.

Though there are many steps in the progression ofbhakti, the most common sequence is stated in two verses. In the beginning (*ādau*), by hearing the scriptures in association with the devotees, one attains faith (*śraddhā*). *Śraddhā* means trust or confidence. Then, after faith appears, again association with devotees takes place (*sadhu-saṅga*), for learning how to practice bhakti*. Niṣṭhā* means continuous bhakti without confusion. *Ruci* means desire for the Lord, but with direction by the intellect. *Āsakti* means desire, which is natural or spontaneous (without intellectual direction). By this one should understand the difference between *niṣṭhā* and *āsakti.*

## Chapter 4

There are sixty-four aṅgas of bhakti. The first ten are rules to be followed:

1\. Taking shelter of *guru;*  
2\. then after initiation, acquiring knowledge ;  
3\. service to guru with respect;  
4\. following the rules according to the scriptures as approved by the ācāryas.  
5\. inquiry about the method of worship;  
6\. renunciation of enjoyment to gain Kṛṣṇa’s mercy;  
7\. residing in Dvārakā or other holy places, and hearing glorification of holy places.  
8\. accepting only what is necessary as food etc. in order to continue practicing bhakti;  
9\. observing the Ekādaśī *vrata;*  
10\. giving respect to the *aśvattha, tulasi, dhatrī, cows, brāhmaṇas and vaiṣṇavas.*

The second ten are things to be avoided:

11\. giving up the association of those opposed to the Lord;  
12\. not being attached to making disciples;  
13\. not being enthusiastic for huge undertakings;

The total is thus sixty-four items.

## Chapter 5

In the phrase *kṛṣṇa-dīkṣādi-sikṣaṇam* the word *ādi* has the sense of “in the beginning.” Thus the phrase means “acquiring knowledge after or accompanied by initiation with Kṛṣṇa mantra.”

“Renouncing for the purpose of Kṛṣṇa” means “renouncing for Kṛṣṇa, to gain his mercy, in order to attain him.” The word *ādi* in the phrase *bhogādi-tyāga* means renunciation of enjoyment and desires for sons, wealth, and followers as well.

There will certainly be sevā-aparādhas and nāma-aparādhas for the person performing sādhana-bhakti. However, after committing them, the person should be careful to avoid those offenses in the future. Āgamas mention thirty-two sevā aparadhas:

## Chapter 6

*sarvāparādha-kṛd api mucyate hari-saṁśrayaḥ*  
*harer apy aparādhān yaḥ kuryād dvipada-pāṁsavaḥ*  
*nāmāśrayaḥ kadācit syāt taraty eva sa nāmataḥ*  
*nāmno’pi sarva-suhṛdo hy aparādhāt pataty adhaḥ*

A person who commits all offenses is freed from all those offenses by taking complete shelter of Hari. But the two-legged animal who commits offense against Hari is freed from those offenses by taking shelter of Hari’s name. However, though the name is the friend of all, by committing an offense against the name, a person falls to the lower regions.

The nāma aparadhas are as follows:

- Criticism of devotees
- Considering Śiva and Viṣṇu as separate supreme Lords
- Thinking guru to be an ordinary human being
- Criticizing the Vedas and scriptures following the Vedas such as the Purāṇas
- Thinking the glories of the Name as exaggeration
- Giving some other meaning to the Name
- Committing sins on the strength of the Name
- Considering the Name as equivalent of other auspicious actions
- Teaching the Name to the faithless
- Having no attraction to the Name though hearing its glories

## Chapter 7

**Vaidhi-bhakti**

*yatra rāgānavāptatvāt pravṛttir upajāyate*  
*śāsanenaiva śāstrasya sā vaidhī bhaktir ucyate*

Where the actions of bhakti arise, not from the attainment of rāga, but by the teachings of scriptures, it is called vaidhi-bhakti.

Characteristics of vaidhī bhakti are described. If one performs hearing, chanting and other devotional acts out of fear of breaking the scriptural orders, it is called vaidhī bhakti.

In undertaking acts of bhakti at the beginning of sādhana, in some cases the cause is greed and in some case the cause is scriptural orders. When the cause of performing bhakti is not greed and only scriptural orders it is called vaidhi-bhakti. Rāga means greed for acts of worship arising from seeing the deity and hearing Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in the Tenth Canto. When actions of bhakti arise by the orders of scripture, because of not attaining rāga, it is called vaidhi-bhakti.

## Chapter 8

**Rāgānuga-bhakti**

When the actions of bhakti are performed with greed to attain service to Kṛṣṇa in one's preferred relationship, it is called rāgānuga-bhakti. It is said:

*sevā sādhaka-rūpeṇa siddha-rūpeṇa cātra hi*  
*tad-bhāva-lipsunā kāryā vraja-lokānusārataḥ*

Following after the inhabitants of Vraja, one should perform service in one’s physical body and in one’s *siddha* body, with a desire for a particular bhāva.

One performs service with *sādhaka-rūpa*—the present body—and with the *siddha-rūpa*—the body which is suitable for serving Vraja Kṛṣṇa in the particular type of *rati* or *bhāva* one desires, and which appears through inner contemplation, with a desire for a particular *rati* directed to one’s beloved Kṛṣṇa situated in Vṛndāvana (*tad–bhāva-lipsunā*). One should follow after the dear devotees of Kṛṣṇa such as Rādhā, Lalitā, Viśākhā, Śrī Rūpa-mañjarī and those following after them**—**persons such as Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmīs (*vraja-lokānuṣārataḥ*). Accordingly, one should perform mental service in one’s *siddha-rūpa,* following after the examples of Śrī Rādhā, Lalitā, Viśākhā, Śrī Rūpa-mañjarī and others. In one’s physical body, one should perform services using one’s body, following after persons such as Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana Gosvāmīs situated in Vraja. By the word *vraja-lokā,* one should understand persons situated in Vraja **—** Rādhā, Candrāvalī and others. Following after them, one should perform service using one’s physical body also.

Some modern persons think that since persons such as Rādhā did not perform services such as surrendering to guru, following Ekādaśī vows, and serving *śālagrāma* and *tulasī,* we also need not do those activities. That idea is defeated here. Jīva Gosvāmī in his commentary has explained this with the words *vraja-lokās tv atra kṛṣna-preṣṭha-janās tad-anugatāś ca tad-anusārataḥ: vraja-lokā* means those dear to Kṛṣṇa (like Śrī Rādhā etc.) and persons following after them (like Śrī Rūpa etc.). One should follow in their footsteps.

## Chapter 9

*kṛṣṇaṁ smaran janaṁ cāsya preṣṭhaṁ nija-samīhitam*  
*tat-tat-kathā-rataś cāsau kuryād vāsaṁ vraje sadā*

Remembering the Vṛndāvana form of Kṛṣṇa and His dear associates who have inclinations for service similar to one’s own, absorbing oneself in hearing topics related to them, one should always live in Vraja.

Now starts the description of the method of rāgānuga-sādhana. One should remember the most dear form of Kṛṣṇa (*preṣṭham kṛṣṇam*), the son of Nanda of kaiśora age, and the devotees of that particular form of Kṛṣṇa (*asya janam*), who have the same type of desires (for serving Kṛṣṇa) as oneself (*nija-samīhitam*). Remembering such devotees, one should live in Vraja. If possible, one should physically live in Vṛndāvana, the place where Kṛṣṇa resided as Nanda’s son. If one cannot do that, one should live there mentally.

## Chapter 10

In rāgānuga, remembrance is the chief process. One remembers Kṛṣṇa absorbed in pastimes suitable to one's bhāva and his dear devotees. One should perform kīrtana and other aṅgas in the same way. But in arcana one should not perform worship or Rukmiṇī and meditation on Dvārakā, along with mudrās and nyāsas, though this is prescribed in scriptures, since it is contrary to one's bhāva for Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana. There is no fault if some aṅgas are performed incompletely in bhakti.

My dear Uddhava, because I have personally established it, this process of devotional service unto me is transcendental and free from any material motivation. Certainly, a devotee never suffers even the slightest loss by adopting this process. SB 11.29.20

There is fault if there is deficiency in the main principles for it is said:

*śruti-smṛti-purāṇādi-pañcarātra-vidhiṁ vinā*  
*aikāntikī harer bhaktir utpātāyaiva kalpate*

Even if a person seems to have attained steadiness in practicing pure bhakti to the Lord, that *bhakti* is a misfortune if it rejects the rules of śruti, smṛti, Purāṇa and Pañcarātra due to lack of faith in them.

If one has rāga internally but performs everything (externally) from the perspective of rules, one attains Rukmiṇī and others in Dvārakā.

## Chapter 11

The following should be considered. When the greed arises "I should have this bhāva" on hearing the sweetness of conjugal and other bhāvas present in the associates of Kṛṣṇa in his Vraja pastimes, there should be no dependence on influence of scriptures, since in its presence greed cannot be achieved. Greed cannot be produced by any means at any time by following the rules of the scriptures. Rather, greed spontaneously develops on hearing or seeing an object worthy of greed.

But there will be dependence on scriptures for inquiry concerning the method of attaining that sweet bhāva, since the method is written in scriptures and nowhere else. The scripture which expounds this worship is Bhāgavatam.

Among the acts of worship there are five types: some acts are filled with bhāva, some are related to that bhāva, some are favorable to the bhāva, some are not contrary to the bhāva, and some are contrary to the bhāva.

Aṅgas of bhakti like dāsya and sakhya are filled with bhāva. Chanting mantra and meditating, arising from surrender to the lotus feet of guru, are related to one’s bhāva. Hearing and remembering pastimes, qualities and names of the Lord suitable to one’s bhāva and his associates, as well as various services to the deity, are aṅga’s related to one's bhāva.

Renunciation of enjoyment by performing Ekādaśī, Janmāṣṭamī or Kārttika vows with a desire to attain one's bhāva, and respecting the āśvattha and tulasi are favorable to one's bhāva. Greeting the deity, offering respects, and wearing deity garlands are not contrary to one's bhāva. All these activities should be done. Mudṛas, nyāsas, and meditating on Dvārakā are contrary to one's bhāva and should not be done on the path of rāgānuga. One should perform those actions prescribed in scriptures for which one is qualified, and avoid all forbidden acts.

## Chapter 12

Bhāva-bhakti arises by the mercy of the Lord or his devotee on the maturation of sādhana-bhakti. Its signs are nine buds of devotion:

*kṣāntir avyartha-kālatvaṁ viraktir māna-śunyatā*  
*āśā-bandhaḥ samutkaṇṭhā nāma-gāne sadā ruciḥ*  
*āsaktis tad-guṇākhyāne prītis tad-vasati-sthale*  
*ity ādayo ’nubhāvāḥ syur jāta-bhāvāṅkure jane*

The anubhāvas or characteristics of a person who has developed the bud of bhāvaare as follows: tolerance, not wasting time, detachment from enjoyment, pridelessness, confidence in the Lord’s mercy, longing for the Lord, taste for chanting the name of the Lord, attachment to discussing about the Lord’s qualities, and attachment to living in the abode of the Lord. BRS.1.3.25-26

At this time one becomes qualified to meet Kṛṣṇa directly. If one sees symptoms of bhāva in persons who desire liberation, this is a reflection of bhāva (pratibimba), not real bhāva. Symptoms seen in ignorant persons are a shadow of bhāva (chāyā).

## Chapter 13

When bhāva matures it becomes prema. Its sign is that there is no decrease in the love at all in spite of obstacles. Because of extreme possessiveness of Kṛṣṇa, special sneha develops after prema. Its sign is melting of the heart. Then praṇaya arises. Then rāga arises. Its quality is intense affection. Its quality is deep trust.

## Chapter 14

Rasa arises from the combination of vibhāva, anubhāvas, sāttvika-bhāvas, vyabhicāri-bhāvas with the sthāyi-bhāva. When the bhāva is directed to Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa is known as the viṣaya ālambana in the vibhāvas. The person endowed with the bhāva for Kṛṣṇa, the devotee, is called the āśraya ālambana in the vibhāvas. Objects like his clothing or ornaments which cause remembrance of Kṛṣṇa are called the uddīpana in the vibhāvas.

Actions which indicate the bhāva, such as smiling, singing or dancing, are called anubhāvas. Sāttvika- bhāvas disturb the heart and body. They are eight: being stunned, perspiration, hairs standing on end, breaking of the voice, trembling, change of complexion, tears and fainting. They appear in five grades from weak to strong, giving successively more happiness: dhūmāyita, jvalita, dīpta, uddīpta and sūddīpta. They manifest as snigdha in the nitya-siddha and as digdha in persons with rati.

They called rukṣa symptoms in persons who do not actually have bhāva. If they appear in persons desiring liberation they are called sāttvika-bhavas arising from raty-ābhāsa. If they appear in practitioners of karma or materialist they are caused by sattvābhāsa (generated by emotions). If they appear in persons with hard hearts simply by practice they are called niḥsattva (fake symptoms). If they appear in persons who hate the Lord they are called pratīpa symptoms.

## Chapter 15

Vyabhicāri-bhāvas nourish the sthāyi-bhāva and are temporary.

*nirvedo’tha viṣādo dainyaṁ glāni-śramau ca mada-garvau*  
*śaṅkā-trāsāvegā unmādāpasmṛtī tathā vyādhiḥ*  
*moho mṛtir ālasyaṁ jāḍyaṁ vrīḍāvahitthā ca*  
*smṛtir atha vitarka-cintā-mati-dhṛtayo harṣa utsukatvaṁ ca*  
*augryam arṣāsūyāś cāpalyaṁ caiva nidrā ca*  
*suptir bodha itīme bhāvā vyabhicāriṇaḥ samākhyātāḥ*

The vyabhicāri-bhāvas are as follows: self-disgust (*nirveda*), remorse *(viṣāda*), thinking oneself unqualified (*dainyam* or *dīnatā*), debility, weakness due to labor *(glāni* or *mlāni*), perspiration from dancing etc. (*śrama*), intoxication from drinking honey *(mada*), pride (*garva*), apprehension *(śaṅkā*), sudden fear *(trāsa*), confusion of the mind (*āvega*), insanity *(unmāda*), epilepsy (*apasmṛti*), sickness such as fever (*vyādhi*), loss of internal awareness (*moha*), death-like symptoms (*mṛti*), sloth (*ālasyam*), indecision (*jāḍyam*), shame (*vrīḍā*), concealment *(avahitthā*), remembrance (*smṛti),* conjecture *(vitarka*), pondering what will happen (*cintā*), finding meaning through scriptural reference (*mati*), steadiness (*dhṛti*), joy *(harṣa*), impatience or longing (*autsukyam*), ferocity (*augrya*), intolerance (*amarṣa*), fault-finding *(asūyā),* inability to remain steady (*cāpalya*), sleep (*nidrā*), dreaming *(supti*), and enlightenment (*bodha*).

## Chapter 16

The bhāvas appear according to the heart of the devotee. In the heart deep like the ocean, they do not manifest or manifest slightly. In the heart shallow like a pond, they manifest intensely. There is no hard and fast rule however and therefore this is not discussed in detail.

## Chapter 17

The sthāyi-bhāva has five forms. Śuddha-rati has three types: sāmānya, svaccha and śānta. The sthāyi-bhavā arising from sāmānya-rati by maturity of general worship of ordinary persons lacking association of devotees situated in a particular rasa is called sāmānya-rūpa.

Five types of rati develop through maturity of worship by indiscriminant association with devotees of all five ratis (sānta, dāsya, sakhya, vātsalya and mādhurya). Sometimes śanta-rati, sometimes dāsyam, sometimes sakhyam, sometimes vātsalya and sometimes kānta-bhāva will arise at different times according to the association with these different types of devotees. The person is not fixed in one bhāva. This is called svaccha-rati.

Among those fixed in a single rasa, there are five types of rati. The śanta devotees have śānti-rati. The devotees with dāsya bhāva have dāsya-rati. Those with sakhya-bhāva have sakhya -rati. Those with vātsalya-bhāva have vātsalya-rati. Devotees with ujjvala-bhāva have priyatā-rati.

The five chief rasas that manifest from these ratis are successively superior: śānta-rasa, dāsya-rasa, sakhya-rasa, vātsalya-rasa and ujjvala-rasa. Those in śānta have their intelligence fixed in Kṛṣṇa. Those in dāsya are fixed in service. Those in sakhya have lack of reverence. Those in vātsalya have affection. Those in ujjvala-rasa experience happiness by offering themselves intimately to the Lord. Those in the higher rasas have superior qualities to those in the lower rasas.

## Chapter 18

In śanta-rasa, Kṛṣṇa with qualities of Paramātmā or four armed Nārāyāna, Parabrahman in human form, is the viṣaya. Practitioners of austerities like the Kumāras are the āśrayas. Jñānis also become the āśraya in śānta-rasa if they give up the desire for liberation and become endowed with desires for bhakti by the mercy of Kṛṣṇa's devotees. The uddīpanas for śānta-rasa are places where there are persons who live in forests or mountains. The anubhāvas are staring at the tip of the nose, activities of the avadhūta, giving up all possessions, not hating persons who hate the Lord, not being too attracted to the devotees, silence, and absorption in jñāna scriptures. The sāttvika-bhāvas are tears, hairs standing on end, perspiration, being stunned, trembling, change of complexion, breaking of the voice, but not fainting. The vyabhicāri-bhāvas are self-disgust, determination and considering scriptures (mati). The sthāyi-bhāva is śānti-rati.

## Chapter 19

In dāsya-rasa, Kṛṣṇa is the viṣaya, as the Lord and master, omniscient and affection to the devotees. The āśrayas are four: adhikṛta-bhaktas (holding posts), like Brahmā, and Śiva, āśritas (those to take shelter), pārṣadas (retinue), and anugas (servants). The āśritas are of three types: śaraṇyas, like the kings imprisoned by Jarāsandha, jñāni-caras and sevā-niṣṭhas. Examples of jñāni-caras are the Kumāras who gave up the desire for liberation and became fixed in dāsya. Examples of sevā-niṣṭhas are Bahulāśva, Candradhvaja and Harihaya, who were engaged in worship from the beginning of their practice. Examples of pārṣadas are Uddhava, Dāruka and Śrutadeva. Anugas (servants) in Dvārakā are Sucanda and Maṇḍava. Anugas in Vraja are Raktaka, Patraka and Madhukaṇṭha.

Among the anugas and pārṣadas some are dhūrya devotees, filled with bhakti to Kṛṣṇa and his followers. Some are dhīra devotees, having great respect for Kṛṣṇa's consorts. Some are vīra devotees, who having received the Lord's mercy, serve only him out of pride.

Also well there are the pālyas, Kṛṣṇa's sons like Pradyumna and Sāmba, filled with affection and respect for Kṛṣṇa as a father (gaurava-prīti).

Some of the dāsyas are nitya-siddhas, some are sādhana-siddhas and some are sādhakas (bhāva).

The uddīpanas are mercy of Kṛṣṇa, his foot dust, mahāprasāda and other items. The anubhāvas are actions like carrying out the order of Kṛṣṇa. In dāsya rasa there is prema, sneha and rāga. The sthāyi in the adhikṛta devotees and āśrita devotees is up to prema. In the pārṣadas it is up to sneha. In Parīkṣit, Dāruka and Uddhava rāga manifests. In Raktaka and all Vraja all these stages manifest. In Pradumnya and other sons of Kṛṣṇa, all the stages manifest.

Separation until seeing Kṛṣṇa for the first time is called ayoga. Separation after first meeting him is called viyoga. Ten conditions manifest in separation: heat, thinness, insomnia, unstable mind, lack of attraction for anything, being stunned, sickness, insanity, fainting and death-like symptoms.

## Chapter 20

Kṛṣṇa as the viṣaya of sakkya-rasa is skilful in the arts and clever. He dresses nicely and loves to enjoy. The āśrayas are of four types: suhṛts, sakhas, priya-sakhas and priya-narma-sakhas.

The suhṛts are older than Kṛṣṇa, and have a little vātsalya affection as well. Examples are Subhadra, Maṇḍalībhardra and Balarāma. The sakhas are slightly less in age and have a mixture of dāsya. Examples are Viśāla, Vṛṣabha and Devaprastha. Those who are equal in age to Kṛṣṇa are priya-sakhas like Śrīdama, Sudāma and Vasudāma. Those touched with śṛṅgara-rasa who assist Kṛṣṇa in pastimes with the gopīs are priya-narma-sakhas like Subala, Madhumaṅgala and Arjuna (cowherd).

## Chapter 21

The uddīpanas are his kaumāra, paugaṇḍa and kaiśora ages, his horn, flute, and musical instruments.

*kaumāraṁ pañcamābdāntaṁ paugaṇḍaṁ daśamāvadhi*  
*kaiśoram āpañca-daśaṁ yauvanaṁ tu tataḥ param*

Childhood (*kaumāra*) ends with the fifth year. Boyhood (*paugaṇḍa*) ends with the tenth year. Youth (*kaiśora*) ends with the sixteenth. After that is manhood (*yauvana*).

Kṛṣṇa performs pastimes in Vraja until his tenth year, eighth month. Kṛṣṇa ages quicker than normal. Thus his kaumāra period lasts till the third year and four months. After that, paugaṇḍa age lasts till six years and eight months. His kaiśora period lasts till he is ten years and eight months. After that, for all time he remained in kaiśora period. The last portion of kaiśora starts in his tenth year. He remains in that state at all times after. Kaiśora began in his seventh year, during Vaiśākha month (six years and eight months). Though it appears that the gopīs performed pastimes when Kṛṣṇa was in paugaṇḍa period, it was not so, since Kṛṣṇa entered kaiśora period early. For this reason the above has been written.

The anubhāvas for sakhya-rasa are wrestling and playing, sleeping on the same bed etc. All the sāttvika-bhavas manifest. The vyabhicāri-bhāvas are joy and pride etc. Sakhya-rati, with special intimacy filled with lack of respect, viewing Kṛṣṇa as an equal is the sthāyi-bhāva. with sakhya-rati, praṇaya, prema, sneha and rāga manifest in sakhya-rasa.

Friends outside of Vraja are Arjuna, Bhīmasena and Śrīdāma-vipra. In viyoga (separation) the ten previously mentioned conditions manifest.

## Chapter 22

In vātsalya-rasa, the viṣaya is Kṛṣṇa endowed with tender limbs, mild nature, and all good qualities. The āśrayas are elders such as parents endowed with the bhāva caring for Kṛṣṇa. In Vraja the āśrayas are Yaśodā, Nanda, Rohiṇī, Upananda and his wife. Elsewhere, Devakī, Kuntī, and Vasudeva are the āśrayas. The uddīpanas are Kṛṣṇa's smile, his talking, and infant activities. The anubhāvas are smelling his head, giving him blessings, and caring for him with affection. The sāttvika-bhāvas are the eight and, in addition, milk flowing from the breast. The vyabhicāri-bhāvas are joy, worry etc.  
The sthāyi-bhāva is vātsalya-rati. This proceeds to prema, sneha and rāga. In viyoga the ten conditions manifest.

## Chapter 23

In madhurya-rasa, Kṛṣṇa, an ocean sweetness in form, pastimes, and prema, is the viṣaya. The āśrayas are his beloved consorts. The uddīpanas are the sound of the flute, spring season, the call of the cuckoo, seeing fresh clouds and seeing the throat of a peacock. The anubhāvas are side glances and laughter etc. All the sāttvika-bhāvas manifest to the sūddīpta stage. All the vyabhicāri-bhāvas except laziness and ferocity manifest. The sthāyi-bhāva is priyatā-rati. This proceeds to prema, sneha, praṇaya, rāga, anurāga and mahābhāva as described in Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi.

## Chapter 24

Some rasas are friendly or inimical to others. Śānta and dāsya are mutual friends. Sakhya and vātsalya are neutral. Vātsalya has no friends among the primary rasas. Ujjvala and dāsya are mutual enemies.

## Chapter 25

Mixture of bhāvas will be discussed. Baladeva and others have a mixture of sakhya, vātsalya and dāsya. Mukharā and others have a mixture of vātsalya and sakhya. Yudhiṣṭhira has vātsalya and sakhya. Bhīma has sakhya and vātsalya. Arjuna has sakhya and dāsya. Nakula and Sahadeva have dāsya and sakhya. Uddhava has dāsya and sakhya. Akūra and Ugrasena have dāsya and vātsalya. Aniruddha and others have dāsya and sakhya.

## Chapter 26

The seven minor rasas - hāsya, adbhuta, vīra, karuṇa, raudra, bhayanaka and bībhatsa manifest in the five types of devotees. The five types of devotees are the āśraya. The viṣayas of the first six minor rasas are Kṛṣṇa, his devotees and others related to them. The viṣayas of bībhatsa-rasa are impure objects like flesh and blood. Kṛṣṇa and his enemies are the viṣaya of raudra and bhayanaka rasas. The anubhāvas are swelling of the cheeks, widening of the eyes etc. According to the rasa, two or three sāttvika-bhāvas manifest as suitable to the occasion. The vyabhicārī-bhavas are joy, etc. The bhāvas are laughter, astonishment, enthusiasm, anger, lamentation, fear and disgust. The sthāyi-bhāvas are thus called hāsya, viṣmaya, utṣaha, krodha, śoka, bhaya and jugupsa ratis. In vīra-rasa there are four types: yuddha-vīra, dāna-vīra, dayā-vīra and dharma-vīra because of enthusiasm for fighting, charity works, giving mercy and performing acts of dharma.  
The total number of rasas is thus twelve.

## Chapter 27

The seven secondary rasaa manifest within the five chief rasas. Hāsya, and yuddha-vīra manifest in sakhya. Adbhuta-rasa manifests in all five primary rasas. Karuṇa, dāna-vīra and dayā-vīra manifest in vātsalya. Bhayānaka-rasa manifests in vātsalya and dāsya. Bībhatsa manifests in śānta-rasa. A small portion of raudra-rasa manifests in persons with vātsaya and ujjvala-rasas. It is understood that the secondary rasas also have friends and enemies among the primary rasas.

## Chapter 28

Rasābhāsa does not occur if the conflicting rasas are remembered, if they are negated by a statement, if the viṣayas or āśrayas are different, if the rasas are being compared or if a friendly or neutral rasa intervenes. Otherwise, two inimical rasas together create rasābhāsa. If two friendly rasas combine, it creates excellent rasa. If both rasas are primary, rasābhāsa occurs even if the viṣayas or āṣṛayas are different (one should be a secondary rasa). However, in descriptions of adhirūḍha-mahābhāva of Rādhā, combination of inimical rasas does not create rasābhāsa. Moreover if Kṛṣṇa is simultaneously the viṣaya or āśraya of all rasas, there is no rasābhāsa.

The following are generally accepted as rasābhāsa. Śanta-rasābhāsa occurs if one does not show more astonishment for Kṛṣṇa than Brahman. Dāsya-rasābhāsa occurs if a servant shows impudence in front of Kṛṣṇa. Sakhya-rasābhāsa occurs if there is attempt at friendship between a person in sakhya with a person in dāsya-rasa. There is vātsalya-rasābhāsa if, by recognizing the strength of one's child, one stops pampering him. There is ujjvala-rasābhāsa if one of the lovers does not desire enjoyment or if there is a bold request for enjoyment. If hāsya and other secondary rasas are not related to Kṛṣṇa they are called rasābhāsa. If these occur in enemies of Kṛṣṇa (with Kṛṣṇa as viṣaya) they are cases of extreme rasābhāsa.

Because some devotees lack knowledge of grammar Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu-bindu has been written by Viśvanātha Cakravarti as a small sampling of Bhaktirasāmṛta-sindhu.
