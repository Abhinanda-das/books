# Bhāgavatāmṛta-kaṇā (BS)

1\. The chief among all forms defined in Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta, who is the supremely independent form of greatness and sweetness, is Śrī Kṛṣṇa. He is called svayam-rūpa.

2\. The form which has almost equal power is called vilāsa. An example is the lord of Vaikuṇṭha. The form which possesses less powers than the vilāsa form is called its aṁśa. Matsya and Kūrma are examples.

3\. A form which has endowment of a single power is called āveśa. An example is Vyāsa.

4\. There are three types of avatāra: puruṣāvatāra, guṇavatāra and līlāvatāra.

5\. Among the puruṣāvatāras the first is the creator of mahat-tattva, Kāraṇārṇavaśāyī. He is the antaryāmī of prakṛṭi and is an aṁśa of Saṅkarṣaṇa. The second puruṣa named Garbhodakaśāyī is the antaryāmī of the whole of a single universe and is the creator of Brahmā. He is an aṁśa of Pradyumna. The third puruṣa, Kṣīrodakaśāyī is the antaryāmī of the individual jīva. He is an aṁśa of Aniruddha.

6\. Among the guṇāvatāras, Viṣṇu, Kṣīrodakaśāyī, is the maintainer of the universe through sattva guṇa. Brahmā who arises from the lotus in Garbhodakaśāyī's navel is the creator of the universe through rajoguṇa. In some kalpas a jīva with suitable puṇyas is Brahmā. This jīva becomes an avatāra by endowment of powers by the Lord. He is not equal to Viṣṇu because of his contact with rajoguṇa. In some kalpas Viṣṇu himself becomes Brahmā, just as the Lord as Yajña sometimes becomes Indra. In that case, Brahmā is equal to Viṣṇu. The gross form of the universe from Pātāla-loka to Satya-loka is Brahmā. Hiraṇyagarbha, a subtle form consisting of all the jīvas, is also Brahmā. His antaryāmī and lord is Garbhodakaśāyī.

The gross form is called Vairāja and the subtle form is called Hiraṇyagarbha. In some kalpas Brahmā is jīva and in some kalpas is Viṣṇu. Śiva is the destroyer of the universe through tamoguṇa. However, Ṣaḍaśiva who is a special limb of svayam rūpa, is beyond the guṇas. He is the aṁśī or source of Śiva. He is superior to Brahmā, equal to Viṣṇu and not the same as a jīva, since the jīva is connected with the guṇas.

7\. The four Kumāras, Nārada, Varāha, Matsya, Yajña, Nara-nārāyaṇa, Kapila, Dattatreya, Hayagrīva, Haṁsa, Pṛṣnigarbha, Ṛṣabha, Pṛthu, Nrsimḥa, Kūrma Dhanvantari, Mohinī, Vāmana, Paraśurāma, Rāma, Vyāsa, Balarāma, Kṛṣṇa, Buddha, and Kalki are the līlāvatāras. They appear in every kalpa.

8\. The manvantāra avatāras are Yajña, Vibhu, Satyasena, Hari, Vaikuṇṭha, Ajita, Vāmana, Sārvabhauma, Ṛṣabha, Viṣvaksena, Dharmasetu, Sudāmā, Yogeśvara and Bṛhadbhānu.

9\. The yugāvataras are Śukla, Rakta, Śyāma and Kṛṣṇa.

10\. Some of these are āveśa forms, some are prābhava, some are vaibhava and some are parāvastha.

11\. The four Kumāras, Nārada, Pṛthu, and others (Paraśurāma) are āveśa forms. Prābhava forms are Mohinī, Dhanvantari, Haṁsa, Ṛṣabha, Vyāsa, Dattatreya, and Śukla and other yuga avatāras. Those with more powers are called vaibhava forms: Matsya, Kūrma, Nara-nārāyaṇa, Varāha, Hayagrīva, Pṛśnigarbha, Balarāma and Yajña. Those with more powers are called parāvasthā. These are three, in order of increasing powers: Nṛsiṁha, Rāma and Kṛṣṇa. Kṛṣṇa is Svayam Bhagavān. There is no one superior to him.

12\. Kṛṣṇa dwells in four places: Vraja, Mathurā, Dvārakā and Goloka. Kṛṣṇa, accompanied by his family members and Balarāma, is most perfect in Vraja. In Mathurā he is more perfect. In Dvārakā is perfect, accompanied by his family and Pradyumna and Aniruddha. In Goloka, though the form is similar to the perfect form, that form is also similar to most perfect form, since Kṛṣṇa performs pastimes there as in Vṛndāvana. Because there is greater and greater sweetness in the forms dwelling in Dvārakā, Mathurā and Vraja, there is less covering by powers and greatness. Because of the decrease in sweetness from Vraja to Mathurā and to Dvārakā, there is greater manifestation of powers.

13\. Above Virajā, acting like a moat, in whose water reside millions of universes generated from the hair holes of Mahā Viṣṇu, lies Mahā-vaikuṇṭha-loka. Its upper portion is Goloka. There the lord of Goloka, Kṛṣṇa, resides with his associates, performing pastimes. His vilāsa form is Paramātmā, lord of the spiritual sky. Brahman is his aspect with no qualities. The lord of Goloka's second vyūha is Baladeva. Baladeva's vilāsa form is Saṅkarṣaṇa in Mahā-vaikuṇṭha. Saṅkarṣaṇa's aṁśa is Kāraṇārṇavaśāyī. His vilāsa is Garbhodaśāyī, the antaryāmī of the universe. He is also an aṁśa of Pradyumna. His vilāsa form is Kṣīrodakaśāyī, an aṁśa of Aniruddha. Matsya, Kūrma and other avatāras are vilāsa forms of Garbhodakaśāyī.

In the three abodes of Dvārakā, Mathurā and Vṛndāvana, Kṛṣṇa has increasing degrees of sweetness because of greater human pastimes.

14\. The pastimes are of two types: prakaṭa and aprakaṭa. The aprakaṭa pastimes, composed of simultaneous bālya, paugaṇḍa and kaiśora aspects, are eternal, with unlimited manifestations of Kṛṣṇa and his associates. When Kṛṣṇa with his associates reveals these pastimes in the material world in specific order in one manifestation, they are called prakaṭa pastimes. The difference is that Kṛṣṇa leaves and returns to Vraja and his other abodes in the prakaṭa pastimes. The prakaṭa pastimes from birth to disappearance are seen sequentially in all the universes by the inhabitants of those universes. One Vṛndāvana, one Mathurā and one Dvārakā are seen by the inhabitants of a universe in Bhārata contained within each of the millions of universes. As the sun's rays seen in various parts of the zodiac as time progresses, so Kṛṣṇa's pastimes after being completed in one Bhārata in one universe, manifest in another Bhārata in another universe, without manifesting in others. Pastimes like birth are eternal, being full of eternity, knowledge and bliss. Pastimes like the disappearance using the iron pestle and the stealing of the queens by thieves are like a magician's illusions, in order to hide the eternal nature of the other pastimes. No one worships these two pastimes.

In the prakaṭa pastimes, some followers may see the jewelled trees and ground of Vṛndāvana and others may not see them, by the desire of the Lord. After the end of the prakaṭa pastimes however the devotee may see these things when he has great longing because of his intense worship. He will see things according to the Lord's desire and the devotee's impressions (due to his particular sādhana).

Among all forms, that of Kṛṣṇa, son of Nanda, is supreme. Among all abodes, Gokula is supreme. Kṛṣṇa has four types of sweetness in Vraja: the prema of the devotees there, the pastimes, the flute and his form.

15\.

*mārkaṇḍeyo ’mbarīṣaś ca vasur vyāso vibhīṣaṇaḥ \|*  
*puṇḍarīko baliḥ śambhuḥ prahlādo viduro dhruvaḥ \|\|*  
*dālbhyaḥ parāśaro bhīṣmo nāradādyāś ca vaiṣṇavaiḥ \|*  
*sevyā hariṁ niṣevyāmī no ced āgaḥ paraṁ bhavet \|\|2\|\|*

**After worshipping the Lord, the Vaiṣṇavas should also worship Mārkaṇḍeya, Ambarīṣa, Uparicara-vasu, Vyāsa, Vibhīṣaṇa, Puṇḍarīka, Bali, Śiva, Prahlāda, Vidura, Dhruva, Dalbhya, Parāśara, Bhīṣma, Nārada and other devotees. If they do not do so, they commit unavoidable offense.** ***Padma Purāṇa***

Among these devotees Prahlāda is the best. Greater than him are the Pāṇdavas. Greater than the Pāṇdavas are the Yādavas. Greater than them is Uddhava. Greater than Uddhava are the gopīs of Vraja. Greater than them is Śrīmad-rādhā.

Bhāgavatāmṛta-kaṇikā, like gold set with jewels, has been written for dedicated devotees who are not proficient in grammar.
