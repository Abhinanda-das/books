## Karma Simplified

Although the complete idea is fairly complex karma ca be summed up in one word: reaction.

It is a reaction to both thought and action. The general features of karma are:

- Karma is a law of the universe, just like the laws of physics, chemistry or gravity, and operates with the same infallible dependability.
- Every desire we have, and every action we perform, generates a karmic reaction that eventually must be fulfilled.
- Actions that generate karma are akin to planting seeds that will fructify at some future time, either in this life, or in a future life.
- The results of karma are awarded when the karmic seeds mature and bear fruit.
- “Good” karma gives us desirable results, while “bad”
- karma gives us pain, suffering and trouble.
- Karma operates on two levels - individual and group karma
- We are currently getting the results of karma created in this life as well as from previous lives
- If the karma sown in this life is not fulfilled in this life, we are forced to be born again in this world to experience it. See points #2 and #3 above.
- We are born into circumstances that will facilitate the fulfillment of our karmic destiny.

## Karma is a law of the universe

Everyone knows the law of physics: “for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.” This is also a way of understanding karma. But instead of operating in the physical realm, karma operates in our own personal realm, and karma is very personal, working uniquely, and flawlessly, for every individual living being. Karma is a universal law that functions beyond our understanding, like gravity, and beyond our perception, like the electromagnetic waves that our mobile phones use (although gravity can be described with mathematical equations nobody really knows why gravity works).

## Every thought and action creates a karmic reaction

Every thought, regardless of how intentional or inadvertent, sets the chain of karma into motion to eventually bring that result to you. Even passing thoughts such as “That would be a good company to work for”, or “that dress is nice, I would look good in it”, will set into motion the workings of karma to bring the result.

This principle of obtaining a desired result by focused intention is taught in the books mentioned at the beginning of this chapter. By the same principle karma generates undesirable results as well. Every “good” act or thought we do/have will bring a “good,” or desirable, reaction, and every “bad” act or thought we do/have brings a “bad”, or undesirable, reaction.

Basically karma comes in two flavors: the results I like and want, and those that I don’t like and want to avoid—good and bad karma.

One of the most important things to understand about karma is that every desire will be fulfilled. Every karmic reaction must be received—however long it takes, regardless of how many lifetimes we must be born again. Indeed, it is the reactions of karma that generate our future births in this world.

## Acts of karma are like planting seeds that will fructify in time

Desires and actions initiate the chain of karma as if planting a seed. As we know seeds take time to germinate, and then the plant grows and produces some fruits. In this analogy the fruits are the results of karma. In other words it takes time to generate the results and the time involved depends on a number of factors. Simple desires can bring results in a matter of minutes. Complex desires can take years, decades, or even lifetimes before the result is given.

## Good karma is given when we have sufficient merit

Say we desire to be very rich. We may engage in daily visualization exercises toward that end. We may work hard and take advantage of all of the opportunities that come our way. We may be qualified in so many ways: smart, quick, and well financed, yet still our efforts may end in failure. While others who don’t have the advantages that we have are successful. This has been seen many times in history. Why? Simply because we do not have – in this lifetime – the required karmic merit for that specific result, and the desired result will therefore not be given to us in this lifetime. However, that desire is not lost. It remains in gestation until in a future life we have built up sufficient “karmic credit” by doing good works, giving in charity, etc. Then the result will be given, either due to circumstances, i.e., we find ourselves born into a wealthy family, or the success we desired in an earlier lifetime is obtained either through hard work or effortlessly. The circumstances depend on what we have done to earn the result.

The concept of karma helps us to understand why good things happen to “bad” people, or bad things happen to “good” people. It is the results of activities (karma) performed in previous lives that bring results seemingly by chance, or disconnected with our actions. Chance is not involved whatsoever.
