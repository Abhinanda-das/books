The Citra-Kavitvani is a collection of 12 amazing verses written by Srila Rupa Goswami. These verses, full of transcendental tongue twisters and playful picture poetry, highlight Rupa Goswami’s genius as a foremost poet among the Gaudiya Vaisnavas.

# Citra-kavitvāni

## Foreword

*atha citra-kavitvāni.*

**Now for the** ***Picture Verses,*** **or** ***Amazing Verses*****.**

Before beginning his commentary on the *Citra-kavitvāni,* Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa prays to Rūpa Gosvāmī, the author of the verses, for empowerment to understand and explain them:

*duṣkarāṇi kavitvāni*  
*yaḥ karoti mahā-balaḥ*  
*śrī-rūpaḥ kavi-bhūpo me*  
*sa deyāt buddhi-sampadam*

**“May Śrī Rūpa, the mighty king of poets who composes very difficult to write poetry, grant methe wealth of intelligence needed to understand his verses.”**

Here Śrīla Baladeva uses the word *duṣkara* (“difficult to compose”), which term the *Agni Puraṇa* uses to name the whole category of *citra* poetry*.* Thus the words *duṣkarāṇi kavitvāni* are equivalent to *citra-kavitvāni.* To define the word *citra,* Śrīla Baladeva then quotes the *Viśva-koṣa* dictionary: *citraṁ syād adbhutālekhya-tilakeṣu vihāyasi* (*Citra* means“amazing” “picture”, “mark on the forehead”, and “sky”.” Taking the first meaning, *citra-kavitvāni* means poetic verses that cause astonishment like “How did he compose a verse using only one or two consonants?” Alternatively, *citra-kavitvāni* can mean verses written in the form of “pictures” like the shape of a disc. This is in accordance with Bharata Muni’s definition, *tac citraṁ yatra varṇānāṁ khaḍgādy-ākṛti-hetutā,* which means “*Citra* is that poetry in which the syllables are put in particular arrangements to reveal such forms as those of swords and discs.”

*tatra dvy-akṣarāṇi.*

**Among these amazing verses are those that have only two** ***akṣaras*** **(“consonants”):**

### Text 1

*rasāsāra-susārorur*  
*asurāriḥ sasāra saḥ*  
*saṁsārāsir asau rāse*  
*su-riraṁsuḥ sa-sārasaḥ*

**Пословный перевод:**

*rasa—*of nectar; *āsāra—*a shower; *su-sāra—*very strong; *ūruḥ—*whose thighs; *asura—*of the demons; *ariḥ—*the enemy; *sasāra—*went; *saḥ—*that same one; *saṁsāra—*for the cycle of repeated birth and death; *asiḥ—*a sword; *asau—*He; *rāse—*into the *rāsa* dance; *su-riraṁsuḥ—*very much desiring transcendental conjugal pleasure; *sa-sārasaḥ—*with a lotus flower.

**Перевод:**

**Filled with transcendental amorous desire, the son of Nanda, who showers nectar, whose thighs are strong, who is the enemy of the demons, and who is the sword that cuts down the cycle of repeated birth and death, entered the rāsa dance holding a lotus flower.**

**Комментарий:**

In this verse the keyword is *rāse.* Rūpa Gosvāmī wants to describe Kṛṣṇa’s entrance into the *rāsa* dance, and since the word *rāsa* contains only the consonants *r* and *s,* all the other words he uses also contain only *r* and *s.* Thus, when recited, this verse becomes a transcendental tongue-twister.

Someone may argue, “How does this verse have only two *akṣaras,* since the *visarga* (*ḥ*) and *anusvāra* (*ṁ*) are also present?” The answer is that in *citra-kāvya,* the genre of amazing poetry, *visarga* and *anusvāra* do not count as consonants. As a general rule, *visarga* and *anusvara* can be considered either vowels or consonants according to the need; they are listed between the vowels and consonants in the Sanskrit alphabet and so can be taken either way (See *vṛtti* 285 of *Hari-nāmāmṛta-vyākaraṇam*). Taken as vowels here, they pose no threat to the verse’s having only two consonants.

### Text 2

*carcoru-rocir uccorā*  
*ruciro ’raṁ carācare*  
*caurācāro ’cirāc cīraṁ*  
*rucā cārur acūcurat*

**Пословный перевод:**

*carcā—*with ointment; *uru—*great; *rociḥ—*whose beauty; *ucca—*broad; *urāḥ—*whose chest; *ruciraḥ—*handsome; *aram—*very; *cara-acare—*in the world of moving and non-moving beings; *caura—*like those of a thief; *ācāraḥ—*whose activities; *acirāt—*quickly; *cīram—*the garments; *rucā—*because of splendor; *cāruḥ—*attractive; *acūcurat—*stole.

**Перевод:**

**Acting like a thief, the broad-chested son of Nanda, the most handsome person in the world, quickly stole the gopīs’ garments. His effulgence made Him very attractive and His beauty was enhanced by the sandalpaste on Him.**

**Комментарий:**

In this verse the keyword is *caurācāraḥ,* which contains only the consonants *c* and *r.* Accordingly, all the the other words Rūpa Gosvāmī uses here also contain only *c* and *r.* Śrīla Baladeva explains that Kṛṣṇa is not actually a thief (*caura*) but only acts like one (*caurācāra*) to give pleasure to His devotees.

### Text 3

*dhare dharādhara-dharaṁ*  
*dhārādhara-dhur-ārudhi*  
*dhīra-dhīr ārarādhādhi-*  
*rodhaṁ rādhā dhuraṁ-dharam*

**Пословный перевод:**

*dhare—*at the hill; *dharā-dhara—*of the hill; *dharam—*the holder; *dhārā-dhara—*of the clouds (clouds are called *dhārā-dharas* because they bear (*dhara*) a shower (*dhārā*)); *dhur*—the burden; *ārudhi*—which blocks; *dhīra—*steady; *dhīḥ—*whose mind; *ārarādha—*She worshiped; *ādhi—*distress; *rodham—*who removes; *rādhā—*Rādhā; *dhuram-dharam—*the Supreme Lord.

**Перевод:**

**Beneath Govardhana Hill, which was blocking the forceful attack of the devastating clouds, steady-minded Rādhā worshiped the Supreme Lord, who held up the hill, removing the anguish of His devotees.**

**Комментарий:**

In this verse the keyword is *dharādhara-dharam,* which describes Kṛṣṇa as the lifter of Govardhana Hill. Here Kṛṣṇa is poetically described as the holder of the holder of the holder. In other words, the earth is called *dharā* because it supports all the living entities on its surface, and hills and mountains are called *dharā-dharas* because they support the earth; they do this by causing precipitation and sheltering the valleys from wind and so on. Finally Kṛṣṇa is called *dharādhara-dhara* because he held Govardhana Hill atop His left pinky for seven days.

In this connection there is an interesting alternate meaning of the word *govardhana.* Usually the *go* in *govardhana* is understood to mean “cow” and *vardhana* is understood as causative form of the verb *vṛdh* (to thrive). The usual meaning of *govardhana* is this “That which causes the cows to thrive (by supplying excellent green grass and fresh water)\].” However, Śrīla Baladeva, while commenting on verse seven of Rūpa Gosvāmī’s *Prathama-govardhāṣṭakam,* says that *go* can also mean “thunderbolt” and *vardhana* can also come from the verb *vardh* (to tear apart or destroy). In this way the meaning is “That which smashes the thunderbolt.” This is another poetical usage. Mountains and thunderbolts are eternal enemies. Usually a thunderbolt is the more powerful of the two; it clips off the wings of mountains, making them collapse immobile on the earth. But Govardhana is the one mountain who, on behalf of all the other mountains, takes revenge on their eternal enemy, the thunderbolt, defeating its pride by smashing it to pieces. For this reason Govardhana is called Girirāja (the king of mountains). When thunderbolts strike Girirāja, it iss not Girirāja but the thunderbolts who feel pain. Girirāja simply feels ecstacy while carried aloft the hand of his master.

*ekākṣaram.*

**Now for a verse that has only one** ***akṣara:***

### Text 4

*ninunnāno-’nanaṁ nūnaṁ*  
*nānūnonnānano ’nunīḥ*  
*nānenānāṁ ninun nenaṁ*  
*nānaun nānānano nanu*

**Пословный перевод:**

*ninunna—*driven away by whom; *anaḥ—*of the cart demon; *ananam—*the life; *nūnam—*certainly; *na—*not; *anūna—*completely; *unna—*wet with tears; *ānanaḥ—*whose \[Brahmā’s\] face; *anunīḥ—*trying to pacify; *nānā*—various; *inānām—*of the demigods; *ninut—*who is the impeller; *na—*not; *inam—*to the Lord; *na—*not; *anaut—*offered prayers; *nānā-ānanaḥ—*the many-headed Lord Brahmā; *nanu—*O disputant.

**Перевод:**

**The many-headed Lord Brahmā, who impels the various demigods, with his faces almost entirely wet, offered prayers, trying to pacify Lord Kṛṣṇa, the killer of Śakaṭāsura.**

**Комментарий:**

In this verse the keyword is *nanu,* which, according to Śrīla Baladeva means “O disputant.” He describes this verse as someone’s reply to a person who challenges “Why are you offering so much praise to Kṛṣṇa, a small cowherd boy?” Initially Brahmā, much like this disputant, doubted Kṛṣṇa’s position. Seeing Him wandering around Vṛndāvana in search of His calves, with a half-eaten morsel of yogurt rice and pickle in His hand, Brahmā thought, “How can this boy be my master?” He sought to test Kṛṣṇa by stealing all of His calves and friends, but what he then saw astonished and bewildered him. When Brahmā came back to inspect the situation after a moment of his time, he saw that an equal number of calves and friends were playing with Kṛṣṇa just as before. Unbeknown to Brahmā, Kṛṣṇa had expanded Himself into all these duplicatecalves and friends and had been expertly playing their roles for one full earthly year, he equivalent of one moment of Brahmā’s time. Seeing them, Brahmā became confused as to who were the real calves and friends and who were the illusory ones. And just then the calves and friends who were expansions of Kṛṣṇa displayed four-handed Viṣṇu forms full of opulence. Brahmā was wonderstruck. He realized his mistake and fell down at the Lord's feet, offering prayers with tears in his eyes. This is recorded in the *Bhāgavatam* [10.13.62–64](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/13.62):

*dṛṣṭvā tvareṇa nija-dhoraṇato ’vatīrya*  
*pṛthvyāṁ vapuḥ kanaka-daṇḍam ivābhipātya*  
*spṛṣṭvā catur-mukuṭa-koṭibhir aṅghri-yugmaṁ*  
*natvā mud-aśru-sujalair akṛtābhiṣekam*  
*utthāyotthāya kṛṣṇasya*  
*cirasya pādayoḥ patan*  
*āste mahitvaṁ prāg dṛṣṭaṁ*  
*smṛtvā smṛtvā punaḥ punaḥ*  
*śanair athotthāya vimṛjya locane*  
*mukundam udvīkṣya vinamra-kandharaḥ*  
*kṛtāñjaliḥ praśrayavān samāhitaḥ*  
*sa-vepathur gadgadayailatelayā*

**“After seeing this, Lord Brahmā hastily got down from his swan carrier, fell down like a golden rod and touched the lotus feet of Lord Kṛṣṇa with the tips of the four crowns on his heads. Offering his obeisances, he bathed the feet of Kṛṣṇa with the water of his tears of joy. Rising and falling again and again at the lotus feet of Lord Kṛṣṇa for a long time, Lord Brahmā remembered over and over the Lord's greatness he had just seen. Then, rising very gradually and wiping his two eyes, Lord Brahmā looked up at Mukunda. Lord Brahmā, his head bent low, his mind concentrated and his body trembling, very humbly began, with faltering words, to offer praises to Lord Kṛṣṇa.”**

These prayers offered by Brahmā are found in the fourteenth chapter of the tenth canto of *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam.* Thus the main idea of this verse is: “If even Brahmā, the creator of this universe, bows down to Kṛṣṇa and offers praises to Him with tears in his eyes, what can be said of you, O disputant. You should also praise Kṛṣṇa like this.”

**The following verse is arranged in the wheel formation:**

### Text 5

*gandhākṛṣṭa-gurūnmadālini vane hāra-prabhātiplutaṁ*  
*sampuṣṇantam upaskṛtādhvani yamī-vīci-śriyo rañjakaṁ*  
*sadyas tuṅgita-vibhramaṁ su-nibhṛte śītānilaiḥ saukhya-de*  
*devaṁ nāga-bhujaṁ sadā rasa-mayaṁ taṁ naumi kañcin mude*

**Пословный перевод:**

*gandha—*by the fragrance; *ākṛṣṭa—*attracted; *guru—*great; *unmada—*whose intoxication; *alini—*in which bumblebees; *vane—*in the forest; *hāra—*of garlands; *prabhā—*with the splendor; *atiplutam—*who is flooded; *sampuṣṇantam—*maintaining; *upaskṛta—*decorated; *adhvani—*the roads in which; *yamī—*of the Yamunā; *vīci—*of the waves; *śriyaḥ—*of the luster; *rañjakam—*the colorer; *sadyaḥ—*immediately; *tuṅgita—*increased by whom; *vibhramam—*the agitation (or the *alaṅkāra* called *vibhrama*); *su—*very; *nibhṛte—*in the solitary (forest); *śīta—*cool; *anilaiḥ—*by breezes; *saukhya-de—*which gives happiness; *devam—*the Lord; *nāga—*like snakes; *bhujam—*whose arms; *sadā—*always; *rasa-mayam—*full of *rasa; tam—*Him; *naumi—*I praise; *kañcit—*particular; *mude—*for the sake of bliss.

**Перевод:**

**In a secluded forest which attracts swarms of maddened bees with its fragrance, has many decorated paths, and gives pleasure with its cool breezes, I always glorify a certain Lord to attain spiritual bliss. That rasa-laden Lord, the maintainer of His devotees, is submerged in the splendor of many necklaces. His arms are like snakes. He excites sudden agitation in the heart and tinges the luster of the Yamunā’s waves with the effulgence of His nails, lips, yellow cloth, necklaces, bracelets, and crown.**

**Комментарий:**

While the first four verses were transcendental tongue-twisters, the remaining eight are picture verses. In this verse the first and last syllables of the first three lines (*ga* and *taṁ, sa* and *kaṁ, sa* and *de*) are shared syllables, located where the spokes join the rim of the wheel; thus they also function as parts of the fourth line, which is contained in the rim of the wheel. The middle syllable of each of the first three lines, *ni,* is also a shared syllable; as the center of the wheel it is recycled three times.

An extra feature of this verse, shown in the diagram below, is that Rūpa Gosvāmī has ingeniously placed a hidden signature inside the spokes of the wheel. It reads, in a circular fashion, *kṛṣṇa-stutir asau rūpa-viracitā* (“This prayer to Kṛṣṇa was composed by Rūpa”). This signature is not obvious in the verse itself, but only becomes visible when it is drawn in the wheel formation.

*sarpa-bandhaḥ.*

**The following verse is arranged in the snake formation:**

### Text 6

*rāse sāraṅga-saṅghācita-nava-nalina-prāya-vakṣa-stha-dāmā*  
*barhālaṅkāra-hāra-sphurad-amala-mahā-rāga-citro jayāya*  
*gopālo dāsa-vīthī-lalita-hita-rava-sphāra-hāsa sthirātmā*  
*navyo ’jasraṁ kṣaṇopāśrita-vitata-balo vīkṣya raṅgaṁ babhāse*

**Пословный перевод:**

*rāse—*in the *rāsa* dance; *sāraṅga—*of bumblebees; *saṅgha—*with a multitude; *ācita—*which is filled; *nava—*new; *nalina—*lotuses; *prāya—*which is abounding with; *vakṣaḥ—*on the chest; *stha—*which is situated; *dāmā—*whose garland; *barha—*of peacock feathers; *alaṅkāra—*with ornaments; *hāra—*and necklaces; *sphurat—*glistening; *amala—*pure; *mahā—*many; *rāga—*with colors; *citraḥ—*the decoration on whose forehead; *jayāya—*for the sake of glory; *gopālaḥ—*Gopāla; *dāsa—*of devotees; *vīthī—*of the rows; *lalita—*pleasing; *hita—*auspicious; *rava—*in response to the sounds; *sphāra—*broad; *hāsaḥ—*whose smile; *sthira-ātmā—*steady at heart; *navyaḥ—*young; *ajasram—*eternall*ṇa—*in the festival; *upāśrita—*resorted to; *vitata—*suitable; *balaḥ—*strength; *vīkṣya—*seeing; *raṅgam—*the dancing ground; *babhāse—*shone brilliantly.

**Перевод:**

**To increase His glory, Lord Gopāla, who was adorned with peacock feathers, ornaments, necklaces, and glistening, multi-colored, pure decoration on His forehead, shone brilliantly when he saw the rāsa dance arena. His chest was decorated with a fresh lotus garland attracting many bees and He was smiling broadly in response to the auspicious pleasing sounds made by His devotees. Steady at heart and eternally young, He expanded Himself into many forms during the dancing.**

**Комментарий:**

This verse in composed in the meter named *sragdharā,* which literally means “wearing a garland.” It is thus interesting that in the first line Rūpa Gosvāmī describes how Kṛṣṇa’s chest was decorated with a fresh lotus garland attracting many bees. Here also many syllables are shared, as can be seen in the powerpoint presentation. Sanskrit in general is a difficult language, and to write poetically is even more difficult. A poet also has to fit his composition into rhythmic meters, certain preset patterns of short and long syllables, much like morse code. The poet can only choose those words that fit the pattern. And on top of that, when making *citra-kāvya* he has to bear in mind the precise structure of the picture—which syllables have to go where and which have to be shared and how many times. For a verse to come out of this process with coherent meaning is a victory, and to come out of it with such an exceptionally sweet transcendental meaning as seen here is simply amazing. Rūpa Gosvāmī is a pure genius.

*padma-bandhaḥ.*

**The following verse is arranged in the lotus formation:**

### Text 7

*kala-vākya sad-āloka*  
*kalodāra milāvaka*  
*kavalādyādbhutānūka*  
*ka-nūtābhīra-bālaka*

**Пословный перевод:**

*kala—*sweet; *vākya—*whose words; *sat—*by the saintly devotees; *āloka—*who is seen; *kalā—*on account of His artistic skills; *udāra—*who is great; *mila—*please become manifest; *avaka—*O protector; *kavala-adya—*with a morsel of food; *adbhuta—*who is wonderful; *anu-uka—*whom Lord Śiva follows; *ka—*by Lord Brahmā; *nūta—*who is prayed to; *abhīra- bālaka—*O cowherd boy.

**Перевод:**

**O Lord who speaks sweetly, O Lord seen by the saintly devotees, O Supreme artist, O protector, O You who appear wonderful carrying a morsel of food, O Lord followed by Śiva, O Lord prayed to by Brahmā, O cowherd boy, please appear before me.**

**Комментарий:**

In this verse Śrīla Rūpa prays for direct perception of the Lord. Lord Brahmā prayed in a similar way in *Bhāgavatam* [10.14.1](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/14.1),

*naumīḍya te ’bhra-vapuṣe taḍid-ambarāya*  
*guñjāvataṁsa-paripiccha-lasan-mukhāya*  
*vanya-sraje kavala-vetra-viṣāṇa-veṇu-*  
*lakṣma-śriye mṛdu-pade paśupāṅgajāya*

**“My dear Lord, You are the only worshipable Lord, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and therefore I offer my humble obeisances and prayers just to please You. O son of the king of the cowherds, Your transcendental body is dark blue like a new cloud, Your garment is brilliant like lightning, and the beauty of Your face is enhanced by Your guñjā earrings and the peacock feather on Your head. Wearing garlands of various forest flowers and leaves, and equipped with a herding stick, a buffalo horn and a flute, You stand beautifully with a morsel of food in Your hand.”**

A special feature of this verse, as seen in the diagram, is that the syllables extending from the centre of the lotus to the cardinal directions are recycled in reverse order. For example, to the north *ka-la-vā* from the first line become reversed in the fourth line as *vā-la-ka,* to the east *ka-lo-dā* from the second line become reversed in the first line as *dā-lo-ka,* and so on.

*prātilomyānulomya-samam.*

**The following verse reads the same backwards and forwards:**

### Text 8

*tāyi-sāra-dharādhārā-*  
*ti-bhāyāta-madāri-hā*  
*hāri-dāmatayā bhāti*  
*rādhārādha-rasāyitā*

**Пословный перевод:**

*tāyi—*expanded; *sāra—*whose essence; *dhara—*of the hill; *ādhāra—*because of His holding; *ati—*superb; *bhā—*beauty; *ayāta—*not gone away; *mada—*whose pride; *ari—*the enemies; *hā—*who killed; *hāri—*charming; *dāmatayā—*because of being with a garland; *bhāti—*He appears splendid; *rādhā—*by Śrī Rādhā; *ārādha—*from the worship; *rasa—*transcendental taste; *ayitā—*who attained.

**Перевод:**

**The presiding Deity of the rāsa dance with the gopīs appears splendid, adorned with a charming garland. His beauty is enhanced by His holding Govardhana Hill, which is increasing in size. That handsome Deity, the killer of His arrogant enemies, relished a sweet taste from Rādhā’s worship.**

**Комментарий:**

One can read this verse normally or in reverse and still get the same result. For example, the first syllable is *tā* and the last syllable is also *tā,* the second is *yi* and the second last is also *yi,* the third *sā* and the third last also *sā,* and so on. Moreover, the first half of the verse when reversed reads the same as the second half.

Govardhana was increasing in size due to the great ecstasy he felt from Kṛṣṇa’s touch.

*gomūtrikā-bandhaḥ.*

**The following verse is arranged in the zigzagformation:**

### Text 9

*sā malla-raṅge rāmeṣṭā*  
*phulla-sārā mud-edhitā*  
*śrama-nīra-dharā tuṣṭā*  
*ballavī-rāsa-devatā*

**Пословный перевод:**

*sā—*that same (Kṛṣṇa); *malla—*wrestling; *raṅge—*in the arena; *rāmā—*by women; *iṣṭā—*who was desired; *phulla—*fully blossomed; *sārā—*whose strength; *mut-edhitā—*full of happiness; *śrama—*from fatigue; *nīra—*perspiration; *dharā—*who was bearing; *tuṣṭā—*became satisfied; *ballavī—*with the cowherd girls; *rāsa—*of the *rāsa* dance; *devatā—*the Deity.

**Перевод:**

**In the wrestling arena, that Deity of the rāsa dance with the gopīs felt great satisfaction. He was desired by all the women there and His strength blossomed. He was elated and perspired due to fatigue.**

**Комментарий:**

The word *rāmeṣṭā* ("desired by all the women") in thise verse corresponds to the words *strīṇāṁ smaro mūrtimān* ("for the women he was Cupid personified") in *Bhāgavatam* [10.43.17](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/43.17):

*mallānām aśanir nṛṇāṁ nara-varaḥ strīṇāṁ smaro mūrtimān*  
*gopānāṁ sva-jano 'satāṁ kṣiti-bhujāṁ śāstā sva-pitroḥ śiśuḥ*  
*mṛtyur bhoja-pater virāḍ aviduṣāṁ tattvaṁ paraṁ yogināṁ*  
*vṛṣṇīnāṁ para-devateti vidito raṅgaṁ gataḥ sāgrajaḥ*

**“The various groups of people in the arena regarded Kṛṣṇa in different ways when He entered it with His elder brother. The wrestlers saw Kṛṣṇa as a lightning bolt, the men of Mathurā as the best of males, the women as Cupid in person, the cowherd men as their relative, the impious rulers as a chastiser, His parents as their child, the king of the Bhojas as death, the unintelligent as the Supreme Lord's universal form, the** ***yogīs*** **as the Absolute Truth and the Vṛṣṇis as their supreme worshipable Deity.”**

The word *śrama-nīra-dharā* (“perspiring from fatigue”) corresponds to the word *śrama-vāry-uptam* (“wet with perspiration due to fatigue”) in *Bhāgavatam* [10.44.11](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/44.11):

*valgataḥ śatrum abhitaḥ*  
*kṛṣṇasya vadanāmbujam*  
*vīkṣyatāṁ śrama-vāry-uptaṁ*  
*padma-kośam ivāmbubhiḥ*

**“Just see the lotus face of Kṛṣṇa as He darts around His foe! That face, covered with drops of perspiration brought on by the strenuous fight, resembles a lotus covered with dew.”**

*muraja-bandhaḥ.*

**The following verse is arranged in the drum formation:**

### Text 10

*śubhāsāra-sasāra-śrīḥ*  
*prabhā-sāndra-masāra-bhā*  
*bhārasā mahasāvitta*  
*tarasā rasa-sāvitām*

This verse can be either be read in four horizontal lines or it can be read following the straps of the drum. Both methods are shown below:

**Пословный перевод:**

*śubha—*of auspiciousness; *āsāra—*from whom a shower; *sa-sāra—*along with fitness; *śrīḥ—*and opulence; *prabhā—*of splendor; *sāndra—*which is full; *masāra—*like that of a sapphire; *bhā—*whose luster; *bhāra-sā—*the destroyer of the burden; *mahasā—*gloriously; *avitta—*He attained; *tarasā—*quickly; *rasa—*of transcendental taste; *sāvitām—*the state of being the producer.

**Перевод:**

**His bodily luster like that of a splendid sapphire, the Lord of the rāsa dance, quickly produced an abundance of rasa. That Lord destroys the burden of the earth just by his effulgence, and from Him comes opulence that showers forth auspiciousness and is accompanied by fitness.**

**Комментарий:**

*Muraja* is the drum used during the *rāsa* dance.

*sarvato-bhadraḥ.*

**The following verse reads the same backwards and forwards as well as upwards and downwards. (Thus the name** ***sarvato-bhadra,*** **“good on every side.”):**

### Text 11

*rāsāvahā hāva-sārā*  
*sā lalāsa sa-lālasā*  
*balāramā māra-lāva-*  
*hāsa-māda-damāsahā*

**Пословный перевод:**

*rāsa—*of the *rāsa* dance; *āvahā—*the bringer; *hāva—*coquettish gestures; *sārā—*whose strength; *sā—*He (the Deity of the *rāsa* dance); *lalāsa—*sported wonderfully; *sa-lālasā*—with desire; *bala—*by means of His strength; *āramā—*who enjoyed himself; *māra—*Cupid; *lāva—*which cut down; *hāsa—*because of His smiles and laughter; *māda—*and because of His intoxication; *dama—*being subdued; *asahā—*who did not tolerate.

**Перевод:**

**That handsome Lord, the initiator of the rāsa dance, is controlled by the coquettish gestures of women. Filled with amorous desire, He sported wonderfully. Taking pleasure in His own strength, He cannot be subdued by anyone else, because He is intoxicated with the pride of youth and His smiles and laughter defeat Cupid.**

**Комментарий:**

As shown in the diagram above, each line of this verse can be read in eight different ways. This kind of diagram is thus also called the “magic square.” In short, each half line of the verse is repeated in reverse to become the other half line. For example, *rāsāvahā* is reversed to become *hāva-sārā, sā lalāsa* is reversed to become *sa-lālasā,* and so on. So once again Rūpa Gosvāmī has managed under severe constriction to convey a most beautiful meaning.

*bṛhat-padma-bandhaḥ.*

**The following verse is arranged in the big lotus formation:**

### Text 12

*tāra-prasphāra-tālaṁ sa-rabhasa-rasa-laṁ bhāsurāsyaṁ su-bhālaṁ*  
*pāpa-ghnaṁ gopa-pālaṁ karaṇa-hara-kalaṁ nīra-bhṛd-vāra-nīlaṁ*  
*cāru-grīvaṁ rucālaṁ rata-mada-taralaṁ cetasā pīta-celaṁ*  
*śīta-prasphīta-śīlaṁ varaya vara-balaṁ vāsudevaṁ su-bālam*

**Пословный перевод:**

*tāra—*characterized by clarity; *prasphāra—*far-sounding; *tālam—*whose rhythmic clapping; *sa-rabhasa*—joyful; *rasa-lam—*giver of *rasa; bhāsura—*radiant; *āsyam—*whose face; *su-bhālam—*whose forehead is handsome; *pāpa—*of sins; *ghnam—*the destroyer; *gopa—*of the cowherds; *pālam—*the protector; *karaṇa—*the senses; *hara—*which charm; *kalam—*whose sweet sounds; *nīra-bhṛt—*of clouds; *vāra*—like a multitude; *nīlam—*dark; *cāru—*beautiful; *grīvam—*whose neck; *rucā—*with effulgence; *alam—*who decorates; *rata—*caused by conjugal pleasure; *mada—*because of the intoxication; *taralam—*who is trembling; *cetasā—*with your heart; *pīta—*yellow; *celam*—whose garments; *śīta—*cooling; *prasphīta—*great; *śīlam—*whose virtues; *varaya—*please accept; *vara—*great; *balam—*whose strength; *vāsudevam—*the son of Vasudeva; *su-bālam—*the beautiful boy.

**Перевод:**

**With all your heart please accept that beautiful boy, the son of Vasudeva, who is the joyful giver of rasa. His loud rhythmic clapping is characterized by its clarity. His sweet songs steal the mind of the hearer. His face is radiant like the moon. His neck and forehead are extremely charming, and He is trembling due to the intoxication of conjugal bliss. Dark like a host of monsoon clouds and wearing yellow garments, He beautifies those around Him with His effulgence. His cooling virtues remove the suffering of material existence. Possessed of great strength, He is the destroyer of sins and the protector of the cowherd men.**

**Комментарий:**

After describing Kṛṣṇa’s form and pastimes in several verses, Rūpa Gosvāmī finally turns towards the reader and makes a personal appeal*.* He asks each of us to accept Kṛṣṇa with all our heart. He thus encourages us to enter a personal relationship with Kṛṣṇa. The benefits of entering such a relationship are mentioned in this verse: Kṛṣṇa beautifies those around Him with His effulgence, His cooling virtues remove the suffering of material existence, He is the destroyer of sins, and He is the protector of His devotees.

*iti citra-kavitvāni.*

**Thus ends the** ***Picture Verses,*** **or** ***Amazing Verses*****.**
