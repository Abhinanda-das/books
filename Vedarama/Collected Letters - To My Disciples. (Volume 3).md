## Collected Letters "To My Disciples". Volume III

**by Niranjana Swami**

## Introduction

*To my readers:*

*This book has been compiled primarily for devotees looking for answers to important questions they were unable to ask me. It may also provide answers to questions that should have been asked, but, for whatever reason, were not asked. And lastly, it is my hope that this book will provide inspiration for all disciples—even those with no questions to ask.*

*I generally select only those letters in which I believe there are instructions relevant enough for all of my readers. I also avoid publishing letters, which may be too personal. What you have here is probably less than five percent of all the letters I have written in the past 2-3 years.*

*This morning, before beginning work on this introduction, I posted the following automatic response to my email, "Email Avalanche. Expect Substantial Delays. Sorry for the Inconvenience. Niranjana Swami."*

*I spent the past six weeks in India, four of which were spent in Mayapura in GBC meetings, the Panca Tattva installation, and the Mayapura Festival. The other two were spent in Mumbai and Pune. There was not much time for correspondence. Situations such as this happen often.*

*Godbrothers often ask, "Why don't you keep a secretary to help you with your correspondence?" My standard reply is, "I've tried that, and it didn't help. I need to see what I'm writing." There are some who can dictate nicely and thus answer letters quickly. Unfortunately, I've tried this but I'm not satisfied with the result. I am not satisfied that I always fall so far behind in correspondence, either. But the letters I do answer, whenever required, I try my best to provide thoughtful and substantial instructions.*

*It is my hope that what has been extracted from those letters and published within will, to some degree, compensate for the losses some of you \[with unanswered letters\] may be feeling. And for those of you who are still waiting, I pray that your questions will be answered in due course of time.*

*I would like to take this opportunity to express my gratitude to all those who are making these publications in English possible. I would like to thank Lila Smarana devi dasi for taking my instruction to publish my books in English so seriously. Several years ago she asked what service she could do for me while living in the United States. I asked if she would publish my books in English, and I can see that this has been her meditation from the moment I asked. I would also like to thank my godbrother and friend, Ganesa dasa, for his friendship and support, without which these books would likely never get published.*

*I would also like to thank Sri Radha devi dasi and Nanda Kumara dasa for their hard work of transcribing and editing. Although minimal editing goes into this letter's book, so much work has gone in my books, "Lectures from a Disciple." They have both been formidable pillars behind this publication, giving whatever time they were not using for their university studies. I would also like to thank Radha Kunda dasi, the pillar of my book publication in Russian, who provides so much support for English publications as well. And I would like to thank Amala Gopi devi dasi for layout, Yadu-srestha dasa for arranging the printing, and Rsi Kumara dasa, Nitai, and Teja dasa for their kind donations for printing this book. I pray that you all progress nicely towards pure devotional service.*

Niranjana Swami

March 15, 2004

Kiev, Ukraine

## Advancing in Spiritual Life

## January 10, 2001

*Dear Bhaktin ,*

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

I am happy to hear that you are feeling some progress in Krsna consciousness. We should always aspire for this progress. It is also satisfying to hear that my lectures are helping you increase these aspirations as well. I sincerely hope that your progress will continue nicely.

Every living entity who has accepted a material body is carrying many attachments in his heart as a result of his cumulative experiences in the material world. These attachments run very deep, and cannot be removed by his efforts alone. The Lord, as Supersoul, removes these attachments in proportion to the living entity's efforts to remember and serve Him.

By executing the practices of Krsna consciousness, beginning with hearing, chanting, and remembering, these attachments are gradually unsettled from within the deep recesses of our hearts, are stirred up, and gradually rise to the surface. It does this much in the same way as when butter is placed on a fire and continuously heated. It gradually clarifies and the impurities come to the surface.

The pain you are experiencing is coming from the instinctive fear of losing these attachments, and the sweet taste comes from the instinctive faith that you will be happier without them. As you advance in Krsna consciousness, this taste will increase and will take on newer and newer characteristics. The desire to give up these attachments will increase in proportion to your taste for Krsna consciousness. Moreover, as your instinctive faith becomes more mature by association with more advanced devotees, the fear of losing the attachments will gradually diminish. Therefore, Krsna gives us His assurance in *Bhagavad-gita* that we should not fear surrendering to Him (*ma sucah*). The more our faith in His words increases, the more this fear will go away. Moreover, Lord Krsna says (*Bhagavad-gita* 2.14):

*matra-sparsas tu kaunteya*  
*sitosna-sukha-dukhah-dah*  
*agamapayino 'nityas*  
*tams titiksasva bharata*

"O son of Kunti, the nonpermanent appearance of happiness and distress, and their disappearance in due course, are like the appearance and disappearance of winter and summer seasons. They arise from sense perception, O scion of Bharata, and one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed."

Therefore, if you just learn to tolerate this pain and go on hearing and chanting, Krsna will take care of the rest. The pain need not be an obstacle to your progress in devotional service. Rather, to the contrary, the pain can be accepted to help us deepen our attachment to Krsna.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

*Your well-wisher,*

*Niranjana Swami*

## May 22, 2002

*Dear devi dasi,*

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

First of all, I want to assure you that you have my forgiveness. I had already given that assurance to you in , but I want to be sure that you know that you have been forgiven by me.

You write that it is very difficult for you to get back what you have lost. It is true that it is difficult, but it is by no means impossible. Such is the power of the Lord's holy name.

Srila Visvanath Cakravarti Thakur compares the devotee who has lost the mercy of the Lord's holy name to a subordinate of a capable and wealthy master. If the subordinate offends his master, the master will withdraw his care and treat him indifferently. The subordinate will then suffer from neglect and is forced into poverty. After suffering for some time, if the servant returns to the master with the desire to render all kinds of services, the master will gradually bestow mercy, and the subordinate's suffering will diminish. Similarly, a devotee who has committed offenses will first suffer, but if after experiencing that suffering he again renders sincere service to devotees, *sastra*, and *guru*, the holy name will gradually manifest His mercy again, and the sinful tendencies of the offender will be destroyed.

As these sinful tendencies become destroyed, one will gradually become restored to his past position. Therefore, as you say, certainly at every stage a sincere devotee is dependent upon mercy. Nevertheless, having received that mercy, the devotee should sincerely repent for his past offenses so that he will not commit them again. That is always the aim of the sincere devotee. He does not want to commit offenses again. He voluntarily accepts suffering through sincere repentance, and by this suffering he remembers to avoid that which was the cause of that suffering.

Srila Visvanath Cakravarti Thakur also gives the example of someone losing the ability to hear and chant about Krsna as a result of offenses. He compares such a person to a person who suffers from a high fever. When the fever is excessively high, one cannot eat. In the same way, a devotee is unable to hear and chant. After suffering from the high fever, in due course the fever will come down a little. At that time, the person cannot fully digest that which he ordinarily would eat when he is healthy. He must take an invalid's diet and the right medication, which means that he must take only small amounts of that which gives him strength and nourishment. Similarly, when a devotee, suffering from high fever (offenses), experiences his fever come down a little bit, he is ready to take small doses of nourishment (hearing and chanting). He takes that which gives him strength. In other words, he seeks out that carrier of Krsna's *svarupa sakti* and takes it in small, measured doses, until his fever comes completely down and he can take an ordinary meal (fully engage in *bhajana kriya*).

Therefore, it seems that you are ready for your regular intake of an invalid's diet. You have suffered enough and the fever has come down.

My humble recommendation is that you daily listen attentively to that divine wisdom which gives you the best nourishment. By taking it in regular, measured doses, you will become healthy again and able to fully appreciate the great wealth of wisdom and good qualities possessed by so many Vaisnavas. Such is the healthy condition of a Vaisnava. He always appreciates the good qualities of others and is eager to seek out the association of those who possess these good qualities. There are so many Vaisnavas worthy of our respect. You will see them in their full glory when this mercy becomes manifest more in your heart.

I hope this letter meets you in good health.

*As always, I remain your well-wisher, Niranjana Swami*

## February 17, 2002

*Dear Bhakta ,*

In the *Srimad Bhagavatam*, it is stated that anyone who has once relished the lotus feet of Krsna will not be able to do anything but remember that ecstasy again and again. Narada Muni spoke this verse to Srila Vyasadeva in his instructions to compile *Srimad Bhagavatam*. His point was that whenever the Lord is glorified purely, the message will act, even if one falls down after once surrendering himself to that message, because Krsna is all-attractive.

Now, after "suffering in *tamo guna*", as you said in your letter, you have come to realize that a life of devotion is superior. The next step is to get your strength back. Just as a person who has a high fever cannot eat because he has no appetite, similarly one who is too materially engrossed cannot find any taste in hearing and chanting about Krsna. However, once the fever comes down a little bit and there is some appetite, one must follow an invalid's diet and take medicine in order to regain his strength and come back to his previous healthy state when he can eat anything. Similarly, now your "fever" is coming down a little bit. You have suffered so much from that high fever that you are ready to take your medicine and some small amount of nourishing food in order to get some strength back. However, you should not think that you can go and eat a feast right away. In other words, you should not think that you should just throw yourself, full-time, into living in an *asrama*, fully surrendering all that you have acquired since you were 17. Now is the time to start getting your strength back. You must find that which gives you some nourishment. In other words, you must hear Krsna *katha* from devotees whose *katha* helps you to get stronger. Listen to recordings of lectures, read Srila Prabhupada's books daily, begin chanting regularly a fixed number of rounds, and associate with devotees as much as possible. Get yourself regulated in following your "invalid's diet" and medicine. Gradually, as your strength comes back, then you can start thinking about what other prescribed duties you will need to perform to make your life more complete. That will come in time. When that time comes, you will know it with the help of others. Nevertheless, please give it a little time.

Too many times people act impulsively to break free from suffering. However, when the suffering goes away, they fall back to their previous habits again. Do not act impulsively for relief from your suffering. Arm yourself with the proper knowledge so that you will not fall back again. Knowledge and renunciation will come in proportion to the degree that you attach yourself to hearing and chanting about Krsna.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

*Your well-wisher,*

*Niranjana Swami*

## November 24, 2001

*Dear devi dasi,*

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada. Regarding your question, it is not true that you will never be able to approach the Lord with prayer for a particular relation. However, there is a minimum qualification.

*tad-tad-bhavadi madhurye*  
*srute dhir yad apeksate*  
*natra sastram na yuktim ca*  
*tal lobhotpatti-laksanam*

"When an advanced, realized devotee hears about the affairs of the devotees of Vrndavana—in the mellows of *santa*, *dasya*, *sakhya*, *vatsalya*, and *madhurya—*he becomes inclined in that way, and his intelligence becomes attracted. Indeed, he begins to covet that particular type of devotion. When such covetousness is awakened, one's intelligence no longer depends on the instruction of *sastra* \[revealed scripture\] or on logic and argument."

(*Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya* 22.155)

*Lobha*, or intense covetousness, usually does not awaken until the devotee achieves steadiness at the completion of *anarthanivritti*. This stage is called *nistha*. This means that *anarthas* must practically be all gone and that one can perform *kirtan* and *bhajan* without any distraction.

As long as the *anarthas* of lust, anger, greed, envy, faultfinding, and desire for respect remain in the heart, and one commits offenses to the holy name, he lacks the qualification to covet such a relationship with Krsna. However, when the heart is purified through chanting and hearing about the Lord's pastimes in Vrndavana from a qualified devotee, then, symptomized by the stage of steadiness in chanting, one can meditate on such prayers to the Lord for perfectional service.

In the meantime, one may pray to the Lord and spiritual master to clean the heart: *guru-mukha-padma-vakya, cittete koriya aikya*. "My only wish is to have my consciousness purified by the words emanating from his lotus mouth." (Srila Narottama das Thakur) As already stated, without a clean heart one cannot covet a particular service to Lord Krsna.

This is the proper understanding concerning this matter. Hoping this meets you in good health.

*I remain your well-wisher,*

*Niranjana Swami*

In fact, only by hearing these topics in relation to the eternal associates of Lord Caitanya can we become qualified to enter into and understand the pastimes of Radha and Krsna in Vrndavana. Without humility we will never be allowed entrance.

## November 17, 2002

*Dear Bhakta ,*

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

I am sorry to hear about your family relationships. I know that it must be painful to think that you were never able to have a relationship with your father. However, if you can become a good devotee of Krsna, then your father will ultimately benefit by having you as his son.

Regarding your thoughts, I will not go into too much detail in this letter, because I cannot print and post the letter to you, but have to depend upon my secretary to do that for me.

To become fully Krsna conscious, we are dependent upon getting a higher taste. If we wish to give up both gross and subtle sense gratification, we cannot achieve a full state of renunciation until this higher taste is genuinely experienced. In the beginning stages of devotional service, there may not be such a higher taste. One practices *vaidhi bhakti* from the platform of rules and regulations, which are meant to regulate the mind and senses in favorable devotional service. But, as Arjuna states in the *Bhagavadgita*, the mind and senses are very difficult to control, and to control them is more difficult than controlling the wind.

In this stage of *bhakti*, it is understood that there will be many disturbances of the mind. We must restrain from the gross pleasures, while simultaneously cultivating a taste for "higher" pleasures. These higher pleasures come primarily by associating and hearing from those who are experiencing this higher pleasure. There is no enjoyment superior for the soul than pleasing Krsna's senses, and the eternal residents of Goloka Vrndavana know how to please Krsna's senses better than other living entities throughout all of the Lord's creation (both material and spiritual). You must hear about these affairs of the spiritual world, and learn to hanker for achieving this pure state of existence in spontaneous loving devotion to the Lord. When this hankering awakens in your heart, you will gradually look upon everything else in this world with disgust. Just keep on chanting, hearing, and especially praying to Krsna to make you qualified to have this higher taste revealed to you, and all these other thoughts will gradually disappear.

Please continue to associate with dasa and devi dasi in T . Devotee association is most important for progress in spiritual life.

Unfortunately, I do not have any plans to visit T in the near future, primarily because I am trying to reduce my traveling and I am getting strong encouragement to do this-at least for now. Nonetheless, I pray that you and the local devotees in T will become strong representatives of Krsna consciousness, and that you will someday be able to deliver this higher taste to attract and keep the many promising devotees who may come from amongst all these students in T .

Hoping this meets you in good health.

## March 22, 2002

*Dear devi dasi,*

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada. You may have not heard, but on March 15, 2002, Srila Tamal Krsna Goswami, one of Srila Prabhupada's most dear servants, departed from this world in a car accident, traveling from Mayapura to Calcutta. It was a very tragic event, and I have been intensely feeling the loss of this great Vaisnava.

I was personally among those who went to investigate the accident and was among the first to see that he was no longer present in his body. Having been involved in all of these matters has made it difficult for me to deal with correspondence. For this reason, I did not answer your message sooner, and I will also be brief in this reply.

You have asked me to answer your letter, but what is there to answer? There were no questions. You just stated that you are burning in the hell of material existence.

There is one nice verse composed by King Kulasekhara

(*Mukunda-mala-stotra*, 4):

"O Lord Hari, it is not to be saved from the dualities of material existence or the grim tribulations of the Kumbhipaka hell that I pray to Your lotus feet. Nor is my purpose to enjoy the soft-skinned beautiful women who reside in the gardens of heaven. I pray to Your lotus feet only so that I may remember You alone in the core of my heart, birth after birth."

For a devotee, "hell" means forgetfulness of Krsna. However, the devotee does not pray to Krsna to be delivered from hell. A devotee simply prays that the Lord will situate him in a place that will allow him to remember the Lord. Whether it is heaven, hell, or in liberation, the devotee rejects the idea that he can be happy without remembering Krsna. But he does not remember Krsna for his own happiness. He remembers Krsna because his mind cannot give up thoughts of Krsna for even a moment.

Of course, we cannot imitate that platform. However, since it is so satisfying when the mind is thinking of Krsna, even for a moment, a devotee hankers more and more to get back the remembrance.

Thus, you should pray like this-even if your motive is to get free from hellish, material existence. Whatever the motive may be, if we come in contact with Krsna, we will become purified.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

*Your well-wisher,*

*Niranjana Swami*

## December 14, 2001

*Dear devi dasi,*

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

(…) You ask what endeavors in the practice of devotional service I would like to see in you. As you may know, I have been speaking a lot on the process of *sadhana bhakti* these days. Since you mentioned the word "practice", this word obviously implies the process of *sadhana bhakti*.

Srila Rupa Goswami describes the process of *sadhana bhakti* as follows:

*krti-sadhya bhavet sadhya*  
*bhava sa sadhanabhidha*  
*nitya-siddhasya bhavasya*  
*prakatyam hrdi sadhyata*

"When transcendental devotional service, by which love for Krsna is attained, is executed by the senses, it is called *sadhana-bhakti*, or the regulative discharge of devotional service. Such devotion eternally exists within the heart of every living entity. The awakening of this eternal devotion is the potentiality of devotional service in practice."

When we talk of *sadhana bhakti*, we are referring to the practice of one, two, or any number of the nine processes of *bhakti*. It was Lord Caitanya who said: *nava-vidha bhakti purna nama haite haya*. "One can complete the nine processes of devotional service simply by chanting the holy name." (*Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya*, 15.107)

Chanting is the most important process of the nine processes of *bhakti*. Certainly, "preaching" is chanting. Srila Rupa Goswami explains that there is *nama-sankirtan*, *guna-sankirtan*, and *lilasankirtan*. In other words, when we are preaching the glories of the holy name, the glories of Krsna's qualities, and the glories of the Lord's pastimes, that is also chanting. Therefore, in this respect, I would naturally like to see you preaching. However, a preacher must have an internal life as well if he wants to continue to preach. This means we must always be trying to improve the quality of our chanting. We should chant with a serious desire to make progress in devotional service. We should be careful to not allow our chanting to become routine, ritualistic chanting. When we chant, we should chant with a desire to chant purely, without any offense, free from *anarthas*. This requires "practice"; and it must be practiced daily.

We should not think that we can progress in devotional service if we are mechanical. *Bhakti* cannot be generated by a mechanical process. *Bhakti* is eternal, which means that it has no beginning and no end. Therefore, as Rupa Goswami described in the verse above, "Such devotion eternally exists within the heart of every living entity." What will "awaken" that devotion? By coming in contact with *bhakti* in the heart of a devotee, "our" *bhakti* is awakened. What will increase that *bhakti*? Continued association with *bhakti*. Therefore, our *sadhana bhakti* must be executed WITH A DESIRE TO INCREASE OUR *BHAKTI*. If we forget to execute our *sadhana bhakti* in this consciousness, then we must daily "hear" as well.

I was recently reading one commentary by Srila Visvanath Cakravarti Thakur on the following verse (*Padma Purana*): *smartavyah satatam visnur vismartavyo na jatucit / sarve vidhi-nisedhah syur etayor eva kinkarah*. "Krsna is the origin of Lord Visnu. He should always be remembered and never forgotten at any time. All the rules and prohibitions mentioned in the *sastras* should be the servants of these two principles." Srila Visvanath Cakravarti Thakur explains that *vidhis* and *nisedhas* (prescribed and prohibited activities) are performed by those who cannot always remember Krsna. If we always remembered Krsna, twenty-four hours a day, then what need would there be to follow the prescribed and prohibited activities? He therefore concludes that the word *"satatam"*, although generally meaning "always", in this verse means "daily".

Therefore, "every day" we must "practice" hearing, chanting, and remembering Krsna. Whatever other duty we may perform in accordance with the nine processes of *bhakti* is ultimately meant to culminate on this point—we must remember Krsna. On the platform of "practice", or *sadhana bhakti*, we must therefore "daily" practice remembering Krsna.

These are the endeavors in the practice of devotional service that I would like to see on your side. Everything else is meant to assist you in this endeavor. Please remember this. Please do not think that my "mission" does not include seeing you—as well as all of my other disciples—making progress in your development of Krsna consciousness.

Preaching will certainly help. While preaching, you are naturally forced to think of Krsna. You must also be attentive when you are preaching. Therefore, whenever possible, you should preach. Of course, your Deity worship can also be preaching if performed in the right consciousness.

Regarding my blessings for preaching, you have my blessings. Whether you preach and serve in or in some other temple, which will give you more inspiration to go out and preach, is a decision which you will have to make. Where you serve is less of a concern for me as long as you are practicing the principles that I have described above. Wherever you are, you must hear.

Without hearing, you will lack inspiration to serve on the platform of *vaidhi-sadhana-bhakti*. Srila Rupa Goswami explains that on the spontaneous platform, the inspiration comes from one's spontaneous attraction to the service itself. On the platform of *vaidhi-sadhana*, the inspiration comes from the instructions of the spiritual master and from the scriptures. On both platforms of *bhakti*, there must be inspiration. If you are not hearing and not associating with those who are giving you inspiration, then you must seek it out.

I hope this letter satisfies your inquiries, and that it meets you in good health.

*I remain your well-wisher,*

*Niranjana Swami*

Actually, when I first came to Krsna consciousness, I never expected to become a "preacher". I was a somewhat withdrawn person. I was not a "loner", by any means; but I just was not a very outgoing person. I came to Krsna consciousness as a result of reading Srila Prabhupada's *Bhagavadgita*. After reading it, everything inside hit me like a ton of bricks. So when I finally decided to move into the temple (leaving aside all of my friends and, as you can imagine, many other attachments as well), I felt ready to make a commitment. My commitment actually came very early, even before I took *sannyasa* almost 14 years later. So when I moved into the temple, I was engaged in various services, but it was not before long that I was asked to go out and distribute books. At first, distributing books was very difficult for me, especially, if you remember, it was because I was not a very outgoing person. Actually, sometimes I would even cry, because it was so difficult. I even remember a time, when I was out on traveling *sankirtan*, when I would lie awake all night, staring at the ceiling, because I knew that if I fell asleep, the night would go by very quickly, and I would have to wake up and get ready to go back out on book distribution again. So, rather than having the night go by so fast, I would lie awake. I remember doing this many nights. But what made me go on? I was convinced that distributing books was pleasing to Srila Prabhupada. And it was one of those books which brought me to Krsna consciousness. So, if I wanted to please Srila Prabhupada, I should go out and at least try to distribute his books.

Therefore, if you do not mind, I will include the relevant part here in my letter:

If you have a desire to go out and distribute books, then you will make the time to go out with whatever free time you have. It simply depends upon your desire. There are no other obstacles. The mind is looking for an opportunity to avoid having to do something that requires some austerity. It thinks we deserve some rest. Therefore, you will have to fight with the mind.

I oftentimes use my own experience as an example when answering this question. When I was a young *bramachari*, I was asked to cook for the Deities, because there was no other *brahmana* available to cook. I was on traveling *sankirtan* at the time, but the temple president felt that for the number of books I was distributing, it would probably be better if I were back cooking for the Deities. I was not distributing many books at the time. However, at that particular time in my book distribution career, I was convinced that book distribution was something that would be most pleasing to Srila Prabhupada. I therefore really did not want to leave my service of book distribution. So I resisted a little bit, but quickly surrendered.

I had to cook five offerings a day. I would go in the kitchen in the morning from about six o'clock, and I would finish sometime in the late afternoon. The temple president also asked me if I would go out on *harinama* when I finished cooking, but I told him that I was hoping that I could go out on book distribution. So he said, "Well, you know how to play *mrdanga*, so you should go out on *harinama*," and that was after cooking in the kitchen for about 10 hours—maybe less, but not much less.

I nevertheless decided that I was going to go out on book distribution, because I felt that since Srila Prabhupada used to say that to go out and distribute books "is" distributing the holy name, then I was doing what I was told. So I used to keep my book distribution clothes downstairs in the basement, outside the kitchen, so that when I finished cooking, I could change without having to go back upstairs. I wanted to sneak outside the back to go out and distribute books. If I were to go upstairs to change, the temple president might see me and ask where I was going. I would distribute books for about 2-3 hours and did it practically every day except for Sunday. I usually had to cook the Sunday Feast on Sunday.

I also had to go somewhere very close to the temple, because if I took too much time to go farther away, where the book distribution was better, I would waste time and would not have very much time out on book distribution. However, every place close to the temple was very difficult to distribute books. There were many people where I distributed, but American people, in general, are proud, and this particular place where I would go was where there were the most proud people you could imagine. It was called Harvard Square, where Harvard University is located. Harvard is practically one of the top schools in America. Most Harvard University students are very proud. Anyone who is accepted to that school is generally very materially qualified.

I would go there every day and tried to distribute books to the students and professors. Although it was very difficult, it was blissful. I continued doing this for about two or three months.

If you perform some austerities, after some time you start getting some taste. The temple president noticed that I was always blissful, and he was trying to figure out how I was so blissful from being in the kitchen for ten hours. However, I never said anything. I just continued trying to distribute books. Once he finally asked me, "Why are you so blissful?" I told him, "Well Radha Gopivallabha are very beautiful and merciful."

After some time, he found out that I was going out and distributing books. One day, he said, "I found out that you are distributing books every day. It seems to me that you want to distribute books." So I said, "Yes I have a desire to distribute books, but I am also doing the service that you asked me to do. I have been cooking for the Deities." To my surprise, he then said, "Well, I have a different plan right now." He took out the keys to the van, handed them to me, and said, "Go out on traveling *sankirtan*!" He named four devotees that he wanted me to take along, and he told me to fill the van with books, and leave. We were gone practically the very same day.

In those days, we used to pack the vans almost to the top. We also used to sleep in the van. Therefore, if you wanted to sleep in the van at night, you had to distribute books to make room for sleeping. The first day, we used to have to stop somewhere close by and distribute as many books as possible so that we could make room for sleeping that night.

That was the beginning of my career as a traveling *sankirtan* leader. Prabhupada was very merciful. He let me go out and distribute books.

I learned a lot during those years. I learned that the service we were given was based upon our desire. Sometimes we think, "Oh I have to fight for my own way. If I want to go out and distribute books, I should be allowed to go out and distribute fulltime. Prabhupada said, "First deserve, then desire." Therefore, if we really want to go out and distribute books, we have to deserve to go out. Of course, it takes desire also. But there are no obstacles. If we simply try to do something to help preach the *sankirtan* movement—if we make the endeavor-then Krsna will make all the arrangements for us. A devotee becomes very blissful when he sees how Krsna is making arrangements for him.

A devotee feels peaceful when he is sheltered. Actually, a devotee feels more and more sheltered the more he sees how the Lord is acting in his day-to-day life. So many wonderful things are happening, but we are not doing it. We are simply the servant. We should be very, very careful and not think ourselves the doers.

Therefore, under the circumstances, one way in which you can get the determination to go out and distribute books is by hearing. Therefore, I have used this example to help you understand that there are no obstacles if you have the desire. Although the mind will find excuses—because that is the nature of the mind—we simply have to become stronger and defeat all the excuses that the mind will offer with the strength of purified intelligence. If we have a strong desire to do something, which is very pleasing to *guru* and Krsna, then Krsna will help us to overcome the obstacles. However, if we try to overcome the obstacles by our own endeavors alone, we will always fail. We cannot be successful in such endeavors. A devotee always has to be dependent upon the mercy of the Lord. That is one of the symptoms of surrender—to be fully dependent upon Krsna's mercy. \[End of lecture.\]

Thus, by going out and distributing books, I was forced to be more outgoing and to start thinking about others. Gradually, as you read above, I got a wonderful taste from distributing books. It took several years, but when the taste came, it gave me enough of a realization to know that that is where the taste was to be found. That spirit which I imbibed from distributing books carried me forward and brought me to the level of preaching, and I began experiencing that same taste from preaching as I got from distributing books. It gradually became more and more obvious to me that preaching was something that must be very pleasing to Srila Prabhupada. Otherwise, how could I feel so much spiritual satisfaction from it? From that time on, I just kept on preaching and, gradually, taking *sannyasa* came naturally as a result of my desire to preach more and more. Srila Prabhupada gave *sannyasa* to his disciples only for the purpose of preaching. He would oftentimes give the example of how Lord Caitanya accepted *sannyasa* because many of his students were not giving him respect. Without respect, he began to consider how He would be able to fulfill the mission for which He came to the material world. Therefore, at that point, He decided to take *sannyasa*.

You asked for some tricks for keeping our faith and strong conviction. I think you will find the answer to that in the excerpt I have provided above. The key is in "hearing." Actually, this is the most effective way for increasing conviction. One must hear from someone who is convinced. Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakur explains in *Madhurya Kadambini* that faith arises in two ways. One is spontaneously, or *svabhaviki* (as a result of some previous contact with devotional service), or if it is not spontaneously there, the second way it arises is called *baladutpadita*—by the forceful preaching of another.

I was using this quote in a conversation with one former *gurukula* boy who comes to visit me regularly. I could tell that when I used it, he did not like the use of the word "forceful." He even asked what I meant by "forceful" preaching. I explained that forceful preaching did not necessarily mean that it could be compared to binding somebody's arm behind their back and forcing them to do something against their will. Srila Prabhupada would oftentimes say that "purity is the force." This means that even though one may speak very simply, without pressuring another into doing anything against his own will, if his motive is pure, there is a particular "force" in his words, and that force has a way of compelling someone who has actually heard to act. In other words, by coming in contact with purity, one gets faith, and when one hears from someone with realization and taste, he develops a desire to obtain that which this person possesses.

Just like Lord Nityananda, who, when serving Mahaprabhu's order to give Krsna consciousness to everyone He meets, would fall on the ground, with tears in His eyes, and would beg everyone, in ecstatic love for the Lord, to chant Krsna's name, worship Krsna, and make Krsna their life. Just by seeing this ecstatic love, and the power of the Lord's desire, others would immediately begin chanting in great ecstasy. Such is the power of purity. It has great force. It is by this forceful preaching that faith is awakened.

I hope I did not give you too long of an answer; but these are the thoughts that came up from your questions.

I also hope that this meets you in good health.

*Yours in Srila Prabhupada's service,*

## June 7, 2002

You mentioned that you are not into the "institution". Believe it or not (coming from somebody who is a "GBC"), I also have big reservations about institutionalization.

Srila Prabhupada not only emphasized the need for "organized" spreading and maintenance of Krsna consciousness "while" establishing ISKCON, but he never changed his view. There is much that I could say about this point, but I do not think that you will disagree with me on this.

Srila Prabhupada's vision was vast. He was not concerned about only a small number of *jivas*. His mood was similar to that of Prahlada Maharaja and Vasudeva Datta. He felt compassion for every living entity and felt pain when any living entity slipped through the loving net which he was constantly throwing out to capture "the most fallen." Prabhupada inherited this vision from Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakur, and what is so obvious to us, his devoted followers, but not obvious to some others, is that he fulfilled Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's vision like no other.

Anyway, I am mentioning this point because you asked me to share with you "encouraging knowledge and things which Srila Prabhupada had said."

We start thinking of persons as designations—how they are connected to the institution. The reason why I like it so much is because the leaders have to sacrifice a week each month "away" from their "organizing" mentalities and just be devotees without managing. It may seem that the organizing will suffer, however, in the long run, not only does it not suffer, but it becomes much more real. Therefore, I am going to send this off to you right now and move on downstairs.

## Offering to H.H. Tamal Krsna Goswami:

*Dear Tamal Krsna Maharaja,*

Please accept my humble obeisances. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

I have never written a *Vyasa Puja* offering to you before, and I am asking myself, "Is the only reason why I am writing this offering to you today, because you recently departed from our association?" Externally, it may seem that way, both to myself and to those who will hear this offering. But after looking deeper within my heart I've realized that that is not the only reason why I am writing. I am writing because I have come to realize just how dear to me you were when you were still physically present. Otherwise why am I feeling so much pain now that you have departed? Had you not been so dear, I would not be suffering as much as I am today by knowing that I can no longer have your association; at least not while I am still in this body. So it is not only just because you have departed that I am writing, but it's also because you are still here in my memory. Physically you may have departed, but you have implanted an indelible memory of your association which will always stay with me, and I would like to thank you for that.

I must also confess to you now. There is another reason why I am suffering. You were so kind to me during those wonderful days when we were together in Mayapura this year; having *kirtans* every evening in your room. You were pouring your kindness upon me for a reason which I could not at all ascertain. I was espe cially moved by how often you would express your gratitude to me for coming and chanting in your room. You repeatedly said that you had become a great debtor to me and you did not know if you would ever be able to find the means to pay back that debt. Although I countered your claims, you would insist that there were no words which you could use to express your gratitude. In retrospect, I am the one who is the debtor, because after a long lapse from your association, you had kindly given me an opportunity to render some service that was pleasing to you.

Nonetheless, I am suffering because on the last night before your departure, you came into the temple during the *kirtans* and apologized that you would not be able to stay for the *kirtans* that evening, and one by one you approached your godbrothers to thank them and say good-bye in case you would not see us again before leaving early the following morning. I clearly remember how you approached some and embraced them, and when you came to me you partially extended your arms as if you wanted to embrace, but at the same time your look was as one who did not want to impose. The fool that I am, I did not reciprocate and continued to sit with folded hands just thanking you for your wonderful association and your kindness. I did not accept your loving embrace, something I now realize that you so much wanted to offer to this ungrateful wretch. And I am suffering—so much suffering that I often cry, thinking that I no longer have the opportunity to accept this embrace from you. Please forgive me for not adequately accepting your kindness, but at least I've come to learn why you were being so kind to me.

This realization came to me last week while I was listening to your "Matchless Memories" of Srila Prabhupada. Each day I would listen for about 30 minutes, and I was relishing so much the memories you were sharing of your association with Srila Prabhupada. In the third tape, you began describing your memories of Srila Prabhupada's last days with his disciples, and with great detail you began to describe how during that time Srila Prabhupada made himself so accessible to everyone, more accessible than he had ever made himself before. You described it as some kind of unfathomable kindness to deepen the disciples' attachment to their spiritual master to give them the strength to go on after his departure. After some thought, I realized that this is what you were doing with me in Mayapura this year. Although your kindness upon me was there, for some unknown reason, Krsna inspired you to pour so much kindness upon me to further deepen my attachment both to you, and to Srila Prabhupada for giving us the association of wonderful godbrothers like you. So although you were claiming yourself as my debtor, it is perfectly clear to me now. You have made me…

*Your eternal debtor,*

*Niranjana Swami*
