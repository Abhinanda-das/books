# Collected Letters - To My Disciples. (Volume 5)

## Collected Letters. To My Disciples. Vol. V

## Introduction

This book is a collection of select letters written by His Holiness Srila Niranjana Swami from 2007 to 2011, as well as Vyasa-puja offerings to Srila Prabhupada from 1974 to 2010.

Unfortunately, due to health reasons, Maharaja has had to reduce his travels in the past few years. However, although devotees may thus have fewer opportunities to have his personal association through his physical presence *(vapu)* and to ask questions in person, we can see that he has taken great effort to infuse such profound knowledge and instructions *(vani)* in these letters and Vyasa-puja offerings.

Maharaja has personally selected each of the letters for this book, hoping that you will find something of value-either answers to your questions or inspiration for your devotional service. You will certainly also derive inspiration from the Vyasa-puja offerings contained in this book, which clearly reveal the deep love and dedication that he has towards Srila Prabhupada and the *sankirtana* mission. We pray that you will drink the nectar of the words contained in this book.

This book was published due to the combined efforts of a number of devotees. We thank Lila Smarana devi dasi for overseeing all the work involved in this publication. We thank Sri Radha devi dasi for editing, Bhaktin Dhvani for proofreading, Godruma-vihari dasa for the layout and the cover design and Kamala-locana dasa for arranging the printing.

We would like to express our gratitude to the devotees who are always eager to facilitate the distribution of Gauranga Press publications. These devotees include: Kisori Rani devi dasi, Radha Gopinatha dasa (Toronto), Amalaratna devi dasi, Sri Radha devi dasi, Rohininandana dasa, Nrsimhaloka devi dasi, Rangavati devi dasi, Gandharvika Giridhari devi dasi, Yajna Purusa dasa, Yad- hunatha dasa, Jagadisa dasa, Arcana Viddhi devi dasi, Krtamala devi dasi and Dina Gauranga dasa.

Even though you described internal struggles, you gave evidence that you are faithful and hopeful with the process of Krsna consciousness. That is important to me, and I’m grateful to you for your efforts. We must never lose hope, even in the face of adversity.

Also thank you for sending the illustrated letter of your program. I read through it and carefully took note of all the nice photos you included of your preaching program. I always like to receive reports like this, so please feel free to continue to send them.

By now, you must have read in several places that I still want to receive letters from devotees.

The following is what I wrote to K... devi dasi:

\*\*\*

“Yes. I do wish to continue to receive reports, but there is a difference between a report and correspondence. A report does not require a reply. Those types of reports are preferred, at least at this point in time.

The reports need not include only happy news. Life in the material world is not always happy. There will always be struggles and difficulties and I don’t expect the counselors to just hide their counselees’ difficulties from me, or their own difficulties, for that matter. But it’s best to include how the counselors are dealing with these difficulties—either their own, their Counselees’, or both—from their own realizations acquired from previous instructions (either given personally, in letters, books or lectures), or from other senior Vaisnavas’ instructions. It’s best not to expect answers, at least at this time, to their problems in the form of a response to a report. It won’t remain this way forever. But I’m convinced that I do need to change the way I deal with correspondence. In fact, just a few days ago I sent, by postal mail, 20 letters to Ukraine—primarily to counselors. But the letters were written by my own choice, to encourage some of the counselors in Ukraine. I intend to post more of the same in the future to other counselors.”

\[...\] Please go on serving with faith that Krsna will recognize you for your devotional service as He does for all of His pure devotees. Srila Prabhupada has given us the perfect process for achieving this result. Chant and hear the glories of Krsna’s name, qualities, form and pastimes in the association of His devotees—and have no doubt that by doing so, you will be recognized.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

## letter 2. New Vraja Dhama, Hungary. October 8, 2007

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

\[...\] I hope you still accept me as your well-wisher, as I most certainly remain in that mood. The relationship you’ve asked for with me is none other than one based upon faith—the most essential ingredient for progressing in spiritual life. I have oftentimes stated that faith cannot be legislated, even by scriptural injunctions. It can only arise by hearing, seeing, and associating with those who have faith in pure devotional service. In recent lectures I have been emphasizing this point a lot.

In the marketplace of the holy name, the most valuable commodity is faith; not material wealth, which is most valuable to mundane investors. But the analogy works in the same way. An investor who wishes to invest his wealth looks for a solid investment which will hopefully give him a good return. In the same way, one who has some faith in pure devotional service and the holy names, but also wants to increase it, has to be willing to invest his/her faith in that person who invokes faith in pure devotional service and the pure Holy Names. The more faith invested, if it’s a genuinely solid investment, the more we’ll get in return.

Please, if you have the time, read the transcription of a lecture I gave in Ventura, California late last year on this subject. I think it will help to elaborate on the point which I am making.

Meanwhile, I am sorry to hear about all the physical pain you have been undergoing. But I sincerely hope and pray that to some degree, that pain has been mitigated by your efforts to remain focused on Krsna consciousness.

I look forward to seeing you and G dasa whenever I visit… next. As it looks now, I do not have any plans for that in the immediate future, but then again,… is one place I cannot stay away from for too long.

Hoping this meets you in improved health.

## letter 3. October 18, 2008

Please accept my blessings.

I’m sorry that I have not written to you for all of this time. Of course, your letter did not solicit a reply, but I have been disinclined to just leave it without replying.

Your letter came while I was on an intense summer tour in the CIS. I was answering very little email and left an auto-reply stating so. I returned to the USA two weeks ago, but I was scheduled for knee surgery within just a few days after I got back. I’ve been recovering from that surgery and came to Hartford last weekend to continue recovery.

\[...\] I must be honest too, and will also try to express myself in a loving way. I do not wish to push guilt on you and I beg you that if my letter makes you feel that way that you will forgive me. But still I have to be honest.

I am genuinely pained when I observe devotees losing faith in Krsna consciousness—especially devotees whom I have personally taken the responsibility to inspire in their devotional practices. I feel responsible, that I have served inadequately as a loving, spiritual force in their lives. You are certainly not the first disciple to tell me that he/she cannot continue practicing *sadhana-bhakti.* I have heard it before and suffered each time I hear it from a disciple. However, I firmly believe that Krsna wants me to feel that way, because I should not take the responsibility I have towards my disciples so lightly. You may try to forget the connection, but I can’t. I also accepted this responsibility towards you before the Deities, the Vaisnavas and the sacrificial fire.

So please forgive me when I say that I can only temporarily accept the content of your letter. I need to maintain the hope that I can live up to the responsibility I’ve taken towards you. I will not try to force anything upon you in a direct way. But I will continue to hope and to pray that Krsna in your heart intervenes and will remind you, when the time is right, that you turned away from the greatest possible treasure in life—the best opportunity to approach Krsna, the most loving personality, through chanting His names

I say this because it is clear to me that you want to experience love. We all want to experience real love. Krsna is that reservoir of loving relationships. But He will also allow us to look for that love elsewhere as a demonstration of His love. As the Lord in the heart, He will do this, knowing that love must be spontaneous and must be based upon our own minute independence. The fact that we cannot be fully satisfied without loving Him is also His kindness. Having already come in contact with Him, our experiences will bring us to that realization because Krsna will not forget that we called out His name with some faith. We may forget, but He will not forget. I pray that someday, you will realize this and that you will accept the service of V , the Personality you have been named as the servant of.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

## letter 4. July 6, 2009

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

It is painful to read about all the difficulties you have been confronting these past few years. I know that you did not want to bring me pain, but it is natural to feel this way when hearing about others’ sufferings.

There are so many standard, Krsna conscious answers for those who are suffering. But usually relief will come, not from the exact answers themselves, but from the motive behind the answer. Everyone wants to feel cared for. It is the innate desire of the soul.

“Real care” means to give someone the best one can offer. I wish I could just give you full Krsna consciousness in a moment, and connect you to your loving service in Goloka-dhama. But I can’t be so idealistic. At best, I can only offer you the assurance that I do not want you to suffer. I want you to be happy by serving with the faith that someday, by the mercy of guru and Krsna, this full Krsna consciousness will come.

Without devotional service, our lives are uselessly spent in the vain search for temporary happiness and relief from suffering. You need the opportunity to connect with this devotional service.

Whether that’s in B , S , or anywhere else in the world is not an important point of concern to me. But wherever you are, you must have the association of devotees who will remind you that it’s this devotional service which is the goal we must continue to strive for. Otherwise, we will be tricked again and again by Maya’s proposals. But any proposal which serves as a support for devotional service can be accepted.

\[...\] I pray that this letter meets you in good health and with strength to continue in devotional service.

## letter 5. New Vraja Dhama, Hungary. July 6, 2009

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

Thank you for your letter and for the sincere sentiments described within. I pray that Krsna will hear the sincere yearning in your heart for stability in His service.

I have noted your description of the conflicting desires in your heart. There’s no need for me to repeat them here. Conflicting desires will always be there until the heart is completely pure.

What to do with them in the meantime? In Krsna consciousness, we do not abandon desires. We purify them by engaging our senses, mind and heart with the one desire we can always depend on for purification—the desire to please Krsna, *guru* and Vaisnavas whose hearts are purely motivated in the same way. The desire to please someone is in everyone. My realization is that we cannot please anyone, including ourselves, without first pleasing them. Any attempt to do so will only lead us into a network of further conflicting desires.

Look deep within and try to fix yourself on fulfilling just one of Krsna’s desires. Everything else, including the fulfillment of all other desires, will fall into place when you fix your mind on doing something that’s pleasing to Krsna. Of course, this requires faith and surrender. I pray that Krsna will help you with both.

From your letter, I sense that you would be happier and better situated if you stayed in... , even if you have to live outside the temple. I can’t stop thinking that it would be better for you to stay near the association of devotees who know you, who speak the same language as you, and who have grown up with you in Krsna consciousness. Their association gives you support and your association gives them support. Once you fixed your mind on this, service would come and everything else you need to remain engaged in devotional service would follow. I realize that this would not be easy for you. But I decided to share these thoughts with you.

To obtain anything of real value, there must always be endeavor. But if we have faith, we can do anything Krsna wants us to do. Krsna is pleased more by our efforts to please Him than by the results. The results we leave up to Him.

I hope this letter meets you in good health.

## letter 7. New Vraja Dhama, Hungary. October 8, 2007

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

Many months have passed since your letter was written. I’m not even sure if there’s any need to address the points you’ve raised about your work and your need to assist R devi dasi in her daily affairs. Time has a way of changing these situations—sometimes for the better, and sometimes for the worse. Either way, I am hoping that the situation has improved and your life has stabilized so that you can remain fixed in daily remembering and serving the Lord.

Srila Prabhupada used to often quote the following verse spoken by Lord Caitanya.

*’dvaite’ bhadrabhadra-jnana, saba—’manodharma’*  
*’ei bhala, ei manda,’—ei saba ’bhrama’*

“In the material world, conceptions of good and bad are all mental speculations. Therefore, saying, ‘This is good’ and ‘This is bad’ is all a mistake.” (Cc. Antya 4.176).

Lord Caitanya spoke this verse to Sanatana Gosvami when Sanatana Gosvami pleaded with the Lord to not touch his contaminated body which was oozing with sores. Lord Caitanya told Sanatana Gosvami that his body was completely spiritual and therefore He took great pleasure in embracing him. And then He said that even if it was material, He could not neglect it because the material body should not be considered either good or bad. He then quoted the above verse along with other verses to establish His point, continuing with His loving embrace.

Anyway, whether your situation is better or worse, you are still a very valuable asset in Lord Caitanya’s *sankirtana* movement— even if you can only chant. That faith in chanting and the desire to participate in the Lord’s *lila* will qualify you for pure devotional service.

Just two days ago I received a letter from P from... You may or may not know, but he is presently staying in the temple for some time (with his family’s permission). He told me that his purpose for living in the temple was to become more fixed in devotional service so that once he returns to his family responsibilities, he will not lose sight of the goal. He specifically mentioned your name (with the words, “especially A dasa”) for helping him to come to Krsna consciousness nine years ago. You still have that potency, as a kind, caring person, delighted to see others becoming attracted to devotional service—and you should look for as many opportunities as possible to use it for serving the Lord.

Within the last several weeks I have been working with Ganga- mbupada Prabhu to set up a “Fileshare” site containing lectures, writings, photos, videos, etc. for downloading. The address is nrs.iskconboston.org, if you should wish to download some of my most recent lectures here in Hungary (or other material). You have to register first, but the registration process is completed within a short time.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

## letter 8. Kiev, Ukraine. May 17, 2008

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

Thank you for your report which I received shortly after my arrival here in Kiev. I was happy that you had written to me, but naturally sorry to read that you have been undergoing such difficulties in your spiritual life.

I am not indifferent towards your present situation. But the only way I see that I can help you is to help strengthen your faith in Krsna. Krsna is the Supreme Mystic. He can do anything He wishes. If we want to draw His attention, then we must be prepared to remain tolerant, humble, confident in His protection, and patient.

In your situation, you must be prepared to remain tolerant of the situation He has put you into. You are living with meat eaters, but Prahlada was living with a powerful demon who was always trying to kill him. Prahlada remembered the Lord from within and prayed for His constant service. The Lord could not be indifferent. Prahlada captured the Lord’s attention by his constant remembrance. Your family cannot stop you from remembering the Lord. That is something they can never take away from you. In the face of adversity, any situation beyond our control, we have an impetus to remember Krsna: “Krsna, only You can protect me from the influence of Your powerful illusory energy. Every moment without the association of Your devotees is like hell. But for some reason You have put me in this situation. Let me see this misfortune as Your will and thus as an opportunity to think of You and to pray to You for protection. Hare Krsna!”

Whether you have an altar or not, or whether your hand is always in your bead bag or not—these are not the ultimate criteria for remaining Krsna conscious. Understandably so, you cannot do these things when you are with your family. But when you are with devotees, you can chant and worship the Deities. You are fortunate that you can still go to to worship the Lord and to chant His names. The devotees who were in prison (as written about in the book “Salted Bread”) did not have such fortune. Your situation is not intolerable, although it is certainly a test.

I pray that Krsna will give you the strength to go on tolerating these inconveniences until He chooses to remove these obstacles from your path.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

## letter 10. New Vraja Dhama, Hungary.

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

First of all I have to apologize for having not replied to your letter for so long, especially given its contents and the advice you asked me for in your difficult circumstances. I believe that you are aware of the reasons for my being unable to answer your letter earlier and that you do not take this delay to mean that I have taken your appeal lightly.

As I understood from your letter, it has been a very difficult period for you lately, with your relatives leaving or abandoning you in one way or another. I hope that by now, after over a year since you wrote to me, the situation or you perception of it has changed for the better, and that you feel securely sheltered in your service to the Deities and Srila Prabhupada, and in the association of devotees.

Of course, nothing can guarantee that similar reversals will not happen again in our lives in some way or another as long as we are still in the material world. As Srila Prabhupada put it, “...\[A\] devotee is always pessimistic about the material world, but he is very optimistic about the spiritual life”. Of course, this does not mean that we have to feel depressed or that we should always expect the worst. This means, however, that we should not believe that we can find the kind of happiness that will satisfy the soul completely anywhere in the material world and its concomitant relations. As *Srimad-Bhagavatam* (1.5.18) says, one should aspire only for the kind of happiness that cannot be found anywhere in this material world, while expecting material happiness and misery to come on their own accord.

Great devotees like Kuntidevi sometimes even pray for difficulties and sufferings so that they can be reminded of Krsna’s exclusive position in their lives as their best friend, unfailing protector, and eternal object of love and service. We cannot and should not imitate Kuntidevi in her ecstatic prayers, but we can certainly remember and be reminded by such difficult situations as yours that it is He who is our closest relative and most trustworthy friend, who will never leave us or let us down.

Sometimes Lord Krsna, on His own admission, takes away our various abilities, possessions or relations that might prevent us from surrendering to Him completely. He says He does it out of His special mercy. And whatever may be the cause in your particular case, by continuing to serve Lord Krsna in the mood of helpless dependence on Him, you may one day become such a great source of inspiration for others that even your relatives may become influenced by your sense of security under the shelter of Krsna’s lotus feet, and will want to join you there. From the way you concluded your letter, I can tell that this is how you are taking your difficulties, and I am very grateful to you for this mature and devotional attitude.

Please feel free to write to me with more realizations, although I have to say that I cannot guarantee a very quick reply.

## letter 11. February 17, 2010

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

Thank you for your reply. I’ll answer within the content of your letter.

\*\*\*

“Dear Guru Maharaja,

Please accept my humble obeisances. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

First of all, let me sincerely apologize for not sending this email for so long. But believe me, I have been thinking about sending it almost every day. Sometimes I am not sure how to write what I want to write. I also feel you will feel disappointed and there is, to some degree, the fear of rejection.

There is no doubt in my heart that I get the greatest joy in being in a *kirtana* or chanting for Krsna, and without fail I experience that every time I go to the temple, especially in the association of devotees, especially devotees. However, in the past few months (or maybe over a year now), I have been disconnected with my own practice of Krsna consciousness. I have struggled to chant my rounds (in fact many times even lost interest). But, whenever I go to the temple, I experience a different kind of joy which is not comparable to anything else. Over the last year, I also have been dealing with a lot of other issues (personal, family related issues), so it has been a little stressful to keep up my spiritual practice.

I am slowly cultivating ways where I can gradually come back and associate with devotees in various ways. I don’t feel very connected going to the temple, so sometimes I go to the temple for their Sunday morning program. However, they currently don’t have a *kirtana* after their program, so that makes me feel a little incomplete. I chant sometimes at home by myself.

I also see this is all a test for faith and maybe two or three years back, I would have said that I can overcome this struggle, but now sometimes it seems difficult. I still carry my beads, chant when I can, and try visiting whenever possible (because that reminds me how I started on this journey and it is very inspiring). Some of the temple devotees also chant in local yoga centers here in... , so I try and go for those programs as well.

G Prabhu has written to me a few times and I have opened up a little and talked to him, but I again go in my fears of being rejected and then hold my communication.”

I can understand why you have concerns about being rejected. In mundane society, to reject an associate or friend for their failure to live up to their own expectations is very common among co-workers, friends, politicians, partners, and even family members. Accepting and rejecting is the business of a mind driven by personal ambition, and that means that accepting and rejecting relationships is no exception for one who possesses such a mentality.

But generally, devotees, even those who are not yet mature in their devotion, are more merciful and kind than mundaners.

Of course, it’s true that materialists can appear a lot kinder than devotees. As long as your association with them will benefit them by helping them to move forward in their personal or collective goals, they will generally be nice to you and also accommodating. Krsna describes this mentality in His conversation with the *gopis:*

“So-called friends who show affection for each other only to benefit themselves are actually selfish. They have no true friendship, nor are they following the true principles of religion. Indeed, if they did not expect benefit for themselves, they would not reciprocate.” (B*hag.* 10.32.17)

In the commentary before that verse, Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura rephrases part of the *gopis’* question to Krsna to which He gave the above answer:

“There are some people who only respect those who respect them. Their respect is conditional. They give respect expecting something, and if they do not get that, then they no longer give respect. This is conditional love.”

In other words (or to put it more bluntly), if someone finds out that you’re no longer interested in feeding their egos or their wallets, then they generally will take measures to reject you.

Of course, there are philanthropically inclined people who are more pious. But they also have their limits within the realm of their philanthropically motivated goals.

But devotees have no personal investment in their dealings— at least serious devotees. They get their fulfillment from a place beyond their day-to-day social dealings. Giving without expectation of return is the platform from which serious devotees operate. And even if you don’t meet their expectations, they will not reject you. They know the power of the illusory energy. And they know how a spiritually weak, but non-offensive devotee can fall victim to her powerful influence. Please remember this and be assured that due to weakness, which is also influenced by mundane association, you will not be rejected by devotees for not living up to their expectations of you. As long as there is the slightest spark, or desire, for Krsna consciousness—and in your case there is a lot more than a spark— devotees will always be there to encourage you, though they generally will not try to force you.
