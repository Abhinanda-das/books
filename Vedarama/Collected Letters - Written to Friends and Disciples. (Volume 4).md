## Collected Letters “Written to Friends and Disciples”. (Vol. IV)

## Introduction

June 28, 2007 Carpinteria, California

At a time when I am not writing many letters, this new publication is timely. I hope and pray that those who have been eager to receive correspondence from me will find something of value in and solace from reading this book.

To tell each and every one of my readers, one by one, how grateful I am to them for their endeavors in devotional service is beyond my limited capabilities. I would therefore like to take this opportunity to thank each and every one of you for your efforts to please Hari, guru, and Vaisnava. I am praying to Srila Prabhupada, Sri Sri Gaura-Nitai, Sri Sri Gandharvika-Giridhari, and Sri Sri Radhika-Ramana (the presiding Deities of Carpinteria) that They bestow Their causeless mercy upon you.

For those of you who are grateful to have this book, let us thank Lila Smarana devi dasi for overseeing all the work to completion, Sri Radha devi dasi for editing and proofreading, Anubhava devi dasi for the cover design, Bhakta Michael Golub for layout, and Arcana Vidhi devi dasi and Amala Gopi devi dasi for overseeing the printing. We should especially thank Teja dasa for his generous donation to cover the cost of printing.

We should also thank the following devotees for their efforts to distribute and make these books available to my disciples: Arcana Viddhi devi dasi, Amala Gopi devi dasi, Amalaratna devi dasi, Sri Radha devi dasi, Rangavati devi dasi, Rohininandana dasa, Daivi Sakti devi dasi, Hrsikesa dasa and Sukhesvari devi dasi, Yajna Purusa dasa, Anandini devi dasi, Radha Gopinath das (Toronto), Yuga Avatara das and Devasreshtha das.

## Letter #1 (NS—&gt;) July 20, 2004

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

Thank you for your letter, which I received on July 6th.

Moving from *brahmacarya* to the *grhastha asrama* will not be an easy adjustment. However, as I personally tell most *brahmacaris,* if there are any thoughts about becoming a *grhastha,* then it is usually an indication that one should become a *grhastha.* Still, *brahmacari* training is always beneficial for everyone, so you should have gotten much benefit from your *brahmacari* experience. Now apply what you’ve learned as a *brahmacari,* especially about the importance of *sadhana,* and mold your household life in such a way that your *sadhana* does not suffer. Every day there must be some chanting and hearing. This is most important for householders.

If you are marrying somebody who already has a child, then you will see that there will be so much pressure to make money. You will also likely find that you will have to be more engaged and work harder than you have experienced for a long time. But if you do not forget what you’ve learned, and keep your relationships with devotees on the spiritual platform, without getting overly involved with them about monetary concerns, then you will be able to survive spiritually as a householder. If you can remember this, then you have my blessings to become a householder.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

## Letter #2 (—&gt;NS) June 16, 2004

All glories to Srila Prabhupada!

I decided to write this letter because I felt the necessity to do so. Please forgive me for taking your precious time away from your devotional service.

This year I went to the holy *dhama* for the first time. I realize that I am not yet a devotee and that I was able to go there only by your mercy and only because I am an initiated disciple of yours. Otherwise, Krishna wouldn’t let me go near to a holy place. I am very grateful to you for this mercy and also for allowing me to chant *japa* with you in the mornings and to listen to your lectures in the evenings. Thinking about this, I remembered a statement from the holy scriptures that says that the biggest fortune of a conditioned spirit soul who wanders in this material world is to meet a pure devotee—a spiritual master—because at that moment, he will get the chance for liberation from the whirlpool of Maya. I am praying for the intelligence to realize this and surrender completely. I have a very big desire to do something significant for you so that I can become worthy of being your disciple.

In this letter I also want to ask for your help and advice. I have a desire to preach to leaders of organizations and preach in colleges. I did it before from 1995 to 2000. I distributed books in offices and colleges and sometimes I had to do presentations or give lectures. I liked it very much. From 2000, I was doing some service in the temple, and in 2002 I started doing service in A and E communities as a leader-servant. I understand that this service— serving Vaisnavas, providing spiritual care and *bhaktivrksa*—is very important, and I like it too. But in these communities, there are not very many people and there is no outside preaching. Therefore, I would like to take this responsibility of preaching and attracting people to our movement. This big responsibility is inspiring to me. Of course, I am going to do outside preaching while I am still taking care of devotees and their education. I think that for the successful development of a community, there has to be a spirit of preaching, which will come from preaching and book distribution. Therefore I would like to start book distribution, preaching to the leaders and, at the beginning of the school year, preaching in colleges.

In this connection, I would like to ask your blessings and your instructions. I also wanted to ask you for help. Nowadays in Russia, to get an opportunity to meet the leaders requires one to have some kind of documents. Is it possible for me to get a document saying that I am, for example, a teacher from the Vaisnava University or the Kiev Spiritual Vaisnava Academy? I am studying *bhakti-sastri,* starting with: *Bhagavad-gita* and *Nectar of Devotion* (I listen to them on CD). In the next half year, I will study *Sri Isopanisad* and *Nectar of Instruction. I* also study other books and listen to many lectures. I have a very strong desire to preach and I want to ask you for your mercy and blessings and to help with the documents. I feel very responsible to help you to distribute Krishna consciousness. Nowadays, so many people have a positive opinion of our movement. So many of them are trying to understand it, and therefore, we have to help them by giving them knowledge and saving them from horrible material life. Unfortunately I am not qualified, but I want to try because I have faith that by your mercy even such ignorant persons like me will be able to say something. You are a living example of devotion to Srila Prabhupada’s mission, and you are my biggest hope. I am very grateful for your compassion unto me. Please forgive me for my pride and insignificance.

## Letter #3 (NS—&gt;) July 20, 2004

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

Thank you for your letter, received on July 21, 2004. I have carefully read it and noted the contents.

You have my blessings to engage in preaching to local leaders and in the colleges. Just recently, I was telling a group of devotees in Moscow about some of my experiences as Temple President in Boston from 1979 to 1983. As Temple President, I had to devote a lot of time to dealing with the devotees’ needs. There was very little time to do anything else. But gradually I began to realize that, although preaching to the devotees and facilitating their service was important, if I was not directly involved in preaching to nondevotees, then I was not able to maintain a clear vision of how to direct the devotees’ lives. Of course, *sadhana* is important, and this must always be emphasized to the devotees. But, Srila Prabhupada also wanted us to distribute Krishna consciousness to those who were less fortunate.

So gradually, after struggling for some time with temple management, I decided to start increasing my preaching. I visited colleges and lectured to students, and gradually Krishna sent me to give more and more lectures. Sometimes I was giving four to five lectures a week in colleges, in addition to my Temple President responsibilities. This preaching became a source of great inspiration for me, and it enabled me to keep the proper vision so that I would not get pulled down into too much management.

You are a *brahmacari.* You should have nothing else to do in your life other than preach, study *sastra,* and give Krishna consciousness to others. I see no reason why you should not utilize more of your time for expanding the preaching and then engaging those devotees whom you are taking care of in assisting you to expand the preaching.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

## Letter #6 (NS’s reply to #5) July 21, 2004

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

Although your letter was dated November 14, 2003, unfortunately, I did not receive a translation of it until June 11, 2004, due to an unexpected delay in translations.

Regarding your first point about preaching to the students, it is always very nice to reach out to students with Krishna consciousness. Srila Prabhupada emphasized this preaching as a great investment for the future of our movement. However, one must be very responsible when making presentations to students or to nondevotees in general. We should be careful to not preach so heavily that it is too much for them to digest. I know many successful college preachers in the West who attracted many students to Krishna consciousness simply by becoming their friend. In fact, I also developed a preaching strategy similar to this more than 20 years ago when I saw how successful Radhanath Swami was in attracting college students to become full-time devotees. First let them get some attraction to associating with devotees, learning about our basic philosophy, tasting Krishna *prasadam,* and everything else will naturally follow. You should especially take advantage when there are other preachers visiting your city. Introduce the friends you’ve made amongst the college students to the visiting preachers and let them “preach”. So if you can develop something like this, then you have my full blessings.

Regarding your question about balancing internal and external activities so that neither suffers, the solution is to place your emphasis on your internal activities. This does not necessarily mean that you give your internal activities more time than your external activities. It simply means that you establish it as your topmost priority and don’t deviate from your *sadhana.* By cultivating an attraction for your internal activities, you will be able to execute your external activities with less attachment. They must be performed, but simply as a matter of duty. You will not be dependent upon satisfaction from your external activities because the satisfaction coming from your internal activities will be far superior. But if there is excessive attachment to external activities, then it will adversely affect everything else that you do, and both your internal and external activities will suffer.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

To face it properly, however, those who are “devotees” should not allow themselves to focus exclusively on the projects themselves as their connection to devotional service, but must also focus internally, on the work which they themselves need to accomplish to achieve pure devotional service. Such a thoughtful approach to one’s service enables one to remain focused, both internally and externally, despite external impediments, on the actual goal of devotional service without becoming disappointed when there appears to be no external reciprocation. Nothing goes unnoticed by Krishna when our consciousness is fixed upon Him.

## Letter #13 (NS’s reply to #12) August 16, 2004

Please accept my blessings. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

Bhaktin… should have no fear that her thoughts on this matter are offensive to me. I am not at all offended if she should decide to accept another spiritual master.

In the past, I would say the very same thing to aspiring disciples who live in distant places I did not visit often: “Are you sure that you want to accept me as your spiritual master? Don’t you think that it would be better to accept somebody who you could see more often than me? Will you be satisfied with a connection that is largely dependent upon someone who is acting as my representative? ” I would ask these questions quite often, even before I had a lot of disciples, because I sincerely felt that aspiring disciples should be aware of the potential consequences that could arise by accepting a spiritual master who they will not see very often. But in recent years, I stopped asking these questions, because my books and lectures had become more available; older disciples were starting to take more responsibility for aspiring disciples; disciples were appearing in cities all over the former Soviet Union; and more than anything else, practically every time I asked aspiring disciples these questions, they would think that it was a test from me. The result was that they would become even more determined to become my disciple. After observing that common response, along with the other factors mentioned above, I stopped asking these questions to candidates in distant places. Nonetheless, the same concern is there. Unless they are able to feel the connection through books, recorded lectures and senior disciples, it will become a problem.

Of course, in Bhaktin ...’s case, it very well may be that she has hardly ever seen me. Sometimes even a little personal association could form a lasting basis with an aspiring disciple. But if she were to wait for that from me, the earliest it could come would be some time next year (when I actually do hope to visit the region).

So considering all of these points, Bhaktin should at least make a decision to keep her ears more open to other visiting spiritual masters, to pray for Krishna to reveal Himself to her through the words of one of these Vaisnavas, and to patiently wait until Krishna reveals to her who to accept as her spiritual master. Unless there is the awakening of *Sraddha,* there is no question of simply “switching” to somebody else. As you yourself said, it is a very serious step, and Bhaktin should at least make the preliminary decision, if she already hasn’t, to keep her ears and heart open to the possibility of having her *Sraddha* awakened through the words of somebody else. If and when that should happen, then at least she has a foundation for “switching”. At that time, she should know that she has my permission to accept a formal connection with that Vaisnava.

Hoping this meets you in good health.

## Letter #16 (NS—&gt;) Frankfurt Airport. October 13, 2004

Hare Krishna. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

Thank you for sharing some of your inner thoughts with me. Your realizations are no doubt genuine and I pray that they will be lasting realizations that will help you continue on the difficult path towards *prema-bhakti.*

You are right about suffering. It is definitely an impetus for taking shelter of the Lord. But, unfortunately most people make connections with the Lord only for the reason of relieving their suffering. When their suffering is relieved, their connection is reduced to an occasional prayer of thanks until suffering appears once again. Srila Bhaktivinode Thakura comments on Lord Krishna’s verse in *Bhagavad-gita,* where He states that four types of pious people will approach the Lord—those in distress, those in need of money, those who are inquisitive and those who are wise. All four are described as pious, but real *sukrti* means “activities in connection with transcendental personalities, which give rise to an intense desire to serve Krishna. ” In other words, the distressed, the inquisitive, those in need of money, and the wise, can all become qualified for devotional service, only when these desires are gone from the heart by the grace of the association with the Lord or with His devotees. By such knowledge and faith, one can establish a genuine and lasting connection with the Lord through devotional service, the only means to please Krishna.

Hare Krishna. All glories to Srila Prabhupada.

Thank you for sharing your inner thoughts with me on this. It is not a burden for me, although it is not an easy letter to answer. To be honest, it’s the kind of topic that would be better to discuss in person rather than by email. We must get *sambandha-jnana* from guru if we want lasting shelter under his guidance. *Sambandha-jnana* is the knowledge and necessary level of faith for establishing our connection with Krishna. Once the connection is established, the guru gradually clears the path towards the goal by keeping the disciple moving in the right direction through his instructions and by providing the disciple strength to overcome obstacles *(anarthas).*

Guru is “one”, in that there is no difference in the objective of *diksa* and *siksa* gurus. Their aim is to assist their dependents in their advancement by destroying ignorance with the torchlight of knowledge. In other words, the *diksa* guru wants to see his disciple advance towards pure devotional service and the *siksa* gurus want to see their *siksa* disciple advance towards pure devotional service. They are happy—especially when *siksa* comes in Srila Prabhupada’s line—even if the disciple receives the strength and knowledge to advance from a source other than himself. Therefore from wherever strength and knowledge come, the disciple should take it—with the blessings of his guru. There may even be many who provide that for us at different stages of our advancement.

To serve with devotion requires cultivation. The experience is dynamic and therefore increases in proportion to one’s surrender to and faith in guru and one’s attraction to everything related to Krishna. When one serves in this consciousness, then one can be confident that he can progress in “devotional” service. Srila Prabhupada writes about this cultivation in *The Nectar of Instruction:*

“Bhakti is a sort of cultivation. As soon as we say ’cultivation, ’ we must refer to activity. Cultivation of spirituality does not mean sitting down idly for meditation, as some pseudo-yogis teach. Such idle meditation may be good for those who have no information of devotional service, and for this reason it is sometimes recommended as a way to check distracting materialistic activities. Meditation means stopping all nonsensical activities, at least for the time being. Devotional service, however, not only puts an end to all nonsensical mundane activities, but also engages one in meaningful devotional activities. Sri Prahlada Maharaja recommends:

\[SB, 7. 5. 23\]

*sravanam kirtanam visnoh smaranam pada-sevanam*  
*arcanam vandanam dasyam sakhyam atma-nivedanam*

“The nine processes of devotional service are as follows:

“‘1) Hearing the name and glories of the Supreme Personality of Godhead; 2) chanting His glories; 3) remembering the Lord; 4) serving the Lord’s feet; 5) worshiping the Deity; 6) offering obeisances unto the Lord; 7) acting as the Lord’s servant; 8) making friends with the Lord; 9) surrendering oneself fully to the Lord. ’

“Sravanam, or hearing, is the first step in acquiring transcendental knowledge. One should not give aural reception to unauthorized persons, but should approach the proper person, as recommended in *Bhagavad-gita* (4. 34):

*tad viddhi pranipatena pariprasnena sevaya*  
*upadeksyanti te jnanam jnaninas tattva-darsinah*

“‘Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him. The self-realized soul can impart knowledge unto you because he has seen the truth. ’

“It is further recommended in the M*undaka Upanisad, tad- vijnanartham sa gurum evabhigacchet* \[MU, 1. 2. 12\]: ‘To understand that transcendental science, one must approach a bona fide spiritual master. ’ Thus this method of submissively receiving transcendental confidential knowledge is not merely based on mental speculation. In this regard, Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu told Rupa Gosvami:

*brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva*  
*guru-krsna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija*

“ ‘In the course of traversing the universal creation of Brahma, some fortunate soul may receive the seed of *bhakti-lata,* the creeper of devotional service. This is all by the grace of guru and Krishna. ’ \[Cc., *Madhya,* 19. 151\]

“The material world is a place of confinement for the living entities who are by nature *anandamaya,* pleasure-seeking. They actually want to be free from the confinement of this world of conditional happiness, but not knowing the process of liberation, they are bound to transmigrate from one species of life to another and from one planet to another. In this way the living entities are wandering throughout the material universe. When by good fortune one comes in contact with a pure devotee and hears from him patiently, one begins to follow the path of devotional service. Such an opportunity is offered to a person who is sincere. The International Society for Krishna Consciousness is giving such a chance to humanity at large. If by fortune one takes advantage of this opportunity to engage in devotional service, the path of liberation immediately opens. ” (*The Nectar of Instruction,* Verse 3) \[End of quotation\]

So cultivation is the activity of hearing with faith that increases progressively through continued hearing and service (both being mutually interdependent). Our service has substance when we hear nicely, and from that comes the inspiration that awakens and nourishes selfless devotion to Krishna.

In *Bhakti-tattva-viveka,* Srila Bhaktivinode Thakur writes:

“What are the *svarupa-laksana* or intrinsic attributes of *bhakti!* To answer this question, it is said that *bhakti* is *anukulyena krsnanusi- lana,* the cultivation of activities which are meant exclusively for the pleasure of Sri Krishna. In his *Durgama-sangamani* commentary on *Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu,* Srila Jiva Gosvami has explained that the word *anusilanam* has two meanings.

“First, it means cultivation through the endeavors to engage and disengage one’s body, mind, and words. Second, it means cultivation towards the object of our *priti,* or affection, through *manasi-bhava,* the sentiments of the heart and mind. Although *anusilana* is of two types, the cultivation through *manasi-bhava* is included within cultivation by *cesta,* one’s activities.

“Hence, one’s activities or endeavors *(cesta)* and one’s internal sentiments *(bhava)* are mutually interdependent, and in the end it is the *cesta* which are concluded to be the sole characteristics of cultivation. Only when the activities of one’s body, mind, and words are favorably executed for the pleasure of Krishna is it called *bhakti...*

“The word *bhakti* is derived from the root verb form ‘*bhaj.’* It is said in the *Garuda Purana”* (*Purva-khanda*, 231. 3):

*bhaj ityesa vai dhatuh sevayam parikirtitah*  
*tasmat seva budhaih prokta bhaktih sadhana-bhuyasi*

“‘The verbal root *bhaj* means to render service. Therefore, thoughtful *sadhakas* should engage in the service of Sri Krishna with great endeavor, for it is only by such service that *bhakti* is born. ’ “According to this verse, *krsna-seva,* or loving devotional service to Krishna, is called *bhakti.* Such service is the intrinsic attribute of *bhakti. ”* \[End of quotation\]

The details about “one’s cultivation through the endeavors to engage and disengage one’s body, mind, and words” may differ from one guru to another. But the direction for “cultivation towards the object of our *priti* or affection” (Krishna) is the primary objective of anyone in the position of guru—which brings us back to my opening sentence about the connection established with Krishna through *sambandha-jnana.*

Thus the encouragement for a particular service that we may receive from one guru may differ from what we receive from another. But, still, their aim is always one—to see their dependent advance along the path of *saranagati.* Without a clear vision of this ultimate goal, the impetus for service will become disoriented.

You should not feel too discouraged if your Guru Maharaja does not encourage you in your present aspiration for service. He has encouraged you so far and he could very well do so again at some later point. The guru will not reject a disciple for having a different vision regarding such secondary details about service. He will always transparently accept the essence of a disciple’s service, which is born from the disciple’s faith in pure devotional service.

It is good that you remain ever grateful for all that your Guru Maharaja has done for you. Always keep that gratitude in your heart, but also keep your heart open to other sources of inspiration and strength needed to conquer *anarthas,* strengthen your connection to Krishna, and to move forward towards pure devotional service— as you already do.

I hope this helps.
