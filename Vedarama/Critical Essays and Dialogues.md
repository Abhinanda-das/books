# Critical Essays and Dialogues

## Synthesis of Science and Religion: Singh

In 1984 I had the opportunity to spend some time with Dr. T. D. Singh while he was preparing for the First World Congress on the Synthesis of Science and Religion. Dr. Singh, a small Indian man from the the Indo-China border state of Manipur, was educated in the West receiving a doctorate in Physical Organic Chemistry from University of California, Irvine. He had departed considerably, however, from the type of behavior one would expect from a research scientist—he had become a monk, a scientific monk. The World Conference was inspired by Singh in his role as Chairman of the Bhaktivedanta Institute, an institution founded for the purpose of presenting Vedantic science and theology, which focuses on a consciousness-based paradigm. The Institute maintains that such a paradigm has profound significance for both science and religion as well as all other fields of human endeavor.

Synthesis of Science and Religion is but one of the results of the World Conference—a rare anthology examining the reflections of some of the most profound thinkers in an unusually wide variety of disciplines that fall under the headings of science and religion: the Dalai Lama, Harvey Cox, Houston Smith, Eileen Barker, Willis Harman and Fritjof Capra along with five Nobel Laureates, the president of the World Council of Churches, India's Chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission and many other scientists and theologians from around the world. All together forty-one papers/interviews are presented in seven sections including the "Search for the Perennial," "Physics and Beyond," "Mind Brain and Consciousness" and the "Basis for the Unity of All Religions."

The book for the most part breaks down the barriers between these two great fields that have arisen out of a shallow, popular understanding of both and identifies the study of consciousness as meeting ground for science and religion. The general consensus: religion and science are both valid means of acquiring knowledge regarding the nature of reality, with religion or spiritual pursuit offering greater potential for ultimate understanding. There are, however, a few dissenters from this opinion which add an overall balance. Reflections on the Institute's consciousness-based Vedic paradigm are offered in two presentations and are well received by the general body.

Science and social concerns are also addressed—what is the role of a scientist in an immoral world? The threat of nuclear war surfaces and re-surfaces as does the relationship between science and values. A sense of urgency and a call for integrity make the Synthesis of Science and Religion exciting reading and personally compelling. At the same time a healthy pessimism pervades, helping to keep the reader in touch with the awesomeness of the task of creating a better world.

It is not at all a dry, technical presentation intended only for the initiated; thus it remains relatively free from scientific jargon and mathematical formulas. With the exception of one or two of the essays, the book takes the reader into domains he might otherwise have difficulty understanding without sufficient background, including: Physics, Philosophy, Artificial Intelligence, Biology, Mathematics, History, Theology and the Social Sciences.

The setting of the conference, India, seems to have surrounded it with an air of humility and brilliant minds consistently point in refreshingly frank terms to the shortcomings of their particular fields and on occasion admit to their own personal limitations.

The schism between science and religion which is often attributed to Rene Descarte and his contemporaries is addressed in the Synthesis of Science and Religion as one of the most debilitating events in Western history. Although a rapprochement between the two is almost universally viewed as beneficial, it seems that only now with the global and historical perspective provided by modern technology and the emerging higher consciousness is it possible. Readers will find this book a premier and essential contribution in this area where individual self education and commitment are crucial.
