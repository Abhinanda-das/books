# Defeating Svakīya and Defining Parakīya

## Defeating Svakīya

1\. Some persons speak of marriage of the Rādhā with Kṛṣṇa in Vraja and argue on this point. Do they propose that the marriage took place before leaving for Mathurā? Or, after leaving Mathurā and living in Dvārakā, did it happen at Kurukṣetra? Or after killing Dantavakra and returning to Vraja, according to Padma Purāṇa, did it happen in Vraja? Are the couple nitya-siddha as Viṣṇu and Lakṣmī (married)? Was Rādhā given to Kṛṣṇa by her parents after being enjoyed by another man? Hearing that the previous marriage to Abhimanyu was false after being given to him, did she get married for a second time to Kṛṣṇa?

2\. The marriage could not have taken place in Vraja since Nanda was a vaiśya, and Kṛṣṇa's marriage could take place only after Kṛṣṇa obtained his sacred thread. After killing Kaṁsa in Mathurā, Vasudeva had the ceremony performed. One cannot claim that Kṛṣṇa got his first thread in Vraja and again as a kṣatriya in Mathurā, since this is not related in Bhāgavatam or other scriptures.

*garbhādhānād aṣṭame janmato vā*  
*mauñjī-bandhaḥ śasyate brāhmaṇānām \|*  
*rājanyānāṁ navame ekādaśe’bde*  
*vaiśyānāṁ ca dvādaśe veda-vidbhir \|\|*

**Knowers of the Vedas say that a brāhmaṇa should take the thread in the eighth year from birth or conception. A kṣatriya should take the thread in the ninth year or eleventh year. A vaiśya should take in the twelfth year.**

It is well known that Kṛṣṇa stayed in Vraja only till the beginning of his eleventh year.

3\. The marriage could not have taken place in Kurukṣetra. All the people of Vraja, after meeting Kṛṣṇa in Kurukṣetra returned with their families to houses in the same condition as previously. The gopīs at the time of meeting Kṛṣṇa privately said:

*āhuś ca te nalina-nābha padāravindaṁ*  
*yogeśvarair hṛdi vicintyam agādha-bodhaiḥ*  
*saṁsāra-kūpa-patitottaraṇāvalambaṁ*  
*gehaṁ juṣām api manasy udiyāt sadā naḥ*

**Dear Lord, whose navel is just like a lotus flower, your lotus feet are the only shelter for those who have fallen into the deep well of material existence. Your feet are worshiped and meditated upon by great mystic yogīs and highly learned philosophers. We wish that these lotus feet may also be awakened within our hearts, although we are only ordinary persons engaged in household affairs. SB 10. 2.48**

In explaining Kṛṣṇa's reply, Jīva Gosvāmī says in Kṛṣṇa Sandarbha 180:

He says "I have come now. Please experience me directly at every moment." They say, "We are not independent. We belong to others’ houses (*gehaṁ juṣām*) so we cannot do that." According to these words, marriage to Kṛṣṇa could not have taken place there.

4\. The marriage could not have occurred after Kṛṣṇa killed Dantavakra and returned to Vraja. Padma Purāṇa 6.252.26 says only that Kṛṣṇa played incessantly with the cowherd women. After describing that he stayed for two months with the juice of prema, the Purāṇa states that Kṛṣṇa disappeared along with the people of Vraja, with their sons and wives (with no mention of marriage). As well, one cannot find a scriptural description of marriage at that time in any other scripture. One cannot argue that marriage should be accepted since he otherwise could not indulge with the gopīs incessantly. Kṛṣṇa in any case does not indulge in continual amorous behavior like Śiva in the form of Hāṭakeśvara since such incessant acts would spoil śṛṅgāra-rasa.

5\. "The later pastimes are established to be eternal in Kṛṣṇa Sandarbha, Gopāla-campū and Locana –rocani. This fact denies a permanent status to the pastimes previous to killing Dantavakra, which are pastimes of the avatāra form. It is described that all the gopīs had two forms, one real and one false. The false forms resided in houses with husbands and later Kṛṣṇa married the real forms. How can anyone raise a new doubt after this explanation?"

Kṛṣṇa's pastimes are said to be eternal in śruti, smṛti, Purāṇas and āgamas and are accepted as life-giving forms, worthy of worship by previous ācāryas. You discredit the earlier pastimes and speak of the eternal nature of the Lord's later pastimes. This is a conception invented by yourself. Bhagavat Sandarbha with great force refutes your view. Why consider it further? This is discussed in the conclusion.

Following the prose passage of the Padma Purāṇa quoted in Laghu Bhāgavatamṛta, why do you make false claims? You should consider what is written by inspecting the introductory and concluding statements of the incident described there. Having killed Dantavakra, Kṛṣṇa crossed the Yamunā and met all the cowherds headed by Nanda. For two month he played with the cowherd women, and then made all the inhabitants of Vraja disappear along with their sons and wives. That is all that is written. Nothing more is said. The cowherds of Vraja along with their wives continue to live in aprakaṭa forms. How is it possible for others to distort the conclusion of the author, which simply follows the Purāṇa? If one maintains a belief that only the later pastimes after marriage are eternal, what is the proof?

Even if it is accepted that Kṛṣṇa married the gopīs, how can any well-bred person consider Kṛṣṇa marrying the gopīs, after it is explained to everyone about the illusory forms of the previous marriage? It is repulsive in tradition and scripture to speak of a man marrying a woman who was previously married to another man (even if it is explained that the previous marriage was an illusion). Thus this proposition is clearly baseless.

6\. To claim that they are eternally situated as Lakṣmī and Nārāyaṇa is not suitable since it is established that the prakaṭa and aprakaṭa forms (Kṛṣṇa and the gopīs) are the same in all places at all times. *Lokavat tu līlā-kaivalyam:*  the Lord is absorbed in his pastimes which appear human-like. (BS 2.1.33)

"According to Brahma-saṁhitā, Kṛṣṇa is established as the ādi-puruṣa and the gopīs are Lakṣmīs. That is clear. Since we are dedicated to the Goloka pastimes, what use have we for the frightening earthly pastimes (parakīya)? One cannot say that the taste of sweetness cannot take place without the prakaṭa pastimes, since it everything is available in the aprakaṭa pastimes in Goloka."

Let me say this. Though Kṛṣṇa is well known as the puruṣa and the gopīs are known as Ramā (Lakṣmī), one cannot say that Kṛṣṇa is Viṣṇu and the gopīs are Lakṣmī since it is determined in Pañcarātra, Gautamīya-tantra and Krama-dīpikā, when describing the method of worship using the best of mantras spoken by Brahmā, that the gopīs are cowherd women and Kṛṣṇa is the son of Nanda. No Kṛṣṇa mantra in the Pañcarātra is devoid of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes described by Vyāsa in Bhāgavatam. Kṛṣṇa is described everywhere as the object of worship, while endowing him with pastimes like drinking from the breast of Pūtanā. Since he is endowed with human pastimes as his unique feature, nowhere is any other type of worship of him (as Nārāyaṇa for instance) prescribed. And there is no question of worshipping an entity devoid of all pastimes, such as all-pervading Brahman. If one accepts the impersonal as the ultimate, then the worship prescribed everywhere by the ācāryas could not be performed.

Marriage of the gopīs to cowherds (not Kṛṣṇa) is well known to everyone in Vraja, being stated in Vidagdha-mādhava and other works. How can this be denied? One cannot claim the impersonalist theory that everything is illusion. But it is claimed that all the gopīs were illusory and their marriages to the cowherds were illusory. It is impossible that, simply by declaring that everything is illusory like an impersonalist, one can undo the act of marriage performed by brāhmaṇas. Actual events took place and no amount of proclamations about illusion can undo those actions.

After declaring that all the previous pastimes of the gopīs were illusory, how could a second marriage take place after the first marriage in the same town? If it happened it must be explained. Those who first married the gopīs would later claim that the gopīs were married to them, and that Kṛṣṇa’s marriage was a second marriage for the gopīs and was unlawful. Kṛṣṇa would be called a second husband (*didhiṣu*) for the gopīs (which is condemned by society). Enough of speaking of this. Amara-koṣa says that *didhisu* means "being a second husband."

Moreover, if you accept that the gopīs had two identical forms at all times, one illusory and one real, then you must also accept that all the inhabitants, including their parents like Vṛṣabhānu and Kīrtidā, had two forms. If it were not so, the conclusion reached in Kṛṣṇa Sandarbha and Gopāla-campū would be contradicted.

devī vakṣyati—bhojane nirvṛtte man-nirvartitāḥ sva-sva-pati-gṛham anuvartantām \| parās tu kṛṣṇa-mātra-pati-sammati-parāyaṇāḥ sva-sva-pitṛ-gṛham \| mayā tu yamunā-taṭeṣṭa-praśastālaye sthāsyate, yathā yadā mātṛ-caraṇair ājñāpyate, tathā tadā vyavahartavyam \|

Devī will say “After finishing your meal, let the māyā forms go to their respective houses of their husbands. The real forms who take Kṛṣṇa as their husband should go to their fathers’ houses. Gopāla-campū 1.3.303

If they were simultaneously sent to their husbands' and parents' houses, then as wives in their husbands' homes, they would have to give birth to sons in order to nourish perfect household life. In those houses they could not perform all the rituals starting with jāta-karma without the presence of elders from both sides of the family. These persons must thus be accepted along with the illusory forms of the gopīs.

Kṛṣṇa would then marry those who went to their parents' houses according to your proposal. Nanda would invite all the people of Vraja for Kṛṣṇa's wedding. All the married women such as Jaṭilā and Bhāruṇḍā would be invited along with their daughters-in-law Rādhā, Candrāvalī etc.

Here is the doubt. In the marriage ceremony, Rādhā and other gopīs marry Kṛṣṇa, while standing at his side. Are those real forms seen or not seen by their mothers-in-law, by Abhimanyu and other apparent husbands and by Vṛṣabhānu and other fathers of the (illusory) girls? If the real gopīs are seen by them, what do they understand about this? Do the mothers-in-law, apparent husbands and fathers understand, "These (real) gopīs are different from our daughters-in-law, these are different from our wives, these are different from our daughters?" Or do they not determine the truth? Or do they fail to question because of the power of yoga-māyā?

It cannot be the first option. Though you admit that the real gopīs who went to their fathers' houses thought "We are real, the others are false", by the strength of previous impressions (since they understood the truth, with the previous incidents) because of great reservations concerning the husbands like Abhimanyu (whom they now saw before them), the real gopīs could not agree for another marriage. Those gopīs who returned to their husbands' houses (the false ones) did not have such realization. They thus would become greatly disgusted on seeing gopīs with the same form and qualities getting married to Kṛṣṇa. The mothers-in -law, husbands and parents (the second set for the illusory gopīs) would become disgusted on seeing the other forms getting married. They could be confused thinking "Our daughters-in-law, wives and daughters are getting married to Kṛṣṇa." They would think "These forms are not different from our forms" since they would be in illusion, still maintaining their identities. They would always perceive their names, forms and qualities to be completely identical with the real forms.

It cannot be the second option--that they did not recognize different forms at all--for one cannot claim that the mothers-in-law and others would not recognize even a little that there were two names and forms for themselves and their relatives (unless they became completely unconscious).

It cannot be the power of yoga-māyā, the third option either. Yogamāyā is famous for arranging the pastimes in the material world. Seeing and understanding the presence of the bride and groom, relatives would offer presents to them. The relatives and Kṛṣṇa would see the duplicate forms from the perspective of local and scriptural traditions. At that time, on seeing them, Kṛṣṇa would be overcome with fear and shame. The gopīs about to get married to Kṛṣṇa would not consent to a second marriage, out of fear, on seeing their mothers-in-law and apparent husbands. The husbands like Govardhana and Abhimanyu would become angry at Kṛṣṇa on seeing the spread of illusion. Duplicate fathers like Vṛṣabhānu would think, "Those who were born from us are not these getting married. How can they be the same? How can those daughters be given in front of elders and mothers without any regard for them?"

One cannot say the Paurṇamāsī would solve all these problems. Śaṅkarācarya has explained that the whole world is false and he gives śruti evidence to support his claim. Since marriages in this world performed by brāhmaṇas are illusory, it is impossible for anyone to give a bride to anyone (even a second time). This was previously explained.

I do not know how persons can explain pastimes of Kṛṣṇa, which are established as eternal and spiritual, to be illusory. Enough of this repetition.

Veda-vyāsa divided the one Ṛg Veda into four and made a thousand branches with authorities in charge. Since women and śūdras were not qualified for the Vedas, he taught Mahābhārata to deliver them. He showed the conclusions about Brahman in four chapters (in Brahma-sūtras) for persons qualified for liberation, so that they could attain that goal. Seeing people absorbed in conflicting paths, considering their various qualifications, in order to help them, he revealed the Purāṇas. Being unsatisfied in his heart, he attained satisfaction when, by the instructions of Nārada, he wrote Śrīmad Bhāgavatam. Attaining clarity of mind, he realized the form of Bhagavān.

Those modern people who desire the best should absorb their mind in scriptural conclusions which agree with Bhāgavatam, either by confirmation of its conclusions or by denial of the opposite propositions, which give pleasure to the heart. Bhāgavatam is accepted by the great devotees as the highest proof. Its heart is in the Tenth Canto, predominated by Kṛṣṇa's pastimes. Among all the pastimes, the description of rāsa-līlā in five chapters, like the prāṇa in the body, is its very essence. These chapters should be discussed.

*pariveṣayantyas tad dhitvā pāyayantyaḥ śiśūn payaḥ*  
*śuśrūṣantyaḥ patīn kāścid aśnantyo ’pāsya bhojanam*

**Some of them were serving servants and friends food, feeding milk to their infants or rendering personal service to their husbands, or taking their evening meals, but they all gave up these duties and went to meet Kṛṣṇa. SB 10.29.6**

*tam eva paramātmānaṁ jāra-buddhyāpi saṅgatāḥ*  
*jahur guṇa-mayaṁ dehaṁ sadyaḥ prakṣīṇa-bandhanāḥ*

**By meditating upon him they realized his embrace and experienced the bliss of a sudden internal realization of meeting him. Although Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Soul, these girls simply thought of him as an illicit lover and associated with him in that intimate mood. Thus their karmic bondage was nullified and they abandoned their gross material bodies. SB 10.29.12**

*mātaraḥ pitaraḥ putrā bhrātaraḥ patayaś ca vaḥ*  
*vicinvanti hy apaśyanto mā kṛḍhvaṁ bandhu-sādhvasam*

**Not finding you at home, your mothers, fathers, sons, brothers and husbands are certainly searching for you. Don’t cause anxiety for your family members. SB 10.29.20**

*tad yāta mā ciraṁ goṣṭhaṁ śuśrūṣadhvaṁ patīn satīḥ*  
*krandanti vatsā bālāś ca tān pāyayata duhyata*

**So now go back to the cowherd village. Don’t delay. O chaste ladies, serve your husbands and give milk to your crying babies and calves. SB 10. 29.21**

*bhartuḥ śuśrūṣaṇaṁ strīṇāṁ paro dharmo hy amāyayā*  
*tad-bandhūnāṁ ca kalyāṇaḥ prajānāṁ cānupoṣaṇam*

**The highest religious duty for a woman is to sincerely serve her husband, behave well toward her husband’s family and take good care of her children. SB 10. 29.24**

*duḥśīlo durbhago vṛddho jaḍo rogy adhano ’pi vā*  
*patiḥ strībhir na hātavyo lokepsubhir apātakī*

**Women who desire a good destination in the next life should never abandon a husband who has not fallen from his religious standards, even if he is obnoxious, unfortunate, old, inactive, sickly or poor. SB 10. 29.25**

*asvargyam ayaśasyaṁ ca phalgu kṛcchraṁ bhayāvaham*  
*jugupsitaṁ ca sarvatra hy aupapatyaṁ kula-striyaḥ*

**For a woman from a respectable family, petty adulterous affairs are always condemned. They bar her from heaven, ruin her reputation and bring her difficulty and fear. SB 10. 29.26**

*yat paty-apatya-suhṛdām anuvṛttir aṅga*  
*strīṇāṁ sva-dharma iti dharma-vidā tvayoktam*

**Our dear Kṛṣṇa, as an expert in religion you have advised us that the proper religious duty for women is to faithfully serve their husbands, children and other relatives. SB 10. 29.32**

*kurvanti hi tvayi ratiṁ kuśalāḥ sva ātman*  
*nitya-priye pati-sutādibhir ārti-daiḥ kim*

**Expert transcendentalists always direct their affection toward you because they recognize you as their true self and eternal beloved. What use do we have for these husbands, children and relatives of ours, who simply give us trouble? SB 10. 29.33**

*kā stry aṅga te kala-padāyata-veṇu-gīta-*  
*sammohitārya-caritān na calet tri-lokyām*

**Dear Kṛṣṇa, what woman in all the three worlds wouldn’t deviate from religious behavior when bewildered by the sweet, drawn-out melody of your flute? Your beauty makes all three worlds auspicious. SB 10.29.40**

*pati-sutānvaya-bhrātṛ-bāndhavān*  
*ativilaṅghya te ’nty acyutāgatāḥ*  
*gati-vidas tavodgīta-mohitāḥ*  
*kitava yoṣitaḥ kas tyajen niśi*

**Who but a cheater like you would abandon young women who come to see you in the middle of the night, enchanted by the loud song of your flute, though you know the nature of your flute, and though we have completely rejected our husbands, children, mothers, fathers, and brothers to come to you, O Acyuta! SB 10. 31.16**

*evaṁ mad-arthojjhita-loka-veda*  
*svānām hi vo mayy anuvṛttaye ’balāḥ*

**My dear girls, understanding that simply for my sake you had rejected the authority of worldly opinion, of the Vedas and of your relatives, I acted as I did only to increase your attachment to me. SB 10.32.21**

*na pāraye ’haṁ niravadya-saṁyujāṁ*  
*sva-sādhu-kṛtyaṁ vibudhāyuṣāpi vaḥ*  
*yā mābhajan durjara-geha-śṛṅkhalāḥ*  
*saṁvṛścya tad vaḥ pratiyātu sādhunā*

**I am not able to repay my debt for your spotless service, even within a lifetime of Brahmā. Your connection with me is beyond reproach. You have worshiped me, cutting off all domestic ties, which are difficult to break. Therefore please let your own glorious deeds be your compensation. SB 10.32.22**

*kṛtvā tāvantam ātmānaṁ yāvatīr gopa-yoṣitaḥ*  
*reme sa bhagavāṁs tābhir ātmārāmo ’pi līlayā*

**Expanding himself as many times as there were cowherd women to associate with, the Supreme Lord, though self-satisfied, playfully enjoyed their company. SB 10. 33.20**

Parīkṣit asks:

*sa kathaṁ dharma-setūnāṁ vaktā kartābhirakṣitā*  
*pratīpam ācarad brahman para-dārābhimarśanam*

**He is the original speaker, follower and guardian of moral laws. How, then, could he have violated them by touching other men’s wives? SB 10.33.27**

*āpta-kāmo yadu-patiḥ kṛtavān vai jugupsitam*  
*kim-abhiprāya etan naḥ śaṁśayaṁ chindhi su-vrata*

**O faithful upholder of vows, please destroy our doubt by explaining to us what purpose the self-satisfied Lord of the Yadus had in mind when he behaved so contemptibly. SB 10.33.28**

Śukadeva Gosvāmī said:

*dharma-vyatikramo dṛṣṭa īśvarāṇāṁ ca sāhasam*  
*tejīyasāṁ na doṣāya vahneḥ sarva-bhujo yathā*

**The status of powerful controllers is not harmed by any apparently audacious transgression of morality we may see in them, for they are just like fire, which devours everything fed into it and remains unpolluted. SB 10.33.29**

*gopīnāṁ tat-patīnāṁ ca sarveṣām eva dehinām*  
*yo ’ntaś carati so ’dhyakṣaḥ krīḍaneneha deha-bhāk*

**Kṛṣṇa, appearing directly in this world, moves among the gopīs and their apparent husbands as well as the other inhabitants of Vraja, in his eternal spiritual body performing his pastimes. SB 10.33.35**

*nāsūyan khalu kṛṣṇāya mohitās tasya māyayā*  
*manyamānāḥ sva-pārśva-sthānv svān svān dārān vrajaukasaḥ*

**The cowherd men, bewildered by Kṛṣṇa’s yogamāyā, thought their wives had remained home at their sides. They did not harbor any jealous feelings against him at any time. SB 10.33.37**

The following should be considered. After the description in the Bhāgavatam, Veda-vyāsa experienced clarity in his heart. The five chapters (describing parakīya love) were like the five prāṇas in his heart. Śukadeva, omniscient and realizing the Lord's svarūpa, in reply to Parīkṣit's question, to destroy his doubt, said that there was no fault in parakīya love because of the greatness of the Lord. Showing fear by using the example of unqualified people drinking poison as Śiva did, he concluded that, for those persons without ahaṅkāra, good and bad do not arise from performing auspicious or inauspicious acts. So what good and bad can there be for the master of all jīvas? The same goes for persons related to Kṛṣṇa. Satisfied by constant service to the pollen of the Lord's lotus feet, by the power of bhakti-yoga, the devotees wander as they please after destroying all bondage and becoming jīvamuktas. They remain unbound by their actions.

Śukadeva explains that there is no worry of bondage for the Lord who becomes visible and performs pastimes by his will. He is situated as the inspiration within all the bodies of the gopīs and their husbands. He protects them as the prince of Gokula and plays. Even now, in aprakaṭa form, he continues to play. It is stated that anyone who hears these pastimes becomes a worshipper of Kṛṣṇa alone. If people, falling into ignorance, imagine these pastimes of Kṛṣṇa to be something else (illicit acts or illusions), they are involved in forbidden conduct.

Even if one accepts that yogamāyā created their husbands, at the end, the gopīs did not want to leave Kṛṣṇa and return to their houses but Kṛṣṇa did not accept their plea. After he comforted them by promising to meet them again, at the end of night, fearing public opinion, they returned to the beds in their houses. Thus parakīya is confirmed by repetition. If something else had happened would not omniscient Śukadeva mention this?

One cannot even in a dream claim that Śukadeva revealed what he did only because other matters (Kṛṣṇa married the gopīs and parakīya is illusion) were too confidential. Śukadeva spoke directly to everyone in the huge assembly what was commonly accepted in scriptures. Śukadeva would not speak what was not true, for one sees that the same facts are repeated throughout the Bhāgavatam. The essence gathered by the questions and answers of Parīkṣit and Śukadeva in front of brahmarṣis, devarṣis, Nārada, Paraśurāma, Veda-vyāsa seated in the exalted assembly should sustain the life of all present jīvas. That is the conclusion of the wise. To imagine something else means that Śukadeva, fully engaged in the Lord, did not have all knowledge. Denying parakīya would also attribute sin to Kṛṣṇa, who destroys all sin, and it would make the author of Vidagdha-mādhava look foolish. There are many faults with this argument. Thus it has only been briefly discussed.

Some modern persons engaged in mādhurya-rasa think the ideas of Śukadeva, Veda-vyāsa and Parāśara as well as the author of Vidagdha-mādhava to be wrong. They publicize explanations of svakīya according to their imagination. Their hearts are confused by a great ocean of doubts. Ideas of this nature are discussed by immature minds. Jīva Gosvāmī explains these ideas, but are those his ideas or the ideas of others?

## Defining Parakīya

I offer respects to the mind, like an island which is immersed in the ocean of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes. May my mind not enjoy the opposite of this.

I offer respects to the feet of Śrī Jīva who explains his intention at the end of his commentary on Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 1.21. He says:

*svecchayā likhitaṁ kiṁcit kiṁcid atra parecchayā \|*  
*yat pūrvāpara-sambaddhaṁ tat pūrvam aparaṁ param \|\|*

Some portions I have written as my own opinions, and some portions are the opinions of others. What is consistent is my view. What is inconsistent is the view of others.

It is also inspired by his commentaries on of the meaning of *rāgeṇaivārpitātmāna* (Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 3.17) and *patnī-bhāvābhimānātma*. (Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 14.48)

His explanation (accepting parakīya) is repeated constantly while determining with good reasoning the characteristics of the presence or absence of mahābhāva. The perfection of rasa will arise by the highest pastimes (parakīya, rāsa-līlā) since Vyāsa and Śukadeva became absorbed in those pastimes. The author of Vidagdha-mādhava, because of great passion in his heart, was also absorbed in describing these (parakīya) pastimes from beginning to end. The greatly merciful Rūpa Gosvāmī, king of all rasikas, after briefly describing mādhurya-rasa in Bhatki-rasāmṛta-sindhu, describes mādhurya-rasa in more detail in Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi with Kṛṣṇa as the sole object, so that modern people could experience mādhurya-rasa easily by words and thoughts. This means that Kṛṣṇa is the highest type of hero.

**It is said that in the spiritual realm the love of a married woman and her lover is the greatest. Doing what should not be done (in ordinary circumstances) becomes astonishing. It is an ornament of perfection and not a fault, since it is beyond logic. Alaṅkāra-kaustubha**

Therefore, this is not a fault in Kṛṣṇa. Why does he appear in this world? He appears in order that he can taste his rasa (*rasa-niryāsa-svādārtham*) along with his devotees. If Kṛṣṇa were the subject of criticism, these devotees could not taste rasa with Kṛṣṇa as the object.

Or, the meaning can be causative. Why does he appear? He appears in order to make the devotees in the world taste rasa. "The verb *to taste* can have a causative meaning." He himself always tastes rasa in the aprakaṭa and prakaṭa pastimes.

If the birth and other pastimes are shown mercifully to the people of the world, they are called prakaṭa (manifest), and if they disappear from the eyes they are called aprakaṭa. There is no essential difference between prakaṭa and aprakaṭa pastimes. It is said:

*anādim eva janmādi-līlām eva tathādbhutam \|*  
*hetunā kenacit kṛṣṇaḥ prāduṣkuryāt kadācana \|\|*

In the commentary concerning events after Kṛṣṇa killed Dantavakra, Jīva Gosvāmī says:

The verses SB 11.12.10-11 means that previously the gopīs could not see any means of happiness, but now they see it. Separation no longer exists. This is seen in a later verse.

*tā nāvidan mayy anuṣaṅga-baddha-*  
*dhiyaḥ svam ātmānam adas tathedam*  
*yathā samādhau munayo ’bdhi-toye*  
*nadyaḥ praviṣṭā iva nāma-rūpe*

**Their minds bound by constant association with me, they were not aware of their bodies, of this world or the next world, just as sages in samādhi are not aware of the world, and just as rivers, on entering the ocean, lose their names and forms. SB 11.12.12 (end of quotation)**

The meaning of the above verse is this. These gopīs, because of intense longing in separation, experienced distinct mahābhāva, difficult to bear. They manifested the state called mahā-mohana on attaining me, who came to see them sometimes. Their intelligence became bound by that association. Thus they were not aware of possessiveness or ego (*svam ātmānam*), and could not distinguish what were *aprakaṭa* pastimes (*adaḥ*) and *prakaṭa* pastimes (*idam*). They experienced the two as one. Because of non-difference of the pastimes of the *prakaṭa* manifestation with the *aprakaṭa* pastimes, they did not see them as two. Names and forms from the prakaṭa pastimes entered into the aprakaṭa manifestation, since actually they are non-different in essence. An example (the rivers and ocean) is given to show non-difference of names and forms of the prakaṭa and aprakaṭa pastimes.

**Dear ladies, I am the beginning and end of all created beings and exist both within and without them, just as the elements ether, water, earth, air and fire are the beginning and end of all material objects and exist both within and without them.**

*ubhayaṁ mayy atha pare paśyatābhātam akṣare*

**Just as the basic elements of creation pervade the bodies, the prakaṭa objects exist in aprakaṭa objects. See the aprakaṭa pastimes and objects in me. SB 10.82.45-46**

Just as ahaṅkāra (*aham)* is the beginning and end of all elements like ether (ether has ahaṅkāra included in it), just as all the elements like ether are the beginning and end of all earthly or watery things (having ether included in them), so all the spiritual objects (*bhūtāni)* like Vṛndāvana to which you are attached and which you experience in the prakaṭa pastimes exist in the spiritual objects (*bhūteṣu*) of the aprakaṭa pastimes. Your identity in the prakaṭa pastimes *(ātmā*) is pervaded by your identity in the aprakaṭa pastimes (*ātmanā*). See the existence of yourselves and objects (*ubhayam*) in my form (*mayi*), the shelter, which associates with you eternally (*akṣare*) in Vṛndāvana--and which was seen previously as prakaṭa. Because of the different manifestations, there are different designations for the objects there and an arrangement for states of separation (prakaṭa pastimes) and union (aprakaṭa pastimes). That is made clear here. (end of quotation)

Since there is oneness of the aprakaṭa pastimes with the presenting existing Vṛndāvana, it is possible for the people of Vraja to live in the aprakaṭa abode. All the places of the prakaṭa pastimes (including parakīya) belonging to Kṛṣṇa, his parents, followers and other cowherds exist in the aprakaṭa manifestation. This is the meaning.

Jīva Gosvāmī comments on Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 1.21 as follows:

This verse resolves any doubts about the previous statement. This is an explanation of the doubt: that the paramour or upapati is disrespectable *(laghutvam*). Some person will argue as follows.

"It is said:

*śṛṅgaṁ hi manmathodbhedas tad-āgamana-hetukaḥ \|*  
*uttama-prakṛti-prāyo rasaḥ śṛṅgāra iṣyate \|\|*

***Śṛṅga***  **means manifestation of desire or the cause of its manifestation. Thus, this rasa with highest nature is called** ***śṛṅgāra.***  **Sāhitya-darpāṇa**

This ancient definition describes *śṛṅgāra-rasa* as the highest. In the pure rasa or ujjvala-rasa, impure paramour love should not be present as its portion. In the *Tri-khaṇḍa-śeṣa,* the paramour is also called a sinful master (*pāpa-pati*). Thus the paramour is condemned. The paramour is also condemned in literature on dramatics and poetics. What has been said is summarized in *Sāhitya-darpaṇa:*

*upanāyaka-saṁsthāyāṁ muni-guru-patnī-gatāyāṁ ca \|*  
*bahu-nāyaka-viṣayāyāṁ ratau tathānubhava-niṣṭhāyām \|\|*  
*pratināyaka-niṣṭhatve tadvad adhama-pātra-tiryag-ādi-gate \|*  
*śṛṅgāre’anucityaṁ raudre gurv-ādi-gata-kope \|\|*

In śṛṅgāra-rasa, the paramour is improper, since this person, as a secondary character addicted to experiencing love affairs with the wife of a sage or guru, opposed vehemently by the hero, ends up in lower species of life, since he is the dedicated enemy of the hero, and, endowed with raudra-rasa, shows anger to guru and others.

Wherever the upapati is described, he is condemned. According to the proofs of the ancients, it is not the main rasa. Married love produces the main rasa. Even as a secondary form, it is derided. Even Kṛṣṇa, who supports this aspect, says:

*asvargyam ayaśasyaṁ ca phalgu kṛcchraṁ bhayāvaham*  
*jugupsitaṁ ca sarvatra hy aupapatyaṁ kula-striyaḥ*

**For a woman from a respectable family, petty adulterous affairs are always condemned. They bar her from heaven, ruin her reputation and bring her difficulty and fear. SB 10.29.26**

It is dismissed as disgusting and faulty. Even the women of Vraja agree when they say *niḥsvaṁ tyajanti gaṇikā jārā bhuktvā ratāṁ striyam:* prostitutes abandon a penniless man, and a lover abandons the woman he has enjoyed, even though she remains attached to him. (SB 10.47.7-8) Parīkṣit also criticizes it. *Āpta-kāmo yadu-patiḥ kṛtavān vai jugupsitam:* the self-satisfied Lord of the Yadus behaved so contemptibly. (SB 10.33.28) Thus, *laghutvam* in the verse means vulgar and censurable.

According to Bharata Muni, the love in marriage is the best, with obstacles created by cowives etc. as in the marriages depicted in *Ratnāvalī-nātīkā* and *Yayāti-carita*. How then can he say that paramour love is the best (verse 20)?”

The solution is stated in the present verse. The upapati is disgusting when it refers to someone other than Kṛṣṇa. That is what is meant by *prākṛta-nāyaka*. When Kṛṣṇa is the upapati it is not disgusting because it produces the essence (*niryāsa*) of rasa. This means it produces a special type of mādhurya-rasa.

What has been stated is this. When Kṛṣṇa appears on earth, he displays the mode of the upapati. According to the ancients, it is pointed out that both he and the gopīs as well appeared for this purpose. *Āśaṁsayārasa-vidher avatārikāṇām:* with the hope of attaining rasa, the gopīs also appeared on earth. (Nāṭaka-candrikā) Brahmā also explains this:

*vasudeva-gṛhe sākṣād bhagavān puruṣaḥ paraḥ*  
*janiṣyate tat-priyārthaṁ sambhavantu sura-striyaḥ*

**Kṛṣṇa, who has full potency, will personally appear as the son of Vasudeva. Therefore all the eternal consortsshould also appear in order to satisfy him. SB 10.1.23**

By the statements of Uddhava and Brahma-saṁhitā mentioned above, the gopīs must have an eternal relationship with the Lord (since they are superior to Lakṣmī). Therefore they cannot actually have a parakīya relationship with Kṛṣṇa (since they are eteranlly related to him and not to another male).

Because such a situation is unsuitable, the perception of their having parakīya relationship when he appears as avatāra is not actual. This is shown in the drama Lalita-mādhava in the discussion between Paurnamāsī and Gargī.

*gārgī:ṇuṇaṁ goaḍḍhaṇādi-goehiṁ candāalī-pahudīṇaṁ ubbāho bi māāe ṇibbāhido \|*

**Gārgī: It is not that māyā produced the marriage of Govardhana and the other cowherds with Candrāvalī and the other gopīs?**

*Paurṇamāsī: atha kim \| patiṁ-manyānāṁ ballavānāṁ mamatā-mātrāvaśeṣā tāsu dāratā \| yad ebhiḥ prekṣaṇam api tāsāṁ atidurghaṭam*

**The gopas simply thought these girls were their wives, with an appearance of attachment to them. In fact, they never even saw them.**

If Kṛṣṇa is their eternal husband and their being married to other cowherds is illusory, *parakīya* must be destroyed in the end. But if it is not destroyed by some magical mantra and is without beginning, then *parakīya* should be eternal. According to this logic, an improper manifestation of *rasa* will arise. Thus, in the later part of the Lord’s appearance on earth, the married condition becomes manifest. That is the final conclusion and will be proved by the explanations given in Lalita-mādhava.

Because it has been said that the conclusion is an eternal union, in order to resolve the separation which is plentifully described, the rasasin the sequence of pastimes on earth do not produce the perfection of ujjvala-rasa. Since this is dissatisfying, to counteract separation, an arrangement in the form of marriage in the undisputedly best form of *sambhoga, samṛddhimān,* among the four types of sambhoga (*saṅkṣipta, saṅkīṛṇa, sampanna*, and *samṛddhimān*), will be shown in the examples.

In the drama, it is pointed out that *upapati* only exists because of a belief in having *upapati* love. Even writers of materialrasa praise this type of *upapati* love with its condemned portion (since it is a belief in *upapati* only.) However in the case of Kṛṣṇa, it is not derided but rather praised because the rasa of *samṛddhi-sambhoga* is nourished when marriage takes place after separation and the illusion of being *upapati* ends. Thus the text says that the negative aspects of *upapati* do not apply to Kṛṣṇa. The reason is the intensity of rasa that it produces (*rasa-niryāsa*). But without this arrangement, when the hero is not Kṛṣṇa, where there is actual *upapati* occurring, the relationship is condemned (*laghutvam)*.” (end of argument and quotation)

One should not worry that the concept of the *upapati* is rejected by the author of *Sāhitya-darpaṇa.*

Since it has been shown by Jīva Gosvāmī that the love of the married woman and the paramour (Kṛṣṇa) is faultless and full of rasa, it is not proper to take the opinion of Sāhitya-darpaṇa alone and using false proof, say that parakīya love is rasābhāsa, out of fear of sin. The author of *Nāṭikā-candrikā* does not accept the opinions of *Sāhitya-darpaṇa.*  This is stated at the beginning of the work:

*nātīva-saṅgatatvād bharata-muner mata-virodhāc ca \|*  
*sāhitya-darpaṇīyā na gṛhītā prakriyā prāyaḥ \|\|*

**The principles mentioned in** ***Sāhitya-darpaṇa*** **have not been generally accepted in this work, since those opinions contradict Bharata Muni and are not generally consistent. Nāṭaka-candrikā 2**

"All this is written in the scriptures. However, since we are inundated in sin, we will take such faults as the goal of life. Kṛṣṇa killed Pūtanā while he was an infant. He killed Vatsāsura when he was in paugaṇḍa age. He killed Vṛṣāsura when he was in kaiśora age. He killed his maternal uncle Kaṁsa, brother of Devakī, and Śiśupāla and Dantavakra, his relatives. This is well known in Bhāgavatam. In Mahābhārata it is shown that Yudhiṣṭhira told a lie. Kṛṣṇa sided with him at all times. Using trickery he had Droṇācārya killed and gave up his promise not to fight. All these acts are faulty and sinful. One should not say that there is no fault in Kṛṣṇa killing them since Pūtanā and others were demons and that it is a good quality expressing affection for his devotees to protect cows, brāhmaṇas and children, for the benefit of all people. It is well known that there was great sin for Indra, incited by devatās, in killing the bull demon. If Indra, as a devatā, even if bewildered by Viṣṇu, committed such sin, this means there is additional sin for Kṛṣṇa in his human pastimes. Who will consider your words?"

Vyāsa and Śukadeva do not criticize these activities of Kṛṣṇa. Rather these activities are glorified. By hearing about these activities (such as killing Pūtanā) one attains great results. We desire to hear them. Moreover, what is the offense in Kṛṣṇa's pastimes as a paramour, which are famous in Purāṇas and agamas? One cannot utter any proof. By hearing these pastimes, both the speaker and listener attain prema-bhakti:

*vikrīḍitaṁ vraja-vadhūbhir idaṁ ca viṣṇoḥ*  
*śraddhānvito ’nuśṛṇuyād atha varṇayed yaḥ*  
*bhaktiṁ parāṁ bhagavati pratilabhya kāmaṁ*  
*hṛd-rogam āśv apahinoty acireṇa dhīraḥ*

**Anyone who faithfully and continually hears or describes the Lord’s playful affairs with the young gopīs of Vṛndāvana will attain the highest type of** ***prema*** **to the Lord, quickly destroy lust, the disease of the heart, and immediately become steady. SB 10.33.39"**

Someone may argue as follows. "But even if this is so, Nirukti says *uttama-prakṛti-prāyo rasaḥ śṛṅgāra iṣyate*: śṛṅgāra rasa is considered the rasa with the highest nature. This rasa is also called *ujjvala* or pure. Thus it is impossible to include within in it paramour or parakīya love which is impure. The material paramour is defined as most sinful (*pāpa-pati*) in Trikāṇḍa-śeṣa. Furthermore that love is condemned in Bhāgavatam:

*asvargyam ayaśasyaṁ ca phalgu kṛcchraṁ bhayāvaham*  
*jugupsitaṁ ca sarvatra hy aupapatyaṁ kula-striyaḥ*

**For a woman from a respectable family, petty adulterous affairs are always condemned. They bar her from heaven, ruin her reputation and bring her difficulty and fear. SB 10.29.26**

The gopīs condemn such love:

*niḥsvaṁ tyajanti gaṇikā akalpaṁ nṛpatiṁ prajāḥ*  
*adhīta-vidyā ācāryam ṛtvijo datta-dakṣiṇam*

**Prostitutes abandon a penniless man, subjects an incompetent king, students their teacher once they have finished their education, and priests a man who has remunerated them for a sacrifice. SB 10.47.7**

*khagā vīta-phalaṁ vṛkṣaṁ bhuktvā cātithayo gṛham*  
*dagdhaṁ mṛgās tathāraṇyaṁ jārā bhuktvā ratāṁ striyam*

**Birds abandon a tree when its fruits are gone, guests a house after they have eaten, animals a forest that has burnt down, and lovers the woman they have enjoyed, even though she remains attached to them. SB 10.47.8**

Parīkṣit says such acts are low or sinful (*jugupsitam*):

*āpta-kāmo yadu-patiḥ kṛtavān vai jugupsitam*  
*kim-abhiprāya etan naḥ śaṁśayaṁ chindhi su-vrata*

**O faithful upholder of vows, please destroy our doubt by explaining to us what purpose the self-satisfied Lord of the Yadus had in mind when he behaved so contemptibly. SB 10.33.28**

If Kṛṣṇa appears in this world to taste this rasa as a paramour, it is filled with māyā. Since we have a peaceful nature, we cannot hear things related to paramour love."

According to you, there can be rasa only in śānta-rasa. The other rasas should not be heard since they are the cause of condemned sinful life. Śṛṅgāra-rasa is not to be tasted. In describing Kṛṣṇa as the dhṛṣṭa lover it is said:

*nakhāṅkā na śyāme ghana-ghusṛṇa-rekhā-tatir iyaṁ*  
*na lākṣāntaḥ-krure paricinu girer gairikam idam \|*  
*dhiyaṁ dhatse citraṁ vata mṛgamade’py añjanatayā*  
*taruṇyās te dṛṣṭiḥ kim iva viparīta-sthitir abhūt \|\|*

**O Śyāmā! These are not the marks of a woman’s nails on my body. They are marks of thick kuṁkuma. O cruel hearted woman! Do you not know about lac dye? This is red chalk from the mountain. How amazing ! You mistake musk for a woman’s eye ointment. Your immature eyes seen to have produced an opposite situation. Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 1.41**

You may cover the meaning because you cannot tolerate the marks made by the gopīs on Kṛṣṇa's limbs by saying instead that he produced the marks on his body. This reveals indifference to the rasas revealed in this verse.

Of course, the gopīs do not actually do this. It is said:

*at te sujāta-caraṇāmburuhaṁ staneṣu*  
*bhītāḥ śanaiḥ priya dadhīmahi karkaśeṣu*  
*tenāṭavīm aṭasi tad vyathate na kiṁ svit*  
*kūrpādibhir bhramati dhīr bhavad-āyuṣāṁ naḥ*

**O dearly beloved! Your lotus feet are so soft that we place them gently on our breasts, fearing that your feet will be hurt. Our life rests only in You. Our minds, therefore, are filled with anxiety that your tender feet might be wounded by pebbles as you roam about on the forest path. SB 10.31.19**

Instead, they touch his limbs in a joking way with their nails using gentle gestures. These imitations of nail marks that appear with color are not considered to be the same as those inflicted by adversarial women. This is how the poets describe it.

One should not worry that parakīya love is criticized in Bhāgavatam. Acting like others in the material world, Kṛṣṇa tested the gopīs by saying that they would not go to Svarga since the gopīs had committed sin by rejecting their husbands for a paramour. The gopīs say that just as a paramour gives up women he has enjoyed, they had been enjoyed and finally given up by a paramour. Parīkṣit also indicates this type paramour love when he asks about touching other men's wives.

Śukadeva replies:

*dharma-vyatikramo dṛṣṭa īśvarāṇāṁ ca sāhasam*  
*tejīyasāṁ na doṣāya vahneḥ sarva-bhujo yathā*

**The status of powerful controllers is not harmed by any apparently audacious transgression of morality we may see in them, for they are just like fire, which devours everything fed into it and remains unpolluted. SB 10.33.29**

Thus Śukadeva also accepts Kṛṣṇa touching other women using the example of fire (to show that his act is pure). This description is also given in Hari-vaṁśa and Viṣṇu Purāṇa. You may ask me to give proofs for this love being without fault, without the sin of adharma. Please listen. After Parīkṣit asked his question, Śukadeva began with the above verse. Then he said:

*kuśalācaritenaiṣām iha svārtho na vidyate*  
*viparyayeṇa vānartho nirahaṅkāriṇāṁ prabho*

**O Parīkṣit, you can understand! When these great persons who are free from false ego act piously in this world, they have no selfish motives to fulfill, and even when they act in apparent contradiction to the laws of piety, they are not subject to sinful reactions. SB 10.33.32**

*kim utākhila-sattvānāṁ tiryaṅ-martya-divaukasām*  
*īśituś ceśitavyānāṁ kuśalākuśalānvayaḥ*

**How, then, could the Lord of all created beings—animals, men and devatās—have any connection with the piety and impiety that affect his creations? SB 10. 33.33**

*yat-pāda-paṅkaja-parāga-niṣeva-tṛptā*  
*yoga-prabhāva-vidhutākhila-karma-bandhāḥ*  
*svairaṁ caranti munayo ’pi na nahyamānās*  
*tasyecchayātta-vapuṣaḥ kuta eva bandhaḥ*

**The devotees, filled with prema, continually meditating on the particles of beauty emanating from his lotus feet, having destroyed all karma by the power of his bhakti-yoga, wander about performing actions without accruing bondage. So how could there be any question of bondage for the Lord himself, who assumes his forms according to his own sweet will? SB 10.33.34**

Having said this, at the end of the chapter he says:

*vikrīḍitaṁ vraja-vadhūbhir idaṁ ca viṣṇoḥ*  
*śraddhānvito ’nuśṛṇuyād atha varṇayed yaḥ*  
*bhaktiṁ parāṁ bhagavati pratilabhya kāmaṁ*  
*hṛd-rogam āśv apahinoty acireṇa dhīraḥ*

**Anyone who faithfully and continually hears or describes the Lord’s playful affairs with the young gopīs of Vṛndāvana will attain the highest type of prema to the Lord, quickly destroy lust, the disease of the heart, and immediately become steady. SB 10.33.39**

By hearing this topic or by reciting it, even if one has lust in the heart, the person will attain bhakti in the form of prema (*parām bhaktim*) for the Lord. If this is so for the devotee, what to speak of the position of the Lord? If the parakīya affairs are of this great nature, they cannot have a touch of fault. This is concluded by those who know the truth. After hearing, all devoted persons should have the same result. This is the rule.

Moreover, in that royal assembly after the questions of Parīkṣit, Śukadeva transformed Vyāsa, Nārada and other who were gathered like the planets, constellations and stars into cakora birds by filling them with the nectar of siddhānta.

In commenting on the verse *gopīnāṁ tat-patīnāṁ ca*, concerning attractive pastimes, Jīva Gosvamī has confirmed that recent ideas are wrong. By other proofs, Jīva Gosvāmī has very strongly refuted all false views for persons not aware of their foolishness.

In Bhakti Sandarbha 320 he says:

*kāmād dveṣād bhayāt snehād yathā bhaktyeśvare manaḥ*  
*āveśya tad-aghaṁ hitvā bahavas tad-gatiṁ gatāḥ*

**Just as by vaidhī bhaktione can attain one’s spiritual goals*****,***  **many persons have attained suitable forms after asorbing their minds in the Lord out of lust, hatred, fear, and family relationships filled with affection, and after giving up absorption in enmity of the Lord (in the case of hatred and fear). SB 7.1.30**

Just as they attain the goal by absorbing the mind in the Lord by bhakti according to rules, many attain the goal by kāma and other bhāvas which are not according to the rules. They gave up hatred and fear (*tad-agham*) among the various bhāvas. Because fear is connected with hatred it also produces sin. Some consider kāma to be a sin also. The following should be considered. Is kāma for the Lord itself sinful, or is it sinful if kāma means to think of Kṛṣṇa as a husband? Or is it only sinful if one thinks of Kṛṣṇa as one’s illicit lover?

If kāma for the Lord is itself sinful, is it because it is classed with hatred and other qualities, being sinful by its nature? Or is it sinful because one thinks of Kṛṣna as a lusty person or kisses him, though he is most pure? Or is kāma sinful because kāma is said to be a sin in scriptures?

It cannot be sinful by nature, being classed with hatred, anger etc. because it is said:

*uktaṁ purastād etat te caidyaḥ siddhiṁ yathā gataḥ*  
*dviṣann api hṛṣīkeśaṁ kim utādhokṣaja-priyāḥ*

**This point was explained to you previously. Since even Śiśupāla, who hated Kṛṣṇa, again achieved his spiritual body as an associate by thinking of him, then what to speak of the Lord’s dear gopīs. SB 10.29.13**

In this verse, hatred is criticized and kāma is praised. These persons are called *priyāḥ.* The gopīs are not at fault because kāma is essentially filled with prīti or affection. The gopīs’ kāma is actually prema only.

*yat te sujāta-caraṇāmburuhaṁ staneṣu*  
*bhītāḥ śanaiḥ priya dadhīmahi karkaśeṣu*  
*tenāṭavīm aṭasi tad vyathate na kiṁ svit*  
*kūrpādibhir bhramati dhīr bhavad-āyuṣāṁ naḥ*

**O dearly beloved! Your lotus feet are so soft that we place them gently on our breasts, fearing that your feet will be hurt. Our life rests only in you. Our minds, therefore, are filled with anxiety that your tender feet might be wounded by pebbles as you roam about on the forest path. SB 10.31.19**

They have no desire for their own happiness but think only of what will make Kṛṣṇa happy. The love of Kubjā however is criticized since it lacks the gopīs’ pure intention of pleasing Kṛṣṇa alone. Kubjā was predominantly desirous of her own enjoyment. That is made clear when Śukadeva praises her love for its effects (purifying her of lust):

*sānaṅga-tapta-kucayor urasas tathākṣṇor*  
*jighranty ananta-caraṇena rujo mṛjantī*  
*dorbhyāṁ stanāntara-gataṁ parirabhya kāntam*  
*ānanda-mūrtim ajahād ati-dīrgha-tāpam*

**Simply by smelling the fragrance of Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet, Trivakrā cleansed away the burning desire Cupid had aroused in her breasts, chest and eyes. With her two arms she embraced between her breasts her lover, Kṛṣṇa, the personification of bliss, and thus she gave up her long-standing distress. SB 10.48.7**

Prīti finally manifested in her:

*sahoṣyatām iha preṣṭha dināni katicin mayā*  
*ramasva notsahe tyaktuṁ saṅgaṁ te ’mburuhekṣaṇa*

**O beloved, please stay here with me for a few days more and enjoy. I cannot bear to give up your association, O lotus-eyed one! SB 10.48.9**

Thus kāma is not to be classed with hatred or anger. Because of that, kāma (of the gopīs) cannot be considered sinful.

Now, the behavior of Kṛṣṇa being a lusty person involved in kissing cannot considered sinful, for the Lord’s pastimes are accomplished naturally with these acts (not under ignorance). *Lokavat tu līlā-kaivalyam*: the Lord’s pastimes are similar to actions in this world. (Brahma-sūtra 2.1.33)

The Lord has similar pastimes of love with Śrī and Bhū in Vaikuṇṭha. Since the pastimes are eternal there, they should be understood to occur because those pastimes delight the Lord, who enjoys pastimes independently. Kissing the Lord is not unsuitable since the consorts have the purest forms, not less than the Lord’s form, since they are forms of his svarūpa-śakti. And as previously stated, the Lord enjoys this. And one should not find fault with the women, thinking they are material women. Only after attaining a suitable bhāva and a form arising from his svarūpa-śakti, do these women attain him by his will.

Is kāma sinful because of scriptural statements? The kāma of the consorts is not sinful since it is never stated as such in scriptures. And thus women having the mood that the Lord should be the husband is not a fault. Rather it is praised in scriptures.

*yāḥ samparyacaran premṇā pāda-saṁvāhanādibhiḥ*  
*jagad-guruṁ bhartṛ-buddhyā tāsāṁ kim varṇyate tapaḥ*

**And how could one possibly describe the greatness of the worship performed by other women, who serve the Supreme Lord, thinking of him as their husband, by mentally massaging his feet. SB 10.90.27**

It is also recorded that great sages had this bhāva. Madhvācārya quotes the following from Kūrma Purāṇa:

*agni-putrā mahātmānas tapasā strītvam āpire \|*  
*bhartāraṁ ca jagad-yoniṁ vāsudevam ajaṁ vibhum \|\|*

**The saintly sons of Agni attained bodies of women by the path of vaidhi-bhakti*****,*** **and attained as their husband, the unborn, powerful Vāsudeva, source of the universe.**

Such persons are revered in statements like the following:

*pati-putra-suhṛd-bhrātṛ-pitṛvan mātravad dharim \|*  
*ye dhyāyanti sadodyuktās tebhyo’pīha namo namaḥ \|\|*

**I pay my respects repeatedly to those who constantly and eagerly meditate upon the Lord as their husband, son, well-wisher, brother, or the meditator as father or mother. Nārāyaṇa-vyūha-stava**

Approaching the Lord as an illicit lover is also not sinful. The gopīs say:

*yat paty-apatya-suhṛdām anuvṛttir aṅga*  
*strīṇāṁ sva-dharma iti dharma-vidā tvayoktam*  
*astv evam etad upadeśa-pade tvayīśe*  
*preṣṭho bhavāṁs tanu-bhṛtāṁ kila bandhur ātmā*

**Our dear Kṛṣṇa, as an expert in religion you have advised us that the proper religious duty for women is to faithfully serve their husbands, children and other relatives. We agree that this principle is valid, but actually this service should be rendered to you who give instructions. After all, O Lord, you are the dearest friend of all embodied souls. You are their most intimate friend and their very self. SB 10.29.32**

Śukadeva says:

*gopīnāṁ tat-patīnāṁ ca sarveṣām eva dehinām*  
*yo ’ntaś carati so ’dhyakṣaḥ krīḍaneneha deha-bhāk*

**Kṛṣṇa, appearing directly in this world, moves among the gopīs and their apparent husbands as well as the other inhabitants of Vraja, in his eternal spiritual body performing his pastimes. SB 10.33.35**

The Lord himself says that the gopīs’ service is flawless:

*na pāraye ’haṁ niravadya-saṁyujāṁ*  
*sva-sādhu-kṛtyaṁ vibudhāyuṣāpi vaḥ*  
*yā mābhajan durjara-geha-śṛṅkhalāḥ*  
*saṁvṛścya tad vaḥ pratiyātu sādhunā*

**I am not able to repay my debt for your spotless service, even within a lifetime of Brahmā. Your connection with me is beyond reproach. You have worshiped me, cutting off all domestic ties, which are difficult to break. Therefore please let your own glorious deeds be your compensation. SB 10.32.22**

This bhāva is seen in others also.

*purā maharṣayaḥ sarve daṇḍakāraṇya-vāsinaḥ \|*  
*dṛṣṭvā rāmaṁ hariṁ tatra bhoktum aicchat suvigraham \|\|*  
*te sarve strītvam āpannāḥ samudbhūtās tu gokule \|*  
*hariṁ samprāpya kāmena tato muktā bhavārṇavāt \|\|*

**Previously, all the sages living in Daṇḍakāraṇya forest, who after seeing Lord Rāma, desired enjoyment with His form, attained forms of women and appeared in Gokula. Attaining the Lord by that kāma, they became liberated from the ocean of the material world. Padma Purāṇa 6.245.164**

Since this mood of being a woman arises even in them, and is directed to the Lord, it does not arise from material Cupid and it is not material lust.

*tāsām āvirabhūc chauriḥ smayamāna-mukhāmbujaḥ*  
*pītāmbara-dharaḥ sragvī sākṣān manmatha-manmathaḥ*

**Then Kṛṣṇa, a smile on his lotus face, appeared before the gopīs, wearing a garland and a yellow garment. He directly appeared as one who can bewilder the mind of Cupid, though Cupid himself bewilders the minds of ordinary people. SB 10.32.2**

Since the Lord is worshipped in the āgamas as Cupid, this kāma is spiritual, manifested in people by the Lord himself. Great devotees like Uddhava praise this kāma:

*etāḥ paraṁ tanu-bhṛto bhuvi gopa-vadhvo*  
*govinda eva nikhilātmani rūḍha-bhāvāḥ*  
*vāñchanti yad bhava-bhiyo munayo vayaṁ ca*  
*kiṁ brahma-janmabhir ananta-kathā-rasasya*

**Among all persons on earth, these cowherd women alone have attained prefect bodies, for they have achievedmahābhāva for Govinda, the source of all forms of the Lord. Their pure love is desired by those who fear material existence, by great sages, and by ourselves as well. For one who has tasted the narrations of the infinite Lord, what is the use of taking birth as a high-class brāhmaṇa, or even as Brahmā himself? SB 10.47.58**

What is more, it is known from Bṛhad-vāmana Purāṇa that the śrutis had this type of bhāva. It is well known that because the śrutis desired the bhāva of the nitya-siddha gopīs, they became included among them with gopī forms. The śrutis indicate this in the following:

*nibhṛta-marun-mano-’kṣa-dṛḍha-yoga-yujo hṛdi yan*  
*munaya upāsate tad arayo ’pi yayuḥ smaraṇāt*  
*striya uragendra-bhoga-bhuja-daṇḍa-viṣakta-dhiyo*  
*vayam api te samāḥ sama-dṛśo ’ṅghri-saroja-sudhāḥ*

**Simply by constantly thinking of him, the enemies of the Lord attained the same Supreme Truth whom sages fixed in yoga worship by controlling their breath, mind and senses. Similarly, we śrutis, who generally see you as all-pervading, will achieve the same nectar from your lotus feet that your consorts are able to relish because of their loving attraction to your mighty, serpentine arms, for you look upon us and your consorts in the same way. SB 10.87.23**

The meaning is clear. The entity called Brahman which the sages worship with great effort by scriptural methods was attained by the Lord’s enemies simply by remembering him without worship. The gopīs (*te*) who attached their minds to your two snake-like two arms attained the nectar of your lotus feet, the sweetness of prema generated by your touch. We śrutis, having the same bhāva (*sama-dṛśaḥ*), having attained equality (*samāḥ*) with the gopīs, also attained that sweetness of your feet.

According to the context the verb *yayuḥ* should be conjugated in the first person in reference to the śrutis (*yayima* instead of *yayuh*: we attained). The word *aṅghri* (feet) indicates respect. The enemies attained the Lord quickly by remembering him. This indicates that the path of bhāva quickly yields results. Having the same bhāva *(sama-dṛśaḥ*) indicates rāgānuga, and that it is the most effective method. Otherwise the śrutis, who knew all types of sādhanas and sādyas, would have undertaken a different process.

The conclusion is that the śrutis praise the gopīs and desire to follow after them.

"Your conclusion should have a real meaning if the paramour and married woman were real. We raise other points which will act as an axe to cut the root of your proposition. In the whole of Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi, starting with 1.10 (*patiś copapatiś ca*), Jīva Gosvāmī repeatedly explains that the married form (pati) of Kṛṣṇa exists with the women in Dvārakā and the unmarried form exists with the women in Vraja. The women of Vraja manifest when Kṛṣṇa appears on earth, but not at all times:

*rāgeṇollaṅghayan dharmaṁ parakīya-balārthinā*  
*tadīya-prema-vasatir budhair uapapatiḥ smṛtaḥ*

**The wise define the** ***upapati*** **or paramour as the man who has love for the wife of another person, while transgressing dharma because of attraction for her. Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 1.17**

Absorbed in his pastimes when he comes as avatāra, controlled by the prema of his devotees, not conscious of his eternal pastimes, in order to nourish their special prema by illusion, the līlā-śakti encourages the parakīya mood in them and makes Kṛṣṇa appear as the paramour. That is the summary of his statement. Rūpa Gosvamī gives an example of *upapati* by quoting a verse written by some poet from *Padyāvalī.* It is merely an illustration to show the concept of the *upapati.* Something should be said about this verse:

*laghutvamatra yat proktaṁ tat tu prākrṭa-nayake*  
*na kṛṣṇe rasa-niryāsa-svādārtham avatārini*

**The criticism of the material paramour by the author does not apply to Kṛṣṇa, who appeared in this world to taste the thick madhura-rasa as** ***upapati.*** **Ujjvala -nīlamaṇi 1.21**

What has been stated is this. Because such a situation is unsuitable, the perception of their having parakīya relationship when he appears as avatāra is not actual. It disappears.

*kanyakāś ca paroḍhāś ca parakīyā dvidhā matāḥ \|*  
*vrajeśa-vrajavāsinyah etā prāyeṇa viśrutāḥ \|*  
*pracchanna-kāmatā hy atra gokulendrasya saukhyatā \|\|*

**The parakīyā women are of two types: unmarried to other men and married to other men. This generally refers to the women living in Vraja. Their love is concealed. They give happiness to Kṛṣṇa. Ujjvala -nīlamaṇi 3.19**

This is the conventional view by seeing the pastimes on earth, but is not the actual situation. In commenting on Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 3. 33 he says:

By Kṛṣṇa’s māyā each of the husbands through marriage, thinking that their wives, actually made of māyā, were by their sides, did not hate Kṛṣṇa. This means that whenever there was the need of the gopīs breaking the vow of having one husband, Kṛṣṇa revealed forms of māyāto the cowherd men and hid the real forms.

Commenting on Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 3.37 he says:

As was explained previously the idea of their marriages to the cowherds were actually illusory. Using his līlā-śakti, his eternal consorts mistakenly thought that they were married to other men. They desire this for rasa. Inspired by the līlā-śakti, thinking themselves married, they believed they were associating with another man.

Commenting on Lalita-mādhava in Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 15.208, he says:

After everyone understood that they had attraction for Kṛṣṇa, if it were to be revealed that they falsely believed they were the wives of others, it would produce disgust. It should be established according to rites of dharma in the assembly of righteous Nanda Mahārāja. Everything is resolved by yogamāyā. She made arrangements so that the husbands would not hate Kṛṣṇa.

This is considered in many places in Kṛṣṇa Sandarbha and Gopāla-campū:

They thought their wives were by their sides but these wives were made by māyā in order avoid the event that the wives of the Lord, with spiritual bodies, would be oppressed by another person. Kṛṣṇa Sandarbha 175

The spiritual forms were concealed when getting married and sleeping with their apparent husbands. This happened at other times in relation to others also. This was suitable for māyā since her only goal was to increase longing and protect their morality. Kṛṣṇa Sandarbha 175

In Gopāla-campū, previously to the words spoken by the form of Paurṇamāsī riding on a lion (Viṣṇu-māyā) there were duplicate forms. This should not be considered surprising. The second forms made of māyā were very similar to the original forms since they were constructed by māyā with particular rati and consciousness.

Uddhava described the gopīs to Nanda previously, but these must have been the gopīs made of māyā:

*nāyaṁ śriyo ’ṅga u nitānta-rateḥ prasādaḥ*  
*svar-yoṣitāṁ nalina-gandha-rucāṁ kuto ’nyāḥ*  
*rāsotsave ’sya bhuja-daṇḍa-gṛhīta-kaṇṭha-*  
*labdhāśiṣāṁ ya udagād vraja-sundarīṇām*

**When Kṛṣṇa was dancing with thegopīs in the rāsa-līlā, the gopīs were embraced by the arms of the Lord. This favor was never bestowed upon the goddess of fortune, though she was intensely attracted to him, or to other consorts in the spiritual world whose bodily luster and aroma resemble the lotus flower. In samṛddhimat described in Lalita-mādhava, an example of marriage is given.

*sakhyas tā militā nisarga-madhurāḥ premābhirāmī-kṛtā*  
*yāmīyaṁ samagaṁsta saṁstaravatī śvaśrūś ca goṣṭheśvarī \|*  
*vṛndāraṇya-nikuñja-dhāmni bhavatā saṅgo’py ayaṁ raṅgavān*  
*saṁvṛttaḥ kim ataḥ paraṁ priyataraṁ kartavyam atrāsti me \|\|*

**In this forest of Vṛndāvana I have found my beautiful, charming, and affectionate gopī friends, I have found my sister Candrāvalī, and all her friends. I have obtained Yaśodā the queen of Vraja as my mother-in-law, and I have also attained your company, which brings me the greatest happiness. What can be more pleasing to me than all these things?**

This verse is used support your view concerning svakīya love. However this is done without proper consideration. The commentary which closely reflects the essence of the pastimes explains the real meaning. (This is Viśvanātha's commentary on Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 15.206)

*durlabhālokayor yūnoḥ pāratantryād viyuktayoḥ \|*  
*upabhogātireko yaḥ kīrtyate sa samṛddhimān \|\|*

**The great enjoyment of lovers uniting, who have been separated and who find it most difficult to meet because of others’ control is called** ***samṛddhimat.***  **Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 15.206**

The meaning is this. The great enjoyment in meeting which is experienced by a hero and heroine who are separated because of great distance and who can see each other only with great difficulty because of others’ control is called *samṛddhimat sambhoga*. In the other *sambhogas* the cause of not seeing each other is not both parties being under others’ control. Rather only one of them, the woman, is under the control of the mother-in-law, husband and elders, who prevent her from meeting her lover. Kṛṣṇa is not prevented. Though he is dependent on his parents, he is not prevented by his parents to meet the gopīs. The gopīs however, though prevented by apparent husbands and mothers-in-law, are not dependent on them. But according to the story told in Lalita-mādhava both Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are dependent and prevented by Rukmiṇī:

*dagdhaṁ hanta dadhānayā vapur idaṁ yasyāvalokāśayā*  
*soḍhā marma-vipāṭane paṭur iyaṁ pīḍātivṛṣṭir mayā \|*  
*kālindīya-taṭī-kuṭīra-kuhara-krīḍābhisāra-vratī*  
*so’yaṁ jīvita-bandhur indu-vadane bhūyaḥ samāliṅgitaḥ \|\|*

**O Nava-vṛndā! Having maintained this body burned up by the desire to see him and tolerating an intense shower of sharp pain which destroys my vital organs, I again am embraced by this friend of my life who is intent on meeting for pastimes in the bower on the bank of the Yamunā.**

*tavātra parimṛgyatā kim api lakṣma sākṣād iyaṁ*  
*mayā tvam upasāditā nikhila-loka-lakṣmīr asi \|*  
*yathā jagati cañcatā caṇaka-muṣṭi-sampattaye*  
*janena patitā puraḥ kanaka-vṛṣṭir āsādyate \|\|*

**I have been searching for some sign of you, and now I have found you. I worship you, the goddess of fortune, who reigns over all worlds. You are a shower of gold raining upon a person who has fallen in the universe and is wandering about for a treasure of chick-peas. Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 15.207-208**

Since for both parties meeting is rare, caused by others preventing and being dependent, the enjoyment is greater. One should see the greater enjoyment in the story related in Lalita-mādhava. The word *paratantryāt* in this verse means that the inability to see each other is caused by others factors, beyond their control. This excludes separation and difficulty in seeing each other caused by *māna* (which is caused by oneself).

The word *pāratantryāt* in the ablative cannot mean “without dependence on others” since the two examples following in the text do not show absence of dependence on others, but rather show complete dependence on other factors for not seeing each other. It is seen in that story that Navavṛndā, who had made an oath, cannot tell the secret of Rādhā’s identity.

It is also not suitable to say that because of being under others’ control that they were separated (rather than finding it difficult to see each other). **That love which causes great opposition because of being against normal dharma, which necessitates the love being kept secret, and which makes it hard for the lovers to meet is the highest type of love.**

“One cannot say that the intensity of rasa decreased when marriage manifested and all obstacles ceased, and that, on the other hand, his consorting with other's wives caused secret desire and created a natural obstacle (an increase in rasa), since mothers never desired their daughters association with Kṛṣṇa. Obstacles, according to Bharata Muni, create greater rasa. But there are obstacles in marriage life as well, caused by mothers, mistresses etc. This will give raise to greater rasa."

Why do you raise unreasonable arguments? The word *ādi* refers to fathers, uncles, their wives, mothers, sisters, who oppose out of a little affection, and cowives out of rivalry (of the married gopīs). There is delight in rasa by prevention of parents who are absorbed in vātsalya-rasa. This is accepted by the tasters and teachers of Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu. There is rasa created by obstacles in the form of Kṛṣṇa’s shyness and the gopīs' parents. Other obstacles are not seen anywhere by anyone in Bhāgavatam or the bhakti literatures.

*"sa vo hi svāmī bhavati*

**Kṛṣṇa is your master. Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad**

*ārya-putro’dhunās te*

**He is your husband. SB 10.47.21**

*gopīnāṁ patiḥ*

**He is the husband of the gopīs. Bṛhad-gautamīya-tantra**

These are proofs that Kṛṣṇa married the gopīs. Why are these not proofs?"

In Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad, starting with *ekadhā vraja-striyaḥ* in the second part of the book, by taking the initial and concluding statements, by seeing the gopīs' hidden desire, the phrase *svāmī bhavati* has characteristics of an enjoyer who has power. Durvāsā says, "Kṛṣṇa is the *svāmī* or enjoyer of you gopīs with beauty and youth, but not your husband." That is the intention of Durvāsā's statement. One cannot say that this refers to the later marriage with the gopīs described in some places after Kṛṣṇa killed Dantavakra. Durvāsā speaks in the present tense, not the future. Nor can one say that he refers in his mind to their eternal marriage since there is no proof that they did marry Kṛṣṇa. And it is confirmed by previous logic and statements that in his eternal avatāra form Kṛṣṇa consorted with the gopīs married to other cowherds.

In the phrase *ārya-putra* the word *ārya* refers to Nanda for his honorable nature. (Kṛṣṇa is the son of honorable Nanda.) That is the meaning according to the context. One cannot say that it refers to his position as a husband (*ārya-putra* also means a husband). In the context, when the gopīs speak of Kṛṣṇa leaving them with their husbands, it is clear that that is the easiest meaning. And there is no other proof of that marriage taking place in the future.

*Gopīnāṁ patir eva vā* has the same meaning. *Pati* can mean a husband or a paramour. It is like saying "Devatās are worthy of worship." This can refer to devatās or upadevatās. Also the word *gopīnām* can also mean "wives of cowherds" rather than just cowherd women, according to the rule *puṁ-yogād ākhyāyām*. (Pāniṇi 4.1.48) This being so, fault in the opposing argument is unavoidable. The proposed meaning also results in disgust. One cannot argue that the word *gopī* means only a cowherd woman since the sūtra gives the exceptional case. In Gautamīya-tantra Kṛṣṇa says *atra yā gopa-patnyaś ca*: those who are the wives of cowherds. This confirms that the gopīs had husbands. If it were not so, it could not be said that for disobeying orders they would fall to hell.

The commentary on Siddhānta-kaumudī, a summary for the less intelligent, says that the word *gopī* means "wife of a cowherd." Gautamīya-tantra, in the Gopāla-stava, says *gopa-nārībhir āvrṭam*: he was surrounded by the women belonging to cowherds. Also the phrase *gopāṅganā-vastra-hāriṇam* (the clothes of the women belonging to cowherds) indicates that the gopīs were married. Only the ignorant will claim svakīya and ascribe faults to parakīya.

In Bhakti Sandarbha, in the section on rāgānuga, in Bhagavat Sandarbha, in Kṛṣṇa Sandarbha, and in Gopāla-campū, parakīya is supported since it is repeatedly confirmed to be present in both the prakaṭa and aprakaṭa pastimes. After the killing of Dantavakra, in the commentary on the Tenth Canto, Jīva Gosvāmī also indicates that the aprakaṭa and prakaṭa pastimes are one. In the commentary on *laghutavam atra yat proktam* in Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi, Jīva Gosvāmī reveals his intentions by saying that the part on parakīya was written by his own desire and the part on svakīya was written by the desire of others. This should be taken to refer not only to his commentary on Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi but to his other works as well. The explanations which are consistent (*pūrvāpara-sambaddham*) were written by his desire and the other explanations (*param*) were written by the desire of others. At the end of Gopāla-campū he says:

*prāyaḥ sarvā harer līlāḥ kramaśaḥ sūcitā mayā \|*  
*yathā svaṁ labdha-rucibhir āsvādyantāṁ mahātmabhiḥ \|\|*

**I have indicated in order all the pastimes of Kṛṣṇa. The great devotees should enjoy them according to their individual taste.**

Commenting on Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 3.17 Jīva Gosvāmī says:

Those who offer themselves out of inner attraction (*rāgena*), not out of external arrangements of marriage according to dharma, are called parakīya. Having mentioned their union, they are further described. They do not accept a man by marriage according to dharma. This means they accept him by attraction.

In the section on rati it is said:

*sādhāraṇī nigaditā samañjasāsau samartha ca*  
*kubjādiṣu mahiṣīṣu ca gokula-devīṣu ca kramataḥ*

**This rati has been described as sādhāraṇī in Kubjā, samañjasā in the queens of Dvārakā and samarthā in the women of Vraja. These ratis may be compared to a jewel, a cintāmaṇi and a Kaustubha, which are not attained easily, attained rarely, and most rarely attained (only by the gopīs.) Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 14.43**

The qualities of these three types can be understood from the meaning of the words (ordinary, fit, strong). Samartha-rati is that which surpasses all dharma and public opinion and attains the highest level. This is the quality of parakīyā love as described previously.

*rāgeṇaivārpitātmāno loka-yugmānapekṣiṇā \|*  
*dharmeṇāsvīkṛtā yās tu parakīyā bhavanti tāḥ \|\|*

**Those women, who out of great attraction, ignoring their fate in this world and the next, not accepting the rites of marriage, offer themselves to men, are called parakīya women.** ***Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*** **3.17**

*iyam eva ratiḥ prauḍhā mahābhāva-daśāṁ vrajet \|*  
*yā mṛgyā syād vimuktānāṁ bhaktānāṁ ca varīyasām \|\|57\|\|*

***Samañjasa-rati*** **has regard for** ***dharma*** **and public, and thus is not so strong. It does not reach the highest limits of emotion. (end of quotation)**

Describing samañjasā rati it is said:

*patnī-bhāvābhimānātmā guṇādi-śravaṇādijā \|*  
*kvacid bhedita-sambhoga-tṛṣṇā sāndrā samañjasā \|\|*

***Rati*** **arising from hearing about Kṛṣṇa’s qualities and from, with an identity as his wife, sometimes showing distinct desire for enjoyment, but which always remains condensed, is called** ***samañjasa-rati.***  **Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 14.48**

Jīva Gosvāmī says:

Since they identify themselves as wives, they have respect fordharma and public opinion. It is not caused by hearing about the Lord’s qualities, but makes its appearance by this means. The word *jā* means to appear.

It is clear that since they identify themselves as a wife, unlike the samartha woman, they are not subject to criticism (but the love is not as intense as that of the samartha woman with parakīya love).

Mahābhāva is described:

*anurāgaḥ svasaṁvedya-daśāṁ prāpya prakāśitaḥ \|*  
*yāvad-āśraya-vṛttiś ced bhāva ity abhidhīyate \|\|*

**When anurāga attains state of being perceived by itself, is revealed externally through sāttvika-bhāvas, takes shelter of the highest rāga, and spread its influence in all devotees, it is called bhāva. Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 14.154**

Rāga is defined as follows:

*duḥkham apy adhikaṁ citte sukhatvenaiva rajyate (vyajyate)\|*  
*yatas tu praṇayotkarṣāt sa rāga iti kīrtyate \|\|*

**When praṇaya reaches a high state and suffering is removed by happiness in the heart (on meeting Kṛṣṇa), it is called rāga.** **Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 14.126**

The height of suffering is destruction of high respect of the chaste women by their own elders, not fire or death. Though this is perceived as accomplished, the relation with Kṛṣṇa produces happiness. This is the highest level of rāga. Anurāga, taking shelter of this height of rāga, becomes bhāva. This is seen in the women of Vraja with newness. It is not possible in the queens of Dvārakā. The word *nava* in the next verse indicates that newness in the Vraja women. Speaking of them Uddhava says in astonishment:

*āsām aho caraṇa-reṇu-juṣām ahaṁ syāṁ*  
*vṛndāvane kim api gulma-latauṣadhīnām*  
*yā dustyajaṁ sva-janam ārya-pathaṁ ca hitvā*  
*bhejur mukunda-padavīṁ śrutibhir vimṛgyām*

**The gopīs of Vṛndāvana have given up the association of their husbands, sons and other family members, who are very difficult to give up, and they have forsaken the path of chastity to take shelter of the lotus feet of Mukunda, Kṛṣṇa, which one should search for by Vedic knowledge. Oh, let me be fortunate enough to be one of the bushes, creepers or herbs in Vṛndāvana, because the** ***gopīs*** **trample them and bless them with the dust of their lotus feet. SB 10.47.61**

Though it was impossible to give up their families, they did so. This indicates the high sense of propriety in the women of Vraja. Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi 14.154 commentary

Thus samarthā rati ascends to anurāga and then to mahābhāva. This is the svarūpa of mahābhāva as explained by the author, revealing his heart.

Śrī-rādhākā (the embodiment of mahābhāva) is the very form of the ālambana of rasa. That being so, she is definitely to be worshipped. Rasa depends on her. Without her support, all other elements of rasa will be uprooted because of deformity in the ālambana and distaste.

Moreover in Gopāla-campū there are descriptions everywhere of parakīya. Particularly from the twenty-sixth to the thirtieth chapter, this is continually described in detail according to what is narrated in Bhāgavatam. Particularly in Jīva Gosvāmī’s description of samṛddhimat at the end of the seven verses describing it in Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi, the intention of the whole commentary, which clearly defeats all other opinions and is his essential view, is clearly recorded.

Thus, the perfection of the highest rasa is described in the sequence of pastimes (culminating in rāsa-līlā). One can see Jīva's devotion to the author of Vidagdha-mādhava and the ancient devotees who revealed Bhāgavatam. That is fulfilled in his work as well. Therefore those who desire to follow the path of rāgānuga should understand this, following the internal meaning of his explanations. If one does not consult with Jīva, why discuss with others? In Mādhava-mahotsava written by the same author, everything is described as parakīya in the initial and concluding statements, following Dāna-keli-kaumudi.

*kābhiścit paṭu jaṭilāṁ vikṛṣyamāṇāṁ*  
*nardatīṁ dadhi-ghṛta-kardameṣu rādhā \|*  
*śvaśrūṁ sā mahasi niśāmya namra-vaktrā*  
*smeratvaṁ jana-hasa-bimbam āśu dadhre \|\|*

**When some bold women with craft, lured Jaṭilā (Rādhā's mother-in-law), and then covered her with yogurt and ghee, she let out a terrifying scream. Seeing her sister-in-law present, Rādhā bent her head and smiled slightly, responding to the laugher of the others. Mādhava-mahotsava 4.83**

There are many such verses. Therefore one should understand Jīva's intention and nothing else.
