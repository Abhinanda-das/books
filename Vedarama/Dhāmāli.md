# Dhāmāli

## 1 - I´ll tell you my secret

*śuna go marama sai tomāre marama kai*  
*nā kahile nā pāri rahite*  
*ei nava yauvana jāti kula prāṇadhana*  
*sādha haya gauracānde dite*  
*snighdha kānti sumādhuryya dekhiyā ke dhare dhairyya*  
*garabiṇīra garaba lukāya*  
*bede śuna raṅga āra kone kone abalāra*  
*anurāga antare bārāya*  
*mana tāra kare curi diye anurāgera dhuri*  
*ānanda-rasera nidhi gorā*  
*emana kariyāche hiyā e deha gaurāṅga diyā*  
*rasera bhikṣāri hai morā*  
*rasānanda rase bhorā bhāle bhulāila gorā*  
*baulī haila saba nārī*  
*e dāsa locana bale naraharira padatale*  
*gaurāṅgera jāo balihāri*

”Oh my dearest sakhī. I´m confiding in you because I cannot go on without revealing my secret; so listen:

My new youth, my caste prestige, my family attachment, plus the wealth of my love – I shall offer it all to Gorācānda.

Seeing the irresistable sweetness of His effulgent limbs – who can remain patient? For even arrogant women hide their pride when viewing Him. And what is more amusing...listen: young, inexperienced girls just can´t check their attraction which generates from within. For seeing their anurāga – He ropes their minds – and pulls them to Him! Hence Gorā is the Jewel of pleasure. So I´ve made up my mind: I´m just going to offer my body to Gaurāṅga...and let´´s all become beggars for His prema! Āha! Gorā´s intoxicating rasa-ānanda is overwhelming! And His magnificence devastates every young girl. Hence they are all going mad! Catching hold of Narahari´s lotus feet, the servant Locana exclaims, all glories to Gaurāṅga!”

## Gaura-kṛpā-kaṇikā -

Although very attractive, nāgarī padas are not for everyone. They are reserved for those longing to be lead into Gaurāṅga´s intimate sambhoga rasa. And the nāgarīs themselves are the guides. Yet when talking to each other, they are mercifully sharing the rasa with the reader. Just imagine: If a newly married, young girl breaks her religious principles to fall in love with some handsome boy of her village...who will she tell? Would she tell her hsuband? Or her relatives?

How about her neighbours? And if not any of these, surely she wouldnt leak out her secret to the people in general. For only a scandal would arise. Then the good name of her beloved would go to ruin. And what could be more painful to a faithful heroine? Moreover, she herself would become scandalized; and lose the chance to serve her beloved for life. So the only person that the nāgarī can trust would be an intimate girlfriend, one who shares the same romantic desires as she. One who will protect her heart´s fantasy. Indeed, such love is nurtured by secrecy.

Hence, when the Gauḍīya Mahājanas write such padāvalī about Gaurāṅga´s private life, they are leaving a great deal of responsibility with their readers. After all, which bhakta could bear to see Śrīman Mahāprabhu´s name disgraced? Just think: on one hand He is an emeplary world teacher, one who even takes sannyāsa to promote His mission. Yet, on the other hand (like Śrī Kṛṣṇa) He is very actively involved in parakīyā rasa affairs. For this is Svayaṁ Bhagavān´s eternal activity and greatest source of pleasure.

An interesting matter for the sādhakas wishing to perform mānasī sevā in Gaura-līlā is that the nāgarī padas present an ideal bhūtaśuddhi for the reader. In other words, rasika bhaktas can view the young girls of these songs with themselves, with their internally conceived Nadīyā nāgarī svarūpas. Entering the līlā to meditate in this way can awaken Gaura prema; and hence, award the sādhaka´s suitable nāgarī siddha deha for directly taking part in such pastimes. In his ’Bhakticandrikā paṭala’, Śrīman Narahari Sarkāra presents the dhyāna for such internal worship1. He states that sādhakas should meditate upon themselves as young premika brāhmaṇa women of Nadīyā who look upon Śrī Viśvambhara´s body with love. Their faces are smiling with red-tinted eyes; they wear divine dress, adorn themselves with flower mālās and their bodies are constantly decorated sāttvika emotions such as tears and horripilation.

Let us relish Locana´s poetry in this way.

## 2 - Pūrva Rāga

*śacīra gorā kāmera corā dekhlāma ghāṭera kūle*  
*cācara cule beriyā bhāle nava mālatīra māle*  
*kācā soṇā lāge ghṛṇā rūpera tulanā dite*  
*hena cittacorā manoharā nāiko avanīte*  
*ki āra balcho go sai, tomāya bhujāba ki*  
*snāne jete sakhīra sāthe gaura dekhechi*  
*se rūpa dekhi duti ākṣi phirāite nārī*  
*punaḥ tāre dekhbāra tare kata sādha kari*  
*ki āra balbo go sai, tumita ācha bhāla*  
*āmāra maramera kathā maramei rahila*  
*jāgite ghumāte sadā gaura jāge mane*  
*locana bale ye dekheche sei se uhā jāne*

”Oh sakhī! Mother Śacī´s Gorā is a Kāmera korā (abode of kāma)...and I just saw him by the Gaṅgā´s ghāṭa! His lovely, curling hair tossed so elegantly over His forehead...and His curā (topknot of hair) was bound with mālatī garlands. Uh! Now even molten gold irks me – for it is incomparable with His effulgent limbs. So is there such a heart-stealer or mind-thief anywhere else in the whole creation? But what are you saying, sakhī?...And how can I explain it to you? When I went with the sakhīs to bathe – I saw Gorā. Then I couldn´t retract my eyes from Him...And now I only hanker to see His exquisite form again. Oh sakhī, what more can I say? You´re still acting normal...So I guess that my heart´s confession will have to remain inside. For during wakeing, or sleeping, Gorā always fills my mind. Locana says, only those who see Him will know what I´m talking about.”

## Gaura-kṛpā-kaṇikā -

In ’Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi’, pūrva rāga is thus described:

*ratirya saṅgamāt pūrvaṁ darśanaśravaṇāditya*  
*tayorūnmilati prājñaiḥ pūrvarāgaḥ sa ucyate*

”The rati (attraction) which is relished by the nāyaka or by the nāyikā, before their first meeting (through seeing or hearing about the beloved) is called ’pūrva rāga’ by the paṇḍitas.”

And in rasa śāstra, the nāyikā´s pūrva rāga is first described; for this is the ’rasa-riti’, established custom for relishing transcendental love stories. With the two nāgarīs mentioned in this pada, we find ideal examples of how pūrva rāga awakens for Gaurāṅga; one has directly seen Him, the other is hearing about his qualities. And one of Kṛṣṇa´s qualities in madhura rasa is that He is ’nārījana manohārī’ (attractive to all women). This is described in Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi´s nāyakabheda-prakaraṇa. Yet here, Locana´s nāgarī goes on to tell that kāmera korā Gorā is not only manohara (enchanting to the mind), he is also citta-corā (heart-thief). Because Gaurāṅga has Kṛṣṇa´s full ’mādhurīmā’ (splendour) plus Rādhā´s mādhurīmā combined – he is the ’Rasarāja śiromaṇi’ (Crown jewel of all rasikas). This is affirmed by Advaita Ācāryya:

*naṭarāja śiroratnaṁ śrī nāgara śiromaṇiṁ*  
*aśeśa rasika sphujjanmauli bhūṣaṇa bhūṣaṇaṁ*

”Śrī Gaurāṅga is the Crown jewel amongst all dancers, the Crown jewel amongst all nāgaras – and thus the Ornament of ornaments amongst all rasika persons.”

-Śrī Gaurāṅga-aṅga-pratyāṅga stavarāja

So when the high pitch of Kṛṣṇa´s sensational mellows surcharges in Gaurāṅga, owing to Him being the form of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa combined - so does the super-excellence of his bhaktas´ prema excel in their rasa exchnages with Him.

Hence, the Nadīyā nāgarīs´ prema may be labelled: high voltage gopī bhāva.

This is because, on one hand, it very closely resembles the madhura tie in which Kṛṣṇa enjoys with the gopīs; yet, in Nadīyā, the nāgarīs have some unique and sensational qualities of their own which even excel the gopīs´. In this essay we hope to uncover some of the mysteries within this realm.

Returning to the pada and the two nāgarīs, let´s lend an ear to a closer look into what is going on:

The first nāgarī is Locana, who is elatedly scurrying back from the Gaṅgā´s ghāṭa after seeing Gorā. And noticing that she is transported in rapture, and unable to contain her composure, the second damsel (who hasn´t experienced Gaurāṅga´s mādhurī yet) looks on with a puzzled look, as if to express, 'Hey sakhī! What´s happened to you?' Overwhelmed by compassion, Locana nāgarī decides to introduce this inexprienced girl into the secret realm of Gaura līlā. Thus she begins describing Gaura kiśora´s bhuvana mohana rūpa mādhurī. The second damsel can´t believe it, and continues to look on with a puzzled look. But Locana, after spilling the first hint, finds it impossible to conceal her feelings. She just has to confide in someone. (Here we can appreciate how the nāgarīs are 'apāra karuṇāmayī', like a boundless ocean of mercy. For even at the risk of scandalizing themselves they hanker to share the rasa with others.)

Hearing Locana, the second damsel begins to offer counsel: 'Oh sakhī! You´re a kulavatī (chaste, married woman), you must try and control yourself.'

But Locana answers, 'Oh, you´re a true satī (chaste wife) as yore doing fine, Ican now see that it was a mistake to inform you. Only those who have seen Gaurāṅga will know what I´m talking about.' In other words, after seeing Him, you´ll be in the same predicament as me.

Locana´s last statement also carries a subtle hint for those opposing nāgarī bhāva: If they could be properly informed, would they go on criticizing? Rather, wouldn´t their attraction awaken too?

## 3 - The Saṅkīrtana party

*nadīyā nāgarī sārī sārī sārī calilā gaṅgāra ghāṭe*  
*hena rūpa chaṭā yena vidhu ghāṭa gagana chāriyā bāṭe*  
*śacīra nandana karaye narttana saṅge pāriṣada laiyā*  
*dekhibāra tare suradhunī tīre āilā ākula haiyā*  
*o rūpa mādhurī dekhiyā nāgarī sabāi bibhora haiyā*  
*aṅga parimale haiyā cañcale parite cāhe uriyā*  
*kāru galita ambara tāha nā sambara*  
*kāhāro galita veṇī*  
*yena citrera putalī rahe sabe meli*  
*dekhe gorā guṇamaṇi*  
*keha bhāvabhare pare kāru kore nayāne bahaye dhārā*  
*kāhāro pulaka aṅge parateka keha murachita pārā*  
*locana bale gela kula bhaye lājera māthāya bāja*  
*dhairyya dharma ādi sakala vināśi nāce gorā naṭrāja*

"In a long line a group of nāgarīs are heading for the Gaṅgā´s ghāṭa; their bodily effulgence, like the moon´s rays, lights the pathway and the sky.

But while dancing along - here comes Śacīnandana! Being eager to see the nāgarīs, He has brought His bjaktas (who are performing saṅkīrtana) to the Suradhunī´s banks.

Viewing Gaurāṅga´s rūpa mādhurī the nāgarīs are spellbound! And as His bodily aroma enters their nostrils, they become like restless bees hankering to soar to Him! Unable to hold on, someon´s garments fall, and someone´s hair loosens. Aho! They stand like dolls in a picture, agape, viewing Gaura guṇamaṇi! Overwhelmed by bhāva, a nāgarī falls into her girlfriend´s lap, her eyes filled with tears. Someone´s body erupts with goose bumps, and another nāgarī almost faints. Locana says, family fear is fleeting and shyness appears like a lightning strike on the head! Thus stealing their patience and religious principles, Gorā naṭarāja dances on."

## Gaura-kṛpā-kaṇikā -

Using the excuse of cowherding, Kṛṣṇa gets a chance to meet with Rādhā and Her sakhīs in the forest. But here we learn about Gaurāṅga´s sneaky behaviour:

Under the guise of performing His Yuga dharma saṅkīrtana, He slips away from home for a peak at the nāgarīs. And the Gaṅgā´s beautiful, forest-laden banks serve as the ideal saṅketa (meeting place).

In order to relish this pada, let´s enter a svarasikī meditation following Locana´s bhāva dhāra (flowing mood):

The time is early morning. Gaura-maṇi has just returned to Lakṣmī and Viṣṇu-priyā after spending the night in Śrīvāsa Ṭhākura´s six seasonal flower gardens. Gadādhara Prabhu´s group is very careful, however, not to be there. They return straight home from Śrīvāsa Aṅgana instead. If Gaurāṅga´s two priyās notice their rati-cihnas (love marks) surely a prema-kalaha (love skirmish) will commence. For seeing Gaurāṅga´s tired, red roling eyes alone is enough to trigger their durjaya māna (deep jealous anger)

Let´s trail behind Gadādhara´s group as they head for Campahāṭī Village in the dhāma´s southwest corner. Here Mādhava Miśra and his rāṇī, Ratnāvatī devī, (Gadādhara´s parents) live in a palatial moonstone palace. There is a maṇimaya mandira for Gopīnātha´s sevā, adjoining their mansion. The whole area is situated on the Alakāna Gaṅgā´s banks in a grove of campā trees. There are nine species of campā, such as Goloka campā, Nāga campā, Svarṇa campā, etc. Gadādhara´s followers live in palatial homes surrounding Mādhava Miśra´s estate. Let´s study their divine characteristics and mood. Caitanya-caritāmṛta describes them as follows:

*paṇḍitera gaṇa saba bhāgavata dhanya*  
*prāṇa ballabha sabāra śrī kṛṣṇa caitanya*

"Gadādhara Paṇḍita´s associates are all mahābhāgavatas and very fortunate, for their prāṇa-vallabha is Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya."

The very term 'prāṇa-vallabha' means that they are Gaurāṅga´s consorts. Yet now as they scurry home, they appear in male forms to conceal their previous night´s affairs. This is the mystery of Gaura-līlā, Gaurāṅga´s male associates all have an internal prakṛti bhāva. And this mood manifests their nāgarī-svarūpas. Thus everyone carefully slips back into their bedrooms just at sunrise.

After a brief rest, everyone assembles on the veranda outside Gadādhara´s room. Hearing their madhura voices, Gadādhara awakens. And to relieve fatigue He yawns and stretches. His nose-pearl bobs as His eyes roll. A few servants assist Him in rising and face-washing, etc. Then coming into the bhaktas´ assembly, He sits down on a comfortable pillow in their midst - like the fullmoon circled by a galaxy of stars. This is when 'rasodgāra' begins, when the nāgarīs describe about their previous night´s fascination with Gaurāṅga. In his 'Gaura-carita-cintāmaṇi', Śrīla Narahari Cakravartī writes extensively on this topic. Here is a sample taken from there:

*bhetinu nāgara gorā*

"Oh sakhī! Heading for the Gaṅgā´s ghāṭa along this pathway I´m seeing my Nāgara Gorā!"

"Look, look at His madhura baṅgima (sweet pose)!"

Not too far off in the distance, a saṅkīrtana party of bhaktas are singing and dancing along. And within their midst is one exquisite golden Naṭarāja mūratī. Raising His two golden arms, Gaurāṅga dances in circles as a fragrant mālatī-māla tosse from his broad chest.

"But aren´t they headed the other way?", one nāgarī exclaims.

"Just wait a moment", Gadādhara replies. And with an enticing lalita-sundara pose, She remains poised, Her gaze affixed upon Gaurasundara´s mukhāravinda. A moment passes. "Hey, look!", says another nāgarī, that saṅkīrtana party is turning around; it´s heading in our direction now!" Covering their heads with a veil, everyone proceeds along, one by one, in a long line towards the Gaṅgā´s shore.

Then the episode in Locana´s pada takes place. But owing to his bhaktas being present, a milana (meeting) doesn´t happen. Rather, Gaurāṅga continues dancing along. Then the nāgarīs have their bath, and return home in a dazed condition. But unable to forget what they saw, a mix-up takes place upon entering the kitchen.

## Gaura-kṛpā-kaṇikā -

Parakīyā-rasa offers a challenge. The nāgarīs wishing to serve Gaurāṅga with their madhura bhāva always face obstacles. At home there is the śāśura (father-in-law), the śāśurī (mother-in-law), the nanadī (sister-in-law) and the nāgarī´s pati (husband). All of these persons keep a tight vigil of her kula dharma (household duties and obligations to the family saṁsāra). Outside the home is the very strict loka-samāja (society) which will at once condemn the slightest deviation of a young, married woman´s character. And moreover, within society, these nāgarīs, as brāhmaṇa women, have a jāti (high status) to maintain to protect their parents´ good name and will. So the nāgarīs are watched from all sides. And no one will tolerate their looking at Gaurāṅga´s beautiful limbs...even for a second. Uh, the frightening fate they must bear for their coveted Gaurāṅga sevā certainly invokes sympathy. Indeed, they are like frail and lovely deer trapped in an iron cage.

## Gaura-kṛpā-kaṇikā -

To enter the rasa, let´s first say a few words about the Hindu marriage tradition in Bengal. This pada mentions about two ceremonies which happen at such weddings: the gātra haridra, and the bāsara ghara.

Ghatra haridra, or gāya haluda means rubbing turmeric over the bride or the groom´s body. This ritual takes place a few days before the wedding; it is part of the function called āśirvāda (blessings) when gifts are exchanged. During the groom´s āśirvāda, it often happens that the bride´s girlfriends bathe the groom, after smearing his body (from head to foot) with turmeric. This is an exciting moment for those involved. But in the case of Gaurasundara´s gāya haluda and bathing ceremony performed by the nāgarīs it becomes the most auspicious moment of their lives.

The moment when they have the priviledge to fulfill their long cherished desire to touch Him. Let´s switch over to Caitanya-maṅgala, where Locana describes how the nāgarīs assemble at Śacīs house, bring water from the Gaṅgā and bathe Gaurāṅga.

**Translation -**

"At the end of night, the wedding´s ādivāsa (āśirvāda) ceremony begins. The nāgarīs are jubilant to draw water from the Gaṅgā. All sorts of instruments play in tumult - as if to break the chaste girls´ chastity. Every young girl of Nadīyā is excited to dive within the waves of Gaurāṅga´s wedding samudra. **The Bāsara Ghara**

Because a newly married girl is genereally too shy to directly enter her husband´s arms on the first night of their wedding, her dearest girlfriends accompany her along with the new groom into the bedroom. This is called the bāsara ghara.

Here is Locana´s description from Caitanya-maṅgala:

"Hundreds of nāgarīs enter the bāsara ghara with slacking garments and faltering voices. Some have tired, rolling eyes, and others are intoxicated with Cupid´s passion to touch Gaurāṅga. Thus they roll onto His lap. Some nāgarīs look at Gaurāṅga´s moonface with unblinking eyes - like cakora birds sipping the moon´s rays. Thus their lotus eyes worship His face. In order to touch His body, group after group of sundarī jubatīs come near. Gaurāṅga´s flower petal glances sting their deer like resisting power and defeat them. Seeing the rising moon of Gaurāṅga´s smile, the nāgarīs´ darks shyness flees. As they sit before Him, His bodily scent makes them tremble.

One ramaṇī embraces Gaurāṅga to say:

'O listen to me, Lakṣmī want some pān, so why don´t you place some in her mouth...and let all of the sakhīs see?'

Someone fulfills her desires and combs Gaurāṅga´s hair tying it into a cuḍā. Someone joyfully places pān in Gaurāṅga´s mouth as her heart palpitates. Another pushes on His side and places her cheek up to His. Then a group of sakhīs lift Lakṣmīpriyā up and place her in Gaurāṅga´s lap. And a nāgarī exclaims:

'Where is such a fortunate girl as Lakṣmī to have Gauracānda as Her husband? What type of penances has She performed? And what type of reward has She gotten? Has anyone reaped such a reward from worshipping the devas, or by performing satīdharma (chastity to the husband)? Gaurāṅga´s beautiful face is like Cupid´s abode - and the thief of every prideful girl´s jewel of patience. Because His arms are like golden shafts, every pretty ramaṇī hankers to feel their embrace. As Lakṣmīpriyā will enjoy vilāsa with Gaurāṅga´s body, when shall we become fortunate to touch her? Let´s all become Her dāsī, then we can regularly view the beauty of Gaurāṅga´s limbs.'

Enjoying such frolic, the night comes to an end."

Before going on to Dhāmāli´s next pada, there is an important siddhānta that needs mentioning in context with the nāgarīs discussed in this purport. Previously it was shown that the nāgarīs expand from Śrīla Gadādhara Paṇdita, just as the Vraja gopīs are the expansions of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī. This is the largest group. But there are many other groups also. For example here, in connection with Śrīmatī Lakṣmīpriyā´s marriage with Gaurāṅga, all of the nāgarīs mentioned are Her own personal expansions, or the majority of them. In Caitanya-maṅgala, Locana Dāsa presents this tattva as follows:

*āra devī satyabhāmarūpe guṇa anupama*  
*saba rasa vaidagdhira sīmā*  
*līlā vilāsa lābaṇyasarva kāla rasa dhanya*  
*trijagate ramaṇī paramā*

"Bt there is Satyabhāma also who is unequalled in beauty and qualities. She is the limit of rasika cleverness. Hence the beautiful ramaṇīs expanding from Her (along with Rādhā and Rukminī) excel all other women in the three worlds ith Their pastimes, lovemaking, prowess, beauty and unlimited rasika skills."

Satyabhāma is Viṣṇupriyā in Gaura-līlā. So from the above mentioned information, we learn that the countless millions of Nadīyā-nāgarīs expand basically from three groups: those headed by Gadādhara, Lakṣmīpriyā and Viṣṇupriyā devī. Bhaktas hankering to follow in Lakṣmīpriyā or Viṣṇupriyā´s footsteps take birth in Nadīyā as females. But those in Gadādhara´s group are males; their unseen nāgarī-svarūpas manifest at select moments.
