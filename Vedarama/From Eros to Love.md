# From Eros to Love

## Author’s Note

The content of this book comes mainly from the transcript of lectures and seminars held by myself on the homonymous theme.

Conscious of the depth of the subject and of the demanding task to convey cultural, philosophical and psychological concepts of the traditional Vedic-vaishnava tradition, in the dialogue between East and West, I ask the gentle kindness of readers if any shortcomings of my work will be detected.

I hope, however, that it may constitute an initial response to the questions of my students and of any genuine spiritual researcher while, for a discussion of the topic, I refer to the consultation of the texts listed in the bibliography.

I would like to thank the C.S.B. members for their precious collaboration, particularly those who have generously assisted me in the various phases of this writing.

## Introduction

Love is possible, it is available to everyone, as each one of us is made to love and to be loved. Love is not something out of reach, but rather a great goal and as such it requires effort, dedication, care, sacrifice, consistency, loyalty, honesty, and truth.

Love is a diamond and compared to it, Eros is just a piece of broken glass. However, the diamond of Love is for everyone; each person is born to attain perfection, nobody is a failed project, a wrong project. On the contrary, all genuine Traditions tell us that we have been specifically designed for happiness and love is undoubtedly the major component of happiness.

The art of loving is not eroticism. Today there is a lot of confusion: everyone searches in the other something to get for him or herself, thus inevitably remaining in the sphere of ego; however, this is not love.

Love is an overwhelming feeling, a natural and spontaneous product of our deepest nature, of our spirituality. It is a constant offering. We will see that there are different categories of love, not simply the relationship between lovers.

There is a path for living and a path for dying, a

path for suffering and a path for genuine happiness, a path for agony and a path for bliss.

It is not possible to attain the perfection of existence and full, true satisfaction, by negating or demonizing the body, but by using many instruments among which the body is one of the most valuable tools we have.

In a course of psychology I explained that we have a device — the subconscious mind — that works like a machine, but it is an extremely intelligent machine, as it has been designed to attain without mistakes the final purpose, the lasting benefit — a state of well being that does not cease with that typically human crisis called death.

## The Sphere of Feelings

We will examine this extraordinarily complex topic by first studying the sphere of feelings, in the widest sense of the word.

After clarifying some prejudices and throwing light on some dark corners that Western minds have been keeping since times before the Middle Ages, we will be able to speak about the perception of reality; in fact, if reality is not perceived we cannot even discuss about happiness, because happiness is inherent to reality, and remains unattainable from a level of illusion. Real happiness consists in a state of consciousness that is permanent, unchangeable, and independent from external conditions; for this reason the sages and seers {rishis) of Indian Tradition call this state of being as ananda, ‘bliss’, indicating spiritual happiness.

The Vedanta Sutra, one of the most important texts of Vedic Tradition, comparable to the heights of Western theology, states: Athato Brahma jijnasa: “Now let us enquire about Brahman”. The word, jijnasa can be translated as ‘investigate’ therefore we are encouraged to investigate or explore the dimensions of spiritual reality or Brahman.

The definition of reality, equivalent with truth, is translated in various ways in Sanskrit language. For example, sat is the root word of satyam, or truth, as distinguished from lies or deception; it refers to the common and fundamental truth in every day’s things.

Unless we realize and apply this level of truth, we cannot think of higher levels. From the same root we find sattva, ‘luminosity’, ‘lightness’. A person who lives in a sattvic way, under the influence of sattva-guna, is one who sees truth, who perceives truth. Among the constituent elements that influence the conditioned being, sattva is the most transparent, allowing us to better approach reality. The word sadhu, derived from the same root, indicates a person who lives in reality and truth. Truth and reality are therefore born from the same root. Another term that is connected to reality and truth is ritam, the cosmic truth, the cosmic order, pertaining to the heavenly level. When you wonder about the reason of the cyclic manifestation of seasons, including the cosmic seasons, or why a great heavenly body remains in its orbit without deviating of a single degree, you should know that the answer lies in a higher order to which everything is subject.

However, Vedic texts describe the highest level of reality and truth with the term tattva, which pertains to the spiritual level: this is the highest truth, the original truth, the origin of everything that exists and is true in essence, in a physical but especially in a metaphysical sense.

These three levels of truth and reality correspond to the three levels of existence — earthly, heavenly and spiritual — called in Sanskrit as adhibhautika, adhidaivika and adhyatmika.

Today there are many sciences that can contribute to the development of an open vision, but the Western man has grossly neglected the deep and careful observation of the subject - himself — in spite of all his specialization in the study of objects. Even sciences studying personality are mostly examining the phenomenal or material aspect of the individual. Then who is studying the subject, who observes what happens to the observer? Mainstream science already finds difficult to admit theoretically the existence of a subject separate from his physical structure, but the Vedic texts, authoritative sources of knowledge, describe two universes, two dimensions: the material and the spiritual.

Samkhya philosophy names and describes two energies or principles: prakriti and purusha, or matter and spirit, substance and essence. The source of these two entities is the same: Parabrahman. Therefore it is not proper to say that there is a real dichotomy between them, although once the two dimensions are manifested, their differences become evident and seemingly impossible to reconcile.

Parabrahman or Parameshvara possesses innumerable potencies or shaktis that can be grouped in two large categories: antaranga-sbakti and bahiranga-shakti, or internal and external energies. The former exhibits the nature of Brahman, unchangeable and transcendent, while the latter is subject to constant changes and creates the cosmic manifestation.

Man is constituted of both energies and therefore he can be described as a paradox, with pressing inner aspirations that collide and clash with equally powerful physical needs. We must become constantly aware of the difference between the self and the body inhabited by the self because, when the individual (whose characteristics will be analysed shortly), identifies with what he is not, he becomes subject to a series of false conceptions, distorted perceptions and wrong actions. Therefore he makes many mistakes and is subject to much suffering.

When people are imbalanced and too concentrated on the outside, something cracks inside. When we think that the external world is important, so well defined and concrete, even complete, soon we will be forced to the painfully realize that we have committed some serious mistake. The art of life consists in keeping a perfect balance between these two spheres, so that they do not contradict but rather help each other. In fact body and Nature, with all their elements, must not be demonized; on the contrary, they must be appreciated and maintained with all care, because they are valuable instruments that offer us a comfortable and progressive journey through existence. We must put them in the correct context and understand what is their use, otherwise we may misuse and damage them.

The subject that we always deal with is the spiritual principle, the atman. Today people do not give much attention to the soul and God: they believe that the human body is the centre of reality. I don’t want to denigrate culture, but this trend has also been helped by Humanism. The Humanistic movement had positive aspects but also committed a great mistake by placing man at the centre of the universe, in the position formerly occupied by God. Thus the soul had disappeared from people’s sight and perception.

We must carefully avoid to inflate the importance of the soul by diminishing the value of the phenomenal world and its components like food, social peace or personal relationships: we don’t need to escape into spiritualism, but we should reclaim that transcendental dimension that has been grossly neglected or even denied.

Let us examine now the characteristics of prakriti and purusha. The eight elements of prakriti are earth (bhumib), water (apahY fire {analah), air (vayub), ether (kham), mind (manas), intelligence (buddht) and the distorted perception of the self or false ego (ahamkard). These last three elements, which can be called the internal senses, can either trap atman and become his torture chamber, or take him back to his original state of bliss and immortality.

These eight elements represent the bricks building the phenomenal world, but three archetypal energies pervade prakriti and are present in each element: tamoguna, rajoguna and sattvaguna. Tamas is synonim of darkness, inertia, lethargy, sleepiness, dullness, dirtiness, lack of vision. This structuring force of the universe has a specific use: for example, tamoguna enables us to sleep. However, we must learn how to manage it because, when it becomes prominent in an individual, it causes many problems. Rajas on the other hand creates an intense, frantic and egotic activity, pushing the individual on and on, never to stop even when he would like to, chasing newer and newer illusions that are inevitably followed by painful disappointments and dissatisfaction. Sattva indicates cleanliness, lightness, awakening, enlightenment, goodness, vision and foresight, and the desire to see everybody in a happy state. This quality predominates in genuine spiritual researchers and becomes their natural lifestyle, the mode in which they eat, sleep, act, work, and relate to others.

We can say that prakriti belongs to the bahiranga-shakti, the external energy of the Cosmic Being, therefore it is subject to the time-space paradigm, which is characteristic of the phenomenal world. Thus it is transient and always transforming.

On the other hand, the living entity is a spark of pure consciousness and belongs to the antaranga-shakti. He is constituted by divine energy situated beyond time and space but somehow, for a reason that we will not explore in this context, he finds himself inside the space-time paradigm. Substantially, the living entities as we know them are spiritual sparks wrapped in a cloak of prakrit.

The characteristics of the purusha are sat, cit and ananda. Sat means ‘existence, eternity’ as well as ‘reality’; as we have seen, this is the same root of the term satyam or truth, and sattva, that means ‘goodness’. Cit means ‘awareness, knowledge, consciousness’. Ananda is ‘bliss’ as experienced only in an inspired and pure environment; it has nothing to do with the so-called happiness which merely consists of sense excitement. All these three characteristics constitute the nature of the spiritual being, irrespective of the psycho-physical body he is wearing at a given historical moment. They are inalienable traits and they always constitute the real nature of atman, even when he is in a condition of confusion and deep lack of awareness. As Patanjali says in one of his famous sutras'--, “ignorance is mistaking what is permanent with what is transient, what is pure with what is impure, joy with suffering and the self with what is different from the self. This is precisely what happens when the purusha identifies with prakriti due to ahamkara, the distorted perception of the self.

Citta or the psychic structure, is formed by mind {manas), intelligence or intellect {buddhi) and false ego {ahamkara). Manas, or the external mind, is endowed with five channels called indriyas or senses. In this specific case we consider the jnana indriya, the sensory faculties, the psychic organs of sense, that will subsequently form their respective action organs for perception {karma indriya). This is still the level where the mind uses the five psychic organs meant to acquire knowledge.

In books on psychiatry or psychology we will see that the entire study is concentrated on the stimulation produced by the external objects on the senses. However, there is an inner world as well. It was named subconscious when the science of psychology made a leap forward at the beginning of last century, but the great Indian sages had already known the subconscious for thousands of years, and they have described it so well and in a detailed way, that neglecting their knowledge would be a great mistake.

Buddhi is the light that gives us the power of discrimination, the flash we recognize in each intuition. Intuitions or ideas are products of buddhi. On the other hand, ahamkara is chaotic element capable of confusing all our logic potential: it is a separation from pure consciousness (the spiritual principle or purusha) and is activated when we identify with what is illusory — asat or maya, the veil of ignorance that is hiding the reality of things. Any confusion that arises on the psychic level and is then expressed on the physical level derives from this identification with illusion — which is not as our senses perceive it.

Now, at the dawn of the third millennium, we need to review and integrate our knowledge. The separation between secular and religious knowledge is now outdated, deviating and confusing, because scientific investigation can easily become meditation, religious and mystic experience, spiritual realization. When science is genuine and we are sincere in the search for reality through the path of spiritual realization, the two paths do not oppose but rather integrate each other. They illuminate and complete each other: the external world and the internal world are again seen as components of the same reality, which is one in nature.

The universe is constituted by expanding consciousness. Parts of this consciousness crystallize and become the perceivable objects that can be touched, seen, measured and lifted.

Each object that comes into our psychic field can be framed into a space-time paradigm, without which our mind does not work. Each experience, each perception, are deposited on the cortical brain cells according to the three modes of memory: the sensory memory only lasts a few seconds, the operative memory lasts a few minutes to a few years, and the long lasting memory is practically without limits in terms of both time and .quantity of stored data.

Vedic literature, however, tells us that there is also another kind of memory, a dormant memory called samskara. Each experience we live, either physical or psychic, leaves a trace (called samskara} not only on the conscious memory but also in the deep and dark part of psyche, that is usually inaccessible by the conscious self. From that position it can dictate conditions to the behaviour of the individual and generates the automatic responses, compulsive ideas and the complexes defined by the modern science of psychology.

All these mechanisms were very well known by the Vedic sages. Freud has thrown some light on a territory unknown to Westerners when he said: “The idea that we are masters of our own house is just an illusion, because this is not the fact”, intending that we also have to deal with the subconscious. The science of self realization also teaches how to bring the subconscious elements to the conscious platform, because when we keep a large portion of ourselves buried, armoured or obscured, we are undermining all our project of happiness.

It is possible to find effective instruments to work on our own character; not only and not specifically with the purpose of becoming efficient in our historical and temporary roles, but to operate at a deeper level, a higher level. Such a higher level is our real nature and at the same time it does not clash with our social, political, economic duties, or with the logic we are accustomed to use every day. It is a higher and wider point of view that includes all the knowledge we have already developed but also transcends it and goes beyond.

There are various levels of knowledge of an object: the external or more superficial is called nama, corresponding to the literal or nominal knowledge of a given object. The name is certainly important because it identifies a specific reality by separating it from its undistinguished background, but today people use names in a mechanical way, creating vicious circles in their minds with compulsive ideas, fixations and obsessions. We must go beyond the name level to attain the innermost components of the object we want to know. Already by combining name and form (nama-rupa) we can obtain more information; if we can proceed further to access the energetic nature constituting that object, our chances of confusion are greatly reduced.

'There is a famous book, a best seller story about a man who is convinced that his wife is a hat and treats her likewise. At first this idea can make us laugh... but in fact the human mind can disfigure things and confuse them.

Let us now consider more attentively the perception of reality: what reality we want to examine? The superficial reality, the facade, which is as thin as a paint coat? Or do we want to search deeply until we find the consciousness, the higher values of the object of our contemplation? Perception of reality is an immensely important issue in everybody’s life, because each time we mistake something for something else we get innumerable complications, and invariably much suffering.

Reality is an inner dimension. When we can access it, we use it as a reference for everything we observe, because our vision of the world is projected by our own mind. People project their own inner world on the external one, so in the end the internal world is what appears externally, especially in its most hostile aspects, because joy and suffering depend on our own inner world. A mind who is badly structured will see everything in a bad way and perceive everything as evil and negative. A person who suffers from inner conflicts will only be able to see conflicts in the external world; he cannot solve them because solutions can only come from the inner world and his inner world is made of conflicts and not of solutions.

There a science, contained in several books, especially in Bhagavad-gita, that gives great importance to some aspects of life. The first of such aspects is harmony with the cosmic order, which we could generally define as having a good relationship with God, or being in harmony with the universal order. On the human level such universal order is called dharma, while on the cosmic level is called ritam, but there is not a qualitative difference between the two, especially because microcosm and macrocosm are always connected and mirror each other.

The cosmic order pervades the entire creation, it gives structure and sense to all the creatures and may allow us to perceive not only the Creator’s hand but the Creator himself, visible in all the physical and astro-physical phenomena. Harmonization with the cosmic order is defined as dharmya behaviour; it is born from an inner attitude, from the strong determination to vibrate in harmony with the energies and the laws of the universe.

What we need is wisdom, not a mere acquisition of data, because certainly data are not sufficient to create wisdom, sense of proportion, balance, or sobriety. While we are tentatively perceiving things here and there and mistaking (as in the classic Vedic metaphor) the rope for a serpent, we cannot honestly hope for a better life. In that stage our perception of the actual external reality is very limited, and the projections of our mind prevail. When our inner vision is clear and focused on reality, we can have the correct vision of the outside world as well.

We must open the cage of the mind by breaking its many rigid schemes. Especially in the West, people have such vague ideas on spirituality that spirit is considered almost equal to vacuity, vagueness, abstractness or void, while on the contrary it is the only reality. Actually, except for spirit, everything else is precarious, temporary, and is constantly on the verge of bursting away, like a bubble. The event of our own death or the death of someone we love, a financial disaster, a serious disease, a medical report that says, “your days are numbered”, make us to realize (almost always too late) that the phenomenal world does not offer a safe support, simply because after all we don’t belong here. This does not amount to denigrating or diminishing the value of the body and the world, or demonizing human relationships, because it is precisely by exalting all human faculties that we can come to get a glimpse of that light at the end of the tunnel, our reality of happiness and bliss.

We should therefore think well, reason well, try to make deep and precise evaluations without being swept away by prejudice and mental patterns, no matter how difficult it is to consider others’ thoughts without filtering them through our mental patterns or prejudice. If we succeed in this effort we will be glad to find a very concrete reality... we could say, the only concrete reality. A reality that stands solid against the shocks and waves of time.

Happiness is found much beyond the cage of space and time; every time we try to lock it up inside a specific time and space, it transforms into anxiety. This happens precisely because by its nature it is unlimited and endless, therefore it is in contrast with the temporary and. limited dimension we know at present.

Feeling also means seeing, perceiving, realizing, knowing by intuition. Scientific minds can be likened to poetic and artistic minds. Religion and science are not two opposed paths: when they are genuine, they both lead to spirituality. It is very interesting to note how poetic inspiration, artistic creativity and mystic vision all come from the same source. In fact all these paths, when followed with sincerity, determination and purity, lead to the same result: the perception of Reality.

The individual’s specific and limited way to see things could hamper this result. Everything is based on thought; thoughts generate words, words generate actions, repeated actions generate habits, habits form the character, the character shapes one’s destiny. Thus destiny is not something that is imposed on man from the outside or something that he has to accept in a passive way, but rather it consists of a personal mental attitude that leads him to a pre-determined journey. So, before studying the topic of transcendence we will clear the path from a series of different obstacles. Let’s try to localize some of them and analyse them with an open mind to find answers and clarify the subject, not only on the physical level but on the psychological and spiritual levels as well. A good mind frame also takes into account the spheres of feelings and enables us to understand things not only through logic but with intuition as well.

Sanskrit literature speaks of two ways of living, two directions: sura and asura-, sura means ‘enlightened’, asura means ‘living in darkness’. Why do living entities choose a path over the other? Are the positions of sura or asura ontological realities? Not at all. They are determined by the choices made by people — and which lead to a condition or to the other. A person who finds himself in a particular condition could, by changing his path with a conscious and determined effort generated by an intense desire, take the opposite direction in his life journey.

The more a person walks towards the asura direction, towards darkness, the more he will experience various forms of disease and uneasiness. There are two categories of disease: primary and secondary. The secondary form of disease (vyadht) relates to the body, while the primary form of disease relates to the mind and is called vasana, technically indicating tendencies and inclinations born from desire (kama). Desires produce the primary disease, or the mental disease. Whatever happens in the body has already happened first within the mind: this is why the ancient Indian medical science, based on thousands years old texts, the Vedas, says that mental disease is the primary disease, while physical disease is secondary. If we solve dis-ease on the psychic plane we will naturally see that bodily afflictions will disappear. Someone may object that in a moment of intense pain (a strong toothache or a wrecking migraine) the mind is subject to a negative pressure, therefore it seems that the body is influencing the mind. This is also true, because whatever happens on the physical plane affects the psychic plane as well, but the influence of the mind on the body is stronger than the influence of the body on the mind.

These two bodies, called citta and sharira or the mental and the physical bodies are interacting with each other, therefore the sphere of feelings must be dealt with at the same time from these two perspectives. The two spheres must be harmonized first, then transcended. We must reclaim harmony above these two sources of perception and then rediscover a form of perception that does not require a psycho-physical body: this is the purpose of spiritual realization.

In this society many people act under the dictation of impulses and, even when they feel that such a behaviour is negative or alienating (as they waste energies to the point of bringing the mind itself to extreme weakness), they generally can’t avoid it. However, attraction for some goals is irresistible and makes us lose our wisdom. Wisdom does not consist merely of a bunch of data, news and information, but it is an energy that enables us to manage all these data well.

Our generation boasts of enormous scientific discoveries; we have become able to communicate in real time with any part of the world — real time communication also means a great flow of information — but is the amount of wisdom increasing or decreasing?

I am not suggesting that we should distance ourselves from the practical, physical, social, psychological levels, because this is not what spiritual realization means. Spiritual realization is not an escape from reality: it rather means to actually remain in contact with reality, the only one reality, from which everything else emanates. The world is made of crystallized thought. All physical objects are supported by a psychic substratum, by a desire. Thought is the foundation of everything: if the mind works well, if it is strong and healthy, the physical body also remains strong up to the last breath. It is not absolutely necessary to experience old age as diseased and bed-ridden invalids: we can also stand in front of death in a good state of consciousness and free from complaints. We need satisfaction and deep convictions, not created out of blind faith, but coming from a genuine and concrete spiritual realization.

The following shlokas from Mahabharata, the most important literary work ever written on this planet, can give us a sense of the real meaning of wisdom, positive, concrete, healing thinking. The story of Mahabharata is 5000 years old. A few very wise persons, the Pandavas, were banished from the world capital of those times after a conspiracy organized by an evil prince surrounded by other power greedy politicians. The wise people still living in that city wondered what is the purpose of continuing to live in a place where wisdom has been banished: their considerations contain very important teachings regarding sanga, company or association.

As essence perfumes a dress, water, sesame seeds and earth with the fragrance of flowers, in the same way qualities are created by association.

Certainly the net of illusion is generated from the company of foolish people, while regular association with saintly persons generates virtue.

Those who search peace of mind should therefore keep company with those who are wise, enlightened, benevolent, ascetic and honest.

One should serve those people whose culture, education and activities are pure, because their company is more valuable than the study of the Scriptures.

In fact, even without a conscious effort from our part, the association of good and saintly people makes us more virtuous, just like we can acquire impious tendencies by honouring evil people.

By observing, touching, speaking to, or somehow associating with impure people one loses one’s dharmya behaviours and cannot attain success.

In the company of evil people intelligence is lost. In the company of mediocre people it becomes mediocre, while in the company of the best people it attains the highest levels.

In his Yogasutras Patanjali encourages us to concentrate our mind on those people who are free from attachment and envy, who have become free from vice and greed, and who have a pure mind because they are pervaded by elevating thoughts that develop the highest qualities. Thus we read in the Gautamiya Tantra-.

I offer my respectful obeisance to my Spiritual Master who, by the bright torchlight of knowledge opened my eyes which were blinded by the darkness of ignorance.

This mantra explains that revealed knowledge is like a lightning bolt that illuminates the most unfathomable layers of the mind, defined as subconscious by modern psychology. It is a gift from the guru, a gift from heaven, something that comes from the outside but is revealed within ourselves. The Upanishads frequently refer to internal and external, where internal means the inside of the heart (hridaye), that deep and inner centre of gravity of the being where thoughts and feelings flow together generating real perception, a genuine feeling not distorted by external influences.

Bhagavad-gita dedicates a whole chapter to the influences of Nature (gunas); also Patanjali’s Yoga Sutras, Bhagavata Parana and many other Scriptures give great importance to these illusory influences that distort our vision and cover Reality with a veil. As we have already explained, these are called gunas, or ‘ropes, ties’, because they bind the living entity to a specific conception and vision of the world. Persons, individuals, or in other words all those who have a body, are all defined by Sanskrit literature as conditioned beings. But conditioned by what? By those ties that do not allow us to see reality as it is. The process then consists in opening the knots that bind us to those cognitive grids constituted by prejudice and mental patterns, that prevent us from seeing things as they really are.

The process consists in decontaminating the mind, purifying it, re-opening channels that have not been lost but simply de-activated. Clotted energy has become trapped within the mind coils, encapsulated in psychic bubbles; for this reason the individual feels depressed, discouraged, suffering, sometimes even desperate up to suicide - which is ultimately a mere attempt at self-elimination. However, due to his own nature the individual is immortal, and therefore one can only kill the covering, the body, and certainly not the self, who is the immortal atman.

To generate healing thoughts we must first of all create the core of these thoughts by re-creating the original conditions that strengthen them and make them positive, that is binding it to a pure image. This is the eternal meaning of prayer or mantra therapy: the name of God is the quintessence of purity. There are divine names having an extraordinary power to influence not only the psyche but also the physical body and the environment with the creation of positive thought.

It is true that the environment influences the individual, but man is not necessarily subject to environment because he has the capacity and the strength, through positive thinking, to transform the configuration of his own mind to produce words that are transformed, transfigured, elevated, and radiant, creating positive situations and actions, that can solve one’s own problems and the problems of others.

By repeatedly performing correct actions we create beneficial habits that lead us to healing in a wider sense and enable us to shape our character into a radiant, positive, beneficial instrument to solve our problems and others’ problems: character becomes our destiny, a destiny we can shape with our own hands, because we are not pieces of wood swept around by the waves of the ocean of life. We can swim, we can choose our direction by generating all kind resources by ourselves, including strength, intelligence, renunciation, knowledge, and - why not - even beauty and wealth. Good and evil are present everywhere and they can always be transformed into each other.

What is good? What is evil? From an historical mundane point of view these are concepts subject to a constant evolution. To attain more stability we must visualize a higher sphere of existence that is beyond good and evil: this is the sphere of the spiritual dimension, to which we belong ontologically.

## From Eros to Love

Happiness is something like the Phoenix, the mythological Fire Bird, so rare and out of reach. Generally people are familiar with the excitement of the senses, something they mistake for happiness until they discover, in pain and frustration, that excitement is not something that really nourishes us - it is not that happiness to which everyone aspires, on the contrary it often robs body and mind of their valuable energies. Genuine and lasting happiness, also called bliss or ananda, in Sanskrit, is realized by living in full awareness of the higher reality and is characterized by permanence and immutability, therefore it is completely independent from what is external to the self.

Love cannot be invented: it is either there, or not there. If it is not there we must develop it, but it is important to understand what it is and distinguish it from imitations.

The purpose of life is to give and receive love. Every time we cheat we cheat ourselves, every time we betray we betray ourselves - every time we fail someone, it’s our own failure.

An individual may possess the entire world, but if he doesn’t have good relationships, he remains miserable. He can possess gold, he can be supported by a political party or be at the head of a great company, have a prestigious position as university professor, but if he is not rich in feelings and does not have satisfying relationships his life will be painful. Because the real wealth of life consists in relationships, good feelings, mutual respect, affection, esteem, friendship, and loyalty.

Today in society we see an inordinate quantity of references to eroticism, both veiled and explicit. We find eroticism everywhere, from the pages of magazines to the commercials in TV, and often while we surf the Web looking for very different things. Even the literary production attached to eroticism is endless because of the excessive value assigned to the erotic attitude, which is creating many physiological problems, including impotence and neurosis, anxiety and panic.

By exaggerating it, inflation has subtracted power to the sense or eroticism, thus creating a sense of void within it.

About the 82% of crimes are triggered by passion and lust, including some of the most heinous crimes and merciless killings. The present society is particularly fond of eroticism because it sells well - it helps the advertisement of products because it excites and allures the fantasy of people. For a less educated and more conditioned public, pushed faster and faster to accept emotional loads that they cannot digest, assimilate and elaborate, this situation is extremely stressful. People are induced to think of themselves in a false way, to have an artificial perception of themselves, to pursue goals that can never possibly be attained, and inevitably they only find disappointment and depression. When an individual has a young and healthy body - maybe even an attractive body - eroticism does not seem so bad and the body can be used like a pleasure-generating toy, but human life passes quickly, its four seasons quickly follow one another, and that toy that used to give so much pleasure will soon become a source of suffering. At the same time, the individual is always induced to strongly identify himself with it: his psyche will be stuck, rooted in that physical perception, and he will be incapable of perceiving himself in his original spiritual nature.

The journey to freedom requires shifting one’s attention from eroticism to love. Imagine you are the new Argonauts - we could say Intronauts, embarked in a journey to explore the self: eroticism keeps you far from success, constantly distracting you from the inner world by magnetizing your attention towards the external world and making you fall victim to other people and things outside you.

Someone could misunderstand this point and think that this body has no value, but this is not our conclusion. The Vedas explain that there are different dimensions, different spheres of existence, and we can live in different ways according to the direction of consciousness we choose: depending where we stand at the level of our consciousness we will experience a specific quality of life, a world of joys and sorrows. If.we raise our consciousness level and consequently our perspective, we will see pain decreasing, uncertainty dissolving, anxiety and fear disappearing. If we raise higher still we can have a darshana, a direct perception of our own immortality, and the body we wear appears to us as very temporary, albeit not less valuable. The body is always the same, but our new perception depends on our new perspective. The body does not lose its value, rather the more we understand its reality, its peculiar reality, the more we will be able to use it better, and the less illusions will be created. Illusions are very attractive, but they inevitably entail disappointments, which are painful.

If a child only has one toy, when you try to take it away from him he will clutch his finger and grind his teeth, refusing to give it up, no matter how many good reasons you will offer to him. But if you offer him a more attractive toy, something of a better quality, he will be ready to give up the old one. Thus it is not sufficient to say, “stop eroticism!” The secret is to sublimate this energy, elevate our perspective and deeply meditate on the real meaning of love, on its effects, on genuine experiences of love and on the transforming, enlivening and healing power of love.

In fact, just like in the case of many other concepts, we have even lost the original meaning of the word eros, which for Plato and for all ancient Greeks did not define the instinct of the flesh binding two lovers to each other, but rather a cosmic force of union that is meant to elevate the spirit, an aspiration towards all that is good and beautiful.

The concept of ananda, that we have already mentioned, is one of the three inalienable characteristics of the living being, together with awareness and eternity. Ananda is fundamentally love, albeit a love that flies very high, similar to the love we only know by name. We all know that everyone wants to give and receive love. Even the word love is today subject to a sort of inflation, easily misunderstood and lending itself to an endless series of sentimentalistic ideas, but in fact even when deformed it remains the most powerful force that can move actions, thoughts, creativity - something that occupies such a great part of our existence that we cannot avoid to consider it.

But what are we talking about, when we speak of love? There is love for a spouse, love for motherland, for one’s parents, for one’s children, for one’s brothers, love for one’s work, for a pet cat, love for classical music, love for nature or love for God. All these forms of love (and who knows how many more we could add to the list) have different shades, but we will analyze them by referring to some larger categories, or “love-greed” and “love-gift”. In the first category we search something we like and need, something that we lack in a sense, and that therefore seems to be capable of enriching us. In this case the subject is concentrated in obtaining something, in satisfying his own “love-greed” and his attitude is therefore prominently selfish. On the other hand, in the case of “love-gift” the prevailing sentiment is giving rather than taking - giving something or giving oneself. Here the attitude of the subject is oriented towards the outside. It seems, however, that these two movements, one to the inside and the other to the outside, can and actually should exist at the same time in the human being, who needs both to love and to be loved, as we have already mentioned.

Speaking of balance, in life in general and in love relationships as well, we need to properly harmonize two elements, sentiment and rationality. Sentiment without rationality becomes sentimentalism, something that does not benefit anyone and obscures reality. On the other hand rationality without sentiment becomes a dry attitude creating psychological and emotional deserts, and everything becomes barren. Rationality must help us to verify the validity of sentiments, and sentiments must give light and taste to rationality. Fiery passions burn people to ashes and finally they even consume themselves, because they are totally based on bodies and objects and therefore on temporary things that are destined to be destroyed by time in spite of all our efforts to preserve them. As eternal beings we aspire to infinite and eternal things, things that last forever; this dimension of love does not pertain to the human sphere but to the divine sphere, as explained by texts that are thousands of years old and by the realizations of many mystics. In this perspective the absolute protagonist, the unsurpassed model, is God, whose unconditional Love is primarily manifested in the action of caring for creatures and for creation, in the effort to free us from oppression, from the real slavery of conditionings. Man is expected to develop the same attitude and establish for himself the goal of loving others with selfless benevolence without necessarily expecting something in return, by sacrificing his time, his energies and even his life to realize his own spiritual nature and to help others in the same pursuit.

Let us now examine four categories of love by utilizing a pattern I have devised to better understand the phenomenon.

The first category is passionate love, mainly consisting in physical attraction for another person, together with a strong emotional interaction. Passionate love is thus a mixture of physical and emotional factors, where emotions are strongly connected with the body and with a series of biologic and physiologic phenomena; in the ensuing relationship between two person who share this level of love, emotions and the physical component will have a primary role, creating a number of perception - smells, tastes, colours, actions and thoughts that will have specific characteristics according to the mixture of tamas and rajas guna. Tamas is lethargy, heaviness, dullness, sleep, dirtiness, oblivion, mental confusion. Tamas requires and favours a lack of consciousness, a diminished power of intellect. On the other hand rajas is movement, intensity, burning and acute desire, all connected to the attainment of a result, generally within the purview of prakriti or matter, so when rajas dominates we will have elation, tension, anxiety, action, impulsiveness. Therefore rajas and tamas are respectively inertia and action, darkness on one side, and blinding lightning, sleep and elation on the other side: this combination is the foundation of passional love, where one needs to obscure the intellect and silence the voice of reason, while instincts prevail. Passion is indicated, defined and perceived like a river in spate, murky and warm. Tamas has “anaesthetized” the intellect and thus one continues to do things that rationally he wouldn’t do, and that overwhelm him.

Fundamentally and inevitably (given the circumstances) a relationship based on passionate love cannot be a lasting one; probably it will be a stormy and short-lived affair characterized by major and sudden changes of attitudes and moods, often tainted by selfishness, neurosis and even betrayals. People dominated by rajoguna have in fact a strong tendency to adultery, as rajas is synonym of burning lust, strong need to do things, agitation, anxiety, excitement, that will transform into depression and finally again plunge the individual in the darkness of tamas.

A typical, very famous example of passionate love has been wonderfully represented in Shakespeare’s Othello, where the diabolical influence of Iago fans the protagonist’s uncontrolled and unhealthy passion for his woman, Desdemona, into burning jealousy, madness, and finally mourning.

A higher level of love is sentimental love, characterized by attraction focused on the qualities of an individual rather than on his/her physical appearance. At this level physical attraction and emotional interactions are still strong but are less important, because one considers qualities that are connected with the sattvic platform, or the platform of reality. In sentimental love one starts (starting only, because there is still a long way to go from here) to consider the inner qualities or a person, although the presence of rajoguna still maintains some attachment to the temporary and illusory forms of existence. At this stage there is still emotional interaction, but emotions shift from the physical level to the mental level.

Sentimental love is a combination of the rajas and sattva gunas. Sattva translates as generosity, goodness, cleanliness, intelligence, foresight, acumen. This sattvic understanding is aimed at what is good to everybody and differs from the rajasic form of intelligence that only looks for what is favourable to one’s ego. Sattvaguna is the lightest energy of the prakriti, the most radiant and highest, and in its most elevated form it enables one to see reality as it is. While rajas is like a deforming lens that does not allow us to see reality as it is, sattva is fundamentally characterized by the vision of reality or a clearer approach to reality.

This analysis belongs to one of the most delicate sectors of psychology — the psychology of sentiments. This is an extraordinarily complex field, but also very useful for those who become expert in it. The work we do here will indeed be rewarding, because through the knowledge of these dynamics we become able to manage sentiments and feelings instead of being swept away by them. Most people speak about love and feelings of affection in a fatalistic way, as if they were exclusively governed to fate, if there were some forces that above and beyond individual choices could determine the events of life - but this is not true. Or better, it is true for those who do not have an adequate level of knowledge, which obviously should not be mere theory but involve an intelligent and protected experience. For this type of love, too, we can find a good example in a famous tragedy written by Shakespeare: Romeo and Juliet. Here we see the presence of ideals situated on the mental platform rather than on concrete reality. A relationship based on sentimental love will still be obfuscated in regard to reality, but in a much lesser extent than the passionate love.

Rising still, we find ideal love, mainly characterized by sattvaguna. Our figure shows there are still some small proportions of rajas and tamas-, we are not yet on the level of pure sattva, therefore we still see the effect of forces that bind us to the level of physical elements. However, although we still are in the sphere of immanence, it is a level of higher love because it contemplates the ideal qualities of an individual. We mentioned that in sentimental love we focus on the inner qualities of a person; in ideal love we go even deeper and we can perceive the higher and more intimate nature of these same qualities. As we approach the sphere of sattvaguna, or the level of actual reality, this kind of love will be a good way of uniting people in the relationships it supports. At this point clashes practically disappear and relationships become serene, pleasurable and long lasting.

In this specific case the reciprocal exchange of love is not an end in itself or intended to satisfy body and mind or to attain mundane goals, but it is generally a journey that two persons consciously decide to make together with the intent of helping each other in the realization of their spiritual nature and in the development of bhakti, pure love for God, something on which we will discuss later on.

Finally, transcendental love takes us to a much higher quality level, because in this case we fall in love with the spiritual characteristics of an individual, that are and remain our focus. This type of love, based on pure sattvaguna, free from any tinge of rajas and tamas, is not subject to failure, betrayal or disasters, because it is built on eternal elements, concepts and purposes that transcend the world of matter and therefore is not subject to space-time paradigms. This is in fact pure love, divine love that reaches out to the Supreme and involves a reciprocal exchange between God and his devotee on a level of consciousness and emotions that does not belong to the world of prakriti.

Saints of all traditions have lived this integrating and transcendental experience of divine love that is also reflected in a cosmic love for all creatures. For example, in their mutual relationship and in their relationship with creation and creatures St. Francis and Clara were certainly situated on this level and reflected divine love in all their actions and feelings.

This transcendent love or bhakti, pure love for God, is obtained through divine mercy: it is not a human faculty. Success descends from above... it invests us, sweeps us away, catches us with the typical force of love, suddenly solves all remaining problems; it dazzles, fascinates, drags the loved and the lover in an union that is impossible to express or explain, but can only be experienced by living it.

It does exist: all traditions speak about it. Persons of great intelligence, virtue and merits have abandoned royal palaces, places of power, prestigious positions, sacrificing youth, beauty, money, in order to have this experience. It is an overwhelming experience, and for one who has tasted it, it is impossible to live without it. It is compared to light, to happiness, to the infinite.

But the real mystic, the real yogi, the genuine saint who has tasted this experience does not despise, demonize or deny the world, but rather appreciates it. Why? Because he sees it as a manifestation of the Supreme and as a laboratory where one can experiment on liberation.

The world is a project and offers special opportunities to attain perfection. The perfection of transcendent love can not be attained without work, without acting in the world, without relating with the creation and the creatures, but we can attain perfection through daily experience in the world, through the world itself. Working in the world, executing work for the supreme good, offering models of reality for the pleasure of offering others the same opportunity for such an experience and partly also for the pleasure that one gets simply from working for the benefit of others. This is the characteristic of love when it is devoid of the frenzied search for selfish pleasure, when we discover that we get pleasure by giving pleasure.

These issues are important for the life of everyone of us, not only for a cultural or formal education; their analysis has a strong influence on the deeper mind, on that part of the personality that is not so well known to the individual himself, but that activates the processes shaping his life. These mechanisms operate at a deeper level in the mind, where usually the conscious self cannot access: the subconscious. We must therefore seriously consider not only the aspects of the conscious personality but also the emotional aspect that is much more difficult to manage. If indeed changing the direction of thoughts takes time, resources, effort, dedication and determination, it is still nothing compared to what is required to work on emotions.

Adjusting a point of view or a thought pattern can be demanding, but it’s nothing when compared to what is required to correct, heal, and rectify a situation on the emotional level involving affection or impulses. Yet, these aspects of the deep psyche constituting the foundation of everybody’s character are generally very much neglected because Western psychology has mostly studied the extroverted mind, or better that part of the mind that functions in an extroverted way, the relationship between the mind and the world, the mind and the objects. Even universities and specialists rarely consider the introverted functions, the interaction between the mind and the self, the mind and the higher self, the mind and the roots of personality.

The great majority of people believe they are their own emotions, their own aspirations, their own ideas, because they identify with these psychic products so that they appear as their very essence. However, by analyzing some “witness meditation” techniques and experiencing them with a sufficiently mature and conscious detachment, we will discover that it is not so. Someone could ask, “if I am the witness, who is acting?”... Well, even in this field we need to discover that actually we are not so much the actors, just as we are not what we think we are. In fact we are moved by automatic responses of the mind that take care of the primary needs of life, of functions that are considered essential in relationship with our fellow humans in society and with the entire environment.

What the five senses tell us, and especially how they tell it, is not reality.

What the senses can tell us is very limited but it is possible, through a correct knowledge and practice, to come to see a world that appears much more complex, vast and especially more interesting.

Consciousness is the cause of everything that happens to us, for good or bad, in joy or sorrow; it is also the cause of our changing moods, and partially also of our encounters. In fact we meet (or don’t meet) people according to our specific vibrations, to the contents of our psyche and to our unconscious desires coming from that part of ourselves we just mentioned and that, while hidden to the self, has a very strong influence on the individual and determines his destiny.

Therefore we should ask ourselves, as suggested by the incipit of the Vedanta, what is our true nature, who we really are. The embodied being, albeit “carrying a body” and therefore described as female or male, must not identify with that identity because gender and body are temporary facts, a mask deriving from guna and karma, samskaras and vasanas, from our individual tendencies.

The sage does not destroy the ego completely, but keeps it under the protection of reason, of a higher consciousness, so in spite of being in a male body he does not think he is a male. Or, being born on a specific day he does not think he is that old, but rather that he has a body with that age. He has relatives, disciples or masters, but he does not think that such relationships are physical or mental, but he rather sees them as connections with eternal beings, on a higher level.

In our study we should look further, take a journey in evolution and transformation that will lead us precisely from Eros, or the contact with the body, to Love, the contact with the self: from the lower self to the higher self.

Real knowledge is wisdom, not simply a heap of theoretical data but a special awareness, a realized knowledge to which the ancient philosophical spirits aspired. Between the data and their realization there is a huge difference, described with great precision by the science of Vedas even at the language level.

As we have seen, the first level of knowledge is the level of “names”, or nama\\ this is a very superficial level that comprehends only a very small part of the nature of what we are studying.

A greater layer of reality is rupa, the knowledge of form that together with the name immediately offers more strength to our understanding. However, rupa does not simply refer to form, but also includes colour, smell, texture, sound etc.

The topic we will discuss now is an introduction to the basic knowledge of the self and of the bodymind system.

We are not the ego. The ego is a structure, made of all the conditionings that have determined that type of body, that type of mentality, a set of tastes and so on. Attaining freedom from that conditioning and overcoming the ego is the goal of spiritual search, of spiritual realization.

Eroticism pertains to what is finite, temporary, a transitory personality, while love belongs to the atman, it is precisely one of its fundamental and ontological characteristics. For this reason each creature needs to receive and give love. Then, due to conditionings, to the combination of the gunas and to a series of alterations of the consciousness, we will have a number of conditions (more of less neurotic and pathologic) in our relationships and affections, but all of them will be based on that inexhaustible spark of divine love that exists in each living entity.

What is distorted is consciousness, not the self. All deformations of consciousness at the basis of personality disturbances can be cured with proper therapies: in no stage a person is considered incurable. One who knows the science of the soul, the science of the self, knows that the bhakti therapy, the therapy of divine love, is wonderfully efficient: it enables us to break the external coverings and to reach the person who has locked himself up in a fortress he had started to build to defend himself and that later became his prison and sometimes his tomb.

There is a cosmic order that can be expressed with the Sanskrit word dharma-, this order pervades and maintains everything, from the blade of grass to the great heavenly bodies, from the mosquito to the whale, up to the Nobel laureate scientist: all their physical and psychic bodies are governed by dharma.

When the mind comes in conflict with dharma the psychic balance deteriorates and hence mental disease develops; when on the contrary we work in harmony with dharma we develop a good psychophysical health, fortune and peace of mind.

Dormant good sentiments we did not know we had, suddenly appear on the level of consciousness if we associate with individuals who are by nature carrying those good sentiments. At the same time vices and perversions that we probably weren’t conscious about (we didn’t know that our subconscious was haunted by such “monsters”) will surface to consciousness when we associate with people who carry them in themselves. Eroticism is the product of a tangled mass of conditionings; it may be based on love, but it is a kind of love which is polluted, tainted with slag. We can remove the contamination from eroticism by sublimating its contents. The psychological process of sublimation is very important and happens when some of the psychic ingredients are taken upwards and transformed through a process that is supported and guided by wisdom and will power. Blind will power cannot help because it denies, suppresses, represses and damages the impulse, while impulse should be treated like a child, giving it space to explain its reasons, recognizing the correct ones (in case there are any) and then convincing it to give up distorted fantasies.

The management of psychic and sexual energy through sublimation can lead us to levels of activity that we may not even be able to imagine at present.

One who applies techniques with wisdom, determination and consistency can develop his faculties and produce literary, artistic, social, humanitarian work. In other words, we must transform negative energy in positive energy, destructive impulses in constructive impulses that are always the basis of success both in private and public life, in intra-psychic relationships and in the relationships with the outside world.

Impulses are forces of nature, they must not be condemned wholesale but rather managed and directed towards the development of personality, character and sentiments; in this way we can raise the quality of life.

A sexual impulse that cannot be satisfied due to moral or social limitations creates pain. Generally people don’t think of sublimation, but they consider two different options: the removal or denial of the impulse on one side, and the satisfaction of the impulse on the other side. Both methods produce damages. We have already discussed repression and denial: it simply hides the impulse from the sight of the individual, who then loses his chances to control and modify it.

The satisfaction of all impulses depletes all internal resources, creates chronic fatigue, lack of interest in life, melancholy and hedonism, and sometimes conscious or subconscious suicidal tendencies.

Love means acknowledging the reality of others. There is no difference between us and the others, in fact what we do to others comes back to us, for good or bad; we are all completely connected just like all things are connected among themselves in this universe - they are interacting and interdependent.

On the most elevated platform we find love for all creatures, as well as for the Creator and the creation. We cannot be cruel with creatures and think that we love their Creator, or worship the Creator and despise creation, or again say we love and respect the animals or the environment and not show any interest for the Creator. The three aspects of Creator, creation and creatures are inseparable; respect and appreciation for these three aspects is a first step in the development of bhakti, love and devotion, the faith that loves, the divine love that includes all other categories of love.

This supreme love has an eternal nature as well, and in fact it is called nitya bhakti. Each one of us is potentially rich in this supreme love but due to the innumerable experiences we have had as embodied souls in the world of matter, this spark of pure love has been covered by a thick layer of egotism and conditionings. The Vedas explain that human life is meant to awaken bhakti, to be able to live it fully and transmit it. Among so many dharmas, says Bhagavata Parana, the supreme dharma for the human being is yato bhaktir adhokshaje, or the development of bhakti for Adhokshaja, God, “who cannot be perceived through material senses”.

However, when we talk of bhakti we do not intend a theoretical love only, something that directs us to reject the world and the body, deny the senses, and erase the mind in the name of a total vacuum. No: bhakti is experienced on the level of the sense as well, within this world. We can have spiritual experiences with our hands, tongue, ears, nose, eyes, spiritual experiences confirmed by all the acharyas, great saints and Masters who have enlightened humankind in the course of millennia. The path of bhakti is not a sad, painful, melancholic and boring practice: many people still hold this prejudice about spiritual life. Rather, it is the opposite: susukham kartum avyayam, this path is extremely pleasurable, and it practiced easily and joyfully. The more the consciousness field is purified, widened and elevated, the more we become able to give up attachments and uproot anarthas, the more this supreme happiness explodes with intense fulfilment of the experiencer, who perceives his own nature as atmarama, fulfilled in the self.

Real, genuine love, the love each one of us is trying to give and receive from the heart of our hearts, is not at all different from bhakti.

Eroticism pushes people to search for erotic zones to pasture their senses, while in bhakti one searches for the heart to share elevated sentiments and develop that power of love that is so beneficial for all creatures.

As the great saints of all traditions teach us, without the Gum, the spiritual Master, prema bhakti cannot be developed. We can invent some process to develop ego and eroticism, but this kind of approach will not work for bhakti. This is why in Gita, IV.34 Krishna states: “Know that, if you will approach them humbly, by asking questions and serving them, those who see the truth will impart you the knowledge”.,

This is the prescribed system, and without following it the exalted goal of prema bhakti remains unattainable. The Guru teaches the disciple how to develop Divine Love, love for God, through practical and daily application of that same love towards all the creatures.

The Vedic scriptures, starting from Shvetash-vatara Upanishad, state that parabhakti is focused on the spiritual Master and God. To those who equally direct this supreme bhakti towards God and the Guru, knowledge is revealed, the sacred wisdom opens, enlightenment is granted. Of course parabhakti, the source of all love feelings, is not obtained without a price. Also in this case and even more than in other relationships, the exchange goes in both directions and develops, like all concrete and genuine relationships, mostly by being together, cooperating to a project aimed at spiritual realization — ours and others’. However it is not sufficient to see the spiritual Master as the means or the bridge to attain God: this vision still contains some selfishness debris. Without genuine Love for the Guru it is not possible to cross that bridge. We can perceive it, we can understand it rationally, but we cannot cross over it: this is one of the last traps of illusion: seeing the Guru as a bridge or an instrument. However, Shvetashvatara Upanishad states that we can attain and receive enlightenment only through love. The Guru is not God, but a servant and lover of God, and therefore he can teach the supreme art of divine love. Gita (XVIII.54) says that when a person is free from the influence of the gunas he makes the greatest discovery and obtains the highest gift, love for God: “Having become Brahman \[having reclaimed the consciousness of one's own spiritual nature\] and being internally satisfied, he does not lament or hanker; he is equally disposed towards all beings and obtains my supreme love”.

This type of love, this capability of loving, is therefore unknown to those who maintain a selfish attitude towards life. In this case the original love feeling, bhakti., is transformed into kama that fundamentally means possession and enjoyment. Love means giving, but then it becomes taking and snatching; the natural feeling of generosity turns into greed and the pleasure of giving pleasure becomes neurotically focused on ego. At that point the atman cannot perceive himself anymore and cannot even perceive others: instead of experiencing reality, he lives in a world of distorted perceptions.

On the other hand by developing bhakti the individual can find himself, harmonize all his faculties and functions, introverted and extroverted, and again establish his eternal relationship with the Supreme, involving a benevolent attitude towards the entire environment and love for all creatures, irrespective of the body they wear at that time.

Reclaiming one’s connection with the self means reconnecting to a source of unlimited power and energy; in this energy there is not only the awareness of immortality but also a very intense feeling of happiness, a consideration that enables us to “close the circle” of our analysis. The happiness emanating from bhakti, from the loving relationship with God and creatures, is totally fulfilling and directly satisfies our real self, the soul (prasannatma), not simply the body or the mind. It is never diminished or lost, nor transformed into its contrary: it is the centre of gravity of the self, the heart of life, an integrating feeling that increasingly fills us up.

The path of love, bhaktimarga, triumphs over the titanic powers of Nature, neutralizing all their conscious and subconscious contents that have destructive and self-destructive charges. Love for God is the ripe fruit of the human and spiritual life. Bhakti means reconnecting to God with the pleasure born from a verifiable relationship of mutual affection, because God is not absent or insensitive in front of sincere devotion, but rather He reciprocates with His own devotee by the same kind of sentiment that is offered to Him. The devotee will find that God is indeed very near and will thus be able to harmonize everything on the higher level of transcendence.

Tantras offer different perspectives or darshanas-, for example the Gautamiya Tantra states:

“I offer my respectful obeisance to the Guru, who opened my eyes closed by ignorance, with the torchlight of spiritual knowledge”.

In all the traditions derived from the Vedas we see that the Guru grants the vision, dissipates ignorance, enlightens the disciple and finds the best way to do that according to the disciple’s nature, therefore he will use the solar path for some, the lunar path for others. These are the path of immediate and direct light, or the path of shadows that gradually transforms the consciousness of the individual to bring him to the light.

Bhagavata Purana states that one should worship the Supreme by choosing one of the three paths (methods) by which sacrifice is accepted. Sacrifice means “the action of making sacred” and it can be started with thoughts, then executed with words and then with actions. When work in the world is the result of thinking, speaking and acting in a purified way, they become spiritual activities that purify those who perform them and all those who take part in them.

“Artificial imitation of elaborate Vedic sacrifices will give no good results, and certainly it will not give the perfection of life. One should rather perform sacrifices according to the injunctions of the Scriptures and the teachings of the Lord, who recommends the chanting of His holy names for this era”.

Let’s see now what is the meaning of Tantra in the context of Vedic literature and in our modern society, what is the practice of the left hand in the Agamas, what is its path, the beginning and the goal.

The path of Tantra is also called the path of the five M’s: it begins with mada, that means ‘madness’ (the same term applies to liquors, that generate madness). In the 60s many people used intoxicating drugs with the idea of attaining liberation. A kind of priest, professor at Berkeley university, called Timothy Leary, literally preached to his students that they had to take LSD in order to break free from social prejudice. This is an example of tantra. However, he damaged an entire generation of young people, as did Rajnesh and others who induced people to tantra without explaining them what is the correct path to follow.

The sacred nature of Tantra has been practically lost and more or less reduced to sense gratification, but sex and other practices become self-destructive when they are separated from a genuine perspective of personal growth. They can seem pleasurable in the beginning and make them feel liberated, but then one finds himself more confused and weaker than before.

As we mentioned, the path of the left hand uses five instruments, the first being mada. The second is mamsa, or eating meat. This is also a form of intoxication because the violence required to kill the animal and eat its body while it is still warm and bleeding can push one out from the control of the ego, just like the intoxication caused by mada, by speed or by fear (some people love watching thriller movies because scary scenes have the power to push us out from the ordinary consciousness and from the mind patterns of social structure where we are imprisoned). The third is matsya, fish, and it has the same function of mamsa. The fourth is madra, the ritual gestures that can be instrumental in the transformation of the character. The Tantric texts were not written in the Middle Ages as some people believe: they are extremely ancient and they are already mentioned in the Bhagavata Parana. During the Middle Ages they have become popular in society and subsequently they have been diffused in the West, but always quite superficially.

Instead of the madras, the ancient Greeks and Romans used theatrical masks: if we want to play the role of an angry person, a suitable mask will better convey the feeling. In a similar way some gestures (precisely the madras) help us to attain the state of consciousness we desire. This also happens by setting the mind in a particular way - the solar path, for example. In his Yogasutras, Patanjali explains that if we fix our mind in the meditation on the Guru, a saintly figure, we approach that state of consciousness, while by fixing the mind on a lion that devours its prey or on some other scene of violence, consciousness exactly slides to where our attention is focused. The powerful force of the model shapes the consciousness.

The fifth instrument is maithuna, the ritualistic sexual intercourse where the subject attains a kind of relaxation that is necessary to liberate him, through the release of blocked biologic and psychic forces, from the conditioning of “normality”, consisting of a series of inhibitions, constrictions, and mental cages. The Guru could choose that type of experience to throw a particular disciple beyond a specific pattern of consciousness.

A great researcher and scholar, Danielou, explains that in the Agamas the path led to a higher and higher abstraction. The process started with sublimation and in the intermediate stages it was rejected by the application of repulsion, until the practitioner came to the stage when mada meant becoming conscious of the dangers of anger, intoxication and of the influence of the external world on the internal world.

Those who evolved through the highest levels of tantra transformed the initial practices in a totally different way: instead of hunting animals and devouring meat, mamsa became killing the internal beasts like kama, kroda, lobha, moha, matsara (lust, anger, greed, illusion and envy). This idea was also elaborated by Nietzsche with his tale of the superman who must conquer the five ferocious animals within.

The Agamas also speak about cooking the fish of jealousy, evil intentions, and misperceptions of the intellect, in order to attain a clear vision, the eternal goal of the risbis, the Vedic sages. In fact, their works are called darsbana or visualizations.

The madras become gestures to worship the divine spirit that pervades the world and similarly maitbuna becomes a loving connection with the Godhead who is present in all things.

Even in religions that have been strongly schematised like Catholicism we can find examples or perfectly erotic ecstasies of great mystics, who did not know anything about tantra.

A Jesuit priest called Nobili had visited India and sent Tantric texts and the Bbagavata Parana to the

Vatican, explaining that the local populations were not heathens or polytheists, but had the concept of one God, a Supreme Divine Personality who takes any form when He decides to descend when and how He likes. The Vatican answered that the priest’s job was to be a missionary, to give and not to take.

Western people had therefore already discovered the existence of well geared instruments of thought that could transform the character and the impulses.

Some individuals are just above the animal level, others (although very rare) are already liberated in this life, as elaborately explained in the Vedic literature where we read the description of thejivan muktas. It is evident that different instruments, methods and practices will be required for these two different categories of people and for all the intermediate categories as well.

Let’s see what works must be executed: first of all the transmutation of energy. Naturally each individual is allowed to start with the means he has, otherwise there will be too much disadvantage for those who have no chance of evolution compared to those who can evolve. In order for everyone to have this opportunity, the different individuals must be allowed to evolve from the level where they are currently situated, just patients with different pathologies will be admitted to a hospital; the diseases may be more or less serious and the treatments will be different but the program is the same for all. The Vedas, too, have a program for everyone, that consists in the liberation from illusions and conditionings, so whether one uses the Vedic way or the Tantric way or a mixture of the two, the important thing is that the Guru must be a great Master of life and knowledge, capable of recognizing which path is best for a disciple: the first, the second, or more likely, a mixture of the two.

The Vedas state th^t both paths (the left hand and the right hand) are genuine ways to attain liberation from conditionings. Methadone is a substitute narcotic in the treatment of heroine addiction: although it is a drug itself, it constitutes a cure for drug addicts. In the same way the Tantras work like a detoxifying therapy: they are not the goal but if executed correctly under the guidance of a true Guru they enable one to reach the goal.

Methadone, however, should not become a mere substitute for heroine, as it may happen sometimes when the addict just switches from one addiction to the other. The idea is to gradually substitute a powerful intoxicating substance with another which is less powerful and helps the patient to overcome addiction; in the same way Tantras and Agamas offer a kind of gradual detoxifying process. The path of transformation of the psychic and sexual energy is also a primary purpose of the Tantra.

In the solar path these attachments are automatically solved as they belong to the night of consciousness, to the primitive aspect of the being. We need to clarify here that such a primitive aspect is not defined historically at a collective level: an attachment that is already overcome for someone may still be at the dawn of personal development for others. An artificial attempt of unification of the various religions in the world, by force or by denial, can only complicate the problems and make them more violent.

Different religions are absolutely necessary for the different levels of evolution of the various individuals. To reach the top of a mountain we can take very different paths, but the nearer we come to the top, the more we see the paths converge, so that at the top all the paths become one.

The top of the mountain is pure love, the origin of all love, also the source of degenerated love. Love degenerates due to the action of the prism of the ego that refracts and distorts it in a thousand different ways, just like a beam of white light becomes multicoloured while meeting with a prism crystal. There are so many types of spirituality: the aborigines for example worship the wind, lightning, the mountain, the forest, the spirit in the bear... and these are all forms of religiosity that we should appreciate, encourage and recognize. If we don’t, this means that our realization puzzle still has several important pieces missing. These same aborigines can be helped to access higher levels, although such help must be simply offered and not imposed, because freedom is the principle of the path, to reach love. Love without freedom is not love anymore: it has already turned into something else.

The purpose of the Vedas is to help the auspicious journey of thejiva who wishes to be awakened, so that he can become free from death and from the pain of the repetition of births and deaths, by travelling on a path of knowledge and elevation, towards the awareness of immortality and bliss via the connection with the super consciousness.

Krishna in Bhagavadgita explains to Arjuna that, if he cannot totally surrender to Him, he can work for Him. If he can’t do that, he can at least offer Him the fruits of his activities, and so on. Krishna clarifies that the goal of life is surrendering to Him, but He knows that genuine surrender cannot be forced, because artificial imposition would create further conditionings.

There are many paths that ultimately lead to bhakti and also many realizations of God and various ways to perceive Him, as Brahman, Paramatma or Bhagavan. Brahman corresponds to the Spirit of Western theology, or the omnipresent, all powerful, all pervading and impersonal aspect of God. By reading works like The Heavenly Rule of Lao Tzu we find the writer absorbed in the vision of the all-pervading Spirit: this is a dimension of spiritual realization.

Paramatma is God in the localized form. Each living entity - a dog, a cow, a dog-eater, a learned brahmana — can be seen not simply as an expansion of God but as God Himself, not in external appearance but in essence. Few people have eyes to see the essence, because sensory perception focuses on solid matter, on things we can touch, but those who have a vision that penetrates physical matter and are able to see the essence of creatures can enter into a spiritual relationship with them: this is how St. Francis could talk to a famished wolf, or how Daniel sat in the pit of lions, or Chaitanya made deer and tigers dance.

Everything depends on the level of consciousness, which corresponds to the development of our psychic and even sexual energies. The waste of sexual energy depletes our psychic energy: as explained in Ayurveda, the medical science of ancient India, psyche is influenced by the seventh data that is shukra, sperm. The waste of sperm is a problem for the physical body and also destabilizes psychic structure; concentration and determination decrease, while the search for self realization requires the maximum activation of such faculties.

Just like in the material world it is possible to lose one’s way in a forest, in the psychic world it is possible to lose one’s way in a labyrinth without end, because there are different dimensions in reality. Today the practices of Tantra are not recommended because of the very great danger of losing the main path and end up in alienation alleys. One of the reasons why these rituals were kept secret, even in Rome and in the European Middle Ages, was the high incidence of very serious accidents, in other words, the number of people who went stark mad.

My work has offered me the opportunity to witness phenomena of ecstasy and pathologic stupor, that can actually be described as mental paralysis. In a holy place of India, on the bank of the river Yamuna, I once met a person who believed he had become a peacock.

Regarding the relationship between the individual being and the Supreme being, we can say that the supreme complete emanates beings who are complete in themselves and of the same nature of the whole; therefore the complete, by emanating its parts, is never diminished and never loses anything. This totally subverts our material mind patterns since, when we subtract something from a material object, even a tiny fragment, that object is not complete any more.

Brihadaranyaka Upanishad explains that we must consider perfection and completeness as our spiritual nature, and that we come back to that perfection through spiritual discipline and the reunion with God (yoga) through bhakti.

The communion or relationship between the supreme being and individual being does not destroy individuality. In fact the quality is identical but the identities remain separate: this is the foundation of love, implying a relationship between two entities.

Narcissism is a deformation of personality, a pathologic condition; a narcissist is generally also egotistic and selfish, all concentrated on himself, clashing against the world and in an opposite position in respect to the principle of love. It is extremely difficult to work or live with a narcissist.

Eroticism is a dark forest inhabited by unlimited animal instincts producing a mixture of pleasure and pain. In our ontological constitution there is no pain (dubkha) but rather ananda, bliss, with all the categories of suhka or happiness; this is why pain seems so artificial to us, and all creatures try to escape from it. Even a sadistic or masochistic individual resorts to pain only to get pleasure because his mind is distorted and deformed by a conditioning.

In a sattvic environment all the symptoms are sattvic, while in a rajasic environment all the symptoms become coloured with passion, intense desires, jealousy, and envy, ultimately leading to violence. Where the environment is tamasic there is a blockage of energies and depression increases.

A sage is emotionally free; he has developed vairagya, a great quality that enables one to remain detached from the events and observe them in an objective way, so experience is not marred by confusion. Vairagya is born from abhyasa, constant discipline. Living in this way is a wonderful experience; the sages do not even consider the idea they could be living in a different way: it would be a plain, dull life, devoid of inspiration. It is difficult to explain the feelings of a healthy life to a person who has always been diseased: he will not even believe that health may exist.

*Does the presence of erotically oriented images in the city environment capture our psychic energy? While listening to you, my concentration was heightened and my restless mind became more peaceful. I even had an intuition on how to solve a problem connected with the organization of my work. I think that this erotic psychism hampers the concentration of the mind. So in order to arrive at sublimation, sexual energy must absolutely concentrate on one point, to get out of “Matrix” and avoid the paralysing effect of this psychism on our psychic energy. Is this true?*

It is precisely true. However, this sexual psychism invading the media is deliberate, it has been planned carefully. The financial establishment, the multinationals and big finance groups, use sex like a bait to sell their products, so they load the advertisement market with a huge quantity of sexuality. On one side this diminishes the actual value of sexual energy but on the other side it attracts the psyche of the weakest people, precisely because sexual attraction is a natural force that contains a fundamental and sacred principle: continuation of life, procreation.

The logic of profit brings the managers of the industrial multinational companies to see humankind as a mass of people who must consume products, but the massive exploitation of sexual material pollutes the psychic atmosphere. First of all because it vilifies the sexual energy and then because it distracts people and conditions them (especially if they have a weak will power) in the choices, even for objects to which sex has absolutely no connection.

*Ego distorts the clear perception of things; is it possible to get rid of this obstacle?*

We must be careful not to demolish our ego prematurely, because this, too, can produce alienation. We can maintain our ego and make it lighter by the clear and bright influence of sattva; at that point it will not be a burden any more, it will not be something that confuses our direction or distorts our vision. It is like wearing eyeglasses with transparent lenses: the self can see through the mind and the ego without distortions.

When a person is affected by materialism or even by an excess of rationalization, all his evaluation capacities get blocked at a certain level. Someone who is on a higher level will not underestimate the perceptions of such person or push him to throw away his instruments of perception, rather he will offer him the opportunity to overcome his limits.

We cannot live in the phenomenal reality without an ego, but we should not identify with its expressions like our name, gender, role and so on. However, if we try to work at the level of reality, by remaining connected with what is good and supreme, our actions, too, will be connected to what is good and supreme and their fruits will have the same quality. If our action is directed towards the Divine, even our apparently material activities in the world become a vehicle to bring us higher, closer to our self and to the supreme Self.

If we follow the rules of a divine order, dharma, in choosing our lifestyle, particularly our diet, relationships and social contacts, even if we appear engaged in mundane activities these will help us to overcome our limitations, because they are consciously directed to a higher order of consciousness.

The ego doesn’t have to be eliminated, but we must free ourselves from its dictatorship. The ego is an accident in the journey of consciousness: at a certain point we have taken a wrong turn to the road of psychic involution and the distortion of the historical ego was created. We can undo it through the process of mind decontamination.

Even sexual energy, when channelled.in a project with a high ethical value, is transformed and prevents us from sliding into degenerate forms. If on the contrary we let it wander freely or we merely repress it, sooner or later it will manifest in a violent explosion even on the physical level.

The two energies, the psychic and the sexual, are strictly connected: there is no sexual love or better no sexual relationship that is not characterized by extremely powerful psychic influences. Even in a brutish sexual intercourse, for example in rape, the psychic component is stronger than the physical one; those who want to see the sexual act as a merely physical fact do not understand anything.

The sexual impulse must neither be repressed nor always indulged in without discrimination; we must welcome, redirect, regenerate it. The transformation of the impulse can best happen when we put it in the service of a noble purpose, when its status becomes more elevated.

Then the emotional charge is successfully taken higher, other emotional charges aggregate to it and the converging effect creates a current that takes in many other forming deviations, neutralizing their negative power. When the positive channelling, transformation and sublimation is triggered, its power attracts active degenerations or dormant ones (simply waiting for the moment to come into the effective stage and become destructive energy), absorbs them into the flow of sublimation, and the mind clears up.

*In what measure are we influenced by our coverings, and how can we break free from this conditioning?*

The coverings (koshas) are all those layers that have solidified over the self, birth after birth and death after death, and are kept in place by conditionings.

The grossest and most external layer is called annamaya, or the energy of food, corresponding to the physical body that is formed by consuming food. Next to it, is the vital and energetic covering that supports the body, called pranamaya kosha. In turn, this energy covering is supported by manomaya kosha, the mind covering.

By modifying our mind attitude the energetic body or prana is modified; by modifying this, the constitution of the body is modified, as well as the health of organs, tissues, and systems.

Further upwards we find vijnanamaya kosha, the intellectual covering; above the mind is the intellect, the bright lamp light, the inner sun. One who has a brightly radiant intellect usually does not make serious mistakes, but when the light of intellect dims, troubles and disgraces keep coming.

Finally we have anandamaya kosha, the lightest, subtlest and most radiant covering: the one made of bliss, precisely the one that is closest to the self, the original source of that bliss.

Those who see and live Reality are connected to the centre, the atman, and although they carry a material body they are jivan muktas, liberated in this life. They have penetrated the five coverings born from avidya, the lack of awareness of our own spiritual nature.

An intellect that is blissful and enlightened finds no difficulty in balancing the mind; a controlled mind can dominate the impulses of the senses, and controlled senses are like powerful but obedient horses that help us to experience the world without illusion or delusion.

Krishna tells Arjuna in Gita (III.43):

“Knowing himself to be transcendent to the senses, the mind and the intelligence, o Arjuna, one must stabilize the mind with a resolute spiritual intelligence and thus conquer with spiritual force this insatiable enemy, lust”.

This is an extraordinary principle of sublimation, the last stage that begins by situating oneself above the senses, and therefore by seeing the world from a different perspective, which is much more elevated and true in comparison to the perspective of the sensory system. From here, from this higher level of consciousness, we can begin our journey in the light, a process that dissolves blockages, complexes and old problems of the historical self. Harmony and well being pervade the individual on all his anthropological levels, his relationships run upwards on higher tracks, and he rediscovers the taste for life and love, in the fullest and most real sense of the word.
