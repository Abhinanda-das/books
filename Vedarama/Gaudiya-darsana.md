# Gaudiya-darsana

## Introduction

By the causeless mercy and inspiration of Paramaradhyatama Sri Guru-padapadma Nitya-lila-pravista Om Visnupada astottara-sata Sri Srimad Bhaktivedanta Narayana Gosvami Maharaja we are overjoyed to present the first English edition of this priceless jewel among spiritual discourses entitled Gaudiya-darsana. Based on a lecture by Prabhupada Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura at the Albert Hall, Calcutta, on 11th August 1929, Gaudiya-darsana was originally printed in Bengali in the Gaudiya weekly journal. This English translation is based on the third Bengali edition printed by the Gaudiya Mission (21st September 2015) and the Hindi edition published by Sri Srimad Bhaktivedanta Narayana Gosvami Maharaja (1st September 2009).

Expressing his astonishment, Sri Srimad Bhaktivedanta Narayana Gosvami Maharaja has commented:

"Although at the beginning of Srila Prabhupada’s speech the churning of the subject of sphota may seem to be somewhat technically challenging, as his discourse progresses, it appears just as if someone is distributing without restraint the vast treasure of profound realizations from the storehouse of his personal bhajana.

"I have unflinching conviction that, upon reading this Gaudiya-darsana, all impartial faithful seekers will recognize the divine splendour of the Sri Brahma-Madhva-Gaudiya Sampradaya and not fail to be intensely attracted. Moreover, committed Gaudiya devotees will comprehend the inestimable wealth of their own sampradaya and, becoming imbued with its divine dignity, will feel immense pride in their glorious dcaryas."

I have appended a commentary to this edition, entitled Sri Sarasvati-tosani, with the intention of further supporting Srila Prabhupada’s presentation with scriptural evidence from the Gaudiya Sampradaya’s foundational Gosvami literatures. The Sri Sarasvati-tosani commentary also illustrates the essential precision and clarity attained by the practical application of the hermeneutical principles set forth in Gaudiya-darsana. It is sometimes proposed that Prabhupada Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura has somehow innovated away from the original tradition. It is our aim herein to demonstrate that Srila Prabhupada has sought to address the perennial crisis of scriptural interpretation by lovingly re-establishing the original fundamental epistemology of the Gaudiya Sampradaya, which had been largely forgotten and neglected.

From the outset, it is necessary to make a disclaimer. Bhakti-tattva, the principle of devotion, is extremely confidential and difficult to understand. The true import of this book, or of any other transcendental literature for that matter, can never be ascertained by an empirical academic study.

Srila Jiva Gosvami comments in Durgama-sangamani 1.1.45:

**Logic (yukti) which is dry (kevala), without ruci, cannot give an understanding of bhakti. However, logic along with taste for bhakti can give an understanding of it. The same meaning of yukti should be applied in the later statement sastre yuktau ca nipunah: the uttama-adhikariis endowed with skill in scriptural knowledge and logic (with taste). (Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu 1.2.17, Durgama-sangamani 1.1.45)**

Thus, a clear understanding of bhakti-tattva is the preserve of the uttama-adhikari. Only those who remain under the guidance \[anugatya\] of such uttama-adhikari vaisnavas, through selfless service and regular hearing, gradually ascend to the platform of uttama-adhikari wherein the true nature of bhakti-tattva is self-manifest. No amount of personal study can ever be a substitute for hearing sabda-brahma (transcendental sound) from the lips of a pure vaisnava. All the scriptures proclaim the glories of sadhu-sanga, direct association with Sri Krsna's intimate devotees. If this commentary convinces one soul of the necessity of sadhu-sanga, we will consider our insignificant efforts worthwhile.

## Gaudiya-darsana

The word darsana is made by applying the suffix lyut to the verbal root drs. The verbal root drs means to see, to observe (avalokana), to directly experience (saksatkara), to perceive, realize or understand (upalabdhi), etc. The direct experience of Paramatma is genuine darsana. The sadhana or the mercy of the scriptural instructions by which one attains the vision of Bhagavan is also referred to by the word 'darsana'.

*atma va are drastavya*  
*Brhad-aranyaka Upanisad 2.4.5*

**O dear Maitreyi! The atma is to be seen.**

The subject of direct perception or vision of the Paramatma prescribed in this statement of Sruti is called 'darsana'. One whose consciousness is bewildered by Maya cannot see Bhagavan even after investigating the tattva through the various darsana texts of the philosophical traditions such as Nyaya (logic).

There is an inseparable relationship between the word darsana and drasta (the seer), drsya (the seen) and darsana-kriya (the act of seeing). The Jiva (soul) is not drasta. Paramatma is the real drasta and the jiva is drsya, the object of His vision.

*yam evaisa vrnute tena labhyah*  
*Katha Upanisad 1.2.23*

**Only that jivatma whom the Paramatma chooses, to whom He bestows mercy, is eligible to directly perceive or have darsana of the Paramatma.**

Therefore, the establishment of this relationship between the drasta and the drsya-vastu, the seer and the object to be seen, is called 'darsana'. Ordinarily, the sense instrument by which a seer sees an object is called ‘caksu’, the eye. Characteristics such as the external form and size of an object are realized through the function of the eye. But seeing is not accomplished by the eye alone. The mind must be accepted as the overseeing guardian or driver of the external senses in the act of seeing. Even in circumstances when the eye is unobstructed, the initiator of the act of seeing, in the absence of whom the function of sight does not take place, is known by the name of 'mana’, the mind. This mind is not only the leader (nayaka) of the eyes. Rather, just as the eye is subordinate to the mind, so are all the other four instruments of perception. By these five knowledge-acquiring senses, the mind collects and stores the various experiences of the objects of perception. If a substance has no external form or shape, or if it is minute, enormous or covered, or if it is very far away, the eye fails to gather experience of the external object despite repeated attempts. In such a case, one can understand the position of the external object by the assistance of the other four senses. With the assistance of the knowledge-acquiring senses, the mind, which is the master of the senses, is capable of perceiving even substances that are not perceived independently by the various senses themselves. Even without direct sense perception, the mind, by the path of inference, can ascertain knowledge that the knowledge-acquiring senses are generally incapable of collecting. Although direct empirical perception is the only path of gathering experience, still, the path of inference also supports sensory perception because it is not contaminated with faults. However, sometimes direct perception, while seemingly presenting reality, renders the mind bereft of a true experience of the substance. For example, by the influence of intoxicating substances, sense perception becomes the cause of confusion.

Although the word 'darsana' is conventionally understood to refer to the function of the eyes, the experience of objects through the other senses is also known by the name of 'darsana'. The darsana of insentient material objects is called 'jada-vijnana'. Beyond that, the darsana of the objects that reflect consciousness (cetanabhasa) is called 'mano-vijnana'. In the philosophical texts of India it is seen that buddhi is the cause of mana, ahahkara is the cause of buddhi, citta or mahat-tattva is the cause of ahahkara, and prakrti or avyakta is the cause of citta. Prakrti, citta, ahahkara, buddhi and mana are situated in a sequence mutually related as amsa-amsi (part and whole) of each other.

## Various darsanas prevalent in India

From ancient times in India six darsanas are famous— the Vaisesika-darsana of Kanada, Gautama's Nyaya-darsana, Kapila's Sankhya-darsana, Patanjali’s Yoga-darsana, Jaimini’s Purva-mimdmsd, and the Uttara-mimamsa or Vedanta-darsana of Sri Vedavyasa. In addition to these, in the middle period there was the Nastikya-darsana of Carvaka, Nakula’s Pasupat-darsana, Rasesvara-darsana, Arhat-darsana, also known as Jaina-darsana, and Saugata-darsana, also known as Bauddha-darsana, and another ten philosophical trains of thought. One can acquire basic knowledge of all these opinions from the text of Sayana-Madhava.

Srila Vyasadeva has accepted apta-vakya sabda or the Sruti as the mula-pramana, or root of all evidence. By accepting direct perception and logical inference as assistants to the apta-vakya, Srila Vyasadeva has refuted all the other philosophical opinions. In this way, after reconciling all the conclusions of the Vedas, he has composed one grantha called Brahma-sutra, which is the exclusive embodiment of the essence of the Vedas' headlines, the Upanisads. "May the ultimate purpose of the Upanisads be realized sequentially in the vision of the material spectators (prakrta-drasta).” Deliberating in this way, Srila Vyasadeva has written the Brahma-sutra. This Brahma-sutra has become famous by names such as Uttara-mimamsa, Sariraka Sutra or Vedanta-darsana. All Indian systems of Vedic dharma are constructed more or less on the basis of Vedanta-darsana. We can see innumerable commentators who have presented explanations on this Sariraka-mimamsa. Among them, the ancient commentators such as Baudayana, Tanka, Bhauruci, and Dramida are particularly acclaimed. Many persons are respected as acariyas of Vedanta after presenting their commentaries such as the Sariraka-bhasya composed by Sri Sankaracarya. Scholars such as Yadavacarya, Prabhakara and Bhaskara Bhatta have also propagated texts instructing various divergent interpretations of Vedanta. In the sampradaya following Sri Sankaracarya we find that the writings such as those of Anandagiri and Sayana-Madhava and the ‘Bhamati' tika of Vacaspati Misra aim to support the theory of kevaladvaita-vada (absolute monism).

Several centuries earlier, many profoundly intelligent acaryas who were absorbed in devotional service to Bhagavan had appeared in this world to create faith on the basis of Brahma-sutra or Uttara-mimamsa in opposition to the opinion of kevaladvaita-vada, which is based on faith in an impersonal undifferentiated reality. They aimed to promote the conception of Brahma as a personal being with distinct characteristics.

They alone were the preachers and protectors of the personal savisesa-brahma-darsana.They were not only khanda-darsanika, philosophers with a fragmental vision. Rather, they were imbued with sambandha-jnana and they were expert in siddhanta, scriptural conclusions. Therefore, they were not averse to the darsana of abhidheya (the process) and the prayojana (goal) related to the abiding true substance (vastava satya-vastu).

Prominent philosophical thinkers such as Sri Sankara had refuted the jadiya-bheda-darsana, the vision of material differences. Still, because they established the jivatma as being the drasta (the seer), the bhokta or visaya (the enjoying subject), and the jagat as the drsya (seen), bhogya (enjoyed) and asraya (object), they are situated very far from the wonderfully variegated loving pastimes (vicitra-vilasa) of the parama-satya, the Supreme Truth. The capability to behold this wonderful variety of pastimes of the parama-satya is included only in vaisnava-darsana.

## Vaisnava-darsana

There are two types of commentary on the Brahma-sutra by two types of sampradaya. One is based on kevaladvaita-vada or nirvisesa-vada and the other is based on savisesa-vada. The first type of sampraddya, which remains absorbed in the darsana of reality based on the impressions of Maya or khanda-jnana (fragmental awareness), is a mayavada interpretation of Vedanta. The second type of sampradaya is comprised of the vaisnavas who are tattva-vit, knowers of truth. They see that the substance of this world follows in the wake of the self-illuminating reality (svaprakasa-vastu). The tattva-vastu is not only Maya. Rather, it is akhanda parama-satya advaya-jnana, the indivisible, supreme eternal nondual conscious entity. Therefore, the tattva-vit personalities, that is, vaisnavas, having three types of vision or experience, give three names to this one sac-cid-ananda advaya-jnana-tattva vastu, namely, Brahma, Paramatma and Bhagavan. The experience of this tattva-vastu by only cit, or samvit-vrtti, is called 'Brahma-darsana', by sat and cit-vrtti the realization is called 'Paramatma-darsana', and the realization arising from all three vrttis, sat, cit, and ananda is called ’Bhagavad-darsana' or 'Visnu-darsana'. The first, Brahma-darsana, is asampurna, incomplete vision. The second, Paramatma-darsana is khanda-darsana, partial or fragmental vision. And the last, Bhagavad-darsana is sampurna-darsana, complete vision. It is seen that the first two darsanas are based on aroha-pantha and the third darsana is based on avaroha-pantha. The members of the first sampradaya are recognized by the name 'jnani', the second 'yogi' and the third 'vaisnava' or 'bhakta'.

In the Sruti it is stated:

*om tad visnoh paramam padam*  
*sada pasyanti surayah*  
*diviva caksur atatam*  
*Rg Veda 1.22.20*

**The saints continuously see the abode of Sri Visnu, like an eye expanded throughout sky.**

The transcendentally situated great devotees always see the subject to be beheld (drsya-vastu) in the form of the supreme abode of Sri Visnu. They do not bind vastutva (substantiality) or visnutva (all-pervading Godliness) in the material vision (acit-darsana), which is unacceptable, being devoid of excellence (upadeya-rahita) and divided (paricchinna) by place and time. They never address as 'Visnu' an experience (pratiti) of a substance which is a transformation of Visnu's acit-sakti or cit-sakti. And, except for Visnu, they also do not accept any other foundation (adhisthana). The clear ascertainment of devoted entities and existence related to Visnu is accepted by the name of 'cit', whereas the ascertainment of entities and existence indifferent to Visnu is accepted by the name ‘acif or 'jada'. Since vaisnavas see the eternal difference between the cit and the acit, they cannot be called 'bahvisvara-vadi' (polytheists). Vaisnavas have the darsana of only one Supreme Controller or 'visnu'-vastu. Only Visnu is the 'tad-vastu' (Absolute Truth) and only the vaisnavas are His ‘tadiya’ (belonging to the Absolute Truth). Visnu and vaisnavas are the nitya-saktimana (eternal omnipotent Lord) and sakti-parinata (transformation of potency) respectively. Being the embodiment of the object of love (visaya) and the abode of love (asraya) respectively, they are engaged in a mutual relationship as the support of nitya-rasa. The function (vrtti) of the object of service and servitude is eternal in both of them. Therefore, because they are not disturbed by time, they are imperishable and not bound by karma. Rather, they are anadi (beginningless).

In vaisnava-darsana the tattva-vastu is called Bhagavan. Bhagavan is not a perishable substance included within Maya. Only among the substances included within Maya is there a mutual bheda (difference) between an entity’s name, form, attributes and activities. However, there is no such bheda between the nama, rupa, guna and Iila of Bhagavan, who is mayatita, beyond Maya. Thus, Bhagavan is the advaya-jnana-vastu, the nondual conscious substance. Although it is imagined that there is a difference between the sac-cid-ananda-vigraha of Bhagavan and Paramatma and Brahma in mayika or baddha-jnana (illusory or conditioned consciousness), in the liberated perspective beyond Maya (mayatita mukta-vicdra'j such an influence of Maya cannot be perceived.

Kalpana (imagination) in regard to the nature of Bhagavan arises only when one considers Bhagavan's nitya-nama, nitya- rupa, nitya-guna and nitya-lild to be made by Maya. This kalpana or absence of vastava-darsana (factual vision) lies in the root of sadhya-sadhana-bheda, the conception that the final goal and the method to attain it are completely different. In turn, sadhya-sadhana-bheda has created pancopasana, the worship of five gods. However, only the worshippers of Visnu are genuine philosophers or visionaries (vastavika darsanika)—they are not pancopasakas. In other words, they do not inadvertently reveal their own absence of saksma-darsana (subtle spiritual vision) by harbouring the notion that sadhana and sadhya must be different because the method is anitya, non-eternal.

The servants of Visnu are called vaisnavas. The opposite is called avaisnava, namely, the servants of anitya, ajnana and nirananda, the temporary, ignorant and joyless. The vastu which is satya, that is, situated in eternal time, in which there is not even the slightest scent of Maya, and which is eternally full of complete ananda, is Visnu. Vaisnavas are exclusively under the shelter of the Paramesvara Visnu. They never serve any other object but Visnu.

Only the vaisnava who knows his eternal svarupa (constitutional position) is guru-dasa, under the shelter of a vaisnava-guru. Satya-jhana, realization of the truth, is manifested from satya Visnu by sruti-patha, the hearing process, descending sequentially through the guru-parampara. The vaisnava demonstrates the success of his own gurudasya by making the kirtana of this truth appear in the hearts of many jivas. Only such guru-dasas are genuine darsanika (spiritual visionaries). Vaisnavas are eternal. Therefore, they do not confound the 'nitya-svarupa' with the mind, composed of the mixed citta-vrtti of jivas whose self-identity does not correspond with the aforementioned characteristics of a vaisnava, or the gross physical body, composed of the acit-vrtti. Considering the body to be the self, that is, the mistake (bhrama) of accepting one substance for another, is called vivarta-buddhi (illusioned intelligence). In the language of Veddnta-darsana, a vaisnava is:

1\)    Brahma’s bheddbheda-prakasa—a manifestation both different and non-different from Brahma.

2\)    Saktimana Paramatma's tatastha-sakti—the marginal potency of the potent indwelling Oversoul.

3\)    Bhagavan Visnu’s nitya-dasa—the eternal servant of Lord Visnu.

Thus, the vaisnava is not a vivarta-vadi, a proponent of the theory of illusion. He accepts only sakti-parinama-vada, the theory of transformation of energy.

When an ignorant non-vaisnava takes shelter of a realized vaisnava gurudeva's lotus feet and becomes devoid of all vrttis except for surrender, submissive inquiry and service, then he can be guru-dasa. He continuously cultivates the selfdisclosed visnu-vastu descending in the form of the holy Sri Nama he has heard from the mouth of Sri Guru. By this, he gradually overcomes the anarthas (impediments) arising from dvitiyabhinivesa (absorption in duality) and, with sraddha, he enters into the eternal rasamaya-Iila of Hari. Only such guru-dasas are adhoksaja-sevaka-vaisnava-darsanika, visionary servants of the Lord who is beyond the perception of our material senses. Becoming united with the seva-pravrtti, the live current of transcendental service in samadhi-yoga (trance), guru-dasa, the disciple of Sri Narada and the original compiler of Vedanta-sutra, Srila Vyasadeva, had darsana of the vastava-vastu. As described in Srimad-Bhagavatam (1.7.4):

*bhakti-yogena manasi*  
*samyak pranihite 'male*  
*apasyat purusam purnam*  
*mayam ca tad-apasrayam*

**By the influence of bhakti-yoga, Sri Vyasadeva’s mind became completely focused in one-pointed concentration and immaculate. Then he saw the complete person, Sri Krsna, along with His pastime associates. He also saw that the external energy Maya, having shyly taken shelter of Sri Krsna, was standing behind Him.**

Srimad-Bhagavatam, the natural commentary on Brahma-sutra or Vedanta-darsana, is eminently famous from beginningless time in the society of realized paramahamsa-vaisnavas as the sarva-darsana siromani, the crest-jewel of all philosophies, because however many philosophical truths there are, they have all been presented in detail in this scripture, which is the essence of all Vedanta.

From a lecture on Gaudiya philosophy by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura at the Albert Hall, Calcutta, on 11th August 1929.

## Auspicious invocation and the speaker's humility

*mukam karoti vacalam*  
*pangum langhayate girim*  
*yat krpa tam aham vande*  
*sri gurum dina-taranam*

**I pray to that Sri Gurudeva, the deliverer of the wretched, by whose mercy the mute can become eloquent speakers and the lame can cross mountains.**

I am wretched, fallen and unqualified in all respects. I am not qualified to speak in the presence of all of you profound thinkers. However, from the day I heard this mantra of Svetasvatara Upanisad from the mouth of Sri Gurudeva, tremendous courage appeared in my heart:

*yasya deve para bhaktir*  
*yatha deve tatha gurau*  
*tasyaite kathita hy arthah*  
*prakasante mahatmanah*  
*Svetdsvatara Upanisad 6.23*

**Only in the heart of that great soul in whom there is pure bhakti to Sri Bhagavan, and similarly, pure bhakti to Sri Gurudeva, are all the confidential purports of the Srutis self-manifest (prakasa).**

## Inviting the chairman of the assembly (sabhapati)

Sri Guru-padapadma has refuted the doctrine of one jagad-guru by appearing in the amnaya-parampara and showing the beauty of mahanta-jagadguru-vada. Dispelling all kinds of distortion, he has shown the realm of beauty. By his words, thoughts and behaviour, he has bestowed the extreme good fortune of service to this divine realm. May that very Sri Guru-padapadma gracefully decorate the chair of the chief of the assembly today. After being established as the chair of today’s assembly, may Sri Guru-padapadma be the regulator of my words today.

You do not see anyone present before you sitting upon the wooden seat of the chief of assembly; however, factually this chair is not empty. Sri Gurudeva is not a perishable mass of flesh and blood. Bhagavan Himself has explained this fact to us in Srimad-Bhagavatam (11.17.27):

*acaryam mam vijaniyan*  
*navamanyeta karhicit*  
*na martya-buddhyasuyeta*  
*sarva-deva-mayo guruh*

**One should know the acarya as Myself and never disrespect him in any way. One should not envy him, thinking him an ordinary man, for he is the representative of all the demigods.**

Sri Gurudeva is sarvatantra-sva tantra, independent in all respects. By his own independent sweet will (svecchavasatah), in the form of mahanta-guru, he mercifully becomes a traveller on the path of our vision, and again, by his independent sweet will, disappears from worldly vision. In both prakata and aprakata-lila, manifest and unmanifest pastime, he is eternal. Therefore, he is forever located as the regulator who instigates our buddhi-vrtti, intellectual functions. May that very Gurudeva today be established on the seat of the chief of our assembly and correct us. He who can infuse the power of poetry in a mute person and who can deliver such fallen persons as us, may that Sri Gurudeva kindly become the ornament of the seat of the sabhapati. I am nominating Sri Gurudeva, who is Sri Krsna's dearmost associate, to accept the chair of this assembly. Therefore, it is not necessary to invite any other worldly chairperson.

## The speaker introduces himselfand Sri Guru

You may ask, "Who are you?" In reply, I say, "I am one unqualified servant of Om Visnupada Sri Srila Gaura Kisora Prabhupada. Although I am extremely unqualified, I am his eternal menial servant." On enquiring about his earthly identity, I am prevented from saying that he used to introduce himself as a 'Gaudiya'. It is not that he should have called himself 'Gaudiya' due to his being a native of Gaudadesa (Bengal), because there was no expectation that he would introduce himself as indigenous to any gross physical place. Sri Sri Radha-Madana-mohana, Sri Sri Radha-Govindadeva and Sri Sri Radha-Gopinatha have made the Gaudiyas Their atmasat. That is, only those persons whom these three Thakuras have assimilated to Themselves have been recognised as Gaudiyas. The uncommon otherworldly characteristics of the Gaudiyas are impossible to find in human beings. Therefore, out of humility, not even introducing himself as 'Gaudiya', he used to introduce himself as dasanudasa, the servant of the servant of the Gaudiyas. He had realized atma-tattva and krsna-tattva. From the perspective of mahanta jagadguru-vada, he was an especially dear associate of Sri Caitanya and he had appeared in this world in order to deliver fallen persons like me. He was not a karma-vadi. He was an intimate associate of lilamaya Sri Bhagavan.

## Karma and lila in Gaudiya-darsana

Among us there are many persons who do not understand that there is a heaven and hell-like difference between karma and lila. The plane of karma is this material universe. The subtle and gross designations are the basis of karma. Karma is anitya, temporary, whereas lila is nitya, eternal. Karma is the experience of the threefold miseries or punishment for souls who have no independence in the matter. Lila is anandamaya-krida, blissful play arising from the unfettered desire of the completely independent self-existent Supreme Person. The plane of lila is beyond the fourteen worlds of this universe, beyond the Viraja, and even beyond Brahma. The plane of lila is Vaikuntha and Goloka. Although lila is manifest in the universe by the desire of the lila-sakti of the playful Lord, it is beyond the senses (atindriya) and beyond material conception (avicintya). By its very nature, lila is not smeared in any way by insentient matter or dependent on physical nature. This is the perspective of Gaudiya-darsana.

## Guru and disciple in Gaudiya-darsana

Our Sri Gurudeva was not included in the category of karmi, jnani, yogi, tapasvi or any type of fraud posing as a devotee yet harbouring ulterior motives (anyabhilasa). He was an intimate associate and participant in the Hid of Lila-purusottama, the playful Supreme Person. Thus, our identity is that of a menial servant (kirikara) of srauta Guru-padapadma, the lotus feet of the preceptor who is the conduit of spiritual sound.

## Deliberation on Sruti and srauta in Gaudiya-darsana

The ordinary conventional meanings (sadharana rudhi-vrtti) of the words 'sruti' and 'srauta' which are accepted by followers of the empirical tradition (adhyaksika-sampradaya), who derive knowledge by collating sensory perceptions, is not accepted by the vidvat-sampraddya, who are servants of Adhoksaja, the Lord who exists beyond sense perception. The life-giving current (sanjivani-dhara) of the nectar of the eternal truth (vastava-satya-sudha) emanates from the mouth of srauta-guru with unadulterated love (avimisra-bhava). That nectar which is infused into the pure sky of the heart (visuddha-hrdaya-akasa) of the affectionately serving disciple (seva-snigdha-sisya), through the door of his anjali (folded hands in prayer and supplication) in the form of his ears, is called 'sruti'. The affectionate disciple who is attentive to service attains the truth from Sri Guru-padapadma. If that very Guru-padapadma is not nitya and srauta, that is, if that Gurudeva has not heard the eternal truth (nitya-satya) from his own srauta-gurudeva, then Sruti will not manifest itself in the merely theatrical imitation (abhinaya) of such a conventional guru-sisya-parampara. This is the meaning of the verse:

*yasya deve para bhaktir*  
*yatha deve tatha gurau*  
*tasyaite kathita hy arthah*  
*prakasante mahatmanah*  
*Svetasvatara Upanisad 6.23*

**Only in the heart of that great soul in whom there is pure bhakti to Sri Bhagavan, and similarly, pure bhakti to Sri Gurudeva, are all the confidential purports of the Srutis self-manifest (prakasa).**

## Guru-tattva in Gaudiya-darsana

In this verse, the word para-bhakti means pure bhakti, the causeless and uninterrupted cultivation of all activities and moods for the benefit of Sri Krsna, devoid of all extraneous desires and completely free from the coverings of tendencies such as karma, jnana and yoga. Although one’s mother, father, elder brother and seniors are all worldly gurus, they are destroyed by time. Thus, they are not eternal. Although the schoolmaster and the musician who teaches one to play musical instruments are addressed by the name 'guru', their guruship does not extend throughout eternity. Furthermore, those who profess a difference between the method and the goal (upaya-upeya-bheda-vadi), such as the jnanis, yogis and tapasvis, are all accepted as gurus, but they are also not eternal (nitya). The guru-disciple relationship does not remain after triputi-vinasa, the destruction of the distinction between the seer, the seen and the act of seeing (drasta, drsya and darsana). After attaining absolute singularity (kaivalya) in the perfection of yoga, there is no awareness of the necessity of service to Sri Guru. Therefore, there is no para-bhakti in the acceptance of this type of temporary (tat-kalika) or momentary guru. As Devata (God) is eternal, Sri Guru is also eternal. The word 'devata' means Sri Krsna, the supra-mundane Kamadeva. Sri Gurudeva is a form (svarupa) of this very Sri Krsna. He is non-different from Sri Krsna. Sri Guru is Sri Krsna’s prakasa-vigraha (bodily expansion).

*saksad-dharitvena samasta-sastrair*  
*uktas tatha bhavyata eva sadbhih*  
*kintu prabhoryah priya eva tasya*  
*vande guroh sri-caranaravindam*  
*Sri Gurvastakam, verse 7*

**Although all the scriptures have glorified him as Sri Krsna’s direct svarupa, and the saints also consider him as such, still, he is Sriman Mahaprabhu’s exclusively loyal loved one. I offer my obeisances unto the lotus feet of Sri Gurudeva, who is Bhagavan’s acintya-bhedabheda-prakasa vigraha, inconceivably different and non-different bodily expansion of Bhagavan.**

## Amnaya

This is the deliberation on guru-tattva in Gaudiya-darsana. The Gaudiyas are endowed with para-bhakti to Sri Gurudeva because they remain in a state of awareness of the non-difference between Sri Gurudeva and Sri Krsna (nitya-abhinna-buddhi). The srauta-vani from the mouth of Sri Guru only enters completely into the ears of one in whom this para-bhakti-vrtti is present to the fullest extent. The Sruti cannot be infused into the holes of the ears that remain blocked by contaminations, ears covered with attraction to activities devoid of service to Hari such as karma, jnana,yoga, and tapasya. The succession of eternal sound (nitya-sabda) descending from the transcendental sky (paravyoma) that enters into ears intent on service (sevonmukha-karna) is called 'Sruti'. Instead of knowledge polluted by the faulty vision of exploitation (yisaya-darsana), the jiva, who is imbued with the four defects beginning with bhrama, must understand the flawless 'amnaya', the root of knowledge (jnana-mala).

## Sabda

Sound (sabda) is of two types, eternal and non-eternal. The Sanskrit grammarians beginning with Panini consider that sound in its original unmanifest form as sphota is eternal, whilst sound in the form of manifest audible syllables is non-eternal. Patanjali also agrees with the opinion of Panini.

## Sphota-vada-vicara - Deliberation on the Sphota theory

## Panini's analysis

Panini considers unmanifest sound, referred to as sphota, to be eternal. This eternal sphota is the original cause of the universe. Thus, he identifies sphota with Brahma, the Absolute Truth.

*jagan nidanam sphotakhyo niravayavo*  
*nityah sabdo brahmaiveti*

**The first primary cause of the universe called sphota is the indivisible, eternal sound, which is itself the ultimate reality, Brahma.**

Another grammarian, Bhartrhari (5th century), has stated in the Brahma-kanda (1.1) of his Vakyapadiya:

*anadi-nidhanam brahma sabda-tattvam yad aksaram*  
*nivartate' rthabhdvena prakriya jagato yatah*

**The beginningless and endless Brahma is the principle of sound. From this imperishable sound alone, the functions of the universe are initiated.**

The question may be raised, "What is it that gives rise to clear knowledge of the meaning of any particular word? Is it the individually articulated syllables of the word, or is it the effect of all the syllables collectively?" The great sage Panini says that neither individual syllables nor a combination of syllables have the capacity to awaken a sense of meaning. Individual syllables are obviously incapable of relating meaning. For example, the word bhaksana (eating) cannot be inferred from any of the separate syllables bha, ka, sa or na. Furthermore, since all the syllables are temporary, it is also impossible for them to exist collectively. In pronouncing a word, the sound of each syllable disappears before the sound of the next syllable appears, so there is no opportunity for the syllables to form a collective entity capable of inspiring an awareness of their intended meaning. A commonly given example is that of a needle piercing through a stack of a hundred lotus petals. It seems as if the needle pierces them all at once, but actually the tip of the needle pierces each petal individually in rapid succession with the result that all the petals attain the state of being connected by the needle shaft. However, this example is inapplicable in regard to the formation of a word from its component syllables because each syllable disappears before the next one appears. Thus, there is no opportunity for them to form a composite entity.

Yet the meaning cannot be accomplished in any way other than these two options, namely, by the divided or the connected syllables. Consequently, it remains unproven that something other than the sphota, the subtle unmanifest form of all syllables, has the self-evident power to convey meaning. As such, it must be concluded that there is an agent possessed of the potency to awaken the experience of meaning. This agent is called the sphota.

*tasmad varnanam vacakatvanupapanno yad*  
*balarthapratipattih sa sphota iti varnatirikto*  
*varnabhivyango'rthapratyayako nityah*  
*sabdah sphota iti tad vido vadant \|*  
*ataeva sphutyato vyajyate varnair iti sphoto varnabhivyanga*  
*sphutibhavaty asmadartha iti sphoto’rthapratyayak iti sphota-sabdartham ubhayatha nirahuh \|\|*

**Since it is inconsistent to assume that the power of reference is present in all syllables, the agent upon the strength of which the experience of meaning arises is called sphota. Learned persons who have realized the principles of reality say that distinct from the syllables of a word, that which fully manifests clear awareness of the meaning of words is only the eternal sound referred to by the term sphota. Its name is sphota because it is varnabhivyanga. Abhi means ‘in all respects’, and vyanga means vyakta, ‘revealed’ by the syllables. Vyanga can also mean sphutita, 'burst'. That which causes the clear meaning of a word to burst into one’s consciousness is called sphota. Thus, the meaning of the word sphota has been explained in these two ways.**

The grammarians Patanjali, Kaiyata (the author of Pradipa, a commentary on Patanjali’s Vyakarana Mahabhasya) and others have presented their various deliberations upon the nature of sphota. The Bhattacaryas who adhere to the principles expounded in the Mimamsa-slokavarttika of Kumarila Bhatta have also analysed sphota-vada (with a view to refute the conjecture of the grammarians). I present herein a summary of Sri Madhava Acarya's reflections on the sphota based on his Sarva-darsana Samgraha (Compendium of all Philosophies).

Panini and the main sphota-vadis (proponents) of the grammarian tradition state that the meaning of words cannot be understood by the sound of articulated syllables without the intervention of the sphota. For example, the four letters a, g, n and i combine to make the word agni, which is understood to mean 'fire'. The sense of fire cannot be derived from any of the individual letters. When the four letters are combined, we understand they refer to 'fire', but this understanding cannot be accomplished merely by the combination of the letters. If the meaning could be conveyed by each one of the four syllables, then why do we not comprehend the sense of‘fire’ upon hearing the syllable 'a' or just from the utterance of 'g'? If someone proposes that it is not possible to understand the meaning from the individual syllables, but when combined they convey the sense of 'fire', the proponents of sphota theory reply that this type of reasoning is simply puerile boisterousness because every syllable is rapidly destroyed. As each syllable arises, the previous syllable disappears. Furthermore, what to speak of semantic comprehension of the combined syllables, there is no foundation for their combination to take place. Therefore, it should be understood that comprehension of meaning has two phases. First, the sphota is revealed by the four syllables. Then, by the manifestation of the sphota, one comprehends the meaning of the word ‘fire’.

Some have objected on the grounds that if one accepts sphota to be revealed by each syllable, then one contradicts the foundational premise of sphota theory itself, to wit, that meaning cannot be conveyed by individual syllables. Furthermore, if one accepts that the sphota is revealed by all the syllables, then again, the same contradiction arises since the sphota theory’s second foundational premise is the impossibility of combination. When this glaring inconsistency is present in both of the two possibilities, what is the necessity of invoking the theory of sphota?

Responding to this objection, sphota-vadis analogize that the purport of a treatise, read only once, is not clearly understood, but by repeated analyses, one can firmly grasp its essential meaning. Similarly, although there is a slight manifestation of the sphota by the first syllable 'a', with the utterance of the second, third and fourth syllable, the sphota becomes progressively more manifest until the complete manifestation of the sphota reveals the meaning, 'fire'. It is not that by a slight manifestation of the sphota the comprehension of meaning occurs. Just as complete knowledge of a substance is not garnered from a first impression, but clear cognition occurs within the consciousness after some time. Similarly, the consciousness of the living entity is first attracted by the sound; then, on maturity of the manifestation of the sphota with the articulation of the final syllable of a word, the intelligence ascertains its meaning.

## Patanjali's analysis of the Sphota

Patanjali comments that unless all the syllables of a word arise simultaneously, they cannot assist each other to establish the meaning. The individual syllables do not touch the word or illuminate the word since they immediately appear and disappear before the word can be illuminated. Therefore, the individual syllables are not counted as components of the constitutional form of the word. Rather, every syllable is itself a complete word endowed with the potency to illuminate all meanings. Although words convey different meanings and have distinct forms due to variations in the sequencing of their syllables, there are fixed rules established in regard to the sequence of the syllables for the ascertainment of any particular meaning. Thus, it is demonstrated that meaning is indicated only by the aggregate of syllables in compliance with the rules of sequencing. It is only because each of the articulated sounds 'g', 'au' and 'h' contain the potential to illuminate all meanings that when they are arranged in a specific sequence, such as in the form of the word 'gauh', the meaning becomes evident as a reference to a particular type of creature with a dewlap, horns and tail. Therefore, after the utterance of all syllables, although the syllables themselves are destroyed, one can ascertain the meaning of the word on the strength of recollection of their sequence. The illumination of the intelligence that takes place the very moment one catches the meaning of the word is called pada-sphota, and the object referred to is firmly understood in the form of its signifier.

## Jaimim’s analysis of the Sphota

in order to establish the eternality of sabda, Jaimini Rsi argues:

*nityas tu syad darsanasya pararthatvat*  
*Mimamsa-sutra 1.1.18*

Why should one accept the eternality of sound? Jaimini points out that one must accept the eternality of sound because the articulation of a previously comprehended sound is the only cause of understanding that sound later. Sound pre-exists its articulation. Before becoming situated in the intelligence (buddhi), the pre-existing sound is already inherently related with one specific meaning. First, the sound and its meaning are seen within the intelligence of the speaker. Then the speaker reveals the sound in its audible form.

Afterwards, the hearer, on becoming aware of the sound, realizes its meaning by the appearance of sphota. Sphota is different from audible sound (dhvani). For example, one may say, "By the combination of light and the power to see, a substance has now become the object of my vision." This statement does not imply that the substance was newly created by the light. Similarly, with the assistance of the act of speaking, when the sabda appears in the intelligence, it cannot be considered newly created. In reality, sabda is an eternal real substance that does not depend for its existence upon the appearance of audible sound.

## The Sahkhya perspective

The sphota theory of the grammarians is completely rejected by the followers of the Sahkhya tradition.

*pratityapratitibhyarh na sphotatmakah sabdah*  
*Sahkhya-pravacana-sutra 5.57*

All syllables are considered subject to destruction in the third moment. The effort required to make the sound takes place in the first moment, the syllable is spoken in the second moment, and the syllable vanishes in the third moment. Since the syllables are incapable of establishing any meaning in their combined form, authors such as Patanjali do not accept the eternality of sabda in its form of articulated syllables. However, by uttering the syllables, a separate substance called sphota is revealed. This sphota is the inherent nature (svarupa) of sound, which the grammarians imagine to be the establisher of meaning.

To refute this conception, the author of Sankhya-pravacana replies in this sutra (5.57), "You accept (imagine) a substance called sphota to be the direct cause of the apprehension of meaning. If this sphota were directly perceived, the meaning of all words would be comprehended just by uttering them (which is not proven by experience). Then there would be no need to imagine an additional 'sphota' substance mediating between the audible sound and its meaning. Alternatively, if you say that sphota is not directly experienced, rather, it is the cause of apprehending meaning, then it must be admitted that any substance that is not an object of experience is incapable of being the cause of apprehending another substance. (For example, light is both seen and the cause of seeing other objects. If light were invisible, it would not be able to make anything else visible.) Therefore, since it is impossible to establish the value of the sphota as either perceived or not perceived, sound is not of the nature of sphota."

## The Internal Sphota

Some acaryas have proposed the simultaneous existence of two kinds of sphota, antara-sphota (internal) and bahih-sphota (external). In regard to the internal sphota, the Srimad-Bhdgavatam (12.6.39) states that the indivisible Brahman, the unmanifest source, becomes self-manifest automatically in the heart from the vibration of the illuminating sound composed of three letters A, U, and M. This omkdra is the door to understanding Brahman, Paramatma, and Bhagavan, and is a feature of the intrinsic nature of the Absolute.

*tato 'bhut tri-vrd omkaro*  
*yo 'vyakta-prabhavah sva-rat*  
*yat tal lingam bhagavato*  
*brahmanah paramatmanah*

Now the eternality of the syllables of pranava (omkara) will be proven in accordance with the evidence of Srimad-Bhagavatam. Since space is an eternal substance, it follows that sound, a quality of space, is also eternal. Logic dictates that the qualities of an eternal substance must also be eternal, nityam gatam nityam (Tarka-sangraha 3.6). Since sabda is an eternal substance, either it is manifest to our perception, or it remains unmanifest, depending on whether or not the opportunity for its appearance is instigated by the movement of air. The experience of this eternal syllable in the antah-karana is called antara-sphota or sabda-brahma. This antara-sphota is indivisible, being without parts. Thus, it is non-different from the syllable itself. And since this sound and its meaning are inseparable, it is the embodiment of eternal knowledge. It naturally follows that all the Vedas have manifest from pranava, omkara. Because it is realized within, pranava is the example of the unmanifest antara-sphota. Those who propound this viewpoint say that the precise ascertainment of the meaning of sabda takes place spontaneously in the antah-karana even of one who is blind, deaf and dumb from birth. Although the visual and auditory senses of such a person are not functioning, the meaning of words and statements is still awakened: this is the antara-sphota.

## The External Sphota

Grammarians have pointed to bahih-sphota as the cause of understanding sabda. In their opinion, a word’s meaning becomes apparent when the final impression (samskara) created by hearing the last syllable of a word connects with the accumulation of samskaras made by hearing the previous syllables. This comprehension based on the final impression of the word (pada) is called pada-sphota.

Similarly, the impressions created by hearing words in the present reveal and connect with impressions made by the previous words in a sentence. Thus, the comprehension of a whole statement (vakya) based on the final resulting impression revealed with the completion of the last word is called vakya-sphota.

The cause of comprehension of an extensive thesis (maha-vakya) made of many words such as advitiya (one without a second), nitya (eternal), padabhivyangya (made perceptible by a word), vakyabhivyangya (made perceptible by a statement) and akhanda (undivided) is called jati-sphota'. Grammarians consider that jati-sphota manifests the meaning of a thesis with the assistance of vakya-sphota. In their opinion, when a word is spoken, the sound is only understood through the sphota. They propose that this theory can be proven both by direct perception (pratyaksa) and inference (arthapatti).

For example, from the sound 'gauh' (cow) one does not apprehend the meaning as a reference to the letters 'g', 'au' and 'h', but rather to a creature with a dewlap, horns and tail. This is pratyaksa-pramana, the evidence of direct experience. Neither do the letters 'g', 'au' and 'h', individually or collectively, arouse the experience of understanding. As previously mentioned, grammarians posit that the individual syllables cannot be responsible for comprehension. If it were so, we would understand every word upon hearing the first syllable and the rest of the syllables would become redundant. And since syllables disappear as soon as they are uttered, there is no possibility of their being understood as a composite entity called the word. Thus, the existence of sphota has been established on the basis of arthapatti-pramana, inferential evidence.

The impression left by the first syllable of a word undergoes successive modifications with the utterance of each of the following syllables until the final modification is made by the last syllable. This proposition must be so since syllables produce impressions, but the impressions themselves have no inherent sequence. The nature of bahih-sphota is that it awakens an understanding of the word by utilizing the final impression. If one does not accept this conclusion, but rather mistakes the cognition of a word for the knowledge of each syllable, then instead of 'rasa', one might understand 'sara', or instead of 'nadi', one might understand 'dina'. Furthermore, one might comprehend the sound in both ways. However, the same experience of a sound can never arise by uttering the syllables in reverse order, due the impression of the natural order of the syllables. If it were not so, there would be no difference in the perception of a word whether pronounced forwards or backwards.

## Srila Jiva Gosvami on Sphota-vada

The spiritual master of Gaudiya philosophers, Srila Jiva Gosvami, has thoroughly demolished the aforementioned common theories of sphota and established the eternality of Vedic sound in the form of syllables imbued with inherent meaning. His analysis in Sarva-samvadini is as follows:

"If all Vedic sound is self-evident in regard to all its meanings, then how does it manifest meaning? Now this subject will be explained. Some philosophers contend that since all syllables are destroyed in the third moment, they are incapable of being combined to establish meaning. Therefore, they claim that meaning is illuminated when the last syllable is combined with the samskara arising from the utterance of the previous syllables. This samskara, being apratyaksa, not experienced by the senses, can only be inferred on the basis of one's experience of its effect, namely, the revelation of meaning.

"Others say that as the previous syllables are gradually uttered in sequence, the effect of samskaras in the form of memory is also experienced in a sequence, not simultaneously. Therefore, the assistance of the last syllable cannot possibly give rise to the cognition of meaning because all the syllables are not experienced at once. With this intention, they say that there must be a special substance responsible for awakening the awareness of meaning, to wit, the 'sphota'.

"Since all syllables are individual in nature, a single cognition is impossible. Therefore, it should be understood that a samskara arises from the cognition of each of the articulated syllables of a word. When this seed samskara is joined with the cognition of the last syllable, then the sphota makes the subject visible in the form of a single cognition.

"Therefore, the Veda is eternal because it is sphota-svarupa, inherently of the nature of sphota. The reason being that knowledge of the previous successive samskaras is present in the utterance of every syllable.

"To refute sphota-vada, the proponents of varna-vada cite the opinion of Bhagavan Upavarsa, the earliest known commentator on Brahma-sutra. Bhagavan Upavarsa says, varna eva tu sabda—‘The nature of sound is simply the aggregate of articulated syllables.' This perspective is illustrated by the following example: dvir-go-sabdo 'yam uccaritah na tu dvau go-sabdau—'If the word "cow" is spoken twice, it is to be understood that the same word has appeared twice, not that two words have been spoken.’ Therefore, even sabda in the form of syllables has been accepted as eternal because words are eternally related to their specific meanings. The spoken syllables, situated in a line like a row of ants and received in a sequence, are bound together with a particular meaning. Even in conventional experience, after uttering each syllable, it is the intelligence that recognizes all the syllables in relation to a particular prescribed meaning. Therefore, the use of imagination is minimized in the explanation of the followers of varna-vada.

"Two faults arise in sphota-vada by neglecting the function of the observed syllables in the cognition of meaning: first, the denial of direct experience, and second, acceptance of the unobserved. Furthermore, sphota-vada proposes that when all the syllables of a word are uttered in a sequence, they illuminate the sphota, then again, this sphota illuminates the meaning of the word. Thus, kalpana-gaurava-dosa occurs, the defect of overly cumbrous argumentation due to the needless multiplication of causes. Therefore, it is accepted that Vedic sound in the form of syllables is both eternal and possessed inherently of the power to awaken realization of its own significance." Thus concludes the analysis of sphota-vada from Sarva-samvadinil11.

A great deal of analysis of sphota-vada can also be found in commentaries on Sankaracarya’s Brahma-sutra-bhasya such as Govindananda's Ratnaprabha, the Nyaya-nirnaya of Anandagiri, Vacaspati Misra's Bhamati, and the Nyaya-manjari of Jayanta Bhatta. However, a deliberation on sphota-vdda as exquisite as that presented by Sriman Mahaprabhu in His pastimes as both a teacher and a spiritual figurehead (acarya) has never been seen before in the entire history of spiritual realizations. Srila Jiva Gosvami, by refuting the mundane sphota-vada in his commentary on Tattva-sandarbha, has accepted the eternality and potency to convey meaning of Vedic sound in the form of syllables (varna). By this, he has established the vidvad-rudhi understanding of sphota-vada that Sriman Mahaprabhu had shown during His pastimes as a teacher. Sri Jiva Gosvamipada compiled his Sri Harinamamrta-vydkarana with the intention of refuting the ordinary sphota-vada of Panini and establishing the factual vidvad-rudhi of sphota, the understanding envisioned by enlightened sages.

The power of a word as it presents itself before the pure consciousness of a perfectly realized person is called vidvad-rudhi (from vidvat: enlightened visionary, rudhi. conventional meaning). This vidvad-rudhi of sphota-vada has been fully developed only in the essential instructions of Gaudiya-darsana in regard to Sri Nama-bhajana. In Siksastakam, the victory drum of Sri Nama-sankirtana has loudly resounded through the words 'vidya-vadhu-jivana'. This is the fully blossomed expression or vidvad-rudhi form of sphota-vada.

The Vedanga scriptures defining the proper methods of pronunciation of Vedic mantras are called 'siksa'. The science of pronouncing the mantras with the correct intonations such as udatta and anudatta is the subject examined in siksa, the Vedic sub-branch of phonetics. However, Sriman Mahaprabhu’s Siksastakam is not merely like that Vedanga. All the Vedas along with their six angas are included within Siksastakam. The siksa within Siksastakam, not being limited merely to the vidvad-rudhi exegesis of the Vedanga scriptures, incorporates the Vedas themselves and all their auxiliary texts. In Siksastakam the subject revealed is the sphota-vada of the Holy Name, the transcendental wish-fulfilling gem who is non-different from the named, the fully independent, conscious embodiment of rasa, Sri Krsna.Considering meaningful words or the sphota to be different from the syllables, grammarians create disagreement among the various philosophical traditions. However, all the sectarian disputes arising from ordinary sphota-vada are resolved in the Gaudiya-darsana of Sri Nama. In ordinary sphota-vada there is a difference between the syllables of a word and the subject to which they refer because this deliberation on the nature of sound takes place within the ether of prakrti or this material universe. However, the vidvad-rudhi perspective on sphota-vada precludes any type of intervention or obstruction of Maya, the material energy. In this sphota-vada there is no difference at all between the syllables and their referent, the sound and its meaning, the signifier and the signified. There is no impossibility of all syllables bearing meaning because the syllables and their referent, the sound and its meaning, the signifier and the signified are all subjects of the spiritual space (paravyoma). In regard to the vidvad-rudhi sphota-vdda it has been stated in Srimad-Bhagavatam (12.6.40-41):

*srnotiya imam sphotam supta-srotre ca sunya-drk \|*  
*yena vag vyajyate yasya vyaktir akasa atmanah \|\|*

*sva-dhamno brahmanah saksad vacakah paramdtmanah \|*  
*sa sarva-mantropanisad veda-bijam sanatanam \|\|*

**When the sense of hearing does not function in its covered state, the Purusa, who by nature is full of knowledge, hears the sphota, that is, the unmanifest sound of the pranava, omkara, without the assistance of material senses. He is the Paramatma. The statements of the Vedas, which illuminate all meanings, are expanded only by this sphota. The sphota personally manifests to the jivatma in the ether of the heart. The sphota directly denotes its own cause, Paramatma, the brahma-vastu. This is the mystery of all mantras and the eternal seed of all the Vedas.**

## The Vidvat-tatparya (realized purport) of Sphota-vada

Sphota is the direct appellation of brahma-vastu, the supreme substance of reality. From the general point of view, the word 'sphota' means brahma. The best philosophy surpasses all narrow-minded deliberations. In that philosophy brahma refers only to the most elevated of visions. Indeed, the truth has been called 'brahma' only to assert the philosophy that makes one aware of brhatva, the ultimate greatness. One must vanquish all petty, limited conceptions. Only then can one deliberate on the sphota, which signifies the ultimate greatness, for the purpose of experiencing the descent of sabda-brahma (spiritual sound), meaning brahma-sambandhi, sound which is inherently related to brahma.

The followers of Hiranyagarbha propose that this deliberation on sphota is confined to the subject of Paramatma. Such a subject cannot be grasped by this type of deliberation. The words 'nava' (new) and 'vana' (forest) consist of the same syllables, but their meanings are completely different. The words 'nadi' (river) and ‘sara’ (motion) indicate objects distinct from the words formed by the same syllables in reverse order, 'dina' (wretched) and 'rasa' (juice). But the word 'brahma' is not a word like all these words. The true meaning of the word 'brahma' is not illuminated by explanations proffered by the material mind (manodharma). The actual meaning is selfmanifest only in the presence of those who have taken shelter of the lotus feet of Sri Guru.

## Three types of rudhi (conventional meaning)

When deliberation on sphota is complete, it reveals rudhi. (Rudhi refers to the power of a word to clearly manifest a meaning not derived from its etymology.) Rudhi is of three types—ajna-rudhi, sadharana-rudhi and vidvad-rudhi. Vidvad-rudhi makes visible advaya-jnana, nondual awareness. Sadharana-rudhi binds the functions of the material intelligence in concentration on substances as they are customarily defined in worldly dealings. Ajna-rudhi increases the contraction of meaning even more than sadharana-rudhi and establishes the customs of rajo-guna and tamo-guna in the world of general experience. These distinct angles of vision in relation to the meaning of words arise in conjunction with the various perceptions of the living entities. Factually, the vidvad-rudhi of sphota-vada indicates para-brahma. In this case, sphota is not merely a meaningbearing sound (vacaka); rather, experience of the referent (vacya) is fully manifest within it. From this, we can understand that the name and the named are non-different.

## The path of hearing (srauta-pantha) and rendering the Sphota ineffectual

Srauta-pantha, the path of hearing, embodies the practical application of the Vedic epistemology. The factuality of srauta-pantha cannot be obstructed by any other opposing method of argumentation. If the vidvad-rudhi of sound is covered grossly or subtly in one’s consciousness, the sphota is rendered ineffectual. Then sound conveys insignificant paricchinna-vastu-jnana, limited knowledge in the form of fragmented information of things in isolation from their participation in the complete whole. Although the sound is sung and it enters into the ears, it has no opportunity to enter into the truly discerning ear in the spiritual ether (cid-akasa). When one fails to discover the factual realization of the sphota, it remains covered, and instead sound is experienced as a conveyance for fragmental knowledge (paricchinna-jnana), contracted knowledge (sankirna-jnana), distorted knowledge (vikrta-jnana), and delusive knowledge (vivarta-grasta-jnana).

## Sriman Mahaprabhu and the Gaudiya-acaryas on Sphota-vada

Teachers of Gaudiya-darsana such as Srila Jiva Gosvami and Srila Baladeva Vidyabhusana Prabhu have presented their deliberation on sphota-vada in terms of acintya-bhedabheda-siddhanta. Sriman Mahaprabhu’s Siksastaka presents the fully developed realization of the nature of sphota. He has summarized sphota-vada in very few words—kirtaniyah sada harih—Sri Hari’s name, form, qualities, and pastimes are to be glorified at all times. Sriman Mahaprabhu has not given instruction to cover the whole world with words that will impede advaya-jnana, nondual awareness. In vidvad-rudhi-vrtti every word indicates

Visnu, the para-brahma. In every word, the vidvad-rudhi is revealed through the sphota-dharma. When the karna-vedha samskara, the purificatory ritual of ear-piercing, is performed by the mahanta-guru, in other words, when divya-jnana is attained through diksa, at that time the vidvad-rudhi arising from sphota-dharma is revealed. Vidvad-rudhi-vrtti illuminates Sri Murti, the divine form of Sri Krsna. In ajna-rudhi and sadharana-rudhi, there is a difference between the signifier and the signified, the sound and its referent; thus, pauttalikata or prakrta-sahajiya-vada becomes present. Pauttalikata means 'idolatry', an imitative worship of the divine directed to matter. Prakrta-sahajiya-vada involves accepting one’s conditioned nature (saha—with, ja— born) arising from prakrti (material energy) to be capable of accommodating the transcendental reality. However, in vidvad-rudhi there is no place for pauttalikata because there is no covering between the word and the reality it indicates. Srila Jiva Gosvami in his Sat-sandarbhas, Srila Rupa Gosvami and Srila Sanatana Gosvami in their Laghu and Brhad-Bhdgavatamrtas respectively, and all genuine Gaudiya philosophers present this specific deliberation spoken by Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu.

## Inert form, conscious form and Sabda-arca (sonic form)

As long as a substance is not united with the quality of form, then it remains invisible. The conception of mistaken philosophers that only formlessness lies beyond the element of ether is simply imagination. A formless, impersonal substance that maintains a gross or subtle inert material shape for some time and then dissolves it again is incapable of maintaining a purely conscious shape. Such a substance is wholly imaginary and can never be the embodiment of eternity, consciousness, and bliss.

The sac-cid-ananda-vigraha, the cause of all causes, the ultimate governing Lord of reality described in Gaudiya-darsana, is the eternal conscious form. This Paramesvara, the supreme master of reality, is the fundamental principle established by genuine philosophers. Paramesvara is revealed by the vidvad-rudhi of sphota in the ears and in the speech of spiritually realized bona fide disciples in the form of sabda-arca-vigraha, the sound deity.

By the influence of our relationship with those who carry the shoes of the gurus of Gaudiya-darsana, we are declaring at every moment that one should not listen to these discourses as if they were discourses upon ephemeral worldly activities. In Gaudiya-darsana, there is a heaven and hell difference between 'karma' and 'lila'. Karma is experienced through the extrovert inert mundane sense organs, whereas lila is experienced only through spiritual senses that are rapt in uninterrupted devotional service. It must be understood that lild cannot be illuminated by knowledge arising from the material senses as is the case with insentient objects of worldly enjoyment. The concentration that can be focused on inert forms by the mind establishes the categorical distinction of those forms from the genuine form of the real substance. However, sabda forcibly cleaves one’s consciousness from the impenetrable citadel of the body-mind complex and becomes self-manifest upon the platform of sandhini, its own inherent potency of transcendent existence. The direct experience of this sound in the form of the personal audience of Sri Hari is the central thesis of the Gaudiya philosophers.

## Throughout the scriptures there is only Sri Hari-kirtana

The glories of Sri Hari are sung everywhere. In sastra, that which is called 'scripture' in English, in 'Veda', 'Bhagavata' and 'Purana', only the topics of Sri Hari are described:

*vede ramayane caiva*  
*purane bharate tatha*  
*adav ante ca madhye ca*  
*harih sarvatra giyate*  
*Mahabharata, Svargdrohana-parva 6.93*

**In the beginning, the middle and the end of the Vedas, the Ramayana, the Puranas and the Mahabharata, only the virtues of Sri Hari have been praised.**

## Krsna-tattva is not included within the category of karma

In scriptures such as the Vedas, one may say in the language of lila, the word 'hari' has incarnated. Due to not understanding in accordance with the vidvad-rudhi consideration, many people have covered the Sri Krsna-tattva described in Sri Bhagavad-gita with their own imaginary thought constructs or the dharma of their material minds. 'Gaudiya-darsana' does not support covering sastra with mental conjecture because the genuine meaning of sastra cannot be understood in this way. He who is svarat, sarvatantra-svatantra, sovereign and exclusively independent can never be subordinate to karma. In Gaudiya-darsana, the topic of this ultimate truth concerning the self-determination (svecchamayata) of the Truth and the attribute of divine play (lilamayata) has been described in the verse beginning janmadyasya yato 'nvayad itarata (Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.1.1). Srimad-Bhagavatam is the definitive exposition (bhasya-grantha) of Gaudiya-darsana.

The doctrine of abstinence from fruitive work (naiskarmya-vada) in Srimad-Bhagavatam does not simply point to the absolute monism \[kevaladvaita-vada\] which criticizes the doctrine of fruitive rituals (karma-vada).

*śrīmad-bhāgavataṁ purāṇam amalaṁ yad vaiṣṇavānāṁ priyaṁ*  
*yasmin pāramahaṁsyam ekam amalaṁ jñānaṁ paraṁ gīyate*  
*tatra jñāna-virāga-bhakti-sahitaṁ naiṣkarmyam āviskṛtaṁ*  
*tac chṛṇvan su-paṭhan vicāraṇa-paro bhaktyā vimucyen naraḥ*  
*Srimad Bhagavatam 12.13.18*

**The immaculate Purana, Srimad-Bhagavatam, is extremely dear to the vaisnavas. Therein, the paramahamsas have sung only the pure, supreme knowledge. Freedom from mundane work (naiskarmya), along with jnana, vairagya and bhakti, has been revealed. Those humans who hear with devotion, duly recite and reflect upon it become liberated from all bondage.**

## The speciality of Gaudiya-darsana

As long as one does not take shelter of the conceptions of bhakti, no one can attain freedom in any way from the grip of worldly and incomplete considerations such as karma, jnana, and yoga. One cannot attain deliverance from the grip of misapprehensions (kudarsanika-vicara) and rise to the complete position of genuine considerations until one embraces Gaudiya-darsana. The dharma of the mind and the dharma of the body have invited many types of dharma. As long as the conceptions and obligatory duties of various mental and bodily dharmas prevail within the heart, one will not attain entrance into the unspoiled kingdom (akalusita-samrajya) of Gaudiya-darsana. A Gaudiya-darsanika does not waste any further time in all those topics in which the world is absorbed.

*satāṁ prasaṅgān mama vīrya-saṁvido*  
*bhavanti hṛt-karṇa-rasāyanāḥ kathāḥ*  
*taj-joṣaṇād āśv apavarga-vartmani*  
*śraddhā ratir bhaktir anukramiṣyati*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 3.25.25*

**In the best association of sadhus, one attains the opportunity to hear katha which is filled with my potency. Such katha bestows joy upon the heart and the ear. By serving such katha, the living entity is immediately impelled upon the path of liberation and he sequentially attains faith, rati and prema-bhakti.**

## Eternal worship of Sabda-brahma is one’s only duty

It is one's sole responsibility to behold at all times the selfdisclosed visible form (sphotakara) of the nondual conscious (advaya-jnana) self-existent narrative (svatah-siddha katha) appearing as sabda. Not once; not twice; not taking rest; not with interruption; rather, one must for all time (nitya-kala), without rest (visrama-rahita), without interruption (badha-rahita), behold this sphota-brahma. That is, the name of Bhagavan must eternally dance upon all of one’s senses.

## Lila-pravesa - entering the divine pastimes

Only such self-revealing sound (sphotakara sabda) makes the heart and the ears united with transcendental rasa. In the association of those who are not sadhus, or by being alone without any association besides oneself, or even more so, by simply being udasina, indifferent to the world, the heart and ear will never be united with transcendental rasa. By gradually realizing one’s svarupa, through the medium of sadhu-sanga in the form of hearing their katha with ears attentive to service, the heart and the ears become united with rasa (rasa-yukta). Whoever begins to associate with sadhus cannot give time to other activities. They attract the master of the senses, Sri Hrsikesa, by their spiritual senses, and the supranatural Hrsikesa, the genuine enjoyer of His prakrti, also personally attracts them. This is lila-pravesa, entrance into the divine play.

## Sadhu-sanga is the root

Entrance into the nama, rupa, guna and lila of Lila-purusottama, the worshipful Lord of the highest liberated persons, is only possible once one is liberated from the method of deliberation which remains bound on the path of kalpanika-mukti, imaginary liberation, and released from the knowledge arising from sense perception, by the power of the most excellent sadhu-sanga. Then how much more should one not remain bound by perspectives arising from kartrtva-satta, the state of identifying as the agent of material activities?

## Material comprehension (jada-abhijnana) and comprehension infused by the mercy of Sri Guru and Sri Krsna

The meaning of the word ‘sraddha’ is dependency (nirbharata). Those who desire to be honoured as dutiful heroes (karma-vira) consider that truth is established by knowledge derived from the senses in relation to the material world. After depending on such knowledge, the very next moment, this knowledge derived from the senses cheats them. If I place my sraddha in phenomena, then I will simply become bound in the karma-cakra, the wheel of the reactions of fate. However, when our deliberation arises on the sreya (auspicious prospect) described in the verse:

*yasya deve para bhaktir yatha deve tatha gurau*  
*tasyaite kathita hy arthah prakasante mahatmanah*  
*Svetdsvatara Upanisad 6.23*

—then, the vidvad-rudhi-vrtti of the sabda of the Vedas becomes known to us. Whatever interpretive indulgence (artha-vilasa) of sabda takes place through the senses, that is, through the eyes and nose, or through mental inspection, that is acit-vicara, a material analysis. The operation of all these senses is simply for the sake of enjoyment. The objects of the external universe distract us from the srauta-patha. They lead us astray from the abiding truth (vastava-satya) because we cannot attain communion with the Supreme Lord by this method. One may say that the sound from within the material ether (bhuta-akasa) has assumed the form of sphota and that this is ‘brahma’. However, there cannot be genuine knowledge of the subject of the Absolute Truth by drawing this type of analogy (sadrsya anumana). The perspective of karma can never be excellent. And jnana is of two types, khanda-jnana (divided) and akhanda-jnana (undivided), but not sampurna-jnana (complete). One cannot enter into jnana without renouncing karma. Furthermore, by jnana there is only 'atannirasan' (not this, not this). However, when bhagavat-sambandha-jnana arises, the jiva discovers the path of service. In atannirasan, 'tat', meaning 'that', is indicated. When this 'tat'-vastu personally reveals Himself, only then is this self-disclosed form, the sphotakara svarupa of 'om tat sat', illuminated in nirmala cetana-vrtti, our immaculate cognitive faculty.

As He has stated:

*yāvān ahaṁ yathā-bhāvo*  
*yad-rūpa-guṇa-karmakaḥ*  
*tathaiva tattva-vijñānam*  
*astu te mad-anugrahāt*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 2.9.32*

**May you attain by My mercy complete realization of the extent of My natural shape, My characteristics, My forms (syama, four-armed, etc.), qualities (such as being affectionate to My devotees) and karma (lila).**

By Bhagavan’s mercy, as sound, this form of the Lord becomes present. He assumes form (rupa), full of attributes (guna), replete with pastimes (lila), surrounded by His associates in His own dhama. This world is just the distorted and meagre reflection (vikrta tuccha pratiphalana) of the excellent variegatedness of His nama, rupa, guna, lila, parikara. This Sri Hari, after bestowing mercy, illuminates the cognitive faculty (citta-vrtti) with all the details of His form, complexion, and so on.

## Gaudiya and Agaudiya-darsana; jada-visesa, nirvisesa and cid-visesa

There is the complete vision of the substantial truth in Gaudiya-darsana. In agaudiya-darsana, that is, in non-gaudiya-darsana, there are efforts informed by distorted, incomplete, fragmental vision. In Gaudiya-darsana we do not know the eternally sightworthy substantial truth as the object of our own enjoyment. A person who conceives of himself as the enjoyer (bhokta) is absorbed in kartrtva-abhimana, identification as the instigator or agent of material activities. Whatever efforts are undertaken to see while accepting the kartrtva-abhimana are not darsanas of Bhagavan. In the deliberations of suddhadvaita and visistadvaita, we can abandon vivarta-vada. There is nothing bad in the perspective of nirvisesa, because the characteristic of being endowed with material attributes (jada-visesata) is the feature of an idol (pauttalikata) involving only the imitation of truth. It is necessary to be devoid of jada-visesa. However, it is not that the condition of simply being without jada-visesa is the final or ultimate state. Passing beyond jada-visesa, to rise up to the kingdom of eternal spiritual characteristics (cit-visesa), the individual subjective conscious being (cetana-vastu) arrives at his own intrinsic nature (svabhava).

## Gaudiya-darsana harmonizes everything (sarva-samanvaya)

If those persons posing arguments and counter-arguments from the ‘sakara-vada’, ‘nirakara-vada’ or 'nirguna-vada' points of view see clearly from the complete perspective of Gaudiya-darsana, then they can be released from the limitations of the aforementioned unilateral (ekadesiya) visions and the inter-traditional polemics (sampradayika-vivada) arising therefrom.

Gaudiya-darsana does not support the partial perspective of the proponents of the theory of inert material form (jada-sakara-vada). Moreover, it also affords no shelter to the partial perspective of the proponents of the theory of attributelessness (nirguna-vada). The sakara-vada of Gaudiya-darsana is not jada-sakara-vada, concerned with inert material form.

Gaudiya-darsana, rejecting jada-sakara-vada, does not invite sin by taking the spiritual embodiment of eternal existence, consciousness and bliss, who is endowed with extremely inconceivable potency, as a formless entity.

Nor does Gaudiya-darsana, feigning rejection of jada-sakara-vada, invite sin by engaging in the fraudulent idolatry of some other imaginary type of inert material form such as that of the subtle material akasa (i.e. worshipping a so-called 'formless spirit’ which is nothing but one’s own mental material image of empty material sky.)

Gaudiya-darsana does not recklessly cast the supreme opulence of the inconceivably powerful Paramesvara into the mold of manodharma or the limited human brain, and run to imagine an offensive form.

Gaudiya-darsana does not, invoking the name of the aforementioned theory of formlessness, attempt to ponder by the inauspicious human current of material thoughts (akalyanamaya cintasrota ) that ocean of auspiciousness, whose body is composed of pure existence beyond the three gunas, and then commit heresy by intending to sacrifice Paramesvara, the Supreme Lord, by binding Him like a sacrificial animal to the pillar of nihilism (sunyavada) in the yajna-mandapa (sacrificial pavilion). Gaudiya-darsana accepts spiritual attributes (cit-visesa) or the theory of spiritual form (cit-sakara-vada). Rejecting jada-sakara-vada, the theory of material form, in Gaudiya-darsana, the 'atannirasan' (not this, not this) aspect of nirakara-vada is also reconciled. However, the unilateral perspective and emptiness (tucchata) of nirakara-vada is not accepted.

The genuine ultimate meaning of nirguna-vada has been included within Gaudiya-darsana because in Gaudiya-darsana the material qualities—the mixed modes of goodness (misra-sattva), rajas and tamas—are not superimposed upon the complete conscious Paramesvara, and Paramesvara is considered to be a body of pure existence (suddha-sattva tanu) and the abode of all auspiciousness.

## Seeing by the grace of hearing

To what is sphota referring? What form does it indicate? When sphota bursts (sphutita), that is, when sphota becomes manifest (abhivyakta) within one's consciousness, then only this manifestation, or factual realization of the Lord's transcendental nama, rupa, guna, lila, is called 'darsana'. On receiving the grace of hearing, when this factual realization of transcendental form becomes present, only this can be called 'darsana'. Only one who hears from the mouth of a sadhu the sphota of nama, rupa, guna and lila, that is, the name of nama, the form of nama, the qualities of nama and the katha of the lila of nama, is capable of realizing the real meaning of sphota-vada.

## For how long does the conditioned identity of the jiva persist?

The characteristics of both being constituted of knowledge (jnana-svarupa) and being a knowing subject (jnatrtva-svarupa) are present in the nature of the jiva. As long as the function of bliss (ananda-dharma) has not burst forth, then the identity of being a conditioned soul remains. When, in the course of associating with sadhus, he attains the fortunate opportunity to hear the kathas of Bhagavan, then the jiva can understand that in him there is also ananda-dharma. Leaving aside the investigation of aprakrta-ananda, the jiva embarks upon the path of jnana.

*jñāne prayāsam udapāsya namanta eva*  
*jīvanti san-mukharitāṁ bhavadīya-vārtām*  
*sthāne sthitāḥ śruti-gatāṁ tanu-vāṅ-manobhir*  
*ye prāyaśo ’jita jito ’py asi tais tri-lokyām*  
*ŚB 10.14.3*

The purport is that the attempt to attain that which is beyond the senses (indriyatita-vastu) by the assistance of knowledge derived from sense perception (indriyaja-jnana) is called aroha-vada, the ascending method, or asrauta-pantha, the path opposed to the method of hearing sabda-brahma. In contrast, the aforementioned verse states:

**Those who do not make any effort to attain knowledge and instead spend their lives, by body, mind and words, directly experiencing Your lila-kathas emanating from the mouths of sadhus, even though they do no karma, certainly defeat You who are unconquerable. That is, You become subordinate to their prema.**

## The incompleteness of sense perception

Ravana tried to ascend towards heaven through empiric activity (aksaja cesta). If we try to imitate him by building a stairway of understanding based upon the foundation of things in the external world, then we will not attain the good fortune of genuine ananda which is the most cherished desire of our soul. Even scholars such as Sir Isaac Newton have heartily accepted the insignificant nature of worldly knowledge and science stating, “What to speak of entering into the ocean of knowledge,

I have not even touched it. I am only standing on the shore, collecting a few fragments of broken stone."

## Meta-sensory knowledge is inaccessible in abhijnata-vada

It is never possible to attain meta-sensory knowledge (adhoksaja-jnana) by taking shelter of the understanding (abhijnata) which is attained with the help of knowledge arising from the senses’ vision of the external world. When we totally abandon all identification with all worldly comprehension, then we can realize the adhoksaja-jnana. "I have studied many scriptures. I have seen the whole world. I can persuade people to change their conceptions." With this type of attitude, we can never attain complete knowledge (purna-jnana), pure knowledge (suddha-jnana), or knowledge pertaining to the state of liberation \[muktavastha-jnana).

As it is stated in the Talavakaropanisad (Kena):n

*aham manye suvedeti no na vedeti veda ca \|*  
*yo nastadveda tadveda no na vedeti veda ca \|\| 2 \|\|*  
*Kena Upanisad 2.2*

**I do not think I know Him fully; though others say, "We know Him fully." Nor do I say I know Him not at all, for I know. Amongst us who says, "1 know Him," he knows Him not. He who says, "I do not know Him," he knows indeed.**

*yasyamatam tasya matam matarhyasya na veda sah I*  
*avijnatam vijanatam vijnatamavijanatam // 3 //*  
*Kena Upanisad 2.3*

**Of whom the opinion is "Brahma is not to be thought of,” by him He is rightly thought. He who thinks, "Brahma has been thought of by me," he does not know. By those who consider "we have realized Brahma," He has not been realized. By those who consider "we cannot fully realize Brahma" He is realized.**

## Knowledge of divisible substances does not give rise to complete knowledge

Complete knowledge is not accomplished by the sequential accumulation of partial information (krama-purvaka-vikasita-jnana) or by knowledge of individual divisible substances (khandita-vastu-jnana). The only intrinsic nature (svabhava) of the function of our minute atomic consciousness (anucetana-vrtti) is to be surrendered, that is, we must accept the shelter of the infinite consciousness (brhat-cetana). By taking shelter of things of the external world we cannot become faithful (sraddha-yukta) and rati, one's sthayi-bhava, will not arise. Those who do not long for any worldly substance, who are without a sense of material proprietorship (akincana), who do not take shelter of the vision of the prakrta world, only they can become sraddha-yukta.

By repeatedly hearing hari-katha (bhavadiya-varta), the instructions of scripture from the living scripture (jivanta-sastra), that is, from the living bhagavata devotee, we can directly perceive that complete knowledge (purna-jnana) which lies beyond the range of sense perception (adhoksaja). The aggregate of khanda-jnana (fragmental knowledge) manifests its splendour for one who takes shelter of Maya. With the exception of purna-jnana, everything is Maya:

*rte 'rtham yat pratiyeta*  
*na pratiyeta catmani*  
*tad vidyad atmano mayam*  
*yathabhaso yatha tamah*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 2.9.34*

**Whatever appears to be distinct from the factual ultimate purpose (vastava-prayojana-tattvd), or, although something may be possessed of existence, if it appears not to be based in Me, you should understand that to be My Maya.**

For example, although there are not two moons, in reflective surfaces such as glass, the reflection of two moons is seen; or, although Rahu remains within the circle of planets, it is not seen. The essence is this. When looking at a luminous substance, one does not see a reflection or darkness. And, when seeing a reflection or darkness, one also does not see the luminous substance. Furthermore, in the object causing the reflection and the darkness there is no independence from the luminous substance. This is the relationship between Bhagavan and His Maya. Sri Bhagavan is the luminous substance and His Maya is of two types. The reflection (abhasa) in this example is jiva-maya and the darkness is guna-maya. Although both are under the shelter of Sri Bhagavan, in the internal experience of Bhagavan there is the absence of the experience of jiva and Maya, and in the experience of the jiva and Maya there is no experience of Bhagavan.

## Whatever is not purna-jnana is the province of Maya

Other than the experience of Bhagavan or the indivisible substance \[akhanda-vastu\], whatever is experienced is Maya. Whatever knowledge is obtained by the assistance of sound coming from the class of teachers concerned with the external world is all nothing but illusion (Mayamaya). If guru is not atma-tattvavit, a knower of the truth of the soul, then his words and the scriptural instructions emanating from his mouth cannot bring about auspiciousness.

## One cannot arrive at the factual truth by the ascending process of logical argument

Although we may collect the sixteen philosophical systems described by Sayana-Madhava or the various philosophical opinions stated by the western philosophers, still, we will not be able to know anything about the subject of adhoksaja-darsana, vision beyond the range of sensory perception. There will only be a series of arguments and counter-arguments investigating the philosophical truth. By this, one will not easily find the path to search out the genuine truth (vastava-satya). If the factual substance is cultivated by engagement of all the senses in attentive service twenty-four hours a day, then the philosophical truth will become manifest directly before us.

## Attentiveness to service is the only qualification for attaining auspiciousness

"I have no qualifications. I do not have advanced knowledge of Sanskrit. I have not performed any karma, jnana, yoga or austerities." There is no need to become hopeless or frustrated on account of such considerations. No type of insentient worldly expertise (jada-abhijnata) can give a vision of the conscious reality. The conscious reality is seen only by the eye of consciousness, by the functions of consciousness. Therefore, Gaudiya-darsana says: sthane sthitah—"Wherever a human is, let him remain there." It is of no consequence. However, in the human’s cetana-dharma, which is kathd of the advaya-jnana, he should hear by his spiritual ear (cetana-karna). He should not take shelter of the path of logic. Rather, he should deliberate on the verse:

*tad viddhi pranipatena pariprasnena sevaya*  
*upadeksyanti te jnanam jnaninas tattva-darsinah*  
*Sri Bhagavad-gita 4.34*

If he offers his attentively listening (sravanonmukha) ears, that is, if he is ready to engage in hearing, at that time he will achieve auspiciousness. Considerations such as, "It is necessary to continue to pursue my academic career" belong to the arohavadis, self-elevationists or adherents of the ascending process.

## Aprakrta krama-vikasa siddhanta -the principle of transcendental evolution

In western countries, the theory of evolution of the physical body has excessively elevated material science. However, India’s theory of spiritual evolution has, with respect to the evolution of the service tendency (seva-vrtti) of the soul, revealed the transcendental science of the eternal name, rupa, guna and lila of the Supreme Soul, who is ontologically the worthy object of our service. Along with the atma's service tendency in the contracted state (sankucita-avastha), the crippled state (kharva-avastha), the budding state (mukulita-avastha), the blossoming state (yikasita-avastha) and the fully blossomed state (purna-prasphutita-avastha), all the Paramatma’s names, forms, etc. become manifest. The description of the avataras of Visnu—Matsya, Kurma, Varaha, Nrsimha, Vamana, Parasurama, Rama, Balarama, Buddha and Kalki—shows the evolution of the human heart. Sri Vamanadeva has the form of a small human being (dwarf), not the form of a complete male. Before Sri Vamanadeva, there is Sri Nrsirhhadeva, the combined form of half man, half lion, the king of animals. After the dwarf stage, there is the uncivilized or barbaric human stage (Parasurama). After that, the civilized or ethical human stage (Rama). After this, the unimpeded independence (nirankusa-svecchamayata) of the human or the stage of full knowledge (Balarama). In Buddha there is the stage of extreme sagaciousness and in Kalki the stage of annihilation.

## Worship of the material hero arises on the basis of impersonalim

We observe in the hero worship (nayaka-puja) of those who endeavour to understand reality through sense perception (adhyaksika-gana) an imitation of the eternal philosophers’ science of the avataras. This hero worship has arisen from nirvisesa-vada, which is one type of atheism (nastikya-vada). In imitation of the twenty-four avataras of Bhagavan, the worship of twenty-four jina', or 'tirthankura' are included within this nayaka-puja. The jnatiputra of Varddhamana, the worship of heroes such as Siddhartha or the nirgrantha-vicara of the Jainas, deliberations on detachment of the sramanas (Buddhist or Jaina renunciates) etc. are founded on ‘acinmatra-vada’ (doctrine of exclusive inert materialism) or 'sunya-vada'(nihilism). Whatever worship of acaryas is seen on the basis of the nirvisesa-vada of the mayavadis, that is also an especially atheistic doctrine included within the category of nayaka-puja. The worship of ancestors and demigods performed by fruitive workers (karmis) and the worship of literature by the mundane literati are also included in nayaka-puja. There is not even a trace of the worship of God (bhagavat-upasana) or genuine theism (astikata) in karma-vada because of the prevalence of the dharma of the perishable body and mind, which are bound by the reactions of work. The followers of Carvaka worship the insentient material body as the atma. With the purpose of opposing the worship of perishable heroes, the followers of Carvaka have described the karmis who worship the forefathers and demigods as charlatans, rogues, and demons. Furthermore, they compare the karmis’ concept of vag-vaikhari (the power of speech) to a hunter’s trap to catch monkeys and rats. In later times, nastikya-vada (atheism), sandeha-vada (skepticism), ajneyata-vada (agnosticism) have offered more oblations of ghee into the sacrificial fire of these types of empiricism.

In Gaudiya-darsana, the worship of the avataras of Bhagavan is not included among these types of nayaka-puja. The GaudTyas do not worship any type of hero who is bound by the fruit of karma. Therefore, in Gaudiya-darsana there is no arrangement for the worship of ancestors and demigods who are bound by the reactions of karma. Carvaka cannot attack Gaudiya-darsana. The philosophy of Carvaka reaches to the limit of the deprecation of the karmis. The philosophy of the jnanis becomes exhausted of value after its limit of 'atannirasan' (the repeal, "Not this. Not this."). However, Gaudiya-darsana is adhoksaja-darsana.

Traversing beyond the deliberation upon the pious and impious karma of the karmis, beyond the repeal of the jnanis, moreover, even crossing beyond the opulent perspective (aisvarya-vicara) of the supranatural realm, only the complete service of the fully independent undisputed emperor of heroes (ekachatra-nayaka-svarata) is the determined subject of Gaudiya-darsana.

## Acaryas of the middle period

On reflecting upon faith in Bhagavan during the middle period in the writings of Sayana (Sarva-darsana Sangraha), considering the uselessness of karma, jnana and arguments indifferent to God, we refer to Sri Ramanujacarya’s visistadvaita-vada, Ananda Tirtha Sri Madhva’s dvaita exposition of Vedanta and, following him, Sri Jayatirtha's Nyayasudha, Sri Vyasatirtha’s Nyayamrta and so on. Sri Jayatirtha in his Nyayasudha and Sri Vyasatirtha in his Nyayamrta have thoroughly demolished nirvisesa-vada. Srila Laksmana Desika’s (Sri Ramanujacarya’s) visistadvaita-vada has made the tongues of the mayavadis become seized with paralysis, therefore, the crypto-Buddhist mayavadis have called Sri Ramanujacarya a crypto-logician (tarkika) in an effort to conceal themselves.

Among all the acaryas who, taking shelter of visnu-bhakti, have refuted mayavada in this world, Sri Ramanuja and Sri Madhva are particularly famous. In the present commentary written in the name of Sri Nimbarka, the impersonalist theories are not so clearly refuted. Modern kevaladvaita-vadis have cunningly tried to change the ancient doctrines of suddha-advaita presented in the Sarvajna-sukta of Sri Visnusvami into an impersonalist doctrine by accrediting the commentary to 'Sarvajnatma Muni’. The vaisnava-acaryas have propagated the subject of visnu-bhakti in this world by taking support of the opinions of various rsis. Acaryas such as Sri Yamunacarya took shelter of the opinions of Bodhayana Rsi. Sri Nimbarka took shelter of the opinions of Audulomi. Sri Madhvacarya took shelter of the opinions of Sri Vyasadeva and Sri Visnusvami took shelter of the opinions of Sri Rudra.

Something has been written in Rasesvara-darsana by Sayana-Madhava about the method of deliberation of the ancient suddhadvaitavadi, Sri Visnusvami, through the medium of the deliberation of the more recent kevaladvaita-nirvisesa-mayavadis. However, in the philosophical literature of Sridhara Svamipada, one can find an explanation of the conceptions of the original Sri Visnusvami. Sri Nrpancasya, otherwise known as

Sri Nrsirhhadeva, is the worshipful deity of Sri Visnusvamipada. Some differences are found between the opinion of the ancient Sri Visnusvamipada and the opinions of Sri Visnusvamipada propagated by the more recent Sri Vallabhacarya. All the concepts written with the aim of refuting mayavada presented in texts such as the commentary on Vedanta-darsana, Prameya-ratnarnava, by panditas such as the recent Sri Purusottama Maharaja, Sri Vrajanatha, or Sri Vallabhacarya, differ somewhat from the method of deliberation of their predecessor, Sri Sridharasvamipada, who styled his commentary on the basis of the ancient Sri Visnusvami. Therefore, Sarvajna Muni’s (Sri Visnusvamipada's) ancient opinions of suddhadvaita are not found in their completeness in these further commentaries (anubhasya). In the beginning era of the formulation of the imaginary conceptions of kevaladvaita, two godbrothers named Sri Laksmidhara and Sridharasvamipada propagated the glories of Sri Nama on the basis of the ancient Sri Nrsimhadeva worshipping Visnusvami-sampradaya. These two godbrothers' firm faith in Sri Nama has been manifested in the collection of slokas written by Sri Sridharasvamipada and Sri Laksmidhara Svamipada cited in Sri Laksmidhara’s Nama-kaumudi and in Srila Rupa Gosvamipada’s Padyavali. Sridharasvami has presented an extremely succinct explanation of the essential conceptions of his own sampradaya, the Sri Visnusvami-sampradaya, in his commentary on the first sloka of Srimad-Bhagavatam.

In summary, the deliberation of Sarvajnatma Muni's Sanksepa-Sariraka is distinct from suddhadvaita-vada. The ancient suddhadvaita has been distorted in the form of the new kevaladvaita-vada propagated by Sri Sankara.

## Differences among the Brahma-sutra commentaries, and the natural commentary, Srimad-Bhagavatam

The various commentators on the sutras written by Sri Vyasadeva have established such a diversity of opinion that the general public cannot possibly comprehend the actual purport of the sutras by any means. Therefore, in the Mahabharata it is said:

*tarko 'pratisthah srutayo vibhinna*  
*nasav rsir yasya matam na bhinnam*  
*dharmasya tattvam nihitam guhayam*  
*mahajano yena gatah sa panthah*  
*Mahabharata, Vana-parva 313.117*

**Logic is naturally inconclusive. The Srutis also prescribe divergent processes. One cannot become a 'rsi' if his opinion is not different from others. Subsequently, the truth of dharma is profoundly hidden. It is very difficult to ascertain the truth of dharma by reading the scriptures. Therefore, the sadhus have determined who are the ‘mahajanas’. All other persons should follow the path those mahajanas have called the ‘sastriya-pantha’.**

For this reason, the author of the sutras, Sri Vyasadeva himself, has written a commentary on the sutras. All conceptions are factually harmonized in this natural commentary, Srimad-Bhagavatam. The influence of numerous imaginary novel explanations or commentaries replete with divergent opinions has been vanquished by the appearance of the sun in the form of the natural Vedanta commentary.

Sri Krsna Caitanya Mahaprabhu is the very embodiment of the lila of this Srimad-Bhagavatam. He is also the crest-jewel of all mahajanas, past, present and future. ## The path of logic is full of calamities

Many births will be spent on the path of logic, which is full of terrible calamities. On the path of abhijnatavada, the theory that one can comprehend truth by one’s own intellectual prowess, catastrophes will come, each one being greater than the previous one. When one becomes an abhijnatavadi, the endeavour to accumulate knowledge in the search for truth blocks one’s progress.

## One who hears Hari-katha is never ruined

Even if a person who regularly hears hari-katha falls down or dies in an immature stage, he does not attain inauspiciousness. However, giving up hari-katha, even if a person attains the state of samadhi such as dharma-megha by the eight practices of yoga beginning with yama and niyama, he still cannot attain genuine auspiciousness.

*tyaktvā sva-dharmaṁ caraṇāmbujaṁ harer*  
*bhajann apakvo ’tha patet tato yadi*  
*yatra kva vābhadram abhūd amuṣya kiṁ*  
*ko vārtha āpto ’bhajatāṁ sva-dharmataḥ*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.5.17*

After abandoning nitya-naimittika-karma, the daily and occasional prescribed duties of this world, or renouncing the occupations within varnasrama-dharma, a person who has entered into bhakti, which is atma-vrtti, the occupation of the soul, may remain in this world and, by misfortune, somehow fall from bhajana or die before his bhajana is mature. Nevertheless, because his desire for service, that is, for the awakening of his svarupa, partially remains like a tiny spark of a fire, he attains no inauspiciousness. However, those persons who are devoid of devotion, who do not hear hari-katha, who are bereft of service, who are mixed with ignorance and who continuously take pleasure in observing their temporary worldly dharma, do not attain any type of eternal goal.

In the eleventh canto of Srimad-Bhagavatam, Bhagavan Sri Krsna said to Sri Uddhava, "O Uddhava! Only take shelter of kevala-bhakti. Auspiciousness is never attained by taking shelter of paths such as karma, jnana and yoga. He who listens to My katha never attains inauspiciousness."

*bādhyamāno ’pi mad-bhakto*  
*viṣayair ajitendriyaḥ*  
*prāyaḥ pragalbhayā bhaktyā*  
*viṣayair nābhibhūyate*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.14.18*

**Although from the point of view of ordinary persons, sometimes My devotee has not conquered his senses and appears to be attracted towards sense objects, still, by the influence of supremely powerful bhakti, he is never bewildered by the sense objects.**

## The association of Bhagavad-bhaktas destroys grha-vrata dharma

On attaining the association of Bhagavan’s devotees, the jiva forgets all the sensory objects transmitted (sankramita) into the body. For example:

*te na smaranty atitarāṁ priyam īśa martyaṁ*  
*ye cānv adaḥ suta-suhṛd-gṛha-vitta-dārāḥ*  
*ye tv abja-nābha bhavadīya-padāravinda-*

*Srimad-Bhagavatam 4.9.12*

**O Sri Padmanabha! The hearts of all the bhaktas who are graciously present in this universe are captivated by the fragrance of Your lotus feet. Those who hear Your katha in their association forget everything about their own extremely dear human body and the body’s home, wealth, friends, sons and wife.**

## Futility of the self-elevationists’ (arohavadis’) endeavours and methods

What can we attain by being absorbed in study while residing in gurukula, engaging in refuting opposing views and being absorbed in the practice of yoga? If enemies such as lust and anger attack us before we become perfect in yoga practice, then what kind of cherished desire will we attain? If we go to the jungle to tear branches and cut wood but a tiger attacks us, then we will not be able to cut more wood and we will not be able to kill the tiger. Rather, the tiger's claws will kill us. If you are dying and you call out to the doctor, when the doctor comes, what will he do? If the tiger of lust and anger arrives before you are infused with dharma-megha-samadhi (in the form of restrained citta-vrttis) by the methods of pranayama such as recaka-puraka-kumbhaka, then what auspiciousness will such yoga-samadhi do for us?

Among all the many paths in this world such as yoga, vows and austerities, there is no goal or attainment. Even those who traverse these paths universally accept this point. In contrast, bhakti, which is the atma-vrtti, is both the method and the goal, the process \[sadhana\] and the attainment (sadhya). Therefore, on the path of bhakti, to whatever extent progress is made to that extent it is auspicious. On progressing to completion there is complete auspiciousness. Paths such as yoga, which are devoid of bhakti, are not like this. When one falls from other paths, the pigeon has flown and again you have to catch it in your hand. This is all experimental drohavada. When one experiment fails, you have to make a new experiment again. One can pass his whole life repeatedly making experiments in this way. While cutting a branch, one can be killed by the claws of a tiger. Then the goal (prayojana) of attaining peace by killing the tiger on the strength of the method (abhidheya) of preparing a stick will not be accomplished. Sri Gaurasundara and Gaudiya-darsana never accept methods of sadhana arising from this type of abhijnatavada of the drohavadis.

## The complete flawlessness of Gaudiya vaisnava-dharma

Although bhakti mixed with yoga and other paths is evident in personalities such as Srl Ramanujacarya’s previous guru, Sri Gosthipurna, in Gaudiya vaisnava-dharma even such methods of yoga have been abandoned. Gaudiya-darsana has established only the subject of Krsna-darsana through engagement in krsnanusilana, the cultivation of all activities and moods favourable to Sri Krsna and the abandonment of all types of coverings such as anyabhilasa (extraneous desires), jnana, karma, astanga-yoga, vrata, and tapasya. In Gaudiya-darsana, the ultimate perfection of the word "darsana" is illuminated as denoting the direct manifestation of the name, form, qualities and pastimes of Bhagavan Himself. Just as it is inappropriate to consider the lila-katha of Purusottama Bhagavan to be included within the perishable realm of karma, similarly, to take Gaudiya-darsana as a type of philosophical analysis of the self and God is not the symptom of exceptional intelligence.

## Mahanta-guru and eka-jagadguru-vada

There is a difference between the nondual conception of the mahanta-guru in Gaudiya-darsana and the eka-jagadguru-vada of the tradition following Mahatma Jesus Christ. Mahanta-guru refers to a presently manifest spiritual master. Eka-jagadguru-vada refers to the theory that there is only one spiritual master for the entire world for all time. The eka-jagadguru-vadis will not accept any guru other than Mahatma Jesus. If a mahanta appeared in a later period of history making the teachings of their own previous jagadguru reach their ears, then they would not be ready to select him as jagadguru or bhagavat-prakasa-vigraha, the empowered representative of Bhagavan. By accepting the opinions of the eka-jagadguru-vadis, one is prevented from obtaining the unbroken current of the srauta-pranali, the channel through which transcendental sound descends, by not directly attaining a mahanta-guru in the present. A person may accept only Jesus as jagadguru 1900 years after his publicly manifest presence and endeavour to investigate the ideal instructions of Jesus with the assumption that they are available only in the scriptures. However, if one's ears are not fully satisfied by the fully conscious instructions of a direct living mahanta-guru coming in the srauta-parampara, and if, by such ideal hearing, the asmita (ahamta: the sense of 'I-ness') of the consciousness is not animated (anupranita), then various types of manodharma (mental speculation) arise. This manodharma can throw us away from the Absolute Truth and further from the actual opinions preached by Mahatma Jesus. Therefore, in Gaudiya-darsana, although the nonduality of Sri Gurudeva is accepted, in the srauta-parampara inspired by Bhagavan, His own associates approach us and, bestowing the supremely conscious mantra in our ears, deliver us from our manodharma. I can touch the lotus feet of a mahdnta-guru only by the mercy of that mahanta-guru who has mercifully come to me. The lotus feet of the mahanta-guru and the lotus feet of the jagadgurus arising at different times in the srauta-parampara are a nondual principle of reality, non-different from the lotus feet of the guru of the jagadgurus, Sri Krsna. In the theory of eka-jagadguru-vada, I can fall from the genuine conception. However, with the conception of the nonduality of the mahanta-guru, whichever guru-padapadma mercifully appears before me, he, having been inspired by Bhagavan, cannot be deviated even by the breadth of a hair from the factual satya-dharma of the srauta-marga (path of hearing). Via the current of the guru-parampara, the bhakti-siddhanta inspired by Bhagavan begins its journey from the lotus feet of Sri Krsna and arrives at its destination, the consciousness of a person inclined to service (sevonmukha cetanata) exactly in its own factual nature (vastavika-svarupa).

## Guru-tattva in Gaudiya-darsan

The unique conception of guru-tattva in Gaudiya-darsana cannot be found anywhere else. By considering Sri Guru to be non-different from Bhagavan, Sri Guru is the highest limit of the object of worship (parakastha-upasya). Gaudiya vaisnavacarya Sri Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura has said:

*saksad-dharitvena samasta-sastrair*  
*uktas tatha bhavyata eva sadbhih*  
*kintu prabhoryah priya eva tasya*  
*vande guroh sri caranaravindam*  
*Sri Gurvastakam, verse 7*

**Although all the scriptures have glorified Sri Guru as the non-different form of Sri Hari and the sadhus also conceive of Sri Guru in this way, he is Bhagavan’s beloved one-pointed devotee. I praise the lotus feet of such a Sri Guru, who is Bhagavan’s acintya-bhedabheda-prakasa vigraha.**

Sri Gurudeva is the dsraya-jatiya-tattva and Sri Krsna is the visaya-jatiya-tattva. Sri Gurudeva is sevaka-bhagavan and Sri Krsna is sevya-bhagavan. Sri Gurudeva is svayam-prakasa prakatya (manifest to our vision) and Sri Krsna is svayam-rupa. Sri Gurudeva is mukunda-prestha, Mukunda’s dearest. And in raga-marga, according to the vision of the svarupa-siddha disciple, Sri Gurudeva is the abhinna-prakasa, non-different manifestation of Sri Krsna’s sakti Sri Varsabhanavi, Sri Radha.

## Deliberation on the Saiva theory

All the analyses found in the commentaries propounding saiva-advaita-vada, saiva-dvaita-vada and saiva-visisthadvaita-vada have been refuted by sound philosophical considerations. Sri Gaurasundara has said:

*brahma sabde mukhya arthe kahe bhagavan*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila 7.111*

## The primary meaning of the word ‘brahma’ is Bhagavan.

Among the errant philosophers, the impersonal featureless conception of God has sprouted in various forms. The fragmented perspective of sakara-nirakara (reality with and without form) among this class of people is all included within the category of manodharma (mental speculation). Nakulisa-pasupata worshippers of Siva say that Mahadeva is the Supreme Lord and the Jivas are pasu. The Nakulisa-pasupata theory has tried to establish the impersonal conception in opposition to the siddhanta of Madhva. The speculative theory of Nakulisa-pasupata says, "The notion that liberation is the attainment of servitude to Bhagavan, as explained by Sri Madhvacarya, is empty eulogy because if a liberated person is bound in the chains of subordination in the form of servitude then how can this be called liberation?"

Since Nakulisa-pasupatas cannot comprehend the liberated souls' servitude to Bhagavan, they say, "A person bound by shackles made of priceless jewels, rubies and pearls is still understood to be bound. Just like calling a blind person Kamaladala-locana (one whose eyes are like lotus petals), calling a person bound in servitude to Bhagavan 'liberated' is extremely opposed to reason and ridiculous." However, exposing the falsehood of this speculative knowledge, Gaudiya-darsana replies:

*muktir hitvanyatha rupam*  
*sva-rupena vyavasthitih*  
*Srimad-Bhdgavatam 2.10.6*

**When the living entity who was inflicted with anarthas realizes his own eternal nature as the servant of Sri Krsna, then, becoming eternally attached to his eternal dharma in the form of servitude to Bhagavan, he attains the prema-dharma of the absolutely liberated souls.**

This is the ultimate limit of independence (svadhinata). Being bound with Sri Krsna is not the bondage of the insignificant so-called independence in the perishable worldly realm of Maya or the servitude of satiating the senses in the form of the false liberation of the nirvisesa-vadis. Only servitude to the independent Supreme Person is the most complete form of independence and only that servitude completely tears asunder all worthless insignificance. Even the topmost liberated persons continually hanker for this state of captivity. In the Chandogya Upanisad of the Sama Veda it is said:

*sayatha sakunih sutrena prabaddho disam disam*  
*patitvanyatrayatanam alabdhva bandhanam evopasrayata \|*  
*evam eva khalu somya tanmano disam disam*  
*patitvanyatrayatanamalabdhva pranam evopasrayate \|*  
*prana bandhanam hi somya mana iti \|\| 6.8.2 \|\|*

**Just as a vulture tethered by a thread repeatedly tries to fly away in one direction after another, however, he cannot find shelter anywhere else, so in the end he accepts his bondage, similarly, a person impelled by manodharma investigates various ways to become free from manodharma.**

How many endeavours he makes for the sake of liberation. Some think that independence entails the acquisition of insignificant worldly pleasures. Some think that independence will only be attained after some time when they achieve political sovereignty in this world. Some think that independence entails achieving the happiness of Svarga. Some think that independence is attained only on becoming one with Brahman. In this way, people are continually endeavouring for jadabhogamaya svadhinata, independence based on the conception of material enjoyment, that is, independence from desires rooted in different kinds of bondage. When people who are continually endeavouring for jadabhogamaya svadhinata become quiet and exhausted, when they understand there is no independence or shelter to be found anywhere, then they take shelter of the prana of all pranas. They take shelter of the lotus feet of the independent Supreme Person among all the most independent persons and they understand that only eternal bondage to those lotus feet, prema-bandhana, the bondage of love is the ultimate limit of svadhinata, independence. Sri Gaurasundara has instructed us:

*age haya mukti, tabe sarva-bandha-nasa*  
*tabe se haite pare sri-krsnera dasa*  
*Sri Caitanya-Bhagavata 2.17.106*

**Only after attaining the condition of prema for Bhagavan does one become a mukta or liberated soul and all his worldly bonds are opened. Only when one is liberated, does he become the servant of Sri Krsna.**

Sri Visnusvamipada has stated in his Sarvajna-sukta that service to Bhagavan is rendered only by mukta-purusas, liberated persons.

*mukta api lilaya vigraham krtva bhagavantam bhajante*

**Even liberated persons accept a form for the sake of pastimes and render service to Bhagavan.**

Sriman Mahaprabhu has spoken about the service of Bhagavan rendered by liberated souls in His explanation of the 'atmarama'verse:

*sūta uvāca*  
*ātmārāmāś ca munayo*  
*nirgranthā apy urukrame*  
*kurvanty ahaitukīṁ bhaktim*  
*ittham-bhūta-guṇo hariḥ*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 1.7.10*

No one can understand the katha of cid-vilasa, the discussions concerning the realm of spiritual love-sports, unless and until one is a liberated soul. Whoever is mistakenly imagining that endeavouring for paltry material pleasures or impersonal liberation is independence, their qualification and their aspiration is extremely meagre. In Gaudiya-darsana, people are not driven to deviate from the purpose of life by the discussion of topics concerning such insignificant contracted aspirations. Some say that the highest mukti will only be attained by taking shelter of mercury. Illness is destroyed by unconscious mercury. The dead can be brought back to life by mercury. The power to move arises from empty mercury. Mercury transports one beyond the excruciating pain of the ocean of repeated birth and death. The worship of a siva-linga made of mercury is superior to the worship of Siva in the sacred places of pilgrimage such as Kasi. Some people think that the mind can be made peaceful by keeping the body in a state of health with Ayurvedic medicines such as makaradvaja that have been purified by the six qualities. By taking shelter of Mahadeva, we will become this kind of aspirant for worldly enjoyment and liberation. All these paltry opinions or endeavours for an insignificant degree of independence are only the efforts of jivas in their materially bound condition. This type of discussion based on the materially bound condition or the education and skills that take the jiva even further into his conditioned state is not spoken of in Gaudiya-darsana. In Gaudiya-darsana, katha of the factual liberation of the non-liberated and the katha of service of the spiritual love-sports of the highest liberated persons by the liberated persons is glorified in an exceedingly beautiful way. The Rasesvara worshippers of Siva think that if there can be a system for temple worship of a carved deity, then why will there not be a system for the worship of Siva made from mercury? They have targeted the worship of the temporary idols of the pancopasakas. In Gaudiya-darsana, whatever has been described about the worship of the sri vigraha is far, far away from all this worship of mannequins. All the conceptions of the errant philosophers among the six darsanas of Bharata, namely, the kevaladvaita-vadis' imaginary, self-conceived anti-Vedanta commentary and the conceptions of the other five philosophical schools, Sri Gaurasundara has demolished in just a few words.

## The primary meaning of 'brahma' is Visnu

The instrumental and ingredient causes are situated in brahma. The word 'brahma' denotes Visnu. Isvara is not an emotion derived from the insentient material world. Brahma is omnipotent, meaning that He is independent in every respect (sarvatantra-svatantra). Bhagavan is not a hired gardener or our rental tenant. Therefore, He is not even bound by prakrti. He will not come to fulfil our concocted expectations.

The theoretical analysis of materialistic pleasure seekers is that Visnu will also be bound by the fruits of karma', He will satiate our senses. If the service of Isvara is not accomplished by every activity, rather, if one acts for another purpose, one’s activities will not be the service of truth. Instead, because of increasing lust, anger and greed, time will be wasted in partisan bickering. Should a human's time go in vain? If one becomes intoxicated with extraneous desires and eager for these kinds of karma, if time will be squandered in activities other than Bhagavan's service, then he must taste the fruit in the form of happiness and distress. Does a human have so much time that he has spare time to waste in useless things, making this wager and gambling his own life?

## Bhagavan is the source

We must make Bhagavan the centre of all our activities. We will make all endeavours for the satisfaction of Bhagavan's senses. Considering the heavens above and the underworld below to be one, with forceful enthusiasm, we will render service to God. Except for the remnants of Bhagavan and His devotees, we consider solid substances the same as stool, and liquid substances the same as urine. Accepting substances other than prasada brings inconvenience and inauspiciousness.

## The conception and goal of nirvisesa-vada

Many people are busy trying to sever the hands, feet, mouth and other features of Bhagavan. They think, "Why should Bhagavan have limbs? We will make Him without hands and feet. Then only we will have eyes, mouth, nose, and ears. We will exhibit a pretend Krsna in the temple and personally enjoy everything for ourselves. Sometimes we will claim to be followers of the Sruti, and rendering the verse apani-padah, ‘He has no hands and feet’ devoid of its real meaning, we will enjoy His bhoga offerings ourselves.” Those who abandon the great festival of pleasing the senses of the one incomparable enjoyer and instead engage in indulging themselves, only succeed in becoming trapped in a noose of their own making.

## Only Sri Krsnanusilanam produces complete auspiciousness

If Sri Krsna does not remain on the path of our memory in every step, every movement, while awake, while resting, while visiting places of pilgrimage, at the time of performing all eternal and temporary duties, then we will undoubtedly be deviated from our true purpose. Our welfare will not be achieved by adherence to the distorted varnasrama-dharma that has been introduced in one’s local nation or foreign nations. Complete auspiciousness for the living entities lies only in the paramahamsya-dharma, that is, the uninterrupted cultivation of favourable service to Sri Krsna by all our senses. There is no other way, no other way, no other way. Therefore, Sri Gaurasundara has said:

*eta saba chadi' ara varnasrama-dharma*  
*akincana hana laya krsnaika-sarana*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila 22.93*

**Abandon all paths, including varnasrama-dharma. Become divested of worldly possessions and take exclusive shelter of Sri Krsna.**

## Surrender and its antithesis, nirbheda-jnana, undifferentiated awareness

Without taking full shelter of Sri Krsna, the jivas cannot attain full auspiciousness. Being imbued with a strong sense of 'I' and ‘mine’, the knowledge acquired by the senses will not bring auspiciousness. If we are covered by the disposition that "I am the enjoyer and the material world is the object of my enjoyment," we will fall into degeneracy. We are cit, conscious spiritual beings, and the inert material world is acit, unconscious. Whatever we can enjoy is called jada, insentient matter. When we conceptualize in one direction, "The unconscious substance is our object of enjoyment and we are the enjoyers," then we become under the control of this type of ahankara, or the illusory identity of being a material agent. Our bhoktrtva-vada, the sense of being the enjoyer of matter, expands and culminates in the ahankara. When that ahankara becomes more powerfully established, then the conception arises that "I am brahma” or "I am God or Allah.” "I will become great. I am everything. I am all-pervading." Being covered by such conceptions, the jiva’s path of auspiciousness becomes completely blocked. When the thirst to be great gradually increases, the impersonal conceptions of ghatakasa-patakasa and bimba-pratibimba-vada take authority in our hearts. Then there is an extreme urge to accept kevaladvaita-vada.

## The futility of karma and jnana is established by discovering narrations about devotional service

In the astrological scriptures propagated in the middle ages, the sun and the stellar constellations were considered to be rotating around the earth. It appears in this way to our immediate vision. Ordinary people say, "Every day 1 directly see that the sun is moving and the earth remains still." By the influence of time, the earth's movement was logically defined and the earth is now accepted as continually in motion. On the arrival of the heliocentric conception, the uselessness of the geocentric conception was established. Similarly, from the point of view of direct perception, material enjoyment (bhoga) seems to be real. However, when one discovers the subject of complete service to Bhagavan, the uselessness of considerations of both bhoga (enjoyment) and tydga (renunciation) is established.

*striputradikatham jahurvisayinah sastrapravadam budha*  
*yogindra vijahurmmarunniyamajaklesam tapastapasah*  
*jnanabhyasavidhim jahusca yatayascaitanyacandre param*  
*aviskurvati bhaktiyogapadavim naivanya asidrasah*  
*Sri Caitanya-candrdmrta 113, Srila Prabodhananda Sarasvati*

**When the path of transcendental bhakti was revealed by Sri Caitanya-candra, persons who were immersed in tasting material sense objects abandoned talk of their wives, children and relatives, scholars renounced engagement in scriptural arguments, the best yogis gave up all the Mesas (causes of suffering) in the form of practices to arrest the movement of prana (vital air), the ascetics rejected their austerities, the sannyasis on the path of jnana gave up all investigation into the nondifferentiated impersonal Brahman; then no other rasa was visible in this universe except for bhakti-rasa, the mellow of transcendental devotion.**

## Sri Visnu is situated in the centre of everything

The idea that "I am God” is instigated by the theories of bhoga-vada (enjoyment) and tyaga-vada (renunciation). In tyaga-vada, the opposite of bhoga-vada, when one goes on repeatedly eliminating external substances, the extreme urge to ignore the Personality of Godhead arises. This, in turn, culminates in kevaladvaita, the theory of impersonal absolute monism. Accepting the conception of the Personality of Godhead is like accepting the heliocentric conception. All the planets and stars are orbiting the sun. Similarly, all entities are orbiting the visnu-vastu. Sri Visnu alone attracts all existence to Himself.

## Nirvisesa-vada is the source of many types of atheism

The conception of absolute monism prevails everywhere in India. Many atheistic branches and sub-branches of this nirvisesa-vada have spread throughout the world. Nirvisesa-vada is another form of sunya-vdda (voidism), or Buddhism in disguise. From this crypto-Buddhism, uncountable opinions antagonistic to the service of Bhagavan have proliferated, such as the tantrika and baula belief systems. Pancopasana is also just the embodied anguish of this mayavada. By considering Visnu to be bound by the reactions of karma, the henotheist sampradaya invite nirvisesa-vada. Theories such as polytheism consider their ultimate goal to be impersonal.

## The katha of purna upasana (complete service) is made available only in Srimad-Bhagavatam

Only in Srimad-Bhagavatam has the subject of the upasana of Bhagavan, service to the independent purusa, been fully described. Sri Caitanyadeva has manifested in His own life this realization of Bhagavan. Sri Caitanya’s servants, in the course of preaching this katha of the eternally abiding truth before the eyes of the world, act as if they have become struck with calamity. Truth is such a substance in which untruth does not remain—there can never be duplicity in satya (truth). In this regard, one should reflect upon dharmah projjhita-kaitavo (Srimad-Bhdgavatam 1.1.2). In Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila 1.90,92 and 94 it is also stated:

*ajnana-tamera nama kahiye 'kaitava'*  
*dharma-artha-kama-moksa-vancha adi saba*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila 1.90*

**The darkness of ignorance is called kaitava (selfdeception), the way of cheating, which begins with religiosity, economic development, sense gratification and liberation.**

*tara madhye moksa-vancha kaitava-pradhana*  
*yaha haite krsna-bhakti haya antardhana*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila 1.92*

**The foremost process of cheating among these four is the desire to achieve liberation by merging into the Supreme, for this causes the disappearance of loving service to Krsna.**

*krsna-bhaktira badhaka —yata subhasubha karma*  
*seha eka jivera ajnana-tamo-dharma*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila 1.94*

**The living entities’ svadharma (intrinsic teleology) is krsna-bhakti and krsna-prema. The desire for pious activities, sinful activities and impersonal liberation has entered in the form of the distorted teleology (vikrta-svadharma) of the living entities, thus making them engage in actions in the darkness of ignorance.**

All instructions in pursuance of karma and jnana are simply deception. Therefore, they follow tamodharma, the occupation of ignorance. Both pious and impious activities are obstacles to the rising of prema for Sri Krsna. This karma is the fruit of the living entities darkness of ignorance.

## Parinama-vada

In the Brahma-sutra, Sri Vyasadeva has described only sakti-parinama-vada, the theory of transformation of potency. Vastu-parinama-vada, the theory of transformation of substance, is not the opinion of Sri Vyasadeva. Acarya Sri Sankara theorized that Sri Vyasadeva had made a mistake in presenting vastu- parinama-vada, the theory of transformation of substance. Since Sri Vyasadeva has described only sakti-parinama-vada, the theory of transformation of potency, in the final analysis, there is no opportunity at all for Acarya Sri Sankara to raise such a doubt. Sri Gaurasundara has said:

*vyasera sutrete kahe 'parinama'-vada*  
*'vyasa bhranta'— bali’ tara uthaila vivada*  
*pariṇāma-vāde īśvara hayena vikārī*  
*eta kahi’ ‘vivarta’-vāda sthāpanā ye kari*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila 7.121-122*

**In the sutras of Sri Vyasa, factually (sakti) parinama-vada has been accepted. In parinama-vada, Isvara is said to be vikari (transformed). Raising this counter-argument, on considering parinama-vada, Sri Sahkaracarya calls Vyasa ‘bhranta’ (mistaken). By this logic, 'vivarta-vdda' (the theory of illusion) is established.**

*vastutah parinama-vada — sei se pramana*  
*dehe atma-buddhi — ei vivartera sthana*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila 7.123*

**In truth, in the Brahma-sutra only parinama-vada is proven. The soul who is embodied in a human form conceives of himself as the inert material body. This is called the situation of ‘vivaria’.**

*avicintya-sakti-yukta sri-bhagavan*  
*icchaya jagad-rupe paya parinama*

*tathapi acintya-saktye haya avikari*  
*prakrta cintamani tahe drstanta ye dhari*

*nana ratna-rasi haya cintamani haite*  
*tathapiha mani rahe svarupe avikrte*

*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila 7.124-126*

**Although Bhagavan, who possesses inconceivable potency, by His own independent desire, is transformed into the form of the material universe, He remains personally untransformed by the influence of His inconceivable potency. This principle is illustrated by the example of a material cintamani (wish-fulfilling gemstone). Although the cintamani produces many jewels, it naturally remains itself untransformed.**

*prakrta-vastute yadi acintya-sakti haya*  
*isvarera acintya-sakti, — ithe ki vismaya*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Adi-lila 7.127*

**If this type of acintya-sakti can be present in a material cintamani, then what would be astonishing if there were more inconceivable potencies with unlimited virtues in the Supreme Lord?**

Srila Jiva Gosvamipada has refuted pariccheda-vada, the theory of division of brahma, and pratibimba-vada, the theory of reflection of brahma, with one concise and elegantargument. Srila Jiva Gosvami does not accept that upadhi, the limiting principle, is fabricated by avidya. Nevertheless, if the factuality of such an upadhi were to be accepted hypothetically for argument's sake, it would still not be possible for avisaya brahma, that which is not an object, to have pariccheda-visayata, the characteristic of being a divisible object. Where is the possibility of something with no distinguishing characteristics being subject to upadhis? When brahma is all-pervading, how can there be a difference or separation between bimba (the original) and pratibimba (the reflection)? How could a substance that has no parts and is not an object of vision have a reflection? Reflection is only possible for visible luminous objects in the sky, such as the sun and the moon, by the intercession of an upadhi.

## How is a wonderful plurality possible in oneness?

From the perspective of equality, it is legitimate to string together many substances on one thread. The truth is that 'bahutva’ (plurality) obstructs prema-dharma. However, if 'bahutva' is devoted to the self-interest of ‘ekatva’ (oneness), this is called 'prema’. The effort to demolish plurality may be respected from the point of view of worldly understanding, but, in the case of plurality within the nondual one, the endeavour to destroy plurality is only to oppose the one. The subject of the plurality of the one nondual tattva is heard in the Sruti-mantra nityo nityanam, "One eternal being among many eternal beings."

## The plurality of the material world is the root of the threefold suffering

Plurality is necessary due to the absence of variety in the oneness of the external world. And this plurality is manifest in the varieties of the threefold miseries. Wherever there is a difference between the suffering and the sufferer, there is inconvenience. However, in the service of the advaya-jnana, the experience of misery or cause of distress visible from the external perspective is not the threefold misery which is the fruit of the worthlessness of inert plurality. The absence of bhava-dharma has arisen in the realm of the difference between the body and the embodied.

## The obstruction of the paramanu (atom)

In the covered darsana of an object, the paramanu (atom) comes and obstructs. In the darsana of the Antaryami, the paramanu comes and blocks the purna-vicara (complete deliberation) of the consciousness.

## An example of the absence of the nondual consciousness of the bound soul

When the sun is reflected in numerous mirrors, the same number of reflected suns are produced. Similarly, for the bound souls who are not absorbed in the one exclusive purpose of rendering service to Bhagavan, Maya creates exactly as many different or erroneous conceptions.

## To what degree is there unity between cinmatra-vada and cid-vilasa-siddhanta?Discussion by unqualified persons on the subject of cinmatra-vada

It seems that there is not much distance between the opinions of the adherents of cinmatra-vada and the devotees of Bhagavan. The jnani and the bhakta both accept that the gross and subtle bodies are lifeless and fit to be rejected. They both say that the unconscious substance can never enter into the conscious. They both say that jada-vilasa (material enjoyment) is to be rejected. However, on seeing the rejection-worthiness and non-eternality of acit-vilasa (non-spiritual enjoyment), the jnani also considers the cit-vilasa (spiritual enjoyment) of Bhagavan's lila to be non-eternal. By applying anumana, the method of inference, which has no eligibility to enter into the siddhanta of Bhagavan's Hid, the jnani opposes sabda-pramana and genuine truth.

## Anumana (inference) is incapable of entering the realm of cit-vilasa

When inference has no authority to fully enter into all aspects of trtiya-mana, the third dimension, then how will anumana be capable of entering the realm of cit-vilasa which lies beyond the fourth dimension? What more need be said in this regard?

## The specialities of‘vilasa’, ‘viraga’ and 'cit-vilasa'

Indriya-calana, joyriding the senses, is called vilasa, and anasakti, detachment from this, is called viraga. In the realm of acit, joyriding the senses brings threefold miseries to the insignificant Jivas. Therefore, detachment from this is revered. However, the senses of the independent Lila-purusottama are not made of inert atoms. They are not moving in inert space. All His senses are completely spiritual. In this conscious realm where there is no difference between the senses and the controller of the senses, the joyful movement of the senses can never be a matter pertaining to characteristics such as non-eternality like those of jada-vilasa, mundane sensory activities.

## The superimposition of mundane rejectability upon the cid-vilasa is illogical

Let us not mix the inferiority and rejectability of the acit with the cit-vilasa. May we not superimpose concepts of saguna or so-called nirguna, pauttalikata (idolatry) or apauttalikata (non-idolatry), upon the sexuality of the cit-lila. When the door of our consciousness opens, we will attain the fortunate opportunity to have darsana of the svarupa of this independent personality. Then we can directly perceive all these subjects.

## Contemplation upon matter or upon the negation of matter bestows no eligibility to enter the realm beyond the senses

We can see material substances with our material eyes. We can deliberate upon material substances with our material minds by utilizing cognitive operations such as inference. By contemplating matter or the negation of matter, one cannot acquire knowledge of the complete spiritual substance that lies beyond our powers of sensory perception. Therefore, sabda is necessary.

## The necessity of Srauta Sabda

Although factual knowledge beyond the senses descends making aprakrta-sabda its vehicle, this sabdi is not different from sabda. Only when this sabda is infused into our ears from the holy mouth of Sri Gurupadapadma, the door of the storehouse of knowledge beyond the senses is opened. Therefore, we recite this mantra of the Sruti:

*yasya deve para bhaktir*  
*yatha deve tatha gurau*  
*tasyaite kathita hy arthah*  
*prakasante mahatmanah*  
*Svetasvatara Upanisad 6.23*

**For that great soul who has transcendental devotion for Sri Bhagavan, and exactly as he is devoted to Sri Bhagavan, he also has pure devotion to Sri Gurudeva, all the essential meanings ofthe Srutis are illuminated within his heart.**

*ajnana-timirandhasya jnananjana-salakaya*  
*caksur unmilitam yena tasmai sri-gurave namah*  
*Sri Prema Bhakti Candrika, verse 1*  
*Srila Narottama dasa Thakura*

**"I offer my obeisance to that Sri Gurudeva who, by applying the ointment of knowledge with the utensil of his instructions, opened my eyes which were blinded by the darkness of ignorance.”**

## Gaudiyas are not worshippers of material form or formlessness

This vastu (eternal substance) is beyond sense perception. Sri Krsna cannot be known by persons who are absorbed in or dedicated to deliberation upon that which is perceivable by the senses. Gaudiyas are not worshippers of material form or of formlessness. They are continually engaged in the worship of Bhagavan, whom they directly perceive.

## Sri Gurupadapadma's complete service in the prakata and aprakata-lila

Paramesvara is the only shelter of the cit and acit. He is the only support of the tatastha-sakti. At the time of the expansion 84 of the prakata-lila in this world, Sri Gurudeva is completely engaged in the service of Sri Krsna to the utmost degree. Sri Gurudeva also enters the eternal pastimes of Sri Krsna in the aprakata-lila.

## Sri Radha-Krsna-lila and Sri Sita-Rama-lila descend to restrain unlawful dissoluteness and lawful dissoluteness respectively

Sri Krsna-lila has descended to destroy dissoluteness and show the pinnacle of the wonder of supranatural rasa. And Sri Sita-Rama-lila has manifested in this world to prevent lawful attachment to women.

## The superiority of service to Sri Radha-Govinda

There is nothing comparable to the highest conception of Sri Radha-Govinda’s service. Whatever limited mayika (illusory) conceptions of God that presently exist, or will exist in the future, among human beings, cannot be superimposed upon Sri Krsna. Although Sri Krsna's krsnatva (krsna-hood) is an inconceivable action, still, He is certainly known by the servants of adhoksaja Bhagavan. Sri Narayana's mightiness alone does not constitute krsnatva.

## Where is the jurisdiction of worldly morality and immorality? What is nastikata (atheism)?

Abhedavadis can say that the Godhood must be non-different from worldly morality. Morality can forbid the weaknesses of materially conditioned living beings. The necessity of morality arises only in the case of one who conceives of himself as a great enjoyer and who is afflicted with the weaknesses of lust and anger, which are fit to be discarded. However, in the case where there are no mundane weaknesses, worldly degradation or covering, wherein only the independent Supreme Personality is present in the form of the embodiment of autocratic desire, then morality has absolutely no jurisdiction. Even the shadow of worldly immorality cannot enter there. The attempt to obstruct the activities of the senses of the autocratic Lila-purusottama is the very identity of faithlessness in God or atheism.

## The concept of truth endowed with attributes turns into the concept of truth without attributes due to the accumulation of offences

When the idea arises that Sri Sita and Sri Ramacandra are human, then one thinks that the worship of Sri Laksmi-Narayana is best. Furthermore, when faith in the worship of Sri Laksmi-Narayana becomes somewhat weak, then the conception of worshipping Sri Vasudeva becomes present. Again, abstracting the genderless impotence from this, one accepts the notion of cinmatra, that the truth is just an impersonal state of awareness.

## Introduction to the material heavenly realm and the elements

The mahat-tattva arises from prakrti. Bhagavan’s external potency, Maya, accomplishes the functions of the creation, maintenance and destruction of the universe of five gross elements. In Srimad-Bhagavatam it is stated:

This Maya has three colours—red, white and black. Maya creates the material world by the colour red, maintains it by the colour white, and destroys it by the colour black. Three types of ahankara are situated in this world. The five gross elements arise from tamasa-ahankara, the eleven senses and buddhi (intelligence) arise from rajasa-ahankara, and the characteristics of the devatas arise from sattvika-ahankara. At the time of the annihilation, the five gross elements merge into tamasa-ahankara. The eleven senses and buddhi enter into rajasa-ahankara. The mind and the devatas enter into sattvika-ahankara. The totality of ahankara enters into the mahat-tattva, which then takes shelter of prakrti.

When gandha is divested from prthvi by vayu, prthvi merges into water. When rasa is divested from water, then the basis of water cannot be seen in fire. When rupa is dispelled from fire by darkness, then fire merges into vayu. When the quality of sparsa takes leave of vayu, the foundation of vayu is dissolved. Vayu, divested of sparsa, merges into akasa. When the quality of sabda is divested from akasa, then the foundation of akasa becomes dissolved (see Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.3.13-15).

Sabda, sparsa, rupa, rasa and gandha are present in prthvi. Sabda, sparsa, rupa and rasa are present in water. Gandha is absent. In fire, since rasa and gandha are absent, sabda, sparsa and rupa are present. In vayu, because there is no gandha, rasa and rupa, sabda and sparsa are present. In akasa, because there is no gandha, rasa, rupa and sparsa, only sabda is present. In akasa, when there is no kala (time), the essence of akasa is located in the mahat-tattva.

## The example of the qualities of the elements figuratively illustrates the progressive excellence ofthe rasas

By the material example of the qualities of the gross elements beginning with akasa, Gaudiya philosophers have illustrated the hierarchy of the rasas. Akasa, vayu, agni, jala and prthvi—these are called the panca-mahabhuta, five gross material elements. Akasa has the quality of sabda. Vayu has the two qualities of sabda and sparsa. Agni has three qualities, namely, sabda, sparsa and rupa. Jala has four qualities, namely, sabda, sparsa, rupa and rasa. Earth has all five qualities, namely, sabda, sparsa, rupa, rasa and gandha. Beginning with akasa, all the elements have a sequentially increasing number of qualities. All five qualities are perceived to be included in prthvi. In the same way, in the rasas of santa, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya and madhura, the qualities sequentially increase. Five qualities are only found to the fullest extent in madhura-rasa.

## The visaya-tattva is inside and outside the asraya-tattva

Just as the five gross elements remain present within and outside of all living entities, in the same way, the sphurti (vision) of the bhagavat-vastu is attained within and outside asritas, the sheltered entities.

*yatha mahanti bhutani*  
*bhutesuccavacesv anu*  
*pravistany apravistani*  
*tatha tesu na tesv aham*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 2.9.35*

**Just as the elements enter into the forms of all higher and lower living beings such as demigods and animals and also exist independently outside those forms, in the same way, I enter all creatures in the material universe (in the mood of Paramatma, the shelter of all living beings) and also, in My separate svarupa as Bhagavan, I am manifest inside and outside of everyone.**

## Eternal, supranatural personhood is established by the repetition of ‘aham’ three times in the verse ‘aham evasam evagre’

In the first verse of the Catuhsloki, aham evasam evagre (Srimad-Bhcigavatam 2.9.33), the word 'aham', meaning 'I', has been used three times. The word 'aham' is present in the first line, 'aham eva’, the third line, 'pascad aham', and the fourth line, 'so 'smy aham'. By this repetition, the personal form of Bhagavan has been established. Thus, He is not an entity devoid of characteristics. Nirvisesa-vadis do not accept the supranatural form of Bhagavan possessed of attributes. Therefore, this impersonal conception is full of errors and should be discarded in every respect. Bhagavan has repeated the word 'aham' three times just to establish this understanding of relationship.

## Jnana (knowledge), vijnana (realization), viveka (discrimination) and anubhava (experience)

Jnana (knowledge) arises from sastra (scripture) and vijnana (realization) arises from anubhava (experience). With the exception of viveka (discrimination) attained from guru and sastra, whatever viveka one attains is often manodharma (mental concoction) or inclined to impersonalism. Furthermore, ‘anubhava’ is also not anartha, that is, cetana-dharma avaranamayi vrtti, a mental function covering the nature of consciousness. When viveka arises by aprakrta-anubhuti (supranatural cognition), there is the direct perception of Bhagavan’s form. Until the appearance of realization, it is not known to the jiva that the form of Bhagavan is categorically distinct from inert Maya and Maya's actions. Just as the sun is different from the rays emanating from the sun, and again, without the sun there is also no selfmanifestation of the rays, similarly Bhagavan and Maya—these two are experienced as separate categories. As long as the jiva is not beyond the influence of Maya, he cannot have anubhava (spiritual experience).

## Deliberation on four principles of dharma-sastra and tattva-sastra

*‘abhidheya' sadhana-bhaktira sunaha vicara*  
*sarva-jana-desa-kala-dasate vyapti yara*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila .25.120*

**Now please hear from Me about the process of devotional service, which is applicable in any country, for any person, at all times and in all circumstances.**

*‘dharmadi' visayeyaiche e ‘cari’ vicara*  
*sadhana-bhakti — ei cari vicarera para*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila 25.121*

**As far as religious principles are concerned, there is a consideration of the person, the country, the time and the circumstance. In sadhana-bhakti, however, there are no such considerations. Sadhana-bhakti is transcendental to all such considerations.**

Just as in dharma-sastra there is deliberation on four principles, namely, dharma, artha, kama and moksa, similarly, in tattva-sastra, the four principles, jnana, vijnana, tad-anga (its limbs) and tad-rahasya (its mystery) have been analysed. Dharma, artha, kama and moksa are included within the category of standard worldly ethics (niti). However, the four principles of jnana, vijnana, tad-anga and tad-rahasya are not included within worldly ethics. The first among these four principles is sadhana-bhakti. Even this is superior in every way to the four principles of dharma-sastra. The abhidheya (method of attainment), sadhana-bhakti does not depend on the place, the time, the person or the circumstances.

*sarva-desa-kala-dasaya janera kartavya*  
*guru-pase sei bhakti prastavya, srotavya*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila 25.122*

**It is the duty of living beings to engage in sadhana-bhakti in all places, at all times and in all circumstances. One should inquire about this sadhana-bhakti from Sri Gurudeva and one should hear his answers with great attentiveness.**

## Purusottama is paratpara-tattva (transcendental reality) Why not prakrti?

Krsna is 'purusa'. The meaning of 'purusa' is 'who is not str?, that is, 'for whom there is no obstruction or obligation (badhya)'. Stri is the follower of purusa. Stri is without shelter (anatha) and without strength (abala), and purusa is the Lord (natha) and the embodiment of the strength of prakrti. The saktitva (potencyhood) of sakti lies only in accepting subordination to saktimana

(the potent). There is no independent saktitva in sakti. Purusa is the enjoyer and stri is the enjoyed.

## Suddha-sakti and viddha-sakti

By not considering the sevya-vastu (substance to be served) to be the 'purusa', rather, considering Him to be stri, one more or less must establish Him as bhogya (to be enjoyed). Prakrti can never be an independent enjoyer. The Gaudiyas serve with the consideration that Srimati Varsabhanavi or the gopis are the objects of Sri Krsna’s enjoyment. Srimati and the gopis are the supranatural Lila-purusottama Sri Krsna’s eternal most beloved objects of enjoyment. Gaudiyas understand themselves as souls to be 'prakrti'. Being in subordination to the category of the mula-prakrti, they serve Lila-purusottama exclusively for the sake of the mutual happiness of parama-bhokta, the supreme enjoyer, Sri Krsna, and Sri Krsna’s parama-bhogya, the supreme objects of His enjoyment. Therefore, the Gaudiyas are called 'suddha-sakta', pure worshippers of potency. However, those who superimpose characteristics such as stritva and matrtva (womanhood and motherhood) upon the Supreme Controller, and want to become purusa or 'Siva' (saying sivo 'ham "I am Siva”) and who, by imagination, verbally bestow upon themselves the identity of Isvara’s independent enjoyerhood and consequently become Bhavani-bharta, the husband and enjoyer of Durga, they are 'viddha-saktas', corrupted worshippers of potency.

## What is the harm of considering ‘prakrti’ to be the Supreme Controller?

If the sevya-vastu (object of service) is not purusa, the servant cannot attain sarvangina-purna-seva, complete service, by all limbs. If the object of service is 'stri, then the jiva, being prakrti, cannot render service to the object of service by all limbs. If the jiva and Paramesvara are both prakrti, then one prakrti cannot completely satisfy all the desires of another prakrti. If the jiva considers himself 'purusa' and accepts Paramesvara as prakrti, then the jiva with the purusa-abhimana, the conceit of being the enjoyer, in going to satisfy the desires of prakrti, his object of service, will simply enjoy prakrti in one way or another. The jiva has been designated as prakrti in scripture:

*apareyam itas tv anyam*  
*prakrtim viddhi me param*  
*jiva-bhutam maha-baho*  
*yayedam dharyate jagat*  
*Sri Bhagavad-gita 7.5*

**However, this inert prakrti, which has eight divisions, is inferior. Know that superior to this is another prakrti of mine, in the form of the jivas, by whom this universe has been accepted for the sake of enjoyment through karma, reward-seeking activities.**

Only Purusottama, the Supreme Predominating Person is referred to by the word paramesvara, Supreme Controller. Only his enjoyer-hood is independent of others (nirapeksa) and exclusively self-reliant (svatantra'). This conception is vividly manifest in Gaudiya-darsana.

## Only purusa can be the enjoyer of all rasas— a feat impossible for prakrti

From an impartial perspective, madhura-rati is superior to all others. It is even seen in worldly examples that, on the basis of adi-rasa, the original rasa or romance (madhura-rasa), the intensity with which anxious agitation, attraction, love, possessiveness, stimulation, and the activities of all limbs arise are not seen in any other rasa.

If Lila-purusottama is not the object of service, then this madhura-rasa cannot be manifest in any way at all. If Paramesvara is 'prakrti' then the status of madhura-rati cannot remain. The relationship of the son with his mother is one of desiring and supplying respectively. Wherever the jiva considers himself as offspring, he is entitled to receive something from his mother and she is obliged to supply it. Therefore, in this situation there is only dohana-kriya, literally milking or exploitation. Especially between a mother and her offspring, madhura-rasa is unnatural. However, purusa may be the enjoyer of madhura-rasa, or additionally the enjoyer of vdtsalya-rasa—in both fields purusa is the enjoyer. Becoming the object of vatsalya-rasa, 'purusa' exploits as a suckling child the enjoyable substances from His servants in the form of mother and father. Furthermore, in madhura-rasa this dohana-kriya, exploitation, is manifested with even more affection, anxious agitation and service by all the limbs.

## Hierarchy of wonderful variety and excellence in aprakrta-rati

After taking bath in the viraja, the state of equilibrium of the three punas or 'santa-rati' is manifest. When mamata (possessiveness) is united with santa-rati, 'dasya-rati' is manifest. When this mamata is joined with sama-bhava (the sense of equality) and visrambha (trust), 'sakhya-rati' arises. When sakhya-rati is united with a sense of protectiveness, 'vatsalya-rati arises. In vatsalya-rati, when whatever remains hidden from the object of tenderness also becomes unrestrained, that is, when the excellence of service by all the limbs in every way becomes illuminated, then madhura-rati is manifest.

## Purusottama is the enjoyer of all types of rati—The impossibility of prakrti being an independent enjoyer

Paramesvara is the purusa of all the aforementioned types of rati. Nowhere is prakrti the Supreme Controller. The reason is that, except for the purusa, there cannot be another independent enjoyer. The cows, the stick, the horn-bugle and flute imperceptibly render service to Lila-purusottama Sri Krsna in santa-rasa. Raktaka, Patraka and Citraka render service to Lila-purusottama Sri Krsna in dasya-rasa. Friends such as Sudama, Sridama, and Subala, climbing on the shoulders of Lila-purusottama, give Him pleasure by their service in sakhya-rasa. Sri Nanda and Sri Yasoda also serve Lila-purusottama Sri Krsna considering Him their vulnerable child in need of care. The gopis of Vraja also serve Lila-purusottama Sri Krsna by all their limbs. Therefore, Purusottama, the Supreme Predominating Person, is the object to be served in all the rasas.

## Demonstrating the merit of considering Paramesvara one’s mother or father and oneself as a son on the basis of ordinary logic

If we superimpose motherhood or fatherhood on Bhagavan, we certainly cannot engage in the service of a mother or father prior to our own visible appearance. And it is doubtful whether we would serve afterwards. Even if we were to serve afterwards, it would just be a conventional worship based upon the reciprocation of gratitude. However, a mother and father continually serve the son even before his birth. This service rendered by the mother and father is not performed out of gratitude or based on obligation. It is based on natural anuraga.

## Although Purusottama is the supreme palaka (protector)—He becomes the palya (protected) by the influence of prema

Sri Krsna is the palaka (protector). Furthermore, although He is the palaka, he is palya (protected) for the loving devotees who have aprakrta parental prema. The service of this Gopala, who is to be nourished and supported, is the object of vatsalya-rati.

## The speciality and mystery of Gaudiya-darsana

Persons who suffer from the threefold miseries of material existence consider that the highest auspiciousness lies in becoming free from suffering and attaining peace. However, those who are naturally completely free from suffering, that is, who have long ago completed their study of the elementary texts of the Upanisads that give instructions to stimulate santa-rasa, are anxious for variegated supranatural seva-rasa in the service of Sri Krsna, the embodiment of all rasas. When service is advanced, there is darsana of Sri Krsna everywhere in the vidvad-rudhi of sabda. This is the mystery of aprakrta sphota-vada or Gaudiya-darsana.

## In Gaudiya-darsana the meaning of svarapa-darsana is not only visvarupa-darsana

'Gaudiya' gives perception of the inner substance. Visvarupa-darsana is attained as multiple individual visions related to the universe. However, svariipa-darsana is only fully developed and presented in Gaudiya-darsana. Therefore, after being initiated into Gaudiya-darsana, Prakasananda Sarasvati has stated:

*parama karana isvara keha nahi mane*  
*sva-sva-mata sthape para-matera khandane*

*tate chaya darsana haite 'tattva'nahi jani*  
*'mahajana'yei kahe, sei 'satya'mani*

*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila 25.55-56*

**The authors of all the darsana-sastras have tried to establish their own personal opinions and refute the opinions of others. Thus, on the strength of my intelligence, I have not understood the genuine root truth from the six darsanas. Therefore, whatever the Mahajanas have said, I consider that to be the truth.**

Sri Krsna Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s instructions are like a stream of nectar. That which He calls 'vastu' is tattva-sara, the essence of truth.

## Factual tattva-darsana is possible only in Gaudiya-darsana

The vision of reality is only available in Gaudiya-darsana. 'Tat' refers to that substance exactly as He is. The name for "As He is, not as He is conceived by man or by many" is 'svarupa'.

Such svarupa-darsana is possible only in Gaudiya-darsana.

## Nirvisesa-vada is not ‘darsana’—rather, it is the absence of darsana

In the abhava (absence) of consideration of the tattvas of visisthadvaita-darsana such as paratattva, vyuha, vaibhava, antaryami and area, there is nirvisesa-darsana. Nirvisesa-vada cannot be called a 'darsana'. It can be called 'darsanabhava', the absence ofvision, similar to the condition of blindness. The vision of persons with eyes relays the experience of variegatedness. Persons without eyes, in the absence of vision, experience all substances as a darkness, devoid of characteristics and form.

## Consummation of the four Satvata-darsanas by Sri Krsna Caitanya Mahaprabhu

In order to remove this absence of darsana in the form of nirvisesa-vada, the four weapons situated in the hands of Sri Visnu, the conch, disk, club and lotus, have been manifest as avataras in the form of the satvata-siddhanta (yaisnava conclusions) or sudarsana (beautiful philosophy). The acarya of all acaryas and Paramesvara, Sri Krsna Caitanyadeva, has revealed in Gaudiya-darsana the samanvaya (harmonization) and purnata (completion) of these four weapons (satvata-siddhantas) and appended further remaining considerations (parisista-vicara).

## Aisvarya, madhurya and audarya

Aisvarya (opulence), madhurya (sweetness) and audarya (generosity) are manifest in Paramesvara. Madhurya does not submit to the servitude of aisvarya. Audarya is manifest only as the greatly generous dispensation of aisvarya gandha-sunya madhurya, the gift of sweetness without the slightest scent of the mood of opulence.

## The attainment of divine eyes for Gaudiya-darsana in the association of Rupanuga-Gaudiyas

Without the association of Rupanuga-Gaudiyas one cannot attain the divine eyes for Gaudiya-darsana. The supranatural service of Sri Krsna, which is vipralambhamaya, in the mood of love in separation, is everywhere in Gaudiya-darsana.

*panca-bhuta yaiche bhutera bhitare-bahire*  
*bhakta-gane sphuri ami bahire-antare*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila 25.125*

**Just as the five gross elements are situated inside and outside of all living entities, similarly, I am manifest to the internal and external vision of the devotees.**

*yatha mahanti bhutani*  
*bhutesuccavacesv anu*  
*pravistany apravistani*  
*tatha tesu na tesv aham*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 2.9.35*

**Just as the elements enter into the forms of all higher and lower living beings such as demigods and animals, and also exist independently outside those forms, in the same way, I enter all creatures in the material universe (in the mood of Paramatma, the shelter of all living beings), and also in My separate svarupa as Bhagavan, I am manifest inside and outside of everyone.**

*bhakta ama preme bandhiyache hrdaya-bhitare*  
*yahah netra pade tahan dekhaye amare*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila 25.127*

**Sri Bhagavan says, "The devotees have bound Me in the lotus of their hearts by love. Thus, wherever their glance falls, they see only Me."**

*visrijati hridayam na yasya sākshād*  
*dharir avaśābhihito ‘py aghaugha-nāśah*  
*pranaya-rasanayā dhritāńghri-padmah*  
*sa bhavati bhāgavata-pradhāna uktah*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.2.55*

**"Sri Hari destroys all the sins of one who utters His name even unintentionally or in a helpless condition. He becomes bound in the heart of His devotee by the ropes of transcendental love. Such a devotee, whose he art Sri Hari is incapable of abandoning, is called an uttama-bhagavata.**

*gāyantya uccair amum eva saṁhatā*  
*vicikyur unmattaka-vad vanād vanam*  
*papracchur ākāśa-vad antaraṁ bahir*  
*bhūteṣu santaṁ puruṣaṁ vanaspatīn*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 10.30.4*

**The gopis assembled together and, loudly singing like mad women about the qualities of Sri Krsna, began to search for Sri Krsna from one forest to another. They began to inquire from the trees, plants and creepers about Sri Krsna, the Supreme Person who, like akasa, is situated externally and internally.**

## The basis of the origin of various darsanas and their results

Gaudiyas have the aforementioned vision. Therefore, they are 'Gaudiya' — in the shelter of Mahaprabhu. Divergent philosophies have arisen by focusing the external vision on the external covering. All those philosophies are covering the intellect of the living entities. The considerations of Gaudiya-darsana remain uncovered by those philosophies. Some philosophers have been concerned with the external gross covering, some with the subtle covering and some with the extremely subtle covering. However, all these darsanas are prakrta (material) or simply entail the abstract negation of the prakrta.

## Gaudiya-darsana is the only unblemished, immaculate aprakrta-darsana

This word 'aprakrta' is found only in Gaudiya-darsana. Various persons who consider themselves philosophers scoff and make a spectacle because they cannot understand the purport of this word 'aprakrta’.

## Ajnata hi dosi—Only ignorance is the culprit

There is no fault in them for this. Their ignorance is the culprit. They cannot even imagine that the material peephole, through which they see a distorted image of the aprakrta realm, is just one action of the aprakrta name, which is the root of all material varieties.

## Padma-niti

Kamsa’s mother, Padma, used to think, "Why are the gopas and gopis in Vrndavana so distressed over Sri Krsna’s leaving? It seems that Sri Krsna must owe them something." Therefore, Padma advised, "Let the accounts be settled once and for all. How much has Krsna earned while tending the cows of the gopas and gopis? And how much did the gopas and gopis spend on Krsna’s care, protection, food and clothing from His childhood until His departure from Vraja? After accounting for all this, if Krsna still owes anything to those gopas and gopis, He can have that amount of wealth sent to them and terminate this relationship."

This old woman has put on the spectacles of self-interest. By viewing Sri Krsna and the gopas and gopis through the lens of her own selfish attitude, she will not see anything else. How can this old woman understand the eternal and natural attraction between Sri Krsna and the gopis? If one tries to understand Gaudiya-darsana by this Padma-niti, the moral framework of Padma, then one's vision will not be Gaudiya-darsana. Rather, it will be 'agaudiya-darsana'. Gaudiyas are atmavit, knowers of the spiritual self. Viewing them through anatma-darsana, non-spiritual vision, will not work. One's conceptions of all the wonderful varieties of cit-vilasa are the deliberation of Gaudiya-darsana only after becoming atmavit, a knower of the self, and after being situated in one's svarupa. Those who are not situated in the svarupa of the Gaudiyas do not have a vision of the vastu (true substance) due to their anyabhilasa (extraneous desires), karma (fruitive activities), jnana (impersonal consciousness) or the desire for moksa (liberation).

The conception of Gaudiya-darsana is a post-liberation conception. Those who are not liberated have anarthas (harmful tendencies). Temporary goals (tatkalika-prayojana) remain present in all their activities. In contrast, Gaudiya-darsana is a conception that occurs after being liberated.

## Derived forms of Gaudiya-darsana in all grammatical cases

Gaudiya-darsana is integrated in all the grammatical cases from the first case (nominative) to the seventh case (locative).

1\) Gaudiya-vision - prathama vibhakti (nominative)

2\) Vision having Gaudiya as its object - dvitiya vibhakti (accusative)

3\) Vision by the Gaudiya - tritiya vibhakti (instrumental)

4\) Vision for the sake of the Gaudiya - caturthi vibhakti (dative)

5\) Vision from the Gaudiya - pancami vibhakti (ablative)

6\) The Gaudiya's vision - sasthi vibhakti (genitive)

7\) Vision in the Gaudiya - saptami vibhakti (locative)

Gaudiya-darsana has propagated its own aprakrtatva (supranaturalness) in all these terms.

## The object of Gaudiya-darsana

The object of Gaudiya-darsana is Gaudiya-natha, the Lord of the Gaudiyas. Sri Gaurasundara is Gaudiya-natha. Sri Gaurasundara is Sri Krsna. Sri Krsna is the cause of all causes, the embodiment of eternity, consciousness and bliss, beginningless, the origin of all. He is Govinda. The word 'darsana' in Gaudiya-darsana does not mean sambhoga (union). Rather, it indicates seva (service). In the extremely profound state of service, the cetana-vrttis, modifications of supranatural prana within the pure mind, become visible by pervading all the limbs (sarvangina-bhava). Gaudiya-darsana is only possible by all these vrttis.

## Gaudiya-darsana through absorption of the ears and tongue in service

Just as the proverb raja pasyati karnabhyam states, "The king sees through the ears," similarly Gaudiyas have darsana of Gaudiya-natha through the ears hearing 'gaudiyah’ (nominative), 'gaudiyam’ to the gaudiya (accusative), 'gaudiyena’ by the gaudiya (instrumental), 'gaudiyaya’ for the gaudiya (dative), 'gaudiyat' from the gaudiya (ablative), 'gaudiyasya' of the gaudiya (genitive), and 'gaudiye' in the gaudiya (locative). And by the supranatural tongue which is absorbed in service, the Gaudiyas always experience Gaudiya-darsana when speaking 'gaudiya-darsanam' (nominative), 'darsanam gaudiyam’ vision having the gaudiya as its object (accusative), ’darsanam gaudiyena’ vision by the gaudiya (instrumental), 'darsanam gaudiyaya’ vision for the gaudiya (dative), 'darsanam gaudiyat' vision from the gaudiya (ablative), ‘darsanam gaudiyasya’ vision of the gaudiya (genitive), and 'darsanam gaudiye’ vision in the gaudiya (locative).

## Gaudiya and Gaudiya-bruva

'Gaudiya' and 'gaudiya-bruva’ (Gaudiya in name), 'viddha-gaudiya’ (corrupted Gaudiya) and 'agaudiya’ (non-Gaudiya) are not the same. A natural (genuine) Gaudiya and an artificial (fraudulent) Gaudiya, simply dressed as a Gaudiya, can never be equal. Without being Gaudiya internally and externally, while inhaling and exhaling, resting and dreaming, in every way, in all places at all times and in all circumstances, one will not enter into Gaudiya-darsana.

## Darsana is sambandha, darsana is abhidheya and darsana is prayojana

Action (kriya) is after darsana and after action is ista-siddhi, the perfection of cherished desires. On deliberating on darsana in the form of sambandha, the darsanamaya abhidheya and darsanamaya prema-prayojana arises. The endeavour of fraudulent bhajana which is devoid of darsana is nothing but shooting an arrow in the dark. Darsana gives clear ascertainment of the reality of the vastu. After this, bhajana (service) to the real substance takes place. Without darsana, doing bhajana is simply "shooting an arrow by guesswork”. Gaudiya-darsana does not occur with eyes made of blood and flesh. Therefore, Sri Bilvamangala Thakura removed both of his eyes made of blood and flesh and began to render service with darsana of the aprakrta-vastu. Furthermore, only 'darsana' is bhajana. Where there is no obstacle or severance between bhajana and darsana, there is no obstacle between bhajana and the prayojana. Where there is no difference between bhajana and the prayojana, only there lies the asana (seat) of Gaudiya-darsana.

## In Gaudiya-gaurava - 'sambandha'. In Gaudiya-sahitya - 'abhidheya'. In Gaudiya-darsana - the glorification of ‘prayojana’

In order to situate those who eagerly long for their ultimate goal in sambandha-jnana, the subject of Gaudiya-gaurava (dignity) has been spoken. In order to convey the abhidheya of the Gaudiyas, the subject of Gaudiya-sahitya (beneficial literature) has been spoken. Srila Jiva Gosvami Prabhu has said in his commentary that the meaning of the word 'sahitya' is bhakti to Bhagavan. And, in order to indicate by a hint, the inner intention of prayojana, the subject of Gaudiya-darsana has been spoken. All these combined are 'Gaudiya-darsana'. In Gaudiya-darsana there is Gaudiya-gaurava, Gaudiya-sahitya and Gaudiya-prayojana.

## Sri Krsna-priti is the foundational subject of discussion in Gaudiya-darsana

Where priti (love) for Bhagavan and one’s own priti are one substance or one in purpose, there is no inconvenience at all. Gaudiya-darsana is situated in this unbroken flow. However, where aupadhika-priti (love with material conditions) or love for liberation has covered love for Bhagavan, love for Sri Krsna is not found. In this way, the hankering for love in the form of freedom from the threefold miseries is dharma-priti, artha-priti, anartha-priti, kama-priti or moksa-priti. These are all subjects of agaudiya-darsana. In Gaudiya-darsana, except for love for Sri Krsna, there is not the slightest scent of any other priti.

## The necessity of experiencing Gaudiya-darsana only with eyes given by Gaudiyas

Gaudiyas see in such a way that simply by seeing they can attain their cherished desire. In other words, they can see Sri Krsna’s name, vigraha (deity) and svarupa as non-different. If we do not fully appropriate Gaudiya-darsana, then there will be no purpose of human life, and we must remain like an owl, blind in broad daylight. Therefore, in Srimad-Bhagavatam, the king of Gaudiya-darsana scriptures, it has been stated:

*labdhva su-durlabham idam bahu-sambhavante*  
*manusyam artha-dam anityam apiha dhirah*  
*turnam yateta na pated anu-mrtyuyavan*  
*nihsreyasaya visayah khalu sarvatah syat*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.9.29*

**This human birth is attained after many births; therefore, it is extremely rare. Although this birth is temporary, it can bestow the highest purpose of life. Thus, an intelligent person, without delaying for even a moment, should endeavour for his ultimate welfare before death comes.**

## Gaudiya visionaries are veda-drk not mamsa-drk

It is necessary to emphasize repeatedly that there is no Gaudiya-darsana through eyes made of skin or eyes engaged in enjoying. The Gaudiya visionaries are not mamsa-drk, seers of flesh with eyes of flesh. Rather, they are veda-drk, the highest visionaries of the Vedas. Therefore, Sri Brahmaji, the first acarya of Gaudiya-darsana has stated:

*premanjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilocanena*  
*santah sadaiva hrdayesu vilokayanti*  
*yam syamasundaram acintya-guna-svarupam*  
*govindam adi-purusam tarn aham bhajami*  
*Sri Brahma-samhita 5.38*

**I worship the original purusa Govinda, Syamasundara Krsna, replete with inconceivable qualities, whom the devotees see within their hearts by devotional eyes smeared with the ointment of prema.**

## Eyes of devotion are aprakrta (supranatural)

Only the sadhu whose eyes of bhakti are smeared with the ointment of prema sees within his heart the embodiment of inconceivable virtues, Sri Syamasundara. Gaudiya-darsana occurs through the eyes of bhakti, not through the eyes of kalpanika (imaginary) or mithya (false) bhakti. Many people consider the slippery eyes of sentimental persons imbued with prakrtika-svabhava (mundane nature) to be ‘bhaktimaya-netra’, eyes of devotion. Therefore, the inconceivability of Gaudiya-darsana is not the object of their darsana.

## Acintya-bhedabheda-siddhan ta and the authentic Maha-vakya

Acintya-bhedabheda-siddhanta is Gaudiya-darsana. After the jiva has attained the condition called bhuta-suddhi (purification of his existence), that is, when he is free from anartha (harmful obstacles), he realizes his svarupa. Then he can understand that the svarupa (essential nature) of the Vedic mantras such as 'tat tvam asi', 'aham brahmasmi', 'sarvam khalvidam brahma' and 'prajnanam brahma' is expressed clearly in the verse 'trnad api sunicena’. He can understand that the instruction of Sri Caitanya, 'kirtaniyah sada harih’, is the authentic Maha-vakya, the analysis of the ubiquitous Maha-vakya of the Vedas and the fully expanded form of pranava, om. He discovers the jewel casket of Gaudiya-darsana in Sri Krsna’s name. In other words, simply by cultivating the name of 'Sri Krsna’ he opens the jewellery box of Gaudiya-darsana. Then he recites the mantra of the Rg Veda and says:

*om asya jananto nama cid-vivaktan mahas te visno*  
*sumatim bhajamahe om tat sat*  
*Rg Veda 1.156.3*

O Visnu! Because your name is cit-svarupa, spiritual, it is self-revealing. Thus, if anyone utters this name, that is, if they simply repeat the syllables (aksarabhyasa), then, upon uttering this name appropriately or not even being aware of its glories, knowledge of Bhagavan will be attained. The reason is that the substance illuminated by pranava is sat, meaning svatah-siddha, self-existent. Therefore, it is heard that even if one utters the name out of fear or enmity, one attains the sphurti of Sri Murti, a vision of Bhagavan’s spiritual form. Even in such conditions, liberation will be attained on uttering the name because the attainment of mukti is the fruit of the four types of namabhasa beginning with 'sanketya'.

## Deliberation on Sri Nama-bhajana in the Sruti and Brahma-sutra

This first lesson in spirituality is also contained in the Sruti-mantras. There is much deliberation on the subject of bhakti to Bhagavan in the Upanisads. In the beginning and end of every sutra of Vedanta, Sri Nama Prabhu’s unexpanded form (asphuta/ aspastarupa'), the murti of pranava, is established. The ultimate fruit of brahma-jijnasa, being desirous to inquire about the Absolute Truth, is the visaya-vigraha of Gaudiya-darsana, Sri Nama, or the service of sabda-brahma. And only this Sri Nama-seva has been glorified in the final sutra of Vedanta, anavrttih sabdat anavrttih sabdat. \[Brahma-sutra 4.4.22)

## The speciality of the name ‘Sri Krsna’

Various names are prevalent in different countries such as Jehovah, Jesus, Jupiter, Ahura, Malad, Allah, God, Isvara, Paramatma and Brahma. However, the complete rasa-madhurya, the sweetness of rasa in the primary names of Bhagavan such as Sri Krsna, Sri Syamasundara, Sri Nandanandana and Sri Vrndavanendra is not seen in any other name. The name of Sri Krsna is the object of Gaudiya-darsana, the fully expanded form of pranava and the very embodiment of the nectar of all rasas.

## Sri Sarasvati-tosani Commentary by Sri Prema Prayojana dasa

Sri Sri Guru-Gaurangau jayatah!

Every generation of devotees faces a crisis of interpretation in regard to the correct understanding of Gaudiya-siddhanta. Prabhupada Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, who endeavours whole-heartedly to fulfil the innermost heart’s desire of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, has presented his thesis entitled Gaudiya-darsana to deliver all future generations from the epistemological misconceptions that lie at the root of all controversies. We bow down eternally at his lotus feet and pray that he may be pleased by this insignificant offering, the Sri Sarasvati-tosani commentary.

**Divisible and indivisible knowledge**

The Supreme Absolute Truth, Lord Sri Krsna, is described as advaya-jnana-tattva, the nondual conscious reality. Since He is akhanda-tattva, indivisible reality, He cannot be grasped by the familiar method of learning with which students of divisible subjects are accustomed. The method of imbibing a material subject involves engaging the material intellect in the systematic accumulation of individual units of information, from which the overall picture of the total conception is gradually constructed. However, since Sri Krsna is akhanda-tattva, the indivisible reality beyond the purview of material intellect, conceptual constructions derived from piecemeal accumulation are wholly inadequate to approach Him. The inhabitants of the material world cannot conceive of another method of understanding a subject, so they inevitably carry the burden of this mundane psychological habit with them even on their journey towards the Sri Brahma-Madhva-Gaudiya Sampradaya. To relieve such travellers from this unnecessary burden, Prabhupada Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, impelled by boundless compassion, has presented the sacred text of Gaudiya-darsana.

One may ask, "If not by the accumulation of units of information, then by what method is the truth to be known?" The scriptures reply, "sabda-brahma." The truth makes His appearance on the lips of His own pure devotees in the form of transcendental sound. Sri Krsna, who is always visible to the spiritual vision of the pure devotee spiritual master, manifests Himself in the form of sabda-brahma emanating from His devotee’s lips. When Sri Krsna, in the form of sabda-brahma, is received by the ear of the affectionate and surrendered disciple, He manifests His Divine Form within the purified heart of that fortunate disciple. Thus, the advaya-jnana-tattva, nondual conscious reality, is transmitted in His undivided glory from Sri Guru to disciple and the parampara continues in its pristine purity.

This transmission of the direct experience of Sri Krsna constitutes the anga of bhakti called sravana. In the absence of direct experience, sravana does not take place. Rather, only the imitation of devotion is present. If the speaker is not realized, transmission of transcendental knowledge is impossible.

If the speaker is realized, but the hearer is not surrendered—if the hearer is afflicted by kartrtva-abhimana, the sense of bodily-identification and material agenthood, if the hearer maintains a spirit of independence from Sri Guru and ambitions for worldly honour and profit—then the sabda-brahma, impeded by these psychological obstructions, does not manifest its self-revelatory nature (sphota-dharma). In such a case, knowledge is received within the disordered mind of the immature disciple in four corrupt forms:

1\)    paricchinna-jnana - incomplete theoretical thought-constructs subject to division by time and space.

2\)    sankirna-jnana - contracted knowledge lacking in detail and mixed with paricchinna-jnana.

3\)    vikrta-jnana - distorted knowledge.

4\)    vivarta-grasta-jnana - inverted knowledge.

In this instance, the so-called disciple, driven by ulterior motives or social and familial conventions, may attempt to insert himself into the Sri Guru-parampara. However, since sabda-brahma has not been successfully transmitted, that particular branch of the parampara becomes a lifeless imitation. Although the essence of the Guru-parampara is no longer present, the confused masses of neophyte devotees are incapable of distinguishing between the genuine current of transcendental sravana and kirtana and its theatrical simulacra. All apasampradayas (deviant sects) arise in the same way when the impeded aural reception of sabda-brahma results in the nonmanifestation of its sphota-dharma (self-revelatory nature).

**Bhakti coincides with direct experience (darsana)**

All scriptures glorify the power of bhakti to bestow the direct experience of Sri Krsna.

*raja-vidya raja-guhyam pavitram idam uttamam /*  
*pratyaksavagamam dharmyam su-sukham kartum avyayam*  
*Sri Bhagavad-gita 9.2*

**This (bhakti) is the king of knowledge, the king of secrets, the most purifying of all methods, bringing direct realization of the Lord. It is easy to perform and eternal.**

*bhaktih paresdnubhavo viraktir*  
*anyatra caisa trika eka-kdlah*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.2.42*

**Devotion, direct experience of the Supreme Lord, and detachment from other things — these three occur simultaneously.**

*ye tu tvadiya-caranambuja-kosa-gandham*  
*jighranti karna-vivaraih sruti-vata-nitam*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 3.9.5*

**O Master! Your devotees smell the fragrance of Your lotus bud feet through their ears carried on the wind of the sound of the Vedas.**

From verses such as these, it is understood that the method of hearing sabda-brahma is fully competent to immediately bestow transcendental realization, without depending on material sense perception (pratyaksa) or intellectual conceptualization and logical analysis (anumana).

In Sarva-samvadini, anuccheda 9:

*sabdasya tu nairapeksyam, yatha—*  
*"dasamas tvam asi"ity adau \|*  
*sa esa sabdo dasamo'ham asmiti pramdyas tiraskdrinam*  
*moham sravana-patha-pravesamatrad*

**Sabda is however independent of other proofs (such as perception and inference). In the statement "You are the tenth person” the correct knowledge "I am the tenth person" removes obscuring bewilderment just by entering the ear. it is clearly independent. (Sarva-samvadini, anuccheda 9)**

Here, an example has been given to illustrate the independent power of hearing. Ten persons cross a river. After crossing, one person is deputed to check if all persons have arrived safely. He counts only nine persons but cannot find the tenth. A bystander then informs him, dasamas tvam asi "You are the tenth.” Simply by hearing, all confusion is removed by the immediate appearance of correct knowledge. Similarly, for one who hears the pure Holy Name of Sri Krsna, or even the abhasa of the pure Holy Name of Sri Krsna, jhatiti pratita, immediate experience takes place. If there is no immediate experience, then it cannot be said that actual sravana has occurred.

*atrayaih sastre'tiprasiddhaih sri-bhagavan*  
*eva jhatiti pratito bhavati,*  
*yesam ca sankety-adav api tadrsa-prabhavah sruyate*  
*Bhagavat-sandarbha, anuccheda 47*

**It is heard that those names of the Lord, which are famous in scripture, invoke immediate experience of the Lord. Even the four types of abhasa of those names, beginning with sanketya, manifest such potency.**

**Direct experience of Sri Krsna in the stage of sadhana**

It is necessary to understand how direct experience of Sri Krsna takes place during the stage of sadhana. The subtle body is composed of five elements:

1\) citta

2\) prana (sutra-tattva)

3\) ahankara

4\) buddhi

5\) manas

Pradhana is the total material ingredient cause in the state of unmanifest potential. When pradhana is agitated by time, mahat-tattva is the first element to appear. In the cosmos, this element is called mahat-tattva, but the portion of the same element appearing in the subtle body of the jiva is called citta. Being highly sattvika, it is bright, luminous, steady and reflective. When rajas becomes prominent in citta, it grossifies into sutra-tattva, otherwise known as prana. Prana is the active principle in nature. When citta becomes influenced more by tamas, the transformations of prana \[citta-vrttis\] solidify into impressions (samskaras). The condensed mass of impressions that darken the citta is called ahankara (ego). Further condensation of the ego occurs producing phase transitions into three evolutes. The relatively more sattvika portion of ego becomes the element manas. The relatively more rdjasika portion of ego becomes the element buddhi and the senses. This concludes the formation of the subtle psychological body. The relatively more tamasika portion of ego becomes the element sabda (material sound), which in turn grossifies into akasa (space), air, fire, water and earth. This concludes the formation of the gross physical body.

Just as water condenses through the phase transitions of gas, vapour, liquid and ice, the subtle psychological body is essentially nothing but citta undergoing progressively gross phase transitions to become prana, ego, buddhi and manas. Citta is the naturally sattvika pre-egoistic mind, whereas the mind accessible to the general person is the impure sub-egoistic mind.

**The place of perception and service**

Sridharasvami and Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura both describe the special characteristics of citta, the sattvika pre-egoistic mind:

*svacchatvam bhagavad-bimba-grahitvam*

**The citta is possessed of clarity, that is, citta has the capacity to be made to catch the reflection of Bhagavan. Bhavartha-dipika and Sarartha-darsini on Srimad-Bhagavatam 3.26.22**

Furthermore, Sridharasvami refers to the citta as:

*upalabdhi-sthanam: the place of perception of Bhagavan.*

*Bhavartha-dipika on Srimad-Bhagavatam 3.26.22*

And Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura elaborates calling the citta:

*upasana-pitham: the place of service to Bhagavan.*

*Sarartha-darsini on Srimad-Bhagavatam 3.26.22*

From this analysis, it is concluded that the disciple’s citta is at first obscured by the element of material ego. As he is gradually purified by service and surrender to Sri Guru, his citta proportionately becomes exposed to his subjective awareness. In that bright and reflective citta, he then attains darsana of the refection of Sri Krsna simply by hearing hari-katha from the lips of Sri Guru. Thus, he realizes the true nature of the anga of bhakti known as sravana.

In this way, we have presented the metaphysical details concerning genuine sravana, which produces simultaneous sphurti (vision) arising from the sphota-dharma (self-revelatory nature) of transcendental sound in the stage of sadhana.

**The stages of hearing**

In Bhakti-sandarbha, Srila Jiva Gosvami has presented the sequence of divine revelation through hearing:

*prathamam namnah sravanam*  
*antahkarana-suddhy-artham apeksyam \|*  
*suddhe cantahkarane rupa-sravanena*  
*tad-udaya-yogyata bhavati \|*  
*samyagudite ca rupe gunanam sphuranam sampadyate \|*  
*tatas tesu nama-rupagunesu tat-parikaresu ca samyak*  
*sphuritesv eva lilanam sphuranam susthu bhavatity*  
*abhipretya sadhana-kramo likhitah \|*  
*evam kirtana-smaranayor jneyam \|*  
*Bhakti-sandarbha 256*

**"At first a person must hear the Holy Name to achieve purity of the antah-karana, or psychological faculty \[citta\]. Thereafter, when his citta has become pure, it is a fit appearance place for the Lord’s beautiful form, and as soon as the Lord’s form thoroughly manifests in his heart as a result of hearing descriptions of that form, the devotee experiences revelations \[sphurti\] of the Lord’s qualities. As the Lord's qualities fully unfold, the defining characteristics of His associates (and thus His own defining characteristics and His unique interrelationship with each of them) are exhibited. After that, when the Lord's name, form, qualities and associates have completely manifested in the bhakta's heart, the Lord’s pastimes blossom in their full splendour. The progression of sadhana has been delineated in this way with the intention of acquainting the devotees with the sequential process involved in the Lord's appearance within their hearts. The same sequence is applicable in regard to the practice of kirtana, chanting, and smarana, remembrance.”**

Note that until one step in the sequence has become complete, that is, unless that stage has clearly awakened as a sphurti (vision) within the citta, one is not eligible for further realization of the next step in the sequence.

On the path of Sri Nama-bhajana, Sri Nama Prabhu Himself is karta (the doer). The droha-vadi (self-elevationist) is always averse to the process of soliciting the mercy of the Holy Name and adopting the receptive attitude due to his kartrtva-abhimana, self-identification as a material agent. Bhakti is avaroha-pantha, the path of descending grace. The aggressive attempt to forcefully progress faster than one's level of adhikara will result in frustration. One is bound to lose faith and give up bhakti out of exhaustion when a great expenditure of effort proves futile due to the misapplication of one’s energy. Many such cases have been documented in the past and the present. One should be careful to avoid adding to the grim statistics. A peaceful soul, considering himself weak and insignificant, remains fully dependent on the mercy of the Lord in the performance of all devotional activities and soon becomes the beneficiary of the charming sweetness of Sri Krsna’s favour.

**The difference between darsana in the stage of sadhana and darsana in the stage of bhava**

As genuine hearing gives darsana, similarly, the other angas of bhakti instantly bestow perception. Bhava-bhakti is the essence of samvit and hladini-vrttis. Samvit-vrtti bestows realization of Sri Krsna. In the stage of sadhana, this samvit-vrtti is experienced as a reflection (abhasa) in the sattvika-citta. In the stage of bhava, the citta melts and comes under the control of bhava-bhakti. Thus, the perception of Sri Krsna becomes direct rather than reflected. This has been explained by Lord Siva to Sati (Srimad-Bhagavatam 4.3.23). Srila Jiva Gosvami comments:

*apavrta dvarana-sunyah san prakasate \|*  
*prakrtam sattvam cet, tarhi tatra pratiphalanam evavasiyate \|*  
*tatas ca darpane mukhasyeva tad-antargatataya tasya*  
*tatravrtatvenaiva prakasah syad iti bhavah  \|*  
*Bhagavat-sandarbha, anccheda 88*

Apavrta means that the Lord manifests without any covering in the stage of visuddha-sattva (from bhava onwards). If he were perceived in material sattva (citta), then the form would be a reflection. Just as a face is indirectly perceived in a mirror, the form of the Lord perceived via material sattva would be of a covered nature.

It is this mirror-like nature of the citta to which Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu refers in Sri Siksastakam (1) with the phrase, ceto-darpana-marjanam bhava-mahadavagni-nirvapanam, "The chanting of the Holy Names of Sri Krsna cleanses the mirror of the citta and extinguishes the great forest fire of material existence.”

**Darsana of Sri Krsna in the stage of nistha**

The vision of Sri Krsna's form and opulent qualities appear reflected in the citta in the stage of nistha.

*tatah prakrstat sangad anartha-nivrttikah katha bhavanti \|*  
*tatas ta eva katha nistham utpadayantyo mama*  
*viryasya man-mahatmyasya samvit samyag*  
*vedanam yatas tathabhuta bhavanti \|*

**In excellent association, My katha causes anartha-nivrtti. Then those stories cause nistha and those narrations become directly realization (samvidah) of My great qualities (virya). (Sarartha-darsini on Srimad-Bhagavatam 3.25.25)**

**Darsana of Sri Krsna in the stage of ruci**

*rucih papa-bija-nasad bhaktau madhuryanubhavah*

*Artha-ratnalpa-dlpika 1.4.15*

**In the stage of ruci, due to the destruction of the remnants of the subtle impressions of past sins, the citta becomes suitable to accommodate realization of the sweetness of Sri Krsna in the activities of bhakti.**

The vision of Sri Krsna’s sweet qualities and associates appear reflected in the citta in the stage of ruci. Then one may develop a taste for serving in the wake of those associates. Without perception of the associates it is not possible to attain the transcendental greed to follow them.

*tasya tadrsa-raga-sudhakara-karabhasa-samullasita-hrdaya-sphatika-maneh sastradi-srutasu tadrsya ragatmikaya bhakteh paripatisv api rucir jayate /*

*Bhakti-sandarbha 310*

**When a person hears from Sri Guru about the raga of the eternal associates of Sri Krsna described in scripture, his heart becomes like a radiant crystal reflecting the moonlike rays of their raga and he develops a taste for the methods of service of those eternal associates.**

In fact, in the absence of ruci, one cannot even understand the methods of service of the eternal associates of Sri Krsna.

*tasminn eva nana-viksepavati rucy-abhavena*  
*ragatmika-bhakti-sailim anabhijanati*  
*Bhakti-sandarbha 312*

**In the absence of ruci, a person afflicted with various types of mental distraction cannot understand the methods of service of the eternal associates.**

Since the Absolute Truth is indivisible, sabda and sabdi (the signifying sound and signified subject) are one; nama and nami (the name and the named) are one. Therefore, genuine sravana implies darsana. Kirtana implies darsana. Smarana implies darsana. Pada-sevana implies darsana. Arcana implies darsana. Vandana implies darsana. If darsana is not taking place, it is understood that one has not begun the sadhana of uttama-bhakti which is characterised by anyabhilasita-sunyam, the complete absence of any desires extraneous to the service of Sri Krsna.The first twenty angas of the sixty four angas of bhakti such as taking shelter of the spiritual master, receiving diksa and instruction, faithfully serving the spiritual master, inquiring about the eternal dharma, and so on, constitute the door to enter the realm of bhakti.

*asyas tatra pravesaya dvaratve 'py anga-vimsateh*

*Sri Bhakti-rasdmrta-sindhu 1.2.83*

**These twenty angas serve as the door for entering bhakti.**

Only when these preliminary limbs of bhakti have been performed with implicit faith and steadiness does one enter into the realm of devotion characterized by simultaneous realization of Sri Krsna and detachment from all sensory objects of the external physical world. One should not confuse the subjective experience of hearing and chanting performed by those who have yet to enter the realm of uttama-bhakti with the experience of those who have attained nistha and beyond. Many persons miss the chance to experience uttama-bhakti by trying to circumvent pravesa-dvara, the door to enter the realm of uttama-bhakti. Independent efforts to enter the realm of bhakti are always fruitless.

*mahat-krpa vina kona karme 'bhakti' naya*  
*krsna-bhakti dure rahu, samsara nahe ksaya*  
*SriCaitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-Illa 22.51*

**"Unless one is favored by a pure devotee, one cannot attain the platform of devotional service. To say nothing of krsna-bhakti, one cannot even be relieved from the bondage of material existence."**

Daily service to and hearing from the bhakta-bhagavata, pure devotee of Sri Krsna, removes the otherwise intractable effects of namaparadha and one is blessed with the eligibility for uttama-bhakti.

The sphurti of nama, rupa, guna etc. will not take place until one attains the requisite degree of suddhata of the antah-karana. Since namabhasa quickly purifies the citta, one should understand the absence of realization to be the result of aparadha.

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura has written in his article Vaisnava Ninda:

"If one commits namaparadha, a sphurti (divine revelation) of ndma will never take place, and without such sphurtis it is not possible to become a Vaisnava." (Sajjana-tosani, Volume 5 May-July issues, 1893)

If the sphurti is not appearing, the insufficient purity of heart is due to aparadha. There is no esoteric meditation or secret technique of artificial smarana to remedy this situation. The Holy Name is fully independent. If one makes the Holy Name subordinate to some imaginary secrets of mental arcana, he compounds offences and becomes acikitsya-namaparadhi, an incurable offender to the Holy Name. Such an unfortunate person is incurable because he is convinced that his offensive notion of bhajana is the actual path recommended by sastra. He may even recruit countless scriptural statements as evidence to support his misconceptions. This brings us to the subject of correct and incorrect scriptural interpretation.

**Response to the perennial crisis of scriptural interpretation**

Controversy surrounding the correct exegesis of scriptural passages is ubiquitous in religious communities. As the poet William Blake famously wrote:

"Both read the Bible day and night, but thou read black where I read white."

The community of aspiring devotees of the Gaudiya tradition is no exception to this rule. Reaching a broad consensus is also no guarantee of validity, as conditioned souls are prone to accept as settled orthodoxy a set of completely erroneous doctrines. This was the condition of most of the Gaudiya community at the time of Prabhupada Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati. In order to correct the course of the ship of Gaudiya Vaisnavism, Srila Prabhupada established Sri Gaudiya Matha as the vehicle for his profound conceptions.

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati has beautifully defined Sri Gaudiya Matha as follows:

"In a world inundated with ajna-rudhi, Sri Gaudiya Matha is the sacred place of the descent of the vidvad-rudhi of words."

The whole world will benefit by taking note of this sublime declaration. The terms used here require a brief explanation. Every pada (word) has artha (meaning). The potency by which a word conveys meaning is called vrtti. Vrttis are of three main types:

1\)    abhidha-vrtti - primary meaning (mukhydrtha)

2\)    laksana-vrtti - associated meaning

3\)    vyanjana-vrtti - suggested meaning

Abhidha-vrtti is also of three types:

1\)    yaugiki - The etymological meaning derived from the combination of verbal roots and suffixes e.g. pacaka (chef) from рас dhatu (to cook).

2\)    rudhi - The conventional meaning derived from usage, independent of etymology e.g. the conventional meaning of 'mandapa' is a temporary pavilion for festivities. In this case, the etymological meaning, mandam pati 'one who guards the ornaments’ is not considered.

3\)    yoga-rudhi - A combination of the etymological meaning derived from the combination of verbal roots and suffixes and the conventional meaning derived from usage. E.g. pankaja -etymologically (yoga), pankaja means 'that which grows from mud'. Many things grow in mud, but by conventional usage (rudhi), everyone understands pankaja to refer only to a lotus flower.

Rudhi is of three types - ajna-rudhi, sadharana-rudhi and vidvad-rudhi:

1\)    vidvad-rudhi makes visible advaya-jnana, nondual awareness within the consciousness of the vidvan (perfectly realized saints).

2\)    sadharana-rudhi binds the functions of the material intelligence in concentration on substances as they are customarily defined in worldly dealings.

3\)    ajna-rudhi increases the contraction of meaning even more than sadharana-rudhi and establishes the customs of rajo-guna and tamo-guna in the world of general experience. These distinct angles of vision in relation to the meaning of words arise in conjunction with the various perceptions of the living entities.

The vidvad-rudhi of all words indicates Para-brahma. In vidvad-rudhi a word is not merely a meaning-bearing sound (vacaka), rather, experience of the referent (vacya) is fully manifest within it. From this we can understand that the name and the named are non-different.

When Sri Gaurasundara returned to Sri Navadvipa-dhama after His pilgrimage to Gaya and His initiation by Srila Isvara Puri, He began to teach His students the meaning of all words in accordance with vidvad-rudhi-vrtti.

Srila Prabhupada writes in The Harmonist vol XXX No. 2, September 1932:

“On His return from Gaya, He told His pupils that there is no other word except Krsna. In vidvad-rudhi received by the unalloyed soul there would be no demarcation between the sound and the object. If we talk of things transcendental, our present senses will fail to grasp them. Ordinary sounds have only a present-day need not required after death. The Transcendental Sound will bring us in touch with the Oversoul.

“The name, as a product of Nature, should not be confounded with the Name of Godhead Who is Indivisible

Knowledge (advaya-jnana). All sounds are of help if they are meant to be identified with the Absolute and not with phenomenon. If we are recipients of sounds for our purpose, it will make us miss the Absolute Sound. The Absolute Sound is required to carry us to the region of the Absolute. There is no other agency. Our eyes are defective and make us see only the mirage by way of apparent sight. Any departure from the method will prevent us from catching the spirit of the transcendental communication.” (Harmonist vol XXX No. 2, September 1932)

By His pastime of instructing His students in accordance with vidvad-rudhi, Sri Gaurasundara illustrated the conclusion of Vedanta-sutra:

*caracara-vyapasrayas tu syat tad-vyapadeso*  
*'bhaktas tad-bhava-bhavitvat*  
*Vedanta-sutra 2.3.15*

Words denote primarily the Lord, because He abides as the self-dependent support of all things moveable and immoveable. Therefore, it will be learned from scripture that every word is primarily one of His names.

**The Vaisnava-manjusa**

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati understood that the conclusions of Gaudiya Vaisnava literature were being obfuscated by the widespread misinterpretation of many key terms and definitions. It is a demonstrable fact that misunderstandings regarding the correct interpretation of scripture arise from accepting the ajna-rudhi and sadharana-rudhi of words rather than the vidvad-rudhi. Therefore, Srila Prabhupada wanted to compile the Vaisnava-manjusa, a comprehensive lexicon presenting the vidvad-rudhi of terminology found in Gaudiya Vaisnava literature.

To that end, in 1920 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati visited Maharaja Manindra-candra Nandi of Kashimbazar to convince him of the great necessity of the Vaisnava-manjusa. After hearing about the project, the Maharaja of Kashimbazar agreed to sponsor its publication. However, the project remained unfinished. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati declared that if he did not complete the task of compiling the Vaisnava-manjusa in his lifetime, then he would take birth again just for that very purpose.

**The practical application of vidvad-rudhi**

Let us apply the epistemic principle of vidvad-rudhi-vrtti to some critical verses that are central to the practical life of Gaudiya vaisnavas.

In regard to the eligibility for raganuga-bhakti sadhana, Srila Rupa Gosvamipada has written:

*tat-tad-bhavadi-madhurye srute dhir yad apeksate I*  
*natra sastram na yuktim ca tal-lobhotpatti-laksanam*  
*Sri Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu 1.2.292*

**After hearing about the sweetness of the loving moods of the inhabitants of Vraja, the appearance of greed is indicated when the intelligence does not depend on rules of scripture and logic.**

Upon reading this statement, many persons might conclude, "Yes. After hearing about the sentiments of the associates of Sri Krsna in Vraja, I feel attraction for such moods without depending on logic or scriptural injunctions. I must be eligible for raganuga-bhakti sadhana."

However, one must ask, "Am I interpreting the verse in accordance with vidvad-rudhi-vrtti or in accordance with ajna-rudhi?

In ajna-rudhi, the word srute (after hearing) denotes ‘hearing’ in the conventional sense it is used for everyday matters. For example, one might ‘hear’ the latest news or ‘hear’ the sound of vehicles passing in the street. Similarly, one may also ‘hear’ about the moods of the residente of Vraja.In vidvad-rudhi, the word ‘hearing’ implies "darsana". In other words, when a person who is sufficiently advanced to see the beautiful form, qualities and associates of Sri Krsna reflected in his citta witnesses the sweetness of their loving exchanges, without dependence on logic and scriptural injunctions, he spontaneously desires to also attain loving moods like those associates. This is the symptom of the awakening of greed (lobha). This interpretation based on the vidvad-rudhi-vrtti of the word srute is confirmed by Srila Jiva Gosvami who comments:

*srute sravana-dvara yat kimcid anubhute*

*Durgama-sangamani 1.2.292*

**‘After hearing’ implies ‘after perceiving the sweetness of the associates' moods to some extent by means of the hearing process’.**

Bear in mind that Srila Jiva Gosvami has explained that realization of the characteristics of Sri Krsna’s associates occurs only after the stage of completely realizing Sri Krsna’s form and qualities through the sphota-dharma (self-revelatory nature) of sabda-brahma (see Bhakti-sandarbha 256 cited previously). In other words, a person who has not realized Sri Krsna’s form and qualities certainly has no realization of the moods of His associates and thus is not in any way eligible for ragdnuga-bhakti. If, after understanding this tattva, a person insists on cheapening the exalted path of raganuga-bhakti by interpreting Srila Rupa Gosvami's words through ajna-rudhi-vrtti, then such a svakuhaka (self-deceiver) commits a severe transgression and thereby deprives himself and others of the priceless gift of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu.

Let us examine another key verse in accordance with vidvad-rudhi.

*krsnam smaran janam casya prestham nija-samihitam I*  
*tat-tat-katha-ratas casau kuryad vasam vraje sada*  
*Sri Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu 1.2.294*

**Remembering Sri Krsna along with a dear associate whose service is similar to one’s own desired service and absorbing oneself in hearing topics related to them, one should always reside in Vraja.**

Again, one may consider, "I am remembering Sri Krsna and His dear associate in whose wake I aspire to serve. Thus, I am practising raganuga-bhakti sadhana"

In ajna-rudhi, the word smarana denotes ‘remembering’, in the conventional sense it is used for everyday matters. For example, one might ‘remember’ one’s relatives or ‘remember’ the description of a historical person. Similarly, one may also ‘remember’ a dear associate of Sri Krsna in Vraja.

However, in vidvad-rudhi, the word ‘remembering’ implies 'darsana'. In Bhakti-sandarbha (anuccheda 275), Srila Jiva Gosvami defines smarana as manasanusandhanam: the close inspection with the mind of Sri Krsna’s name, form, qualities, associates, etc. In other words, a person who is sufficiently advanced to experience a sphurti of the beautiful form, qualities and associates of Sri Krsna reflected in his citta and who closely inspects that vision by his purified mind is actually engaged in the sadhana of raganuga-bhakti described in the verse: krsnam smaran janam casya... "Remembering Sri Krsna along with His dear associate.”

To accept any other meaning would entail rejecting all metaphysical claims regarding the aprakrta nature of Sri Krsna by considering the prakrta material mind capable of conceiving of the aprakrta-vastu.

*atah sri-krsna-namadi*  
*na bhaved grahyam indriyaih*  
*sevonmukhe hi jihvadau*  
*svayam eva sphuraty adah*  
*Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu 1.2.234*

**The material senses and mind cannot perceive or conceive of Sri Krsna’s name, form, qualities and pastimes. When one becomes attentive to service by chanting continuously, Sri Krsna’s name, form, qualities and pastimes become self-manifest (sphurati') upon the tongue and other senses.**

*aprakrta vastu nahe prakrta-gocara*  
*veda-puranete ei kahe nirantara*  
*Sri Caitanya-caritamrta, Madhya-lila 9.194*

**Spiritual substance is never within the jurisdiction of the material conception. This is always the verdict of the Vedas and Puranas.**

Sri Narada has assured us that the mind cannot visualize anything that has not been directly experienced in this life or in previous lives.

*sraddhatsvananubhuto 'rtho*  
*na manah sprastum arhati*  
*Srimad-Bhagavatam 4.29.65*

**It is not possible for anything to appear in the mind which has not been previously experienced.**

Those engaged in imaginary visualizations via the rajasika-vrttis of the impure sub-egoistic mind have no connection with suddha-bhakti. Purity of the citta, achieved through surrender, hearing and chanting, is the essential prerequisite for beginning the practice of smarana.

*atha sarandpatty-adibhih suddhantah-karanas cet nama-*  
*kirtanaparityagena smaranam kuryat*  
*Bhakti-sandarbha, anuccheda 275*

**"If the internal sense instrument (citta) has become purified (to its natural sattvika state) by surrender, service, hearing and chanting, etc., then, without giving up nama-kirtana, one can perform smarana."**

Even then, smarana cannot begin with lila-smarana. The following method involving sequential steps (krama) must be observed:

*atha purvavat krama-sopana-ritya sukha-labhyam guna-*  
*parikara-seva-lila smaranam canu-sandheyam*  
*Bhakti-sandarbha, anuccheda 278*

Just as we have discussed how hearing and chanting proceed in the order of name, form, qualities and finally pastimes - similarly, remembrance should also be taken up in the order that facilitates the attainment of each step in the progression.

Thus, after remembering Bhagavan's name and form, one should remember His qualities, then His associates, service and finally, pastimes.

Those who disregard the necessary sequence (krama) of naturally unfolding internal vision by artificially trying to jump ahead to an imaginary notion of lila-smarana can never be considered followers of Srila Rupa Gosvami.

By the application of vidvad-rudhi-vrtti the actual standard of suddha-bhakti is gloriously illuminated and the inauspicious darkness of the misconceptions spread by the cheap imitationists is dispelled for the benefit of the whole world. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati has expertly resolved the crisis of interpretation by his discourse on vidvad-rudhi-vrtti. In one stroke, Srila Prabhupada has re-established the siddanta of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu as presented by Srila Rupa Gosvami in Sri Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu, Srila Sanatana Gosvami in Sri Brhad-bhagavatamrta and Srila Jiva Gosvami in his Sat-sandarbhas, which had all but disappeared due to the influence of time.

"In a world inundated with ajna-rudhi, Sri Gaudiya Matha is the sacred place of the descent of the vidvad-rudhi of words."
