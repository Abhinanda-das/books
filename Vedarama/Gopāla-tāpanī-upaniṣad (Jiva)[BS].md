Gopala-tapani Upanisad, part of Atharva Veda, is often quoted by Gaudiya acaryas since it is a sruti text which clearly praises Krsna and the path of bhakti as supreme. Its main subject is Krsna. It also explains the Krsna mantra in detail. jiva, Prabhodananda, Visvanatha, and Baladeva have written commentaries on this work. I have taken Visvanatha’s commentary from the Sarasvata Gaudiya Asana and Mission edition. However, it may be an enlarged edition of jiva’s commentary, since the text is largely similar to his, and the concluding verse (which I did not include) is the same as jiva’s.

# Gopāla-tāpanī-upaniṣad (Jiva)\[BS\]

## Foreword

Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad, part of Atharva Veda, is often quoted by Gauḍīya ācāryas since it is a śruti text which clearly praises Kṛṣṇa and the path of bhakti as supreme. Its main subject is Kṛṣṇa. It also explains the Kṛṣṇa mantra in detail. Jīva, Prabhodānanda, Viśvanātha, and Baladeva have written commentaries on this Upaniṣad.

## Part One (Pūrva)

### 1.1

*oṁ*  
*sac-cid-ānanda-rūpāya kṛñṇāyākliñṭa-kāriṇe \|*  
*namo vedānta-vedyāya gurave buddhi-sākñiṇe \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Kṛñṇa, the form of eternity, knowledge and bliss, who performs actions without fatigue, who is to be known by the conclusion of the Vedas, who is the guru of all knowledge, and who reveals himself by guiding the intelligence of the jīva.**

**Комментарий:**

I offer respects to Rādhā and Kṛñṇa. The great sage Gautama in Gautamīya-tantra has proven and shown what is also recorded in the Pūrva-gopāla-tāpanī Upaniñad. It is said in Gautamīya-tantra:

*klīṁ-kārād-asṛjad viśvaṁ prana sruteḥ siraḥ*  
*la-kārāt pṛthivi jātā kārāj jala-sambhavaḥ*  
*ī-kārād vahnir utpanno ādād vāyur ajāyata*  
*bindor ākāśa-sambhutir bhutātmako manuḥ*

**The Upaniñads state that the universe was created from the syllable klīṁ. Earth from the l, water was produced from the k, fire from the ī, air from m, and ether from the bindu.**

Similarly in Pūrva-gopāla-tāpanī 27 it is said:

*tad iha kād āpo lāt pṛthivī \| īto’gniḥ \|*  
*bindor induḥ \| tan-nādād arka iti klīṁ-kārād asṛjam*

**Water arose from k. Earth arose from l. Fire arose from ī. The bindu is the moon and nāda is the sun. Thus from klīṁ arose the universe.**

Containing such a verse, the śruti named Gopāla-tāpanī of the Pippalāda branch of the Atharva Veda handed down by brāhmaṇas of the Parāśara gotra offers respects to Kṛñṇa, the subject of praise in the work, indicating his supreme position with the approval of all Vedānta texts in the first verse.

I offer respects to Kṛñna. This Kṛñṇa is the son of Devakī for he is described as brahmaṇyo devakī-putra in the Chāndogya Upaniñad of the Sāma Veda and is famous as bhagavān svayam in the Bhāgavatam.

For this reason he is offered respects. Namaḥ means “I offer my very self along with body, mind and words.” It is impossible to offer such respect to other persons.

His form is described. It is sat: untouched by time or place. It is cit: self manifesting. It is full of bliss (ānanda): intensely blissful, with no compare. He has a form (rūpāya), which will later be described in the questions and answers.

His supreme power is described: he is the untiring cause (akliñṭakāraṇe), since without effort he performs all actions. This means he has acintya-śakti or inconceivable power. He showed all the universes while he simultaneously dwelled within the universes to Brahmā. He gave liberation to Aghāsura and others, though it is very rarely attained by great jñānīs; and he immediately gave a rare spiritual form similar to a mother to Pūtanā. By playing his flute he suddenly gave great prema with hairs standing on end to Brahmā and Śiva and even to the non-moving entities. At every moment he astonishes all beings with his amazing form. He is the greatest friend to his most fortunate, eternal, intimate associates who are cherished by the greatest devotees like Brahmā and Lakñmī and by paramahaṁsas like Śukadeva.

But what is the proof of this? This is described by mentioning thathe is to be known by scriptures: he is to be known by Vedānta (vedānta-vedyāya). He is to be known by the best of the Vedas or by the final conclusion of all the Vedas.

The nature of his form is finally described by Bhāgavatam.

*yo ’yaṁ kālas tasya te ’vyakta-bandho*  
*ceñṭām āhuś ceñṭate yena viśvam*  
*nimeñādir vatsarānto mahīyāṁs*  
*taṁ tveśānaṁ kñema-dhāma prapadye*

**O inaugurator of the material energy! Time which starts with second and ends with years, which rotate to make the life time of Brahmā, and by which the universe moves, is said to be your movement. I surrender to the Supreme Lord, the abode of fearlessness and happiness. (SB** [**10.3.26**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/3.26)**)**

*na cāntar na bahir yasya na pūrvaṁ nāpi cāparam*  
*pūrvāparaṁ bahiś cāntar jagato yo jagac ca yaḥ*  
*taṁ matvātmajam avyaktaṁ martya-liṅgam adhokñajam*  
*gopikolūkhale dāmnā babandha prākṛtaṁ yathā*

**Thinking of the invisible Lord—who has no inside or outside, has nothing previous or after him, who existed in the past and will exist in the future, inside and outside of the universe, who is the universe— as her son, appearing with a human form though he was beyond material senses— she tied him to the mortar like a normal child with rope. (SB** [**10.9.13-14**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/9.13)**)**

*sa tvaṁ kathaṁ mama vibho ’kñi-pathaḥ parātmā*  
*yogeśvaraḥ śruti-dṛśāmala-hṛd-vibhāvyaḥ sākñād*  
*adhokñaja uru-vyasanāndha-buddheḥ syān me*  
*’nudṛśya iha yasya bhavāpavargaḥ*

**O almighty one, how is it that my eyes see you here before me? You are the Supreme Soul, whom the greatest devotees can fix within their pure hearts only by employing the spiritual eye of the Vedas. Then how, O transcendental Lord, are you directly visible to me, since my intelligence has been blinded by the severe tribulations of material life? Only one who has finished his material entanglement in this world should be able to see you. (SB** [**10.64.26**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/64.26)**)**

*aho bhāgyam aho bhāgyaṁ nanda-gopa-vrajaukasām*  
*yan-mitraṁ paramānandaṁ pūrṇaṁ brahma sanātanam*

**How greatly fortunate are Nanda Mahārāja, the cowherd men and all the other inhabitants of Vrajabhūmi! There is no limit to their good fortune, because Kṛñṇa gave them prema, which is the highest bliss, eternal and complete. (SB** [**10.14.32**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/14.32)**)**

Proofs of Kṛñṇa’s untiring ability to act are also found in the Bhāgavatam. That he is to be known by the conclusion of the Vedas is also stated in Bhagavad Gītā. Vedaiś ca sarvair aham eva vedyaḥ: I am to be known by all the Vedas. (BG [15.15](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/15.15))

This is also stated in Bhāgavatam.

*māṁ vidhatte ’bhidhatte māṁ vikalpyāpohyate tv aham*  
*etāvān sarva-vedārthaḥ śabda āsthāya māṁ bhidām*  
*māyā-mātram anūdyānte pratiñidhya prasīdati*

**The Vedas indicate bhakti as the action and indicate me as the meaning. I am the meaning of all the Vedas. I, as jñāna, am proposed and rejected as alternatives. The Vedas, taking shelter of me, proposing jñāna and then rejecting them as māyā, becomes happy by giving the devotees bliss. (SB** [**11.21.43**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-11/21.43)**)**

From where does the knowledge of such meaning bringing about the conclusion of the Vedas arise? This is answered by the word gurave: the knowledge arises from Kṛñṇa who is the guru. Kṛñṇa has appeared as the teacher of the meaning of the Vedas. And he is the cause of realization (buddhi-sakñiṇe). He causes realization of his form as described in the verse by being the presiding deity of intelligence. The conclusion is that one should surrender to him since he is most worthy of surrender.

Since respects are offered without indicating material results, the practitioner of śuddha-bhakti is the person who is qualified to hear this Upaniñad. The subject of the work is the real object known as Kṛñṇa. The goal or prayojana is śuddha-bhakti. The sambandha or relationship of items mentioned in the work is that sādhana of pure bhakti leads to the goal of pure bhakti for Kṛñṇa.1

### 1.2

*oṁ. munayo ha vai brāhmaṇam ūcuḥ—*  
*kaḥ paramo devaḥ ? kuto mṛtyur bibheti?*  
*kasya vijñānenākhilaṁ vijñātaṁ bhāti ?*  
*kenedaṁ viśvaṁ saṁsaratīti.*

**Перевод:**

**The sages spoke to Brahmā: Who is the supreme object of worship. Who does death fear? By knowing which person does all knowledge become manifest? By whose power does this universe move?**

**Комментарий:**

The nature of Kṛñṇa is now shown by questions and answers. Oṁ indicates the beginning of a work and invokes auspiciousness. It stands for the power of all words such as the Lord’s names and mantras. The sages (munayaḥ) are the Kumāras since they were the first sages. Ha vai indicates remembrance of the event. Devaḥ indicates a person worthy of worship. They ask his details in order to understand who is really worthy of that position. Who does death along with his descendents fear? Complete knowledge is revealed by knowing which person? By whom does this universe carry out its functions (saṁsarati).

### 1.3

*tad u hovāca brāhmaṇaḥ \| kṛñṇo vai paramaṁ daivataṁ \|*  
*govindān mṛtyur bibheti \|*  
*gopī-jana-vallabha-jñānenataj-jñānaṁ bhavati \|*  
*svāhayedaṁ saṁsaratīti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Brahmā, knower of the Vedas, then spoke with clarity. Kṛñṇa is the supreme object of worship. Death fears Govinda. By knowing the lover of the gopīs, all knowledge is revealed. The universe moves because of svāhā.**

**Комментарий:**

The answers to each of the questions are given in order, as if they were separate topics. Brahmā spoke to them (tat). U indicates certainty. Ha indicates that he spoke clearly. He who knows Brahman, he who knows the meaning of the Vedas (brāhmaṇaḥ), he who realized the supreme Brahman spoke. In this case it is Brahmā, since later he is also called Hairaṇya, meaning Hiraṇyagarbha.

### 1.4

*tad u hocuḥ \| kaḥ kṛñṇaḥ \| govindaś ca ko’sāv iti \|*  
*gopī-jana-vallabhaḥ kaḥ \| kā svāheti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The Kumāras spoke. Who is Kṛñṇa? Who is Govinda? Who is the lover of the gopīs? Who is svāhā?**

**Комментарий:**

The Kumāras again asked a question in order to understand the meaning of words in the answer just given.

### 1.5

*tān uvāca brāhmaṇaḥ \| pāpa-karñaṇo*  
*go-bhūmi-veda-vidito viditā gopī-janāvidyā-kalā-prerakas*  
*tan-māyā ceti sa-kalaṁ paraṁ brahmaiva tat \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Brahmā spoke to them. Kṛñṇa means he who destroys offenses. Govinda means he who is known among the cows, on earth and in the Vedas. He is to be known as Govinda. Gopī-jana-vallabha means he who inspires the gopīs with prema. Svāhā indicates his spiritual energy. Kṛñṇa defined as above is the supreme Brahman.**

**Комментарий:**

Brahmā answers the questions in order. Kṛñṇa is he who destroys all sins, even the offenses of the demons (pāpa-karñaṇaḥ). Therefore he is most worthy of worship (parama-daivatam) (mentioned in verse 3). The word Kṛñṇa can be grammatically analyzed to mean “he who destroys (karñati) all offenses.” He who is most famous in this manner is described as Kṛñṇa in Bhāgavatam. Some scholars explain Kṛñṇa as follows:

*kṛñibhūr vācakaḥ śabdo ṇaśca nirvṛti-vācakaḥ*

**Kṛñ means existence and ṇa means bliss.**

This has been stated in the first verse with the phrase sac-cidānandarūpāya.

By taking various meanings of the affix go, the word Govinda is explained. Go can mean cow, earth or the Vedas. Vinda means “is known or attained.” Thus Kṛñṇa is he who is famous, is known or attained among the cows, on earth or in the Vedas. In this context the cows are those in Nanda’s Gokula. He is famous for being with the cows. The cows are always seen in Nanda’s Gokula. And he is famous for his pastimes with the cows. In Mathurā and Dvārakā pastimes he limits himself to fighting or killing demons. In Gokula however he awards the destination of a devotee to demons like Pūtanā that he kills. In Gokula death transforms by his independent play. Thus death fears Govinda. (govindān mṛtyur bibheti verse 3) Kṛñṇa is praised on earth and in the Vedas (Govinda). Viditā the same meaning. He is known on earth and in the Vedas because of his actions in Gokula in his cowherd boy form.

It is stated that by knowing the lover of the gopīs all knowledge is revealed. As the enjoyer he initiates (prerakaḥ) his pastimes with forms (kalā) of complete knowledge (āvidyā) — special forms of prema-bhakti—the gopīs. Knowledge is equated with bhakti as in rāja-vidyā rāja-guhyam. (BG [9.2](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/9.2)) Kalā indicates the gopīs’ forms, as in the following:

*ānanda cinmaya rasa pratibhāvitābhis*  
*tābhir ya eva nija rūpatayā kalābhiḥ \|*  
*goloka eva nivasaty akhilātma bhūto govin-*  
*dam ādi puruñaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi \|\|*

**I worship the Supreme Lord Govinda who, though regarding all the inhabitants as his very self, resides in Goloka exclusively with the young gopīs (kalābhiḥ), who are embodiments of madhura-rasa, accepts them as his wives, while they respond to his conjugal affection reciprocally. (Brahma-saṁhitā** [**5.37**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bs/37)**)**

He is known as the instigator of enjoyment (prerakaḥ) in the following:

*sa vo hi svāmī bhavati*

**He is your husband. (Uttara-Gopāla-tāpanī)**

This nature is also understood by instructions on meditation in other texts. It is also said:

*yatra vidyāvidye na vidāmo vidyāvidyābhyāṁ bhinna*

**Kṛñṇa is he who is different from vidyā and avidyā, in whom vidyā and avidyā do not exist. (Uttara-gopāla-tāpinī 2.19)**

*hlādinī sandhinī saṁvit tvayy ekā guṇa-saṁśraye \|*  
*hlāda-tāpa-karī miśrā tvayi no guṇa-varjite \|\|*

**In you who are the shelter of all spiritual qualities exists the energies of hlādinī, sandhinī and saṁvit. In you who are devoid of material qualities, the mixture of happiness and distress found in the material world does not exist. (Viñṇu Purāṇa 1.9.44-45)**

*hlādinyā saṁvid-āśliñṭaḥ sac-cid-ānanda īśvaraḥ \|*  
*svāvidyā-saṁvṛto jīvaḥ saṅkleśa-nikarākaraḥ \|\|*

**The Lord is eternity, knowledge and bliss, embraced by the saṁvit and hlādinī śaktis. The jīva covered by his ignorance accepts forms of suffering. (Śrīdhara Svāmi’s commentary)**

By the embrace of the gopīs, Kṛñṇa takes on greater excellence.

*tatrātiśuśubhe tābhir bhagavān devakī-sutaḥ*  
*madhye maṇīnāṁ haimānāṁ mahā-marakato yathā*

**Kṛñṇa, though naturally endowed with all powers, appeared most brilliant in the midst of the dancing gopīs, like a sapphire in the midst of golden gems. (SB** [**10.33.6**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/33.6)**)**

Thus even in knowing Govinda, one attains all knowledge (bhakti) by special knowledge of highest prema (as gopī-jana-vallabha). Uddhava explains this.

*etāḥ paraṁ tanu-bhṛto bhuvi gopa-vadhvo*  
*govinda eva nikhilātmani rūḍha-bhāvāḥ*  
*vāñchanti yad bhava-bhiyo munayo vayaṁ ca*  
*kiṁ brahma-janmabhir ananta-kathā-rasasya*

**Among all persons on earth, these cowherd women alone have attained perfect bodies, for they have achieved mahābhava for Govinda, the source of all forms of the Lord. Their pure love is desired by those who fear material existence, by great sages, and by ourselves as well. For one who has tasted the narrations of the infinite Lord, what is the use of taking birth as a high-class brāhmaṇa, or even as Brahmā himself? (SB** [**10.47.58**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/47.58)**)**

*nāyaṁ śriyo ’ṅga u nitānta-rateḥ prasādaḥ*  
*svar-yoñitāṁ nalina-gandha-rucāṁ kuto ’nyāḥ*  
*rāsotsave ’sya bhuja-daṇḍa-gṛhīta-kaṇṭha-*  
*labdhāśiñāṁ ya udagād vraja-vallabhīnām*

**When Kṛñṇa was dancing with the gopīs in the rāsa-līlā, the gopīs were embraced by the arms of the Lord. This favor was never bestowed upon the goddess of fortune, though she was intensely attracted to him, or to other consorts in the spiritual world whose bodily luster and aroma resemble the lotus flower. And what to speak of worldly women? (SB** [**10.47.60**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/47.60)**)**

*cintāmaṇi prakara sadmasu kalpa vṛkña*  
*lakñāvṛteñu surabhir abhipālayantam \|*  
*lakñmī sahasra śata sambhrama sevyamānaṁ*  
*govindam ādi puruñaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi \|\|*

**I worship the Supreme Lord Govinda who affectionately tends the cows in stables whose walls are made of cintāmaṇi, surrounded by desire trees. He is eagerly served by countless gopīs. (Brahma-saṁhitā** [**5.29**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bs/29)**)**

One should understand that the Maha-lakñmīs (gopīs) of Gokula are much greater than the Lakñmī of Vaikuṇṭha.

Svāhā refers to his māyā. Māyā here means Kṛñṇa’s yoga-māyā, his spiritual energy, or his mercy.

*triguṇātmikātha jñānaṁ tathā cic-chaktir eva ca \|*  
*māyā-śabdena bhaṇyante śabda-tattvārtha-vādibhiḥ*

**Māyā refers to the illusion produced by the three guṇas, to knowledge, and the cit-śakti according to knowers of the meaning of words. (Śabda-mahodadhi)**

Māyā means knowledge according Nighaṇṭu: māyā vayunaṁ jñānam. Viśva-prakāśa says māyā dambhe kṛpāyāṁ ca: māyā means deceit or mercy. His energy or mercy gives rise to movements in all things. (Thus by his mercy or by his yogamāyā, the world operates.) In this way the questions have been suitably answered.

*ko hy evānyāt kaḥ prāṇyāt yad eña ākāśa ānando na syāt*

**Who could breathe without this Brahman, if this blissful Brahman were not present? (Taittirīya Upaniñad** [**2.7.1**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/taittiriya/2.7.1)**)**

*cakñuñaś cakñuḥ*

**He is the eye of all eyes. (Bṛhad-āraṇyaka Upaniñad** [**4.4.18**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bau/4.4.18)**)**

*yasya bhāsā sarvam idaṁ vibhāti*

**By his effulgence all things shine. (Kaṭha Upaniñad** [**2.2.15**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/katha-bs/2.2.15)**)**

One should not think that there is another meaning to the words māyā and saṁsarati. By taking svāhā to indicate an offering to the Lord (The world develops by offering sacrifice to the Lord), one will produce a meaning contrary to the context. The intention is to show that the Lord is the cause of saṁsāra. According to the question, a specific answer has been given after defining a specific arrangement. (The world moves by svāhā, his mercy or his māyā.)

*sva-śabdena ca kñetra-jño heti cit-prakṛtiḥ parā \|*  
*tayor aikya-samudbhūtir mukha-veñṭana-varṇakaḥ \|*  
*ataeva hi viśvasya layaḥ svāhārṇakaḥ bhavet \|\|*

**Svā means the knower of the field (the Lord) and hā means his spiritual nature. The two combine as one word (the Lord’s spiritual energy). Thus svāhā means “the resting place of the universe.” (Gautamīya-tantra 2.16-17)**

Thus, according to Gautamīya-tantra, the Lord is one with the universe.

### 1.6

*yo dhyāyati rasati bhajati so ’mṛto bhavati so ’mṛto bhavatīti.*

**Перевод:**

**He who meditates on Kṛñṇa, develops taste and serves him without self-interest attains a form of the highest bliss.**

**Комментарий:**

The conclusion is reinforced by praising Kṛñṇa. Another version has etad yo dhyāyati. Anyone who meditates on Kṛñṇa, anyone who has extraordinary powers, anyone so qualified, attains a form of the highest bliss (amṛtaḥ). He develops great taste and serves the Lord without any conditions. Repetition of the phrase so ’mṛto bhavati indicates the end of the topic.

### 1.7

*te hocuḥ \| kiṁ tad-rūpam \| kiṁ rasanam \| kathaṁ vāho tad-bhajanam \|*  
*tat sarvaṁ vividiñatām ākhyāhīti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The sages spoke. What form is the object of meditation, what is taste and how should one serve the Lord? Please explain this to those who desire to know.**

**Комментарий:**

They ask about the form to be used for meditation, the taste and the service.

### 1.8

*tad u hovāca hairaṇyaḥ \| gopa-veśam abhrābhaṁ taruṇaṁ kalpa-drumāśritam\|*

**Перевод:**

**Brahmā spoke to them. The form of Kṛñṇa wears the dress of a cowherd. He has the complexion of a cloud. He is youthful and sits under a desire tree.**

**Комментарий:**

His form is described.

### 1.9-11

*tad iha ślokā bhavanti—*  
*sat-puṇḍarīka-nayanaṁ meghābhaṁ vaidyutāmbaram \|*  
*dvi-bhujaṁ jñāna-mudrāḍhyaṁ vana-mālinam īśvaram \|\|*  
*gopa-gopī-gavāvītaṁ sura-druma-talāśrayam \|*  
*divyālaṅkaraṇopetaṁ ratna-paṅkaja-madhya-gam \|\|*  
*kālindī-jala-kallola- saṅgi-māruta-sevitam \|*  
*cintayan cetasā kṛñṇaṁ mukto bhavati saṁsṛteḥ \|\| iti \|*

**Перевод:**

**Meditating with concentration on two-armed lotus-eyed, silent Kṛñṇa, who has the complexion of a cloud, who wears cloth flashing like lightning and a garland, who is surrounded by cowherds, gopīs and cows, who is bedecked with shining ornaments, while sitting under a desire tree on a jewelled lotus, who is served by a breeze mixed with water drops from the Yamunā, one becomes liberated from samsāra.**

**Комментарий:**

The verse is a meditation mantra (dhyāna-mantra). His cloth shines like lightning.

Jñāna-mudrāḍhyam means that he is silent. This is the practice (mudra) of a jñānī.

It is said avacanenaiva provāca: he spoke without words. (quoted in Bhagavat-sandarbha, apparently from Madhva.) He is silent because of the rasa created by his playing the flute with great feeling. Another version has mauna-mudrāḍhyam. The cowherds accompanying him are Śrīdāmā and others. The gopīs are headed by Rādhā. He is surrounded (āvīta) by the cows such as the kapila (brown ones). The word iti indicates the end of the dhyāna-mantra.

### 1.12

*tasya punā rasanam iti \| jala-bhūmīndu-sampāta-kāmādi-kṛñṇāyety*  
*ekaṁ padam \| govindāyeti dvitīyam \| gopījaneti tṛtīyam \|*  
*vallabhāyeti turīyam \| svāheti pañcamam iti \| pañca-padaṁ japan*  
*pañcāṅgaṁ dyāvābhūmī sūryācandramasau sāgnī tad-rūpatayā*  
*brahma sampadyate brahma sampadyata iti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**After meditation, taste for that form arises. The first part of the mantra is kliṁ kṛñṇāya. The second part is govindāya. The third part is gopījana. The fourth part is vallabhāya. The fifth part is svāhā. Uttering these five parts while thinking of their presiding deities— sky, earth, sun, moon and fire— one attains Brahman in the form of Kṛñṇa by this mantra.**

**Комментарий:**

The second question concerning rasa is answered in this verse. After meditation, a taste for the form (tasya) develops. This means prema. Another version has rasyaṁ purārasanam evam: what is to be tasted, his prema, becomes experienced. This taste is a realization of the sweetness of the Lord. This becomes prema. It is intense love of the Lord in the mind, filled with desire for the Lord. When the Lord becomes experienced, this intense love spontaneously becomes an excellent object called prema. It appears as the highest goal. The tasting experience becomes one with prema, the object of the experience.

Service or worship (bhajana) should then be explained. Thus the mahā-mantra is again taught in order to establish a relationship with the Lord. Jala refers to the syllable k since ka means water according to the dictionary. Bhūmi refers to the syllable l since the bīja mantra of earth is laṁ. Fire refers to ī and the moon (indu) is the anusvara (since the moon and anusvara have a similar form of a crescent.) Their combination (samāta) produces the kāma-bīja known as klīṁ. This bīja in front of (ādi) kṛñṇāya produces the first phrase (kliṁ kṛñṇāya). The five phrases of the mantra are chanted on different limbs such as the heart while performing nyāsa.2 One should meditate on the various presiding deities such as the sky, earth, sun, moon and fire, who represent the Lord, in the five parts of the mantra. One will attain Brahman (Kṛñṇa) by this form of chanting the mantra (tad-rūpatayā).

Or one will attain Brahman with these five parts, by what was revealed (rūpatayā) in the words (tat) just mentioned: the selfmanifesting sun and moon and fire, and sky and earth which give shelter to all things above and below (dyāvābhūmī).

The first two parts (klim krñṇāya govindaya), representing sky and earth, indicates the supreme shelter. The other three parts (gopījana vallabhāya svāhā), represented by sun, moon and fire, indicate what he manifests.

The first part (kliṁ kṛñṇāya) is in the highest position (sky), since this is the root name which reveals all śaktis. The second part (govindāya) is associated with earth because his powers are revealed on earth. The third part (gopī-jana) is associated with the sun since the gopīs shine brighter than all else. The fourth part (vallabhāya) is associated with the moon since the moon gives the greatest bliss by its light. The fifth part (svāhā) is associated with fire since the word is always associated with offerings during sacrifice.

Taking either meaning, one attains Brahman in human form as Kṛñṇa. This will be stated later in the examples of Brahmā and Candradvaja. Repetition of the last phrase indicates the end of the first Upaniñad (section).

### 1.13

*tad eña ślokaḥ \|*  
*klīm ity etad ādāv ādāya kṛñṇāya*  
*govindāya gopījana-vallabhāyeti \|*  
*bṛhad-bhānavyāsakṛd uccared yo’ sau*  
*gatis tasyāsti maṁkñu nānyā gatiḥ syāt \|\| iti \|*

**Перевод:**

**There is this verse: one who utters this mantra kliṁ kṛñṇāya govindāya gopījana vallabhāya svāhā will quickly attain Kṛñṇa. He will not attain any other goal.**

**Комментарий:**

The mantra is explained. He who utters this mantra with its respective parts ending with svāhā (bṛhad-bhānavyā) even once, will quickly (maṁkñu) attain the goal Kṛñṇa, as previously mentioned. He will not attain anything else. Therefore one should learn this mantra from a guru. Iti indicates the end of the explanation of the mantra.

### 1.14

*bhaktir asya bhajanam \| tad ihāmutropādhi-nairāsyenāmuñmin*  
*manaḥ-kalpanam \| etad eva ca naiñkarmyam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Bhajanam or service means devotional service to Kṛñṇa. It means concentration of the mind on Kṛñṇa without desires for enjoyment in this life or the next. It destroys all karma.**

**Комментарий:**

In order to explain about worship, the word bhajanam is explained. The word bhakti is well known to mean service to the Lord. Bhajanam thus means service to Kṛñṇa (asya bhaktiḥ). This is further explained. Service to Kṛñna means offering the mind to Kṛñṇa (manaḥ-kalpanam), while rejecting all the desires for enjoyment in this life and in Svarga. This meaning is clear in the mūla-mantra which has names of the Lord in the dative case (kṛñṇaya, govindāya, gopījana-vallabhāya) followed by svāhā (I make an offering unto Kṛñṇa). The meaning of worship is explained after showing the meaning of the mūla-mantra. The mind is like a tree and the other senses are like branches. Offering the mind therefore means offering the senses as well.

How is it possible to quickly attain Kṛñṇa when there are beginningless births and karma? Service to the Lord destroys all karma (naiñkarmyam). Since bhakti necessarily destroys karma, it is indicated to be non-different from the Lord.

Or jñāna is non-different from bhakti: this service or worship (etad) is the same as jñāna (naiñkarmyam). Jñāna means the opposite of karma (naiñkaryam). Naiñkarmyam comes from the word niñkarma and means something without karma. Thus the condition of jñāna arises from the appearance of Kṛñṇa through a particular engagement of the mind.

### 1.15

*kṛñṇaṁ taṁ viprā bahudhā yajanti*  
*govindaṁ santaṁ bahudhā’rādhayanti \|*  
*gopī-jana-vallabho bhuvanāni dadhre*  
*svāhāśrito jagad aijat suretāḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Many persons following scripture worship Kṛñṇa. Many serve him as the cowherd boy Govinda with devotion. As Gopī-jana-vallabha he shows mercy to the universe. The person who takes shelter of svāhā, who becomes filled with great power, fills the whole world with prema.**

**Комментарий:**

Having shown the meaning of bhajanam using the mūla-mantra, the Upaniñad now shows what is not stated in the mantra: the special nature of the fourth śakti (worship) in the three names of Kṛñṇa. Those who follow scripture (viprāḥ) worship Kṛñṇa, who appears (santam) in the form of a cowherd (tam). In some versions of the text, the word viprāḥ is absent. The meaning however is implied because of the worship.

Many serve Govinda, who appears (santam) as the hero of Gokula, and they have variegated rāga like that of the inhabitants of Gokula. They have taste for persons who have prema for Kṛñṇa.

When Govinda appears as Gopī-jana-vallabha, he shows mercy (dadhre) to the universe. Gaining his mercy depends on his worship through a parampara of similar worshippers.

Those who takes shelter of the word svāhā, indicating offering the self, who takes shelter of Kṛñṇa, make the whole world tremble (aijat) in ecstasy of prema since they manifests great power (suretāḥ). The intention is to show that the power arises from the names in the mantra.

### 1.16

*vāyur yathaiko bhuvanaṁ praviñṭo*  
*janye janye pañca-rūpo babhūva*  
*kṛñṇas tathaiko’pi jagad-dhitārthaṁ*  
*śabdenāsau pañca-pado vibhātīti*

**Перевод:**

**Just as air alone, entering the earth, takes on five forms in various bodies, Kṛñṇa, though one, manifests as five by his various names in order to give benefit to the universe.**

**Комментарий:**

An example is given to make clear the special nature of the appearance of the Lord in the parts of the mantra, though he is one person. Just as the one air enters the earth and becomes five different prāṇas in different bodies (janye janye)3 so Kṛñṇa, though one, by the different names, is known by five phrases in the mantra.

### 1.17

*te hocuḥ \| upāsanam etasya paramātmano*  
*govindasyākhilādhāriṇo brūhīti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The Kumāras spoke. Tell us about the worship of Govinda, who give life to all beings and is the shelter of all beings.**

**Комментарий:**

The Kumāras ask about the arrangements for worship, since these must be known from scripture. Tell us about the worship of he who gives life to all jīvas (paramātmanaḥ), the shelter of all (akhilādhāriṇaḥ). Ādhāriṇaḥ comes from the word ādhāra, which means support.

### 1.18

*tān uvāca \| yat tasya pīṭhaṁ hairaṇyāñṭa-palāśam ambujaṁ tadantarālike ’nalāsra-yugaṁ tad antarādy-arṇākhila-bījaṁ kṛñṇāya*  
*nama iti bījārḍhyaṁ sa-brahmāṇam ādhāya, anaṅga-gāyatrīṁ*  
*yathāvad vyālikhya bhū-maṇḍalaṁ śūla-veñṭitaṁ kṛtvāṅga-vāsudevarukmiṇy-ādi-sva-śaktīndrādi-vasudevādi-pārthādi-nidhy-āvītaṁ yajet \|*  
*sandhyāsu pratipattibhir upacārais tenāsyākhilaṁ bhavaty akhilaṁ bhavatīti\|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Brahmā spoke to the Kumāras about Kṛñṇa’s pīṭha (throne). One should establish the throne with a golden lotus of eight petals in whose center are two interlocking triangles. One should place the kāma-bīja, the seed of one’s actions, within that. One should write the Kṛñṇa mantra around that. One should write the syllables of kāma-gāyatrī on the stamens and, drawing a square, surround it with eight thunderbolts. One should then worship various deities in the seven āvaraṇas. One should worship the Lord with the sixteen items, with devotional meditation, at the three junctures of the day. The worshipper will attain all four puruñārthas by this worship.**

**Комментарий:**

Now Kṛñṇa’s pītha in the form of a yantra is described. Brahmā spoke to them (tān) about Kṛñṇa’s (tasya) pīṭha. The explanation is for the most part according to what is written in Sanat-kumārasaṁhitā. One should establish a washed pītḥa (throne) in one’s house and place it in a golden lotus with eight petals. Or one should draw a lotus using sandalwood paste mixed with perfume. Within the lotus’s center one should draw two interlocking triangles (analāsra-yugam). In its center one should write the kāma-bīja, the seed of one’s actions (ādyārnākhila-bījam) and the accomplisher of the result.

It is said in the Sanat-kumāra-saṁhitā:

*karṇikāyāṁ likhed vahni-puṭitaṁ maṇḍala-dvayam \|*  
*tasya madhye likhed bījaṁ sādhyākhyaṁ karma-saṁyutam \|\|*

One should draw in the center two interlocking triangles and in that draw the bīja which allows one to accomplish the result. One should write the six syllables kliṁ kṛñṇāya namaḥ on the tips of the six points. This location is described in Krama-dīpikā (7.27). One should then place the eighteen syllable mantra (sa-brahmāṇam) the kāma-bīja previously written, in the center. The mantra is called Brahmā because the mantra and its sage or devatā are non-different. Or Brahmā refers to Kṛñṇa as parabrahman. This is described in the Sanat-kumāra-saṁhita: tataḥ śiñṭair manor varṇais taṁ kāmam veñṭayet sudhīh: the intelligent person should surround the kāmabīja with the remaining syllables of the mantra.

*śriyaṁ ñaṭ-koṇa-koṇeñv aindra-nairṛta-vāyuñu \|*  
*ālikhya vilikhen māyāṁ vahni-vāruṇa-śūliñu \|\|*

**One should write the śṛīṁ bīja in the eastern, southwestern, and northwestern points of the six-pointed figure. One should write the hrīṁ bīja on the southeastern, western and northeastern points. (Sanat-kumāra-saṁhitā)**

One should write three syllables of the kāma-gāyatri on each of the eight stamens (to make the complete twenty-four syllable mantra). One should then write the māla-mantra of forty-eight syllables namaḥ sarva-jana-priyāya sarva-jana-sammohanāya jvala jvala prajvala prajvala sarva-janasya hṛdayaṁ mama vaśīkuru vaśīkuru svāhā, placing six syllables on each of the eight petals.

Making a circle around the eight petals, one should surround it with the letters of the alphabet. This is explained in the Sanatkumāra-saṁhitā also:

*akñaraiḥ kāma-gāyatryā veñṭayet keśare sudhīḥ \| kā*  
*ma-mālā-manor varṇair daleñv añṭasu mantra-vit \|\|*  
*likhed guhānanair bhaktair māntrikāṁs tad-bahir likhet \|\|*

**The intelligent person should surround this with the syllables of the kāma-gayatrī on the stamens. The knower of mantras should write the syllables of the māla-mantra on the eight petals. Outside of this he should write the syllables of the alphabet divided into six parts.**

guhānanair bhaktair means divided into six parts.

One should then draw a square bhū-maṇḍala and surround it with eight thunderbolts (bhū-maṇḍalaṁ śūla-veñṭitaṁ kṛtvā). This is explained in the saṁhitā as bhū-gṛhaṁ caturasraṁ syād añṭa-vajrayutaṁ mune: O sage, there should be a square enclosure of earth, endowed with eight thunderbolts. Since this is an amulet yantra, one should also write the goal in the beginning. The method of wearing the yantra and its result are mentioned in the Sanatkumāra-saṁhitā:

*hutvā sahasram ājyena yantre sampāta-pūrvakam \|*  
*mārjayitvāyutaṁ hutvā dhārayed yantram uttamam \|*  
*trailokyaiśvaryam āpnoti devair api supūjitaḥ \|\|*

**After offering a thousand oblations of ghee, purifying the yantra with the ghee remnants each time, and then uttering the mantra ten thousand times to purify it, one should wear the best yantra. Worshipped by the devatās, one will attain the wealth of the three worlds.**

When making the yantra for deity worship however one should do as follows:

*maṇḍūkādi-pṛthivy-antaṁ pūjayet karṇikopari \|*  
*agny-ādi-pīṭha-pādeñu dharmādīṁś caturo yajet \|\|*  
*tāra-varṇa-prabhinnāni maṇḍalāni kramāt tataḥ \|*  
*sattvaṁ rajas tama iti yajed ātma-catuñṭayam \|\|*  
*ātmāntarātmā paramātmā jñānātmeti catuḥ-kramāt \|*  
*vimalotkarñiṇī jñānā kriyā yogeti pañcamī \|\|*  
*prahvī satyā tatheśānānugrahā navamī tu tāḥ \|*  
*prāgādy añṭasu patreñu karṇikāyāṁ yajen muniḥ \|\|*

**One should worship devatās from Maṇḍūka4 to the earth in the center of the lotus. One should worship dharma, adharma, ajñāna and avairāgya and anaiśvarya in the southeastern and other directions and dharma, jṇaṇa, vairāgya and aiśvarya in the eastern and other directions. One should worship the three parts of oṁ and then worship sattva, rajas and tamas and the four ātmās: jīva, all pervading ātmā, Paramātmā beyond everything, and jñānātma (intellectual function of ātmā as witness).5 The sage should worship Vimalā, Utkarñinī, Jñaṇa, Kriyā, Yogā, Prahvī, Satyā, Iśānā, and Anugrahā on the eight petals and center (9 total).**

One should then place the words oṁ namo bhagavate viñṇave sarvabhūtātmane vāsudevāya sarvātma-saṁyoga-yaugapadya-pīṭhātmane namaḥ on the lotus as instructed in the Saṁhitā.

*tataḥ pīṭhaṁ samabhyarcya devam āvāhya nārada*  
*arghyādi dhūpadīpadīsu upacārān prakalpayet*

**Worshipping the piṭha one should call the Lord and worship him with items such as arghya, incense and lamps.**

Then one should worship the āvaraṇas or outer circles of associates. The first circle (worship of the aṅgas or bodily limbs) is described.

On four of the six points worship the limbs (aṅga): heart, head, śikhā and arms on the southeast, southwest, northwest and northeast. In front worship the eyes and in east, south, west and north directions worship the weapon with astrāya phaṭ.

The second circle is indicated with the word vāsudeva. One should worship Vāsudeva, Saṅkarñaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha, on the east, west, south and north petals respectively. In the southeast, southwest, northwest and northeast worship Śānti, Śrī, Sarasvatī and Rati.

The third circle is indicated with the word rukmiṇī. This refers to Kṛñṇa’s śaktis (sva-śakti). They are worshipped on the petals.

*rukmiṇī satyabhāmā ca jāmbavaty aparā tathā \|*  
*nāgnajitī mitravindā kālindī ca tataḥ parā \|*  
*lakñmaṇā ca suśīlā ca pūjyā etā śubha-pradā \|\|*

**The auspicious Rukmiṇī, Satyabhāmā, Jāmbavatī, Nāgnajitī, Mitravindā, Kālindī, Lakñmaṇā and Bhadrā should be worshipped.**

The fourth circle is indicated with the word vasudevādi. Vasudeva and others are in the fourth circle.

Worship Vasudeva, yellow in color in the east. Worship Devakī, blackish in the southeast. Worship Nanda, white and yellow in the south. Worship Yaśodā, gold and red in the southwest. Worship Balarāma, white like a conch, a jasmine flower or the moon, in the west.

Worship in the northwest Subhadrā, dark like a peacock feather. Worship cowherds in the north and gopīs in the northeast.

The colors of Devakī and Yaśodā are mentioned in Sanat-kumārasaṁhitā and recorded by Viśveśvara in his commentary on Gopālatāpanī. The following is written in Gautamīya-tantra.

*devakī śyāma-subhagā sarvābharaṇa-śobhanā \|*  
*yaśodā hema-saṅkāśāsita-vastra-yugāvṛtā \|\|*

**Devakī is blackish, shining with all ornaments. Yaśodā is golden colored and covered with two pieces of blue cloth.**

Mādhva bhātta, who wrote Śārada-tilaka, has rejected this, by quoting what is found in Krama-dīpika. Devakī-yaśode kīdṛśyau aruṇa-śyāmale: Yāśodā was black and Devakī was pale. Some accept the colors in reverse as sequence given in Krama-dīpika. In any case, the mood of the worshipper is the proof. Let us return to the topic.

The fifth circle is indicated by pārthādi. These are Arjuna, Niśaṭha (son of Balarāma), Uddhava, Dāruka, Viśvakñena, Sātyaki, Nārada and Parvatā.

The sixth circle has the treasures. In the east is Indranidhi, in the southeast is Nīlanidhi. In the south is Kunda. In the southwest is Makara. In the west is Anaṅga. In the northwest is Kacchapa. In the north is Śaṅkha. In the northeast is Padmanidhi.

The seventh circle is indicated by indrādi. Indra, yellow in color is on the east petal. Agni, red in color, is on the southeast petal. Yama, blue like a lotus, is in the south. Nairṛti, black in color, is in the southwest. Vāyu, grey, is in the west. Varuna, white, is in the northwest. Kuvera, blue in color, is in the north. Īśāna, white in color, is in the northeast. Between the east and north east is Brahmā, red like a palāśa flower (representing upwards). Between southwest and west is Śeña, white in color (representing downwards).

The eighth circle has Vajra, yellow, on the eastern petal. Respectively there are Śakti, white in color, Daṇḍa, blue in color, Śaṅkha, white in color, Pāśa (noose), lightning color, Dhvāja, red in color, Gadā, blue in color, and Triśūla, white in color.

One should worship the Lord with five or more items and with devotional meditation (pratipattibhiḥ), at the three junctures of the day. The worshipper will attain all four puruñārthas (akhilam) by this worship. Repetition of the last phrase indicates the end of the second chapter.

Some give a different meaning because of different version of the text. In a triangle with flat side up within the center of the lotus, write the kāma-bīja, the first syllable of the mantra, to which all others are inferior. One should place there the six syllables of the mantra and then place there the gāyatrī with oṁ (sabramanam) praised in the Vedas. Place around this the kāma-gāyatrī with the kāma-bīja. In the surrounding square of bhū-maṇdala, place spears in the eight directions. Then worship the circles with Vāsudeva etc. and his aṁśas (aṅgam) Rukmiṇī and others, his śaktis, Indra and other devatās, Vasudeva and others, Arjuna and others, and the treasures. At the three junctures of the day one should worship with meditation and rare items or easily obtained items (atipattibhiḥ) such as flowers.

### 1.19

*tad iha ślokā bhavanti—*  
*eko vaśī sarvagaḥ kṛñṇa īḍya*  
*eko’pi san bahudhā yo vibhāti \| taṁ*  
*pīṭhagaṁ ye’nuyajanti dhīrās teñāṁ*  
*sukhaṁ śāśvataṁ netareñām \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**There is a verse concerning this: Kṛñṇa is supreme, all-pervading, and most worthy of praise. Though he is one, he is also many. The wise who worship him in the pīṭha obtain constant happiness not available to others.**

**Комментарий:**

Approval of this mantra in the above worship is described. Kṛñṇa is called ekaḥ (one) because there is no one equal or superior to him, since he is Svayam Bhagavān. Svayaṁ tv asāmyātiśayas tryadhīśaḥ: no one is equal to him or superior to him as master of the three energies and three Viñṇus. (SB [3.2.21](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-3/2.21)) He is the controller of all (vaśī) since he pervades everywhere (sarva-gaḥ). He who is famous in verses like kṛñṇas tu bhagavān svayam is Kṛñṇa. Therefore he is to be praised by all (īḍyaḥ). “Kṛñṇa comes as many avatāras. How can he be said to be one?” By his acintya-śakti, though he is one, he appears as many.

*citraṁ bataitad ekena vapuñā yugapat pṛthak \|*  
*gṛheñu dvy-añṭa-sāhasraṁ striya eka udāvahat \|\|*

**It is quite amazing that in a single body Lord Kṛñṇa simultaneously married sixteen thousand women, each in a separate palace. (SB** [**10.69.2**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/69.2)**)**

Persons like Śukadeva possessing discrimination worship Kṛñṇa in this pīṭha. They have constant happiness not available to others— such as the worshippers of Mahā-narāyaṇa. It is said in Bhāgavatam:

*yan martya-līlaupāyikaṁ sva-yoga*  
*māyā-balaṁ darśayitā gṛhītam \| vis*  
*māpanaṁ svasya ca saubhaga-rddheḥ*  
*paraṁ padaṁ bhūñaṇaṁ bhūñaṇāṅgam \|\|*

**Kṛñṇa possesses that form suitable for human pastimes to show the full capacity of his yoga-māyā. That form astonishes even the lord of Vaikuṇṭha. It is the pinnacle of auspicious qualities and enhances the beauty of his ornaments. (SB** [**3.2.12**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-3/2.12)**)**

### 1.20

*nityo nityānāṁ cetanaś cetanānām*  
*eko bahūnāṁ yo vidadhāti kāmān \|*  
*taṁ pīṭhagaṁ ye’nubhajanti dhīrās*  
*teñāṁ siddhiḥ śāśvatī netareñām \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The wise, who worship Kṛñṇa in his pīṭha— Kṛñna who is the principal being among many, who is the chief eternal among eternals and the chief knower among knowers, who satisfies their prema— attain eternal bliss not available to others.**

**Комментарий:**

This verse also shows approval of Kṛñṇa worship. Because of his eternal nature, others have eternal nature. He gives consciousness to beings that have consciousness.

*sarveñām eva vastūnāṁ bhāvārtho bhavati sthitaḥ \|*  
*tasyāpi bhagavān kṛñṇaḥ kim atad-vastu rūpyatām \|\|*

**Real existence of all this is situated in the final cause. That cause is the Lord endowed with all powers in the form of Kṛñṇa. Can anything be found without Kṛñṇa? (SB** [**10.14.57**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/14.57)**)**

“But there are unlimited worshippers. Would they not be limited, if there was only one form for their worship?” He is one, but expands as many to respond to their desires filled with prema (kāmān). The wise who worship him have eternal perfection, unlike other worshippers. Viśveśvara explains: Those who worship Kṛñṇa with concentration have eternal bliss (siddhiḥ). Those without devotion to Kṛñṇa do not have this bliss. It is like a person without eyes trying to see a form.

### 1.21

*etad dhi viñṇoḥ paramaṁ padaṁ ye*  
*nityayuktāḥ saṁyajante na kāmān \|*  
*teñām asau gopa-rūpaḥ prayatnāt*  
*prakāśayed ātma-padaṁ tadaiva \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**For those who constantly worship Kṛñṇa in the pīṭha, and who have no other desires, Kṛñṇa in his cowherd form reveals his own abode.**

**Комментарий:**

Those who worship the form in the pīṭha previously described do not have material desires. Another version has na kāmāt. This means they do not worship because of material desires. Ātma-padam means his eternal abode.

### 1.22

*yo brahmāṇaṁ vidadhāti pūrvaṁ yo*  
*vidyās tasmai gopāyati sma kṛñṇaḥ \|*  
*taṁ ha devam ātma-buddhi-prakāśaṁ*  
*mumukñur vai śaraṇam anuvrajeta \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Kṛñṇa created Brahmā and preserved knowledge of the eighteen syllable mantra for him. Those who desire to give up everything surrender to Kṛñṇa the revealer of all knowledge, who is endowed with all pastimes.**

**Комментарий:**

Having shown that the Lord’s mercy is necessary in order to attain perfection, the text now shows that the Lord remains eternally in this form. He preserved the knowledge contained in the eighteen syllables. He reveals all knowledge (buddhi) in himself: he is the source of knowledge.

### 1.23

*oṁkāreṇāntaritaṁ ye japanti*  
*govindasya pañca-padaṁ manum \|*  
*teñām asau darśayed ātma-rūpaṁ*  
*tasmān mumukñur abhyasen nitya-śāntyai \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**To those who utter the five part mantra of Govinda enclosed at both ends by oṁ, the Lord shows his form. Therefore those who desire to give up all other desires repeatedly chant this mantra for eternal bliss.**

**Комментарий:**

The direct result from chanting the mantra enclosed by oṁ is described. Oṁ should be uttered before and after the mantra (oṁkāreṇāntaritam). To those who chant in this way the Lord shows his form. Another version has tasyaiva of teñām. This means that among all of persons, the Lord shows his form to a person like Brahmā. Those who desire to give up all other things (mumukñuḥ) repeat this mantra for constant bliss without misfortune (nityaśāntyai).

### 1.24

*etasmād anye pañca-padād abhūvan*  
*govindasya manavo mānavānām \|*  
*daśārṇādyās te’pi saṅkrandanādyair*  
*abhyasyante bhūti-kāmair yathāvat \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Other mantras of Govinda appeared from this five part mantra for men with various desires. The ten syllable mantra and other mantras were repeated by Indra and others to fulfill their respective desires.**

**Комментарий:**

This mantra is the seed of other similar mantras. Other Govinda mantras appeared to Sanaka and others for jīvas having various desires (mānavānām) in order that they could attain their various desires (bhūti-kāmaiḥ). These mantras were practiced by Indra (saṅkrandana) for wealth, Sanaka for liberation and Nārada for bhakti. This is understood from the tantra.

### 1.25

*yad etasya svarūpārthaṁ vācā vedayanti te papracchuḥ \|*

**Перевод:**

**The Kumāras asked how these mantras can reveal such results arising from realization of Kṛñṇa’s form, simply by words.**

**Комментарий:**

The reason for the mantras fulfilling all desires is explained. The Kumāras asked why the mantras (te) reveal (vedayanti) things derived from Kṛñṇa’s svarūpa, things which satisfy all desires, by words? They asked about the real nature of the five part mantra.

### 1.26

*tad u hovāca \| brahma-savanaṁ carato me dhyātaḥ stutaḥ paramaḥ*  
*parārdhānte so’budhyata \| gopa-veśo me puruñaḥ purastād āvirbabhūva \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Brahmā then explained. When I passed the first half of my day in meditating on and praising the Lord, after that, the Supreme Lord awoke to my presence. He appeared before me wearing the dress of a cowherd.**

**Комментарий:**

Brahmā then verily (ha) explained the nature of the five part mantra (tat). What did he say? When I passed the first part of my day (brahma-savanam), meditating on the Lord and praising him, the supreme Lord became aware of me at the end of the first half of the day. Wearing the dress of a cowherd he then appeared before me. Some have another version. Brahmā spoke clearly (ha) about what (yat) the sages (te) asked. The sages asked how the mantra manifested the śakti of various objects (artha) which were situated in the svarūpa of the ten syllable mantra (etasya). What did he say? The rest is the same.

### 1.27

*tataḥ praṇato mayā’nukūlena hṛdā mahyam añṭādaśārṇaṁ svarūpaṁ*  
*sṛñṭaye dattvāntarhitaḥ \| punaḥ sisṛkñato me prādurabhūt \|*  
*teñv akñareñu bhaviñyajjagad-rūpaṁ prakāśayan \|*  
*tad iha ka-kārād āpo la-kārāt pṛthivī īto’gnir bindor indus*  
*tat-sampātāt tad-arka iti klīṁ-kārād asṛjam \| kṛñṇāya*  
*padād ākāśaṁ khād vāyur ity uttarāt surabhiṁ*  
*vidyāṁ prādurakārñam \| tad-uttarāt strī-puṁsādi*  
*cedaṁ sakalam idaṁ sakalam idam iti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offered sincere respects. He gave me the eighteen syllable mantra for creating the universe and then disappeared. When I desired to create, he again appeared and revealed the future universe within the syllables of the mantra. From k arose water. From l arose earth. From ī arose fire. From m arose the moon and from the combination klīm arose the sun. From kṛñṇāya I created ether and from govindāya I created air. From gopījana-vallabhāya I created the fourteen types of knowledge, a desire cow. From svāhā I created men, women and moving and non-moving beings.**

**Комментарий:**

I offered him respects with a thankful heart. He gave me the ten syllable mantra generated from his svarūpa so that I could create the universe. This shows that the mantra and the Lord have the power to generate all things. According to the application, results appear. The Lord again appeared and revealed the future universe within the mantra.

When the universe was revealed in the mantra (tad iha) I created the five elements: water from k, earth from l, fire from ī, the moon from m and the sun from the combination klīm. I created ether from kṛñṇāya and air from govindāya which is like ether (khāt), since govinda means “to know (vinda) sound (go),” which is the essence of ether. From gopījana-vallabhāya I created the kāmadhenu, the fourteen types of knowledge. From svāhā I created all moving and non-moving beings, male, female and neuter. Repetition of the last phrase indicates the end of the third chapter.

Others explain the meaning according to a different version of the text. He again appeared, desiring that I create. He personally made the revelation that the universe existed subtly in the syllables of the mantra. Therefore (tat), in this universe (iha), I created water by uttering the sound k. Uttering l, I created earth. From ī, I created fire and from m, I created the moon. From the combination of ī and m, I created the sun. From kṛñṇāya I created ether. From ether I created air. From govindāya I created cows and fourteen types of knowledge. From gopījana-vallabhāya I created men, women and everything else.

Though the Lord carries out creation, Brahmā speaks of his own creation, since he creates secondarily. He causes slight transformation to the elements rather than creating them. It is said he created water from k. This simply means that Brahmā made the śakti in water become manifest.

### 1.28

*etasyaiva yajanena candra-dhvajo gata-moham ātmānaṁ vedety*  
*oṁkārāntarālikaṁ manum āvartayet saṅga-rahito’bhyānayat \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**By worship using the mantra, Candradhvaja, free of illusion,realized ātmā. Chanting the mantra enclosed with oṁ using concentration, he saw the Lord.**

**Комментарий:**

Gaining results according to one’s particular use was mentioned in the previous explanation (Brahmā created the universe).Other special results were not mentioned. By worship of Śrī-gopāla mentioned in the eighteen syllable mantra, the king named Candradhvaja got free of illusion and understood ātmā. In order to attain this result, one must chant the eighteen syllable mantra enclosed by oṁ without other thoughts. By repeated chanting, one will attain direct vision of Śrī-gopala. Viśveśvara explains that the name Candradhvaja means Śiva.

According to another version the explanation is as follows. Becoming aware of the ātmā, with that realization, Candradhvaja chanted the mantra enclosed by oṁ. Becoming free of other thoughts, he obtained direct vision of Kṛñṇa.

### 1.29

*tad viñṇoḥ paramaṁ padaṁ sadā paśyanti sūrayaḥ \| divīva cakñur ātatam \|*  
*tasmād enaṁ nityam abhyasen nityam abhyased iti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The devotees always see the supreme abode of Kṛñṇa, which spreads in space like the sun. Therefore one should constantly chant the mantra.**

**Комментарий:**

The place called Gokula where one meets the Lord is described in this verse. This place is spread in the sky (divi) like the sun (cakñuḥ). It is said namaḥ savitre jagad-eka-cakñuñe: I offer respects to the sun, the sole eye of the universe. (found in sandhyā-vandanam prayers)

### 1.30

*tad āhur eke yasya prathama-padād bhūmir dvitīya-padāj jalaṁ tṛtīya-padāt*  
*tejaś caturtha-padād vāyuś carama-padād vyoma iti*  
*vaiñṇava-pañca-vyāhṛti-mayaṁ mantraṁ kṛñṇāvabhāsakaṁ*  
*kaivalya-sṛtyai satatam āvartayet satatam āvartayed iti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Some give this explanation of the mantra. From the first part of the mantra, earth arises. From the second part, water arises. From the third part, fire arises. From the fourth part, air arises and from the last part, ether arises. One should continually chant this Vaiñṇava mantra of five parts which brings about the appearance of Kṛñṇa as the method to perfect bhakti.**

**Комментарий:**

The knowers of the mantra give this explanation to the eighteen syllable mantra (eke). The meaning is clear. The conclusion is that Vaiñṇava mantra with five parts previously described causes the appearance of Kṛñṇa (kṛñṇāvabhāsam). One should practice mantra as a method of worshipping the Lord (kaivalya-sṛtyai) to attain the Lord. Kaivalya-sammata-pathas tv atha bhakti-yogaḥ: the path of kaivalya is bhakti. (SB [2.3.12](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-2/3.12))

### 1.31-32

*tad atra gāthāḥ \|*  
*yasya pūrva-padād bhūmir dvitīyāt salilodbhavaḥ \|*  
*tṛtīyāt teja udbhūtaṁ caturthād gandha-vāhanaḥ \|*  
*pañcamād ambarotpattis tam evaikaṁ samabhyaset \|*  
*candradhvajo’gamad viñṇoḥ paramaṁ padam avyayam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**From the first part of the mantra came earth. From the second part came water. From the third part arose fire. From the fifth part arose ether. One should repeat this mantra alone. Candradhvaja attained the supreme abode of Kṛñṇa by this means.**

**Комментарий:**

There are excellent verses (gāthāḥ) concerning this mantra (atra).

### 1.33

*tato viśuddhaṁ vimalaṁ viśokam*  
*aśeña-lobhādi-nirasta-saṅgam \| yat*  
*tat-padaṁ pañca-padaṁ tad eva sa*  
*vāsudevo na yato’nyad asti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The abode is pure sattva, without contamination, without lamentation, without unlimited greed. The abode is the mantra of five parts and is Vāsudeva, since nothing exists separately from him.**

**Комментарий:**

The abode is viśuddha-sattva and thus devoid of rajas and tamas (vimalam). Thus it is without lamentation (viśokam). Goloka is without unlimited greed and other discrepancies. It is made of the mantra and is non-different from Vāsudeva. The three—the abode, the mantra and the Lord—are one. One person appears in three forms. The power of Vāsudeva is described: nothing is extremely different from the Lord. This means everything is included within the Lord.

### 1.34

*tam ekaṁ govindaṁ sac-cid-ānanda-vigrahaṁ*  
*pañca-padaṁ vṛndāvana-sura-bhūruha-talāsīnaṁ*  
*satataṁ samarud-gaṇo’haṁ paramayā stutyā toñayāmi \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Along with the Maruts, I eternally satisfy Govinda, a form of eternity, knowledge and bliss, nondifferent from his mantra, seated under a desire tree in Vṛndāvana, by offering praise.**

**Комментарий:**

At the end, meditation on the Lord is taught. Along with the Maruts I continually satisfy Govinda who is non-different from the five part mantra (pañca-padam). It should be understood that he meditates on praising the Lord along with the Maruts in Goloka, since the material devatās cannot enter Goloka. Or in the spiritual abode, Brahmā praises the Lord since he has a spiritual form there, nondifferent from the Lord.

It is said in the Padma Purāṇa, following after puruña-sūkta (227.76):

*atra pūrve ye ca sādhyā viśvadevāḥ sanātanāḥ \|*  
*te ha nākaṁ mahimānaḥ sacantaḥ śubhadarśanāḥ \|\|*

**In Vaikuṇṭha, the eternal Sādhyās or Viśvadevas, pleasant to behold, great souls, serve the Lord.**

### 1.35

*oṁ namo viśva-rūpāya viśva-sthity-anta-hetave \|*  
*viśveśvarāya viśvāya govindāya namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Govinda the form of the universe, the cause of maintenance and destruction of the universe, the Lord of the universe, nondifferent from the universe.**

**Комментарий:**

This verse of praise indicates Kṛñṇa’s sweet aspect as well as his powers. It thus satisfies all types of worshippers. He is called viśvarūpa because he bewildered Brahmā and showed that form to him.

### 1.36

*namo vijñāna-rūpāya paramānanda-rūpiṇe \|*  
*kṛñṇāya gopīnāthāya govindāya namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Govinda, Kṛñṇa, master of the gopīs, the form of realization, the form of supreme bliss.**

**Комментарий:**

The meaning is clear.

### 1.37

*namaḥ kamalanetrāya namaḥ kamalamāline \|*  
*namaḥ kamalanābhāya kamalāpataye namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Kṛñṇa with lotus eyes, with lotus garland, with lotus navel, who is the master of the gopīs.**

**Комментарий:**

He is lord of the gopīs (kamalā-pataye). Kamalā means Lakñmī, but it is said śriyaḥ kāntāḥ kāntaḥ parama-puruñaḥ: the supreme Lord resides with many Lakñmīs in Goloka.(Brahma-saṁhitā). Gautamīya-tantra says gopīnāṁ patir eva saḥ: he is the master of the gopīs. This statement in Gautamīya-tantra is an explanation of this verse of the Upaniñad.

### 1.38

*barhāpīḍābhirāmāya rāmāyākuṇṭha-medhase \|*  
*ramā-mānasa-haṁsāya govindāya namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Govinda, with peacock feather ornament, most attractive in form, with unlimited intelligence, who is like a swan in the mind of the gopīs.**

**Комментарий:**

Rāmāya means “unto Kṛñṇa whose portion is Rāma.” Or it refers to Kṛñṇa with an attractive form.

### 1.39

*kaṁsa-vaṁśa-vināśāya keśi-cāṇūra-ghātine \|*  
*vṛñabha-dhvaja-vandyāya pārtha-sārathaye namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the destroyer of Kaṁsa’s dynasty, to the killer of Keśī and Cāṇūra, to the driver of Arjuna, to Govinda, worshipped by Śiva.**

**Комментарий:**

While fighting with Bāṇa, he bewildered Śiva, who has the bull on his flag. He acted as the driver of Arjuna during the Kurukñetra war.

### 1.40-41

*veṇu-vādana-śīlāya gopālāyāhi-mardine \|*  
*kālindī-kūla-lolāya lola-kuṇḍala-dhāriṇe \|\|*  
*vallavī-vadanāmbhoja-māline nṛtya-śāline \|*  
*namaḥ praṇata-pālāya śrī-kṛñṇāya namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Kṛñṇa, who plays a flute, who herds cows, who defeats Kāliya, who is eager for the banks of the Yamunā, who possesses swinging earrings, who wears a lotus garland of the eyes of the gopīs, who dances continually, and who protects those who are surrendered.**

**Комментарий:**

Another version has lola-kuṇḍala-valgave. The lotus eyes of the gopīs staring at him are like his garland

### 1.42

*namaḥ pāpa-praṇāśāya govardhana-dharāya ca \|*  
*pūtanā-jīvitāntāya tṛṇāvārtāsu-hāriṇe \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the destroyer of sin, the lifter of Govardhana, the killer of Pūtanā and Tṛnāvarta.**

**Комментарий:**

The meaning is clear.

### 1.43

*niñkalāya vimohāya śuddhāyāśuddha-vairiṇe \|*  
*advitīyāya mahate śrī-kṛñṇāya namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Kṛñṇa, who is beyond māyā, who is devoid of illusion, who is pure, who is the killer of the demons, who is without equal, and who is therefore great.**

**Комментарий:**

He is without māyā (niñkalāya) and without illusion (vimohāya). He is the destroyer of the demons, the impure (aśuddha-vairine). He has no equal (advitīyāya). Therefore he is great.

### 1.44

*prasīda paramānanda prasīda parameśvara \|*  
*ādhi-vyādhi-bhujaṅgena dañṭaṁ mām uddhara prabho \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**O supreme bliss, be pleased with me. O supreme Lord! Be pleased with me. O master! Deliver me, since I have been bitten by the snake of internal and external pain.**

**Комментарий:**

I am afflicted with internal pain because of not attaining you (ādhi) as well as external pain (vyādhi). Deliver me (uddhara). This means “Deliver me and bring me near your feet.”

### 1.45

*śrī-kṛñṇa rukmiṇī-kānta gopī-jana-manohara \|*  
*saṁsāra-sāgare magnaṁ mām uddhara jagad-guro \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**O Kṛñṇa, dear to Rukmiṇī. O Kṛñṇa, attractive to the gopīs! O guru of the universe. Deliver me, who have fallen in the ocean of saṁsāra.**

**Комментарий:**

The meaning is clear.

### 1.46

*keśava kleśa-haraṇa nārāyaṇa janārdana \| govinda*  
*paramānanda māṁ samuddhara mādhava \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**O Keśava, destroyer of suffering! O Nārāyanā! O Janārdana! O Govinda, full of bliss! O Mādhava! Please deliver me.**

**Комментарий:**

The meaning is clear.

### 1.47

*athaivaṁ stutibhir ārādhayāmi yathā*  
*yūyaṁ tathā pañca-pādaṁ japantaḥ*  
*śrī-kṛñṇaṁ dhyāyantaḥ saṁsṛtiṁ*  
*tariñyatheti hovāca hairaṇyaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Brahmā thus said: Just as I have worshipped with prayers, you should chant this mantra of five parts, meditate on Kṛñṇa, and you will cross saṁsāra.**

**Комментарий:**

Brahmā spoke as follows (athaivam). The words in the middle are his statement.

### 1.48

*amuṁ pañca-padaṁ mantram āvartayed yaḥ*  
*sa yāty anāyāsataḥ kevalaṁ padaṁ tat \|*  
*anejad ekaṁ manaso javīyo*  
*naitad devā āpnuvan pūrva marśad iti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The person who repeats this mantra of five parts easily attains the pure abode of the Lord. The Lord does not move. He is beyond the powers of the mind. The devatās cannot attain him. He is able to pervade everything in time and space.**

**Комментарий:**

The abode of Goloka is pure (kevalam). The Lord is shown to be Brahman with the last two lines quoted from śruti. He is without movement (anejat). He is quicker than the mind: he is beyond the powers of the mind. The devatās like Brahmā cannot attain him. He pervades (marśat) everything in front of him (pūrva) in time and space. It is said:

*iti sañcintya bhagavān mahā-kāruṇiko hariḥ*  
*darśayām āsa lokaṁ svaṁ gopānāṁ tamasaḥ param*

**Thus, deeply considering the situation, the all-merciful Supreme Lord revealed to the cowherd men his abode, which is beyond prakṛti.**

*satyaṁ jñānam anantaṁ yad brahma-jyotiḥ sanātanam*  
*yad dhi paśyanti munayo guṇāpāye samāhitāḥ*

**Kṛñṇa revealed the place which was existence, knowledge, infinity, eternity, and self-effulgent. Sages see that place in trance, when their consciousness is free of the modes of material nature. (SB** [**10.28.14-15**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/28.14)**)**

In Hari-vaṁśa, Indra speaks to Kṛñṇa:

*tasyopari gavāṁ lokaḥ sādhyās taṁ pālayanti hi \|*  
*uparyupari tatrāpi gatis tava tapomayī \|*  
*na vidmo vayaṁ sarve pṛcchanto’pi pitāmaham \|\|*

**Above this is the planet of cows, protected by the perfected devotees. O Kṛñṇa, it is all pervading, great, attained after realization of the Lord. Above that is your abode full of great powers (Gokula), which I cannot understand, though I have asked Brahmā. (Hari-vaṁśa 2.19.30-31)**

### 1.49

*tasmāt kṛñṇa eva paro devas taṁ dhyāyet taṁ rasayet taṁ*  
*yajet taṁ bhajed iti oṁ tat sad iti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Therefore Kṛñṇa is the Supreme Being. One should meditate on him, experience his sweetness, worship him and serve him.**

**Комментарий:**

Because of the above statements concerning Kṛñṇa’s supremacy, Kṛñṇa is concluded to be the Supreme Being.

## Part Two (Uttara)

### 2.1

*ekadā hi vraja-striyaḥ sakāmāḥ śarvarīm uñitvā*  
*sarveśvaraṁ gopālaṁ kṛñṇam ūcire \| uvāca tāḥ kṛñṇaḥ \|*

**Перевод:**

**Once the women of Vraja, desiring his constant association, inquired from Gopāla, the Supreme Lord, after spending the night with him. Kṛñṇa replied to them.**

**Комментарий:**

In the first part of the Upaniñad the conclusion was tasmāt kṛñṇa eva paro deva: therefore Kṛñṇa is the Supreme Lord. This is a statement of the major theme of the work (mahā-vākya). With that statement, the second part of the Upaniñad explains in a different way about Kṛñṇa who was previously described.

The gopīs had continuous desire for association with Kṛñṇa (sakāmāḥ). They had spent the whole night near Kṛñṇa in pastimes. The women of Vraja spoke to Kṛñṇa. He spoke to them. The text of this section is their conversation.

### 2.2

*anu kasmai brāhmaṇāya bhakñyaṁ dātavyaṁ bhavati \|*

**Перевод:**

**To which brāhmaṇa should we give food afterwards?**

**Комментарий:**

The women of Vraja speak. To whom should we give food afterwards (anu), so that by giving to him, we will not be separated from you?

### 2.3

*durvāsaseti \|*

**Перевод:**

**Give food to Durvāsā.**

**Комментарий:**

Kṛñṇa speaks. Durvāsasā should actually be durvāsase. Give to Durvāsā. The meaning is this. I instruct that you give food to Durvāsā. But that is not all. He will reveal to you the method of obtaining your desires, since he has understood the meaning of this Upaniñad through the sampradāya of Brahmā and Nārāyaṇa. At the time of my appearance in this world, he will live near Vraja and obtain a particle of affection similar to that of the people of Vraja. Since he is indifferent to all else, his affection will be recognized by the gopīs. Kṛñṇa’s intention by speaking in this way was that the gopīs should receive his teachings.

### 2.4

*kathaṁ yāsyāmo’ tīrtvā jalaṁ yamunāyā yataḥ śreyo bhavati \|*

**Перевод:**

**How can we cross the water of the Yamunā so that we can attain benefit from Durvāsā?**

**Комментарий:**

The women speak. How can we cross the water of the Yamunā since it is very deep? They were on the southern bank and Durvāsā was on the northern bank.

### 2.5

*kṛñṇeti brahmacārīty uktvā mārgaṁ vo dāsyati \|*

**Перевод:**

**After saying the words kṛñṇa and brahmacārī, the Yamunā will give you a path.**

**Комментарий:**

Kṛñṇa again speaks. After saying the words kṛñṇa and brahmacārī the Yamunā will give you a path. The form kṛñṇeti instead of kṛñṇaḥ iti is Vedic usage. The word Yamunā, though not mentioned, should be supplied. Or after saying to the Yamunā,”The brahmacārī is named Kṛñṇa” go across the Yamunā. The Yamunā will give you a path.

### 2.6

*yaṁ māṁ smṛtvā’gādhā gādhā bhavati \| yaṁ māṁ smṛtvā’pūtaḥ pūto bhavati \|*  
*yaṁ māṁ smṛtvā’vratī vratī bhavati \| yaṁ māṁ smṛtvā sakāmo niñkāmo*  
*bhavati \| yaṁ māṁ smṛtvā’śrotriyaḥ śrotriyo bhavati \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Remembering me, a deep river becomes shallow. Remembering me, the impure becomes pure. Remembering me, the person without vratas obtains the results of all vratas. Remembering me, a person with desire loses all desires. Remembering me, a person with no ears develops ears.**

**Комментарий:**

How is this? The answer is given. Remembering me, the deep water whose bottom cannot be touched by a man in the water becomes shallow.

But that is nothing. Remembering me, the impure demons become pure and attain liberation. Those who perform no vratas gain the results of all vratas. A person with all material desires becomes desireless since, by desiring only me, all a person’s other desires disappear. Another version has “A person without desire develops desires” This means that the ātmārāma develops desire to see me. A person without ears develops ears. This is representative of all the senses. A person devoid of all senses develops all senses: ātmārāmas and trees and other plants with undeveloped senses develop senses. By the power of the flute or hearing his pastimes, the tree and the ātmārāma develop senses.

*pariniñṭhito ’pi nairguṇya uttama śloka līlayā \|*  
*gṛhīta cetā rājarñe ākhyānaṁ yad adhītavān \|\|*

**Though I was fixed in Brahman and beyond the guṇas, my mind became attracted to the pastimes of the Lord. I thus learned about his pastimes. (SB** [**2.1.9**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-2/1.9)**)**

*gā gopakair anuvanaṁ nayator udāra*  
*veṇu-svanaiḥ kala-padais tanu-bhṛtsu sakhyaḥ \|*  
*aspandanaṁ gati-matāṁ pulakais tarūṇāṁ*  
*niryoga-pāśa-kṛta-lakñaṇayor vicitram \|\|*

**My dear friends! When Kṛñṇa and Balarāma pass from forest to the forest with their cowherd friends, with their cows, carrying ropes to bind the cows’ rear legs at the time of milking, by the generous sound of his flute, among all living beings, those which move things become inert and trees which cannot move sprout shoots in ecstasy. This is most amazing. (SB** [**10.21.19**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/21.19)**)**

### 2.7

*śrutvā tad-vācaṁ hi vai raudraṁ smṛtvā tad-vākyena tīrtvā tat-sauryāṁ hi*  
*gatvāśramaṁ puṇyatamaṁ hi natvā muniṁ śreñṭhatamaṁ hi vai raudraṁ ceti\|*

**Перевод:**

**Remembering the words, and remembering Durvāsā, they crossed the Yamunā using those words. They went to his pure hermitage and offered respects to the best of sages.**

**Комментарий:**

This verse explains that the women did as they were told.

The word hi indicates certainty and vai indicates remembering the event. Remembering his words, becoming bold by words which would produce great ability, remembering Durvāsā, an expansion of Rudra (raudram), since he was the goal of their going there, by the utterance of those words “Kṛñṇa is a brahmacārī,” they crossed the Yamunā (sauryām) which, though deep, became shallow. Hi indicates that it is well known. Going to his pure hermitage, they bowed to the best of sages. The reversal noun and verb (natvā munim) is seen in later verses also. Iti indicates they performed other types of worship of him also.

### 2.8

*dattvāsmai brāhmaṇāya kñīra-mayaṁ*  
*ghṛta-mayam iñṭatamaṁ ha vai miñṭatamam \|*  
*tuñṭaḥ sa tv ābhuktvā hitvāśiñaṁ prayujyānvājñāṁ tv adāt \|*

**Перевод:**

**They gave the brāhmaṇa sweet rice cooked with milk and ghee. Satisfied, he ate it completely. Rejecting some small remnants and giving them blessings, he told them to return home.**

**Комментарий:**

They gave him the rice cooked with milk and remained silent. He completely consumed it (ābhuktvā). Rejecting the remnants stuck to the plate (hitvā), he gave blessings to them and told them to go home (anu āvajñām). The meaning is that out of great respect he said nothing. Abhuktvā instead of ābhuktya a Vedic usage.

### 2.9

*kathaṁ yāsyāmo’tīrtvā sauryām \|*

**Перевод:**

**How will we go home without crossing the river?**

**Комментарий:**

They then spoke. How will we go without crossing the Yamunā?

### 2.10

*sa hovāca muniḥ \| dūrvāśinaṁ māṁ smṛtvā vo dāsyatīti mārgam \|*

**Перевод:**

**The sage spoke clearly. Remembering me, who had not eaten, the river will give you a path.**

**Комментарий:**

Durvāsā spoke clearly (ha). Remembering me, the river will give you a path. Dūrvāśinam “he whose food is far away” or “he who eats dūrvā grass.” This means “he who has no food.” The order is reversed (dāsyati mārgam).

### 2.11

*tāsāṁ madhye ha śreñṭhā gāndharvī hy uvāca—taṁ*  
*ha vai tābhir evaṁ vicārya \|*

**Перевод:**

**Among them, the best woman named Gāndharvī spoke to the sage after consulting with her friends.**

**Комментарий:**

Among them the best woman named Gāndharvī spoke clearly (ha) to the sage. Sometimes hi is seen instead of ha. She did this after consulting with her friends (tābhiḥ).

### 2.12

*kathaṁ kṛñṇo brahmacārī \| kathaṁ dūrvāśano muniḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**How can it be said that Kṛñṇa is a brahmacārī? How can it be said that the sage has no food?**

**Комментарий:**

What did she say after considering with friends? Why is Kṛñṇa a brahmacārī? Why has the sage not eaten? He had said, “Remember me, who had not eaten.” Some take the word in verse 10 as durvāsinam māṁ smṛtvā: remember me a person with poor cloth, an ātmārāma. But the above verse they take as durvāśinah muniḥ and they explain it properly, as a sage with no food.

Not eating properly is against the sage’s position since he takes pleasure in eating, and being a brahmacārī is contrary to Kṛñṇa’s position, since he has relationship with the gopīs. (Thus the questions are asked.)

### 2.13

*tāṁ hi mukhyāṁ vidhāya pūrvam anu kṛtvā tūñṇīm āsuḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Taking her as the chief person and putting her in front, they remained silent.**

**Комментарий:**

What did the others do? That is explained in this verse.

### 2.14

*śabdavān ākāśaḥ \| śabdākāśābhyāṁ bhinnaḥ \| ākāśe tiñṭhati \|*  
*sa hy ākāśas taṁ na veda \| sa hy ātmā’haṁ kathaṁ bhoktā bhavāmi \|\|*  
*sparśavān vāyuḥ \| sparśa-vāyubhyāṁ bhinnaḥ \| tasmin vāyau tiñṭhati \|*  
*vāyur na veda taṁ hi hi sa hy ātmāhaṁ kathaṁ bhoktā bhavāmi \|*  
*rūpavad idaṁ hi tejaḥ \| rūpāgnibh yāṁ bhinnaḥ \| tasminn agnis tiñṭhati \|*  
*agnir na veda taṁ hi sa hy ātmāhaṁ kathaṁ bhoktā bhavāmi \|*  
*rasavatya āpaḥ \| rasābbhyo bhinnaḥ \| apsu tiñthāti \| āpas taṁ na viduḥ*  
*sa hy ātmāhaṁ kathaṁ bhoktā bhavāmi \|*  
*gandhavatīyaṁ bhūmiḥ \| gandha-bhūmibhyāṁ bhinnaḥ \| bhūmau tiñthāti \|*  
*bhūmis na veda taṁ hi\| sa hy ātmāhaṁ kathaṁ bhoktā bhavāmi*

**Перевод:**

**Ether is endowed with sound. The ātmā is different from both. It is situated in the ether. The ether does not know the ātmā. How can the ātmā be the enjoyer?**

**Air is endowed with touch. The ātmā is different from both. It is situated in the air. The air does not know the ātmā. How can ātmā be the enjoyer?**

**Fire is endowed with form. The ātmā is different from both. It is situated in the fire. The fire does not know the ātmā. How can ātmā be the enjoyer?**

**Water is endowed with taste. The ātmā is different from both. It is situated in the water. The water does not know the ātmā. How can ātmā be the enjoyer?**

**Earth is endowed with fragrance. The ātmā is different from both. It is situated in the earth. The earth does not know the ātmā. How can ātmā be the enjoyer?**

**Комментарий:**

Durvāsā speaks these verses.

The statements establish that the jīva is not the enjoyer. There is a substance called ether which is endowed with sound. The ātmā is different from both. It is situated in the ether with sound, situated in the body. That ether, the object supported, does not know the ātmā, its support. Ātmā is separate from ether, unknown by ether. How then can I, the ātmā, be the enjoyer? The ātmā cannot be the enjoyer of the ear’s perceptions since ether cannot enter the ātmā. The same explanation may be given for the other elements such as air endowed with touch.

### 2.15

*idaṁ hi manas teñv evedaṁ manute \| tān idaṁ hi gṛhṇāti \|*

**Перевод:**

**The mind, situated in the senses, thinks according to the particular sense. The mind accepts ether and other elements as well as their senses.**

**Комментарий:**

“How then do I, the enjoyer, hear sound?” The mind is the perceiver. It is situated in all the senses such as the ear. It then thinks according to the ear or other sense. Becoming one with that sense, it experiences sound etc. Therefore the mind accepts ether and other elements.

### 2.16

*yatra sarvam ātmaivābhūt tatra vā kutra manute, kva vā gacchatīti*  
*sa hy ātmāhaṁ kathaṁ bhoktā bhavāmi \|*

**Перевод:**

**When the ātmā becomes manifest to the realizer of ātmā, at that time how can he think that the ātmā is the enjoyer or that the ātmā goes anywhere?**

**Комментарий:**

“Since you have an antaḥkaraṇa like other people, the ātmā will have an imposition (adhyāsa) of being the enjoyer.” This verse shows that such imposition does not take place on the ātmā.

When (yatra) for the person who knows ātmā, ātmā becomes manifest because of knowledge of the basis of all cause and effect, in the manner of recognizing shell to be distinct from silver, in that state (tatra vā) or in any object (kutra) what experiencer will think in terms of a sense organ?

The same should be considered for all the knowledge senses. All the action senses should be considered in the same way. Who goes in what direction by what sense? Since I am in a state of realizing ātmā, I am not an enjoyer related to the body.

My enjoyment with pure bliss arises from association with you gopīs, who are dearest to the Lord. (Thus Durvāsā has no interest in food.) He is like Śukadeva:

*harer guṇākñipta matir bhagavān bādarāyaṇiḥ \| ad*  
*hyagān mahad ākhyānaṁ nityaṁ viñṇu jana priyaḥ \|\|*

**Śukadeva, the son of Vyāsa, whose mind became distracted from trance by the qualities of Kṛñṇa, and who became attracted to the Vaiñṇavas, studied this great Bhāgavatam. (SB** [**1.7.11**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-1/7.11)**)**

### 2.17

*ayaṁ hi kṛñṇo yo vo hi preñṭhaḥ śarīra-dvaya-kāraṇaṁ bhavati \|*

**Перевод:**

**This Kṛñṇa, who is dear to you, is the cause of the two bodies (individual and total).**

**Комментарий:**

Kṛñṇa, being the cause of everything, cannot be defeated by the power of effects since he is the possessor of all great powers. The word ayam is used to indicate that the word Kṛñṇa cannot be interpreted to meaning anything else. He is the Kṛñṇa who is dear to you. He is the cause of the two bodies. This means he is the cause of all effects. This statement is spoken to give realization of the truth immediately. He is the cause of all people being manifested.

### 2.18

*dvā suparṇau bhavato brahmaṇo’ṁśabhūtas tathetaro*  
*bhoktā bhavati \|anyo hi sākñī bhavatīti \|*  
*vṛkña-dharme tau tiñṭhataḥ \| ato bhoktrabhoktārau \|*  
*pūrvo hi bhoktābhavati tathetaro’abhoktā kṛñṇo bhavatīti \|*

**Перевод:**

**Two conscious entities exist. The jīva, an aṁśa of the antaryāmī, thinks he is the enjoyer. The other, the antaryāmī, is the witness. The two reside in the tree of dharma. One is the enjoyer and one is not the enjoyer. The jīva is the enjoyer. The other, Kṛñna, is not the enjoyer.**

**Комментарий:**

In considering the two bodies vyañṭi (individual) and samañṭi (total— universal form), there is great difference between jīvas like me and antaryāmī, though they are both situated in one body. The two are living together (dvau) and conscious (suparṇau). The two exist in this way. Of the two, Brahman, the antaryāmī of the jīva, is like the sun globe. The jīva (itaraḥ), expansion of antaryāmī (brahmaṇaḥ amśa-bhūtaḥ), is like a particle of a sun ray. The jīva is the enjoyer.

The jīva considers himself the enjoyer in the body, out of ignorance. The other, the aṁśī, the Lord, is only the witness. Iti indicates the end of the mantra.

The word tathā indicates that the jīva is an aṁśa of Brahman. How can the Brahman have an aṁśa? Śrutes tu śabda-mūlatvāt: the Veda is authoritative proof. (Brahma-sūtra [2.1.27](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vs/2.1.27)) It is possible because of the acintya-śakti of the Lord. Some may say that Brahman becomes the jīva because of ignorance but that is not possible since ignorance cannot exist within Brahman or Brahman cannot be subjected to ignorance. Enough of speaking on this subject at length.

The statement is repeated to show that the antaryāmī is non-different from Kṛñṇa. The jīva and antaryāmī reside in the body, which has the quality of a tree (vṛkña). Vṛksa means “that which is cut down.” Though they both reside there, the jīva enjoys since he is not the Lord and the other does not enjoy, since he is the Lord. That is explained. The first is the enjoyer but (tathā) the other, the Lord, is not the enjoyer. “The Lord is not the enjoyer (and thus he is a brahmacārī). That is accepted. But how is this related to Kṛñṇa?” Kṛñṇa is the Lord. Kṛñṇa exists in this form of antaryāmī, as his expansion. Viñṭabhyāham idaṁ kṛtsnam ekāṁśena sthito jagad: I pervade everything in the universe by one expansion. (BG 10.42)

### 2.19

*yatra vidyāvidye na vidāmo vidyāvidyābhyāṁ bhinnaḥ \|*  
*vidyāmayo hi yaḥ sa kathaṁ viñayī bhavatīti \|*

**Перевод:**

**He who is separate from material vidyā and avidyā, near whom we do not know vidyā and avidyā, he who is filled with the cic-cakti— how can he be a material enjoyer?**

**Комментарий:**

Kṛñṇa’s great difference from jīva is stated. Vidyā and avidyā are aspects of māyā.

*vidyāvidye mama tanū viddhy uddhava śarīriṇām \|*  
*bandha-mokña-karī ādye māyayā me vinirmite \|\|*

**O Uddhava! Understand that vidyā and avidyā are my śaktis.They are created by my māyā, are without beginning, and create liberation and bondage for the living beings. (SB** [**11.11.3**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-11/11.3)**)**

Just by coming into his proximity (yatra) we realize that vidyā and avidyā do not exist in him, since, expanding as antaryāmī, he controls both.

Yasyāyutāyutāṁśāṁśe viśva-śaktir iyaṁ sthitā: the śakti of the universe is situated as a portion of a portion of a ten thousanth of Viñṇu. (Viñṇu Purāṇa 1.9.52) Thus, he is different from both vidyā and avidyā. He is situated separately. Arjuna says:

*tvam ādyaḥ puruñaḥ sākñād īśvaraḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ \|*  
*māyāṁ vyudasya cic chaktyā kaivalye sthita ātmani \|\|*

**You are the original Lord, the controller, beyond material energy. You are situated in your spiritual form, being one with your spiritual energy, and separate from the material energy. (SB** [**1.7.23**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-1/7.23)**)**

The reason is given. Filled completely with the cic-cakti of mahā-vidyā (vidyāmayaḥ), how can he be a material enjoyer?

### 2.20

*yo ha vai kāmena kāmān kāmayate sa kāmī bhavati \| yo*  
*ha vai tv akāmenakāmān kāmayate so’kāmī bhavati\|\|*

**Перевод:**

**He who desires objects with desire to enjoy them is an enjoyer. He who desires objects without a desire to enjoy them is not an enjoyer.**

**Комментарий:**

“We have experienced that Kṛñṇa is the enjoyer.” This verse answers. He who desires material objects (kāmān) because of desire to enjoy is an enjoyer (kāmī). He who desires objects without desire to enjoy them, with favorable attitude only, out of prema, is not an enjoyer. It is said ātmārāmo’py arīramad: though Kṛñṇa is ātmārāma he enjoyed. (SB [10.29.42](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/29.42)) Prāmṛjat karuṇaḥ premṇā śantamenāṅga pāṇinā: merciful Kṛñṇa lovingly wiped their faces with his comforting hand. (SB [10.33.20](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/33.20)) Nāhaṁ tu sakhyo bhajato’pi jantūn bhajāmy amīñām anuvṛtti-vṛttaye: but the reason I do not immediately reciprocate the affection of living beings even when they worship me, O gopīs, is that I want to intensify their loving devotion. (SB [10.32.20](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/32.20))

*yat tv ahaṁ bhavatīnāṁ vai dūre varte priyo dṛśām \|*  
*manasaḥ sannikarñārthaṁ mad-anudhyāna-kāmyayā \|\|*

**But the actual reason why I, your beloved, have stayed far away from your eyes is that I wanted to intensify my meditation upon you and thus draw my mind closer to you. (SB** [**10.47.34**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/47.34)**)**

From these statements it is shown that Kṛñṇa desired the gopīs, but without desire for himself (akāmena). Therefore he is not an enjoyer. He had prema. This quality should be considered to be a necessary quality, since he is the shelter of all beings and is accepted as the most worthy of worship. The gopīs’ desire for him is the same.

*yat te sujāta-caraṇāmburuhaṁ staneñu*  
*bhītāḥ śanaiḥ priya dadhīmahi karkaśeñu \|*  
*tenāṭavīm aṭasi tad vyathate na kiṁ svit kūr*  
*pādibhir bhramati dhīr bhavad-āyuñāṁ vaḥ \|\|*

**O beloved! Your lotus feet are so soft that we place them gently on our breasts, fearing that your feet will be hurt. Our life rests only in you. Our minds, therefore, are filled with anxiety that your tender feet might be wounded by pebbles as you roam about on the forest path. (SB** [**10.31.19**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/31.19)**)**

Durvāsā also indicates that the gopīs do not have material desire. They cannot be considered ordinary enjoyers. The relationship between Kṛñṇa and his gopīs gives happiness to liberated devotees and desiring liberation. This is stated by Uddhava:

*vāñchanti yad bhava-bhiyo munayo vayaṁ ca*

**Sages fearing material life desire the gopīs’ pure love. (SB** [**10.47.58**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/47.58)**)**

*nāyaṁ śriyo ’ṅga u nitānta-rateḥ prasādaḥ*  
*svar-yoñitāṁ nalina-gandha-rucāṁ kuto ’nyāḥ*  
*rāsotsave ’sya bhuja-daṇḍa-gṛhīta-kaṇṭha*  
*labdhāśiñāṁ ya udagād vraja-vallabhīnām*

**When Kṛñṇa was dancing with the gopīs in the rāsa-līlā, the gopīs were embraced by the arms of the Lord. This favor was never bestowed upon the goddess of fortune, though she was intensely attracted to him, or to other consorts in the spiritual world whose bodily luster and aroma resemble the lotus flower. And what to speak of worldly women? (SB** [**10.47.60**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/47.60)**)**

Therefore, I see extraordinary qualities in Kṛñṇa and in you.

### 2.21

*janma-jarābhyāṁ bhinnaḥ sthāṇur ayam acchedyo’yaṁ yo’sau saurye*  
*tiñṭhati yo’sau goñu tiñṭhati yo’sau gāḥ pālayati \| yo’sau gopeñu tiñṭhati \|*  
*yo’sau sarveñu vedeñu tiñṭhati \| yo’sau sarva-vedair gīyate \|*  
*yo’sau sarveñu bhūteñv āviśya bhūtāni vidadhāti sa vo hi svāmī bhavati \|*

**Перевод:**

**He who is beyond birth and old age, who is steady in form, qualities, pastimes and abode, who is indivisible, who resides on the bank of the Yamuna, who lives among the cows, who protects the cows, who lives among the cowherds, who resides in all the Vedas, who is praised in all the Vedas, and who, entering all beings, makes them special, is none other than the husband of you gopīs.**

**Комментарий:**

Like Nārāyāṇa’s relationship with Lakñmī, Kṛñṇa’s relationship with you, his svarūpa-śakti, is eternal. It cannot be called material desire since it is perfect love. Saying this, Durvāsā lists qualities of Kṛñṇa to make his point. The list shows that Kṛñṇa is devoid of the six material transformations. It is similar to the following.

*ekas tvam ātmā puruñaḥ purāṇaḥ*  
*satyaṁ svayaṁ jyotir ananta ādyaḥ \|*  
*nityo’kñaro’jasra-sukho nirañjanaḥ*  
*pūrṇo’dvayo mukta upādhito’mṛtaḥ \|\|*

**You are the one supreme soul, the supreme person, the oldest, the truth, self-manifested, unlimited, the beginning, eternal, indestructible, unlimited happiness, pure, full, without a second, free of upādhis and deathless. (SB** [**10.14.23**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/14.23)**)**

Kṛñṇa is fixed (sthānuḥ) in his form, qualities, pastimes and abodes. This is illustrated: he resides in the sun (saurye), in an expanded form, as the object of meditation in kāma-gāyatrī. Or, he resides in a place near the Yamunā (saurye). The suffix ya is added to indicate a place near to the named place. (Pāṇinī 4.2.0) Saurye can also refer to Kṛñṇa-vana and Bhadra-vana, which will be mentioned later. Thus the word saurya refers to two places on the bank of the Yamunā. This is similar to the word suyamunā in Viñṇu-sahasranāma, where it refers to people who reside on the banks of the Yamunā. This is explained by the commentator.

Having explained about Kṛñṇa, now his name Govinda is explained by Durvāsā. Kṛñṇa resides among the cows (goñu). He resides among persons who reside in Nanda’s Gokula (gopeñu). This refers to the cowherds who reside there out of great attachment to Kṛñṇa.

*vatsair vatsatarībhiś ca sākaṁ krīḍati keśavaḥ \|*  
*vṛndāvanāntara-gataḥ sa-rāmo bālakair vṛtaḥ \|\|*

**Kṛñṇa with Balarāma, surrounded by young boys, plays with the calves and new borns in Vṛndāvana. (Skanda Purāṇa)**

Previously, in explaining the meaning of the word Govinda, it was said go-bhūmi-veda-vidita: he is known among the cows, on earth and in the Vedas. Having shown the esoteric meaning (as the cowherd), the external or common meaning is now shown: he is known in the Vedas. The last statement in the present text says that he enters all beings and supports them. This is an explanation of term bhūmi (earth) in go-bhūmi-veda-vidita (he is perceived on earth). The Lord enters the earth in two ways: by his śakti, when he is aprakaṭa, and directly when he is prakaṭa (coming as avatāra). Vidadhāti means “he makes special” or “establishes.” When he comes as avatāra he enters living beings and makes them special.

Explaining the meaning of the third name gopījana-vallabha (after explaining the words Kṛñṇa and Govinda), Durvāsā also indicates the conclusion: he is your husband (svāmī). He who was described by all the words above is your husband. The word hi indicates affirmation. The word saḥ is connected with all the previous phrases (yaḥ). He alone is your husband or vallabha and no one else. The gopīs’ steady relationship is approved by saying that they are husband and wife. A relationship of the gopīs with anyone else would be illusory. In describing the rāsa dance it is said:

*nāsūyan khalu kṛñṇāya mohitās tasya māyayā \| manyamānāḥ*  
*sva-pārśva-sthān svān svān dārān vrajaukasaḥ \|\|*

**The cowherd men, bewildered by Kṛñṇa’s yogamāyā, thought their wives had remained home at their sides. They did not harbor any jealous feelings against him at any time. (SB** [**10.33.37**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/33.37)**)**

Separation from Kṛñṇa is occasional or incidental. Kṛñṇa says directly to the gopīs:

*mayi bhaktir hi bhūtānām amṛtatvāya kalpate \|*  
*diñṭyā yadāsīn matsneho bhavatīnāṁ madāpanaḥ \|\|*

**Rendering devotional service to me qualifies any living being for eternal life. But your prema for me, which has existed to bring the highest fortune, has caused you to attain me directly. (SB** [**10.82.44**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/82.44)**)**

### 2.22

*sā hovāca gāndharvī kathaṁ vāsmāsu jāto’sau gopālaḥ \|*  
*kathaṁ vā jñāto’sau tvayā mune kṛñṇaḥ \| ko vāsya mantraḥ \|*  
*kiṁ vāsya sthānaṁ \| kathaṁ vā devakyāṁ jātaḥ \| ko vāsya jyāyān*  
*rāmo bhavati \| kīdṛśī pūjāsya gopālasya bhavati \| sākñāt prakṛti-paro yo’yam*  
*ātmā gopālaḥ kathaṁ tv avatīrṇo bhūmyāṁ hi vai \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Gāndharvī then spoke. Why was this cowherd born among us? How is Kṛñṇa known to you? What is his mantra? Where is his residence? How was he born to Devakī? Who is his elder brother Balarāma? How does one worship Gopāla? Why has this Gopāla, who is beyond prakṛti, made his appearance on earth?**

**Комментарий:**

Hearing about her lover, she again inquired. The questions are clear. Why was he born to Devakī? She had heard that Garga spoke to Nanda and said prāg ayaṁ vasudevasya kvacij jātas tavātmajaḥ: your son was previously the son of Vasudeva.6 (SB [10.8.14](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/8.14))

### 2.23

*sa hovāca tāṁ ha vai \| eko ha vai pūrvaṁ nārāyaṇo devaḥ \| yasmin lokā*  
*otāś ca protāś ca \| tasya hṛt-padmāj jāto’bja-yonis tapitvā tasmai hi*  
*varaṁ dadau \| sa kāma-praśnam eva vavre \| taṁ hāsmai dadau \|*

**Перевод:**

**Durvāsā spoke to her. The one Nārāyaṇa within whom the universe is woven like threads, bore Brahmā form his lotus navel. After Brahmā had performed austerity, Viñṇu gave him a boon. Brahmā requested his boon and Viñṇu gave it to him.**

**Комментарий:**

Durvāsā (saḥ) spoke clearly (ha) to Gāndharvī. Vai indicates that it is well known. Another version has eko hi vai nārāyaṇa āsa iha. This means that one Nārāyaṇa existed in the material world (iha). How did Nārāyaṇa get his name? Within him the universe is woven as if by cross threads.

*nārāj jātāni tattvāni nārāṇīti vidur budhāḥ \|*  
*tasya tāny ayanaṁ pūrvaṁ tena nārāyaṇaḥ smṛtaḥ \|\|*

**The wise know that nāra refers to the elements born from water. These elements were under his shelter (ayana) at the beginning of creation. Therefore he is called Nārāyaṇa.**

“Lotus of the heart” means “lotus from his navel.” First Brahmā was accepted in Viñṇu’s heart and then appeared from his navel. Then the Lord gave a boon to Brahmā who was waiting there, after performing austerity (tapitvā). Or, revealing himself, the Lord gave him a boon. The Lord pleased him with a boon.

Brahmā (saḥ) requested what he desired (kāma-praśnam). Nārāyaṇa gave him the boon.

### 2.24

*sa hovācābja-yoniḥ \| yo’vatārāṇāṁ madhye śreñṭho’vatāraḥ ko bhavati \|*  
*yena lokās tuñṭā devās tuñṭā bhavanti \| yaṁ smṛtvā muktā asmāt*  
*saṁsārād bhavanti \| kathaṁ vāsyāvatārasya brahmatā bhavati \|*

**Перевод:**

**Brahmā spoke. Who is the best among the avatāras, by whom all people and devatās become satisfied, by whose remembrance people become liberated from saṁsāra? How can this avatāra be Svayam Bhagavān?**

**Комментарий:**

Who is the best among the avatāras? Because he is the best, by him all people and devatās are satisfied. How can the best among the avatāras be Svayam Bhagavān? If he is simply an avatāra (expansion), he cannot be the greatest among all and therefore cannot be the best.

### 2.25

*sa hovāca taṁ hi nārāyaṇo devaḥ \| sa-kāmyā meroḥ śṛṅge yathāsapta-pūryo*  
*bhavanti \| tathā niñkāmyāḥ sa-kāmyāś ca bhū-gola-cakre sapta-pūryo*  
*bhavanti \| tāsāṁ madhye sākñād brahma gopāla-purīti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Nārāyaṇa spoke to Brahmā. Just as there are seven delightful cities on top of Meru, there are seven cities which bestow spiritual opulence and liberation on earth. Among them is the city of Gopāla, which is directly Brahman.**

**Комментарий:**

Gāndharvī asked “Why has Bhagavān appeared among us cowherds?” Durvāsā already answered by explained that Kṛñṇa lives among the cows eternally (in verse 21). He plays eternally with you. Now however he has made an appearance with you which is called a birth. Because of absorption in the pastimes, you cannot detect this eternal situation. Why does this Gopāla or ātmā beyond prakṛti appear on earth? (verse 22) The answer is found in Brahmā’s question: he comes to satisfy the people and devatās. (verse 24)

He should now answer the remaining questions. Among them the answer to “Where does he live?” is answered directly. Nārāyaṇa illustrates to Brahmā in the present verse that Kṛñṇa is the best of all avatāras since he is Parabrahman, using an example of various cities. Sakāmya means full of enjoyment. The reference to Meru indicates places on higher planets, the highest of which is the supreme Vaikuṇṭha.

*yā yathā bhuvi vartante pūryo bhagavataḥ priyāḥ \|*  
*tās tathā santi vaikuṇṭhe tat-tal-līlārtham ādṛtāḥ \|\|*

**Just as there are cities on earth dear to the Lord, there are cities in Vaikuṇṭha for his pastimes. (Skanda and Padma Purāṇas )**

The cities there give liberation (niñkāmyāḥ) and all enjoyment (sakāmyāḥ). The enjoyment is sāṛñṭi etc. in the spiritual world. There are seven cities in both places—the material and spiritual worlds.

*ayodhyā mathurā māyā kāśī kāñcī avantikā \|*  
*purī dvārāvatī jñeyāḥ saptaite mokña-dāyikā \|\|*

**The seven cities which bestow liberation are Ayodhyā, Mathurā, Māyā, Kāśī, Kāñcī, Avanti, Purī, and Dvārakā.**

The conclusion is then given. Among them, the city of Gopāla is the highest shelter (sākñād brahma) (and thus Kṛñṇa, who lives there, is Parabrahman).

### 2.26

*sakāmyā niñkāmyā devānāṁ sarveñāṁ bhūtānāṁ bhavati \|*  
*yathā hi vai sarasi padmaṁ tiñṭhati tathā bhūmyāṁ tiñṭhatīti*  
*cakreṇa rakñitā hi vai mathurā tasmād gopāla-purī bhavati \|*

**Перевод:**

**This place satisfies all desires of devatās and other beings both material and spiritual. Just as a lotus rests on water untouched by it, this abode rests within the material world. Protected by the cakra, Mathurā, the city of Kṛñṇa, is the highest abode.**

**Комментарий:**

This verse shows the intense pleasures and spiritual assets available there. Material vision of the place is excluded by comparing it to a lotus in the water. Because of its spiritual nature, it is the city of Gopāla, who is Parabrahman in human form. By its intrinsic nature, this city must be considered the supreme shelter (sākñad brahma) among all places.

The city appears in two forms, lower and higher (sakāmya and niñkāmya) because of differing types of worship, just as Gopāla himself appears in various forms according to varieties of worship.

This city is the highest because Gopāla is its ruler. He is described as brahmaṇo hi pratiñṭāham: I am the basis of Brahman (BG [14.27](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/14.27)) He is thus situated as the supreme Brahman. In this pure condition he appears as avatāra in this world.

### 2.27

*bṛhad bṛhad-vanaṁ madhor madhu-vanaṁ tālas tāla-vanaṁ kāmyaṁ kāmya*  
*vanaṁ bahulā bahulā-vanaṁ kumudaṁ kumuda-vanaṁ khadiraḥ khadira*  
*vanaṁ bhadro bhadra-vanaṁ bhāṇḍīra iti bhāṇḍīra-vanaṁ śrī-vanaṁ loha*  
*vanaṁ vṛndāyā vṛndā-vanam etair āvṛtā purī bhavati \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**This city is surrounded by twelve forests: the large Brhad-vana; then Madhu-vana, named after the demon Madhu; Tāla-vana, filled with tāla trees; Kāmya-vana, famous for attractive (kāmya ) Kṛñṇa; Bahulā-vana, where Bahulā lived; Kumuda-vana, filled with lotuses; Khadira-vana, filled with khadira trees; Bhadra-vana, where Balarāma performed pastimes; Bhāṇḍira-vana, where the famous banyan tree grew; beautiful Śrī-vana; Lohavana, where the Lohajaṅgha dynasty lived; and Vṛndāvana, named after Vṛndā.**

**Комментарий:**

Having described placement of his abode, now in this verse the sage describes the surrounding forests. Bṛhad-vana (great forest - Mahāvana) is called by this name because of its great size, or because of greatness of Kṛñṇa’s first pastimes in Gokula. Madhuvana is named because a demon named Madhu lived there and was killed there, just as Keśī-tīrtha is so called because Keśī was killed there. Or it is called Madhuvana because the Madhu dynasty (Yadus) lived there. Tālavana is named from the tāla trees growing there which caused a special pastime. Kāmya-vana is a place of many types of pastimes, where Kṛñṇa, most attractive (kāmya), performed pastimes. Bahulā was a wife of the Lord according to Skanda Purāṇa. The forest Bahulāvana is named as such since she stayed there. Another version has bahulo bahula-vanam. These means it contains a place called Bahulā where Bahulā stayed. The long lā is similar to the name Miśrakā-vanam. Kumuda-vana produced many special pastimes because lotuses always blossomed there. Khadira is named because of the khadira (betel) trees growing there.

Bhadra-vana is so called because of Balabhadra, Balarāma, who assisted Kṛñṇa. Thus Kṛñṇa’s forest is named after Balarāma. Balarāma performed special pastimes there. The place is non-different from him. Bhāṇḍira is a special banyan tree which inspired streams of rasa in Kṛñṇa’s pastimes. That tree grew in Bhāṇḍira-vana. Śrī means wealth and beauty. Thus the forest is called Śrī-vana. Loha-vana is named after the Loha-jaṅgha dynasty that lived there, just as Madhuvana was named after the Madhu dynasty living there.

Vṛndāvana is named after Vṛndā, a special personification of Kṛñṇa’s great līlā-śakti, mentioned in the Padma Purāṇa, Kārtika-māhātmya. The greatness of the place is shown by explaining how it became the shelter of all of Kṛñṇa’s pastimes. Vṛndāvana is placed last according to the principle that one should conclude with sweetness (madhureṇa samāpayet). The method of visiting the forests is explained in Varāha Purāṇa.

### 2.28

*tatra teñv eva gahaneñv evaṁ devā manuñyā*  
*gandharvā nāgāḥ kinnarā gāyantīti nṛtyantīti \|*

**Перевод:**

**Within Mathurā, in those forests, the devatās, humans, Gandharvas, Nāgas and Kinnaras sing and dance in their particular styles.**

**Комментарий:**

In Mathurā area (tatra), in those forests, the devatās sing and dance since it is the eternal abode, since it is Brahman itself (evam). It is said atra devāś ca bhūtāni vartante sūkñma-rūpataḥ: the devatās and elements exist there in subtle forms. (Gautamīya-tantra) The word iti repeated indicates the manner of their singing and dancing.

### 2.29

*tatra dvādaśādityā ekādaśa rudrā añṭau vasavaḥ*  
*sapta-munayo brahmā nāradaś ca pañca vināyakā vīreśvaro*  
*rudreśvaro ambikeśvaro gaṇeśvaro nīlakaṇṭheśvaro viśveśvaro*  
*gopāleśvaro bhadreśvara ādyāni liṅgāni caturviṁśatir bhavanti \|*

**Перевод:**

**The twelve Ādityas, eleven Rudras, eight Vasus, seven sages, Brahmā, Nārada, the five Vināyakas, the eight liṅgas—vīreśvara, rudreśvara, ambikeśvara, gaṇeśvara, nīlakaṇṭheśvara, viśveśvara, gopāleśvara, and bhadreśvara— as well as twenty-four other liṅgas, reside there.**

**Комментарий:**

The sage enumerates others, starting with the twelve Ādityas and ending with the Vināyakas, who reside there. The liṅgas, in which Śiva resides, also exist there eternally. Eight are listed starting with Vīreśvara. Ādyāni means others as well.

### 2.30

*dve vane staḥ kṛñṇa-vanaṁ bhadra-vanam \| tayor antar dvādaśa-vanāni*  
*puṇyāni puṇyatamāni \| teñv eva devās tiñṭhanti siddhāḥ siddhiṁ prāptāḥ \|*

**Перевод:**

**There are two forests Bhadra-vana and Kṛñṇa-vana. The twelve forests are contained within these two. Some are pure and others even purer. Devatās, nitya-siddhas, and sādhakas who have attained perfection, remain there.**

**Комментарий:**

The forests are further explained, with more particular knowledge. It was previously explained that the devatās and others resided here eternally. Now their positions as sādhakas and siddhas is mentioned. Bhadra-vana contains Bhadra-vana, Śrī-vana, Loha-vana, and Bṛhad-vana. Kṛñṇa vana includes the other eight. Some of them are pure. Others are most pure (puṇyatamāni). The devatās such as Ādityas and the siddhas such as nitya-siddha cowherds, the associates of Kṛñṇa, are situated in perfection there. Sādhakas such as Candradhvaja, who have attained perfection (siddhim prāptāḥ), also dwell there.

### 2.31

*tatra hi rāmasya rāma-mūrtiḥ pradyumnasya pradyumna-mūrtir*  
*aniruddhasyāniruddha-mūrtiḥ kṛñṇasya kṛñṇa-mūrtiḥ \|*

**Перевод:**

**In Mathurā area, there is a deity of Balarāma called Rāma, a deity of Pradyumna called Pradyumna, a deity of Aniruddha called Aniruddha and a deity of Kṛñṇa called Kṛñṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

The Lord in four forms also resides there. In Mathurā area, in those forests (tatra), it is well-known (hi) that there is a deity named Rāma, (which is a form of Balarāma.) It is not named Śeña. The others are understood in the same way. All four forms are not found everywhere in Mathurā, since in Vṛndāvana one finds forms of Balarāma and Kṛñṇa only.

### 2.32

*vaneñv evaṁ mathurāsv evaṁ dvādaśa-mūrtayo bhavanti \|*  
*ekāṁ hi rudrā yajanti \| dvitīyāṁ hi brahmā yajati \|*  
*tṛtīyāṁ brahmajā yajanti \| caturthīṁ maruto yajanti \|*  
*pañcamīṁ vināyakā yajanti \| ñañṭhīṁ vasavo yajanti \|*  
*saptamīm ṛñayo yajanti \| añṭamīṁ gandharvā yajanti \|*  
*navamīm apsaraso yajanti \| daśamī vai hy antardhāne tiñṭhati \|*  
*ekādaśameti svapadaṁ gatā \| dvādaśameti bhūmyāṁ hi tiñṭhati \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**As in the forests, so in Mathurā, Kṛñṇa appears in twelve forms. The Rudras worship the first form. Brahmā worships the second form. Brahmā’s sons worship the third form. The Maruts worship the fourth form. The Vināyakas worship the fifth form. The Vasus worship the sixth form. The sages worship the seventh form. The Gandharvas worship the eighth form. The Apsarās worship the ninth form. The tenth form is invisible. The eleventh form is in the spiritual sky (his own abode). The twelfth form is on the earth.**

**Комментарий:**

Kṛñṇa appeared differently to different people when he entered Kaṁsa’s arena:

*mallānām aśanir nṛṇāṁ nara-varaḥ strīṇāṁ smaro mūrtimān*  
*gopānāṁ sva-jano ’satāṁ kñiti-bhujāṁ śāstā sva-pitroḥ śiśuḥ*  
*mṛtyur bhoja-pater virāḍ aviduñāṁ tattvaṁ paraṁ yogināṁ*  
*vṛñṇīnāṁ para-devateti vidito raṅgaṁ gataḥ sāgrajaḥ*

**The various groups of people in the arena regarded Kṛñṇa in different ways when He entered it with His elder brother. The wrestlers saw Kṛñṇa as a lightning bolt, the men of Mathurā as the best of males, the women as Cupid in person, the cowherd men as their relative, the impious rulers as a chastiser, His parents as their child, the King of the Bhojas as death, the unintelligent as the Supreme Lord’s universal form, the yogīs as the Absolute Truth and the Vṛñṇis as their supreme worshipable Deity. (SB** [**10.43.17**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/43.17)**)**

Similarly, according to the bhāva of the Rudras and others, the Lord appears in twelve forms, which are different and non-different from Kṛñṇa. As the Lord appears in the twelve forests (evam) he appears in Mathurā district in twelve forms.

The worship of the twelve forms is described. The tenth form is invisible (antardhāne). The eleventh famous form is in the Lord’s abode (sva-padaṁ gataḥ). The twelfth famous form resides on the earth. Explanations will be given later.

### 2.33

*tāṁ hi ye yajanti te mṛtyuṁ taranti muktiṁ labhante \|*  
*garbha-janma-jarā-maraṇa-tāpa-trayātmakaṁ duḥkhaṁ taranti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Those who worship these forms surpass death and attain liberation. They surpass the suffering of birth, old age, death, and the three types of pain.**

**Комментарий:**

Those who worship all these forms situated on earth (tām) surpass death and the other items mentioned. Since they attain liberation from ignorance they surpass the suffering of birth etc.

### 2.34

*tad apy ete ślokā bhavanti \|*  
*samprāpya mathurāṁ ramyāṁ sadā brahmādi-sevitām \|*  
*śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-śārṅga-rakñitāṁ musalādibhiḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**There are these verses concerning this: Having attaining beautiful Mathurā, which is served constantly by Brahmā and others, and is protected by the conch, cakra, club, bow, pestle and other weapons, people attain all success.**

**Комментарий:**

A mantra supports the previous statements. Having attained the beautiful city of Mathurā, people become successful. The last verb (people attain all success) should be added to complete the sentence.

### 2.35

*yatrāsau saṁsthitaḥ kṛñṇas tribhiḥ śaktyā samāhitaḥ \|*  
*rāmāniruddha-pradyumnai rukmiṇyā sahito vibhuḥ*

**Перевод:**

**There the powerful Lord Kṛñṇa resides, situated with excellent pastimes, accompanied by Balarāma, Aniruddha and Pradyumna and by his śakti Rukmiṇī.**

**Комментарий:**

Kṛñṇa is living there, situated with excellent pastimes (saṁsthitaḥ), accompanied by three persons —Balarāma, Aniruddha and Pradyumna and by his śakti Rukmiṇī. This is only representative of the many queens and the host of Yadus accompanying the Lord. The reason for being situated with excellent pastimes is given: he is vibhuḥ, all powerful.

Putting Balarāma’s name in this group answers Gāndharvī’s question, “Who is the elder brother of Kṛñṇa named Balarāma?” Pradyumna and others are similarly part of this vyūha or group. Devakī is also indirectly indicated by mentioning Rukmiṇī. Kṛñṇa appeared with four arms in Mathurā by his avirbhāva-śakti in Devakī’s womb, which is indicated by Garga saying prāg ayaṁ vasudevasya: previously he was the son of Vasudeva. (SB [10.8.14](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/8.14))

This answers the question “How was Kṛñṇa born to Devakī?” The answer to “Why was he born among us?” is also hinted again. He resides eternally with you and with them but appeared to take birth among you with a two handed form and among them with a four handed form. Brahmā’s question, “Who is the best among the avatāras?” is answered: Kṛñṇa is the best. Gāndharvī’s question “Where does Kṛñṇa reside?” is answered. He lives in Mathurā.

### 2.36

*catuḥ-śabdo bhaved eko hy oṁkāraḥ samudāhṛtaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**It is declared that the four Vedas become one as oṁkāra.**

**Комментарий:**

In order to reply to Gāndharvī’s question “How does one worship Gopāla?” first, praṇava alone is taught in reply to the question “What is his mantra?” Through the words of Nārāyaṇa, Durvāsā also teaches non-difference of self from the Lord, to produce special affection for Kṛñṇa, in order to make the worship excellent.

Just as a tree appears from a seed, so from oṁkāra appear the four Vedas, which become one with oṁkāra.This means that the final meaning is one. Therefore oṁ is the mahā-mantra. Another version has śabdo hy eko bhavet oṁkāra udāhṛta: the one omkāra is the Vedas, since the sound and meaning of the Vedas is identical with oṁkāra. That is the conclusion.

### 2.37

*tasmād eva paro rajaseti so’ham ity*  
*avadhāryātmānaṁ gopālo’ham iti bhāvayet \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Therefore one should understand that the Lord is beyond the guṇas, and one should identify one’s interests with his by using the mantra so’ham. One should thus meditate “I am Gopāla.”**

**Комментарий:**

For this reason (tasmāt eva) one should think of oneself as identical to the Lord who is superior to rajas and other guṇas, using the statement so’ham (I am he), which is called the ajapa-gāyatrī. The fourth part of the gāyatrī is also called ajapa or silent and hinted at in the text. This fourth part of gāyatrī is namas te turīyāya darśatāya padāya parorajase: I offer respects to the fourth beautiful part, beyond rajas and other guṇas. (Bṛhad-āraṇyaka Upaniñad [5.14.7](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bau/5.14.7))

Affirming that one exists because the Lord exists, like rays existing because of the existence of the sun, one should meditate that “I am Gopāla.” By satisfying the root one satisfies the branches. Therefore, whatever one would do for oneself one should do for the Lord. Complete non-difference between the jīva and the Lord is not considered, since the two are never completely identical. Jagadvyāpāra-varjam: the Lord is completely devoid of the influence of the material world. (Brahma-sūtra [4.4.17](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vs/4.4.17))

He, the Lord who is the cause of everything, named Gopāla in his highest form, is beyond the three guṇas of prakṛti and is imperceptible, since that is the conclusion of scripture. Gopāla is also the individual soul called the jīva, who is perceptible. How can this be? He is the root form of the jīva, and he is represented by oṁ. It will be shown in verse 53 that the meaning of oṁ ultimately is Gopāla and Balarāma, the son of Rohiṇī. This statement again indicates that Kṛñṇa is none other than Brahman.

### 2.38

*sa mokñam aśnute \| sa brahmatvam adhigacchati \|*  
*sa brahma-vid bhavati \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**He becomes free of ignorance. He attains a spiritual form. He realizes Kṛñṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

The result of worship is described and continues in the first part of the next verse. First, one becomes free of avidyā (mokñam). One then attains a form equal to Brahman (brahmatvam) since one attains a form of pure cit. One becomes a knower of Brahman: one realizes Kṛñṇa, who is Parabrahman in human form.

### 2.39

*yo gopān jīvān vai ātmatvenāsṛñṭi-paryantam ālāti sa gopālo bhavati hy*  
*oṁ tad yat so’haṁ \| paraṁ brahma kṛñṇātmako nityānandaika-rūpaḥ so’ham\|*  
*oṁ tad gopāla eva paraṁ satyam abādhitam \|*  
*so’ham ity ātmānam ādāya manasaikyaṁ kuryāt \|*  
*ātmānaṁ gopālo’ham iti bhāvayet \| sa evāvyakto’ nanto nityo gopālaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**He is called Gopāla because he accepts the jīvas as himself from the beginning to the end of creation. I identify with Gopāla who is oṁ. Kṛñṇa is the supreme Brahman, full of eternal bliss. I identify with him, the supreme Brahman. Gopāla is the highest truth. One should think of the self and identify the self with Gopāla in the mind. One should think “I am Gopāla.” But Gopāla does not show himself to all. He is unlimited and eternal.**

**Комментарий:**

He who extends over all time from creation to destruction and accepts (ālāti) the dependent jīvas or gopas, thinking of them as himself (ātmatvena) out of great affection, is called Gopāla (gopa— jīva, ālati—accepts). Like a cowherd, he stays close to them as if non-different from them, out of affection.

It will be said:

*yathā tvaṁ saha putrais tu yathā rudro gaṇaiḥ saha \|*  
*yathā śriyābhiyuko’haṁ tathā bhakto mama priyaḥ \|\|*

**Just as you are affectionate to your sons, just as Śiva is affectionate to his associates, just as I am affectionate to Lakñmī, so I am affectionate to my devotee. (Gopāla-tāpanī** [**2.48**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/2.48)**)**

Without such qualities Kṛñṇa would not be supreme.

Sṛṭi means the time of creation. Paryanta means the destruction. The combination means the time from creation to destruction. Gopān is a synonym for jīvān.

Vai means “in any place.” If that is the case, what is the special quality of Kṛñṇa? The answer is given. He alone (hi) who is called Kṛñṇa is Gopāla. No one else is. He said to Devakī:

*adṛñṭvānyatamaṁ loke śīlaudārya-guṇaiḥ samam \|*  
*ahaṁ suto vām abhavaṁ pṛśnigarbha iti śrutaḥ \|\|*

**Since I found no one else as highly elevated as you in character and great qualities, I became your son and was known as Pṛśnigarbha. (SB** [**10.3.41**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/3.41)**)**

Then who are you? Oṁ tat. What is known as param brahma or oṁ is I. (tat yat paraṁ brahma so’ ham)

“Is the Parabrahman different from Kṛñṇa who is Parabrahman in human form?” No.

What is called Brahman is only Kṛñṇa (kṛñṇātmakaḥ) and nothing else. Therefore I am that form of eternal bliss (nityānandaika-rūpaḥ so’ham). Therefore he who is oṁ, the Parabrahman, is I (om tat). Gopāla remains unrefuted as the highest truth. This means “I can identify with him because he is the root form.”

Thinking of the self, others also should worship in the mind with the thought of oneness with him. This is explained: one should think of the self as Gopāla. The superiority of Gopāla to the self is then shown: he is not made visible by anyone (avyaktaḥ). Gopāla is unlimited and eternal. He is eternally perfect.

### 2.40

*mathurāyāṁ sthitir brahman sarvadā me bhaviñyati \|*  
*śaṅkha-cakra-gadā-padma- vana-mālāvṛtas tu vai \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**O Brahmā! Endowed with conch, cakra, club, lotus and forest garland, I reside at all times in Mathurā.**

**Комментарий:**

The supreme excellent of Śrī-gopāla has been stated. Nārāyaṇa now shows that he himself is obedient to Gopāla.

At all times, also in the future, I reside in Mathurā. I reside as Kṛñṇa’s āvaraṇa-devatā, a member of his entourage. In the Pūrvatāpanī, his position as Vāsudeva in the āvaraṇa was shown.

### 2.41

*viśva-rūpaṁ paraṁ jyotiḥ- svarūpaṁ rūpa-varjitaṁ \|*  
*hṛdā māṁ saṁsmaran brahman mat-padaṁ yāti niścitam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**He who in his heart remembers me, the shelter of all existence, devoid of material elements, endowed with extraordinary form and supremely effulgent, certainly attains my abode.**

**Комментарий:**

Nārāyaṇa describes worship of himself. Viśva-rūpam means a form in which everything exists. Cit-svarūpam is an alternative reading. He has a form which is most brilliant (paraṁjoytiḥ). He has a remarkable (sva) form (rūpam). His form is devoid of matter. Matpadam means “my abode.”

### 2.42

*mathurā-maṇḍale yas tu jambu-dvīpe sthito’pi vā \|*  
*yo’rcayet pratimāṁ māṁ ca sa me priyataro bhuvi \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**He who worships me and my deity form in Mathurā or anywhere in Jambudvīpa is dearest to me on earth.**

**Комментарий:**

In worship, the superiority of worshipping the deity is shown. He who worship me and the deity form (pratimam) while residing anywhere in Jambudvīpa, what to speak of Mathura district, is dearest to me.

### 2.43

*tasyām adhiñṭhitaḥ kṛñṇa-rūpī pūjyas tvayā sadā \|*  
*caturdhā cāsyādhikāra-bhedatvena yajanti mām \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**You should always worship the form of Kṛñṇa in Mathurā. People worship me according to different qualifications in four forms.**

**Комментарий:**

Having answered Gāndharvī’s question “What is the worship of Gopāla?” Nārāyaṇa now shows the supreme position given to worshipping the form of Kṛñṇa. You, most qualified for worship, should worship the form of Kṛñṇa, not the form of Viñṇu with lotus navel, in Mathurā. Some worship Kṛñṇa with his different aspects in the vyūha. That is explained. People worship me, the form of Kṛñṇa, according to different qualification, in four forms.

### 2.44

*yugānuvartino lokā yajantīha sumedhasaḥ*  
*gopālaṁ sānujaṁ rāma-rukmiṇyā saha tat-param*

**Перевод:**

**Following the process for the yuga, the intelligent people worship Gopāla with Balarāma and Rukmiṇī, along with Pradyumna and Aniruddha, with great devotion in Mathurā.**

**Комментарий:**

Anujam means “he who was born later.” This refers to Pradyumna and Aniruddha. The wise worship Gopāla with Pradyumna and Aniruddha (sānujam). He is situated with Balarāma and Rukmiṇī. Rāma-rukmiṇyā is in the singular, following the rule that dvandva compound becomes singular optionally. One should worship Gopāla with devotion (tat-param). Param in this compound means “having the highest objective in mind.”

### 2.45

*gopālo’ham ajo nityaḥ pradyumno’haṁ sanātanaḥ \|*  
*rāmo’ham aniruddho’ham ātmānam arcayed budhaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The wise man should worship eternal, unborn Paramātmā, understanding that he is not different from Kṛñṇa, Pradyumna, Balarāma and Aniruddha, with whom one should identify.**

**Комментарий:**

As previously the worship using identification of self with the Lord is shown. Ātmānam means Paramātmā.

### 2.46

*mayoktena sva-dharmeṇa niñkāmeṇa vibhāgaśaḥ \|*  
*tair ayaṁ pūjanīyo vai bhadra-kṛñṇa-nivāsibhiḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Kṛñṇa is worshipped in various ways by the residents of Bhadravana and Kṛñṇa-vana according to the pure methods explained by me in scriptures.**

**Комментарий:**

Kṛñṇa is worshipped in various ways by the residents of the two forests (bhadra-kṛñṇa-nivāsibhiḥ) earlier. I, in the form of Kṛñṇa, have personally explained this worship by which to attain me (svadharmena) in works like Gautamīya-tantra.

### 2.47

*tad-dharma-gati-hīnā ye tasyāṁ mayi parāyaṇāḥ \|*  
*kalinā grasitā ye vai teñāṁ tasyām avasthitiḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Those who are devoid of devotion to me, those who are surrendered to me in Mathurā, and those who are addicted to sin because of Kaliyuga, are all qualified to reside in Mathurā.**

**Комментарий:**

The great glory of Mathurā is stated. Those who are devoid of the goal of worshipping the Lord, or those who, situated in Mathurā, are surrendered to me, and those who have become sinful because of Kali-yuga, all these, without considering distinctions, become qualified for staying in Mathurā. Other tīrthas require purity. It is said yeñāṁ kvāpi gatir nāsti teñāṁ madhupurī gatiḥ: those who have no goal take shelter of Mathurā. (Ādi-varāha Purāṇa)

### 2.48

*yathā tvaṁ saha putrais tu yathā rudro gaṇaiḥ saha \|*  
*yathā śriyābhiyukto’haṁ tathā bhakto mama priyaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Just as you are affectionate to your sons, just as Śiva is affectionate to his associates, just as I am affectionate to Lakñmī, so I am affectionate to my devotee.**

**Комментарий:**

The devotees are shown to be special and have a special destination.

### 2.49

*sa hovācābja-yoniḥ – caturbhir devaiḥ katham eko devaḥ syāt \|*  
*ekam akñaraṁ yad viśrutam anekākñaraṁ kathaṁ bhūtam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Brahmā spoke. Why does Kṛñṇa become four forms? How does the one syllable oṁ known in the scriptures become many syllables?**

**Комментарий:**

Brahmā has doubt about Kṛñṇa taking four forms. He asks questions. Why does one Kṛñṇa become four forms? What is the cause? Praṇava is famous as one syllable. This is the only syllable and not four syllables. You should explain how it becomes four. That syllable oṁ known as one syllable, Brahman, is understood from scriptures. How does it become many syllables?

### 2.50

*sa hovāca taṁ hi vai –*  
*pūrvaṁ hy ekam evādvitīyaṁ brahmāsīt*  
*tasmād avyaktam avyaktam evākñaraṁ*  
*tasmād akñarāt mahat tattvaṁ mahato vai haṁkāras tasmād evāhaṁkārāt*  
*pañca tan-mātrāṇi tebhyo bhūtāni tair āvṛtam akñaraṁ bhavati \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Nārāyaṇa spoke clearly to Brahmā. Before creation there was only one Brahman. From that Brahman arose his śakti, which was avyakta akñara oṁ. From oṁ arose mahat-tattva. From mahat-tattva arose ahaṅkāra. From ahaṅkāra arose the five tanmātras and from them arose the gross elements. The akñara became covered by them.**

**Комментарий:**

Nārāyaṇa spoke clearly (ha). He teaches that all differences arose from only one substance by the acintya-śakti. Brahma’s question about the one akñara mentioned in scripture can be understood as the eternal cause of everything (akñara) and as the syllable (akñara) oṁ. What to speak of expanding into four syllables, the Lord is the cause of everything in the material world. That is explained in this verse.

Before the creation it is well known (hi) that only one Brahman existed. Though there was the manifestation of his svarūpa-śakti at that time, it is said that only Brahman existed since the svarūpaśakti is similar to Brahman. There was nothing else since the dissimilar material śakti with its effects had disappeared.

From that Brahman (tasmāt) arose avyaktam— the total śakti which causes all the universes. Prakṛti and its transformations like ether were avyakta. This state is called akñaram or Brahman (avyaktam evākñaraṁ) since it is Brahman’s śakti. Ekam evādvitīyam brahma: there is only one Brahman and nothing else. That Brahman possessed śakti. Brahman and its śakti are considered the same since śakti and its possessor cannot be differentiated in the supreme substance.

From the akñara, actually avyakta-prakṛti, which is non-different from the Brahman (akñara), arose mahat-tattva (mahat). From mahat arose ahaṅkāra. The letter a is dropped in ahaṅkāra for metrical reasons. The rest of the text is easily understood. The akñara thus became covered by these material forms (tair āvrṭam). Śruti says parāñci khāni vyatṛṇat svayambhūs tasmāt parāṅ nu paśyati: the Lord made the senses turn outwards and thus no one can see him with those senses. (Kaṭha Upanisad [2.1.1](https://vedarama.com/en/books/katha-bs/2.1.1))

### 2.51

*akñaro’ham oṁkāro’ham ajaro’maro’bhayo’mṛto brahmābhayaṁ*  
*hi vai sa mukto’ham asmi \| akñaro’ham asmi \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I am Brahman and I am oṁ. Oṁ is without old age, without death, without fear, and full of bliss. Since Brahman is without fear, so is oṁ. I am liberated. I am unchanging Brahman (akñara).**

**Комментарий:**

Nārāyaṇa after showing his two forms of akñara—as Brahman and praṇava— now shows how they are both akñara (unchanging). I am akñara Brahman and I am oṁ. The form of oṁ is described. It is without old age, without fear, and filled with the highest bliss (amṛtaḥ). Because (hi) Brahman is without fear, so is oṁkāra (saḥ). Nārāyaṇa confirms his identity as Brahman. I am liberated. I am unchanging Brahman.

### 2.52

*sattā-mātraṁ viśva-rūpaṁ prakāśaṁ vyāpakaṁ tathā*  
*ekam evādvayaṁ brahma māyayā tu catuñṭayam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**That one Brahman is sac-cid-ānanda, has variegated forms and qualities, has the power to reveal qualities in himself and others and is spread everywhere. That one Brahman manifests as four forms by his mercy.**

**Комментарий:**

Having shown the difference created in the universe as an example, Nārāyaṇa now shows that the four forms are also inherent in the Lord. He then shows the difference in the syllables of oṁ in two verses.

The one Brahman is qualified with eternity, knowledge and bliss (sattā-mātram). Being conscious (cit) Brahman is the opposite of insentience. As bliss, it is the opposite of suffering. Brahman has these two qualities as its nature (tan-mātram). By his śakti, Brahman has a form with unlimited forms, qualities, actions and powers (viśva-rūpam). He has the power to reveal (prakāsam): he is the revealer of qualities in himself and others like light. Therefore he is pervasive.

Brahman, which was described as one, manifests as four forms to give mercy to his worshippers (māyayā), given out liberally. Māyā means mercy according to Viśva-prakāśa. Under conditions of mercy, that one substance appears as me, the puruña with name and form and as the complete form of Bhagavān (Kṛñṇa) in four different forms according to the type of worship.

### 2.53-54

*rohiṇī-tanayo rāmo a-kārākñara-sambhavaḥ \|*  
*taijasātmakaḥ pradyumno u-kārākñara-sambhavaḥ \|\|*  
*prājñātmako’niruddho ma-kārākñara-sambhavaḥ \|*  
*ardha-mātrātmakaḥ kṛñṇo yasmin viśvaṁ pratiñṭhitam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Balarāma, the son of Rohiṇī, arose from the syllable a. Pradyumna, representing the dream state, arose from the syllable u. Aniruddha, representing deep sleep, arose from the syllable m. Kṛñṇa is the nāda, and the complete sound oṁ, in whom the universe is established.**

**Комментарий:**

The four expansions are explained as syllables of oṁ, which represents the akñara-brahman. By describing Balarāma as the son of Rohinī there can be no mistaking him for the other caturvyūha member Saṅkarñaṇa (Viñṇu form). He manifested from the chanting of the sound a. He is also understood to represent the waking state (viśva), since Pradyumna and Aniruddha are next described as taijasa (state) and prajña (deep sleep). The waking state indicates full manifestation of powers in Balarāma. Dream and deep sleep states indicate lesser manifestation of powers (in Pradyumna and Aniruddha). These forms are said to be related to the waking and other states (but are not affected by them, since these stages belong only to the jīvas and not to the Lord and his expansions), since Balarāma will be described later as existing above the three states. They are described in this way out of thinking of oneness with the jiva.

Ardha-mātrātmakaḥ refers to nāda since it is the fourth element. By ājahal-lakñaṇā (the word not losing its original meaning of “halfsyllable”), being at the end of the word, ardha-mātra means the whole praṇava, since it is impossible to chant that syllable by itself. By indicating the whole praṇava, the complete nature of Kṛñṇa is shown. Thus, in him the universe is established. This praṇava should be considered an avatāra of Bhagavān.

### 2.55

*kṛñṇātmikā jagat-kartrī mūla-prakṛti-rukmiṇī \| vraja*  
*strī-jana-sambhūta-śrutibhyo brahma-saṅgataḥ \|*  
*praṇavatvena prakṛtiṁ vadanti brahma-vādinaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Rukmiṇī is the cit-cakti, producer of all things, non-different from Kṛñṇa. The knowers of Brahman describe the cit-cakti as praṇava, since oṁ manifests as Kṛñṇa, Parabrahman in human form (and he is non-different from his śakti). This is understood on the authority of the śruti arising from the questions of the women of Vraja to Durvāsā,**

**Комментарий:**

Some say that the word māyā means illusion, a type of ignorance. They do not know the truth. Māyā refers to the cic-cakti.

*svarūpa-bhūtayā nitya-śaktyā māyākhyayā yutaḥ \|*  
*ato māyāmayaṁ viñṇuṁ pravadanti sanātanam \|\|*

**The Lord is endowed with an eternal śakti called māyā, arising from his svarūpa. Therefore the wise say that eternal Viñṇu is composed of māyā. (Caturveda-śikhā, cited by Madhvācārya)**

*māyāṁ tu prakṛtiṁ vidyān mayinaṁ tu maheśvaram*

**Know that his nature (prakṛti) is called māyā and that the Lord is composed of this māyā. (Śvetāśvatara Upaniñad** [**4.10**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/4.10)**)**

Prakṛti means svarūpa or nature according to Amara-koña. In the Third Canto when the Kumāras go to Vaikuṇṭha the word yogamāyā is explained to mean cic-cakti. (SB [3.15.26](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-3/15.26)) Nigantu says māyā tu vayunaṁ jṇānam: māyā means knowledge. The word jñāna is equivalent to the cic-śakti (energy of knowledge). Trikhanda-śeña says that māyā means intelligence. Viśva-prakāśa says that māyā means mercy. Thus the general meaning of māyā is the cic-cakti.

This verse shows that the śakti possesses a form. Since the śakti and the Lord are non-different, this śakti is also the producer of everything (jagat-kārtrī). This svarūpa-śakti (mūla-prakṛti) is named Rukmiṇī. The compound is karmadharāya. She is indicated by praṇava since she is included within Kṛñṇa. The word Rukmiṇī also indicates Rādhā. Rukmiṇī dvāravatyāṁ tu rādhāvṛndāvane vane: as Rukmiṇī is the śakti in Dvārakā, Rādhā is the śakti in Vṛndāvana. (Skanda, Matsya Purāṇas) Rādhayā mādhavo devo mādhavenaiva rādhikā vibhrājante janeñu: Mādhava is revealed to the people through Rādhā and Rādhā is revealed through Mādhava. (Ṛg-pariśiñṭa)

Kṛñṇa is also indicated by praṇava. How can you say that praṇava manifests Kṛñṇa? This Upaniñad says that oṁ manifests as Parabrahman in human form (brahma-saṅgataḥ). “It is not obvious that Kṛñṇa with his vyūha arises from praṇava. Brahmā, Viñṇu and Śiva arise from praṇava according to the scriptures.” This fact is stated in the śruti Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniñad, which arose from the women of Vraja when they questioned Durvāsā (vraja-jana-strī-sambhūtaśrutibhyaḥ). The last three verses starting with rohiṇī-tanayaḥ are the essence of śruti. They represent the final meaning of all scriptures. This verse also shows the supreme position of the women of Vraja: this essential knowledge has arisen because of their questions. Without those questions Kṛñṇa would not be revealed in this manner.

Prakṛti means the cit-cakti or svarūpa-śakti, but is it also praṇava? The last line answers. It is also known as praṇava (praṇavatvena), since it is included in Kṛñṇa.

### 2.56

*tasmād oṁkāra-sambhūto gopālo viśva-sambhavaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Therefore Gopāla, arising with oṁkāra, is spread throughout the universe.**

**Комментарий:**

This verse summarizes. Viśva-samsthitaḥ(different version) means spread everywhere.

### 2.57

*klīm oṁkārasyaikyatvaṁ paṭhyate brahma-vādibhiḥ \|*  
*mathurāyāṁ viśeñeṇa māṁ dhyāyan mokñam aśnute \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Those who know the Vedas say that klīṁ and oṁkāra are one. Meditating on me in Mathurā with attention, one quickly becomes free of obstacles to attaining me.**

**Комментарий:**

“Klīṁ (rather than oṁ) is famous as the seed of the mahā-mantra.” This verse explains. The wise say that klīṁ and oṁ are one (aikyatvam). The two are one. They have the same svarūpa, have the same power and indicate the same thing. By explaining praṇava, the meaning of klīṁ is also explained. Those who truly know the meaning of the Vedas say this. Meditation and the particular place are mentioned in connection with worship. In Mathurā one quickly attains liberation. This means one becomes free of obstacles to attaining the Lord.

### 2.58-61

*añṭa-patraṁ vikasitaṁ hṛta-padmaṁ tatra saṁsthitam \|*  
*divya-dhvajātapatrais tu cihnitaṁ caraṇa-dvayam \|\|*  
*śrīvatsa-lāñchanaṁ hṛt-sthaṁ kaustubhaṁ prabhayā yutam \|*  
*catur-bhujaṁ śaṅkha-cakra-śārṅga-padma-gadānvitam \|\|*  
*sukeyurānvitaṁ bāhuṁ kaṇṭhaṁ mālā-suśobhitam \|*  
*dyumat-kirīṭa-valayaṁ sphuran-makara-kuṇḍalam \|\|*  
*hiraṇmayaṁ saumya-tanuṁ sva-bhaktāyābhaya-pradam \|*  
*dhyāyen manasi māṁ nityaṁ veṇu-śṛṅga-dharaṁ tu vā \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**One should constantly meditate on me, situated in Mathurā, situated on a eight-petaled lotus in the heart, endowed with two feet marked with attractive flag and umbrella, wearing the shining Kaustubha on my chest marked with Śrīvatsa, endowed with four arms holding conch, cakra, bow, lotus and club and decorated with arm bands, wearing a garland on my neck. One should meditate on me, offering fearlessness to my devotee, holding a flute and horn, with an attractive, shining form decorated with effulgent crown and bracelets and sparkling makara earrings.**

**Комментарий:**

Meditation is explained until verse 61. The worshipper meditates on a lotus in his heart. He should mediate on the two lotus feet situated in Mathurā (tatra). The verb dhyāyet is found in verse 61. He should meditate on his form arms endowed with conch, cakra, bow, lotus and club. But holding five objects in four arms cannot be done. The bow is placed in front. Dyumat means shining. The form is pleasing and sweet (saumya-tanum). Hiraṇyam means greatly effulgent. One should meditate on his arms with bracelets and his throat decorated with garlands. One should meditate on me, holding a flute and horn. This indicates Kṛñṇa in Mathurā area.

### 2.62

*mathyate tu jagat sarvaṁ brahma jñānena yena vā \|*  
*tat-sāra-bhūtaṁ yad yasyāṁ mathurā sā nigadyate \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The place is called Mathurā because in that place, Bhagavān, who is most excellent, the essence of the whole universe, is made manifest (mathyate) by knowledge and by bhakti-yoga.**

**Комментарий:**

By analyzing the word Mathurā the great glory of the place of meditation is shown. The place in which the nature of Bhagavān (brahma), the most excellence object (sāra-bhūtam), the whole universe (jagat sarvam), is extracted by knowledge or by bhaktiyoga (yena vā) is called Mathurā. The word vā indicates an unstated factor (bhakti-yoga). Just as by churning yogurt, butter is produced, so by knowledge of Bhagavān and bhakti, the most excellent object is extracted. The means of churning (mathyate) —jñāna and bhakti sādhana— is called mathu. Because of that, the place is called Mathurā. Mathurā is formed from the root math by adding an uṇādi meaning “possessing qualifies of.” (Thus Mathurā would mean the place possessing the quality of “churning and extracting.”)

### 2.63

*añṭa-dik-pālibhir bhūmiḥ padmaṁ vikasitaṁ jagat \|*  
*saṁsārārṇava-sañjātaṁ sevitaṁ mama mānase \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**One should worship the blossoming lotus of my heart, served in my mind, as the earth or universe which has arisen from the ocean of pralaya and is endowed with eight protectors of the directions.**

**Комментарий:**

For persons qualified for Mathurā, who are worshippers of the virāṭ form, arrangement is made in the form of meditation performed as an aṅga of the Lord’s worship. A blossoming lotus in the form of earth, the whole universe which has arisen from the ocean of pralaya, with eight dikpālas, served in my mind, should be worshipped. The lotus is the lotus in the heart. It should be worshipped in this manner. Another version has dik-pālabhiḥ. It should actually be dik-pālebhih.

### 2.64

*candra-sūrya-tviño divya-dhvajā merur hiraṇmayaḥ \|*  
*ātapatraṁ brahma-lokam adhordhvaṁ caraṇaṁ smṛtam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Worship my effulgence as the sun and moon, worship my astonishing flag as Meru, worship Brahma-loka as my umbrella and worship the lower planets as my feet.**

**Комментарий:**

My astonishing effulgence should be worshipped as the moon, sun and other shining planets. You should worship my golden flag as Meru. You should worship my umbrella as Brahmaloka. You should remember my feet as the seven lower planets situated from lower to upper.

### 2.65

*śrīvatsaṁ ca svarūpaṁ ca vartate lāñchanaiḥ saha \|*  
*śrīvatsa-lāñchanaṁ tasmāt kathyate brahma-vādibhiḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The Śrīvatsa and totality of jīvas exist together with the limbs of the universal form. Therefore the knowers of Brahman call the Lord Śrīvatsa-lāñchana.**

**Комментарий:**

Śrīvatsa is naturally white like the moon according to Gautamīyatantra. This is confirmed in Bhāvartha-dīpikā. It exists with its characteristics (lañchanaiḥ saha). It has hairs turning to the right. And in terms of the universal form, the totality of jīvas (svarūpam) exists along with limbs of the universal form (lāñchanaiḥ saha). The Śrīvatsa is said to be the jīvātmā because of the similarity in brightness. Thus the Lord is called he who has Śrīvatsa among his characteristics (śrīvatsa-lāñchanam). The phrase śrīvatsa-lāñchanam is like rāja-danta with the words of the compound reversed. The meaning is actually lāñchana-sahitam śrīvatsam.

### 2.66

*yena sūryāgni-vāk-candraṁ tejasā sva-svarūpiṇā \|*  
*vartate kaustubhākhyaṁ hi maṇiṁ vadantīśa-māninaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The worshippers of the universal form say that the Lord’s jewel is called Kaustubha because the sun, fire, speech and moon shine by its light, which is the shelter of all jīvas.**

**Комментарий:**

They call the jewel, a form of the sun, the Kaustubha, because by its essence, its light, which is the shelter of all jīvas, the aggregate of sun, fire, speech and moon manifest. One should therefore worship the Kaustubha as the sun and other luminaries. Those who know the Lord as virāṭ (īśa-māninaḥ) say this. Kaustubha is analyzed as follows. K represents the sun. Because of similarity, fire is represented by the same letter. A represents speech, since a is the first sound. Aus the moon because that sound is present in the word glauḥ (moon). But (tu) these objects spread light (bhāti) because of Kaustubha’s nature. (The combination is k + a + aus=kaus +tu +bha.)

### 2.67

*sattvaṁ rajas tama iti ahaṁkāraś catur-bhujaḥ \|*  
*pañca-bhūtātmakaṁ śaṅkhaṁ kare rajasi saṁsthitam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Sattva, rajas, tamas and ahaṅkāra are his four arms. The conch made of the five elements is situated in his hand made of rajas.**

**Комментарий:**

By imagining the gunas and ahaṅkāra his arms, one meditates on the universal form with four arms. One worships the guṇas and ahaṅkāra as four arms of the Lord.

### 2.68

*bāla-svarūpam atyantaṁ manaś cakraṁ nigadyate \|*  
*ādyā māyā bhavec chārṅgaṁ padmaṁ viśvaṁ kare sthitam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The mind, very fickle, is called the cakra. Māyā is the bow and the universe is the lotus situated in his hand.**

**Комментарий:**

The five elements are worshipped as the conch along with a hand of rajas. The other hands mentioned represent sattva and tamas and ahaṅkāra, though not mentioned as such. The mind, most fickle like a child, is called the cakra. This means it is worshipped as the cakra. Instead of bāla-svarūpam sometimes cala-svarūpam is seen. The cakra should be understood to be held in the hand representing sattva since the order is creation (rajas), maintenance (sattva) and destruction (tamas). The bow is māyā in its capacity to disturb,7 which is its first action (ādyā māyā). The lotus represents the universe. It is situated in the hand representing tamas. The lotus should be worshipped as the universe.

### 2.69

*ādyā vidyā gadā vedyā sarvadā me kare sthitā \|*  
*dharmārtha-kāma-keyūrair divyair divya-mahīritaiḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The club, representing knowledge which should always be known, is situated in my hand along with outstanding arm bands, greatly praised, representing dharma, artha, kāma and mokña.**

**Комментарий:**

The club represents knowledge. It is held in the hand representing ahaṅkāra. The form is endowed with arm bands representing dharma, artha, kāma and mokña. Mokña is not explicitly mentioned but understood as the final goal. The arm bands are praised (īritaiḥ) of their outstanding (divyaiḥ), great (divya) glory (mahī).

### 2.70

*kaṇṭhaṁ tu nirguṇaṁ proktaṁ mālyate ādyayājayā \|*  
*mālā nigadyate brahmaṁs tava putrais tu mānasaiḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The impersonal Brahman is worshipped as the neck. It is covered by a garland of māyā when it displays its propensity to cover. Māyā is called the Lord’s garland by the Kumāras.**

**Комментарий:**

The neck is worshipped as knowledge of impersonal Brahman (nirguṇam). It is covered (mālyate) by the shadow known as the āvaraṇa-śakti (ādyayā ajayā). Māyā is to be worshipped as the Lord’s garland (mālā).

### 2.71

*kūṭasthaṁ yat svarūpaṁ ca kirītaṁ pravadanti mām \|*  
*kñarottamaṁ prasphurantaṁ kuṇḍalaṁ yugalaṁ smṛtam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**They say that my crown is the unchanging form. My sparkling earrings are known as sāṅkhya and yoga.**

**Комментарий:**

They call my (Nārāyaṇa’s) crown the unchanging (kūṭastham) form which is the cause of everything. This unchanging form is also described by the word akñara.That unchanging form should be worshipped as the crown. They say (understood) the two earrings are known as the best things (uttama) in the world (kñara): i.e sāṅkhya and yoga. Bibharti sāṅkhyaṁ yogaṁ ca devo makara-kuṇḍale: in the form of his two shark-shaped earrings, the Lord supports the processes of sāṅkhya and yoga (SB [12.11.12](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-12/11.12)) Whenever another path is described using the same genuine śruti and Purāṇas, the different path is mentioned because scriptures cater to different sampradāyas.

### 2.72

*dhyāyen mama priyo nityaṁ mokñam adhigacchati \|*  
*sa mukto bhavati tasmai ātmānaṁ ca dadāmi vai \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Meditating in this way, he who is dear to me attains eternal liberation. He gives up material bondage. More than that, I give myself to him.**

**Комментарий:**

Having described the meditation suitable for a person qualified for the mantra, Nārāyaṇa now speaks of the results of meditation. Liberation is taught as a secondary result. The person gives up the bondage of saṁsāra (sa mukto bhavati). But more than that, I give myself to him. I am controlled by his prema.

### 2.73

*etat sarvaṁ bhaviñyad vai mayā proktaṁ vidhe tava \|*  
*svarūpaṁ dvividhaṁ caiva saguṇaṁ nirguṇātmakam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**O Brahmā! What I have said to you concerning the two forms— the material form and the non-material form—should be realized and taught in the future.**

**Комментарий:**

The conclusion is reached. All this should be realized and taught (bhaviñyat) in the future. What was taught is described: the form with material qualities (saguṇam)—the universal form described from verse [2.63](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/2.63) and the form without material qualities (nirguṇātatmakam)—the four armed form or the two armed form of Kṛñṇa with the flute—described in verse [2.58](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/2.58).

### 2.74

*sa hovācābja-yoniḥ \| vyaktānāṁ mūrtīnāṁ proktānāṁ kathaṁ*  
*tvāmaraṇāni bhavanti kathaṁ vā devā yajanti rudrā yajanti brahmā*  
*yajati brahmajā yajanti vināyakā yajanti dvādaśādityā yajanti*  
*vasavo yajanti apsaraso yajanti gandharvā yajanti \| svapadānugā*  
*antardhāne tiñṭhati \| kāṁ manuñyā yajanti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Brahmā spoke. How does one decorate the visible deity forms and those described by you? How do the devatās, Rudrās, Brahmā, Brahmā’s sons, the Vināyakas, the twelve Ādityas, the Vasus, Apsarās and Gandharvas worship? What forms do they worship? What is the form in the sky? What is the invisible form? What form do men worship?**

**Комментарий:**

This shows the doubt of Brahmā. What are the ornaments of the deities which are visible to the worshipper or have been described by words (such as [2.31](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/2.31)) in this Upaniñad? How do the devatās the forms and which forms do they worship? Instead of vyaktānām vyaktīnām (distinct forms) is seen. The word devā refers to the Maruts. What is that eleventh form in the sky? ([2.32](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/2.32)) How do they worship that form? What is the tenth form, which is invisible (antardhāne)? Which form do men worship? Also it was said that the twelfth form was in the earth. What is that form?

One question is “How does one decorate the visible forms?” Another question is “How do the devatās and others worship these forms?” Another question is “Which forms do they worship?” This is indicated by the word vā in the question “How do the devatās worship?”

### 2.75

*sa hovāca taṁ hi vai nārāyaṇo devaḥ \| ādyā avyaktā dvādaśa-mūrtayaḥ sarveñu*  
*lokeñu sarveñu deveñu sarveñu manuñyeñu tiñṭhanti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Nārāyaṇa spoke to Brahmā. The eternal, invisible twelve forms reside in all planets, in all devatās and all men.**

**Комментарий:**

Nārāyaṇa answers the first question (How do they decorate the deities?) within the answers to the other two questions (How do they worship and what forms do they worship?) The twelve forms are eternal (ādyāḥ) and appear wherever the worshipper worships. They appear to the respective worshippers everywhere, in all planets, among all devatās in the upper planets and among all men in the middle planets—among all people living in their respective planets. It was previously described that the devatās resided in the twelve forests. Teñu eva devās tiñṭhanti: the devatās remain there. ([2.30](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/2.30)) However, others worship the forms elsewhere by meditation on those forests. That is explained in this verse.

### 2.76

*rudreñu raudrī brahmaṇy evaṁ brāhmī deveñu daivī manuñyeñu mānavī*  
*vināyakeñu vighna-nāśinī ādityeñu jyotir gandharveñu gāndharvī apsaraḥsv*  
*evaṁ gaur vasuñv evaṁ kāmyā antardhāne ’prakāśinī \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Raudrī manifests to the Rudras. Brāhmī manifests to Brahmā and his sons. Daivī manifests to the devatās. A human form manifests among men. A form which destroys obstacles manifests to the Vināyakas. A form of light manifests to the Ādityas. Gāndharvī manifests to the Gandharvas. Apsarī manifests to the Āpsarās through song. Vāsavī manifests to the Vasus and fulfills all desires. Aprakāśinī manifests invisibly.**

**Комментарий:**

The forms and qualities manifest according to the bhāva the particular worshipper as illustrated in the appearance of Kṛñṇa in different ways to different people in the wrestling arena of Kaṁsa. (mallānām aśanir SB [10.43.17](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/43.17)) This answers the question “Which form do they worship?” The answer to the question “How do they decorate the forms?” is answered in the same way: according to their bhāva. Instead of brahmaṇi sometimes brāhmaneñu is seen. This means that brahmī is manifest to Brahmā and the residents of Brahmaloka. Brahmā’s name is mentioned before the devatās’ names in the answers (but not the question) because he is superior to the devatās. “Humans” refers to the Kumāras and others, who are sons of Brahmā. These are mentioned after the devatās. They are considered as both devatā and human.

The form Apsarasī (missing in the text) appears to the Apsarās, and is glorified in song (gauḥ). This form appears in the form of singing. Vāsavī (not mentioned) manifests among the Vasus. This form gives all that one may desire (kāmyā). The form which exists invisibly is called “invisible.” This form is so called because some authorities have heard this name (without seeing the form).

### 2.77

*avirbhāva-tirobhāvā sva-pade tiñṭhati tāmasī rājasī sāttvikī mānuñī*  
*vijñāna-ghana ānanda-ghana saccidānandaika-rase bhakti-yoge tiñṭhati \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The form which appears in the material world and then disappears remains in the spiritual abode. The forms for humans are tāmasī, rājasī and sāttvikī. The form of Kṛñṇa, filled with knowledge and bliss, resides in bhakti-yoga, which is also full of eternity, knowledge and bliss.**

**Комментарий:**

The form which sometimes appears in the world and then disappears resides in its own abode, called Goloka, a special manifestation of Śrī-vṛndāvana (sva-pade). “Living in his own abode” (sva-pade tiñṭhati) means that he lives in his abode which is most attractive, manifested with all perfection. Similarly the other eleven forms like Raudrī have individual forms: the first nine are covered with particularly guṇas (according to the particular worshipper) and the other two (tenth and eleventh - that residing in the spiritual sky and that which is invisible) are without covering of the guṇas. Kṛñṇa describes this place in Gautamīya-tantra:

*idaṁ vṛndāvanaṁ ramyaṁ mama dhāmaiva kevalam \|*  
*pañcayojanam evāsti vanaṁ me deha-rūpakam \|\|*  
*kalindīyaṁ suñumṇākhyā paramāmṛtavāhinī \|*  
*tatra devāś ca bhūtāni vartante sūkñma-rūpataḥ \|\|*  
*sarvadevamayaś cāhaṁ na tyajāmi vanaṁ kvacit \|*  
*āvirbhāvas tirobhāvo bhaven me’tra yuge yuge \| tejo*  
*mayam idaṁ ramyam adṛśyaṁ carma-cakñuñā \|\| iti \|*

**This pleasant Vṛndāvana is my only abode. This place, measuring five yojanas, is non-different from my body. The Yamunā, flowing with the sweetest nectar, is the suñumnā (central nerve) of that body. All the devās and the elements exist there in a spiritual form. I, the embodiment of all devatās do not leave that forest for a moment, though I appear and disappear in this world age after age. But this radiant forest full of pleasure is invisible to the material eye.**

*ānanda-cin-maya-rasa-pratibhāvitābhis*  
*tābhir ya eva nija-rūpatayā kalābhiḥ \|*  
*goloka eva nivasaty akhilātma-bhūto*  
*govindam ādi-puruñaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi \|\|*

**I worship the Supreme Lord Govinda who, though regarding all the inhabitants as his very self, resides in Goloka exclusively with the young gopīs, who are embodiments of madhurarasa. He accepts them as his wives, while they respond to his conjugal affection reciprocally. (Brahma-saṁhitā** [**5.37**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bs/37)**)**

Though Kṛñṇa has the supreme form, that form is manifested according to the bhāva of the particular worshipper.

That was shown by the Lord to the people of Gokula when he lifted them out of Brahma-hrada:

*darśayāmāsa lokaṁ svaṁ gopānāṁ tamasaḥ param:*

**He showed them his own planet beyond matter, undecaying and unchanging, composed only of bliss. (SB** [**10.28.14**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/28.14)**)**

That is what is meant by stating that the form resides in its own abode.

The twelfth form for humans is of three types. That is mentioned in the verse. This follows the principle that the same Lord appears differently according to the bhāva of the devotee as in the verse mallānām aśanir. (SB [10.43.17](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/43.17))

The various forms of the Lord appear through pure bhakti for the inhabitants of his spiritual abode. That has been discussed. Now the form of that pure bhakti itself is indicated. Kṛñṇa has a body (ghanaḥ) completely composed of knowledge (jñāna), opposite of dull matter, endowed with special attributes and form (vi). Or he is the very form of knowledge. And he is the very form of bliss, the opposite of suffering (ānanda-ghanaḥ). That Kṛñṇa manifests in bhakti which is full of eternity, knowledge and bliss.

### 2.78

*oṁ tat prāṇātmane*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa, the praṇava. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. Therefore I offer myself to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

The question “How do they worship?” is answered by giving mantras which represent the worship. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of the air called prāṇa, the antaryāmī which is represented by praṇava, with waking, dream, deep sleep states. Oṁ expresses agreement. I offer respects to the three worlds, bhūh, bhuvar and svaḥ, which are the powers of his svarūpa (tat sat). Therefore I offer respects to him: I offer myself to him in surrender.

### 2.79

*oṁ śrī-kṛñṇāya govindāya gopī-jana-vallabhāya*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to Kṛñṇa, Govinda, dear to the gopīs.**

**Комментарий:**

Who is that antaryāmī of prāṇa? This is clearly stated. Bhagavadgītā says visṭabhyāham idam; kṛstnam ekāṁśena sthito jagat: I pervade everything in the universe by one of my portions. (BG [10.42](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/10.42))

### 2.80

*oṁ apānātmane*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas tasmai*  
*vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the antaryāmī of the apāna air. I offer respects to the antaryāmī whose power is expressed in the three worlds. Therefore I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

This, like the previous verse, is easy to understand.

### 2.81

*oṁ kṛñṇāya rāmāya pradyumnāyāniruddhāya \|*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Kṛñṇa, to Balarāma, to Pradyumna and to Aniruddha. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

### 2.82

*oṁ vyānātmane*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas tasmai*  
*vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the antaryāmī of the vyāna air. I offer respects to the antaryāmī whose power is expressed in the three worlds. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

### 2.83

*oṁ śrī-kṛñṇāya rāmāya*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Kṛñṇa and Balarāma. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

### 2.84

*oṁ udānātmane*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the antaryāmī of the udāna air. I offer respects to the antaryāmī whose power is expressed in the three worlds. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

### 2.85

*oṁ kṛñṇāya devakī-nandanāya*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Kṛñṇa, the son of Devakī. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

Since prāṇa is the chief air situated in the heart and vyāna pervades everywhere, these airs are linked to the chief names of Kṛñṇa, whereas the other airs (apāna and udāna), being secondary, are linked with secondary names (Pradyumna, Aniruddha, Devakīnandana).

### 2.86

*oṁ samānātmane*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the antaryāmī of the samāna air. I offer respects to the antaryāmī whose power is expressed in the three worlds. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

### 2.87

*oṁ gopālāya nija-svarūpāya*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Kṛñṇa, the cowherd, in his own form. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. Offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

Gopāla, the cowherd, should be understood to be the chief form since that is called “his own form” (nija-svarūpaya) in this verse. Brahmā prays to him as the chief form:

*naumīḍya te’bhra-vapuñe taḍid-ambarāya*  
*guñjāvataṁsa-paripicchala-san-mukhāya \|*  
*vanya-sraje kavala-vetra-viñāṇa-veṇu*  
*lakñma-śriye mṛdupade paśupāṅgajāya \|\|*

**O worshipable Lord! I offer my obeisances to you to attain you, Nanda’s son, having a body the complexion of a rain cloud, wearing cloth flashing like lightning, with face shining among peacock feathers and guñja berry decorations, with colorful forest garland, and possessing soft feet, his beauty enhanced by yogurt rice, stick, horn and flute. (SB** [**10.14.1**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/14.1)**)**

### 2.88

*oṁ yo’sau pradhānātmā gopālaḥ*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the cowherd, who is the antaryāmī of prakṛti. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

### 2.89

*oṁ yo’sāv indriyātmā gopālaḥ*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the cowherd, who is the antaryāmī of the senses. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

### 2.90

*oṁ yo’sau bhūtātmā gopālaḥ*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the cowherd, who is the antaryāmī of the five elements. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

Bhūtātmā means the antaryāmī of the five great elements.

### 2.91

*oṁ yo’sāvuttama-puruño gopālaḥ*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The cowherd is the supreme person. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

“Supreme person” means that the Lord is superior to the jīva.

*uttamaḥ puruñas tv anyaḥ paramātmety udāhṛtaḥ \|*  
*yo loka trayam āviśya bibharty avyaya īśvaraḥ \|\|*

**The highest person is different from this. He is described in the scriptures as the Paramātmā, who, by entering the three worlds, supports the living entities, as the unchanging controller. (BG** [**15.17**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/15.17)**)**

### 2.92

*oṁ yo’sau paraṁ brahma gopālaḥ*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The cowherd is Parabrahman, a person. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

Brahman is impersonal, without qualities. Parabrahma is personal, with qualities, the basis of the impersonal Brahman. Brahmano hi pratiñṭāham: I am the basis of impersonal Brahman. (BG [14.27](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/14.27))

### 2.93

*oṁ yo’sau sarva-bhūtātmā gopālaḥ*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The cowherd is the antaryāmī of all beings. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

Kṛñṇa is the antaryāmī of all beings. One after the other, it has been stated that Kṛñṇa is the controller of all these items.

### 2.94

*oṁ yo’sau jāgrat-svapna-susuptim atītya turyātīto gopālaḥ*  
*oṁ tat sad bhūr bhuvaḥ svas*  
*tasmai vai prāṇātmane namo namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The cowherd boy is beyond the fourth state and beyond the states of waking, dreaming and deep sleep. I offer respects to the three worlds, which represent his power. I offer respects to the antaryāmī of prāṇa.**

**Комментарий:**

Turya is described as follows, and is called Vāsudeva:

*virāḍ hiraṇyagarbhaś ca kāraṇaṁ cety upādhayaḥ \|*  
*īśasya yat tribhir hīnaṁ turīyaṁ tat-padaṁ viduḥ \|\|*

**The Lord’s universal form, his Hiraṇyagarbha form, and māyā are all coverings, but the fourth state which is not covered by these three is known as turīya. (Bhāvārtha-dīpika 11.15.16) Kṛñṇa is beyond that fourth state called Vāsudeva.**

### 2.95

*eko devaḥ sarva-bhūteñu gūḍhaḥ*  
*sarva-vyāpī sarva-bhūtāntarātmā \|*  
*karmādhyakñaḥ sarva-bhūtādhivāsaḥ*  
*sākñī cetā kevalo nirguṇaś ca \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I alone have entered all beings. I am all -pervading. I am the antaryāmī of all beings. I am the controller of karma. I am the basis of all beings. I am the witness and the giver of consciousness. I am the possessor of śakti. I am beyond the guṇas.**

**Комментарий:**

Nārāyaṇa teaches Brahmā “I am the guru.” I alone (ekaḥ) have entered (gūḍhaḥ) all beings. Tat sṛñṭvā tad evānupraviśat: having created everything, the Lord entered everything. (Taittirīya Upaniñad [2.6.2](https://vedarama.com/en/books/taittiriya/2.6.2)) However the Lord does not just enter and do nothing else: the Lord is all-pervading (sarva-vyāpī). The Lord is not pervasive like ether however: he is the conscious monitor of all beings (sarvabhūtāntarātmā). The Mīmāṁsa conclusion that action independently gives results is rejected: the Lord is the giver of the results of action (karmādhyakñaḥ). Phalam ata upapatter: the Lord gives the result, because of evidence. (Brahma-sūtra [3.2.39](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vs/3.2.39)) The Nyāya view that īśvara needs the assistance of matter is refuted: the Lord is the basis of all things (sarva-bhūtādivāsaḥ). This means that he is also the material cause. Prakṛtiś ca pratijñā-dṛñṭāntānuparodhād: the Lord is the upādāna or material cause since there are statements and examples of this. (Brahma-sūtra [1.4.23](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vs/1.4.23)) That the Lord is the material cause by transformation is refuted: the Lord remains the witness (sākñī). This means that he is without change in spite of being the material cause. (This is inconceivable but possible because) śrutes tu śabda-mūlatvād: The statements of scripture are the root of real knowledge. (Brahma-sūtra [2.1.27](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vs/2.1.27))

Though the Lord is cit in his essential form, he also possesses the cic-cakti by which he operates as the giver of consciousness (cetā). Cetāḥ comes from cetas, from the root cet, to become conscious. This is according to sarva-dhātubhyo’sun. (Uṇādi-sūtra 631) Īkñater nāśabdam: because it is seen in the scriptures, the Lord is not beyond description. (Brahma-sūtra [1.1.5](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vs/1.1.5)) The Lord is the possessor of śakti (which are the cause of his qualities that are described) by his very nature. This is explained by the word kevalaḥ (one’s own) modifying cetā. Prakāśāśrayavad vā tejastvāt: because the Lord is effulgent (conscious) he is like a source of light. (Brahma-sūtra [3.2.29](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vs/3.2.29))

*na tasya kāryaṁ karaṇaṁ ca vidyate*  
*na tatsamaś cābhyadhikaś ca dṛśyate*  
*parāsya śaktir vividhaiva śrūyate*  
*svābhāvikī jñānabalakriyā ca \|\|*

**He has no body and no senses. No one is equal to him or higher. His supreme power is manifold. His actions are naturally revealed with knowledge and strength. (Śvetāśvatara Upaniñad)**

Possession of śakti as his nature is also expressed with nirguṇaḥ: the Lord has no material guṇas since he impels the guṇas to action.

### 2.96

*rudrāya namaḥ \| ādityāya namaḥ \| vināyakāya namaḥ \| sūryāya namaḥ \|*  
*vidyāyai namaḥ \| indrāya namaḥ \| agnaye namaḥ \|*  
*yamāya namaḥ \| nirṛtaye namaḥ \| vāyave namaḥ \| kuberāya namaḥ \|*  
*īśānāya namaḥ \| brahmaṇe namaḥ \| sarvebhyo devebhyo namaḥ \|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to Rudra. I offer respects to Āditya. I offer respects to Vināyaka. I offer respects to Sūrya. I offer respects to Vidyā. I offer respects to Indra. I offer respects to Agni. I offer respects to Yama. I offer respects to Nirṛti. I offer respects to Vāyu. I offer respects to Kubera. I offer respects to Īśāna. I offer respects to Brahmā. I offer respects to all devatās.**

**Комментарий:**

Nārāyaṇa teaches about his vibhūtis by offering them respects in fifteen mantras.

### 2.97

*dattvā stutiṁ puṇyatamāṁ brahmaṇe sva-svarūpiṇe \|*  
*kartṛtvaṁ sarva-bhūtānām antardhāne babhūva saḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Nārāyaṇa gave Brahmā within whom he had entered this most pure scripture and the ability to create all beings and then disappeared.**

**Комментарий:**

This is a summary. Giving to Brahmā—in whom Nārāyaṇa had entered with his own form (sva-svarūpiṇe) — the most pure verses—and, giving him creatorship of all beings, Nārāyaṇa disappeared.

### 2.98

*brahmaṇe brahma-putrebhyo nāradāya ca yathā-śrutam \|*  
*tathā proktas tu gāndharvi gacchadhvaṁ svālayāntikam \|\| iti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I taught this knowledge to Brahmā. Brahmā’s sons heard from Brahmā. From them Nārada heard. I heard from him and have spoken it to you. O Gāndharvī! Go to a place near your house!**

**Комментарий:**

In order to give authority to his teachings, Durvāsā recites his sampradāya. One version has nāradā instead of nāradāya. This indicates the ablative case according to the rule supāṁ suluk pūrvasavarṇācche-ñāḍāḍyāñājālaḥ (Pāṇinī 7.1.39) There is another version with tathā-śrutam but the meaning is yathā-śrutam. The second tathā has its usual meaning.From Brahmā the Upaniñad was heard by his sons. From them it was heard by Nārada. From Nārada I heard it. From me, you have heard it. The phrase svālāyāntikam gacchadhvam (go near your house) is used instead of svālāyam to indicate “go to a place near your house, where Kṛñṇa comes out of the forest while herding the cows.”

May my mind take pleasure in Kṛñṇa, who gives joy to the land of Vṛndāvana, who, with his consort Gāndharvī, is the abode of delightful fragrance.

## About Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī

In the Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā (195) it is said that Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī was formerly Vilāsamañjarī gopī. Born in Rāmakeli, he was the nephew of the illustrious brothers Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana, who were the objects of Śrī Caitanya's unlimited mercy. From his very childhood Jīva Gosvāmī was greatly fond of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. He later came to Navadvīpa to study Sanskrit, and, following in the footsteps of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu, he circumambulated the entire Navadvīpa-dhāma. After visiting Navadvīpa-dhāma he went to Benares to study Sanskrit under Madhusūdana Vācaspati, and after finishing his studies in Benares he went to Vṛndāvana and took shelter of his uncles, Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana. This is described in the Bhakti-ratnākara. After the disappearance of Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī and Sanātana Gosvāmī in Vṛndāvana, Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī became the ācārya of all the Vaiṣṇavas in Bengal, Orissa and the rest of the world, and it is he who used to guide them in their devotional service. In Vṛndāvana he established the Rādhā-Dāmodara temple. When Jīva Gosvāmī was still present, Śrīla Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja Gosvāmī compiled his famous Caitanyacaritāmṛta. Later, Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī inspired Śrīnivāsa ācārya, Narottama dāsa ṭhākura and Duḥkhī Kṛṣṇadāsa to preach Kṛṣṇa consciousness in Bengal. Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī awarded the designation Kavirāja to Rāmacandra Sena, a disciple of Śrīnivāsa ācārya’s, and to Rāmacandra’s younger brother Govinda. His disciple Kṛṣṇadāsa Adhikārī listed all the books of the Gosvāmīs in his diary.

In the year 1476 Śakābda (A.D. 1554)Sanātana Gosvāmī completed the Bṛhad-vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī commentary on Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. In the year 1504 Śakābda (A.D. 1582) Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī published an edited version of the Bṛhad-vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī named Laghu-toṣaṇī.
