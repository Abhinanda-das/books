# Govinda Dāsera Kaḍacā \[BS\]

## Govinda Dāsera Kaḍacā

My birth place is Kāñcana Nagara in Barddhamān. My father’s name is Śyāmā dāsa. My name is Govinda. Caste is blacksmith (kāmāra), maker of weapons, utensils, chains and vehicles. My mother’s name is Mādhavī.

My wife’s name is Śaśi-mukhī. One day she quarreled with me and spoke roughly. Calling me a fool with no good qualities, she rebuked me.

In the morning I left the house because of that offense. In 1430 śāka, I left. I did not want to go back as I was filled with anger and pride.

Eventually I came to Katwa. Arriving there I heard the name of Śrī Caitanya.

Everyone praised him. Hearing that praise, I hurried to see him.

Travelling all day, passing many fields, in the morning, crossing the Gaṅgā, I came to a ghāṭa on the river. Below Nadīyā, the Gaṅgā is called Miśra-ghāṭa. Seeing the capital of Nadīyā, I was filled with bliss. On the right the Sarasvatī river sang, giving bliss to all, in sweet voices.

Above the ghāṭa was Śrivāsa aṅgana. There was a broad canal nearby. The dilapidated house of King Ballālal was also close by. I thought of eating, staying at the banyan tree at the ghāṭa. At that moment Śrī Caitanya came to that place.

He had a cloth tied around his waist and had an astonishing form. With him, there was Nityānanda avadhūta with a smiling face. Dancing with three or four devotees Caitanya descended into the Gaṅgā. The brave avadhūta jumped into the water from the bank, and, swimming about, played in the water.

Later, Śrīvāsa, Dāmodara, Siddha Haridāsa and, to his left, Gadādhara, and finally Advaitācārya came. I have never seen anyone as effulgent as Advaita.

He had full head of hair and a large attractive beard, which hung down, just above his chest. Nityānanda became absorbed in shouting the name of the Lord while swimming to the other shores. Everyone jumped into the Gaṅgā one by one and swam, playing about.

I began to examine the astonishing form of Caitanya. I was entranced by the glow of his form. After bathing, he climbed on the bank. His curly hair hung down his back. His limbs were the color of pure gold. His eyes were long like blue lotus petals. He had beautiful cheeks and a fine forehead. He walked easily, like an actor in a play.

His thighs defeated the beauty of banana trunks. His eyes supported two attractive eyebrows. His feet were as if stained with lac, giving joy to the eyes of a sage. His body was filled with prema. He uttered the name of the Lord. He mercifully embraced whoever he saw. Saying the Lord’s name tears fell from his eyes. Tears shot from his eyes like water from a syringe.

Gaurāṅga began to walk. Nityānanda followed behind. One person introducing himself as the head of the district, introduced others.

I drowned in an ocean of bliss on seeing the water pastimes. Speaking to others, the Lord came to Śacī’s house. Her heart was longing to see him.

On the pretext of giving up my house, by good fortune, I quickly came to Navadvīpa dhāma. At the ghāṭa, I saw such pastimes. I do not know what emotions rose in my mind.

My hairs stood on end, so that my limbs were like kadamba flowers. Perspiration soaked my cloth. My tears of desire covered my feet.

The Lord came with smiling face, hair falling down his back. Seeing me by good fortune, he began speaking to me again and again. I came slowly. Turning limp, I fell on the ground, holding his feet. At his feet, my voice choked up. Merciful Nimāi held my hand and made me sit.

Sitting with folded hands in front of him, I wept. Saying a few words and laughing, sweet streams flowed constantly from him. My heart was enchanted with the fragrance of his limbs. His thin body was mad with chanting the Lord’s name. Several devotees with choked voices were at his feet.

How can I describe my emotions? Millions of devatās came and rolled at his feet. Even though the Lord was standing in a dark room, the effulgence of his body destroyed the darkness. It seems the moon in a solitary place had somehow been kneaded into a paste. Anyone who sees this will become detached completely from material life.

Laughing, he spoke to me. He carefully asked my name. He asked, “What is your caste? What is your name? What is your work? Where do you live?”

Hearing his words, I folded my hands and began to speak to him.

“Why do you show such mercy to me? I am most fallen. My name is Govinda Dāsa. I was a householder and did various jobs in the house. But I have become a beggar on the road. Giving up material enjoyment, I have come to see you. O Lord! Give me a place at your red lotus feet. I live in Kātwa, Barddhamān. My father’s name is Śyāmādāsa, a blacksmith.”

Hearing these words, the Lord said to me, “O Govinda! Stay at my house. You will be protected at my house. Every day you can do nāma-saṅkīrtana in joy. Every day you will take Kṛṣṇa’s prasāda in joy. Your mind will be completely satisfied.

You will be constantly engaged in actions of service, bringing and offering Gaṅgā water and tulasī leaves. You will fill your stomach with prasāda daily, with sauces, spinach, sukutā and banana flower.

Saying this, the Lord wanted to take me with him. I went to the Lord’s house. There was a most attractive house over the Gaṅgā, with five rooms, beautiful to behold. The Lord’s house was at the southern edge of the town.

The Lord was always intoxicated in chanting the holy name. Nearby was a huge pond. Some called it Ballāla-sāgara.

I will tell all the names of the devotees who were always with the Lord. They were Advaita Ācārya, Svarūpa, Śrīvāsa, as well as Advaita’s two sons Acyuta and Kṛṣṇa-dāsa as well as Mukunda, Murāri Gupta, Gadādhara, Narahari, Vidyānidhi, Candraśekharācārya and Śrīdhara.

The Lord would do private bhajana with several intimate devotees. Nityānanda, like a madman, would constantly weep with choked voice. Śacī, the personification of peacefulness, with short stature, would constantly call out, “Nimāi, Nimāi!”

Viṣṇu-priyā was the Lord’s wife. She was busy day and night serving the Lord. She was shy, polite and spoke softly. I went to serve her feet. Staying in Śacī’s house in this way, I cannot describe all the work I did there.

Skillful at eating, I ate in joy. Finishing work, I would walk around with him. Every day there was offering in Viṣṇu’s temple consisting of fruit, roots, cheese, butter, condensed milk, spinach, soup, yogurt, sūktā, modaka, sweet rice, big lāḍḍus, and miṣṭaka, delicious to eat.

Everyday, Śacīmātā cooked and everyone one ate prasāda in bliss. I am the servant of the king of gluttons. For twelve months, I ate from the merciful Lord’s plate. What can I say? There is no comparison with this prasāda of which one morsel is equal to nectar.

In this way I stayed in the Lord’s house. Everyone mercifully called me “the servant of Caitanya.” The master of masters is my Caitanya. I would go everywhere with him.

His heart was always overwhelmed with attachment to Kṛṣṇa. On hearing Kṛṣṇa’s name, he would cry. If someone said “Rādhe!” in a loud voice, immediately he would weep profusely. If someone said, “My life is Kṛṣṇa” he would come running and embrace him.

One day in the evening, at Śrīvāsa aṅgana the Lord sat with the devotees. At that time Nityānanda began shouting repeatedly, “Yamunā! This is the place of the rāsa dance. This is the ghāṭa on the Yamunā. Where are the hundreds of gopīs and where is Kṛṣṇa?”

Hearing Nityānanda’s words, the Lord quickly came running and jumped into Ballāla-sāgara. Overflowing with love, the Lord began swimming while the devotees stood and watched.

In this way, daily the Lord’s anurāga increased. From the intense prema his body grew thin. Merciful Caitanya was not satisfied. He said, “Taking sannyāsa I will teach the jīvas. With grass in my teeth, I will travel to all villages. Giving the name of the Lord, I will deliver all jīvas. Giving up family life, I will go to Katwa. Keśava Bharatī guru will deliver me. I will not take sannyāsa in a noisy house. Otherwise, how will I deliver all jīvas?”

The Lord desired to take sannyāsa. My heart was most disturbed on hearing this. The Lord called Nityānanda and spoke. “I will take sannyāsa. Do not prevent me. In pious Māgha month during uttarāyaṇa I will take sannyāsa. Keep this secret. Say this to Mukunda and Gadādhara. Do not broadcast this news everywhere. Indicate this to Śacī and do not spread it among the devotees.”

Hearing this, all the servants’ hearts seemed to split into two. “Let me die. I cannot endure the suffering. My master Gaurāṅga will take sannyāsa. He will take sannyāsa. My heart will break into hundred pieces. How can I work? I am lighter than a blade of grass. A hundred thunderbolts should fall on my head. How can I tolerate separation from the Lord? How will I pass time without Caitanya?”

The Lord then rose and went to Mukunda. I went along, weeping. Because of the tears, I could not see the road. He called Mukunda and spoke. “I will accept the daṇḍa and water pot. Giving up śikhā and thread, I will accept sannyāsa. If I do not do this, how will I deliver the jīvas?”

Hearing this, Mukunda began crying. Tears flooded his broad chest. He fell on the ground. The Lord took his hand, raised him, and made him sit.

“O merciful Lord! My life departs. What did you make me hear? Hearing your words, my head breaks from misfortune. Spreading the holy name for a few days, I also will leave the house and take sannyāsa.”

Hearing this, the Lord went to Gadādhara and spoke the truth. Hearing his words, Gadādhara shouted. The sky split and fell on his head. He fell on the ground limply. His body became red with streams of blood. Recovering and sitting up, Gadādhara spoke.

“Bring poison. I will drink it immediately. How will I bear separation from the Lord? The pain of being bitten by a million scorpions is insignificant compared to this. If Nimāi, my life, renounces, I will go with him because of my attachment. When Murāri and other devotees hear this, their minds will become vacant.”

“What is the use of the body if Caitanya leaves? At that moment a thunderbolt will strike our heads.”

Then Gadādhara held his feet and began to speak while showering tears. “When your mother hears this, how will she maintain the life air in her body?”

When the other devotees heard this from each other, they began to speak with agitation. Someone said, “A million scorpions bite my body.” Someone said, “My life burns in fire.” Someone fell from a standing position like a cut down tree. Someone fell backwards with clenched teeth.

Hearing all this, my Viśvambara began to inform everyone in detail. The Lord went to the devotees and spoke sweet words to them all. Then, on hearing the news, Śacī fell down unconscious, as if insane. She cried loudly, shedding tears upon her chest, covering it completely. “O child! O Nimāi! Where will you go?”

Hearing her lamentation, the birds and animals began weeping. Then Nityānanda entered the Lord’s yard and heard the news with Śacī. The words like a thunderbolt split her heart.

She fell on the ground with force. Beating her chest, crying at the top of her voice, she turned the ground into mud with her tears. Hearing this news whispered by others, Viṣṇu-priyā sat down, putting her hands on her head. Tears flowed over her chest. It seemed that an ocean of lamentation arose. Gaurāṅga did not glance at her, but went to Śrīvāsa aṅgana. There at Śrīvāsa’s house, he began a huge saṅkīrtana and became unconscious. Overwhelmed by the kīrtana, he began dancing and his clothing fell off.

His whole body became like a kadamba flower. My dharma-vīra danced in an unconscious state. Blood oozed from his hair pores. His limbs became injured as he fell down.

Telling them to dance, wearing a garland and clapping his hands, he danced with the devotees. On the evening of the Pauṣa saṅkrānti, the Lord returned to his own house. Many people came and went and Śacī’s lamentation increased. Saying sweet words to his mother, he came to the kitchen and met her. At two prahāras in the night the Lord ate and slept. I went to my place to rest, but at the order of the Lord woke up.

At the end of night, the merciful Lord suddenly went outside and called me. “Wait here and be ready,” he said. “Bidding farewell to my mother’s feet, I will come.” Saying this he went inside, again came out and called me.

Disturbed, he said to me, “Come with me. I am going to Katwa to cut my bondage. Do not say this here and there. Taking sannyāsa I will deliver the jīvas. The jīvas of Kali-yuga are selfish, sinful, drink intoxicants and eat meat. Tell me, what is the method for them? Dedicated to their genital and belly, devoid of steadiness, wanting money, they constantly lie. Like worms in the womb, they devour the saliva from young women’s mouths. They reject the streams of nectar of bhakti. They follow prostitutes and like their food. They are absorbed in lustful acts, desiring money and women. For this reason, I will give up my śikhā and thread and wander from door to door, giving out the name of the Lord. I will preach the name of the Lord, the mahā-mantra which does not require dīkṣā, at the ghāṭa and on the road. Outcastes, youths, householders, children, elders and women, becoming intoxicated with the name, will stand in lines. Young girls and young boys will chant the name. Pāṣaṇḍās, on the path to hell, will become intoxicated with the name. The flag of the name will fly, piercing the sky. Kings and citizens will become choked up together. If I do not accept sannyāsa and the kaupīna, how will I deliver the sinful, suffering and miserable people? Seeing the contaminated condition of the jīva’s of Kali-yuga, my heart trembles and cannot tolerate it. Accepting the water pot and kaupīna, I will take sannyāsa. Chanting the names of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa I will deliver everyone. My heart breaks always for the sinful, suffering people.”

As he spoke with me in this way, Śacī devī appeared at that time. In a confused hurry, she came outside and stood facing him, her hands spread out. The Lord held his mother’s feet, bid farewell, and left. Standing at the door, his mother cried.

I followed behind him carrying his wooden shoes. Śacī stood like wooden doll, shedding tears constantly. Leaving by the door, the Lord crossed the Gaṅgā and left. Crossing, he went to Katwa. I followed to perform services.

Whatever astonishing pastimes I saw I recorded as a chronicle and keep according to my ability. We arrived at Katwa in the evening. Cymbals, conches, and bells resounded in every house. In the evening, he saw all the devotees—Mukunda, Candraśekhara, Nityānanda, Brahmānanda, Gadādhara, Gaṅga-dāsa, his teacher, and the singer Śiva. At night filled with prema, Gaurāṅga began dancing while chanting. Thousands of people came to see. Seeing the bhakti for Kṛṣṇa, everyone was astonished.

Some gave me fruits and others gave garlands. From the Lord’s form, light emanated in all directions. Million Cupids could not be compared to his beauty. The movements of his waist conquered the middle of a ḍamaru drum.

Wherever his eyes glanced there were showers of blue lotuses. He was tall, with arms extending to his knees. With grass in my teeth, I desire dāsya-bhakti to Gaurāṅga. In this way, the night passed in dancing and singing. Seeing many people, Gaurāṅga spoke.

“O brothers! Everyone, pay attention to my words. There is no difference between Kṛṣṇa and Kṛṣṇa’s name. Worship Kṛṣṇa, think of Kṛṣṇa and say Kṛṣṇa’s name. O brothers! By the power of the name, you will go to the eternal dhāma. Everything you see is illusory. In Vedas this is called the shadow of prakṛti. Only a shadow of your desires will remain. When Yama comes, he will squeeze your throat. There is no difference between sleeping on a bed or on the ground. Those who see difference will lament. The person who becomes proud on obtaining material objects becomes an insect in hell according to the scriptures.

“There is no difference between a king and a pauper. O brother! Seeing difference brings about ignorance. A fistful of food fills the king’s belly. A pauper is satisfied with that also. Lying on the ground, one goes to sleep, breathing happily. The king cannot fall asleep on a priceless bed. The king cannot eat gold, diamonds, emeralds or pearls. But intoxicated with wealth, he does not think of this. Just as the pauper will die at his time, the king will go to the house of Yama.

“Māyā binds up the king’s eyes. He is blind, moving like an ox used for grinding. The ox walks around in one place and dies. He does not change his course in a million years. When the ātmārāma becomes liberated, he leaves his material body, like breaking out of a limp cage. When the revered body rots, jackals and dogs eat it and fill their bellies.

“The intoxicated jīva, sinking in dark saṁsāra, suffers and dies, attached to material objects. In ignorance a person accepts a daughter or son, a palace, a pond, a garden, beautiful women, and gold. Sometimes he is someone’s daughter and sometimes someone’s son and sometimes a husband. Rejecting all material conditions, think of Kṛṣṇa. Sons, daughters, wealth, grains are all illusory. The only true entity is spiritual consciousness. Speaking about rotten householders, absorbed in sons and daughters, one becomes exhausted. When one increases material possessions with consultations, one simply obtains great pain like an insect in a root. The form of Kṛṣṇa shines everywhere. A person with pure eyes can see that form.

“The fool sees with material eyes, being attached to material objects. The devotee, eternally liberated, sees with eyes of spiritual knowledge. How can the person whose eyes are blinded by material dust see subtle tattva? Filled with prema, a person sees prema in everyone. “Who knows what is the nature of prema? Is it service to one’s wife? When one sees man and woman as nondifferent, then certainly the tattva of prema will manifest. If prema means longing for children then that is not prema at all. If one longs for the Lord, then certainly the great souls call that prema.

“With attention hear about the tattva of pure prema. A small wave of prema will cool the burning heart. The devotee does not experience the pain that a young woman feels on seeing a young man. For this reason the devotees worship Krsna. They fix their minds on him in the mood of being his wife. For those who feel longing for the ātmārāma, how can lust remain in his mind? Just as darkness does not remain where there is light, lust becomes ash near Kṛṣṇa. Only the longing of prema remains. Saying this, I give healing prema. Yearning for prema, act to attain it. You will be successful and the pain of saṁsāra will go. Life becomes hell by quarrels, argument, hatred and lying, with a duplicitous mind.

“Desiring wealth a householder lies and sometimes cheats and kills other human beings. He speaks rotten household topics and becomes exhausted taking care of sons and daughters. He does not think, “Who do you belong to? Who belongs to you?”

“He becomes busy earning for the material body, a dull lump of matter. He does not see that death is certain. He only thinks that he will live a long time. Or he studies many scriptures, negating matter. All such people become utter fools. The more he studies and becomes intelligent, the more he becomes selfish. He says that other’s wives are like mothers but when alone, he becomes full of lust. For others, lust and anger are enemies but for himself they are friends.

“One can only imagine the unlimited suffering in hell. As long as there is no bhakti to the Lord, such persons stay in hell. One can give up ordinary wealth for oneself, but one can deceive oneself if there are a million coins. The only essence for the jīvas of Kaliyuga is the name of the Lord. Chanting that name, one goes to the eternal dhāma. Always chant the name of the Lord with hairs standing on end. Why cause difficulty in the market of Kali? What is the difference between a palace and a hut? O brother! Ask and see from a learned person. Just as a king sleeps on a bed in a palace, a pauper sleeps in his hut. The king eats and drinks from gold vessels and plates. A man in a hut drinks water from an earthen pot. Both are relieved of thirst.

“The gold plate of the king is just an illusion. He has a crown for soup and jewels for vegetables. Can the king eat diamonds cooked nicely? The king intoxicated with pride cannot see this. Again and again, he comes and goes.”

Caitanya taught in this way. There was a crowd of people. Sitting beneath a bilva tree in Katwa, he gave various instructions in a loud voice. The instructions from his mouth were the essence of the Vedas, hearing which, the jīvas are liberated from saṁsāra.

In this way the day and night passed. The next day at dawn, the Lord took his bath. The women wept, wiping their eyes with the edge of their cloth. They shed torrents of tears.

Someone said “His form takes away all suffering. How can his mother maintain her life? He defeats the pride of a million Cupids. His hair is beauty for the eyes. His locks of hair are beautiful and pure. His lotus eyes conquer the blue lotus. I have never seen such an amazing form. Why should Nimāi wear a kaupīna and carry a daṇḍa? Keśava Bharatī is made of stone. Shaving off his hair is most cruel. The garland on his neck is splendid. The beauty of his face illuminates all directions.” In this way the women spoke.

At that time, the Lord called me skilfully. He said, “Quickly bring the ingredients. I will shave off my hair to accept sannyāsa. I will no longer remain in the house in a state of bondage. There is hellish pain in the house.”

Hearing this talk, Gadādhara, who was śuddha-sattva, Nityānanda and Candraśekhara, brought the various materials suitable for sannyāsa and filled the sannyāsī’s store room. They called and brought a barber named Deva. The barber came and sat under the bilva tree.

Then Caitanya said to the barber, “O Deva! Shave my head. I will go to Vraja.”

Receiving the order from Bhāratī, the barber sat and began shaving off his hair. When the barber finally began cutting off all his hair, the women wept loudly. The women said, “O barber! Do not do this job. Do not cut off his hair.” Saying this, they began weeping. Then the barber began shaving him.

Thousands upon thousands of people came to Katwa to see the sannyāsa ceremony. At the end of the day, with head shaved, Nimāi put on a sannyāsī’s dress. With daṇḍa and water pot in his hands, he wore a kaupīna and red cloth. Standing in front of Bhāratī, Nimāi excellently illuminated the directions with his beauty. Nityānanda, Gadādhara, and Gaṅgā-dāsa stood beside him. Seeing the Lord’s astonishing form, in his mind Bharatī offered respects at his lotus feet. In his mind he said, “O Gosāi, you are the Lord. The universe of moving and nonmoving beings is dependent on you. Wanting to teach the people, thinking of the suffering people, you put on a kaupīna to show the path of bhakti.”

He became a sannyāsī in the afternoon. The women began to ululate. The creepers, leaves, branches and trees floated in prema. Birds, animals and insects began dancing. Thousands of people showered flowers. Katwa became the Nandana garden of Svarga.

Bhāratī gave him the name Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya. Hundreds of thousands of people gathered. The pious women filled their hands with lāja (popped rice) and showered it on his head. The sound of ululation pierced the sky. Everyone became choked up with the taste of bhakti. The name pierced the ether and wandered in the sky. All the devatās heard this and became mad with bliss.

The Lord woke me up in the night. He spent the night chanting japa madly. In the morning, he spoke to Candrasekhara. “You all go to my house in Nadīyā. Go with Brahmānanda to my mother. Tell her that I took sannyāsa. If she cries, immediately pacify her with words.”

Then Nityānanda went with Gadādhara, taking Bhāratī as well, while playing in various ways. I followed behind carrying his wooden shoes. Caitanya was intoxicated with the name. Hundreds of thousands of people followed the Lord. Many scholars came to see the Lord.

Rudradeva, Ramaratna, Jagāi Paṇḍita, Gaṅgā-dāsa, Śambhucandra, famous on earth, Īśāna, Śaṅkara, Balarāma, Gadādhara, Caṇḍacaṇḍeśvara, the best of scholars, Kāśīśvara Nyāyaratna and Siddheśvara and Pañcānana Vedāntika as well as Ratnākara, all great scholars, went along with the Lord.

Mad with prema, Kṛṣṇa Caitanya went joyfully. Dancing constantly, the Lord ran in front. Sometimes he ran and sometimes jumped and fell on the earth. Tears flowed from his eyes. Bhāratī cried on tasting prema. Fully absorbed the Lord went eastwards. He ran to Ācārya’s house, intoxicated with prema. He stayed some time in Ācārya’s house and at that time, Śacī came and was allowed to see him.

Offering respects at his mother’s feet, Lord Kṛṣṇa Caitanya spoke a few words to satisfy her. Saying a few words, escaping māyā, he journeyed south, leaving everyone. Īśāna, Pratāpa, Gaṅgā-dāsa, Gadādhara, and Vaneśvara went with the Lord.

When we came to Barddhamān, he thought what would be best for me. Glancing at me he spoke. His words fell like streams of nectar. Slapping me on the back he spoke.

“O Govinda! Go to your house.” I was astonished. Laughing he went away in an affected way. When the Lord took sannyāsa, I held his kaupīna. Giving up pride, I became humble. I no longer had a desire to engage in saṁsāra. I went with the Lord while dancing. Going on the road, I folded my hands and spoke a few words in response.

“O Lord! I will not go to Kāñcana nagara again. I gave up low family life, considering it to be equal to stool.”

As I said these words, my wife, somehow hearing, had quickly come there. Tears flowing from her eyes, she fell at my feet. With tearful face, she began speaking these words. “Return to the house. I will go with you. You gave the family life because of some ordinary talk. What did you do, making me a maidservant? Where will I go to beg? Who will mercifully give me alms? By what fate will I go to some door and beg, wandering around hungry?”

Hearing her words, I bowed my head. I began to speak eventually in my mind, “Hari Hari.” By remembrance of the Lord, all bondage is cut. I began to think of the Lord’s feet.

Merciful Caitanya then saw me and began speaking sweet words. Hearing the Lord’s words, the suffering woman became soaked with tears and became intelligent. While she cried, glancing in all directions, the Lord spoke words of truth to her. Suddenly hearing the Lord’s words, she wiped her eyes with the cloth as she wept. The Lord’s mercy arose because of her crying. He then turned to me and spoke. He said, “Govinda! Remain in your house. I will go to Purī with another servant.”

Hearing those words, my eyes filled with tears. I held his feet in pain. My tears covered his feet. The Lord turned and left. My neighbors gathered and began speaking, making me confused. I began speaking about the poison of family life. I explained the secret of women. “O brother! Hear the woman’s message. The wife becomes the life of the husband. If you look at a portion of the ātmā, you will see the wife and husband together. Those who think that this is sweeter than nectar close their eyes and drink the wife’s saliva. The rascal who thinks this happiness is eternal bliss passes his life uselessly. Gaurāṅga is the complete eternal Brahman. Giving up his association I will accept family life.”

While saying this, Dāmodara took me to Kāśīmitra’s house. Kāśīmitra was a most pious person. The Lord went and stayed in his house. Arranging the meal, Mitra offered fine rice. Seeing the rice, the Lord praised it. The Lord said, “This rice is very fine. What do people call this rice?”

Mitra said, “It is called Jagannātha-bhoga. If one prepares a meal from it, all desires are fulfilled.” Hearing the word “Jagannāthabhoga” the Lord was astonished. Immediately waves of prema began to flow. Weeping he said, “O Jagannātha! Quickly bring me to you and accept me.” Cooking spinach, soup and various items, putting them together, the Lord was intoxicated with bliss. The directions became fragrant with the aroma of beto-śāka. My mind became unsteady, with the food being offered.

The Lord said, “Quickly bring tulasī. Make the offering, I will energetically distribute prasāda. Your child here is very hungry. He glances around many times.”

I felt most ashamed at the Lord’s words. I lowered my head. After the offering, he distributed prasāda. The life air was satisfied with sūktā and cooked vegetables. I ate eight pieces of fried bitter melon happily. I took big mouthfuls of food. Cūkra āmla became like nectar with raw sugar. Eating plenty, my face became filled with bliss.

Gaurāṅga left Mitra’s house in the afternoon. He ran to the south, into a trap of prema. Eventually I came to the village of Hājipura. Chanting the name, the Lord made the village insane. Outside the village there was a huge banyan tree. I sat there gently and in the evening the Lord began saṅkīrtana. The name pierced the ether and rose in the sky. The Lord began dancing, intoxicating the place. Where was his kaupīna and cord? His clothing became disheveled. Sometimes he fell with force on the ground. Saliva flowed from his mouth here and there and his voice choked up. Hundreds of people joined and became insane with nāma-saṅkīrtana. All the men and women became mad with the name. By good fortune, that was the excellence of the name’s power. Small boys and girls, elders, as well as youths and young women clapped their hands and danced with devotion.

Half the night passed in this way. The Lord then cooked the begged rice. One devotee of the village brought ghee. The Lord fried my bitter melon with ghee. My king of Nadīyā offered nīma sūktā with ghee and fried bitter melon. The Lord ate only a fistful of prasāda. Then I sat down with a plate. The Lord filled my plate with prasāda. Fidgeting, I took the prasāda. When I filled up my stomach and stood up, I went to the Lord’s feet and surrendered.

The Lord touched my stomach and immediately my stomach became normal. I then shouted “Hari Hari.” The devotees were most astonished. The next day at dawn, beautiful Gaurāṅga got up, called the devotees and spoke in detail. He took permission from the devotees to leave. With his associates, he then left, running. When he reached Medinīpura, hearing the news, people came running.

Among them one wealthy person approached. He was speechless on seeing the form of the Lord. Named Keśava Sāmanta, he was very rich. On various pretexts he spoke on various topics.

Laughing, he said, “O best of sannyāsīs! Take some money, that your heart desires. Give up the kaupīna and wear clothing. Why does a young man take sannyāsa? O yogī! Be happy serving your senses. Why do you die, serving renunciation?”

Hearing the wealthy man’s words, the Lord slightly smiled and with humility gave instructions. He spoke. “Scriptures say that money, gold, and emeralds are transformations of earth. The transformations of earth will always become earth again. Why are you puffed up with pride? Do not step into the Lord’s trap of māyā. If you do so, you will repeatedly have to die. The person who wants to die repeatedly never gives up the bondage of māyā. Giving up everything, a person should worship with devotion. Then the highest bliss will arise in his mind.

“Wandering about thinking, ‘This is mine,’ one does not know one is entering the mouth of death. When death chews you up in his teeth, how many beautiful women will stay with you? How much gold, silver and wealth do you have at that time? How many milk sweets remain at that time? Your respected body will be burned to ashes. If the body does not rot, jackals will eat it. Your head will be mixed with a shoemaker’s stool.

“Worship Kṛṣṇa. Chant his name. Useless time will go away. Why are you proud of all these temporary things? The eternal entity is Kṛṣṇa. He can bring peace. O wealthy man! Hear my words. Can you eat diamonds, pearls and emeralds? One fistful of food allays hunger. Then why are you so proud all the time?”

In this way, the Lord instructed the wealthy man. Saying a few words, glancing at me. “Let us go to Nārāyaṇa-gaḍa. Going there, we will be happy.” Saying only this, he got up quickly and I put the wooden shoes on my shoulders.

Gaurāṅga was absorbed in bliss on the road. In the evening, we came to that place. At Nārāyaṇa-gaḍa, there was a Śiva-dhaleśvara. He ran to see the deity. This was the village deity at Nārāyaṇagaḍa.

The Lord began to weep. He said in a loud voice, “Hara, Hara!” and fell on the ground. He became choked up with prema. His clothing and water pot fell away. Great sāttvika-bhāvas manifested. His hairs stood on end in prema and blood oozed out. His outer garment and kaupīna fell off. Some sannyāsīs as well as vānaprasthas and brahmacārīs came there to see the bhāva of the Lord, the Lord of Nadīyā.

They were all astonished on seeing the prema and bhakti. They fell on the ground saying that he was a devatā. Chanting the Lord’s name, the Lord began to dance. Seeing his great prema, everyone’s hairs stood on end. Everyone danced under a tree. I, most sinful and low, seeing some lāḍḍus, wanted them. I developed greed to eat them and fill my stomach. The Lord, antaryāmī, understanding my intentions, being merciful, gave me lāḍḍus to eat. Eating four or five lāḍḍus, my stomach became full.

One brāhmaṇa brought and offered cool water. Gradually all the people of the village, hearing the news, gathered at that place. Offering bhoga, the Lord divided the prasāda. Assembling, everyone took prasāda. Seeing the devotion when they took prasāda, I felt strong emotion. I was greedy, and first filled my stomach. Then I became repentant in my mind. Wanting to destroy my sins, I held the Lord’s feet. Saying various words, he placed his foot on my head. The confusion in my mind immediately dissipated.

Then the Lord danced with absorption. Everyone danced, intoxicated with the rasa of the Lord. Some danced and some rolled on the ground. Someone ran to a tree, saying, “Kṛṣṇa!” Gradually many people gathered at that place.

Men and women, young and old, everyone listened to the words of the new sannyāsī. One by one they came and sat at that place. Vīreśvara Sena and Bhavānī Śaṅkara came with many people to see the Lord. Four groups of horses and elephants and many vehicles gathered before the Lord. Bhavānī Śaṅkara was a very wealthy man. He came with hundreds of people on the back of an elephant with drums and colorful flags. He made the four directions beautiful.

They were all intoxicated with pride, insects of material enjoyment. Seeing this, the Lord was merciful within. Seeing their condition, merciful Caitanya made them fortunate, giving them bhakti. Giving them teachings on bhakti, the Lord made them intoxicated. Hundreds of thousands of people became like dolls. With grass in his teeth, folding his hands, the Lord spoke.

“Everyone! Hear my ordinary words. Listen, all wealthy people! The world is like a gypsy’s magic. Overwhelmed by sleep, you climb on a throne and think you are a king. You consider hundreds of people to be your friends. Hundreds of thousands of citizens come and offer gifts. You never think what is all this? this is a shadow of a reflection, ignorance in the material world. The material world is a reflection of kṛṣṇa-tattva. The Vedas say it is a reflected dream. Understand there are two dreams. The only difference is that one is while sleeping and the other is while in a waking state. Being a king is a dream in the waking state. The Vedas say that this is really an illusion.

“Gold, silver, gems, and pearls are transformations of earth. The entity to be respected is Kṛṣṇa. This is the real meaning. The Vedas say that Bhagavān is the eternal entity. Everything else you see is false. How can the person who is sinking in water see the entity on the bank? If you come out of the water to the bank, then you can see the object up close. How can the person absorbed in material objects see Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa? The person whose eyes are covered by the blinkers of māyā is always blind, like a bull at the grinding wheel.

“Who knows what is in the mountain cave? Who knows from outside what is the truth? In this way, who can understand in a practical way the subtle nature of the material world? The form of the Lord is the unlimited universe. The person who understands this gives up karma-kāṇḍa. When you give up material thinking and become spiritual, you can see the form of Kṛṣṇa.

“The independent will of Kṛṣṇa has given śakti to the material world. The person who has bhakti can see this. Matter and the jīva are tied together. One who crosses the shore is freed from rajas and tamas. Who can cross the condition of the material world? One remains rotating like an ox at the oil mill. The ox is on a small narrow path going in a circle and falls into difficulty. Like the ox that walks on one path with blinkers on his eyes, going around and around, the jīva rotates in terrifying saṁsāra, wearing blinkers of māyā. For this reason, the jīva cannot see the subtle truth. As long as the jīva seeks other’s material objects and other’s wives, how can he achieve Kṛṣṇa? The Vedas say that Kṛṣṇa is the unchanging tattva. One cannot understand him with a changeable mind.”

In this way, the Lord made many places fortunate. Merciful Caitanya ran to Jaleśvara. At Jaleśvara there was a deity of Śiva called Bilveśvara. Seeing that deity bhakti arose within the Lord. There was one sannyāsī in the Śiva temple. The Lord approached him slowly. Facing that sannyāsī, he offered respects. Seeing the Lord, the sannyāsī was surprised.

He said, “Who are you? You are not an ordinary man. Why do you offer respects to me? By what piety do I see you today? Seeing you, my bondage has been severed. You are the result of austerity. O merciful one! Seeing you, all sin is destroyed.”

Gazing at the Lord, the sannyāsī began weeping and his voice choked up in prema. At that moment my Lord hid his form and saying, “Hari,” raised his arms and began dancing. Saying, “Kṛṣṇa,” he jumped, stood and sometimes fell on the ground with choked voice.

There were many people in nāma-saṅkīrtana. Caitanya stayed awake and passed the night. The next day he went to the Suvarṇa-rekha River. He saw Raghunātha dāsa whose hairs were standing on end. We then went to Harihara-pura. There Nimāi became mad with the name of the Lord. Dancing and dancing, he was without awareness and fell on the ground with force. In this way the day passed. Intoxicated with bliss, the Lord began crying.

The next day we went to Bāleśvara. Seeing Gopāla there, the Lord was filled with bliss. The next day we went to Nīlagaḍa. Going there, Nimāi became mad with the name. Dancing, he became unconscious. Uncountable people came. Singing and singing the name, the bliss increased. Becoming unconscious, the Lord fell on the ground.

In this way, the devotees together became intoxicated with the experience of the highest bliss. The next day the Lord went to the bank of Vaitaraṇī River. Weeping he said, “O Kṛṣṇa! please cross.” Choked with prema, his body extended. Saying, “Hari,” he went, not seeing the surroundings.

The next day, we crossed Mahānadī. On the road we saw the deity of Gopīnātha. Everyone received Gopīnātha’s maha-prasāda. Taking the prasāda, bliss rose in the mind. Wanting to see Sakṣī Gopāla everyone began walking with great attraction. Saying, “Hari,” raising their hands, they ran. The ground became mud because of their tears. Seeing Sākṣī Gopāla from a distance, the Lord choked with prema and fell on the ground. Seeing the deity, what did he think? Seeing the face of the deity, he began crying.

Repeatedly he called out, “Gopāla! Gopāla!” Who can describe the force of his prema? Then going to Niṁrāja’s temple, the Lord began crying. How can it be described? Leaving Niṁrāja we went to Āṭāranāla. Seeing the flag, the Lord held on to me. I have not seen such a flood of tears. The Lord made the earth into mud by the river of his tears.

Saying, “O Prabhu Jagannātha!” he flooded the earth with torrents of tears. Falling on the ground, he cried at the top of his voice. He embraced whomever he saw in front of him.

“Look! My Kṛṣṇa dances as a cowherd. What beauty is in his hair!” Seeing the Lord’s temple, he cried loudly. Sometimes he fell on the ground in lamentation. Running quickly, his body became covered with dust at the gate. Because of the flowing tears he could not see the deity. He fell on the ground, shouted and rolled about.

Hundreds of thousands of people came to see his bhāva. With great difficulty, the Lord stopped his flow of prema. Seeing the Lord, Mahāviṣṇu, rose up, weeping. The devotees came running at the astonishing sound of weeping. Gadādhara placed him on his lap. Coming to the Garuḍa stambha, holding on to it, he cut his forehead. Blood flowed. Seeing this, Dhyāna Purī gave his upper cloth and wiped away the Lord’s blood. Having seen Jagannātha, in a group they went to Miśra’s house.

Staying some days in Purī in this way, the Lord experienced newer and newer happiness constantly. Nityānanda, Kṛṣṇa-dāsa, and Hari-dāsa experienced the greatest bliss with the Lord. Purī became filled with the sound of the name all day and night. All were on the ground with choked voices. Some wore garlands on their limbs and some smeared sandalwood. Some arranged for offering food. Gradually everyone came and assembled. Purī surpassed glorious Vaikuṇṭha.

The brāhmaṇa Kṛṣṇa-dāsa along with pot-bellied Śyāma-dāsa protected the Lord on two sides. Sometimes he fell down, intoxicated with prema. Sometimes he jumped into the ocean. Prema-dāsa and Gopīdāsa Mohanta brāhmaṇa showered nectar in reciting Bhāgavatam. Raghunātha dāsa, Candraśekhara, Dāmodara, Narahari and Gadādhara always went together, surrounding Caitanya, to the temple. Without interruption, there were sweet mṛdaṅgas playing and sometimes karatālas, as he became intoxicated with the name.

In this way my Lord always experienced bliss in Miśra’s house with the devotees. Kāśī Miśra always brought plenty of prasāda and fed Caitanya. It attracted the heart by its mere fragrance. He filled his stomach with fried and roasted items and various amounts of prasāda. There was excellent fried chickpeas, crushed mung dhal, and fine sesame, linseed, wheat and barley. There were hundreds of fruits and roots, and scrapped coconut. The Lord constantly took these in his hands and offered them. There was cinācūra, khuramāra, lāḍḍu, and gajā, āṁdhasā, piṣṭaka, puli, rasapūra-gajā, ghee soaked rice, bhūta-ghaṇṭa and beto-śāka.

He accepted all this prasāda and left nothing. In silence he always ate, filling his stomach fully. He ate till his stomach swelled. In this way, days passed. Gradually all the devotees began coming. Śaṅkara Bhāratī, Parānanda Purī, Dāmodara Svāmī, Pradyumna Brahmacārī, Cidānanda Giri, and Premānanda Sarasvatī would always accompany the Lord.

Many devotees met together and worshipped at Purī with great joy. At this time Sārvabhauma made his appearance. Many devotees came and met with him. Seeing Mahāviṣṇu, the Lord had great attraction. Repeatedly, the Lord offered respects with devotion. The Lord fainted on seeing Govinda. He remained there as if dead. Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya was at that place. Taking him on his lap, he went to his house. He performed many services for Caitanya. His bhakti-rasa grew. Finally the Lord gave him bhakti.

The Lord desired to travel to the south. For three months, Caitanya stayed in Purī with Nityānanda. On the seventh tithi of Vaiśākha, he travelled south, floating in prema-rasa. When he was about to travel, worried Nityānanda said with humility and devotion, “Do not go alone. Go together with us.”

Gadādhara, Premānanda Sarasvatī, Bhāratī and Śaṅkara were ready to go with him. Hearing this, my Lord smiled and said, “I will go alone, not taking anyone.” Hearing those words, Nityānanda said, while shedding tears, “When you go travelling south far away, go with Kṛṣṇa-dāsa Brāhmaṇa. Being pure, the brāhmaṇa will do whatever you tell him. How can I remain giving you up?” Then everyone said, “We will all go with you.” Hearing this, Mahāprabhu smiled slightly and prevented them. He gave instructions to them all. After hearing him, they all said, “Govinda dāsa will go with you.”

Hearing this, the Lord smiled at me slightly. “I like Govinda’s association. Govinda will go where others will not. Govinda will do work for me.”

Saying this the Lord bid farewell and travelled south. All the devotees pursued him. Eventually we all came softly to the temple of Ālāla-nātha. Seeing the deity, the Lord’s emotions increased. He moistened the earth with his tears. Dancing and dancing, the Lord became unconscious and fell to the ground with force. At dawn the next day he bid farewell to all. Three men went on the southern tour.

Sārvabhauma at this time said softly, “Meet Rāya on the bank of the Godāvarī. Rāmānanda is the best devotee who knows rasa. Because of Kṛṣṇa’s name his eyes are always filled with tears. Rāmānanda experiences pure bliss. He experiences bliss within because of the Lord’s name.”

Hearing this, the Lord rushed to the bank of the Godāvarī. At that place Rāmānanda came and met him. Seeing the new sannyāsī, his bhakti arose. Grasping his feet, Rāmānanda began weeping. He said, “You are the Lord. I am most fortunate to see you.”

The Lord said, “O Rāya! Please speak about Kṛṣṇa. The pain in my heart will disappear by your conclusions.”

Rāmānanda said, “O Lord! I know nothing. If you do not make me speak, I cannot speak. Residing in the heart, you do everything. A mute person by your mercy becomes eloquent.”

The Lord said, “By what tattva does the mind become pure?”

Rāya said, “That tattva is association with devotees.”

“I want to know the fine tattva in this.”

Rāya said, “Other than renunciation there is no tattva.”

The Lord said, “The fine tattva is attraction.”

Rāya said, “Higher than that is prema-bhakti.”

The Lord said, “Please say more of the essence. O intelligent one!’ Rāya said, “The essence of everything is having rasa.”

“O Rāmānanda! I want you to speak more about the essence.”

At that time the Lord lowered his head. He said, “Ghee exists hidden in milk. The person who churns attains the taste. O Rāya! I do not know anything. Speak about Kṛṣṇa. I will hear from your mouth. You are a detached Vaiṣṇava, Rāmānanda. Speak kṛṣṇatattva and soothe my heart.”

Hearing the Lord’s words, Rāmānanda, with humility, folded his two hands and spoke.

“O Lord! Why do you deceive the world? Be merciful and make me your servant. You have spread bhakti-rasa in many places. Mercifully, you have purified this earth. O Jagannātha! Be merciful to this fallen person. Give detachment in my heart. Accept me to be with you.”

Hearing this the Lord embraced Rāmānanda. Kneeling, Rāmānanda wept. Tears flooded his chest. Seeing this, with choked voice the Lord spoke.

“You are the best of devotees O Rāmānanda!”

Lowering his head Rāmānanda said, “Rāma, Rāma!”

The Lord said, “You are most fortunate. O Rāmānanda! Your auspicious talks on bhakti cannot be described.”

Rāmānanda said, “I am a most fallen rascal. You have been contaminated by touching me. Mercifully forgive this offense. Dwell in my heart and give a taste for bhakti.”

Rāmānanda and the Lord passed the night speaking in this manner. The next day Rāmānanda held the Lord’s feet. Bidding farewell, he departed to do his work.

The Lord said, “I will go. You go to Purī and stay with me. You, I and Sārvabhauma alone will discuss kṛṣṇa-tattva, giving relief to our lives.”

Saying this, the Lord bid farewell to Rāya and, offering respects, Rāmānanda went to his house. I have not included in this book a hundredth of what Rāya discussed with the Lord. In this way Rāmānanda for ten days came and was blissful, seeing the sannyāsī of Nadīyā. The Lord, seeing Rāmānanda, was very pleased. Seeing the Lord, Rāmānanda became unconscious.

Bidding farewell to Rāya, the Lord entered Trimanda. Many Buddhists lived in Trimanda town. They all came and met Gaurasundara. The Lord discussed with the Buddhists. The king of Trimanda came and acted as a referee. The Buddhists accepted defeat in the deliberation. All the spectators began to laugh. They all said, “This sannyāsī is not human. A human cannot speak what he discussed. The head of the Buddhists, Rāmagiri Rāya, offered respects and said, “Show the path to me. You are not a human being. O young sannyāsī! I would like to remain with you. I was a great learned man in the world. Mercifully show the path of bhakti to me.”

Laughing, Caitanya mercifully spoke. “You are most intelligent, Rāmagiri Rāya. The person who chants the name of the Lord with hairs standing on end is most intelligent. That is the method.”

Hearing the Lord’s words, Rāmagiri Rāya immediately fell on the ground. Falling at the Lord’s feet, Rāmagiri said, “You are merciful to speak to a low person. Dwelling in all jīvas, you see all jīvas. Mercifully give your red feet to me.”

Bhakti manifested in Rāmagiri, the pāṣaṇḍa. Seeing this, my Lord became filled with bliss. All the greatest Buddhist scholars went on the path of Rāmagiri. The young sannyāsī defeated all their arguments. Seeing this, Rāmagiri looked in the four directions.

Finally, a resident of Tuṅgabhadrā, Ḍhuṇḍhi-rāma-tīrtha, desiring debate, came. Thinking himself learned, intoxicated with pride, this jñānī could not understand the path of bhakti by his dry logic. Ḍhuṇḍhirāma was a learned man. He discussed but became full of fear. Ḍhuṇḍhirāma Svāmī, coming to deliberate, rolled on the ground repeatedly with tears in his eyes.

The Lord said, “Listen, Ḍhuṇḍhirāma Svāmī. I will secretly write a letter saying that I was defeated in debating with you. Caitanya was defeated in your place. By the mercy of Sarasvatī, you are a great scholar. Who can defeat you in the scriptures of logic? You are qualified with logic, Sāṅkhya, Pātañjala yoga and Vedānta philosophies, with all scriptures. Being a foolish sannyāsī, I know nothing. Repeatedly I am defeated in your presence. Formerly you were a great scholar in Ḍhuṇḍhi. Your learning was famous in the world.”

Saying this, he bid farewell to Ḍhuṇḍhirāma. Looking in the four directions, Ḍhuṇḍhi did not want him to go. Looking here and there, he rolled at the Lord’s feet, having been purified in mind. The Lord gave bhakti to the pāṣaṇḍa Ḍhuṇḍhi. Remembering the Lord, Gaurāṅga went to Panthaguhā. Ḍhuṇḍhirāma became famous as a servant of the Lord. The learned spoke many stories about him.

The Lord called me while laughing. I was carrying two wooden shoes on my shoulders. Whatever goods were there, the shoes and begging bowl, whatever Rāmānanda had given, I gathered together. There was a banyan tree very far away called Akṣaya. In the evening the Lord arrived there. Śiva, called Vaṭeśvara resided there. The Lord ran there with devotion.

With devotion the Lord offered respects to Vaṭeśvara. He passed the night there without eating. In the morning he went to take bath. I went from door to door to beg food. Begging, I returned at noon. I then cooked and served Gaurāṅga. I accepted prasāda, equal to nectar. At that time, a wealthy man of the tīrtha came there.

He came with two prostitutes to see him, to test the seriousness of his sannyāsa. The two prostitutes named Satyabāi and Lakṣmībāi came to the Lord and spoke. On the instructions of the wealthy man, the two made many arrangements to understand the Lord. Tīrtharāma (the wealthy man) spoke various subjects. The effulgence of the sannyāsī took away their pretense. Lakṣmī and Satyabāi while laughing made various gestures. Smiling Satyabāi sat next to the Lord. She opened her bodice to show her breasts. The Lord addressed her as mother. At the Lord’s words, Satya began to tremble. Seeing this, Lakṣmī became afraid. The Lord’s mind was not at all agitated. She fled and Satyabāi fell at his feet.

“O mother! You make me an offender.” Saying only this, the Lord fell on the ground. His hair became loose and he became covered with dust. His body quivered violently with prema.

He became all disheveled. Where did Lakṣmī and Satya go? They were not seen again.

The Lord began dancing saying “Hari, Hari!” His hairs stood on end and he shed profuse tears. His kaupīna and outer cloth fell off. Naked he danced, breathing deeply. He fell on the ground, not aware of the thorns. The cluster of garlands fell from his throat. Having not eaten, he was flesh and bone. Blood oozed from his thin limbs.

Intoxicated with the name, Gaurāṅga danced. Astonishing effulgence emanated from his limbs. Seeing this, the wealthy person became amazed and fell at his feet in surrender. Though he pressed the Lord’s feet, the Lord was unaware. Saying, “Hari,” with raised hands he danced and walked forward.

Holding Satya in his arms, he said, “Chant the name of the Lord. Say Hari! O Praṇeśvara, Mukunda, Murāri! Where is the Lord? Where is Mukunda? Where is Murāri?”

Seeing the Lord’s ecstasy, everyone became unconscious. Intoxicated with the name, the Lord had no external consciousness. Bending his shoulders, he fell down, his prāṇas being afflicted. Saliva flowed from his mouth. His limbs were covered with dust. He was naked. His hairs stood on end and his eyes were closed.

Seeing his bhāva, many Buddhists said “Hari, Hari.” Hearing this, Gaurāṅga’s eyes flowed with tears. The tears flowed out as if from a syringe. Seeing this Tīrtharāma began crying. He said, “I am a big pāṣaṇḍa. O Lord! Mercifully give me hari-nāma.”

Embracing Tīrtharāma, the pāṣaṇḍa, the Lord said, “O Tīrtharāma! You are a gentleman. I have become pure by your presence. You are the chief devotee.” He said this many times. The wealthy Tīrtharāma fell at his feet. Overwhelmed, he cried and cried. When bhakti arose as he cried, suddenly the Lord took his hand and embraced him.

The Lord said, “Consider wealth to be like grass. Then you will receive the priceless jewel of bhakti, the greatest wealth. Throw far way clothing and ornaments. Reject temporary wealth and worship the eternal wealth for you will repeatedly receive pain. I advise you to become desireless. The body which you find attractive is just covered by skin. In a few days it will rot. When the prāṇa flies from the body like a bird, it becomes insects, ashes or stool. There is no greatness in wealth in the three worlds. The only greatness is in worshipping the Lord. Knowing the fickle nature of wealth, reject it completely and keep it far away.

“Having faith in the Lord, bring the Lord close. There is no need to speak further proof. Unlimited universes are the proof. The learned person does not need to see the proof. He does not see the goal in many arguments. Bring Kṛṣṇa with the faith of a sādhaka. What is use in discussing many scriptures? Having faith, worship Kṛṣṇa.

“The person proud of wealth brings suffering to himself day by day. The person who desires respect and glorifies it cannot prevent suffering. He thinks in terms of ‘This is me and this is mine.’ Closing his eyes, he does not see a second person or another’s possessions. He sees others falsely as his relatives. He grieves for a dead body which is like a broken doll.

“Everyone knows that the son is born from a father. The Vedas do not say that the two hearts are one. If the son dies, this does not accomplish the father’s death. The son is born from the mother’s body, but know for certain that the bodies of the two are not one. No one possesses anything. This is the essential ascertainment. That need not be proved by any proof. The Lord is the object of proof and he is the proof. The proof is present in the heart of a human. A foolish person knows the Lord from very far off. From knowing the Lord close up, a person is considered a jñānī. I have spoken the essential tattva as explained in the Veda. One does not seek it in the world of fools. The person who knows all the true tattvas does not die again and again.”

Hearing all this from the Lord’s mouth, Tīrtharāma gave up all attachment to matter and chanted the name of the Lord. The Lord became intoxicated with saṅkīrtana. Eventually his associates gathered.

Wealthy Tīrtharāma became distressed. The pāṣaṇḍas spoke to him. Tīrtharāma gave up material items considering them to be like grass and spreading his arms danced, saying the name of the Lord. Putting on tilaka, donning a kaupīna, performing bhakti, he delivered the fallen and deprived. Hearing this, his wife, crying, rushed there immediately.

Hearing the blind man’s words, Caitanya said, “O brother! Why make me an offender? The Lord resides in everyone’s heart. If you ask, Durgā will say this. I do not want to hear what you speak. It is false. Why do you make me an offender, O brother? I am an ordinary human being, a fallen rascal. Your heart has fallen in the well of error.”

The blind man said, “There is no use in speaking further. Show your form. This is what I beg.”

The distraught blind man, attached to the Lord, wept. The Lord came to the blind man. Seeing his bhakti, Gaurāṅga gently took his hand. Spreading his arms, he embraced the blind man. When he touched him, the man’s hairs stood on end. Like lightning, he quickly opened his eyes and, seeing the Lord, became successful.

Immediately on seeing my Lord, he left his body. The Lord said “Hari bol” and danced around the blind man, mad with prema. Making a samādhi for the blind man in the yard, Gaurāṅga left Padma-koṭa.

Leaving Padma-koṭa, the Lord went to Tripātra and saw Caṇḍeśvara Śiva. Saying “Babom” in the temple, the sound echoed for a daṇḍa of time. There was a huge bilva tree in the yard. Everyone said that this was a siddha-bilva tree. Many Śiva followers lived there. The learned Bhargadeva was their leader. The big scholar Bhargadeva worshiped Hari daily in bliss in order to see him. There the Lord with closed eyes recited verses with devotion. Seeing Śacīnandana, the old Bhargadeva called all the renounced persons and spoke.

“Listen everyone! One astonishing sannyāsī has come to this place, desiring to see tīrthas. Everyone says that he has astonishing power. This is that sannyāsī, the son of Śacī. He is peaceful, with closed eyes. Seeing him, those who are the sinful and suffering do not remain in that state. He has flooded the place by giving the nectar of the Lord’s names. He has made children, women and elders mad with the name. I have heard that, leaving his own place, he has delivered pāṣaṇḍas by the name.

“O brother! Look at this young sannyāsī. They call him Caitanya Gosāi. O brother! Having heard about him, I have seen him today. I have never seen such a form. This excellent sannyāsī is not human. Why has my heart melted on seeing him? This person is an avatāra of the Lord. Offer respects to him, holding his feet.”

Saying this, Bharga offered respects. Biting his tongue with his teeth, Caitanya recoiled. The

Lord said, “What have you said, Bharga? My birthplace is the town of Nadīyā. It is certain that I am an ordinary human. Why do you say I am an avatāra? This is false and fearful. Repeatedly saying that I am an avatāra, you make me an offender. I have come to make you a tīrtha. Chanting the Lord’s name, raise your arms and dance, brother! Why cause disturbance by saying I am an avatāra? Joining with everyone, chant the name of the Lord. Never say I am an avatāra of the Lord. You are directly Śiva, master of the universe.”

The Lord, folding his hands, offered him respects. Fearful, Bhargadeva became astonished. He became choked up at his feet. His body became covered with dust. Old Bharga said, “Listen, Caitanya! Be merciful. I beg this. I do not know the practice of bhajana. I think of myself as a detached sannyāsī. O Lord! You make a show to that person who does not know the skill of your pastimes. To that person who does not recognize you, you skillfully hide your form.”

The old man said, “I have bad eyesight because of some fault in my eye. Because of that, I am seeing young, black Kṛṣṇa. People say you have a complexion like gold. I see black as a result of unfortunate fate. One time be merciful, O Caitanya Gosāi. Show that form even though I see it. Mercifully give me eyes. O Lord! Be merciful to me, if I am fortunate. If you give mercy to this fallen soul, put your feet on my head.”

Hearing the words of the old man, the son of Śacī said, “Why do you make me an offender repeatedly. I came, desiring to see a devotee. And pure talks have become polluted!”

Hearing these words, Bharga folded his hands, and said, “This is what I request today. That is my message.” The Lord stayed a week in Tripātra. Many people came and gathered. For seven days, the Lord did saṅkīrtana. All people became mad with the name. At this place many people became devotees. On their necks tulasī beads were swinging.

What more can I say about my Lord? He had astonishing influence and remarkable form. At the end of the day Gaurāṅga ate ordinary food. Not eating, his body had become as thin as a stick. All that remained were skin and bones. However, the effulgence from his body was like fire. Everyone was enchanted with the beauty of his limbs. Without effort, at all times his body smelled of lotuses. Whoever looked at him became dazed by the power of his effulgence.

After seven days, giving mercy to Bharga, my sannyāsī left Tripātra. Bharga followed behind him. Taking his hand, the Lord bid him farewell. Hundreds of thousands of people came to see the Lord. The Lord felt no pain in giving the name of Kṛṣṇa. They spoke nothing except the name. Seeing many devotees of Kṛṣṇa, everyone became unconscious. Saying “Haribol” to all, he made many insane by the power of “Haribol.” Chanting the name, many people ran after the Lord.

Hearing the name, my Lord covered his body with dust. Becoming insane with the name, Gaurāṅga became choked up, falling in the dust. When the Lord bid farewell to Bhargadeva, many small children came to that place. Some said, “O brother! He has gone mad. Saying the Lord’s name, all people have gone mad here.” He made many children go mad. The Lord danced with them. Sometimes he laughed and sometimes he cried. Sometimes he fell on the ground. Eventually all people went away.

There was a huge forest on the path. It was called Jhārivana, measuring fifty yojanas. Śacīnandana entered it. Without fear in his mind, he went on the well-formed path. Caitanya continued to move forward. I stood under a tree, with no one present, and ate the tree’s fruit to get relief from hunger. How can I describe that astonishing fruit? I carefully ate that fruit, which surpassed nectar. After three days one group of sannyāsīs came. My joy increased.

In their company we travelled slowly. After a fortnight we came out of the forest. Outside the forest was pure Raṅga-dhāma. Going to that place, the Lord distributed the name. At Raṅgadhāma was a deity of Narasiṁha. Seeing that deity the hearts of pāṣaṇḍas developed bhakti. Prahlāda stood with folded hands in front of Narasiṁha. The Lord was destroying the king of the demons.

I had never seen such a deity. Seeing the deity, Caitanya went insane. Sometimes he fell, sometimes got up and sometimes went into meditation, closing his eyes. Seeing Narasiṁha, an ocean of prema arose. Sometimes the Lord fell on the ground and sometimes talked incoherently like a madman. Foam sprayed from his mouth. Sometimes his upper cloth became damp with perspiration. Trembling he fell on the earth. Some said, “Some madman has come.” Some fell at his feet.

There was a brāhmaṇa sādhaka called Yudhiṣṭhira, the best of devotees, of good conduct. At this place, the brāhmaṇa performed worship and prayers. He would daily recite the Gītā, while weeping. Everyone laughed at the ignorant brāhmaṇa for reading Gītā. He did not understand it but he liked the Gītā. In studying it, he ignored what others said. He read it alone with absorbed heart. When he read it, he cried. Seeing the tears, the Lord’s mind melted.

The Lord said, “O respected brāhmaṇa! Why do you cry?”

The brāhmaṇa said, “When I read the Gītā I experience great bliss. I see Kṛṣṇa on Arjuna’s chariot. O sannyāsī! With that greed, I read the Gītā.”

The Lord said, “You have seen Kṛṣṇa. Give me mercy and embrace me. I have never seen a devotee equal to you. If I worship you, I will be able to see Kṛṣṇa.”

The brāhmaṇa glanced once at the Lord. He rolled at the Lord’s feet.

The Lord said, “O respected brāhmaṇa! Listen. Do not say this everywhere. You are most fortunate. You are the best of devotees. Kṛṣṇa lets you see him always.”

The brāhmaṇa said, “You are Kṛṣṇa. You made this happen.” Saying this, he held the Lord’s feet strongly. Backing away, the Lord spoke to the brāhmaṇa. Leaving everything, the brāhmaṇa went with the Lord. Bidding farewell to the brāhmaṇa, Śacīnandana then went to Ṛṣabha Parvata.

Parānanda Purī lived at Ṛṣabha Parvata. The Lord proceeded to see him. He discussed about Kṛṣṇa with him a lot. Then he went to Rāmanātha town. At that place were Rāma’s footprints. He gazed at those prints with showers of tears. His hairs stood on end and he trembled. He fell unconscious on the ground. He touched those feet and his hairs stood on end. He breathed heavily. After seeing those lotus feet, he went to see many tīrthas. He went to Rāmeśvara and took bath there. Gaurāṅga took darśana of Śiva. Śiva has an astonishing form called Rāmeśvara. Seeing Śiva, Mahāprabhu offered prayers.

Many holy men stayed there constantly. They all came at that time one by one. Seeing the Lord, one detached scholar desired to have a big debate. The Lord said, “I do not want to debate. I have been defeated by you.”

Seeing the astonishing humility in his eyes, the sannyāsī began to think, being ignorant.

The Lord said, “O great sannyāsī! What are you thinking? Do not speak all topics. By strong bhakti all this is removed. O brother! Sing about the qualities of Kṛṣṇa in your pure heart. Worship Kṛṣṇa, speak of Kṛṣṇa, and chant Kṛṣṇa’s name. Chanting Kṛṣṇa’s name, go to the eternal abode. There is no shelter other than Kṛṣṇa. This is my advice.

“You will attain suffering over and over, here and there. What is the use of pride, O devotee! What is the goal in being learned and deliberating? The sinful scholar is imprisoned in hell. All this is written in the scriptures. What will the person do, who, knowing many scriptures, after deliberating, acts out of lust? The pāṣaṇḍa who, wanting money, cheats, does not understand the words of scripture. If one’s mind hankers for women and gold, one’s study of the Vedas is fake. If envy always plays in someone’s heart, Yamarāja will punish him with his own hand. He whose heart melts with the name of the Lord is a very learned man. I have concluded this. The person in whose eyes tears of bliss flow from chanting the name is called a very wise man. O brother! The person who, reading and hearing, does not have taste for Kṛṣṇa, is a fool and most impure.”

Hearing these words from the Lord’s mouth, the yogī was silent for some time. All the detached sannyāsīs gathered around the Lord and began to listen to his message, becoming unconscious. Finally, the Lord raised his two hands, and, mad with the name, became intoxicated. The Lord fell down and cut his chin on the stone. Streams of blood flowed out. The great scholar wiped away the blood carefully.

For three days the Lord performed kīrtana at Setubandha. He went south to see a mādhavī forest. At the mādhavī forest lived one silent austere man. My begging master went to see him. There was an astonishing effulgence from the sannyāsī’s form. A white beard hung over his chest. His nails had grown very long. He was sitting naked and silent. He had no cloth, no daṇḍa and no water pot. Caitanya looked at him steadily. His closed eyes were very peaceful. The base of a tree was his home and the sky was his clothing. He had no material desire. He was absorbed in austerities.

My Lord stood with folded hands in front of him. Caitanya uttered many polite praises. But the best of sannyāsī did not want to return to consciousness. After three days some detached members brought and prepared fruit and roots. After three days, the great yogī ate some food to maintain his life. Breaking his meditation, he looked around. At that time, the Lord began to speak.

The yogī did not understand the Lord’s words. They then spoke in saṁskṛt. The great yogī listened to those words with seriousness. He spoke a few words with the Lord.

“Cāmbani śiṅaḍi,” he said and laughed. Saying “cāmbani śiṅaḍi” with pure mind, he laughed and offered respects at the Lord’s feet. Gaurāṅga offered respects in return. Floating in bliss, he sang the qualities of Kṛṣṇa. Seeing the yogī offer respects, all the sannyāsīs held the Lord’s feet.

At that place the Lord held discussions. After seven days he departed from there. At that place there was a tīrtha named Tattva-kuṇḍī. The Lord went to take bath there. Then he saw Tāmraparṇī River. He went there to take bath. On the day of Māgha full moon, many guests came to bathe at the bank of the Tāmraparṇī. Waiting a fortnight there, they came and bathed on the Māgha full moon day.

Crossing the Tāmraparṇī, the Lord went to see Kanyākumārī on the shore of the ocean. There were no forests or mountains nearby. One could only hear the sound of the ocean. Big waves came in there, consciously singing about the qualities of the Lord. Seeing that condition, the Lord became blissful. By increased prema, his hairs stood on end.

The sand was like a mountain in the shape of a stupa, broadcasting the glories of the Lord. The ocean called out huṁ huṁ constantly. What more can I say? It was most beautiful. I had never seen anything so beautiful. The mind became pure on seeing the beauty there.

The Lord called, “O Govinda!” Slightly smiling, he called me to bathe. The waves like mountains, came in with great speed. With great devotion the Lord bathed there. After bathing, the Lord wept saying, “Hari!” Prema rose in his heart. His hairs stood on end and his forehead perspired. Hairs stood on end upon his withered body.

After taking his bath, he began thinking. Calling me he said, “Which direction shall we go in?”

I said, “Whatever direction you go, I will go in that direction to serve you.”

A large group of sannyāsīs, after bathing, went to Sām? tala Mountain. Nimāi went with them. I followed behind like a shadow. After fifty krośas, we came to Sām? tala. The group of sannyāsīs stayed there. Caitanya went to the base of a tree. I could not think where to go to beg some food. Understanding my thoughts, the Lord smiled slightly and said, “Why think like this? I will spend the night drinking the nectar of the name. Rising in the morning I will go where I please.”

Saying this, Gaurāṅga, closing his eyes, stayed fixed, leaning against a tree. Playing instruments, the sannyāsīs began singing, which was very sweet to hear. At that time one merchant came there and gave food to everyone. He divided up unbroken fruit, roots, milk and sugar and gave to all.

Receiving the food, my hairs stood on end. Taking milk and sugar the Lord prepared an offering. All the sannyāsīs rose in the morning and departed for Tribaṅku province, going through the mountains. The king of Tribaṅku was very pious. He protected the citizens like his sons. The townspeople were all skillful at welcoming guests. Receiving guests, they made an uproar with arrangements. They competed in receiving guests. They served guests with great respect. The king’s name was Rudrapati. He was the father and mother of the destitute, the shelter for persons with no shelter. The citizens were very happy in this king’s province. Their hearts were overwhelmed with attraction to the king. There were horses and elephants at the king’s door. There was no lack of grain in his storerooms. In the town, there were food distribution centers in three places. Travelers would come and stay in these places. The fortunate king said that everyone could stay in those places as long as they liked.

We came to Tribaṅku in the evening. The Lord with joyful heart sat at the base of a tree. A villager brought some ground flour. The Lord spent the night beneath the tree. The next day the news spread and the towns people slowly gathered. Seeing the Lords astonishing bhāva they folded their hands and stood at that place.

With eye closed the Lord chanted the name. The people all stood chanting prayers with pure minds. The Lord was sitting and his limbs did not move. Tears streamed from the tips of his eyes. His hairs stood on end and internally as well. Seeing his bhāva the village people praised him.

Someone said, “O Sannyāsī! Come to my house.” Someone said, “I like to see you.” Some brought and offered fruit and roots. Opening his eyes, my Lord did not see them.

Someone said, “This sannyāsī is not human. Seeing him, why does bhakti manifest so much? Seeing him I desire to give up material life. My mind no longer focusses on material life.”

Someone said, “Today I have happily ended the night. Seeing the sannyāsī my heart has become purified.”

One old lady came and said with devotion, “Where is the sannyāsī? Show him to me.”

Seeing her insistence, my Gaurāṅga quickly rose and went to her. The old lady offered respects to him. She brought and offered fruits, roots and mixed flour. The rumor spread around and hundreds of jñānīs came to see him.

One brahmavādī came and spoke Advaitavāda. Caitanya laughed and on hearing about his ignorance informed him that the Vedas and Vedānta are scriptural proof.

The Lord said, “Listen, respected jñānī. The perfection of all sādhana is prema in the mood of Rādhā. Rādhā’s fine prema is like a mountain. Without bhakti, no one can search it out. Giving up happiness of ātmā, the person mad for Kṛṣṇa and beautiful Rādhā, rejects everything else. The person who understands Rādhā’s deep prema does not die again. The person absorbed constantly in Māyāvāda will not manifest bhakti-tattva. The essence of prema is mahābhāva. Rādhā is for certain filled with that mahābhāva. O respected elder! The person who understands this tattva no longer experiences birth and death.”

After that, the Lord’s greatness spread in that place. Various people began to gather slowly. The king of the place, very eager, sent people to bring the Lord.

The Lord said, “It is not necessary for me to go there. I will not go to a materialist.”

The king’s messenger said, “Listen, respected sannyāsī! Why not go? You will receive great wealth, all the cloth and ornaments that you want. Easily you will attain such wealth.”

Understanding his intention through the messenger, he began to speak to make him understand. The Lord said, slightly smiling, “O messenger of the king! Please listen. Wealth is not necessary. I do not go to associate with insects of materialism. What use is wealth? The insect of materialism desires wealth. Wealth is the root of anarthas. Please believe this. Those intoxicated with pride in wealth forgets talks about tattva. They stay completely in the hell of material life. The wealthy man does not know that the body is temporary. He thinks success in life is wealth.”

Hearing this the messenger became angry. He went to the king’s door to take revenge. On hearing the news from the messenger’s mouth, Rudrapati had no anger towards the sannyāsī. Hearing the complaining words through the messenger’s mouth, the king desired to see the sannyāsī. King Rudrapati went to see the sannyāsī. Quickly, with devotion he came outside. Giving up elephants and horses at a great distance, wearing miserable clothing, he approached the sannyāsī. With a few ministers the king came to the Lord and spoke with devotion.

Folding his hands, Rudrapati said repeatedly, “Mercifully pardon my offense. Not knowing, I called you. Forgive this offense this time. O deliverer of the lowest! Give me teachings on knowledge to deliver this fallen soul. What is the cause of jīva’s lamentation and suffering?” The king was very learned in various scriptures. All people said he knew Bhāgavatam thoroughly. The king brought a few scholars with him to learn.

The Lord said, “O King! You are most fortunate. You know Bhāgavatam. What more can I say? You are most knowledgeable of many scriptures. I do not know anything except Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.”

Chanting Kṛṣṇa’s name, prema arose. Tears began to fall. Mad with kṛṣṇa-prema, the Lord stood up suddenly and began dancing with arms spread.

Gaurāṅga said, “Haribol!” Becoming unconscious, he danced and danced and fell on the ground. The king picked him up. With him, the king went mad. Saying, “Hari!” the king began dancing. His tears soaked his chest. His hairs stood on end. His limbs became covered with dust.

Seeing the king’s bhakti, Nimāi embraced him and said, “Brother, come! The person whose eyes flood with tears because of the name is the pupil of my eye. O respected King! Seeing your bhakti know for certain that my prāṇa is soothed.”

Saying this he bid farewell to the king and went to take bath. The king sent many fruits and roots. The Lord offered this daily. The king kept some people to serve the Lord. Pleased within, the king returned to his city.

Some brought fruits and roots. Others brought flour. Others brought cūṇā with betel leaves. Attracted to Viśvambara, people bustled about. Many people came to see him. They brought and offered whatever they desired. Gaurāṅga did not say what was good or bad.

It was beautiful to see the place surrounded by mountains. Attractive spring water flowed. There were huge neem trees in the four directions. One cannot describe the astonishing beauty.

At that place there was a mountain called Rāma-giri. Everyone described its astonishing glory. Everyone said that Rāmacandra had been on its summit. For three days he lived with Sītā there.

After conquering Laṅkā, Rāma, the abode of qualities, climbed and rested on its peak. Rāma, with Sītā and Lakṣmaṇa rested there.

Hearing this, the Lord became very greedy. He climbed the mountain to see the place. At this place Rāma and Sītā had rested. At this place, my Gaurāṅga offered respects. Several hundred people climbed with the Lord to see Rāma-giri with devotion. The place was huge in length and breath. He went there for a fortnight. Then he entered Payoṣṇi town. Seeing Śiva and Nārāyaṇa, the Lord became happy. Śaṅkara’s students stayed in Śiṅgeri maṭha. The Lord went there and met them. All the followers of Śaṅkara gathered and sitting, began deliberating on tattva. All the students were defeated in deliberation on interpretation. From the maṭha the Lord went to see Matsya-tīrtha. Journeying to Matsya-tīrtha, the Lord came to Kācāḍa. There he saw Durgā. At that place there was a river named Bhadrā, a tributary of the Kṛṣṇā River. Bathing there, he went to Nāga-pañca-padī. All the people there were devotees of Rāma. He told me to beg for food there. I went to three houses to beg. They gave me lots of flour.

The Lord stayed there three days. Going through the mountains everyone went to Citola. Leaving Citola, he went slowly to the bank of the Tuṅgabhadrā to bathe again. Bathing there, Gaurāṅga sang about Kṛṣṇa with pleasure. Going to many places we arrive at Koṭigirī, the origin of the Kāveri River. Bathing at the birthplace of Kāveri, the Lord went to Caṇḍapura village. On the left side a mountain named Satya stood gloriously. I cannot describe its beauty. From far off one could see blue lines. My eyes opened wide on seeing its beauty. Seeing Satyagiri, the Lord offered it respects. Keeping Satyagiri to the left he travelled to the right.

Coming close to Caṇḍapura, the Lord sat at the base of a tree. One renounced person stayed at Caṇḍapura. Hearing about him from others Nimāi went to him. That learned man knew many scriptures well. A golden earring dangled from one of his ears.

Eventually he began to speak from scripture with great pride in his appearance.

The sannyāsī’s name was Īśvara Bhāratī. People said he had all good qualities. Understanding that the sannyāsī was proud, the Lord turned his head and smiled slightly. Viśvambara did not say anything good or bad. Best of sannyāsīs then stopped speaking.

He said to the Lord, “You do not speak. I do not think you are very learned. Everyone says you are very learned. I see that you have no knowledge. You purified the place saying the name of the Lord. I have not seen even a portion of your qualities. I have heard you know scriptures, but you do not speak. You wander about and beg for food here and there. You have no knowledge for debating. Why do you mislead foolish people? Skilfully deceiving people, you show off giving all people some small truth. Why do you come to this place, telling the foolish people to chant the Lord’s name? Being clever, I understand. If you have power, then deliberate. Then I will understand your pure intelligence.” Saying this, Bhāratī left in haste.

Again, Bhāratī came with his associates and sat. Four people sat around the Lord. Seeing the fun, the Lord began to laugh. Bhāratī said, “Running about, you laugh. Please consider what I say. Please tell me who is the worshippable Lord.”

The Lord said, “There is no one else in this world except Kṛṣṇa.”

Bhāratī said, “Hear the proof from scripture. The Vedas say that there is one Brahman which is the Supreme Lord. Wherever you look, you will see Brahman. Defeat this proposal. Just try.”

The Lord said, “I do not know how to deliberate. I accept that you know all tattvas. You are most learned at deliberation. Nimāi has been defeated by you. If you want a victory letter, I will write it. By your deliberation today I accept defeat.”

Hearing this, the yogī spoke unclearly with dissatisfaction. The Lord said, “Perform bhakti and reject logic. Taking to bhakti, deliberate on Kṛṣṇa. You destroy the conclusions of the Vedas and Vedānta. Studying many scriptures, tell what is the result. Except Kṛṣṇa there is no place for anything to stand.”

Saying this, my Lord closed his eyes. With hairs standing on end, his bhakti increased. Tears began to flow on to his chest. The knot of his kaupīna came undone. His heart trembled and his body perspired. He shouted “Chant Kṛṣṇa’s name” and began swaying. “O Kṛṣṇa! O merciful Lord! Where are you? Distribute bhakti and purify my heart!”

Saying this, the Lord began to cry. The agitation in his mind doubled. Not hearing what was good or bad, Viśvambara cried intensely, constantly. Seeing a tamāla tree in front, he ran to the tree and embraced it, saying “Kṛṣṇa!” Seeing the Lord’s bhāva with his own eyes, the yogī embraced the Lord’s feet.

The yogī said, “I beg not to deliberate. My longing for Kṛṣṇa grows. O young sannyāsī! Seeing your bhāva I do not want to deliberate. Give to my mind the astonishing jewel of bhakti. I beg this at your feet.”

The Lord could not hear the yogī’s words. The Lord moistened the earth with his tears. Overwhelmed by mahābhāva, his limbs became stunned. His golden body fell in the dust. Saying “Kṛṣṇa” he rolled on the ground. His dusty body became covered with thorns. The yogī, sitting in front of him, began weeping. At that moment mercy was given to him.

Seeing Bhāratī’s bhakti, the Lord put his hand on his back. Putting his hand on his back, he spoke a few words. By the touch of the Lord, the yogī developed bhakti. His mind became absorbed in bhakti-rasa. How can one describe the mercy of the Lord? The yogī became mad with prema and rolled in the dust.

The yogī said, “You alone will be my Kṛṣṇa. O Lord! When will you come? Let me see you again.”

The Lord said, “Do not tell anyone these words. Kṛṣṇa is the master of Vṛndāvana. This is the conclusion. Without bhakti, the tattva of Kṛṣṇa will not arise. Know for certain that Kṛṣṇa is bound by the thread of bhakti.”

The yogī said, “I know your pretence. Why, being clever, do you put dust in my eyes? I will tie myself to you with the thread of bhakti today. I will take two wooden shoes today.”

Bhāratī, saying this, forcibly pulled on his wooden shoes.

The Lord said, “You should have faith in Kṛṣṇa. From today your name is Kṛṣṇa-dāsa.”

Saying this, the Lord left Caṇḍapura. The best of yogīs walked a long way with him. Laughing, the Lord bid him farewell. Offering respects, the yogī left.

Travelling two days and nights, crossing the mountain, we did not find any village or town. We could not travel on the very difficult path. We saw only rows of kadamba trees. Seeing the trees, the Lord said to me, “My Kṛṣṇa plays under this tree.” Saying this, my Lord became overwhelmed and cried. He walked while swaying, confused by kṛṣṇa-prema. Walking and walking we saw a small lake. I became afraid on seeing a tiger there. The Lord made a signal to the tiger. I did not hear the Lord say anything good or bad. The tiger began drinking water there. It carefully crawled to the Lord’s side. The Lord went to the right and tiger went to the left. His limbs went out of control because of absorption, mad with chanting the name. The tiger did not look back at us. With the tiger behind, we walked quickly. Seeing my state, smiling slightly, the Lord said, “Why are you afraid? Chanting the name, there is no fear of death. Chanting, “Kṛṣṇa! Kṛṣṇa!” have no doubt.”

Hearing this, I attained power. The strength of my body doubled. Walking and walking, we arrived at a small hamlet, desiring shelter. It was a very small village with suffering inhabitants. Nimāi the sannyāsī stayed there. It was beautiful to see the village surrounded by mountains. I went within the village to collect alms. There was one very poor brāhmaṇa. He maintained his life by begging. I went to his house to beg.

The brāhmaṇa said, “Brother! Wait a moment. Sit a moment and do not go. If a guest leaves, I will go to hell. Going begging, I will give you what I receive. Wait for some time here, O brother!”

Saying this, the brāhmaṇa went begging. He brought two coconuts and gave them to me. I brought the alms to the Lord and gave them at the base of the tree. The Lord offered the fruits in joy.

Hearing the brāhmaṇa’s story, Gaurāṅga went in the evening to see the brāhmaṇa. The brāhmaṇa and his wife lived there. He went begging for serving Gopāla. Seeing the Lord at his house, he folded his hands and came and stood in front of the Lord.

The brāhmaṇa said, “With what will I worship the guest? How can I ask him to go away? I have something for Gopāla’s service. Begging, we two do service. I do not have seat. What will I give for him to sit upon?”

His wife said, “O brāhmaṇa! Offer your head to his feet. This guest’s body shines like lightning. Bring tulasī and offer to the guest’s feet.”

Quickly the brāhmaṇa brought tulasī and rushed to offer them to the Lord’s feet. Holding the brāhmaṇa’s hand, Caitanya said, “Offer tulasī to Gopāla’s feet.”

Hearing this, the brāhmaṇa began crying. At that moment, the Lord embraced him. The Lord said, “O brāhmaṇa! You are most fortunate. The Lord is present in your house. How can I speak of your fortune? Your Gopāla plays in your house. Your wife is directly Lakṣmī. Consider this in your mind.”

The brāhmaṇa said, “I consider myself fortunate by your mercy. You are not an ordinary human, merciful one! Why does lightning play upon your body? Why do I infer the fragrance of lotuses on your body? Put your feet on my head.”

Saying this, the brāhmaṇa put the Lord’s feet on his head. Biting his tongue with his teeth at these words, the Lord stepped back. Confused, the brāhmaṇa with his wife rushed forward and lowered his head to the Lord’s feet.

Stretching his arms, the Lord raised the brāhmaṇa. Then he began singing with devotion. The brāhmaṇa’s house became like Vṛndāvana. The village people came to hear the Lord’s name.

Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare

Hare Rāma Hare Rāma Rāma Rāma Hare Hare

Merciful Caitanya began singing this. All the listeners became insane along with him.

Hearing the name from the Lord’s mouth, the villagers became choked up at the Lord’s feet. Singing and singing the song, the night passed. In the morning my Lord bid farewell. Bidding farewell, the Lord went out of the village. The people remained staring like dolls.

Signalling me, saying “Govinda,” I put the wooden shoes on my shoulders. Near Kāṇḍāra province Nīla-giri shone. In the afternoon we went there slowly. He said, “What beauty could be experienced at Nīla-giri! By intense meditation, the Lord becomes present.” Hundreds of caves beautified its base. Its astonishing state was beautiful to the summit. Huge trees grew to its summit. They swayed in the wind like cāmara fans. Waterfalls gurgled. Seeing that, the mind became joyful. Wandering around the mountain, I could see fresher and fresher beauty.

Hundreds of creepers surrounded the trees, showing bondage in marriage with respect. Peacocks lived there and in groups cried out. Various types of birds sang with sweet voices. Many types of flowers blossomed brightly, like garlands hanging on the neck of nature. In the night many creepers shone with flashes of light. In the trees, fireflies illuminated the branches. A small river flowed with gurgling sound. On its bank the Lord stayed and performed evening worship. In the night he sat under one tree. The Lord said to me, “We will spend the night here.”

Saying only that, Gaurāṅga closed his eyes. He spent the night chanting the Lord’s name. By the Lord’s mercy, I did not experience hunger or thirst. I remained around if he wanted anything. When he said to go begging I would go to a householder’s door. In the morning we rose and went to Gurjarī town. Many people lived there happily. At that place there were many large, beautiful houses. Gaurāṅga went and stayed at the edge of the town.

There was a pond called Agastya-kuṇḍa at this place. Bathing in the pond, we experienced bliss. Gaurāṅga bathed in Agastya-kuṇḍa and stayed on its bank, singing the glories of the Lord. Eventually several people appeared. One brāhmaṇa brought and gave milk and sugar. Someone said, “O guest! Come to my house.” Someone else said, “You chant Kṛṣṇa’s name. The name of the Lord is very sweet in your mouth. Hearing the name all suffering and lamentation are destroyed. The name of Kṛṣṇa in your mouth is like nectar. Speak about Kṛṣṇa and sooth my prāṇa.” Word spread and many jñānīs came to see the Lord.

Closing his eyes, Gaurāṅga began to sway. Tears burst from his eyes. My Gaurāṅga did not see the people. Saying, “O Kṛṣṇa!” he dampened the earth with his crying. He began weeping with emptiness. His hair loosened and fell down his back. Hairs standing on end, he wept in distress. With unkempt clothing, the Lord spoke many confused words.

Sometimes the Lord in madness became choked in voice. Sometimes he fell on the ground. Saying “My dear friend Mukunda, Murārī!” Caitanya ran about. Sometimes he said, “Come, Narahari, my life! Hearing Kṛṣṇa’s name, I embrace you.”

In this way, Gaurāṅga spoke various words and ran about. Many great people, hearing of his astonishing powers, came near the Lord.

There was one great scholar named Arjuna. He spoke to the Lord about scriptural proof. He said, “I do not accept jīva-tattva. I consider ātmā-tattva (i.e. Paramātmā) and jīva-tattva to be one.”

The Lord said, “You are a great scholar. Hearing scriptural proof, draw the conclusion. If you know the śruti conclusion of Dvāparṇā (two birds) why do you consider the two to be one?”

Taking the subtle topics of Vedānta, Gaurāṅga made Arjuna understand everything by refuting false concepts. “There is jīva and Paramātmā. The Lord is a huge tree and the jīva is its leaf. O great scholar! When you read, discard all unnecessary topics. Māyā is the shadow of the Lord. The Lord is not contaminated by māyā. By his desire, the jīvas become influenced by māyā. By the power of the name, if a person can drive out māyā, he is a great sage in this world. A person has a screen of māyā between himself and the Lord. Taking away the screen, see the Lord.”

Saying this and saying, “O Kṛṣṇa!” he shouted. Suddenly the place became silent. I heard the name from the mouth of the Lord. The hairs of my body stood on end.

The Lord began calling out, “Rāma! Rāma!” That place became like Vaikuṇṭha. Pleasant wind began to blow. Groups of villagers came to see. Hundreds of people stood all around. Speechless, they were listening to the name of the Lord. It seemed that the devatās came to hear the name. Above our heads, they were listening. The scent of lotuses spread, causing bewilderment. Chanting the name, the Lord became unconscious.

Everyone looked at the face of the Lord. Tears flowed constantly. Many Marathi people came in groups. They gathered and began listening to the name. I glanced behind and hundreds of family women were standing there. Everyone was listening to the Lord’s name with devotion. Women wiped away the tears with the edge of their cloth. Unlimited sannyāsīs of Viṣṇu and Śiva gathered to the name, closing their eyes.

The Lord intoxicated the state with his teachings. I have never seen such power. Sometimes he spoke by mistake in Tamil and sometimes spoke Saṁskṛt, intoxicating the listeners. In this way chanting the name, becoming unconscious, the Lord began to dance. His hair came undone along with his kaupīna. He appeared most miserable with dust on his limbs. Dancing, he became unconscious and fell on the ground. He remained there inert. Seeing this, people approached him.

Someone brought water and splashed it on his face. Someone raised him up carefully. Two daṇḍas later the Lord sat up. Everyone in astonishment chanted the Lord’s name.

In the afternoon, one brāhmaṇa brought food. My Lord offered the food under a tree. Leaving Gurjarī town, my Gaurāṅga wanted to go to Pūrṇa town. For seven days the Lord did not hold discussions. He climbed up a mountain at Bijāpura. The only provision on the road was the name of the Lord. Climbing the mountain, the Lord took rest. Climbing to the summit of the mountain and looking around, the Lord felt bliss. Caitanya climbed down the mountain first, with me following behind.

He went to see completely Sahya-kulācala. Seeing Kulācala, he was thrilled with bliss. I saw Mahendra and Malaya mountains with my eyes. Sahyagiri’s beauty cannot be described. From a distance one could see a blue line. My Lord ran to see that place.

The mountain stands with a grave mood. Seeing the mountain, the heart starts dancing. The Lord said, “This mountain is the abode of bliss. He offered respects to it as the abode of bliss. The Lord was so eager to see Sahyācala that his hairs stood on end. Leaving Sahyagiri and going north, the Lord chanted the name with an unprecedentedly blissful face. Without desire, with most miserable clothing, the Lord floated through various provinces with bhakti-rasa. Wearing a kaupīna, he had dust all over his body. Seeing him, one would think he was a madman.

Eventually Gaurāṅga came to Pūrṇa town. Many scholars came to see. At that place many people studied scriptures. Slowly many people saw him. There was a lake called Accha-sara. Nimāi went and stayed near it. On that lake a huge bakula tree grew. My Lord sat at its base and stayed there. Hundreds of scholars resided at that place. Day and night the scholars explained various scriptures. Hundreds of students came there and studied various scriptures under a guru. At that place, many people were skillful in knowledge. Hundreds of schools shone resplendently there.

Everyone considered the person who studied Bhāgavatam to be a scholar. No one considered a person who did not know Gītā and Bhāgavatam to be learned. One scholar explained Bhāgavatam. On hearing him, the Lord wept torrents of tears.

One brahmavādī scholar came. The Lord defeated all his logical arguments. Many devotees gathered and, seeing the Lord’s bhakti, woke up. Closing his eyes, the Lord said, “Kṛṣṇa! Kṛṣṇa!” Tears flowed from his eyes and fell on his chest. The Lord said, “My life is Mukunda, Murārī. Come and appear in my heart. Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, filled with all śaktis, are the support of the universe. Without Kṛṣṇa, this universe is destroyed. Millions of universes come from his hair holes. How can I see Kṛṣṇa, my life? Eating dirt, he opened his mouth on his mother’s lap. Suddenly, his mother saw in his mouth the universe. Wanting that Kṛṣṇa, my heart is disturbed. Without Kṛṣṇa my life is painful.”

One scholar came near and spoke. “Your Kṛṣṇa is in this lake.”

Hearing these words, the Lord suddenly got up. His hairs stood on end. I never saw such force in his tears. In separation from Kṛṣṇa, distraught Nimāi cried. Saying, “Kṛṣṇa! Kṛṣṇa!” he began to weep intensely. He said, “Without Kṛṣṇa my life is useless.”

He dampened the earth with his tears. Crying and crying he said with his mouth, “Haribol!”

He said to me one time, “There is one disappointment. Without Kṛṣṇa, I cannot bear the pain in my life.”

Again, that austere sannyāsī said, “O sannyāsī! Your Kṛṣṇa is hiding in the water.”

This time, Mahāprabhu heard his words. Overwhelmed with prema he suddenly jumped in the water. Many people also entered the lake. Various people, lamenting, pulled the Lord to the shore. All the scholars said that the person who said that Kṛṣṇa was in the water was speaking foolish words.

The Lord said, “Why do you uselessly scold the great man? Kṛṣṇa is always present in the water, on land and in the sky. Kṛṣṇa is everywhere, Kṛṣṇa is the universe. The devotee can see this. Bhakti is the highest tattva in this world. The person endowed with bhakti kicks liberation on the head. The person who cannot understand the wheel of māyā is most unfortunate in this world. Welfare is false. Land is false. House is false. Wanting food, the fool is disturbed internally. Who is self and who is another? Who is father and who is mother? Whose throat does the hand hold? Say O brother! Men and women are not different. The difference is in the skin. The Vedas say this repeatedly. The jīva is trapped in the darkness of illusion. He never once says the name of the Lord. O brother! Never be falsely proud of wealth. Pass your time always chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa. Sin covers the universe with darkness. There is nothing true in this world except the Lord.

When the two birds leave the tree of the body, the material body drops away. O brother! Why look at a dream after waking up? No one will save you. Everyone will die eventually. O brother! Come, chant the name together. Hearing the name, death trembles in fear. Kings and emperors have great power. One day, tall kingdoms must be destroyed.

“O King! With pride the king makes a kingdom. Why does his heart burn with the three miseries? A valuable pearl will not go with you. Understand by experience that wealth is temporary and worthless. O brother! Chant Hare Kṛṣṇa with devotion. Everyone will remain always in happiness. All are in illusion, bewildered by māyā. Why do you become proud, desiring everything? The seven mountain ranges will be destroyed with time. In the material world, nothing remains. Bhāva with bhakti is true and eternal.

“Everyone! Hold the Lord’s feet tightly. The Lord, holder of the club, will destroy all suffering. The universe will become equal to Vaikuṇṭha. The person intoxicated with material wealth cannot cut the bondage of material life. The person with material desires becomes bound up. Everyone calls him a dishonest fool. Cut the bondage of māyā with the weapon of the Lord’s name. Easily you will go to the eternal abode. You will not experience again and again birth, death and old age. Saying, ‘Kṛṣṇa! Kṛṣṇa!’ you will destroy the darkness. Suffering people will destroy their prārabdha-karma. Then lamentation, pain and suffering will go far away.”

People flocked to see the Lord. Many scholars came to deliberate. Someone said, “This sannyāsī is not human.” Someone said, “This person is a great devotee.” The Lord never spoke to answer these statements. Streams of prema flowed from his eyes at the name of the Lord. Closing his eyes, the Lord chanted the name. He turned over and over on the ground.

The Lord said, “I will go to some tīrtha. Point out the path. Where is Bholeśvara?”

“Nearby Pāṭasa village is Gora-ghāṭa. At that place is a great holy place called Bholeśvara. At Bholeśvara Śiva resides.” An excellent brāhmaṇa named Tunnu Mahārāja gave these instructions. He was very learned. The Lord knew about him. Tunnu said, “Bholeśvara is at that place.”

Hearing this, the Lord went where Śiva was present. Yearly there was a festival at Bholeśvara. Hearing that, the Lord’s heart danced. Looking at me, the Lord slightly smiled and said, “Go to Bholeśvara. I will follow behind.”

Travelling on many paths over the mountains, we came and saw the arrangement of the tīrtha. There was a huge temple on a mountain. In the temple I saw Lord Bholeśvara. At that place was siddha-kūpa. Taking that water, the Lord bathed. Seeing Bholeśvara, the Lord’s prema arose. Folding his hands, he offered many prayers. Becoming unconscious, he fell on the earth. He turned over and over with choked voice.

Seeing Bholeśvara, Gaurāṅga ran to see Devaleśvara nearby. Seeing Devaleśvara, the Lord, jewel of good qualities, offered respects, rolling on the ground, choked with prema, he recited many praises. Seeing the Lord, people came with devotion. Devaleśvara was situated on top of a mountain. In the far distance shone Jijurī town. In Jijurī there was a deity named Khāṇḍavā.

I went with the Lord to see Khāṇḍavā. They say that the women who cannot get married will get married to him by the grace of Khāṇḍavā. The father and mother bring their daughter in front of Khāṇḍavā and offer her to the deity with great devotion. Daughters of poor fathers stay there and serve Khāṇḍavā with respect. Hundreds of girls taking Khāṇḍavā as their husband were on the path begging. Being cheated, at Khāṇḍavā many of these women took to prostitution. As wives of Khāṇḍavā, they committed sin. They would later end up with an unfortunate destination. Many people came to visit the tīrtha. With cleverness, they fell to hell.

The poor girls who came to the place said that they married Khāṇḍavā and were his wives. Hearing this, the Lord went to see the girls in secret. The people called them “Murāri.” Seeing them, the Lord was merciful. Hearing of their suffering, he felt mercy in his peaceful mind. How a father could be peaceful, I cannot say. How could he make his daughter into a Murāri?

Hearing these words, the Lord went to deliver the women at the yard. I said, “You do not need to go there.” Caitanya did not hear my words. The Lord went to the Murāri’s village. He purified everyone with the name. Not able to tolerate the women’s suffering, he wanted to deliver all the women. Hearing with their own ears about the Lord’s bhāva, gradually many women came to that place.

The Lord said to the women, “Chant the name of the Lord. By the power of the name, you will attain the eternal abode. The Lord is most merciful, the shelter for persons with no shelter. Think of him as your husband. Many gopīs performed Kātyāyanī vrata with pure minds to attain Kṛṣṇa as their husband. When Kṛṣṇa is the husband, there is no fear of the material world. Know for certain that Kṛṣṇa is everyone’s husband. Always chant Kṛṣṇa’s name with devotion. Always say, “Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare.”

Saying this my Lord began to chant the name. Immediately his hairs stood on end. Seeing his bhāva all the women came to worship his feet. By the strength of the Lord, I begged at householders’ doors. Since I was most untouchable, they did not touch me.

Chanting the name with devotion suffering will be destroyed. By the power of the name, all sins were turned to ashes. The person absorbed in sin, not knowing this, destroys all sin by the power of the name.

Hearing this teaching, many women of Khāṇḍavā stood in crowds near the Lord. Indirā, a dancing girl came with folded hands and said, “Be merciful to me, O great sannyāsī! Doing sinful acts, I have become old. Deliver me by the dust of your feet.”

Saying this Indirā rolled in the dust. By the name the Lord delivered Indirā. Indirā accepted the name of the Lord. Leaving the house, she renounced everything. From that day, many women of Khāṇḍavā became mad with the name and tears flowed from their eyes. I have never seen such a merciful Lord. I cannot write how many sinners he delivered.

Seeing the bhakti of those women, the Lord desired to go at dawn to Corānandī forest. The villagers said, “What is the purpose in going there? It is a mine of sin. In Corānandī forest live many thieves. Why do you want to go there?”

The Lord said, “I will go to Corānandī forest. Seeing that forest my goal will be achieved.”

The villagers said, “O sannyāsī! Do not go there. It is not good for devotees to go there. Many thieves and dacoits live in that place. Your life is in doubt by going there.”

The Lord said, “What will the thieves get from me? I will go here and there.”

Rāma Svāmī said, “Lord! There is no tīrtha at Corānandī. Why go there? If the thieves there harm you, the people will give up their lives in separation from you.”

The Lord said, “O Rāma Svāmī! do not fear. I will make the thieves mad with the name of the Lord.”

Saying that the Lord went to Corānandī. Going there he sat under a tree. Many wicked men gathered at that place. They maintained their lives by stealing. One person approached, speaking indistinctly.

What he said I could not understand. The Lord could understand what he said. He made him understand, speaking indistinctly. That person looked around for some time. Glancing here and there he entered the forest. One very strong person named Nārojī approached with weapons. A few men gathered and watched. Seeing the sannyāsī, they all offered respects.

Nārojī said, “Go to my place. Stay there tonight.”

Hearing Nārojī’s words, he said to him, “Tonight I will stay under the tree.”

Hearing the Lord’s words, Nārojī told several men to bring food. Hearing his words, everyone left. Nimāi sat in a yoga posture chanting the name. Some men brought wood and sugar. Others brought rice. Others brought milk and ghee. Others brought fruits and roots. They brought piles of food. Seeing all the food my greed increased. I wandered in many places with the Lord. I had never seen such food in any other place. Various foods were spread all around. Nārojī’s followers stood around in groups.

My Lord was chanting the name. At that time he became overwhelmed with prema. What happened to the milk, sugar and rice? By the touch of his feet, all the fruits and roots were scattered.

Several men said, “What a sannyāsī! Intentionally he has destroyed the pile of food.”

Nārojī said, “Never have I seen such a person. Seeing the sannyāsī, why does my prāṇa weep? I cannot say how many sins I have committed. Why do I want to wear a kaupīna today? Why does my prāṇa weep today? Have I put my foot in the sannyāsī’s trap? All the goods have been destroyed. Do not fear. Again, I will supply all the goods.”

Standing to one side Nārojī glanced at Gaurāṅga, the jewel of good qualities. His eyes flooded with tears. Everyone’s hairs stood on end. Hearing about this, gradually groups of thieves left the forest and came to see. In the afternoon, my Gaurāṅga, jewel of good qualities, fainted in prema and fell on the ground. Choked with prema, he covered his body with dust. Tears fell upon his chest.

Crying Nārojī said, “O sannyāsī! Listen. Reveal the mantra you are chanting. Seeing you, your bhāva appears in my mind. I will no longer commit sin, staying in this forest. I am sixty years old. Giving up material life I will not engage in sin. I am a wicked son of a brāhmaṇa. Do not hesitate to give the dust of your feet to me. Having had children, I no longer have a family. Why should I do sinful acts any longer? Begging, I can fill my stomach. Why should I remain among the thieves? I have developed hatred for sinful acts. I will no longer remain as the head of the thieves.”

Saying this, Nārojī glanced at the group. He threw away his weapons.

The Lord said, “O Nārojī! Hear my words. I will speak to you repeatedly some words. Wearing a kaupīna, give up shame. Begging, fill your stomach. Distribute your wealth to whoever wants it. No one is a father, mother, brother, or friend. If you can maintain your life with a fistful of food, why engage in sin to gather wealth? Drink from a waterfall using your hands as a cup. What is the result of collecting many utensils? Men with wealth like Kuvera will one day go to Yama’s abode. The person who goes on the path of poverty, on giving up the body, will attain a kingdom by that path. Why uselessly die, thinking in terms of possessions? O brother! Chant the name with prema-bhakti.”

Hearing these instructions, the brāhmaṇa Nārojī wanted to go with us.

Nārojī said, “I will show all the tīrthas. I will go behind you from tīrtha to tīrtha. For many days, my blind eyes were bewildered by smoke. I have now thrown my weapons on the ground. I have killed many men with this hand. I have spoken harshly to many men with this mouth. I will no longer remain as the head of the thieves. O shelter of the shelterless! You have shown me the path. I stay in the jungle, hiding at all times. I did not see my body ageing with sin.”

Saying this, the chief of the bandits renounced everything, put on a kaupīna and went with the Lord. The thieves went off somewhere. Nārojī went with us.

Journeying from Corānandī forest, the Lord went to see Khaṇḍalā. The Mūlā River flowed swiftly there. Going there, the Lord spoke to me.

“This river is a pious tīrtha. If you bathe here all sins are destroyed.”

At the Lord’s order, I bathed and went to the town to beg. Nārojī followed behind me. I returned from begging after a long time. Later, a few inhabitants of Khaṇḍalā came to see the Lord. Pureminded, they lived on the river. One said, “O Lord! Come to my house. It can serve many guests in Khaṇḍalā.”

They all competed for the Lord. They ended up quarrelling. They were ready to kill each other.

One said, “I saw him first.” Another said, “I have brought food.” In this way they quarrelled among themselves. Seeing their mood, the Lord laughed in his heart.

One wealthy man said, “Take food in my garden today and stay there. Do not be disgraced by clothing. I will give cloth for you to wear. If you need money for the road, I will give whatever you want.”

The Lord laughed and said, “O King! Please listen. I have no business in pleasure and wealth. I consider torn cloth to be best to wear. I know that there is no use in wealth. By associating with wealth, pride increases. By that pride, the weight of impurity increases. See this universe with your eyes. Think where you will go. Look! All wealth and pleasure will be destroyed. Only the lord of the universe will remain. My two associates bring food. What is the use of additional food? It is not that the body will not be maintained. Sometimes they bring no food. What happens if you bring lots of food? The lack for this body will become more for the poor man who is suffering.

“Chant with prema and live under a tree. Then you will cut all bondage. There is no happiness in māyā’s bondage. O brother! Chant the name of the Lord with prema. Prema for the Lord produces rasa. The devotee knows that life is a mixture of poison and nectar. Svarga is in the thread of time. The person who worships Kṛṣṇa attains Vaikuṇṭha. The person who rejects Kṛṣṇa goes to hell.”

Saying this, my Lord did not speak further. Closing his eyes, he began to chant the name. His hairs stood on end and his locks came undone. When he began dancing his clothing fell off. Overwhelmed with prema, his limbs got covered with dust. How can I describe that prema?

He spent the night chanting the name. Nārojī stayed beside him, wiping away his perspiration. Rising in the early morning, the Lord went to Nāsika to purify his heart at that tīrtha. Lakṣmaṇa cut off Śūrpaṇakha’s nose at this place. In the northern area, near Trimuka, there was a place with Rāma’s cottage.

Mahāprabhu went there. Chanting prayers, finally he did kīrtana. The footprint of Rāmā is at that place. Hearing this, the Lord ran to that forest. Amidst a thick forest, by a waterfall shone the footprints on stone. Touching the footprints, the Lord became overwhelmed with deep prema. With hairs standing on end and locks of hair undone he danced. His thin body seemed to swell. The Lord said, “Where is Rāma, the lord of my life? Show yourself and soothe my heart, putting a hook around my throat. Where is my Rāma?”

He began crying. The scent of lotuses emanated from the Lord’s body. The wind began to blow gently. I dread describing that prema. I have never seen such astonishing bhāva. Shouting, “O Kṛṣṇa!” he looked around like a madman. Looking at the sky he called to someone. Sometimes he stood up as if seeing someone. He fasted for a few days. Not eating any food, his body became thin.

Then he entered Pañca-vaṭī and saw Gaṇeśa established by Lakṣmaṇa. One day, in a cave in Pañca-vaṭī forest, I came from begging and secretly looked. That forest was peaceful, without noise and without people. From time to time a few people lived there. Crickets were chirping in the forest. With closed eyes, Gaurāṅga was meditating. From his limbs radiated great effulgence. My young sannyāsī was meditating. Seeing his bhāva my eyes were dazzled. Slowly I came near to see.

Nārojī came from somewhere bringing fruit. I remained standing on one side. Hearing footsteps, the Lord suddenly withdrew his bhāva and looked around. From somewhere, Nārojī had brought fruits and roots. He stood in front of Caitanya with folded hands. Offering the food, Gaurāṅga ate a little. He sat there the whole night.

Gaurāṅga left Pañca-vaṭī in the morning and went to Damana town. Not staying a day there, leaving the place, the Lord went north. Then, wandering for a fortnight, he went on the path in joy. The sannyāsī said, “Come here, respected sannyāsī. I have never seen a devotee like you. Seeing you, bhakti arises in my mind. Please tell me how I will cross the ocean of saṁsāra. How to worship the Supreme Lord? Saying this, my troubles will be destroyed.”

The Lord said, “I do not know the essential truth. Durgā will destroy all darkness of the mind. The rāgātmikā devotee sees Kṛṣṇa just as a handsome hero sees a heroine. In that manner call out repeatedly to Kṛṣṇa. You will become free from the darkness of the mind.”

As he spoke, one brāhmaṇa brought a goat offering into the Durgā temple. The Lord said, “You give her an offering to eat, but you do not know what your destination is later. Durgā is pure according to scriptures. How can she eat something inedible? The king of Suratha has given a hundred thousand offerings. In hell, a hundred thousand swords will fall on him. You do not study the words of scripture. Killing animals, you enact dharma. She has made an arrangement for meat-eating Rakṣasas to eat it when animals are killed. All scriptures say that nonviolence is the highest dharma. Be merciful to the jīvas and bliss will manifest. Māyā ties you up tightly. Without a weapon how will you cut the bondage?

“With attraction for tamasic food you become attracted to Durgā in order to cut up sheep and goats. Hoping for deliverance in killing animals, you come to offer her animals. You make the ātmā leave the body. How can the ātmā be complete within a dead body? If someone with devotion to Durgā cuts off your head as human sacrifice to her, tell me, if you would grieve, O brother! The animals give up their bodies. I see it with my eyes. ‘Eight-armed Durgā eats meat and liquor.’ The devotee, hearing such statements will laugh. Concentrate on sanātana-dharma. According to scriptures, give up low conduct. Durgā, the greatest devotee, does not eat meat. Why do you mistakenly offer her meat? If committing violence to jīvas were dharma, why are dacoits not called devotees? Some fish live on eating other fish. Why do you not call them followers of dharma? Killing humans and killing animals are great sins. Committing these sins, one increases the three sufferings.”

Coming to see eight armed Durgā, he gave these teachings, according to scriptures. The person who came to worship Durgā heard the Lord’s words with devotion. Hearing his words, he became detached. The brāhmaṇa gave up sacrificing the goat. The best of brāhmaṇas worshipped with flowers and bilva leaves and returned home in bliss.

The Lord offered respects to Vāmana’s feet and went to see Yajña-kuṇḍa. Leaving Tāpatī, the Lord went to the bank of the Narmadā to see Yajña-kuṇḍa at Bhaṁroca town. In Āmedābāda many people gathered to see the Lord. Seeing many people around, Gaurāṅga gave them all the name and made them mad with the name.

The Lord said, “Everyone chant the name with devotion. All suffering will go far away. No sorrow will remain. Do not hate anyone, because of being filled with pride. Without pride, chant, “Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare.” A person whose mind is not pure with bhaktirasa is not learned, being proud of his learning. The person who considers a million obstacles to be like grass becomes mad with prema. I consider him a devotee. The śrutis say that the essence of tattva is prema-bhakti.

“The devotee of the Lord, mad with prema, does not want liberation. The person who has prema-bhakti as his neck ornament is an eternal associate of Kṛṣṇa. I will not argue but serve the person who tastes the sweetness of kṛṣṇa-prema. The person who cuts the bondage of this body, being mad with kṛṣṇa-prema, is a great devotee.”

Caitanya removed the dust of māyā from the eye of knowledge, cutting the material knot. “For this reason, foolish people in this world are bewildered and think that they are this material body. The person who gives up identity with the material body is the head of the temple according to the Vedas. The highest devotee is absorbed in kṛṣṇa-prema. He does not cheat, causing disturbances. The person who does not know the chief meaning of Vedānta considers the jīva one with Brahman.”

In the middle of the jungle the king had made a place. With the Lord we went there at the side of a hill. The room was enclosed with forest wood and had no doors. All the sannyāsīs sat down there. Clapping his hands, the Lord began singing the name. Hearing the name, the sannyāsīs become intoxicated.

I went to collect wood and made a fire. Bhargadeva chanted, clapping his hands. I had no fear in the jungle. Chanting the name, everyone passed the night. The next morning, chanting the name, Gaurāṅga went out, remembering the Lord. The jungle was dense on whatever road we walked. Seeing Jhāri-khaṇḍa, my body trembled. Going a great distance, we came to a small canal. There Caitanya took his bath. After bathing we travelled quickly. Going some distance, we sat down.

The Lord told Rāmānanda to go and bring some fruit. Rāmānanda brought fruit and kept it at that place. Gathering the various types of fruit, Gaurāṅga performed worship and offered the fruit in joy. I have never seen such sweet fruit. Everyone gathered and filled themselves by eating the fruit. There was food for thousands on the road. How can I describe the mercy of the Lord? Finishing our noon duties, we departed. In the afternoon we found a house.

At the edge of the forest was an enclosure with pillars. Gaurāṅga joyfully entered. The sannyāsīs brought wood and started a fire. Clapping his hands, the Lord began singing. He sang, “Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare.” As he sang, I saw that he was becoming unsteady. His hairs stood on end and his body trembled. Gaurāṅga began to cry loudly. Seeing his state, Bharga lamented. The next day at dawn we got up and left. A group of travelers were coming on the road. When they saw there were two parties on the road, everyone joyfully chanted the Lord’s names.

In seven days, we traversed Dhanvidhara jungle. Everyone experienced bliss. We all went happily to Gopītalā near Amarāpurī. Everyone called this Prabhāsa tīrtha. I was pleased in mind to see Prabhāsa. The Yadus gave up their bodies there. The Lord went there, weeping profusely. The Yadus, becoming intoxicated with liquor fought with each other and gave up their bodies. By Kṛṣṇa’s desire all the Yadu warriors died, not recognizing themselves and others. Cārudeṣṇa, Suratni, Sātyaki, Yuyudhāna, Śāmba and Gada, all intelligent, fought with each other and died at this place.

The Lord with miserable face cried at this place. When he cried, no one could be joyful. Crying, the blissful Lord fell on the ground. To take away the lamentation and misery of the world, the name has been spread. The Lord at all times gave instruction to the people of this world: chant the name and with prema, worship the Lord. Day and night going from door to door, he drowned the world in spreading the Lord’s name.

“Who will receive the name of the Lord? Come forward. Without a price, I give this jewel to everyone. Carefully take this jewel which is priceless. Easily you will cross the ocean of saṁsāra. If one time you utter the name, you will cut your bondage and go to the eternal abode. Other than the Lord’s name, what can cut the fearful, huge, tough knot of māyā?” Saying this, the Lord went from door to door. He scattered the rasa of prema in the world of saṁsāra.

The people of Amarāpurī gathered and, on seeing the Lord, became blissful. Like a madman, he ran about. Like a madman, he wandered about. Sometimes he exhaled loudly and became unconscious. The pupils of his eyes turned upwards. His hair fell down his back. Tears fell constantly on his chest. Like a madman, his clothing fell off. His limbs were all covered with dust. Gaurāṅga said, “Where is the yajña kuṇḍa?”

The priests, going with him, showed him Prabhāsa. We went south of Prabhāsa and there saw the kuṇḍa. The Lord offered wood in this sacrifice. At this place, the Yadus fought and died. At this place Satyabhāmā made a pleasant forest and came with Kṛṣṇa here. She used to live here in the highest bliss. Seeing this place, intelligent Gaurāṅga began crying. He shouted and constantly wept, calling out loudly. After a while, ten priests came to see the place. Discussing here for three days, the Lord then went to Dvārakā.

He did not see any other tīrthas at Prabhāsa. We arrived at Dvārakā in early Āśvina month. Constantly chanting Kṛṣṇa’s name, the Lord travelled. We went on the shore to Dvārakā. After four days we arrived at the estuary of the ocean. We crossed on a rope. In this way we arrived at Dvārakā. We could see Raivataka mountain. Gaurāṅga looked at the mountain in a daze. He laughed with a smirk. What was he thinking, with happy face? Mahāprabhu, laughing, glanced at me. Looking at me, he said, “Going to Dvārakā, offering respects, everyone should become successful. Rub the dust on all your limbs with devotion. You have seen Dvārakā as a result of puṇya. On the strength of previous births of sukṛti, you all see Dvārakā today.”

Hearing this, they all together offered respects. Gaurāṅga’s well of bliss rose. Saying, “Haribol,” the Lord rolled in the dust. The ocean of bhāva increased, crossing the boundaries. Who could control the Lord? He turned over and over on the ground. Eventually he entered within the city.

With hairs standing on end, his body began trembling. His eyes opened and tears flowed. He said, “Where are you, Dvārakādhīśa?” He flooded Dvārakā with his tears. His hair came loose. In a loud voice, Gaurāṅga cried. One cannot describe this bhāva. The Lord shouted repeatedly, “Kṛṣṇa!” When he entered the temple of Dvārakādhīśa, he became mad with bliss with double feeling. His hairs stood on end like a kadamba. Rolling on the ground, he became covered with dust.

Mad with bhāva, the Lord glazed with intoxication. He rolled on the ground in front of Dvārakādhīśa. As bhakti increased, he fell in the four directions and cried loudly in joy. Closing his eyes, he looked within. What did he see inside? Sometimes he glanced upwards. Tears flowed from his eyes. On seeing Kṛṣṇa, his hairs stood on end. He remained staring at him.

He circumambulated the temple three times and humbly offered respects each time. In joy Gaurāṅga offered respects with eight limbs. Seeing this, Bhargadeva rolled on the ground. Gradually, it became known in Dvārakā. Everyone talked about the Lord.

Someone said, “Brother! Go and see this sannyāsī. No one has seen such a sannyāsī. No one can completely describe what they said. I do not think that there is another such sannyāsī on earth. I have never seen such extraordinary bhāva. That sannyāsī is excellent in form and qualities. If you see him, bhakti to him arises in the mind. One sees tears in his eyes. I desire to go and associate with that sannyāsī. If you see him, brother, you cut material bondage. If you see him, you will no longer have a taste for saṁsāra. Seeing his form, the sinful person becomes immediately pure. I have never seen such a merciful person. He shows mercy, distributing the holy name to everyone. His locks spread over his outer cloth. On seeing his form, desires are fulfilled.

“When he sees an avatāra of the Lord, it seems his heart is always full of bhakti-rasa. The young sannyāsī always intoxicated with bhāva, made the Dvārakā inhabitants most joyful. He has no kāma, no anger, and no material desire. He has flawless faith in Dvārakādhīśa. Calling the sinful, he gives them the name. Seeing him, the heart becomes materially detached.”

Gaurāṅga remained in Dvārakā for a fortnight. Daily he went to see the lord of Dvārakā. Daily he went to see the Lord with devotion and intoxicated the people of Dvārakā with bhakti-rasa.

The people of Dvārakā, surrendered to bhakti, all went to see Gaurāṅga. He discussed with all of them. Doing kīrtana, everyone danced in prema. The city became unsteady with the bhāva of dharma. Everyone’s heart became pure. Gentle breeze began to blow softly at all times so that all direction became fragrant with the smell of flowers. Everyone was blissful with the Lord’s association. All women and men came running.

One could see signs of auspiciousness in the four directions. All directions were pleased with the Lord’s names. Priests and householders gathered and heard instructions of dharma and spread that hearing. The Lord enlightened the persons who could not understand him. The Lord spoke various words and intoxicated them. Sometimes the Lord spoke indistinctly. Speaking indistinctly he made everyone understand. Somehow, the lord, possessor of all śaktis, made people understand. Hearing his instructions, they all became unconscious. Jñānīs and fools all came. Seeing the Lord their hairs stood on end.

One day in the evening, the Lord arrived quietly at the Kṛṣṇa’s temple. Many people followed the Lord. Not saying anything, Śacīnandana went to the door of the temple and offered respects with eight limbs. Seeing this, all the people became choked up. Folding his hands, the Lord offered many praises. Immediately tears gushed from his eyes. His chest flooded with the water of prema. He saw Kṛṣṇa in whatever direction he glanced.

Closing his eyes, he shouted, “Kṛṣṇa!” His hairs stood on end as he became filled with prema. How will I tell the person who has not seen this bhāva that he is a great fool? The river of Caitanya flowed everywhere except the desert. No sinful person remained in the whole province. The Lord delivered the sinful by bhakti.

One day the priests created joy. They made a huge festival and arranged an offering. They invited devotee guests and distributed milk, yogurt, and purīs. Gaurāṅga, jewel of good qualities, went among the lame and personally distributed prasāda. In the night everyone met and went to the cottage. In great joy, we passed the night. In this way, discussions were held for a fortnight. The next day the Lord left Dvārakā. The Lord said, “This time, I will go to Nīlācala. Meeting everyone there, we will pass time in bliss. With everyone I will go to Vidyā-nagara. I will not go to Purī alone, giving up Rāmānanda Rāya. He is a great bhajanānandī. Thinking of his conversations, my heart is pacified. He is the crest jewel of sādhakas. Living alone, he sings about the qualities of Kṛṣṇa. Bhāgavatam says that detached devotee is he who sheds tears on saying, “Hare Kṛṣṇa.” If a cobbler calls out to Kṛṣṇa, a treasure, with devotion, I offer a million respects to his feet. Rāmānanda, a devotee of Kṛṣṇa, is to be worshipped. He is dearer than my life. I am fond of Rāmānanda like my very life. He is the greatest devotee and a renounced sannyāsī. He is detached from material objects. He always sees Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. He considers great wealth to be like grass. Day and night, he calls out “Kṛṣṇa” with prema. He shouts, “I have seen Kṛṣṇa.” Mad with prema, Rāya stands there trembling. By the name of Kṛṣṇa, he discharges tears of prema. Becoming unconscious, he falls on the ground. My prāṇa cannot tolerate separation from Rāya. I will go quickly to Rāya.”

Saying this, the Lord left. Hundreds of people followed. With sweet words he bid the people farewell. He walked near Khāḍī. He took Bhargadeva’s group as support. They came to the bank of the Khāḍī in order to cross. One by one, they all crossed. My sannyāsī came to Gujarāṭ. At the end of Āśvina we returned to Barodā town. The Lord chanted the name. I and Govinda Caraṇa went to a householder’s door to beg. I received alms of fruits, roots and ground wheat flour. In a pure manner I brought the goods back and prepared it. Making a residence at the base of a tree the Lord offered the food. Taking prasāda everyone was satisfied.

The next day, we journeyed from Barodā. The Lord went south. After sixteen days we came to the bank of the Narmadā River. We all bathed in the Narmadā water. The Lord said, “Bhargadeva where will you go? Will you go with me to Purī?”

Bharga, squeezing his hands in front of the Lord said, “I will go south. King Mohanta Āditya serves Raṇa-choḍa-jī with devotion at Bombom Nagara. Receive my respects. Mercifully put your feet on my head.”

Saying this, Bhargadeva rolled on the ground and held the Lord’s feet with his two hands. He said, “You are Kṛṣṇa. You are my Lord. Give me the gift of remembering your feet when I die. You went to Dvārakā to see your own pastimes, to fool the people. Put your feet on my head. You cannot fool me anymore.”

The Lord said, “O Bharga! Why do you say this? Why say this to me? Being detached, I wander on various paths. I have no food and no clothing. I am most deprived. I go door to door begging. Do not say such words to me again. Say the name of Kṛṣṇa with faith. That faith in Kṛṣṇa will enable you to meet Kṛṣṇa. The Supreme Lord is condensed knowledge and bliss. By bhāva for his lotus feet you will cross the material ocean. Think of the Lord’s feet with prema-bhakti. He will definitely show himself to you. The Lord is very merciful to the devotee. Meditate upon him. He is the shelter of the shelterless.”

Saying this, the Lord touched Bhargadeva. At that moment Bharga’s body became pure. Standing with folded hands, Bhargadeva looked around. Having become successful, finally he left.

There were some sannyāsīs with Bharga. They came and stood in front of the Lord. One by one they offered respects to the Lord. The Lord departed from them after saying sweet words. Bhargadeva went south. The Lord travelled to Purī with attraction. With Rāmānanda and Govinda Caraṇa, I spent the day on the bank of the Narmadā. The next day we walked along the bank and arrived at Dohada town. Begging, I brought some flour. The Lord made chapattis and offered them.

We spent the night at Dohada town. Under a tree, Gaurāṅga chanted the name. At dawn we rose and went to Kukṣī town. We saw may devotees there. Wherever we went we saw tulasī groves. All the villagers were devotees of Kṛṣṇa. In the evening, everyone chanted the name. Seeing this, my Lord’s hairs stood on end. One poor brāhmaṇa lived there. In his house he had deities of Lakṣmī and Janardana. He worshipped them with devotion. Hearing this, the Lord went to his house. The brāhmaṇa seeing the Lord welcomed him with many services. He asked many questions in serving the guest. “I am called a brāhmaṇa but I am poor. Why do you put your feet in my house? How can I serve a sannyāsī? I think dharma has been destroyed in my house.”

The Lord said, “Do not worry sir! The creator will give food in plenty. Everyone is mistaken concerning why and how the Lord acts. Kṛṣṇa thinks of everything always. Please listen to the bare facts. The creator says that he gives to all so that they can eat. Why does the person without friends eat under a tree? Who will give food to a miserable insect in a forest? O brāhmaṇa, why do you think falsely?”

At that time one vaiśya brought milk, sugar and flour to the brāhmaṇa’s house and gave it to him.

The vaiśya said, “Listen O respected brāhmaṇa! You have received great mercy. In a dream I saw your Lakṣmī Janārdana. They wanted to eat sweet rice in my house. Nārāyaṇa, in human form, stays in your house. In the dream Nārāyaṇa showed this to me.

I saw this last night in a dream. Thus, I have brought milk and sugar. O brāhmaṇa! Cooking the sweet rice, offer it to Nārāyaṇa.”

Hearing of this incident the brāhmaṇa began crying. He said, “From where has the milk and sugar come?”

The Lord said, “Nārāyaṇa has supplied it.” The brāhmaṇa said, “I am a sufferer. This is astonishing.” The Lord said, “Nārāyaṇa… ”.

The brāhmaṇa said, “I thought about you.”

The Lord said, “Nārāyaṇa has brought this.”

The vaiśya glanced at the Lord’s face, becoming unconscious on seeing something.

The brāhmaṇa said, “O vaiśya! What are you seeing, O brother?”

The vaiśya said, “I see something confusing. Listen to what I say to you, O best of brāhmaṇas! In the dream I saw that human form of this sannyāsī.”

Hearing this, the Lord said to the vaiśya, “Why do you cause trouble with this lie again and again? Who did you see in your false dream? Why do you without reasons cause disturbance? O vaiśya brother! You are most fortunate. Thus, the Lord showed himself in a dream. I am an ordinary sannyāsī. I have come to eat at the brāhmaṇa’s house.”

The brāhmaṇa said, “What is the necessity of this talk? I want to serve the guest, thinking of Nārāyaṇa.”

Weeping, the brāhmaṇa said to the Lord, “Cooking the sweet rice. You should offer it.”

Smiling, the Lord cooked the sweet rice. The brāhmaṇa walked about nearby. In bliss everyone took prasāda. With his own hand the Lord distributed the prasāda. There was huge festival in the brāhmaṇa’s house. The next day in the morning the Lord rose and started his journey. While he walked, the brāhmaṇa came near. He spoke miserable words, with folded hands. The Lord bid him farewell and went out in the morning. The vaiśya hid himself. He followed behind the Lord on the path. Holding the Lord’s feet, he began weeping.

Merciful Caitanya lifted him up. The Lord said, “You are devotee. What are you doing, brother?”

The vaiśya said, “Be merciful to me, O Gosāi. I think I cannot leave you. Give me the mercy of the dust from your feet.”

Laughing, Caitanya offered once the sweet name of the Lord in his ear. By the Lord’s mercy, his sins were destroyed. Being completely detached, the vaiśya went away. By the mercy of the Lord, the vaiśya giving up material life, made a tulasī grove far away and stayed there. He did not converse with people. He always meditated on Kṛṣṇa with flute in his mouth. He said “O Lord! Give me mercy. Destroy all the suffering of this sinner by your mercy.”

Staying in a hut, he did not go to his house. Saying the name of the Lord, he would beg from door to door. Viśvambhara gave mercy to the vaiśya and left the town, going through the jungle.

We all went through the dense jungle and for two days did not see any town or village. Leaving the jungle after two days, we arrived at Āmajhorā town. We were disturbed with hunger pains. The unchanging Lord said to me, “Hari! Hari!”

He said, “When the Lord brings food then I will accept food.”

I begged and brought two seras of flour. The Lord made sixteen chapattis. At that time one girl came. She said, “Give me a little as I am hungry. I have no food, no clothing. I have nothing to eat. Going on the road with children I beg and eat.”

Hearing her words, the merciful Lord gave her his food. The suffering girl went away satisfied and the Lord spent the day without eating. In the evening I begged and brought some fruit. The Lord ate the fruit and passed the night. At this place there was Lakṣmaṇa-kuṇḍa. Hearing of this, in the morning we went there. The attractive pond was at the edge of the town. It was small, surrounded by hills. When Sītā was thirsty, Lakṣmaṇa shot an arrow and made this kuṇḍa. It became famous as Lakṣmaṇa’s kuṇḍa. Sītā said it was a great tīrtha. It was most attractive and very deep. Bathing in it, the body became cool.

Merciful Gaurāṅga bathed in the tīrtha and chanted the name. On hearing this, the heart melted. The next day we went to the top of Vindhya Mountain, where there was beautiful Mandurā town. In a cave in the mountain one man lived, performing austerities. Hearing of this man, Gaurāṅga went there to see him.

The sannyāsī lived there meditating. His form was very sweet. His limbs were the color of molten gold. Rays of his effulgence spread in the four directions. He had long fingernails. His beard and moustache hung over his chest. His body was just skin and bones. He considered his body as a cage. Naked, he meditated with concentration. Seeing that form, bhakti arose. He seemed like a wooden deity. I looked at him repeatedly.

Mahāprabhu came in front of him and stood there. The austere man broke his meditation and began looking around. At that moment their four eyes met. Suddenly the tapasvī laughed. The Lord held a conversation with him. He then descended the mountain and came to Maṇḍala town.

On the left was Vindhya mountain and on the right was the Narmadā river. In three days, we went from there to Devaghara. At Devaghara there was a leper. He was called Ādi-nārāyaṇa. He was the best of merchants. He was wealthy but always mentally overwhelmed.

There was a banyan tree outside the village. The merciful Lord stayed near it. The four directions were illuminated by the Lord’s beauty. This became well known to the public. Several persons came to see the sannyāsī. I went to the town to collect alms. Rāmānanda went to collect flowers. Govinda Caraṇa went to the riverbank.

That day I received ātapa rice. Rāmānanda brought various types of flowers. After bathing, the Lord started worship. Govinda brought dry wood. Offering food, Gaurāṅga began chanting. As he chanted, his hairs stood on end. Choked with prema, he began dancing. Becoming unconscious, he fell on the earth. Hearing this, many people came. The leper came and stood by his side. He came and cried, folding his hands. He said while profusely weeping, “Deliver me.”

Ādi-nārāyaṇa was the best of devotees. The Lord made him eat prasāda. Eating with devotion, he became free of leprosy. Taking prasāda, his leprosy was destroyed. Hearing the news, many sick people came. Seeing the confusion, the Lord looked around. He glanced at me, giving a signal. Taking the wood shoes, I started the journey. On that pretext the Lord left the town. Ādi-nārāyaṇa went along with him.

The Lord said, “You have become freed by the mercy of Kṛṣṇa. Why do you come with me? Go to your house and meditate on Kṛṣṇa’s feet.”

Ādi-nārāyaṇa said, “I will not go home. I will travel with you from place to place.”

The Lord said, “Go home and enjoy your wealth.”

Ādi-nārāyaṇa said, “What is the use of wealth? O merciful Lord! If you do not let me accompany you, I will spend my time locked in a hut.”

The Lord said, “Make a tulasī garden. Live there and pass your time.”

Hearing the Lord’s words, Ādi-nārāyaṇa gave up everything and chanted, “Hari Hari.”

He became the best devotee. He passed his time in chanting Kṛṣṇa’s name. Offering respects to the Lord’s feet, Ādi-nārāyaṇa bid farewell to the Lord.

At thirty krośa’s distance was Śivānī town. Viśvambara went there in two days. Malaya mountain was east of Śivānī. The Lord went there with great attraction. The Lord saw Malaya mountain and then came to the town of Caṇḍīpura. Seeing Caṇḍī devī at Caṇḍīpura, Gaurāṅga went to Rāyapura while remembering the Lord.

At Rāyapura, many people, desiring to see the Lord, came near him. Gaurāṅga, seeing the misfortune of the jīvas, spread bliss of the name at every house. The Lord then went to Vidyānagara. Seeing Rāmānanda, he folded his hands. Rāmānanda Rāya came and offered respects. Holding his hand, the Lord embraced him. The greatest devotee backed away. He began weeping, with great humility.

The Lord said, “O Rāya! Come with me. Together we will go and see Jagannātha. You, I and Bhaṭṭācārya will go to Purī and chant the named to full satisfaction. I will be very happy speaking tattva with you. Come with me, we will go to Purī.”

Rāya said, “You go ahead. A few days later I will go to Purī.”

Hearing this, Mahāprabhu smiled slightly and, rising at dawn, went north. That day we went very far and in six days the four of us arrived to Ratnapura. Leaving Ratnapura, we came to the Mahānadī River. Going along its bank we travelled east. At some distance, Mahāprabhu went to Svarṇagaḍa. The Lord saw the beauty of the town. How can I describe the beauty of the astonishing fort? Looking in four directions it was amazing. A king named Śāntīśvara lived at the fort. A messenger went and spoke to the king. Hearing the pleasant news, the king went humbly to see the Lord.

Seeing the Lord, the very pious king folded his hands and rolled on the ground. The king said, “O respected sannyāsī! Please listen. Please purify my house today. Be merciful and today eat at my house.”

Saying this, the king recited many prayers. Hearing this, the Lord glanced at me. I wanted to take alms from the king. The king gave plenty of alms, standing with folded hands. In the afternoon the king bid farewell. Mahāprabhu spent the night under the tree. In the morning, we all went to Sambala-pura. We arrived in the evening. The city was very beautiful, surrounded by mountains. We spent the night happily in Sambala-pura. At ten krośa’s distance was Bhramarā town. Mahāprabhu went there. Many devotees lived in Bhramarā. The Lord stayed there four days.

An Oriya brāhmaṇa named Viṣṇu-rudra stayed there serving Kṛṣṇa. He was very devotional. The Lord went to see him. The Lord conversed with him and then went happily to Pratāpana town. Giving them the name of the Lord, he then went to Dāsa-pāla, where a pāṣaṇḍa magician who greatly suffered lived. The Lord made everyone insane with the holy name. Gaurāṅga was at all times mad with bliss. By the name he delivered many sinners.

The next day, we went to Rasāla-kuṇḍa. There we saw Kūrma-deva. Seeing Kūrma, the Lord went mad with prema. Tears streamed rapidly from his eyes. Folding his hands, he recited many verses in praise of Kūrma-deva. He fell with force on the ground. The people of Rasāla-kuṇḍa had almost no devotion. Seeing that, the Lord stayed there three days. Calling all men and women, the Lord delivered them by the holy name. By the Lord’s mercy all became intoxicated. They began to chant the name with devotion.

At that place there was a Māḍuyā Brāhmaṇa. His son met the Lord. The son said, “Be merciful to me. Take away my sorrow by the dust of your feet. I am a great rascal. I know nothing. Deliver me by bhakti. My father cannot tolerate Kṛṣṇa’s name. Mercifully give bhakti to his heart. It is sad that my father hates Kṛṣṇa. O merciful Lord! Give bhakti to his mind. When he sees devotees, he scolds them. Mercifully destroy all his sins. I have heard that you are the abode of mercy. O merciful Lord! Give this benediction.”

Hearing the child, the Lord put his hand on his back. Suddenly bhakti arose in his heart. Hearing this, the brāhmaṇa became blind with anger. With stick in hand, he ran to the Lord. The brāhmaṇa said, “Listen, O wicked pretender! You have destroyed my only son. I will beat you with this stick. See who can protect you here.”

Folding his hands, the son while weeping said, “Please by your mercy forgive the offense of my father. My most ignorant father does not know you. Forgive me, as a servant of your feet.” Hearing this, several men came to beat the brāhmaṇa. Not listening to the anyone’s words, the brāhmaṇa started to beat Caitanya with his stick.

The brāhmaṇa said, “You made my son into a devotee. Deceiving him, you will take him away. Bewildering the boy, where will you go? This time I will punish you so you will understand. I have seen many sannyāsīs. You will receive teachings from me.”

Smiling, Caitanya said, “O brother! Listen. When you beat me, I want the name. As many times as you chant the name, that many times you can beat me. If you want to beat me, then chant the names Hare Kṛṣṇa. I offer you my back. One time chant the name and beat me.”

Hearing this, the brāhmaṇa’s son folded his hands and facing the Lord spoke. He said, “O Lord! Forgive my father. Deliver him from hell. I beg at your lotus feet that peaceful Nimāi does not speak like this to people. “

Slightly smiling, the Lord said to him, “Anyone born in this family will not fear hell. Millions of men will go to Vaikuṇṭha.”

Saying this he glanced at the brāhmaṇa and said, “O brāhmaṇa! Say the name of the Lord while beating me. Your hard heart is almost like a desert. May it become sweet by the mercy of Kṛṣṇa! There is no harm if you beat me. O brother! Just say one time Hare Kṛṣṇa.”

Hearing such words, the brāhmaṇa began trembling. In fear he urinated in his clothing. He cowered and could not see. Overcome with weeping, he fell on the ground. By the influence of the Lord, the brāhmaṇa, overwhelmed, held the Lord’s feet with his hands.

The brāhmaṇa said, “O merciful Lord! I request you to deliver me from hell. Having offended you, I am most fearful. By your mercy, forgive my offense. O merciful one! Not knowing, I said many things to you. O protector! Give punishment that you judge fit.”

Seeing the humility of the brāhmaṇa, leaning forward, Gaurāṅga joyfully gave the nectar of the name in his ear. The brāhmaṇa became successful and his mind became pure. He took permission to leave, holding the Lord’s feet.

Having delivered the pāṣaṇḍa brāhmaṇa, the Lord went to the bank of Ṛṣi-kulyā River. On both sides of the river many sages lived. All came and requested Gaurāṅga. When the Lord came to that river, the news reached Purī. The Lord stayed there for three days. He purified Ṛṣi-kulyā with the name of the Lord. When the Lord came to Ālālanātha, Gadādhara and Murārī came running.

Khañjana Ācārya came with deep attachment. Tracing out the Lord, he came before the others. Sārvabhauma came blowing two horns. Narahari was seen with a flag. Haridāsa, Rāmadāsa and Kṛṣṇadāsa came quickly, breathing heavily. Jagannāthadāsa and Devakī-nandana, Choṭa Haridāsa, the singer Lakṣmaṇa, Viṣṇudāsa, Purīdāsa, and Dāmodara, Nārāyaṇa-tīrtha and Giridharadāsa, Sarasavatī Purī and unlimited brāhmaṇas came in lines to see the Lord. Rāma Paṇḍita played a horn. Balarāmadāsa came, his hairs standing on end. Hundreds of scholars were seen. My heart began dancing in bliss.

Some danced, some laughed, and some sang. With one mouth one cannot describe the bliss. Thousands of people surrounded the Lord. Mad with bliss, they all started to chant the name. Murāri and Mukunda came and embraced him. Gupta fell down on his knees nearby. Siddha Kṛṣṇadāsa came and offered respects. Taking his hand, the Lord embraced him. All the devotees together brought the Lord. Many devotees were beating drums, making the Lord’s eyes move about in bliss.

Many devotees joined in kīrtana. Gaurāṅga, swinging his head danced in joy. Seeing Khañjana, the Lord, saying ‘Hari bol,’ spreading his arms, embraced him. Spreading his arms, Gaurāṅga began dancing. Sārvabhauma rolled at his feet. Folding his hands, Sārvabhauma began to speak.

“The arrow of separation pierced my heart. I tolerated your separation as a great fool. I maintained my life for so many days. Mercifully tread upon me with your feet. Then I will know that you have affection for me.”

Saying this, Sārvabhauma’s voice choked up. Gaurāṅga lifted him up and embraced him. While chanting the Lord’s name, everyone went to Purī with the Lord. Hundreds upon hundreds of blue and white flags flapped and many drums sounded. Some people danced, some sang, intoxicated with bliss. Many people stood staring. Leaning and swaying, Śacīnandana walked. Sweet mṛdaṅgas sounded, pleasant to hear. Raising his hands, Gadādhara danced. Raghunātha dāsa and Dāmodara also danced.

Respecting him, the Lord gave attention to Raghunātha. “I attain great bliss seeing you.”

Gaurāṅga embraced him. Raghunātha rolled at his feet. On the third day of Māgha, Gaurāṅga arrived at Purī with all these devotees. In the afternoon Mahāprabhu arrived at Purī. Millions of people came to visit him.

Many people walked with the Lord, with bare feet. They entered the temple and saw Jagannātha. With fixed gaze they looked, and tears began to flow. Gaurāṅga became unconscious and fell forcibly on the earth. His hair came undone and his kaupīna became loose. He looked miserable with dust covered body. The devotees chanted the name in the four directions. Sārvabhauma held him in his lap. His hairs stood on end like a kadamba flower. Thousands of streams of perspiration began to flow. Reviving himself, Caitanya stood up. He fixed his eyes on the deity, Mahāviṣṇu.

Sārvabhauma said, “O Lord! Seeing your form of Jagannātha your well of prema manifests. You teach people by looking at your form. Mad with mahābhāva, you begin to cry. In front of unmoving Viṣṇu you move about. O Lord! Why do you weep? You are pretending. You are directly the son of Nanda. Why do you blind my eyes? Do this to those who do not understand. Do not steal your form from me. You are the holder of Govardhana. You are the master of Vṛndāvana. You are the life of the gopīs. You are the shelter of the shelterless. Those born in the Yadu dynasty did not know you. The unfortunate Yadus knew nothing. Having you in my hand, I will not give you up. Show to me your form holding the flute. On the chest of your golden form is a line by whose power the form of time cannot be seen.”

The Lord said, “O Sārvabhauma! Speak something else. Why do you not speak other subjects? Why do you speak various words, agitated with illusion? Hearing your words, I plug my ears. You say I am Kṛṣṇa. Why without cause do you make me an offender? From your mouth, topics of Kṛṣṇa are like nectar. O Bhaṭṭa! Speak about Kṛṣṇa and soothe my prāṇa.”

Bhaṭṭa said, “O Lord! What can I speak but what you make me speak? I am just a human animal.”

The Lord said, “There is no use in speaking further. Let us go home now together.”

After seeing the ārati, the Lord went to Kaśī Miśra’s house with his associates. At that time Sārvabhauma obtained prasāda and brought it there. Gaurāṅga in joy distributed the prasāda. All were blissful in receiving prasāda.

In the large yard of Kaśī Miśra’s house, many people came to see the Lord. Staying in Miśra’s house, merciful Gaurāṅga in great bliss danced and sang songs about Kṛṣṇa. I cannot describe how many people came and went. There was ever fresh happiness in Miśra’s house. Hearing of the Lord’s arrival by mouth, many inhabitants of Bengal came to see him.

Bhaṭṭa brought prasāda daily. The Lord was blissful on receiving prasāda. With joy, Gaurāṅga received the prasāda. He distributed prasāda to everyone’s hands. Before prasāda there was nāmasaṅkīrtana. Everyone ate prasāda with attraction to prema. I was fortunate to speak this now. Everyone danced in bliss, clapping their hands.

Rāmānānda Vasu and Govinda Caraṇa bid farewell and went to Bengal. Wanting to see Gaurāṅga again, hundreds of people came with attraction. Śrivāsa, Keśava dāsa, Siddha Haridāsa all came gathered near Caitanya. Śāntācārya, Vipra-dāsa, Rūpa and Sanātana came to see him. Chanting the name, they all became mad with bliss. Merciful Caitanya gave bhakti door to door.

“Who will receive the holy name? O my brothers, come!” Nimāi, saying this, distributed the name of the Lord. By the Lord’s mercy no sinner and no sufferer remained. The Lord gave the name of the Lord everywhere. The great tīrtha of Purī became the abode of bliss. Children, women and elders chanted the name. Hearing the name, animals and birds danced. The ocean with arms spread danced in front. Old women, young people and babies danced. Chaste women came to see him on the road. On one side the ocean began dancing. In the other direction, an ocean of prema arose. The trees and creepers became mad with prema. Snow fell on the pretext of showering tears. There was constant, fresh happiness in Purī. Those who saw know. Who should I speak to? Mrdaṅgas, kettle drums and karatālas sounded. Among them, my Śacīnandana danced.

Rāmadāsa very skilfully played the kettle drum. For that reason, he came constantly to the kīrtana. Rāmadāsa was a great devotee, attracted to prema. Playing the kettle drum, he walked in front of the kīrtana. In bliss Pratāparudra gave up royal duties and came to Miśra’s house constantly to see the dancing.

When Mahāprabhu went on nagara kīrtana, the king followed behind wearing humble clothing. With hands raised, his body limp, the Lord danced and danced, mad with prema. With half closed eyes, he stared at the sky. Sometimes he fell on the ground. All men and women became mad with the name of the Lord. Mad with prema, the family women came running in lines. Thousands of people came on four sides. The Lord moved in front, dancing and dancing.

In this way the Lord passed the day. He spent the night with Rāmānanda alone. One day Mahāprabhu with attraction to Kṛṣṇa, ran in front to catch Mahāviṣṇu. Not considering any obstacle, he ran with attraction. In front there was a hidden obstacle and he fell on the ground.

From that day, the Lord did not go into the temple. He saw the deity from far away. Standing at the door of the bhoga-maṇḍapa, he gazed at Mahāviṣṇu. Putting his left hand on the Garuḍa-stambha, he saw the deity, mad with prema. In this way for some days, he stayed in Purī.

Wanting to see Jagannātha out of attraction, one day my Lord, at Miśra’s house, sang about the qualities of Kṛṣṇa with the devotees. He called “Govinda” and said that I should go to Advaita Ācārya. Taking leave with a letter and an order, I departed for Śāntipura and offered respects. Putting his hand on my back, the Lord gave blessings. Hundreds of tears flowed from my eyes. He said, “Do not cry, Govinda, my life! Bringing this to the ācārya, be blissful.” Hearing these words, my eyes flowed with tears. I could not tolerate the arrow of separation from the Lord. How can I tolerate the intense separation from the Lord? Cruel pain arose in my mind.
