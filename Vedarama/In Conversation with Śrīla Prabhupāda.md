## Foreword

In writing this book, *In Conversation with Śrīla Prabhupāda*, Śrīpāda Lokanāth Mahārāja is rendering a great service to the Vaiṣṇava community, and indeed the whole world by his presentation of his exchanges with our beloved ISKCON Founder-Ācārya, Srila A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda. The importance of this endeavor cannot be overstated. If the events that we call history are to be known, they have to be recorded, and it is the responsibility of the participants to ensure that this record is preserved and passed on to future generations. Failure to do so constitutes a great loss for mankind, especially in the matter of spiritual transcendence.

In *Caitanya-caritamrta (Antya* [14.7](https://vedarama.com/en/books/cc-antya/14.7)) it is mentioned that, *“Svarūpa Dāmodara Gosvāmī and Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī witnessed Caitanya Mahāprabhu’s activities firsthand and recorded them in two notebooks. Therefore, without reference to these notebooks one cannot understand the activities of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu…”*

Śrīla Kṛṣṇadās Kavirājā goes on to say, *“In those days, Svarūpa Dāmodara and Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī lived with Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, whereas all other commentators lived far away from Him.”*

In his purport Śrīla Prabhupāda comments: *“Besides Svarūpa Dāmodara and Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī, there were many others who also recorded Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu’s activities. Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura believes that the people of the world would benefit greatly if such notes were available. It is a most unfortunate situation for human society that none of these notebooks is still extant.”*

How important is it then, that the immediate followers of Śrīla Prabhupāda record their experiences with him? We should not think that because modern technology has enabled easy and prolific access to all kinds of information that there is no need to commit our experiences to the written word, nor think that because Śrīla Prabhupāda had many thousands of followers that there is no need for each and every one of us to write down our individual experiences. We do not know what accounts will survive the ravages of time and what will be left for future generations to enable them to understand the true nature of one of the greatest exponents of transcendental life and thought that this world has ever known. Writing this book means that Lokanāth Mahārāja has not left his conversations with Śrīla Prabhupāda to chance encounters of yesteryears, but offers us an eternal legacy.

In 1698 a Christian mathematician friend of Sir Isaac Newton, John Craig, wrote *Theologiae Christianae Principia Mathematica* (*Mathematical Principles of Christian Theology*). Craig presented a formula that describes how the probability of a historical event depends on the number of primary witnesses, on the chain of transmission through secondary witnesses, on the elapsed time and on the spatial distance. Using this formula, Craig derived that the probability of the story of Jesus would reach zero in the year 3150 (*Wikipedia reference*).

Whether one accepts his formula or not, his premise is undeniably important – that without written records, actual historical events soon disappear into myth. When we consider that by the time of Lord Caitanya’s appearance the holy *dhama* of Vṛndāvana and the places of Lord Krsna’s *lilas* were all but forgotten, we should realize the necessity for each and every disciple of Śrīla Prabhupāda to follow the footsteps of Lokanāth Mahārāja and make our mark in history by writing what we know and experienced in our privileged time spent with His Divine Grace. Not to do so would be a travesty, and a disservice to our spiritual master and all devotees.

Lokanāth Mahārāja has not just presented the events of his interchanges with Śrīla Prabhupāda, but he has further enriched his readers’ understanding by his “3Cs” format in each chapter: *Context, Conversation, Contemplation*. He leads us on a very personal yet universal journey into the heart of his surrender and service to his Guru Mahārāja. In accessible and straight-forward recollections, he uncovers many gems and nuggets of spiritual reciprocation which deeply enrich our understandings and feelings for Śrīla Prabhupāda.

In the words of the late Carl Sagan, astronomer and television presenter: “What an astonishing thing a book is. It’s a flat object made from a tree with flexible parts on which are imprinted lots of funny dark squiggles. But one glance at it and you’re inside the mind of another person, maybe somebody dead for thousands of years. Across the millennia, an author is speaking clearly and silently inside your head, directly to you. Writing is perhaps the greatest of human inventions, binding together people who never knew each other, citizens of distant epochs. Books break the shackles of time. A book is proof that humans are capable of working magic.”

Lokanāth Mahārāja has worked his magic by his ongoing efforts to ensure that not only are the events of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s preaching not lost by the shifting sands of time, but that the mind and heart of him and his followers will continue to transform the lives of countless souls for millennia to come.

*Your humble servant,*  
*Hari Śauri Dāsa*  
*Nityānanda Trayodaśī 2014*  
*Māyāpur*

## Introduction

Śrīla Prabhupāda would often say, “if you wish to remember it, write it down”. In writing this book, I reflect upon Śrīla Prabhupāda’s insistent message on writing: “Realisation means you should write. Every one of you. What is your realisation…You write your realisation... what you have realised about Kṛṣṇa. That is required.” (*Śrīla Prabhupāda lecture, 14 August 1972*)

I have always been inspired to write, given Śrīla Prabhupāda’s keenness for his disciples and everyone else to write. Writing this book would mean following one of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s instructions and more importantly, means remembering Śrīla Prabhupāda in an intimate way. This involved sifting through approximately 1,384 conversation transcripts and then selecting and organizing those that had direct relevance to me and in which I was engaged.

In thinking through the title for this book, I recall stumbling upon a book several years ago which caught my attention. *Conversations with God*, by Larry King Walsch in 1995, was a publishing phenomenon and I was somewhat intrigued by the title of this bestseller. His inspiration for writing that book however, was different to mine. He questions God about his failed existence, but mine is about how to develop a fuller and more insightful spiritual life. While Walsch “spoke” to God directly, my conversations are with the representative of God, Śrīla Prabhupāda.

After some deliberation I finally settled on the title for this book: *In Conversation with Śrīla Prabhupāda: Contexts and Contemplations*, which represents a collection of conversations in which I reflect highlights of my interaction with Śrīla Prabhupāda over several years. The conversations are chronologically described from 1972 to 1977, and capture specifically those conversations on morning walks or room conversations in which I had been an active participant. Although I had always taken the opportunity to join Śrīla Prabhupāda on his morning walks, it was often a challenge to have an exclusive conversation with him because many disciples, well-wishers and curious onlookers would join him. The unrecorded conversations were created through recollections that are still vivid for me, so reconstructing these did not present much difficulty.

The reader will find it more meaningful if they understand how the book has been structured. The book consists of more than 40 conversations and each are represented as separate conversations. The conversations are unique and totally spontaneous and are divided into three sections. Firstly, the *context* preambles each conversation and captures the background of each conversation. Secondly, the *conversation*, either recorded or unrecorded and hence created, follows the context. Thirdly, the section on *contemplation* in each chapter is a way of offering closing reflections of each. In some of the chapters, multiple themes are identified and these have been collated because of the common context.

While conceding that memory is selective and may include nostalgia and opinions, the recollections of the past provided a means to recreate these unrecorded conversations. In many ways the recollections create for me new ways of understanding, allows me new ways of continuing with my spiritual quest, and inspires me to continue with my work.

The conversations of Śrīla Prabhupāda are “living moments” in dialogical exchanges with me and other disciples and well- wishers. From the conversations we detect the energetic way that Śrīla Prabhupāda spoke, full of conviction. His speech, just like his movements, especially during the morning walks, was smooth and responsive, never static and repetitive. His ability to reflect both on material and spiritual life, his astute observations about human behaviour and society, his clear stances backed with authority of the śāstra and the divine lineage of spiritual masters, and his gentle as well as incisive comments shaped my thinking and practice, as he did for all of us.

In appraising my own development in relation to the conversations, I trace how as a hapless disciple I was reprimanded for vigorously shaking my leg, to Śrīla Prabhupāda appointing me as a leader (“*Do I Come With You, Śrīla Prabhupāda?”)* for the proposed bullock cart pilgrimage in 1977. In working through these conversations, it seemed that Śrīla Prabhupāda’s incisive comments were “custom made” for me because each conversation had a distinct mellow which created a distinct relationship with Śrīla Prabhupāda. In the conversation*,* “*You Are Already Sannyāsī*” such a mellow is amply demonstrated. I met with Śrīla Prabhupāda in his quarters to request *sannyāsa* initiation in the full knowledge that my godbrother, Śrīdhar Prabhu, was scheduled for *sannyāsa* initiation.

Śrīla Prabhupāda tried to dissuade me, but I pressed for a formal initiation because my godbrother, Śrīdhar Prabhu, was also getting initiated. I persisted on the issue as a little child would do, wanting the same opportunity of initiation as Śrīdhar Prabhu until Śrīla Prabhupāda asked: “*So what will satisfy you? Do you want to undergo the formality? Is that what you want*?” to which I jumped at the chance saying “*Yes*!” In this conversation, Śrīla Prabhupāda’s mood is affectionate and paternal.

The room conversations are face to face with Śrīla Prabhupāda and at times in the presence of a few disciples. These represent the wonderful opportunities for me to engage with Śrīla Prabhupāda in more intimate and cherished interactions. The conversation “*You Can Go by Bullock Cart”*, occurs in Śrīla Prabhupāda’s room. It captures Śrīla Prabhupāda’s elaborate discussion of the programme, his firm belief that the villagers should be introduced to *hari-nāma-saṅkīrtana* process, describes the plight of the traditional village system and takes stock of contemporary India.

The most heart-wrenching conversation I had with Śrīla Prabhupāda, “*You Manage* – *Let Me Travel to All the Tīrtha-sthānas”* occurs in his quarters. This conversation, still deeply entrenched in my heart, describes an extremely frail Śrīla Prabhupāda who expresses a strong desire to undertake a pilgrimage of all the *tīrtha-sthānas*. Śrīla Prabhupāda entrusts me to lead the pilgrimage given my bullock cart experience, and I undertake all the planning and organizing to ensure that Śrīla Prabhupāda will be comfortable. The dialogues which capture the intensification of anxiety and love for Śrīla Prabhupāda, eventually culminates in the decision that Śrīla Prabhupāda was not well enough to undertake the pilgrimage.

In this book my personal recollections and reflections allow me to reconnect immediately with my spiritual master, and feel his divine presence, an experience which allowed me an opportunity to relive those moments with Śrīla Prabhupāda.

This book is an invitation to the reader to explore the many contexts, conversations, and contemplations that continue to be dialogued even when you have long completed a reading of the book. I hope that by recording my personal reflections others will be inspired and that I will be able to communicate that divinity of my spiritual master to the reader.

*Lokanāth Swami*  
*Gītā Jayantī 2014*  
*Noida, India*

## Chapter 1. The First Direct Instruction

*Vṛndāvana, 1972*

In 1972, the auspicious month of Kārttika began on 15 October. Śrīla Prabhupāda had expressed his desire to be in Vṛndāvana at this time of the year as the first ever Kārttika festival was to be held there. Śrīla Prabhupāda had been out of India since April, and had finally made his way back to Vṛndāvana from Manila.

I was embarking on my maiden trip to Vṛndāvana. A dozen or so devotees from ISKCON (The International Society for Krishna Consciousness) Bombay and I, made our way by train to Mathurā. From Mathurā we took a horse cart and arrived at the Rādhā- Dāmodara temple in Vṛndāvana in the early morning hours. We were going to be there for the entire month. The Rādhā-Dāmodara temple is one of the most popular temples in Vṛndāvana with many sets of deities worshipped. The original Rādhā-Dāmodara deities were worshipped by Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī and the same *govardhana-śilā* also on the altar was given to Sanātana Gosvāmī by Kṛṣṇa Himself. The Deity now being worshipped is the *pratibhu-murti* (replica) and is considered non-different from the original Deity.

It was here that Śrīla Prabhupāda would be staying during Kārttika. When we arrived at the temple, a few devotees greeted us at the door. We were then ushered into the temple courtyard, where I beheld the breathtakingly beautiful deities of Śrī Śrī Rādhā- Dāmodara. After offering obeisances to the Deities, we were taken to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters. Śrīla Prabhupāda was sitting on a modest *āsana*, resting against a cushion that was behind him.

We offered our respectful obeisances and seated ourselves on the cool floor. As was Śrīla Prabhupāda’s nature, he immediately made us feel comfortable by asking about our journey. After a few initial enquiries about our journey and welfare, Śrīla Prabhupāda asked about the progress of the Bombay temple. I thought that in Vṛndāvana, Śrīla Prabhupāda would talk about Rādhā Kṛṣṇa, but he was more interested in talking about the Bombay project. I realised that in Śrīla Prabhupāda’s heart, despite the city of Bombay being a hive of material activity, the Śrī Śrī Rādhā- Rāsabihārī temple was an extension of Vṛndāvana.

I was fortunate enough to sit at the front of our group, near Śrīla Prabhupāda. My opportunity for close association was finally realised.

*As Śrīla Prabhupāda spoke, he turned his head from side to side, much like a camera, and made eye contact with each of us. Every now and then, he noticed something, paused and took a good look at me. I wondered why his glances fell so often upon me. Śrīla Prabhupāda could clearly see me from where he was seated on the āsana. I could not fathom why I was getting this special merciful glance. Was my tilaka straight? Or perhaps there was some problem with my dhotī or kurtā? What could it be?*

*I was suddenly jolted to reality when Śrīla Prabhupāda pointed his finger at my leg and in a strong tone firmly instructed, “Stop it!” I had been sitting cross-legged and shaking one of my thighs vigorously.*

This was the first direct instruction I received from Śrīla Prabhupāda. From my younger days, I had the habit of shaking one of my thighs whenever I was listening intently, but after Śrīla Prabhupāda’s instruction I stopped it – except when I narrate this pastime!

Reflecting on Śrīla Prabhupāda’s strictness, I remember a verse from the Nīti Śāstra by Cāṇakya Paṇḍita:

*lālane bahavo doṣās*  
*tāḍane bahavo guṇāḥ*  
*tasmāt putraṁ ca śiṣyaṁ ca*  
*tāḍayen na tu lālayet*

**“Leniency encourages bad qualities in a student or son, and strictness helps develop good qualities. Therefore, a teacher or parent should not pamper a child but administer punishment for bad behaviour.”**

I did not take this instruction only as being limited to the movement of my thigh, but rather as an instruction that encompassed all mundane and unauthorised things. Spiritual life is full of dos and don’ts, and my spiritual life had started with a “don’t”. Later that month, when I received initiation, we were asked to recite the list of the four regulative principles which were the “don’ts”. The most important lesson however, was the “do”, which was to chant a minimum of sixteen rounds of the holy name daily. Through Śrīla Prabhupāda’s keen observation and objective criticism, I realised that he was training me to remove habits which would hinder my spiritual progress.

## Chapter 2. Your Name Is Lokanāth

One of the purposes for my visiting Vṛndāvana during Kārttika in 1972 was to receive initiation. An initiation ceremony was conducted by Śrīla Prabhupāda where Gurukṛpā, Parivrājakācārya and Paramahaṁsa were among those who received sannyāsa. On that day, however, Śrīla Prabhupāda had sent me to Agra with Draviḍa Dāsa to collect large amounts of grains, jaggery and ghee for the upcoming Govardhana Pujā festival.

It was on that morning before leaving to Agra that I had submitted the letter of recommendation for initiation from my Temple President to Śrīla Prabhupāda.

My first or *hari-nāma* initiation took place later in the month on Sunday, 29 October, on the auspicious appearance of Rādhā-kuṇḍa, which is also known as Bahulāṣṭami. Śrīla Prabhupāda had accepted my recommendation letter and I was to be initiated, along with Draviḍa Dāsa (who was to receive *sannyāsa* initiation), Manmohana Dāsa from the Bombay congregation, Śacīmātā Dāsī from Calcutta (a relative of Śrīla Prabhupāda), and a few others whose names I have since forgotten.

Early that morning at the Rādhā-Dāmodara temple, an arena for a fire *yajña* was set up in the courtyard between Rupa Gosvāmī’s Samādhi and Bhajana-kuṭīra. At the initiation ceremony, Śrīla Prabhupāda sat on a modest *āsana* that was placed upon a red striped carpet. The *āsana* was raised a foot higher than our seating. Śrīla Prabhupāda sat in front of us and his demeanour was serious as he attentively chanted on our beads. The mood was sombre, but energetic. We initially sat on a red carpet listening to Śrīla Prabhupāda chanting. We then sat around the fire underneath the Tamāla tree which was swaying slightly in the breeze. The pilgrims on the parikramā path nearby caught sight of the Westerners and a fire sacrifice and some of them stopped to watch the colourful event. Śrīla Prabhupāda presided over the ceremony, as we offered grains into the fire.

Draviḍa Dāsa Brahmacārī, who was taking *sannyāsa*, was asked to come forward first. Śrīla Prabhupāda chanted the *sannyāsa-mantra* and asked him to repeat it, then named him Pañcadraviḍa Swami, and handed him a *daṇḍa*. During the rest of the initiation ceremony the initiates came forward one by one, and were personally handed their beads by Śrīla Prabhupāda, on which he had chanted.

*As I approached Śrīla Prabhupāda and knelt before him, he asked me if I knew the regulative principles. I nodded in the affirmative and recited them for him, “No eating of meat, fish or eggs. No intoxication. No gambling. No illicit sex life.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then handed me my beads and said, “Chant sixteen rounds daily. Your name is Lokanāth.” All the devotees joyously shouted, “Jaya!”*

My initiation took place under exceptional circumstances and I consider myself to be very fortunate for the following reasons: I received initiation directly from Srila Prabhupada in the courtyard of the Radha-Damodar temple; I was initiated in the vicinity of Rupa Goswami’s *bhajan kutir* and Samadhi mandir in Vṛndāvana. It was the month of Kartikka on Bahulastami, the night on which Radha-Kunda appeared. I could not have asked for a more auspicious occasion.

When giving a spiritual name, Śrīla Prabhupāda would usually use the first letter of a person’s birth name as the first letter of one’s initiated name, but for me he kept the last part of my name intact (*nāth*) and changed the first part. Before then my name was Raghunātha, but after initiation, by Śrīla Prabhupāda’s pronouncement, I was to be called Lokanāth. I had thought that my name would perhaps be Rādhā-Ramaṇa or a name beginning with the letter “R”, but it was to be Lokanāth.

*Loka* refers “to the planetary systems” and *nātha* means “master”. Although *Lokanātha* is also the name of the Lord, when I heard the name from Śrīla Prabhupāda, I immediately associated it with Lokanātha Gosvāmī. Whilst I would obviously never come close to Lokanātha Gosvāmī’s level of devotion, he was also born in a small village and had embarked on his spiritual journey to Vṛndāvana as a young man. Due to the passage of time and the invasion of foreign powers, many shrines in the Vṛndāvana area had become concealed. In the early 16th Century, Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu had sent Lokanātha Gosvāmī to Vṛndāvana to rediscover the lost sites of Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes.

Ever since my initiation, Lokanātha Gosvāmī has been a source of inspiration to me. This inspiration is to follow the *gosvāmī* mood of devotion and detachment.

## Chapter 3. Maha-mantra for all occasions

Śrīla Prabhupāda spent a full week in Māyāpur and then left for Vṛndāvana. While there, Girirāja Dāsa arrived with the bad news that the municipality of Bombay had denied them permission to begin construction of a temple on Hare Krishna Land. Scheduled to go to Bombay, Śrīla Prabhupāda was in an urgent mood when he left Vṛndāvana. From 20 March, Śrīla Prabhupāda continued his daily activities in Bombay which also entailed taking every opportunity for preaching.

On the evening of 31 March, there had been a temple programme at the Rādhā-Rāsabihārī temple. The programme had been well attended by the devotees and many guests. Śrīla Prabhupāda had just concluded the programme and I was given the opportunity to thank everyone and announced that the next day was Rāma-navamī. I informed them that there would be a special programme at the temple the next day.

*I encouraged everyone to attend, enticing them with the prospect of huge celebrations for the Appearance Day of Lord Rāmacandra. Laying the foundation of the cornerstone of the new temple was going to coincide with the celebration of Rāma-navamī. I thought that since it was Rāma-navamī, we could do something different like singing raghupati rāghava rājā-rām, patita-pāvana sītā-rām and chanting of Hare Kṛṣṇa. In my excitement I announced, “We will have Rām Dhun tomorrow.”*

*Almost immediately I heard Śrīla Prabhupāda’s voice objecting, “What is this Rām Dhun? We will have Hare Kṛṣṇa kīrtana.”*

Śrīla Prabhupāda was not interested in *Rām Dhun* because it had been popularized by his contemporary, Mahatma Gandhi, in the 1930’s, who eventually became known by the media as ‘the saint among politicians’.

Mahatma Gandhi had deviated from the original *Dhun*. His new version was “*raghupati rāghava rājā-rām, patita-pāvana sītā rām. īśvara allāh tero nām, saba ko sanmati de bhagavān*.” Śrīla Prabhupāda had said that Gandhi’s aim with the *Dhun* was to appease and unite the Hindus and Muslims and to gain the support of the Indian population. This deviated *Dhun* was not in the mood of pure devotional service.

I remember seeing a letter wherein Śrīla Prabhupāda had indicated that he had no problem with the first two lines of the *Rām Dhun* “*raghupati rāghava rājā-rām, patita-pāvana sītā-rām*.” However, he always felt that it was better to adhere to the singing of the mahā-mantra. My spiritual master had shown me mercy by correcting me. We need to maintain the authenticity and purity of our *sampradāya*, as followers of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu. He always said that as Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas, the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra was a mantra for all occasions.

Śrīla Prabhupāda was citing from the *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa* where it is stated that “the pious results achieved by chanting 1000 holy names of Viṣṇu is equal to chanting one name of Rāma and chanting the holy name of Rāma thrice, can be attained by only one utterance of the holy name of Kṛṣṇa.” Thus, any festival could be successfully celebrated with the chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra.

## Chapter 4. Śrīla Prabhupāda Takes Over Kīrtana

*Bombay, 1975*

It was approximately noon on the Disappearance Day festival of Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, the spiritual master of Śrīla Prabhupāda. The Juhu temple overflowed with the presence of many devotees and senior devotees. A joyous, festive mood filled the air. Śrīla Prabhupāda was seated on the *vyāsāsana* and had just finished his lecture.

*After the lecture all the devotees stood up and Śrīla Prabhupāda looked at me and said, “Lokanāth, you recite the Maṇgalācaraṇa.” The devotees’ response was enthusiastic and vibrant. This was followed by puṣpāñjali and guru-pūjā for Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura.*

*I was leading Gurvaṣṭaka prayers to the loud and rhythmic accompaniment of the mṛdaṇga. Śrīla Prabhupāda and a few other devotees were playing karatālas together. The momentum of the kīrtana gradually increased until everyone became completely absorbed in the kīrtana. As I had reached the fifth stanza of the Gurvaṣṭaka prayers, singing “śrī-rādhikā-mādhavayor apāra”, Śrīla Prabhupāda beckoned with his hands, indicating that I should bring the microphone to him. He then took over the singing of the song. He began leading the kīrtana and everyone burst into dance. The dancing became unbelievably joyous. Śrīla Prabhupāda’s eyes were closed; he was deeply absorbed. As he sang, he sometimes opened his eyes, and noticing our enthusiasm he became visibly enlivened. Śrīla Prabhupāda sometimes glanced at different devotees and each devotee who received his glance became immediately overwhelmed in spiritual happiness. When we saw that Śrīla Prabhupāda noticed our enthusiastic participation, it heightened our singing and dancing.*

As Śrīla Prabhupāda took over and sang *Gurvaṣṭaka* prayers there was a noticeable change in the atmosphere. As he chanted these mantras, it seemed that the mercy of the *ācāryas* was flowing through him on this auspicious occasion. It was memorable for me the way in which Śrīla Prabhupāda just took over while I was singing. Normally just one person sings from beginning to end. In this case something exceptional had happened that day – Śrīla Prabhupāda had shared the *kīrtana* with me and it felt special.

## Chapter 5. Chanting Means That Your Tongue Must Vibrate

*Bombay, 1975*

It was 10 November and we were on a morning walk on Juhu beach with Śrīla Prabhupāda. Brahmānanda Dāsa, Yaśomatīnandana Dāsa and Dr. Patel were among some of the devotees present. A lengthy conversation about the chanting of sixteen rounds ensued.

*“Is it possible to chant within one’s mind?” I asked.*

*“Chanting means that your tongue must be engaged, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa” chanted Śrīla Prabhupāda, “that is chanting, real chanting.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda continued, “Chant means you must vibrate your tongue. That is chanting, Hare Kṛṣṇa. Where it is said? It is never said, ‘You chant within the mind?’”*

*Whilst on the topic of chanting, I enquired further, “Why do we chant sixteen rounds? Why that specific number? Why not less or more?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda replied, “If you can chant sixteen thousand you can go. Sixteen rounds is the minimum. But if you are able to chant sixteen thousand rounds, that is welcome. We have got so much engagement. Still, we say, ‘We don’t find engagement.’ This is our misfortune. Hare Kṛṣṇa! Jaya! Haridāsa Ṭhākura was engaged in chanting and the prostitute came. She offered, ‘Let us enjoy.’ ‘Yes, let me finish. Let me finish this chanting,’ he replied to her. So much engagement, and still, we say, ‘No engagement.’ He refused to have sex with a beautiful young girl because he had engagement. First of all let me finish my engagement, and we say we have no engagement.”*

*I then mentioned to Śrīla Prabhupāda that some devotees had set a different number of daily rounds for themselves. Some were doing a minimum of twenty to twenty-five rounds of chanting. “Should we also consider increasing our daily number of rounds?” I asked.*

*“Yes,” said Śrīla Prabhupāda, “It should be increased. But don’t decrease. Don’t decrease… increase. Therefore one number is fixed. At least this much I shall do. That is sixteen rounds.”*

*“But you are recommending sixteen as a minimum, and some devotees are choosing twenty as a minimum,” I persisted. Śrīla Prabhupāda replied, “So who forbids? Who says, ‘Don’t do it?’”*

*“So they can chant?” I continued curiously. I was trying to rationalize and confirm the number of rounds that Śrīla Prabhupāda felt was best to be done daily.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda nodded, “Yes. That is wanted. But because you cannot do it, therefore we have fixed up this minimum. Saṅkhyāta asaṅkhyāta. Saṅkhyāta means ‘with vow, numerical strength’. And asaṅkhyāta means ‘there is no limit’.”*

*I enquired further, “If we chant more than sixteen rounds, how can we know whether we are imitating Haridāsa Ṭhākura or following in his footsteps?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda smiled, “Imitation is also good. If you imitate Haridāsa Ṭhākura, that is also your great fortune. Imitating, it does not mean you are condemned. Even if you imitate, that is also good. If you have some other business and if you say, ‘Now I am imitating Haridāsa Ṭhākura, I cannot do it,’ that is very bad or ‘I am busy in imitating Haridāsa Ṭhākura,’ that is not good. That is very bad!”*

*Brahmānanda quipped, “If the devotees are asked for service they say, ‘Oh, I have to chant’.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda nodded, “If I am imitating. ‘Yes. I am imitating Haridāsa. This is my first business so I cannot do service.’ That is very bad.”*

Śrīla Prabhupāda stressed here that we should imitate Haridāsa Ṭhākura for the right reasons, and that is to aspire to perfect our quality and quantity of rounds chanted, and not as a means to inflate one’s ego or avoid service. Śrīla Prabhupāda’s requirement was also clear. The devotee should chant sixteen rounds a day as a set number. Haridāsa Ṭhākura and the six Gosvāmīs were all engaged in chanting a fixed number of rounds. Fixing the number of rounds was important to maintain consistency. Increasing was fine, but decreasing the number of rounds chanted, was frowned upon.

How loudly you chant is a personal choice. Śrīla Prabhupāda encouraged the devotees to chant the holy name loudly during *kīrtana*, but when you are chanting *japa* together with others in a temple room or elsewhere, you should not interfere with other people’s chanting by being too loud with your own rounds. It is stated in the *Madhya-līlā*, “The chanting is a spiritual call for the Lord and His energy to give protection to the conditioned soul. The chanting is exactly like the genuine cry of a child for its mother’s presence.”

The message was very clear from Śrīla Prabhupāda. “Chanting means that your tongue must be engaged.”

## Chapter 6. Animals Have Complete Intelligence

*Bombay, 1975*

Śrīla Prabhupāda had arrived in Bombay from Nairobi on 2 November. Some devotees and I had the opportunity to accompany Śrīla Prabhupāda on the morning of 15 November for a walk. At that time of the year, the weather was somewhat dry, but pleasant. Śrīla Prabhupāda was always keen to walk.

*As Śrīla Prabhupāda quickened his steps and we hurried along behind him, we glanced upwards to the clear Juhu beach sky and observed a flock of geese flying through the air in complete synergy with each other. Śrīla Prabhupāda stopped, dug his stick steadily into the ground and commented, “Yes... fast, these birds. Airplane cannot do that.” It was as if he knew that we were all in sync with his thinking.*

*I inquired, “How do the birds determine the direction of their movement without clashing into each other?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda looked quizzically, “Huh?” and invited me to elaborate further. I repeated my question paraphrasing the idea about how was it possible for these birds to communicate with each other and not clash in flight.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda responded, informing me, “Yes, they have got intelligence. You are thinking that they have no intelligence. They have got complete intelligence.”*

*I acknowledged Śrīla Prabhupāda’s explanation and pushed the discussion further, “How do they all decide together to turn either right or left without crashing?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda replied with authority, “That is intelligence. You do not know that. If you are given airplane… so many… you’ll… immediately at least half a dozen will be finished. That means you are less intelligent than the birds, at least in the matter of flying.”*

*Brahmānanda, Gopāla Kṛṣna and I were highly amused at Śrīla Prabhupāda’s riposte.*

It is a mystery to science how creatures like animals, honeybees, fish, ants or birds often move in surprising synchronicity, making unanimous decisions with ease. It is indeed a phenomenon that has puzzled researchers for centuries. Scientists keep researching for some logical explanation. The explanation that Śrīla Prabhupāda has given is clear – these animals do possess intelligence. It is the kind of intelligence that science often vehemently denies, explaining it away as a mere animal instinct.

The V-formation of the geese, about which I had earlier enquired, actually assists the birds when they fly. It helps to conserve energy when each bird flies slightly ahead of each other, resulting in less wind resistance. The intelligence of these animals is evidential in this design. According to a national park survey, the birds are even fair to one another, each taking turns to be at the front, and then moving to the back when they get tired. The V-formation also improves communication and co-ordination within the flock. The birds are more orientated and are able to follow their route more directly. There are studies that have shown that the V-formation also ensures that every bird in the flock is accounted for.

Age, sex and body size of the birds also play a role in who leads the V-formation. A group of Swedish researchers assert that in a flock of adults and young birds, juveniles usually do not lead since they are less able to maintain high speeds in the lead position and would slow the entire flock down. The researchers also determined that pelicans that fly in group formation beat their wings less often and have lower heart rates than those that fly alone. In this way, birds that fly in V-formation conserve much needed energy during their long, difficult journeys.

It is stated in *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (*[2.1.36](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-2/1.36)) that “varieties of birds are indications of His masterful artistic sense”. In Śrīla Prabhupāda’s purport to the same verse he explains that “the aesthetic sense of the Lord is manifested in the colourful creation of varieties of birds like the peacock, parrot and cuckoo”. This is further explained in the purport of *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* ([6.4.13](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-6/4.13)) , where Śrīla Prabhupāda describes how “the Supreme Personality of Godhead is situated as the Supersoul within the cores of the hearts of all living entities, whether moving or non moving, including men, birds, animals, trees and, indeed, all living entities”. Therefore the Supersoul directs not only human beings, but all creatures as well. Even when basic intelligence is evident, the source is Kṛṣṇa as the Supersoul.

It is the Supersoul that gives these birds the intelligence to fly in perfect formation, a conviction that Śrīla Prabhupāda strongly held.

## Chapter 7. We Need To Make More Men Like You

*Bombay, 1975*

It was 1975 and the opposition to the presence of the foreign devotees raged on in Bombay. The press and the public were referring to the American devotees as CIA agents, and making comparisons to the British invasion. “Once bitten, twice shy,” they said. The CIA issue was once even on the agenda of the Indian National Parliament. It was not helping our legal battle over the Hare Krishna Land, or the difficulty we were having in obtaining visas for the foreign devotees. Even when we went out to preach, the common people would express these kinds of doubts, sometimes insulting and teasing us. They did not want to accept the fact that Westerners were seriously following Kṛṣṇa consciousness and its regulative principles which many of them had rejected in pursuit of economic development. News of this occurrence reached Śrīla Prabhupāda. He expressed feelings of disappointment and frustration.

*On one occasion Śrīla Prabhupāda called me into his quarters to discuss the difficulties we were experiencing. He told me, “Who knows, they may kick all the foreign devotees out of the country.”*

*I saw his concern and asked, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, is there anything I can do to help?”*

*He began to speak of his long-term strategy for dealing with such problems. Śrīla Prabhupāda envisioned a large number of Indian devotees joining this Kṛṣṇa conscious movement. I added, “I could go out and preach to the Indians.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda proposed, “If we have many Indian devotees, then this movement will not seem so foreign and people will not have a problem with us or feel threatened by us.” His idea was that the spurious arguments used by the press and the politicians would be useless against home-grown Vaiṣṇavas.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then offered a solution, “There is need of creating more Indian devotees. We have to make more men like you.”*

I was humbled by Śrīla Prabhupāda’s words and simultaneously concerned about the predicament. I wished I could do more to help ease his burden and live up to his words. And it was not long before I was afforded such an opportunity.

It was some time during that same year that our new and Indian bodied Governing Body Commission (GBC) representative for Bombay, Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Dāsa, in consultation with Śrīla Prabhupāda, informed me that I had been assigned the post of co-president of Bombay temple. Our Kṛṣṇa conscious movement was being branded an American-European movement because Śrīla Prabhupāda had actually started the movement in those countries. When on his return he had opened temples in India, it was understandable why he had placed foreign devotees in positions of authority – because he had trained them over the years. Girirāja Dāsa, an American born disciple, resided as temple president in Bombay with Yadubara Dāsa holding the post of secretary. Surabhi Dāsa, a Dutch disciple, held the important position as head of construction. All of this was not received well given the emerging issue of the foreign devotees.

I therefore understood that my appointment was also a means of minimising claims that foreign disciples were in charge. It was a means of diverting negative propaganda. Girirāja Dāsa was the real President. I was the instrument needed to deflect the negative attention. I knew the Bombay temple quite well. I had cleaned toilets and washed pots, and served as head pujari for almost a year. I had also served as a life membership preacher and even as the temple commander. So this temple which had reared me was very familiar to me. I vividly recalled preparing for a bigger role as co-president of the Bombay temple.

The *ācāryas* had predicted that the holy names of the Lord would be chanted in every town and village throughout the world. Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura had envisioned devotees from America, Europe, Africa, China and Russia gathered together with their Indian brothers and sisters chanting the holy names and dancing.

Śrīla Prabhupāda has made this a reality. Through his unflinching devotional service, Śrīla Prabhupāda has made Kṛṣṇa consciousness available to everyone the world over. Śrīla Prabhupāda had a great realisation and prophetic vision. He once stated, “First they will mock you, then they will harass you, but finally they will glorify you.”

Indeed, we see Śrīla Prabhupāda’s vision to be true, and exactly how the reception of Kṛṣṇa consciousness in India unfolded.

## Chapter 8. The Occasional Secretary

*Bombay, 1975*

There were a few occasions when I was summoned to assist Śrīla Prabhupāda with Hindi correspondence. This mostly occurred in Bombay and on a few rare occasions in Vṛndāvana. I would read the letter in Hindi to Śrīla Prabhupāda and if needed, translate some parts into English. He would reply partly in English, and partly in Hindi, after which I would write the reply in correct Hindi. On one particular occasion, Śrīla Prabhupāda received a Hindi letter from Madhya Pradesh, so he summoned me for assistance.

“Here is a letter. Read it please. Translate for me,” said Śrīla Prabhupāda.

I examined the contents. It was an invitation from a person in Madhya Pradesh. “They want you to visit their hometown to do a programme, Śrīla Prabhupāda,” I said.

“Madhya Pradesh,” said Śrīla Prabhupāda thoughtfully, “that’s a distance away… how far away?”

“This town is about 15 hours away from Bombay by train,” I replied.

Śrīla Prabhupāda thought about it for a while and being ever prepared in his quest to preach far and wide, he replied, “Yes, we will come, tell them. But we have a request… they must cover the transport and accommodation costs of our party.”

“Yes I will do that,” I replied, quite surprised.

I personally felt that it was a great distance to travel for an invitation from one person. Therefore I was quite taken aback by Śrīla Prabhupāda’s response. He was ready, even eager, to oblige them with just one single condition which was basic and reasonable.

As far as I know, that person never responded to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s offer. This incident however, portrayed Śrīla Prabhupāda’s missionary spirit. He could have said that he had no time for preaching when he was so busy travelling as the jet age *parivrājakācārya* and managing the movement during the fledgling stage of its history. But he was always eager to preach.

Devotees with managerial responsibilities, often find themselves unable to fully participate in the preaching activities of our Society due to the pressure of other commitments. But Śrīla Prabhupāda had set the standard which we all have to follow based on his famous axiom “preaching is the essence.” He was very aware of the difficulties faced by devotees trying to keep abreast of all their commitments, but was not always gentle in his approach to their deficiencies. Once a leading group of *sannyāsīs* in management headed by Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami were giving Śrīla Prabhupāda different managerial reasons why Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami, should not go to China to preach, even though Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted him to go. On one occasion Śrīla Prabhupāda sarcastically proclaimed, “resolution, dissolution, no solution!” He was referring here to the fledgling GBC. He wanted to reinforce the fact that preaching gets priority over management.

Śrīla Prabhupāda wrote over 8000 letters, an amazing feat for someone his age, and with so many responsibilities. It is a preaching spirit and standard that he has set for all of us, to continually strive for.

## Chapter 9. O Lord! Give Us Our Daily Bread!

*Bombay, 1975*

It was also during 1975 that the possibility of a preaching trip to Poona arose. Some disciples were restless to go out and preach. Bhāgavata Dāsa, whom we affectionately called Big Bhāgavata at the time, was equally restless. He was tired of being based solely in Bombay and wanted to travel to Poona. Poona was just over a hundred and fifty kilometres away from Bombay. It was an ideal place to preach as it was fast becoming a bustling city. Businesses were expanding and educational institutes were emerging across the city. A place where people were so engrossed in economic development would surely benefit from a good dose of spirituality. After much discussion, a group of us approached Śrīla Prabhupāda. I was the only native Indian amongst a group of foreign disciples – Germans, Americans and others.

*Big Bhāgavata had once said to me, “I want to preach. I am tired of being around Bombay. I want to take a bunch of devotees out on the train to another city to go preaching. What do you think?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda immediately sanctioned this preaching mission and instructed Bhāgavata Dāsa to gather a team of devotees. I was one of the brahmacārīs selected. Preparations were underway for days. Before departing on our three hour train ride, we arranged a meeting with Śrīla Prabhupāda to seek his blessings.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was staying in his quarters on the top floor and he usually gave darśana on the rooftop of the same building. We met Śrīla Prabhupāda there. Śrīla Prabhupāda sat on a cushion on the rooftop and we sat in a circle around him. He advised us on the attitude and mood of a devotee, preparing us for the preaching trip ahead.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was trying to cultivate a mood of pure devotion to Kṛṣṇa – a mood of dependence and surrender. Not that we should treat God as our carrier as the Christians did, but surrender, and depend on Him in a way that a beloved relative would. He had spoken of the Christians and their inbred nature to perpetually beg. Their prayer resounded of pleading and asking from the Lord – a habit of always wanting and wanting more. “O Lord, give us our daily bread,” they would request.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “Man-made religion has no value. There are so many such religious systems, the Hindu religion, Christian religion, Mohammedan religion or this religion or that religion. That is a kind of faith. But religion means ‘the order or the laws given by God’. Therefore here it is said, dharmaḥ projjhita-kaitavaḥ atra. Kaitavaḥ means ‘cheating types of religious systems’. So Kṛṣṇa says in the Bhagavad-gītā* ([18.66](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/18.66)) , *sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja.* *He says that ‘you have manufactured so many religious systems. So you give up all these. You simply surrender unto Me’.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda pointed out that always asking from the Lord was in glaring contrast to the heart and attitude of a Vaiṣṇava. A Vaiṣṇava would never beg or ask from the Lord. A true Vaiṣṇava is so selfless that he would almost feel ashamed to ask for anything from someone he loved so dearly. In fact, a true Vaiṣṇava is always eager and ready to feed that bread to Lord Kṛṣṇa.*

*I recall Śrīla Prabhupāda speaking of Mother Yaśodā and her dealings with Kṛṣṇa. She would chastise Him if He did not come on time for lunch. She would approach Him with a stick as He played with the cowherd boys, demanding that He come home for lunch.*

*He would obligingly stop playing, always ready to pacify His beloved Yaśodā, and rush off to Nanda-bhavan to carry out His mother’s instructions and eat the lunch she had so lovingly prepared. This was vastly in contrast with the mode of asking and begging.*

Śrīla Prabhupāda was always very favourable to travelling and preaching. Therefore he had sanctioned this trip without hesitation. Personally, I was very pleased to be going to Poona as I would see firsthand what Śrīla Prabhupāda had meant when he had disembarked from the Jaladuta on 19 August 1965 in Colombo, Śrī Laṅkā. In his diary during that historic journey, Śrīla Prabhupāda had written the following, “The city appeared to be nice and clean and the small bungalows nicely decorated. The city is quite up to resembling an Indian city like Madras and Poona.” I remembered a discussion Śrīla Prabhupāda had with a priest in Paris in 1974 where he had again mentioned that, “Poona is a nice place. Climate is nice and the Maharastrians in Poona are educated and advanced.”

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s eagerness to preach Kṛṣṇa consciousness in new places was obvious, and his intentions in relating the pastimes of Mother Yaśodā, just prior to our undertaking to Poona was meant to inspire us. As Śrīla Prabhupāda spoke, I thought that this was a mood that all Vaiṣṇavas should aspire to develop – a mood of loving service.

## Chapter 10. You Are Already Sannyāsī

*Bombay, 1975*

It was November 1974. A year and a half had passed since my brahmanical initiation. Śrīla Prabhupāda was back in Bombay. He had come from Calcutta to deliver a lecture on the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, Lord Kapila* Series. On one such late afternoon, at the end of *darśana,* I found Śrīla Prabhupāda by himself on the rooftop of the building where he stayed. There was a large *vyāsāsana* covered with white sheets. Śrīla Prabhupāda was freshly shaved. Against a backdrop of swaying coconut trees, he was looking thoughtfully into the clear sky.

After most of the guests had left, I approached Śrīla Prabhupāda and requested him to consider me for *sannyāsa.* At that time I was only initiated for two years. There was a short discussion and the concise response was that it would be better if I waited for some time.

A year later, it was now mid November 1975. Even though I did not have a formal appointment, I seized the opportunity to share my aspiration with my Śrīla Prabhupāda.

*I humbly went to him in his quarters, offered obeisances and sat across the low desk from him. I awaited acknowledgement and a gesture that he was ready to talk. I introduced what was on my mind, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, when I had approached you before requesting you to grant me sannyāsa, you asked me to wait. Approximately one year has passed since then. May I request for the same again?”*

*I was aware that Śrīdhar Dāsa, my colleague and early companion in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, was scheduled to take sannyāsa in Vṛndāvana. I was steadfast about being there too. In a family when the parents give something to one child, the other child will also approach the parents asking, “Me too, me too!” It was similar to that. Śrīdhar Dāsa was about to receive sannyāsa initiation and I wanted it as well. I reminded Śrīla Prabhupāda how I had been waiting with great longing.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was smiling when he responded, “You are already sannyāsī.”*

*I wondered what he meant by that. How was that possible? I was just a brahmacārī and had not undergone any ceremony or formality. To convince me further, he quoted the following verse:*

*śrī-bhagavān uvāca*  
*anāśritaḥ karma-phalaṁ*  
*kāryaṁ karma karoti yaḥ*  
*sa sannyāsī ca yogī ca*  
*na niragnir na cākriyaḥ*  
*\[Bg.* [*6.1*](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/6.1)*. \]*

**“The Supreme Personality of Godhead said, ‘One who is unattached to the fruits of his work and who works as he is obligated is in the renounced order of life, and he is the true mystic, not he who lights no fire and performs no duty’.”**

*“You are engaged in preaching work,” Śrīla Prabhupāda told me, “so you are already sannyāsī.” He explained, “Sannyāsa doesn’t mean the false renunciation that impersonalists speak of. It is the practical, beneficial work for the betterment of society. Real sannyāsa or detachment means you work for Kṛṣṇa. That is real sannyāsa.” As far as Śrīla Prabhupāda was concerned, I was already doing that.*

*But I was not convinced. It was not making any sense to me because I was attached. Furthermore, I was adamant that I wanted sannyāsa. Śrīla Prabhupāda obviously knew what was in my heart, yet he asked me, “So what will satisfy you? Do you want to undergo the formality, is that what you want?”*

*“Yes, yes!” I said enthusiastically, jumping at his offer. “That’s what I want, Śrīla Prabhupāda.” My other godbrothers had already undergone the formality, now Śrīdhar was also going to, and I wanted to be like them.*

*“All right” said Śrīla Prabhupāda. “Śrīdhar is going to be initiated in Vṛndāvana, so you can join him.”*

*Overwhelmed with gratitude, I offered my humble obeisances and left to make preparations for my travels to Vṛndāvana. So my sannyāsa was approved by Śrīla Prabhupāda, just like that. There was no sannyāsa board, no waiting list – just Śrīla Prabhupāda.*

*Sannyāsa* is derived from the Sanskrit word, *saṁ-nyāsa*. *Sam* means “to place emphasis on the activity which follows” and *nyāsa* means “renounced”. The word therefore makes it “a complete renunciation”. One who has renounced or given up everything and simply devotes his time for the service of the Supreme is considered a *sannyāsī.*

Upon accepting the *sannyāsa* order, the *sannyāsī’s* main business is to dedicate his life entirely to the service of Kṛṣṇa. You have to completely offer your body, mind and words to the service of the Lord. If this does not happen, you do not actually become a *sannyāsī.* It is not simply a matter of changing your dress. The dress is not *sannyāsa,* but the attitude of service to Kṛṣṇa is.

I recall that Śrīla Prabhupāda gave a short talk based on the *sannyāsa* mantra beginning *etāṁ sa āsthāya parātma-niṣṭhām…* from the *Śrīmad- Bhāgavatam, (*[11.23.57](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-11/23.57)) . He told us that although we were young men in the midst of the very powerful material energy, if we keep full faith in Kṛṣṇa and follow the previous *ācāryas*, we would remain fixed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Śrīla Prabhupāda advised us to do this by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa *mantra*. He even suggested that as *sannyāsīs* we should chant extra rounds.

It seemed that just after I had taken *sannyāsa*; it was like a season of *sannyāsa.* Between December 1975 and the Māyāpur festival of 1976, so many devotees were approaching Śrīla Prabhupāda for *sannyāsa*. However, very early in 1976, the GBC passed a resolution that in future no one should directly approach His Divine Grace for *sannyāsa.* I was lucky I did not have to go through any committee or waiting period. It came to me quite easily.

## Chapter 11. Learn Kṛṣṇa Consciousness and Preach It All Over The World

*Vṛndāvana, 1975*

A few days prior to our *sannyāsa* initiation, Śrīdhar and I had taken a train from Bombay to Mathurā, and a horse-cart from Mathurā to the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma Mandir in Vṛndāvana. We had arrived early to prepare our saffron cloth for our *sannyāsa* initiation. We had also needed to find bamboo and covers for our *tridaṇḍa.*

**Sannyāsa Initiation**

On the morning of 3 December, in spite of the chilly weather, Śrīla Prabhupāda went on his morning walk as usual. He spoke to the devotees about the processes of surrendering to Kṛṣṇa. He returned to the temple within half an hour. A *yajña* pit had been prepared in the courtyard of the temple and the initiations were scheduled to begin after the morning programme. There were three of us taking *sannyāsa* initiation that day; Śrīdhar, Pṛthu-putra and me. Several others were to receive *hari-nāma* initiation. The necessary preparations had been made. All the candidates were seated and the courtyard was filled with devotees having *kīrtana* as Śrīla Prabhupāda arrived and took his seat on the *vyasāsana.*

We offered our new cloth to Śrīla Prabhupāda and the assembled *sannyāsīs*. We asked them to give their blessings by touching it and then went off to put on our new cloth.

*We returned with our sannyāsa clothes. Śrīla Prabhupāda smilingly asked us to come forward one by one and handed us our tridaṇḍas. The sounds of karatāls and mṛdaṅgas, accompanied by cheers of “Jaya, Haribol,” filled the temple courtyard.*

*Akṣayānanda Swami led us in a repetition of the sannyāsa mantra, etāṁ sa āsthāya parātma-niṣṭhām… which means, “I shall cross over the insurmountable ocean of darkness of ignorance simply by taking shelter of the lotus feet of Lord Mukunda, who gives all kinds of liberations and was worshipped by many great previous ācāryas.”*

*Śrīdhar, Pṛthu-putra and I offered our obeisances to Śrīla Prabhupāda, holding our daṇḍas erect in our hands for the first time. When the three of us took our seats Śrīla Prabhupāda began his initiation address, etāṁ sa āsthāya parātma-niṣṭhām. Parātma-niṣṭhām. This sannyāsī life means “to simply devote oneself to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, to keep firm faith in Him.”*

“*There is no other business,” he said. “viṣṇoḥ pādopasarpaṇam refers to the whole system of how to approach the lotus feet of Viṣṇu. So this is the last ceremonial performance and it is not a new thing. Before us there were so many exalted ācāryas, Rāmānujācārya, Madhvācārya, Viṣṇu Svāmī, Nimbārka, and, in our line, Caitanya Mahāprabhu. Our process is to follow the predecessor ācāryas: mahājano yena gataḥ sa panthāḥ. That is the way.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda advised us further, “Although you are young men, there are many difficulties in keeping sannyāsa, but if you keep faith, full faith in Kṛṣṇa, then maya will not be able to touch you. So you should always keep yourself fixed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness by strictly following the rules and regulations. And as sannyāsīs, you should increase your chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra and go on preaching. Then you will be fixed up.”*

*He continued to explain, “Caitanya Mahāprabhu also took sannyāsa at a very early age. He was twenty-four years old only. So He has practically shown by His activities how to preach Kṛṣṇa consciousness all over the world. And He gives this order to everyone, āmāra ājñāya guru hañā tāra’ ei deśa. In whichever country you may live, it doesn’t matter, try to deliver them by becoming their guru. The sannyāsī is supposed to be guru of all other divisions. Sannyāsa is the topmost stage. So if you become guru, teacher, remembering the order of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, then you will never fall down.”*

*“You haven’t got to manufacture anything,” Śrīla Prabhupāda continued. “Simply you try to repeat the instruction of the Bhagavad-gītā, Kṛṣṇa-upadeśa, man-manā bhava mad-bhakto mad-yājī māṁ namaskuru. Everyone can say this. You can say, ‘My dear sir, my only request is that you think of Kṛṣṇa,’ and nobody will kill you for saying this. You do it personally and teach them. Then you become guru.”*

*“When Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī inquired from Caitanya Mahāprabhu, ‘You are a sannyāsī and you are not studying Vedānta, but simply chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa. What kind of sannyāsī are you?’ Caitanya Mahāprabhu replied, ‘My dear sir, I am a great fool. My Guru saw Me as fool number one, therefore he has chastised Me’. We know Caitanya Mahāprabhu was not a fool or rascal. But He represented Himself as a fool, because we are fools – bhārata-bhūmite haila manuṣya-janma yāra, janma sārthaka kari’ kara para-upakāra.”*

*“It is Caitanya Mahāprabhu’s mission that every Indian should learn what is this Kṛṣṇa consciousness and preach it all over the world. That is His order. But our Indians are not taking care of it. Therefore it doesn’t matter—Indian, European or Canadian. Anyone who carries the order of Caitanya Mahāprabhu will be benefited, and be glorified. So don’t hesitate, because the soul is neither Indian nor American. By executing the order of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu you will be glorified, the country will be glorified, and the whole world will be benefited. Thank you very much.”*

Śrīla Prabhupāda*’*s instruction to us was to execute and maintain the highest standard of devotional service as a *sannyāsī.* His order was that we simply devote ourselves to Kṛṣṇa and keep firm faith. He warned that we must strictly follow the rules and regulations and chant to keep *māyā* at bay. I have always maintained my sixteen rounds, of course, and at times I have increased beyond sixteen. But while compiling the book *My Prabhupāda*, I once again stumbled upon this instruction, more than 30 years later. I realized how continuously relevant these words were and I have increased my daily chanting. I understand fully that with those instructions, Śrīla Prabhupāda is guaranteeing me protection from the forces of the age of Kali. S*annyāsī* is, after all, the guru of all other divisions.

Of course, with this tall order of *sannyāsī* comes responsibility. At the time of our initiation, Śrīla Prabhupāda forced us to reflect on this position, making us think about it, reminding us of the additional role and responsibility, and preparing us for the future.

Another point of significance on that day of my initiation was the fact that my two associates who received initiation with me, were not of Indian origin. Śrīdhar was Canadian and Pṛthu-putra was German. Their upbringing was so far removed from India that it was an amazing achievement to have them there—a testament to how Śrīla Prabhupāda had revitalised Bhārata (India) through Mahāprabhu’s words. What is commonly known these days as Bhārata is a shrunken version of the original Bhārata. The original Bhārata encompassed the whole planet. Śrīla Prabhupāda had expanded it back to its actual size by taking Kṛṣṇa consciousness to the Western world. So Śrīla Prabhupāda did not just appeal to Indians—he repeatedly asked his European and American boys and girls to make their lives perfect by practicing and propagating Kṛṣṇa consciousness all over the world.

**The Duty Of A Sannyāsī Is To Travel The World And Preach**

A fire sacrifice followed the address by Śrīla Prabhupāda. I felt a great sense of freedom on the occasion of having taken *sannyāsa*. At the same time, the responsibility that came with this *āśrama* weighed heavily upon me. The s*annyāsa* order is the final order of life and I had accepted it while still in my mid-twenties. Later that day, the three of us went to see Śrīla Prabhupāda in his quarters to seek his blessings and get further direction from him. Śrīla Prabhupāda was seated alone at his desk when we walked in. He had been expecting us. After touching our *daṇḍas* on the floor in front of him, we offered full *daṇḍavats* and seated ourselves in front of his desk across from him.

*Śrīla Prabhupāda offered his mahā-garlands to each of us. I looked at him enquiringly. There was so much on our minds. We were anticipating a name change and with this thought we all asked, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, do we get new names?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda told us, “Just add Swami to your brahmacārī names.” Thus we became Lokanāth Swami, Śrīdhar Swami and Pṛthu-putra Swami.*

*We awaited further instruction from Śrīla Prabhupāda regarding our preaching activities, what he required from us and where he would station each of us. ISKCON was expanding in all directions then and it was not uncommon for Śrīla Prabhupāda to send his sannyāsī disciples to far-off corners of the world. Śrīdhar Swami remembers Śrīla Prabhupāda asking us the question, “North, south, east, west, which direction you will go? You must go out, you must preach.”*

*And then Śrīla Prabhupāda repeated what he had quoted during our initiation address – man-manā bhava mad-bhakto mad-yājī māṁ namaskuru. There are just four things you have to do: become Kṛṣṇa’s devotees, remember Him, offer Him obeisances and worship Him.*

*The important thing, reiterated Śrīla Prabhupāda, is authenticity. “If you do these four things without duplicity, then your success is guaranteed.”*

I recall Śrīla Prabhupāda quoting Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, *yāre dekha, tāre kaha ‘kṛṣṇa’-upadeśa*. We did not have to manufacture anything. Just like Śrīla Prabhupāda was quoting from Kṛṣṇa’s preaching, we were instructed to simply repeat the instruction of the scriptures, especially the *Bhagavad-gītā*, *kṛṣṇa-upadeśa* and our *guru*, Śrīla Prabhupāda. If we especially followed the instruction given by Kṛṣṇa *man-manā bhava mad-bhakto mad-yājī māṁ namaskuru*, then we were really *sannyāsīs.* By strictly following this instruction we would always think of Kṛṣṇa and advise others to do the same.

Śrīdhar and I returned to Bombay and continued preaching there.

## Chapter 12. So Test Yourself, How Much You Have Advanced

*Vṛndāvana, 1975*

The next morning after our initiation, the devotees assembled to hear the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* class by Śrīla Prabhupāda. As we took our seats next to the other *sannyāsīs,* adorned in our new robes embarking on a new journey, three more sticks went up in the small forest of *sannyāsī daṇḍas*.

We were sitting in the front row facing Śrīla Prabhupāda. I felt as if Śrīla Prabhupāda was speaking directly to me and the two other new *sannyāsīs*. He was probably sending a message directed at us to remind us to always stay within our *sannyāsa* order.

*In his opening statement Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “If you become pure devotee of Kṛṣṇa, then as soon as you think of sex, you’ll spit. ‘Eh! Nonsense! What is this?’ This is the result. That is the certificate, whether you have got that attitude, spit on it. Then you understand that you are increasing. Otherwise you are in the same hellish condition. You may show that you are becoming a devotee, very great devotee. So test yourself, how much you have advanced.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda continued further, “So that is the sastric injunction everywhere.” Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (*[7.9.45](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-7/9.45)) *:*

*kaṇḍūyanena karayor iva duḥkha-duḥkham*  
*kaṇḍūtivan manasijaṁ viṣaheta dhīraḥ*  
*tṛpyanti neha kṛpaṇā bahu-duḥkha-bhājaḥ*

*Everyone is practically aware that how sex life is followed by so many miserable conditions of life. Everyone knows it. Either illicit or legal. The world is going on.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda went on to speak about how commonplace abortion had become and the hellish births that materialistic people faced as a result of such sinful activities. He mentioned how Kṛṣṇa promises his devotees that their next birth will be a human birth, and he gave a strong warning for those who use this as an excuse to commit sinful activities. He spoke about how some inhabitants of Vṛndāvana consider that their status as inhabitants of the holy dhāma will allow them to commit all kinds of sinful activities, and how such persons have to take their next birth as dogs and hogs in Vṛndāvana.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda ended the lecture by speaking about the futility of pursuing economic development.*

Although Śrīla Prabhupāda was addressing the assembled devotees in general, I felt as though he was continuing the lecture he had given specifically to us the day before at our *sannyāsa* initiation ceremony. This lecture seemed like a continuation of his instruction to us, a preparation for our new lives ahead and reminders custom-made for us, the new *sannyāsīs,* through his emphasis on celibacy and *vairāgya* or renunciation.

It is important for us as sannyāsīs to maintain the highest mental standards as well. Not only must we not commit certain acts, but thinking along those lines is also very harmful. Therefore Śrīla Prabhupāda imparted guidelines to be followed in controlling the mind – more practical guidelines.

His emphasis on abstinence from sex life and its associated miseries is so blatantly evident from the large numbers of unwanted street children born to the lost, misguided adults who are deluded by lust.

I contemplated that sinful activities committed by anybody including the inhabitants of the *dhāma* are deeply condemned and highly punishable. Economic development can last you this lifetime, but spiritual development goes beyond this single lifespan.

## Chapter 13. I Am Happy That You Always Carry Your Daṇḍa

*Bombay, 1975*

After Śrīdhar Swami and I had been awarded *tridaṇḍa-sannyāsa* in Vṛndāvana, we made our way back to Bombay. Śrīla Prabhupāda was also back in Bombay.

**Carrying The Tridaṇḍa**

Subsequently Śrīdhar Swami and I moved out of our *brahmacārī* quarters in Bombay into an apartment in the same building on the same floor as Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters. We had more interaction with Śrīla Prabhupāda then, and greater freedom to see him whenever necessary.

*I always carried my tridaṇḍa with me wherever I went, even if I was just going into Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters and out again. Śrīdhar Mahārāja was not doing that consistently. He always carried his tridaṇḍa to the temple and on preaching engagements, but when he went to see Śrīla Prabhupāda, he did not always have his tridaṇḍa with him.*

*One day Śrīla Prabhupāda acknowledged my carrying my daṇḍa everywhere, and at the same time he asked, “I am happy that you always carry your daṇḍa. But why is Śrīdhar Swami not carrying his daṇḍa?”*

The Māyāvādī *sannyāsīs* take one *daṇḍa*, or one rod, whereas the Vaiṣṇava *sannyāsīs* take three *daṇḍas*. It is made of bamboo. A *tridaṇḍa* is actually made up of four sticks wrapped in a cloth. The one rod represents the *jīva* or the soul. The other three *daṇḍas* symbolize his body, mind, and speech. So the soul engages himself with his body, mind, and words for preaching Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Taking up this *tridaṇḍa* literally means that you engage yourself and whatever assets you possess in the exclusive service of Kṛṣṇa.

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s appreciation of my carrying my *daṇḍa* gave me a realisation. It was proper for a *sannyāsī* to always carry his *tridaṇḍa.* The *tridaṇḍa* is from Vṛndāvana and Śrīla Prabhupāda handed it to me in the courtyard of the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple. I had been carrying it everywhere. It has gone around the world to all the continents and on all the flights. Śrīla Prabhupāda’s god brother, Bhakti Vaibhava Purī Mahārāja, had on many occasions expressed his pleasure when he saw me carrying my *daṇḍa*.

Often when I am travelling abroad, the airlines try to insist that my *daṇḍa* has to be carried in the belly of the plane with the other baggage. Sometimes they wonder whether it is a fishing rod! When that happens, it prompts me to think that indeed it is a different kind of fishing rod. This existence is an ocean with a misdirected civilization. The people of this age are like fish in that ocean and the travelling and preaching *sannyāsīs* are trying to fish people out of that ocean.

Once when flying from Chicago to Los Angeles on an United Airlines plane, the attendant insisted that my *daṇḍa* be checked in. I said, “I’m sorry, but I cannot release this *daṇḍa* to you. Is it possible to speak to the captain?” It was a lady captain who said, “Oh, just keep it right behind me.” We did just that, and my *daṇḍa* travelled on board in the cockpit. I always try to avoid letting them carry it underneath the plane because I am afraid that it may be lost or damaged. Sometimes I even try to prevent it from being carried in the baggage section by being tricky, and I almost always win.

After the terrorist attacks on the Twin Towers in New York on 11 September 2001, I have opted not to carry my *tridaṇḍa* with me outside of India. The security at airports has become very stringent making it increasingly difficult to carry my *tridaṇḍa* on board. Śrīla Prabhupāda had written a letter in 1976 in which he had stated that if it was too problematic to carry the *daṇḍa*, there was no need to do so. This is also in line with the GBC rules. In March 1983 the GBC had resolved that all ISKCON *sannyāsīs* must carry their *tridaṇḍa* for a minimum of ten years, thereafter it is optional.

**You Study Here and Preach Here**

A short while after my *sannyāsa* initiation, I tried to obtain permission from Śrīla Prabhupāda to go to Vṛndāvana to study his books. I had returned to Bombay after the ceremony and continued my services and preaching there for a while. After a while it dawned on me that as a *sannyāsī,* it was important to have extensive knowledge of the relevant scriptures. I needed to become more serious. It felt crucial that I spend more time reading Śrīla Prabhupāda’s books. I had a definite plan of action and I wanted Śrīla Prabhupāda’s approval. I sought Śrīla Prabhupāda, found him free and went to disclose my thoughts to him.

*I began my conversation by suggesting to Śrīla Prabhupāda, “Since taking sannyāsa initiation, I am getting many more preaching opportunities than before. I feel that I should have a better understanding of scriptures. Will you please grant me permission to go to Vṛndāvana so that I may engross myself in serious study of your books?”*

*I looked expectantly at Śrīla Prabhupāda. I thought my desire was reasonable and justified and there would not be much resistance from Śrīla Prabhupāda. Much to my surprise, Śrīla Prabhupāda’s response was an emphatic “No!”*

*“I understand. You want to study,” he said. “But I want you to preach. Study right here in Bombay. You study here and preach here.”*

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s words were final and I fully understood and surrendered to his instruction. Those were the pioneering days of our movement, the early days of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. These were demanding times. We did not have the luxury of taking off or going anywhere for extended study retreats. There was a shortage of manpower in Bombay. There was especially a shortage of Hindi preachers to preach to the locals in their vernacular. Being fluent in Hindi, my services were much needed right there in Bombay. So I continued with my preaching activities and somehow I found a way to increase my studying a little, but not as much as I would have liked.

It was not long however, that Śrīla Prabhupāda himself asked me to leave Bombay. Haṁsadūta Dāsa was going on a preaching mission and wanted me to join his travelling party. Gopāla Kṛsṇa Dāsa wanted me to stay in Bombay as there was much work to be done there. Śrīla Prabhupāda had also wanted me in Bombay. Yet he sanctioned my travelling with Haṁsadūta. The reason behind this was simple. When it came to preaching or a practical plan to expand the mission, Śrīla Prabhupāda would always approve. The knowledge that I wanted to acquire in Vṛndāvana would come with the practical experience of studying and preaching.

The instruction of the spiritual master therefore supersedes one’s own plans because a pure devotee like Śrīla Prabhupāda has the spiritual potency to understand what is needed for Kṛṣṇa’s service.

Śrīla Prabhupāda instituted “*nityaṁ bhāgavata-sevayā*” for all his followers. This meant daily recitation of *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* and some personal study, on a daily basis. Śrīla Prabhupāda did not want me to give up my services and personal assignments and go to Vṛndāvana just to study.

Śrīla Prabhupāda translated “*nityaṁ bhāgavata-sevayā*” in a unique way – recitation of the book *Bhāgavata* and serving the pure devotee, *Bhāgavata*. The dimension I was giving up by going away to study was rendering service to the person *Bhāgavata*. Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted me to study *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* in Bombay, as well as to serve his mission in Bombay and the pure person *Bhāgavata,* Śrīla Prabhupāda there and not temporarily abandon the one for the other.

## Chapter 14. The Truth About Lord Rāma Worshipping Lord Śiva

*Bombay, 1975*

Girirāja Dāsa, Kīrtanānanda Mahārāja and I were amongst the group of devotees on the morning walk on 23 December with Śrīla Prabhupāda. The subject of Ratha-yātrā was touched on briefly. The conversation then steered to *vātsalya-rasa.* Śrīla Prabhupāda expertly used the example of the loving relationship between Mother Yaśodā and Kṛṣṇa and then referred to the *Rāmāyaṇa* and Prahlada Mahārāja’s *līlā*. The real and false worlds were discussed and the topic of life and death was generated. The eternity of the soul was emphasized with reference to the *Bhagavad-gītā.* We walked alongside Śrīla Prabhupāda at a brisk pace, at times breaking out into a jog, as we could not keep up with Śrīla Prabhupāda’s energetic gait.

*During the morning walk, I mentioned Ratha-yātrā in Bombay. I suggested, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, the Jagannātha Ratha-yātrā has attracted a tremendous number of people from all over the world; I was wondering about the people in Bombay.” I raised this question wondering how the people of Bombay would respond to Ratha-yātrā. Even though the festival of Ratha-yātrā was taking place in the West, there was no such festival in Bombay.*

*Before Śrīla Prabhupāda could answer, Girirāja Dāsa intervened by mentioning to Śrīla Prabhupāda that a question had been raised at a programme. He then asked about the worship of Lord Śiva by Lord Rāmacandra.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda expertly answered: “Sometimes the father takes the child on his shoulder. Does it mean the child is more important than the father? They say in the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, there is no such incidence as Lord Rāmacandra worshipping Lord Śiva. It is later on, interpretation. But even if He does so, what is wrong here?”*

*Lord Kṛṣṇa and Lord Rāma like to worship Their devotees.*

*Lord Rāma had arrived at Rāmeśvaram on His way to Laṅkā to kill Rāvaṇa, the king of Laṅkā, who had abducted Sītā.*

*The topic of the Rāmāyaṇa then concluded with me querying, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, are you planning to comment on the Rāmāyaṇa in the future?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda replied, “I want to complete Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam first before I consider anything else.”*

Lord Jagannātha means “Lord of the Universe”. Lord Jagannātha’s Ratha-yātrā means the travelling of Lord Jagannātha, Lord Balāram and Subadra Devi by way of chariot. Lord Jagannātha is so merciful that he travels by chariot on the streets to give *darśana.*

I had heard reports about the Ratha-yātrā festivals in the west. This festival was successfully held in major cities such as London, New York and San Francisco, amongst others. I really wanted to see a similar festival in India, especially in Bombay and wanted Śrīla Prabhupāda to make some comment. However Girirāja Dāsa intervened and distracted Śrīla Prabhupāda so I did not receive a response from Śrīla Prabhupāda.

Eventually Ratha-yātrā did start in Bombay, but not during Śrīla Prabhupāda’s days. Nowadays, it attracts tens of thousands of people. There is also a Jagannātha temple in Mira Road, Bombay.

With reference to the position of Lord Śiva, Śrīla Prabhupāda had commented that in the original *Rāmāyaṇa* written by Sage Vālmīki that there is no mention of Lord Rāma’s worship of Lord Śiva. This so called pastime was a speculation by the Rāma *bhaktas* and Śaivites. How can Śiva be superior to God or be worshippable by God?

The debate will be better served, if those who claim this try to listen to what Lord Śiva says in the *Padma-purana*:

*ārādhanānāṁ sarveṣāṁ*  
*viṣṇor ārādhanaṁ param*  
*tasmāt parataraṁ devi*  
*tadīyānāṁ samarcanam*

Lord Śiva told the goddess Durgā: “My dear Devī, although the Vedas recommend worship of demigods, the worship of Lord Viṣṇu is topmost.” Śiva is considered the topmost Vaiṣṇava and He worships Lord Viṣṇu. The principles of the *varṇāśrama* are actually meant to organize society to prepare everyone to worship Lord Viṣṇu*.*

Reflecting on Śrīla Prabhupāda’s response to my question about translating *Rāmāyaṇa,* I understood that although the *Rāmāyaṇa* was on Śrīla Prabhupāda’s mind, he wanted to complete the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* commentary first because this was his priority. Śrīla Prabhupāda had presented detailed commentary on the voluminous *Bhagavad-gītā, the Caitanya-caritāmṛta* and *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam,* a major accomplishment recognized by both spiritualists and academics alike. However, Śrīla Prabhupāda only completed *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* purports up to Canto 10, Chapter 13 before leaving this mundane world. Time did not permit him to work on the *Rāmāyaṇa.*

**The Real and False Worlds**

As the conversation progressed, I asked Śrīla Prabhupāda about the real world and this material world in which we live. Śrīla Prabhupāda replied with spiritual precision and logic.

*“The real world is the spiritual world, and here it is simply a reflection, but we are taking this as the real world, and spiritual, there is no spiritual* – *yathābhāso yathā tamaḥ. So Śaṅkarācārya said that this world is false, jagan mithyā. So, but brahma satyam, he gave the same information. But we are taking this jagat satyam, and there is no spiritual world. This is the defect.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda used the example of a foolish animal in a desert who gets bewildered by a mirage. Thinking the mirage to be water, it goes after the water. Similarly, like the mirage, the material world is also false.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda added, “That does not mean there is no water anywhere else. There is water, but this mirage water is false. Similarly, this material world is false, but that does not mean there is no spiritual world. The water is somewhere else. That you do not know. But you are after this mirage, water in the desert.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then quoted from śāstra, “Vyāsadeva offered his respect to the real truth, satyaṁ paraṁ dhīmahi, where there is no mirage, there is no false world; real world.”*

*Presenting further śāstric evidence of the spiritual world from the Bhagavad-gītā, Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “And Kṛṣṇa gives the same information, yad gatvā na nivartante tad dhāma paramaṁ mama. That is My real abode, where if you go you don’t come back. And so long you are in this material world, bhūtvā bhūtvā pralīyate, you accept one body, suffer or enjoy, there is no enjoyment, suffering, and again accept another body; tathā dehāntara-prāptir. This is going on. Therefore they do not understand when you speak of the other world. But if you speak of this world, that ‘I’ll make you happy in this world’, thousands and millions will follow you. And as soon as you say that ‘I shall make you happy in the next world’, the response is ‘This is all nonsense. Crazy fellow!’”*

*I raised the topic of life and death and of foolish people who do not accept that there is life after death, to which Śrīla Prabhupāda responded that such people do not understand the evidence in front of them that a person has one type of body as child, then develops another body as a young man. The body is changing right before their eyes. That logic must be extended to understand how a body will change after death too.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda elaborated, “When the body is finished, that does not mean that you are finished. You have to accept another body. That is real truth, and it is spoken by Kṛṣṇa not any so-called scientist. The Supreme Person says na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre.”*

*I then said, “Well, they will not accept Kṛṣṇa either.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda exclaimed, “Then, how rascal they are!”*

Retrospectively meditating on Śrīla Prabhupāda’s words, I understand that he was imparting such precious pearls of wisdom to all present that day. In *śāstra* it is stated that only in the spiritual world can we experience real life. The spiritual world is real and the material world can be likened to a mirage in a desert. A mirage gives us false hope, that there is water in a place that is actually barren and dry.

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s aim was to ensure that we understand the essential difference between matter and spirit, and then between the material and spiritual worlds. Śrīla Prabhupāda introduced contemporary society to the spiritual world and by doing this; he has shown the inhabitants of the material world the path back to Godhead, making going back home, back to Godhead, a worldwide household practice.

## Chapter 15. Intimate Connection with Truth That Is Sādhana

*Bombay, 1975*

It was 24 December when Śrīla Prabhupāda went on another enlivening morning walk. Śrīla Prabhupāda was going to leave for Sanand the next day, and so we had a myriad of questions for him. In this conversation the topic of truth in relation to chanting is discussed. Śrīla Prabhupāda explains *sādhana-bhakti.*

**Truth and Experimental Knowledge**

*Śrīla Prabhupāda stated, “If truth is there, there is no question of experiment. And if experiment is there, that is not truth.”*

*I offered an example that Śrīla Prabhupāda had previously used to explain how experiments are in fact speculation, “When your mother tells you who your father is, you accept that as truth, and you don’t go about trying to experiment whether he is your father. When you hear the truth, there is no need to experiment.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed and happily commented, “That is perfect experiment.” In pursuing the idea of truth, Śrīla Prabhupāda went on to explain, “Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (*[*1.1.1*](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-1/1.1)*) begins with satyaṁ paraṁ dhīmahi. The beginning is truth... There is no question of experiment. It is not this book, Bhāgavata, is an experiment to find out. It is beginning from the truth.”*

*I asked Śrīla Prabhupāda, “How was it possible that sometimes it is said to karmīs that they should try out (or experiment) with the chanting of Hare Kṛṣṇa, so that then they will realise it.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda responded, “Because he does not know; therefore we suggest experiment… One who knows, there is no experiment… You are asking, requesting to chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, but this is not experiment for me, it is experiment for him. He does not know, therefore you are suggesting, ‘You do like this, and you will understand’. ”*

Śrīla Prabhupāda lectured us on many occasions that “experimental knowledge” is never perfect. That knowledge which is unknowable and inconceivable, which is beyond your perception and imagination, you cannot get by experiment. The so-called scientists, they say, “We are trying.”

“You can try on, but it is beyond your experience, beyond your knowledge. Your senses are all imperfect... You cannot understand the soul by experimental knowledge. You have to hear from the authority.” (*Lecture by Śrīla Prabhupāda, London, August 13, 197*1). Śrīla Prabhupāda’s example of the child confirming with his mother who his father is, illustrates an exceptional way to accept authority without question.

**Sādhana-bhakti Is Practice**

Since I had started a personal dialogue with Śrīla Prabhupāda, I seized the moment to ask Śrīla Prabhupāda about *sādhana-bhakti*.

Ś*rīla Prabhupāda explained briefly, “Sādhana-bhakti is practice. Truth is there, but how to be intimately connected with truth that is sādhana.”*

*Kīrtanānanda then described how one morning while walking, Hayagrīva commented that the sunrise was so beautiful, which Śrīla Prabhupāda quickly corrected by saying that it was not the sunrise that was beautiful, but the one who created the sunrise is in fact beautiful. Śrīla Prabhupāda then elucidated that the point was that people glorify scientists, yet they fail to glorify He who created such a brain.*

I thought that Śrīla Prabhupāda’s explanation of *sādhana-bhakti* was succinct. My path in *sādhana-bhakti* became more serious after that profound statement. Śrīla Prabhupāda said that truth exists, but the process to intimately connect with that truth, is practice.

*“Sādhana-bhakti* means ‘we have to practice it.’ And it does not require much education. It does not depend on your riches in that you have to become a very rich man to follow the regulative principles, rise early in the morning. Anyone can rise early. It is simply practice. You can take bath in the Yamunā. It is simply practice. You can visit the temples early in the morning. It is simply practice. So this is called *sādhana-bhakti*, practicing. Go on.” (*Lecture by Śrīla Prabhupāda, The Nectar of Devotion, Vṛndāvana, 13 November 1972.*)

## Chapter 16. Attraction Between Man And Woman

*Bombay, 1975*

This is the continuation of the conversation on the morning walk of 24 December. Śrīla Prabhupāda elucidates questions on the attraction between man and woman and its effects on spiritual life. The cultivation of detachment and importance of *sannyāsa* is emphasized as well.

*I submissively enquired from His Divine Grace, “What is the difference between the body of a man and that of a woman, because despite being made of the same things (kapha, pitta, vāyu), they are still attracted to one another?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda explained how nature has arranged that man and woman are made attractive for those who choose to want to be bound up by this attraction, puṁsaḥ striyā mithunī-bhāvam etam. “The whole material attraction means a man’s attraction for woman and a woman’s attraction for man.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then explained that when they are actually attracted, then that unity causes the material bondage to become tighter and then there is no chance for spiritual advancement. He then described how Vedic civilization can slacken that bondage by force (namely separation or sannyāsa). Śrīla Prabhupāda then laughingly declared, “Man is good, woman is good and when they are united, they are bad! Both of them are bad. And the material world is thinking, ‘This is the best thing.’ But actually that is not the thing. Man is good, because he is part and parcel of God, and woman is good, part and parcel of God, but when they unite, they become bad, tayor mitho hṛdaya-granthim āhuḥ.”*

*I enquired, “So when they are gṛhastha, they make advancement?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “Hm? That advancement is not very solid. But there is advancement; but that is not very solid.”*

I realised after joining the movement that the attraction for the opposite gender causes bondage which is an obstacle on the spiritual path. I mused over a story that Śrīla Prabhupāda used to illustrate the enduring attraction of a man for a woman. The Mughal Emperor, Akbar believed that old men will not have such attractions. Birbal, his wise advisor and close associate challenged him and asked the King to take his daughter to see a dying man and watch his reaction. The King observed that even though the man was gravely ill, he ignored the king and lustily looked at his daughter, thus confirming Birbal’s contention.

In response to my second query, Śrīla Prabhupāda would often cite the verse from the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Fifth Canto, that there are *gṛhasthas*, “but they are not attached to *gṛhastha* life.” They are attached to Kṛṣṇa, but for convenience they remain *gṛhasthas.* He often reiterated that the real purpose of *āśrama* is self realisation and development of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. He strongly advocated that the *gṛhastha-āśrama* should be undertaken in conjunction with rules and regulations and should be a pathway to Kṛṣṇa consciousness.

Similarly in verse 7 of *Bhaja Govindam*, a moving tribute to the Lord and a song consisting of 14 verse-blossoms, Śrī Śaṅkara Bhagavatpāda encourages the devotee to develop a special attraction for the Lord:

*bālas tāvat krīḍā-saktaḥ*  
*taruṇas tāvat taruṇī-saktaḥ*  
*vṛddhas tāvac cintā-saktaḥ*  
*pare brahmaṇi ko’pi na saktaḥ*

**“Childhood is lost by attachment to playfulness. Youth is lost by attachment to woman. Old age passes away by thinking over many past things. Alas! Hardly is there anyone who yearns to be attached to** ***Parabrahman*****.”**

**Cultivation of Detachment**

I confirmed with Śrīla Prabhupāda that man and woman should remain separate in order to ensure detachment.

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed and further explained how the Vedic system is a gradual one, where a brahmacārī is first taught that it is better not to marry and enter into family life. However, if he is still inclined to marriage, then a concession is made for a while, but then there is compulsory separation from family life* (*vānaprastha) followed by renunciation or sannyāsa. I asked about the advancement made during gṛhastha-āśrama*. *Śrīla Prabhupāda commented that even though there is advancement, it is “not very solid.” He then mentioned that the attraction between a man and woman is due to sex pleasure.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda expanded upon this, using the śāstra as testimony to this reality. Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* ([7.9.45](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-7/9.45)) states, *yan maithunādi-gṛhamedhi-sukhaṁ hi tuccham*. W*hy they are working so hard day and night? Only for that sex pleasure. That is the psychology. There is no other happiness. Their happiness is based on the centre of sex pleasure… In this material world, so-called pleasure means sex pleasure. Why they are working so hard day and night? Only for that sex pleasure. That is the centre.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda emphasized again that detachment is required for sannyāsa, “He has no attachment for this eating, sleeping, mating. He is attached to Kṛṣṇa. And that is sannyāsī. Sannyāsī does not mean dress. Detached. Sat sannyāsī. Sat, oṁ tat sat, the Supreme, and for Him everything, risk all. That is sannyāsa… ’anāśritaḥ karma-phalaṁ kāryaṁ karma karoti yaḥ’. One who does not take the reward of his labour, he is sannyāsī… Everyone works for some profit, and one who does not take the profit, works for Kṛṣṇa, he is sannyāsī.”*

“According to the Vedic system, a man is expected to detach himself from family affairs in the latter part of his life because old age is the notice of death. A householder should leave home and go to the forest and live there in seclusion in order to obtain knowledge from the sages about the real purpose of life, spiritual progress, and about how to prepare for the next life.”(*Back to Godhead, volume 37, 1970, How to check the Age of Kali*).

Śrīla Prabhupāda advises that at the age of 50, the husband and wife should go away from home and travel to places of pilgrimage just to detach them from family affection. “In this way, when the man is a little more advanced, he asks his wife, ‘You go and take care of the family and your sons, grown-up, they’ll take care of you. Let me take *sannyāsa’.”* (*Conversations with Śrīla Prabhupāda, Press Interview, Los Angeles, December 30, 1968*).

I pondered on the frightening thought of material bondage and the cruel effects thereof. When I had approached Śrīla Prabhupāda to give me *sannyāsa* he had also said very similar things about the *sannyāsa* order of life. Once again I was reminded of that conversation and I realized that my decision to take *sannyāsa* was indeed the correct one!

## Chapter 17. Why Are You Becoming Paramahaṁsa?

*Māyāpur, 1976*

Śrīla Prabhupāda had been in Māyāpur since January. He often walked on the roof of the Lotus Building. The roof was forty-feet wide and 150 feet long. It was decorated with a variety of potted plants and shrubs. Śrīla Prabhupāda would circumambulate the two rooms located at the centre. The view from the roof was panoramic making it a pleasing place for Śrīla Prabhupāda to walk and chant early in the morning. He would generally be surrounded by a large group of senior men.

On 11 March, I had joined Śrīla Prabhupāda on one of these walks on the roof. There was a large group of devotees with Śrīla Prabhupāda, amongst whom, I recall seeing Bhavānanda Mahārāja, Gurudāsa Mahārāja, Gurukṛpā Mahārāja, Harikeśa Mahārāja, Jayapatākā Mahārāja, Jayatīrtha Mahārāja, Pañcadraviḍa Mahārāja, Puṣṭa Kṛṣṇa Mahārāja, Satsvarūpa dāsa Goswami, Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami and Viṣṇujana Mahārāja. This was my first year in Māyāpur as a *sannyāsī* and now I had the privilege of greater access to Śrīla Prabhupāda. I offered my obeisances and was about to join the group.

*Śrīla Prabhupāda kindly greeted me, “Hare Kṛṣṇa.” In the same breath, he asked, “So why you are becoming paramahaṁsa? This is paramahaṁsa dress.” He pointed at my clothing.*

*The whole entourage had stopped, and I was facing them all. Śrīla Prabhupāda was in the centre and I was in the limelight. I immediately realised that the colour of the cloth that I was wearing, looked dull and faded. It was not bright saffron.*

*“I have to dye it today,” I said, embarrassed.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda reprimanded me, “Don’t become paramahaṁsa immediately. Paramahaṁsa is the last stage of sannyāsa. The first stage is kuṭīcaka, then bahūdaka followed by parivrājakācārya and then the final stage, paramahaṁsa. Therefore paramahaṁsa stage is the topmost.”*

*“Does the paramahaṁsa wear white?” I humbly enquired.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda resumed walking and responded, “Paramahaṁsa is not subject to rules and regulations. He is not actually in the material world. He is in the stage of mahābhāva. In the kuṭīcaka stage, one is expected to live outside the village and accepts foodstuffs from his family. Someone with little experience lives by mādhukarī.”*

*“Madhukara means the bee,” Śrīla Prabhupāda explained. “He collects a little honey here, little honey there. He does not collect a lump sum of food from any one place. To any gṛhastha he can say, ‘Please give me a little piece of bread’. That is not difficult for them. ‘All right, take’. A travelling sannyāsī may go to many gṛhasthas, begging a little piece from everyone. So he is bahūdaka. Then, when he’s further experienced, preaching place to place, country to country, that is parivrājakācārya. And when he has sufficiently preached, he can sit down anywhere. That is paramahaṁsa.”*

*The topic ended on that philosophical note. Śrīla Prabhupāda didn’t pursue it, and I was relieved to be off the hook. For some time there was silence. A chilly breeze blew across the roof, and from the main gate the sound of melodious shahnāi became audible.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda heard this and said, “In every big temple there is shahnāi. All throughout the year, morning and night. And not only temples, but also rich men’s houses. And at night they go to bed hearing the shahnāi and early in the morning they wake up by hearing it.”*

*The conversation rolled onto the idea of alarm clocks and I said, “People keep cocks and hens for waking up.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed, “Yes. What is that called? Alarm? ‘Kaa- kaa-kaa, Get up please. Kaa-kaa-kaa.’ Nature’s alarm.”*

*The devotees were thoroughly amused at Śrīla Prabhupāda’s excellent mimicking.*

I was not strictly following the right code of dress as a *sannyāsī.* Śrīla Prabhupāda caught me on the colour of my cloth, but used it as a reminder for everybody else that nobody is above the rules except *paramahaṁsa.* He used the term *paramahaṁsa.* Although it means “the highly elevated, perfection stage of life”, Śrīla Prabhupāda was really asking me if I was already above the rules. He wanted to know how I had achieved this stage of life so quickly. Sometimes we have a tendency to overestimate ourselves and easily fall into the trap of thinking, “I am now a *sannyāsī* and advanced. These rules are for the neophytes. So there is no need for me to follow the rules.” Śrīla Prabhupāda*’*s lesson was clear.

We must not imitate, deviate from, or neglect the rules. The rules are there for a reason and therefore they have to be followed strictly. There should be no imitation of the *paramahaṁsa* stages. You have to grow to get to the platform of *paramahaṁsa*. It is a gradual process and the rules assist you to advance through the stages which in turn facilitate growth so that it becomes possible to attain the stage of *paramahaṁsa.*

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s perfect mimicking of the cocks triggered my memory and took me back to my childhood days in the village of Aravade where the cocks were also our alarm clocks. This was also the way of life that dated back to the days when Lord Kṛṣṇa was in Dvārakā with His queens. He was woken up in the very same manner. I realised that the simple way of life using nature’s alarm was quickly disappearing.

It appears as if modern man has taken a united stand against the hen and cock, “You rascal cock! Your early morning crowing is simply a disturbance. For this you need to be punished. I will destroy you by eating you.”

## Chapter 18. The Deity Is Not a Beggar

*Māyāpur, 1976*

**T**his morning walk took place on 12 March 1976 in Māyāpur. In this conversation Śrīla Prabhupāda explained the position of the Deity in relation to mundane business and simultaneously emphasized the importance of allowing everyone to take *darśana* of the Deities without being pressurized to give donations.

*Yaśodānandana suggested that instead of requesting money, the people should offer something to the Deity. But Śrīla Prabhupāda did not see why this was necessary and responded, “Why? Nothing. Let them come and see. That’s all.”*

*Yaśodānandana agreed that the Deity is not a beggar, to which Śrīla Prabhupāda replied, “No. He is bestower. He gives everything. If anyone voluntarily gives, that’s all right. You should not ask. By showing the Deity to earn livelihood is condemned. Better become a sweeper, municipal sweeper, and sweep the road and earn money. He said like that. ‘That is better, honourable. He is working and getting money, instead of making a good show of Deity and earn money. Don’t cheat people.’ That is Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī.”*

*I said, “There are many different views about what is correct.”*

*Pañcadraviḍa added that there was trouble in Navadvīpa because of that and Śrīla Prabhupāda replied, “Yes. He was fighting against it. All, Navadvīpa, Vṛndāvana. Vṛndāvana is not so... But still, they have got rate. One thing, it is a question of heart, that a man should come and visit the temple* – *he must give something. Why he is to be asked? Voluntarily he should give that.”*

*Yaśodānandana said, “Your Divine Grace is writing in the Nectar of Devotion that it is customary in India that whenever one visits the Deity or a saintly person to offer something, even one grain of rice.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed, “Yes, that is the system.”*

*I asked, “But encouraging is not recommended?”*

*It was unclear to Śrīla Prabhupāda what I was asking and Yaśodānandana added, “Requesting should not be done?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda answered, “No.”*

*I then said, “It is voluntary.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda expanded his idea: “Encouraging means your behaviour should be so nice that he voluntarily gives. That is encouraging, not that begging and put something here. ‘My belly is empty. Here is an institution. You kindly become a member. Help us’. That is another thing. But why should you earn by showing the Deity? You work so nicely they will become voluntarily member, contributing. That is nice. But not that ‘Now we have got Deity. He’s starving. Please give me something’. No. That is not good practice.”*

I remember Śrīla Prabhupāda saying that “Temple means people should come, people should learn the science of God. That is temple.” So effectively, a temple should not be converted into a business. I was of the opinion that encouraging people to give money to the temple was fine, whereas literally asking them for money was not. Śrīla Prabhupāda clarified that doubt with a firm “No!”

I remember relating the following incident to Śrīla Prabhupāda. It took place in 1977 at a Gauḍīya Maṭha temple in Bengal. Our bullock cart *saṅkīrtana* party had visited their temple. The Deities were beautifully adorned solely for the purpose of raising money. Some visitors would put the coins in the donation box, while others just threw the coins towards the Deities. The *pūjārīs* would quickly try to grab the money that was thrown. Some coins did not land flush and kept rolling. The *pūjārī* would scramble after these coins, which sometimes landed under the altar.

This made me realise that displaying the Deities for money changes a person’s visit to the temple from a spiritual one to a business transaction.

## Chapter 19. That Is Real Compassion

*Māyāpur, 1976*

On the morning walk of 19 March in Śrī Dhāma Māyāpur, Rādhāvallabha Dāsa brought to our attention an article written by Śrīla Prabhupāda in a *Back to Godhead* magazine that described an increase in women population as a natural disaster.

**Women Are More Potent**

*Rādhāvallabha broached the question, “So when one woman read this article, she became very angry. She came back and was very angry.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda responded calmly, “She may be angry. She is woman and man. Actually this is physiological. If a man is too much addicted to sex life, he’ll become impotent, and if he begets child, it will be a girl. With no potency to give birth to a male child... That requires potency.”*

*Other devotees had also noticed the presence of more girls in other countries as well and nodded in the affirmative.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda delved into more detail, “Yes. The whole world is full of girls, girl children. Why? There is no potency. Potency finished. Or impotent. And if you keep one boy brahmacārī, no sex life, and get him married, the first child must be a boy, must be, without any doubt.”*

*I proposed my view to Śrīla Prabhupāda on the matter, “That means, then, woman is more potent than...” Before I could complete my sentence, Śrīla Prabhupāda exclaimed, “Yes, yes. Yes. The Āyurvedic formula is that when there is discharge, woman’s discharge, more, means girl, and man’s discharge, more, means boy. This is physiological.”*

Even though the perception of the role of women may change in different countries and cultures, Śrīla Prabhupāda always strived to engage everyone in terms of brahminical qualifications. Therefore Śrīla Prabhupāda allowed both men and women to engage in temple worship. This move was not very well received by the *brāhmaṇa* class of people in India. Śrīla Prabhupāda did not waste time trying to defend his decisions. There was no hatred or looking down upon women. God has made them different, with different roles, capabilities, emotions, strengths and weaknesses. Subsequently, some of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s women disciples went to great lengths to serve ISKCON with great success.

**Prostitution**

In the next excerpt the discussion about women continues, where Pañcadraviḍa Mahārāja asked Śrīla Prabhupāda about a particular prostitute that the *gopīs* refer to in the *Kṛṣṇa* book.

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was bemused, “Why you are asking me? As if I have to keep account of prostitute. What is this nonsense?” This was met with laughter.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then spoke about the educationist, Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar who would show compassion to the prostitutes by giving them two rupees and sending them home. Then there was Haridāsa Ṭhākura who converted a prostitute to a devotee. “That’s it. That is real compassion not, ‘Take two rupees and go home’. No. Turn her. That is real compassion, to turn everyone to become Kṛṣṇa conscious. That is the greatest welfare activity in the world. Otherwise there cannot be any peace or prosperity.”*

*In continuing the conversation about prostitutes, I made an observation, “In Bhāgavatam, Śrīla Prabhupāda, you mentioned that prostitutes are needed in the society.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “Yes, for the rascals. Otherwise they will pollute the innocent girls. That is the policy of the Western civilization, that ‘Let the karmīs enjoy new, new girls and be energetic to produce machine.’ This is the European civilization, American civilization. ## Chapter 20. Export And Import Business

*Delhi/Vṛndāvana, 1976*

The Māyāpur festival had just ended and devotees were making their way to Delhi by either train or plane. Most of the devotees had stayed back with Śrīla Prabhupāda who would stay in Delhi for five days before continuing via Modinagar to Vrndāvana where a three day festival was going to be held on the first anniversary of the opening of the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple. Gurudāsa Mahārāja, I and others received Śrīla Prabhupāda at Palam airport.

**Appreciating Śrīla Prabhupāda’s Work in The West**

The three day *paṇḍāl* programme took place at the ‘*Rām Līlā* grounds’. This was a huge *paṇḍāl* programme and I had the opportunity of introducing Śrīla Prabhupāda and his mission to the audience.

*I glanced at the huge crowd in the paṇḍāl, mentally prayed to my spiritual master for his blessings and then addressed the audience.*

*“Hare Kṛṣṇa, yahā pe āye huve sabhi atithi bhakta jano kā Hare Krishna utsava me svāgat hai (I welcome everyone present here today). It gives me great pleasure to introduce to you, His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedānta Swami Prabhupāda Founder-Ācārya of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness and my revered spiritual master. Śrīla Prabhupāda was instructed by His Spiritual Master to go into the Western countries and preach in English. This saw the beginning of preparations for a lifetime journey.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda spent many years in Vṛndāvana training and preparing for this journey. What you may not know, is that he is also not new to Delhi. He has been living here, near the Red Fort Palace. In preparation for this journey and in keeping with his spiritual master’s instructions Śrīla Prabhupāda printed his first set of volumes of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam in Delhi. This is a grand achievement. Finally, in 1965 he began his journey to the West and landed in America.*

America was always used as a yardstick for other countries in terms of progress and many of our Indian leaders would go there to beg for things. As a result, India was known as the ‘beggar’s country’.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was the one to realize India’s wealth and he knows that India is the wealthiest nation in the world. He wanted the rest of the world to know this. So magnanimous is he that he was willing to share this wealth. When Śrīla Prabhupāda arrived in America, he noticed how miserable and frustrated the young boys and girls were. Some of them had even become ‘Hippies’. By Śrīla Prabhupāda’s grace, they became ‘Happys’. This was India’s priceless contribution to enrich the Western world.*

In response Śrīla Prabhupāda had said, ‘Your viceroys came to our country and plundered it and took all the valuables. They were in such haste to take everything that was valuable; they forgot the most valuable items. Now I have come here to make a hand delivery of these items. You had forgotten our country’s culture, the message of the Bhagavad-gītā, the holy name of the Lord. This is our real wealth’.” Śrīla Prabhupāda smiled proudly.*

*“This is how Śrīla Prabhupāda started the ‘import and export’ business,” I continued. “How many of you are aware of this? He is exporting the best products of our country: the holy names of the Lord with the message of the Bhagavad-gītā and ancient Indian culture and importing all these Western sādhus present here. Ladies and gentlemen, I now proudly present to you His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedānta Swami Śrīla Prabhupāda Dhanyavād. Thank you very much!”*

*The crowd cheered loudly in appreciation of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s achievements.*

Śrīla Prabhupāda had single-handedly achieved transforming the Western disciples into fully fledged *brāhmaṇas.* He received no assistance from the Indian Government or from his godbrothers of the Gauḍīya Maṭha. The smile and happiness on Śrīla Prabhupāda’s face displayed his appreciation of the way in which I had introduced my spiritual master.

Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted American *kṛṣṇa-bhaktas*. If you want to imitate Americans, then you could imitate American *kṛṣṇa- bhaktas*. This was Śrīla Prabhupāda’s approach. So now Śrīla Prabhupāda was not just by himself, but with his “white dancing elephants.” His approach had worked and thus his achievements were impressive.

Śrīla Prabhupāda arrived on time to give the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* class. The atmosphere was surcharged with excitement. Many devotees were visiting Vṛndāvana for the first time, and they now had their own temple in the holy *dhāma*, which was opened just the year before.

I applied the cooling paste to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s forehead, then stepped back to offer obeisances, thinking I was finished. Śrīla Prabhupāda called me back, touching his finger to his temples, to indicate that I had forgotten two important places.*

The activities of the past few days were both highly motivating and inspirational. Travelling with Śrīla Prabhupāda meant many preaching opportunities. We were also meeting with influential people who could assist in taking ISKCON forward. I relished every moment spent with Śrīla Prabhupāda as each moment was a learning experience.

Senior disciples get an opportunity to offer sandalwood paste and garlands to Śrīla Prabhupāda. On this day I was afforded the opportunity to do so. ## Chapter 21. Śrīla Prabhupāda Strikes a Bargain

*Vṛndāvana, 1976*

It was April. There were many devotees in Vṛndāvana because of the festival and initiations. Many of us accompanied Śrīla Prabhupāda on his morning walks. These morning walks sometimes took the form of direct instructions as well as practical training for all of us. Śrīla Prabhupāda would perform certain activities that we felt were too inconsequential for our spiritual master to do. Some of the activities would include shopping, while others included giving us instructions or merely quoting from scripture. On that day, we would see Śrīla Prabhupāda display his seasoned business skills.

Walking through a park on the morning of 5 April, we came across a vendor sitting beneath a tree with a basketful of drumsticks. These are long, green vegetable sticks that grow on trees. Śrīla Prabhupāda saw this vendor and walked directly towards him.

Śrīla Prabhupāda started to negotiate seriously in Hindi.

*I thought, why should Śrīla Prabhupāda be involved in these petty matters?*

*Being the only Hindi-speaking devotee present, I asked, “Should we buy some for you, Śrīla Prabhupāda?”*

*But he didn’t want me to interrupt. He responded, “No, no, I’ll talk with him. We can buy the whole thing.”*

*The bearded vendor quoted a high price, and Śrīla Prabhupāda sternly objected, “No! No! That is too much. I want to take the whole basketful.”*

*The vendor must have realised that Śrīla Prabhupāda was a seasoned buyer, so he drastically reduced his price.*

*I assumed he was also calculating the pious credit that would come to him by selling his goods to a sādhu. They ultimately agreed on a price, and the vendor weighed the drumsticks on his iron scale.*

*“So, give him two and a half rupees,” Śrīla Prabhupāda said.*

*When we heard the price, we were amazed. Gurudāsa Mahārāja congratulated Śrīla Prabhupāda.*

*“My father used to do that,” Śrīla Prabhupāda said. “He’d buy the whole basket, which the vendor would sell cheap because otherwise he’d have to sit all day. All that sabji was not required in the family, and my mother became angry, ‘You are bringing so many vegetables; they are getting spoiled’.”*

I remember thinking why was it necessary for Śrīla Prabhupāda to be involved in these activities as there were many disciples that could easily be engaged in this service. Being the only Indian disciple I was thinking it is my duty to assist Śrīla Prabhupāda.

Śrīla Prabhupāda was teaching us that if you are not fluent in the dealings with the vendors you can be easily cheated. Westerners are often cheated without even realising it.

Śrīla Prabhupāda had lived in Vṛndāvana for many years and learnt about the culture and business trends. He had owned Prayāga Pharmacy in Allahabad where he began selling medicines, tinctures, syrups, and other products manufactured by Bose’s Laboratory. A Dr. Ghosh would diagnose patients and give medical prescriptions, which Abhay (Śrīla Prabhupāda) would then fill. In return Dr. Ghosh would receive a 25% commission from the sale of the prescriptions. When Dr. Ghosh was asked to describe Śrīla Prabhupāda as an entrepreneur he said, “Śrīla Prabhupāda is a business-minded man. There was no doubt that Śrīla Prabhupāda was a *pukka* (seasoned) business man.”

Śrīla Prabhupāda was teaching us to become familiar with how Indian businesses operate. He further explained how he had been trained by his father to bargain with the vendors and now he was teaching us. The reasoning behind this was simple—the vendor would have to sell this amount of vegetables for the entire day, so if a good bargain was struck it benefited both people. Śrīla Prabhupāda was imparting good business techniques. The lesson that we were learning, was that when an opportunity presents itself it must not be wasted. Utility is the principle.

I recall Subhaga Swami also mentioning an incident when Srila Prabhupāda purchased a basket of farm fresh vegetables during a morning walk in Mayapur. The vendor followed Prabhupāda, carrying the vegetables back to the temple.

On both these occasions, I consoled myself with the fact that Śrīla Prabhupāda was engaging the vendors in Krsna’s service.

## Chapter 22. You Have Not Seen Your Grandfather, Why Do You Take His Will?

*Vṛndāvana, 1976*

On 8 April, Śrīla Prabhupāda’s car drove well beyond the outskirts of Vṛndāvana. When the car stopped, Śrīla Prabhupāda got off and went on his *japa* walk. It also became a *japa* talk because the devotees walking with him would ask various questions.

**Preaching in The West**

Disciples discussed with Śrīla Prabhupāda about the preaching in the West, and how he was becoming the master of buying churches and converting them into beautiful Hare Kṛṣṇa temples.

*I recollected that during Śrīla Prabhupāda’s previous visit to South America, a large crowd of Christians had gathered to hear him and had applauded him after he had spoken. Śrīla Prabhupāda recalled how a Catholic man had mentioned that St. Francis chanted to the birds, trees and dogs, to which Śrīla Prabhupāda had commented, “That is actually God realization.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda emphasized how the people in Western civilization did not know the aim of life and how they were captivated by the bodily concept of life. He described, “These rascals rise early in the morning and start their car to go to the office, five hours coming and going, and eight hours working there.”*

*To which I added, “…again in the evening they have to drive back.”*

*Pañcadraviḍa then pointed out that people of the Western societies are always taking pills to do their work, and then they take tranquillizers to help them sleep at night.*

*I cited from Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam that in Kali-yuga people will be starving, “As Kali-yuga advances, it is mentioned, that there will be durbhikṣa, meaning not even bhikṣā \[alms\] will be given to sādhus.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed. Pañcadraviḍa Mahārāja then asked Śrīla Prabhupāda what his plan had been when he had arrived in America, to which Śrīla Prabhupāda light heartedly replied, “This idea... I shall speak to... ‘don’t eat meat,’ and they’ll immediately kick me out.” There was laughter.*

*That was my programme… ‘Don’t eat meat. No illicit sex,’ and immediately they will kick me out… And I was asking Kṛṣṇa, ‘I do not know why You have brought me here. As soon as I will say these things, they will kick me out. What is Your programme, I do not know.’ ”*

*I then lovingly praised Śrīla Prabhupāda, “You expertly asked them to chant and didn’t mention any of the rules and regulations until they had developed a higher taste.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then went on to describe how women are fighting for equal rights with men and how if women become pregnant they even resort to killing the child. When the matter of sharing the responsibility of taking care of the child was brought up, Śrīla Prabhupāda then explained how the child naturally goes to the mother to be taken care of – even in animals. He added, “Kṛṣṇa states all of this in the Bhagavad-gītā.”*

*I commented, “People object to our preaching and question us on how we can speak about God when we haven’t seen God.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then cleverly responded, “You have not seen your grandfather, why do you take his will? To inherit the money. You have not seen your grandfather.”*

I thought about how this was the essence of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s teaching. We receive a human birth for a reason. Śrīla Prabhupāda was commenting on the mundane routine lifestyles of the Western world which could be the root cause of people moving away from the churches. People have become caught up in this web of material hankering and have forgotten the purpose of this human birth. If Kṛṣṇa or God is not the central focus of your life, then this life is wasted. Śrīla Prabhupāda was emphasizing the need to make Kṛṣṇa the central focus in our lives. If all of our activities are for the pleasure of the Lord, then our lives are a guaranteed success.

Therefore, God realisation does not depend on material opulence. Material opulence means to take birth in a high family, possess great riches and beauty and be well educated. I remember Śrīla Prabhupāda often said that material opulence is not essential for God realisation. No amount of wealth or power can purchase God. This Kṛṣṇa conscious movement enables you to utilize everything you find in this material world as a means to serve God. In this way because nothing is neglected, you advance in devotional service. As a result, your love for God grows and ultimately this love will become pure and powerful enough to win over God.

**Māyāvādī Philosophy and The Position of Vrajavāsīs**

Śrīla Prabhupāda had the utmost respect for the Vrajavāsīs and he always explained to his disciples about respecting the Vrajavāsīs and the dangers of ego. Śrīla Prabhupāda was also very strict about Māyāvādī philosophy and the dangers of associating with Māyāvādīs. He explained that a moment’s association with a Māyāvādī can cause a devotee to fall, but the Vrajavāsīs were above this.

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was driven further before the car was stopped again. Śrīla Prabhupāda stepped out and began meandering across an uneven field. Someone mentioned a Westerner who had been living in Vṛndāvana for nine years would not even offer obeisances to Śrīla Prabhupāda because of being influenced by Māyāvādī teachings.*

*Just then by coincidence, the farmer who owned the land came over to see us. He respectfully offered praṇāms and told Śrīla Prabhupāda that he and his family felt greatly honoured that Śrīla Prabhupāda was walking on their land.*

*“Jaya, Jaya. Hare Kṛṣṇa”, Śrīla Prabhupāda said to the farmer. Then to us he said, “This is the difference. One is living here nine years and he does not offer respect, and the farmer knows how to offer obeisances. Māyāvādi-bhāṣya śunile haya sarva nāśa, ‘your spiritual progress is finished if you associate with Māyāvādīs’.”*

*I asked, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, what is the position of Vrajavāsīs, those who are living in Vṛndāvana now? What happens to them in \[their\] next life?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda went on to explain the position of Vrajavāsīs. “Generally, Vrajavāsīs are naturally Kṛṣṇa conscious, otherwise, how is this simple farmer offering pranams? Even without a spiritual master they are already elevated to Kṛṣṇa consciousness, because the spiritual master is within – caita-guru. Simply by living here, if they do not commit any sin, they’ll go back home, back to Godhead. Always remember Kṛṣṇa, this is Kṛṣṇa’s land, man-manā bhava mad-bhakto. That will deliver them.”*

*I recollected aloud that Śrīla Prabhupāda once said that Kṛṣṇa acts like their spiritual master. To this, Śrīla Prabhupāda simply responded, “kṛṣṇa-guru-kṛpā.” I understood this to mean that the Vrajavāsīs have the mercy or kṛpā of Kṛṣṇa, who Himself acts as their guru.*

Śrīla Prabhupāda was explaining the eternal position of the Vrajavāsīs and how they are naturally attracted to Kṛṣṇa. The Vrajavāsīs are guaranteed a position back home provided they do not commit any sin. Gauging from the response Śrīla Prabhupāda received from the farmer, I was convinced further of his exalted position.

It is stated in the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* – *māyāvādī kṛṣṇe aparādhī* – “Māyāvādī impersonalists are great offenders unto Lord Kṛṣṇa.” Śrīla Prabhupāda was making us aware of *Kṛṣṇa-aparādhīs.* The Westerner could not understand the exalted position of Śrīla Prabhupāda. Impersonalists have no relationship with God. Vrajavāsīs on the other hand teach by example and serve the Lord and His devotees with love and kindness. They have complete unalloyed faith and stay away from impersonalist philosophy.

I always remember Śrīla Prabhupāda explaining that Kṛṣṇa is the spiritual master internally and the spiritual master is a manifestation of Kṛṣṇa externally. That is the position; a sincere student is instructed both ways, which is called *guru-kṛṣṇa-kṛpā.* One has to simply receive it properly and execute it in an orderly manner.

**Renunciation in Kali-yuga**

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “Vedic civilisation is centred around the forest, and not the villages.”*

*I remarked, “There are cities like Dvārakā.”*

*To this Śrīla Prabhupāda replied, “That is another thing, but the ideal is ‘vanaṁ gato yad dharim āśrayeta’. ‘Go to the forest and depend on Kṛṣṇa and live there’. And that is perfection. That’s all.”*

*I responded by saying, “There are no forests left.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda stated, “There are many forests in America and Africa.”*

*Akṣayānanda said, “We cannot do that in Kali-yuga due to our conditioning.”*

Society during Vedic civilisation consisted of divisions or the *varṇāśrama* system. One began as a *brahmacārī* (single celibate student), and then moved onto *gṛhastha* (family life). After family life, one had to accept *vānaprastha* life by going into the forest on pilgrimage. Here, one would take shelter of the Lord and His disciples. This ashram is finally followed by the sannyāsa or renunciate order.

In Vedic times the tendency was for people to retire and renounce. Modern culture is such that hardly anybody is capable of going to the forest and relying totally on Kṛṣṇa. Today, people have extra special impetus to enjoy, and hence, nurture endless desires. Present day cities have become forests of enjoyment or concrete jungles. If one cannot go to the forest, it is advised that one should at least visit or stay in ISKCON temples. Śrīla Prabhupāda emphasised the building of temples. It is for the purpose of getting *s*ā*dhu-saṅga*. Devotees can also take shelter of Their Lordships in these temples and further develop their spiritual life.

## Chapter 23. Kṛṣṇa Is the Key Man

*Vṛndāvana, 1976*

Haṁsadūta Mahārāja had invited me to join his Nitāi-Gaura World Travelling Saṅkīrtana Party which was was planning to travel and preach throughout India. When my authority in Bombay, Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Dāsa, found out about this, he immediately objected.

It was then three months after my *sannyāsa* initiation in December, and I was attracted to the opportunity to travel and preach. After the Māyāpur festival we all arrived in Vṛndāvana for the planned festivals there. Haṁsadūta had also arrived there with two German buses and was recruiting men to expand his *saṅkīrtana* party. Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Dāsa was also present. There was this unresolved dispute over my joining their respective parties between them and they wanted to present the issue before Śrīla Prabhupāda.

Śrīla Prabhupāda was in his quarters so all three of us made our way to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters. Two of them were like advocates arguing the case in front of His Divine Grace Śrīla Prabhupāda, the Judge.

*Haṁsadūta described the strategic benefits of having me join, “Most of the men in the party are Westerners. We need some Hindi speaking preachers. Lokanāth Maharaja is also very good at kīrtana.”*

*Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Dāsa put forward his argument, “Lokanāth Swami is a Bombay devotee. He has been there a very long time. He is my key man. He is absolutely indispensable.”*

*Despite the objections of the local GBC, Śrīla Prabhupāda was supportive of the travelling party and sympathetic to my desire to travel and preach, and resolved the issue by questioning, “What is this key man? Kṛṣṇa is the key man. If he wants to travel and preach, let him go.”*

So the matter was settled in favour of me joining the party. It was not always possible to avoid having disputes amongst ourselves. We always ran to Śrīla Prabhupāda to settle these differences. No matter where we were, he was always there for us. It was so easy for us to just go to him and refer the matter. His word was final and the matter would be settled.

I recollect a letter Śrīla Prabhupāda had written to Haṁsadūta stating, “You need competent Hindi speaking devotees to assist the programme. I am authorising that Lokanāth Swami can go with the buses.”

I wish that Śrīla Prabhupāda had stayed on forever to settle our on- going differences and disputes. Śrīla Prabhupāda had anticipated disputes and therefore he left us with clear instructions on how to manage differences when he was not with us. He had said, “Your love for me will be shown by how you cooperate with each other and how you cooperate to keep this institution together after I am gone.” I realise that Śrīla Prabhupāda had wonderful foresight in protecting this movement from any individual’s conflicting spiritual beliefs. Śrīla Prabhupāda left us with a unique spiritual body called ISKCON which continues stay to together through the cooperative spirit of his loving followers.

## Chapter 24. Aṅgrej Ka Mandir

*Vṛndāvana, 1976*

The evenings in April in Vṛndāvana were warm and Śrīla Prabhupāda would give *darśana* under the *tamāla* tree in the courtyard of the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple. The chequered marble floor provided cool seating for the gathered audience. Verandas with beautiful wall paintings depicting the pastimes of the lives of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa and Gaura-Nitāi adorned the spacious courtyard. The devotees and visitors would sit around Śrīla Prabhupāda. Sometimes Śrīla Prabhupāda would speak and at other times he would ask the devotees to speak. A tranquil atmosphere prevailed during these friendly interactions with Śrīla Prabhupāda.

On one such evening, Śrīla Prabhupāda was sitting on a chair underneath this tree. Local visitors were coming and going. Some of them were sitting, while others just stood around listening. Śrīla Prabhupāda signaled me to stand up and address the audience. I assumed that Śrīla Prabhupāda selected me because I am an Indian and spoke in Hindi, a familiar language. I knew that he was not happy with the misconception that the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple was called ‘*Aṅgrej ka mandir’* \[English temple\] by the locals. I decided to take the opportunity to clarify this misunderstanding.

*I had heard Śrīla Prabhupāda previously speak about this and I tried to boldly present his concern in my own words. I felt strongly about this misconception and stood up to address them with confidence.*

*“Hare Krishna. I welcome all of you here. It’s good that you have come to take darśana of ISKCON’s Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma mandir and not Aṅgrej ka mandir.”*

*I immediately asked the visitors, “You think every white person is an Englishman? But everyone from the West is not ‘Aṅgreji’\[an Englishman\]. Just look around here. They are all of different nationalities. He is German. And there, that prabhu is American and that one is Australian.”*

*I asked, “Why then do you call this temple Aṅgrej ka mandir? We have not imported the Deities from England. There is no Aṅgreji deity in this temple.” At this point in my address, I raised my voice slightly to drive home my stance, “Don’t you see that Śrīla Prabhupāda has installed Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma on the altar? This is a Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple, not an Aṅgreji temple! Yes, the financial assistance for our Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple did come from the West. This is their service to Kṛṣṇa. That does not make it an Aṅgreji mandir.”*

*I continued, “All these devotees, are Kṛṣṇa bhaktas and not outsiders anymore. Their bodies might be foreign, but they are sincere devotees of Kṛṣṇa. Therefore they have come to* *Vṛndāvana, Kṛṣṇa’s land. In spirit they are all Vrajabasis and not Aṅgreji outsiders anymore.”*

*They were listening intently so I added: “Right now, where we are assembled is Raman Reti. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma played and performed Their cowherd pastimes here.” I looked at Śrīla Prabhupāda and continued, “Our founder Ācārya, Śrīla Prabhupāda, sitting right here, knew this. That is why Śrīla Prabhupāda kindly installed Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma as the main Deities on the altar.*

*I finally concluded: “ISKCON temples are for everyone. There are no restrictions and there is no distinction between different nationalities. Everyone is seen only as a devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa. So I humbly appeal to all of you “Please don’t call this temple Aṅgrej ka mandir anymore. Tell everybody that this is ISKCON’s Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma mandir.” Are you in agreement with this? Will you do that? My Guru Mahārāja, Śrīla Prabhupāda would be really pleased with you if you did.”*

*Judging from the vigorous nodding of their heads, I was convinced that this erroneous judgment was rectified and that from this point onwards they would refer to our temple as the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma mandir and also tell others to do the same.*

*It was as if the smiling expression on Śrīla Prabhupāda’s face was saying to me, “I am really happy with your compelling presentation.”*

Śrīla Prabhupāda had encountered a lot of resistance from the local Indians because of his American and European disciples. Following his spiritual master’s instruction, Śrīla Prabhupāda went to the West to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness. The European and American boys and girls joined the movement and wholeheartedly accepted this Krishna conscious culture and lifestyle. They were duly initiated into the *Mādhva-Gauḍīya-sampradāya.*

The western devotees made up 90% of the full-time devotee population and with their financial help, Srila Prabhupada had built the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple. They were also the people who were now managing this temple. Because of this financial assistance, the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple was referred to as *Aṅgrej* ka mandir. Not too far from ISKCON’s Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple, is the Birla mandir. That temple and all the other Birla mandirs in India were financed by the Birla family. There is the trend in India whereby in some cases temples are named after people who finance them, hence the misconception about the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple.

Local Indians only made up about 5% to 10% of ISKCON’s total devotee population. It is also for this reason that many people recognise the Krsna Balrama temple as a Westerners temple. Another reason for this mistaken belief was that England had ruled India for 150 years and Indians generally view every white bodied person as an Englishman. That is how they saw the foreigners in the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma mandir. A similar perception was prevailing in Māyāpur. The Bengali locals also addressed the ISKCON temple there as ‘*Sāhiber Maṭh*’. In Bengali *‘Sāhib’* means ‘sir’. The locals say it is the temple (math) that ‘belongs to our previous bosses’ (a reference to the English rulers again.)

I recall Śrīla Prabhupāda addressing this issue when the opening of the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple took place. He was sitting on his *vyāsāsana* addressing a packed temple when he gave the following speech, “…This movement is undoubtedly increasing in the foreign countries, and why not in our country? They have taken it very seriously. They are not CIA, not American nor European. They are Vaiṣṇavas. They are servants of Kṛṣṇa. Don’t misunderstand them. And try to cooperate and help this movement for the benefit of the whole human society.”

## Chapter 25. Devotional Service Is Guaranteed to the Preacher of Kṛṣṇa Consciousness

*Delhi, 1976*

Mr. Krishna Modi, an influential member of India’s Parliament had been unsuccessful in trying to set up a meeting with Śrīla Prabhupāda as Śrīla Prabhupāda was travelling all the time. He had finally succeeded in arranging a meeting with some politicians and Śrīla Prabhupāda on 31 August. Several politicians wanted to listen to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s preaching and Mr. Modi was encouraging them to visit the ISKCON temples and to meet with Śrīla Prabhupāda. There had recently been some unfavourable articles in the press about ISKCON, and Mr. Modi wanted the MPs to visit and see and hear for themselves whether there was any truth in the matter.

*An opportunity to address this concern arose when Śrīla Prabhupāda met with Krishna Modi, a favourable member of India’s parliament, and a group of devotees including Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Dāsa and Hari Śauri Dāsa. I also had the privilege to be present. Mr Modi had proposed that a group of parliament members visit ISKCON centres worldwide to see for themselves that ISKCON is bona fide.*

*Prabhupāda said, “No, our mission is that this Kṛṣṇa cult should be preached all over the world. That is the... ya idaṁ paramaṁ guhyaṁ, mad-bhakteṣv abhidhāsyati. Na ca tasmān manuṣyeṣu kaścin me priya-kṛttamaḥ.”*

*In the course of the discussion, Śrīla Prabhupāda asked me to read a verse from the Bhagavad-gītā. Śrīla Prabhupāda would often ask his devotees to read from his books to substantiate the points he was making to the guests. In this case he was asking me to fulfil that role.*

*I asked, “Which verse would you like me to read, Śrīla Prabhupāda?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda instructed, “Read chapter 18 verse 68.”*

*I read the Sanskrit and translation:*

*ya idaṁ paramaṁ guhyaṁ*  
*mad-bhakteṣv abhidhāsyati*  
*bhaktiṁ mayi parāṁ kṛtvā*  
*mām evaiṣyaty asaṁśayaḥ*  
*\[Bg.* [*18.68*](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/18.68)*. \]*

**“For one who explains the supreme secret to the devotees, devotional service is guaranteed, and at the end he will come back to Me.”**

*I asked Śrīla Prabhupāda if I should read the purport and Śrīla Prabhupāda responded positively.*

*“Purport by Śrīla Prabhupāda,” I said and then read, “Generally it is advised that Bhagavad-gītā be discussed amongst the devotees only, and for those who are not devotees will neither understand Kṛṣṇa nor Bhagavad-gītā. Those who do not accept Kṛṣṇa as He is and Bhagavad- gītā As It Is should not try to explain Bhagavad-gītā whimsically and become offenders. Bhagavad-gītā should be explained to persons who are ready to accept Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Personality of Godhead. It is a subject matter for devotees only and not for philosophical speculators. Anyone, however, who tries sincerely to present Bhagavad-gītā As It Is will advance in devotional activities and reach the pure devotional state of life. As a result of such pure devotion, he is sure to go back home, back to Godhead.”*

*Many of India’s politicians and freedom fighters have written commentaries on the Bhagavad-gītā ‘whimsically,’ like Gandhi, Dr Radhakrishnan, and Lokamanya Tilak. They were devotees of their motherland, but not necessarily devotees of Supreme Father, Śrī Kṛṣṇa. Śrīla Prabhupāda was bringing it to the notice of these Members of Parliament that, in India, this is about political leaders interfering in most confidential subject matters which are reserved for devotees only, and not for whimsical philosophical speculators.*

I was listening very intently to Śrīla Prabhupāda and acknowledged how he placed emphasis on *śāstra.* Śrīla Prabhupāda displayed extreme ingenuity and intelligence in his presentation. It was a valuable learning experience, especially on how to cultivate a VVIP (very very important person).

I learnt that with preaching, there should be total dependence on scripture and Śrīla Prabhupāda*’s* books. There is no need to engage in flattery to persuade people, since ISKCON’s philosophy is very sound. I was highly pleased with Śrīla Prabhupāda’s response. The purpose of writing the commentaries by Mahatma Gandhi and others were based on personal political motives for fighting over the motherland, whereas *Bhagavad-gītā As It Is* was inspired purely by the desire to share the teachings of Kṛṣṇa for the benefit of the entire world.

“Those who are not actually in the line of *ācāryas***,** or who personally have no knowledge of how to act in the role of *ācārya*, unnecessarily criticize the activities of the ISKCON movement in countries outside of India,” Śrīla Prabhupāda said. “The fact is that such critics cannot do anything personally to spread Kṛṣṇa consciousness.”

## Chapter 26. Members of Nitāi-Gaura World Saṅkīrtana Party Receive Initiation

*Delhi, 1976*

The Nitāi-Gaura World Travelling Saṅkīrtana Party had been on the road for seven months and had moved through several north Indian states.

On 1 September, on the auspicious day of Rādhāṣṭamī at Delhi temple, some of the young men who had been travelling with us received initiation. Seven or eight recruits had joined us, and some who seemed sincere were recommend for initiation. Śrīla Prabhupāda kindly accepted our recommendation and the date was set.

Śrīla Prabhupāda was in Hyderabad before coming to Delhi and would remain there up until 2 September. The scene was set for the initiation ceremony. Śrīla Prabhupāda sat on his *vyāsāsana* in the tiny temple room of Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Pārthasārathi Mandir, ISKCON’s Delhi temple. The *vyāsāsana* was large and very nicely constructed of hardwood and sported a carved back, shaped like a lotus flower. Śrīla Prabhupāda was flanked by Pradyumna, the temple president and myself. We were asked to assist with the initiation ceremony.

*While we were assisting with the initiation ceremony Śrīla Prabhupāda asked a few questions, making certain the initiates had everything they needed. Śrīla Prabhupāda turned to Pradyumna and asked, “They have got neckbeads?”*

*Pradyumna redirected the question to the initiates present and they responded in the affirmative with a nod.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then called them forward one at a time to recite their vows and receive their new spiritual names. The first to be called was Adhira, he sat before Śrīla Prabhupāda who asked him in Hindi, “So kyā niyam hai?” \[Recite the four regulative principles?\]*

*Adhira responded by listing the four vows in Hindi. Śrīla Prabhupāda then gave him his new name, Abhayāśraya. There were four more devotees initiated after him in the same manner. Bhakta Atmananda’s new name was Ātmatattva Dāsa; Bhakta Lakṣmaṇa’s name became Lakṣmīśvara Dāsa; Bhakta Robin was given the name Atīndra Dāsa and Bhakta Roger would now be called Revatī Kumāra Dāsa. Satyananda received initiation and was named Siddhārtha Dāsa. As Śrīla Prabhupāda announced each of the new initiates names, the crowd of devotees witnessing the initiation ceremony, resounded with a loud, “Jaya!”*

*During the ceremony Śrīla Prabhupāda noticed that some of the initiates had been stepping over or touching the yajña paraphernalia with their feet, which was not the proper etiquette. So he turned to Pradyumna and myself and said, “While coming they should be careful not to let their feet touch anything. These people, they do not know... ”*

*It was one of those days when things went wrong, one after another. Śrīla Prabhupāda had to repeat himself to the devotees coming forward for their beads, not to touch the yajña paraphernalia with their feet, which was an offense. I then announced to all the initiates, in Hindi, “Do not touch any of these things with your feet.”*

*“Walk very carefully. You should come very carefully to get beads,” Śrīla Prabhupāda added.*

*Seeing that they were still not adhering to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s instructions, I again said, “Excuse me, Prabhus. I’ll repeat one more time. Do not touch any of these things. Be careful.”*

*“Not touch nor cross,” Śrīla Prabhupāda added again.*

*“Not even go over it. It is offensive. Please make a way,” I pleaded.*

*There were a few members of the Delhi congregation who were getting initiated that day along with the boys from our travelling team. As we made our way closer to Śrīla Prabhupāda, I brought something to his notice, “There was one gentleman in particular who had not shaved his head.”*

*“No shaving?” Śrīla Prabhupāda enquired from me.*

*“He’s Tejīyas’s man. I don’t know. He is just a householder. He’s in business, a congregational devotee. He has a grocery store, rice, dhal, masala selling,” I responded to Śrīla Prabhupāda.*

*“That’s all right, but give him initiation,” Śrīla Prabhupāda said to me in Hindi.*

*“Initiation, do it first?” I asked not entirely clear about what Śrīla Prabhupāda was requesting.*

*“Yes,” Śrīla Prabhupāda responded. He then continued, “Nobody said to him? You did not know. Let him shave up. Then we shall... You should. You are introducing, you did not know?” I shook my head indicating that it was not me. Śrīla Prabhupāda then asked, “Then who has introduced?”*

*“Tejīyas,” I responded.*

*“Tejīyas is not saying and you also not detect. You should be very careful,” Śrīla Prabhupāda said to me.*

*He went on, “If you remain ignorant, all ignorant, then where is progress? Everything ignorant I have to find out. So many big, big monkeys, nobody could find out. Big, big monkey, big, big belly. You also did not.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was really angry because the initiates were not adequately informed about the rules and requirements and I was held responsible for that shortfall.*

*The newly named Siddhārtha Dāsa turned to the unshaven devotee and said, “You go shave your hair or you ask, ‘Mahārāja, is it too late now, Mahārāja and I wait’.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then said, “I wait.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then waited for half an hour for the devotee to shave before continuing with the initiation ceremony. During the wait everyone was thoroughly engaged chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa, Hare Hare/ Hare Rāma, Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma, Hare Hare. After shaving, the man returned and took initiation. He was initiated as Durdhara Dāsa.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then enquired, “That’s all... Who is next?”*

*Pradyumna responded, “That is all, Śrīla Prabhupāda.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “What are the ten offenses?”*

*Pradyumna then recited the ten offenses a devotee should be aware of while chanting the holy name of the Lord.*

*In spite of these discrepancies, it was a joyous ceremony. I called out the names of the new initiates as they received their names, and the crowd responded with loud cheers, clapping and cries of “Haribol!”*

The temple room at this stage was a large room in a house. A large portion of the room was occupied by the Deities and Śrīla Prabhupāda’s *vyāsāsana*. The *kuṇḍa* for the fire sacrifice was set up between Śrīla Prabhupāda and the Deities, which resulted in a lack of space in the room. The room was so congested that many of the guests were standing outside. The limited space in the room prevented the initiates moving around properly. They constantly touched the sacred paraphernalia with their feet. Further, they were not fully familiar with the rules and regulations on matters of contamination concerning sacred material.

In letters written to devotees between 15 February 1975 and 3 June of the same year, Śrīla Prabhupāda outlines the procedure for requesting initiation: “regarding initiation, it is required that you be recommended by one of our temple presidents to me and then I shall consider it.” He emphasized that “in order to be initiated by us, one has to chant at least sixteen times around his *japa* beads daily (the Hare Kṛṣṇa *mahā-mantra*), and he has to follow strict rules such as, no meat, fish, eggs, no intoxication – including tea and cigarettes, no illicit sex, and no gambling.”

In a letter to me dated 12 June 1976, Śrīla Prabhupāda asserted that “Everyone should take very seriously this vow and make steady and sure progress in Kṛṣṇa consciousness and go back home, back to Godhead in this lifetime.”

## Chapter 27. You Can Go by Bullock Cart

*Delhi, 1976*

The temple was an apartment building in Lajpat Nagar, half of which was rented by ISKCON. Śrīla Prabhupāda would often give regular regular *darśana* in the prasadam hall on the first floor. This was convenient because Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters were adjacent to the hall, across from the kitchen. This occurred so frequently that the devotees had arranged for an *āsana* to be left in the hall to facilitate Śrīla Prabhupāda’s meeting with the devotees. It was six-inches high, covered in pure white sheets and had two bolsters.

It had been an exciting Rādhāṣṭamī, especially with the initiations ceremony of the devotees of the Nitāi-Gaura World Travelling Saṅkīrtana Party. There was insufficient accommodation at the temple and so our travelling party was residing close by. That evening, I asked the newly initiated Ātmatattva Dāsa if he would come with me to see Śrīla Prabhupāda at the temple. Upon reaching there we learnt that Śrīla Prabhupāda had just arisen from taking his afternoon rest and he was now ready to give *darśana* in the hall. As we entered the hall we saw him sitting on his *āsana* leaning against a bolster. There were already some guests with him, sitting respectfully on the floor in front of him on a flatwoven rug.

*Ātmatattva Dāsa and I offered our obeisances to Śrīla Prabhupāda. As I stood up after doing so Śrīla Prabhupāda said to me, “Lokanāth Mahārāja, you haven’t gone for preaching?”*

*I explained, “The carnet \[permits\] on the Mercedes buses that we were using for preaching, has expired. After only six months in India we have to ship them back to Germany.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda asked, “Then what will happen to your travelling and preaching?”*

*I said, “Haṁsadūta has gone to Germany to renew the permits and return the buses to India to facilitate our preaching efforts for the future. Once he brings back more buses, we will continue the Nitāi-Gaura Travelling Saṅkīrtana Party.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda instructed, “The roads are not good and will cause continual damage to the buses. Buses break down, repairs are costly and no one will be able to fix them properly in India anyway.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda’s words that followed next were simple but to the point, “…You don’t need to travel on the bus in India. You can go by bullock cart.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda eagerly informed us, “This bullock cart travelling has been on my mind for a while now. In April 1976 I had received a letter from a person named Premānanda Punjabi who was eager to organise this padayātrā.”*

*I asked, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, how did you advise him?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda replied, “I wrote back advising him that young Indian men should travel and tour from village to village. Also \[I\] told him that my foreign disciples would be impeded by the language barrier, but nevertheless I had encouraged him.”*

*I then said, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, did Premānanda manage to start this bullock cart preaching?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda continued, “I told him that if he was keen on the idea he should immediately take up this programme and get ahold of at least half a dozen young men like himself with another half dozen foreign disciples to tour village to village and town to town. It would be very, very effective. So be it!”*

The village-to-village preaching in India was very close to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s heart and therefore he had been very keen to engage us in the bullock cart travelling *saṅkīrtana* programme. Śrīla Prabhupāda’s words had struck me deeply. His mood was that it was not necessary to be dependent on the buses to preach in India. I had come from a village where cars were not the mode of transport and was therefore familiar with bullock carts. The alternative mode of transport also meant we could easily get into villages near and far without any problems.

Śrīla Prabhupāda had often pointed out the misuse of human advancement. People, he would explain, are so satisfied having a motor car that they do not look for alternatives without realising that a motor car made up of a thousand pieces with all its advancements is more troublesome than the simple bullock cart.

## Chapter 28. End of Haribol! Haribol!

*Delhi, 1976*

The Rādhāṣṭamī festival of 1976 at the Delhi temple was unforgettable. It was a beautiful evening, and despite it being the monsoon season, the weather remained clear and calm allowing us to celebrate outdoors. The devotees had set up a stage outside the deity hall and were holding a *paṇḍāl* programme. From his *vyāsāsana*, Śrīla Prabhupāda looked on as we rendered kīrtana on the lawn at the side of the temple. As I led the *kīrtana* the members ofthe Nitāi-Gaura World Travelling Saṅkīrtana Party danced enthusiastically around me.

*After singing the standard prayers and the mahā-mantra, we began chanting “gaura nityānanda bol, haribol, haribol; gaura śrī-advaita bol, haribol, haribol; gaura śrī-gadādhara bol, haribol haribol.”*

*The air was surcharged with spiritual energy and our kīrtana became more exuberant. As we chanted we placed great emphasis on “Haribol”. As the kīrtana reached its climax, we gathered around in a group singing “nitāi gaura haribol, haribol, haribol, haribol,” and we went on nonstop. We were completely carried away.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was sitting on his vyāsāsana on the makeshift stage when he gestured to me to stop. He motioned with his hand back and forth and said, “Don’t do that anymore!”*

Chanting “Haribol” in this way had become popular in ISKCON. I think most of the devotees had learnt it during Māyāpur festival periods from the Gauḍīya Maṭha *kīrtana* parties in Māyāpur. We were young and easily influenced, and would therefore pick up such things. There were other habits though, which we had learned from the Gauḍīya Maṭha, which Śrīla Prabhupāda liked. On one occasion, when Brahmānanda and Śrutakīrti began dancing with raised arms, we could see that Śrīla Prabhupāda had appreciated the expression. We had never danced like that previously. Now, with arms raised, we were dancing more enthusiastically, and more pleasingly, it seemed, to Śrīla Prabhupāda. However, being easily influenced, we had not only picked up such traditional artistic dance movements, but also something of their *kīrtana* style, more specifically their endless chanting of, “Haribol! Haribol!”

Some of the devotees had become influenced so much so that the entire “Haribol Movement” was formed by one of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s disciples, Śrīdhara Svarūpa Dāsa. The devotees of this so called “movement” simply stood and chanted “Haribol,” over and over again and that was their *kīrtana.*

Actually, Śrīla Prabhupāda was slightly upset by our *kīrtana*. Śrīla Prabhupāda reaffirmed the importance of chanting the mahā- mantra during *kīrtana.*

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s stance was that *“nitāi gaura haribol”* is all right, because they are bona fide names of the Lord, but the real point is that we should strictly follow only what the *ācāryas* have given. That is the process.

Yet, on 17 November 1975, I had asked Śrīla Prabhupāda’s permission to chant in this very way.

*We were on a morning walk with Śrīla Prabhupāda on Juhu beach when I had asked him, “We chant, gaura nityānanda bol, haribol, haribol… And before I could even conclude the question Śrīla Prabhupāda responded, “That is all right.”*

*“And what about ‘jaya jagannātha, jaya jagannātha?’ ”*

*“Yes. That’s all right too,” Śrīla Prabhupāda responded assuringly. He went on to say, “Anukīrtanam, to chant the Lord’s name always. So these are Lord’s names. Jagannātha is also the Lord’s name. Nityānanda is also the Lord’s name. Harer nāma harer nāma eva kevalam. So harer nāma can be chanted. Śrīla Prabhupāda explained that chanting means glorifying and asking the Lord to ‘please engage me’.”*

*I wanted to be certain about what Śrīla Prabhupāda had approved, so I enquired from him further, “But chanting in that particular fashion, saying ‘jaya jagannātha, jaya jagannātha’?”*

*“Yes, Chanting means glorifying, ‘jaya jagannātha’. Please engage me,” Śrīla Prabhupāda responded.*

Śrīla Prabhupāda had motioned for me to stop when I had led the chanting of “Haribol! Haribol” from the stage and this instruction struck me deeply. I took it very seriously, and to this day I carefully avoid such singing. Several other ISKCON *kīrtana* leaders also rectified themselves as a result of these instructions.

## Chapter 29. So, What’s the Answer?

*Delhi, 1976*

Across the street from the ISKCON Temple in Lajpat Nagar, Delhi, was a small park. It was quite a narrow strip of land populated with various fauna and flora. The park was a peaceful, pleasant spot so naturally most of the devotees were attracted to it. One could often find them taking strolls to the park to do some *japa* or just to relax.

On a September day my godbrother, Ravi Dāsa and I were sitting in the park doing our *japa*. For some reason the two of us had not joined Śrīla Prabhupāda’s morning walk. Instead we were doing our own *japa* walk and *japa* talk. Gradually however, our *japa* turned into conversation about the spiritual master. Suddenly Śrīla Prabhupāda surprised us by appearing on the scene. I thought that Śrīla Prabhupāda’s party would just pass us by. Instead Śrīla Prabhupāda walked directly to where we were sitting with everyone from the morning walk party following close behind. The entire party stopped in front of us. When Śrīla Prabhupāda had reached us we were in the middle of the topic of spiritual master vs the demigods.

*Ravi Dāsa had turned to me, his sannyāsī godbrother, and enquired, “How are we to understand the position of the spiritual master in relation to the demigods?”*

*As I took a moment to reply to him I noticed Śrīla Prabhupāda watching us and it was clear from his stance and facial expression that he wanted me to answer Ravi’s question. It appeared as though Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted to see how I would perform. So I dared to answer Ravi’s question in his presence.*

*“One must understand the position of the Vaiṣṇava. The Vaiṣṇava’s position is far, far greater than that of the demigods,” I responded. “The demigods worship the Vaiṣṇavas and shower flowers upon them. So they are exalted personalities, especially the Vaiṣṇava spiritual master. There is no comparison of the spiritual master with anyone else in the world.”*

*I went on to quote what Śrīla Prabhupāda had said in so many of his purports, “The spiritual master is the external manifestation of the Supersoul. Paramātmā is Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, and He becomes manifest before you in the form of a guru or spiritual master. So you cannot compare a spiritual master or guru with any other.”*

*Before I could continue any further Śrīla Prabhupāda interrupted me, “No, his question is why the demigods should not be equal to the spiritual master. Just like Lord Brahmā, he is the original spiritual master. That is his question. Is it not?”*

*Ravi agreed that this was his question. I then misinterpreted the question again and instead answered that the demigods are not equal to the spiritual master.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was patient and clarified again for me, “Why? That is his question.” So again I attempted to reply to Ravi’s enquiry, “As is explained in scriptures the spiritual master is Paramātmā externally manifested, but that is not the case for other demigods.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda rephrased the question for the third time, “Why is the spiritual master given such a more exalted position than the demigods? He then added, “Demigods, they are also Vaiṣṇava, mostly.”*

*“They are not pure devotees,” was my simple response.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda held me to the point, saying, “They are also devotees.”*

*“But their devotion is mixed devotion,” I mumbled.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was not at all satisfied with that response and so he took charge of the situation. He responded thus, “No. Just like Lord Brahmā. We belong to the Brahma-sampradāya. Brahmā is the original spiritual master after Kṛṣṇa, Brahmā, then Nārada, then Vyāsadeva-like that. So all of them are gurus, ācāryas. So why the spiritual master is honoured as good as the Supreme Lord? Sākṣād-dharitvena samasta-śāstraiḥ. Why? That should be his question. Why spiritual master is given the supreme post superior to all others? Sākṣād-dharitvena-as good as God. Why? Is it not? It is clear, his question. First of all, is this his question?”*

*Ravi Dāsa, who was clearly getting nervous because we were in the spotlight, answered Śrīla Prabhupāda, “Yes, this is the question.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda turned to me for the fourth time and asked, “So what is the answer?” Finally getting it right but, not wanting to elaborate fully I cautiously replied, “The spiritual master is very much concerned about our personal welfare.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then added, “He gives direct knowledge of Kṛṣṇa; therefore he is as good as Kṛṣṇa. Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda did not just leave it at that though, he prompted me to speak further saying, “Hmm?” The tone of his voice urged me on and I continued, “The spiritual master is the present ācārya in the paramparā”. I anxiously wanted to change the topic and so went on further to say, “Five thousand years back, until now, there have only been thirty ācāryas listed in the introduction to Bhagavad-gītā As It Is…”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda, ever-attentive, meticulous and as sharp as a pin, saw immediately where I was going with my response and interrupted me by saying, “Chain is broken when there are false spiritual masters. Otherwise it is not broken. Chain is broken if a so-called spiritual master speaks something manufactured. Then the chain is broken. Otherwise chain is not broken.”*

Our discussion ended at this point and Śrīla Prabhupāda resumed his morning walk. Ravi and I also joined the party. I pondered upon my responses as we walked through the park and was somewhat disappointed with myself. I was not confident at all of what I was saying. I just fumbled my way through somehow. The memory of that day has never left me.

Śrīla Prabhupāda was clear in his explanation that the spiritual master is revered because he gives Kṛṣṇa. The *Śrī Śrī Gurvaṣṭaka* written by Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura is a glorification of the spiritual master and the mercy he gives to the disciple. The verses of this poem beautifully describe the vital role of the spiritual master in our lives. The spiritual master is receiving benediction from the ocean of mercy.

It is described that just as a cloud pours water on a forest fire to extinguish it, and so the spiritual master delivers the materially afflicted world by extinguishing the blazing fire of material existence. The seventh verse elaborates the superiority of the spiritual master. It states that the spiritual master should be honoured as much as the Supreme Lord Himself because he is the confidential servitor of the Lord. One should therefore offer their most respectful obeisances to the spiritual master who, as this verse concludes, is the bona fide representative of Kṛṣṇa. Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī so aptly reveals in this verse and in the entire poem, the elevated position of the guru or spiritual master. It is a position far greater than even the most exalted demigods because the spiritual master gives the greatest benediction of all – love of God.

## Chapter 30. It Is a Very Noble Attempt

*Vṛndāvana, 1976*

Śrīla Prabhupāda had come to Vṛndāvana from Delhi nine days ago and was happy that we had taken his instruction seriously by organizing the bullock cart programme.

The bullock cart *saṅkīrtana* party consisted of Gaurasundara, Ananta-padmanābha, Abhayāśraya, Siddhārtha, Kṛṣṇanāma, Ātmatattva, a few others and me from India. The Westerners included Ravi from England and Oṁkāra and Caturātmā who were both from South America. The head *pūjāri* Turīya Prabhu, of Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple had given us his personal set of Gaura-Nitāi Deities for our travelling party.

We had gathered in Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters on 10 September on the occasion of the simple inaugural function of our bullock cart *Saṅkīrtana* party. Śrīla Prabhupāda had a guest, Mr Gupta. Dhanañjaya Dāsa, Caraṇāravindam Dāsa and Sanand Prabhu were also present.

Śrīla Prabhupāda was sitting on the *āsana* in his quarters and began to speak at length with us. He encouraged us and gave us instructions. He advised us that the bullock cart should have rubber tyres. He also explained where we should park when we entered the villages, and how to collect food for distribution. He then started addressing the major phenomenon impacting village communities—that of modernisation.

**Taking Stock of Contemporary India**

*Śrīla Prabhupāda highlighted Gandhi’s failure in the villages. Gandhi had a programme to construct toilets in the villages to promote a healthy and clean lifestyle, but there were far too many attractions in the city for the villagers.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda mentioned that the villagers could develop some real attachment to staying in the village if they could be attracted to chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra and getting delicious prasāda.*

*I immediately recognized a possible solution. “If they add the holy name to their lives, they will...”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed, “Yes, if they could understand that. That will be helpful if you chant. Therefore Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu’s statement will never be false – ceto-darpaṇa-mārjanam.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda emphasized that if the villagers realized that their primary necessities of life were minimal they would be content in the village. However, they had left to the city to work for minimal wages. He continued, “Now I have seen in Delhi, the government is advertising, ‘Go back to the village’. Simply inviting, ‘Go to the village’. They will go?”*

*I agreed, “This is simply propaganda.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda disagreed. The influx into the city had resulted in high levels of poverty and more importantly, moral degradation of the youth.*

It is a fact that people are always looking for “greener pastures”. This is the key reason for the major exodus of people from the villages to the cities. In comparison to the glitter of the cities, the simple life in the villages did not look very attractive. There are many catastrophic results because of this migration. Villages are getting lost as they are slowly disappearing off the face of the Earth. The dreams with which these villagers go to the city are not realised, but still they persevere. In order to survive they are willing to do anything for any amount of money. Still they barely earn enough to survive.

Urbanisation has facilitated the growth of slums. The people inhabiting these slums live in appalling conditions because the government is not equipped to deal with so many people in an area that is grossly overpopulated. The government further fails them by half-heartedly trying to entice them back to the villages by introducing facets of urban life there, instead of encouraging them to go back to the village to re-establish their relationship with the land. There has to be an attractive alternative that keeps them in the villages, a higher taste – of chanting and *kṛṣṇa-prasāda*.

**Bullock Cart Saṅkīrtana Party Will Introduce Higher Taste**

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “But if we can introduce this hari-saṅkīrtana, and if they have little taste, that is success. So if I can get easily in the village, why shall I go three hundred miles away? So that simple life will be possible if one is bhakta. Only by devotion. Not by artificial means, by manufacturing toilet. Only bhakti. If they get little attachment for Kṛṣṇa the questions will be solved automatically. And they will be happy.”*

*I responded, “I come from village, and I’m happy now as I have taken to holy name and prasāda. So more, other villagers... Half of these boys are from villages.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed and I reinforced, “They’re chanting and they’re so happy and they’re going back to village.” Śrīla Prabhupāda was pleased.*

*Caraṇāravindam continued the discussion that the lack of performing sacrifices made it too difficult to live off the land. Śrīla Prabhupāda inquired about the type of sacrifice. Caraṇāravindam explained that the essential sacrifice was to chant the holy name.*

*I tried to clarify, “He says there is no yajña, sacrifice, people are not performing. That is why they are into the trouble.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda enlightened us by explaining that there were no ingredients for performing a fire sacrifice in the age of Kali. Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “Simply chant, and engage others in chanting. That is yajña. First class yajña, recommended.”*

*Caraṇāravindam enquired, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, how would they keep the villagers engaged when they were passing through.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “Lokanāth will guide you. You do not know. He is from village, he knows. Village or no village, wherever you chant, people will come. That is a fact. Either you chant in Calcutta Maidan or in the village they will be... India is still alive in that sense. We have seen it. We have experience. Wherever we have chanted, people have come.”*

*I confirmed, “Sanand, when you went to some place in Gujarat, was it not reported that thousands of people had come. It was a small village...”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed that the villagers were attracted to the kīrtana. They did not understand English, but they identified with the mahā-mantra.*

*I stated, “Nobody outside ISKCON does this kīrtana in spite of the presence of some religious organisations.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda clarified, “What do they know? What they’ll do?”*

*I reminded Śrīla Prabhupāda, “You have said many times you could give complete address of Kṛṣṇa.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “Yes, we give address.”*

*I added, “And also we know the telephone number.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed, “Yes.”*

*I added, “Hare Kṛṣṇa, Hare Kṛṣṇa”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda asked a direct question, “What is God?” Dhanañjaya suggested that God is great.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda stated, “That’s all... But what is that great? What is God, you do not know. We know. Kṛṣṇa says, ‘mattaḥ parataraṁ nānyat kiñcid asti dhanañjaya.’ ‘There is no more greater principle than Me’. That is great. How you are great? Aham ādir hi devānām. ‘I am the origin of all the demigods’. They have simply heard God is great, but how He is great, that they do not know. If he follows simply Caitanya Mahāprabhu’s instruction, yāre dekha, tāre kaha ‘kṛṣṇa’- upadeśa then he’s successful. No ostādi. That is not... “*

*I asked, “Not what?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda repeated, “Ostādi, ostādi.”*

*I added, “Flowery language.”*

*“So anyway, make your effort sincerely. The basic principle is this. Induce them to chant and take prasāda. You’ll be successful. Not very much ostādi.”*

*I declared, “I promise to keep you updated on all our activities which take place in the villages.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was pleased, “Yes. Go from village to village. It is a very noble attempt. And if you sincerely preach, Kṛṣṇa will... ‘buddhi- yogaṁ dadāmi taṁ yena mām upayānti te’. There is no doubt.”*

I grew up in a village so I had firsthand experience of life in a village. Srila Prabhupada’s intention to introduce the holy name into the villages would also prevent the village drain into the cities. He was confident that when the holy name was chanted in these villages, people would come to listen. This would be the attraction that the villages lacked. Srila Prabhupada’s instruction was simple, “chant as much as possible and distribute *prasāda.”* These were the two aspects Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted us to emphasize. In this way they will be occupied and simultaneously develop a relationship with the Lord. No education or philosophy was required. All that was needed was to follow Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s instruction of taking the holy name to every town and village.

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s plan for a travelling *saṅkīrtana* party was an absolutely ingenious one. The villagers could relate to such an activity because they could identify with a bullock cart as it was the mode of transport in the villages. The programme is without a doubt a success. Above all, Śrīla Prabhupāda’s blessing still rings in my ears today and brings a smile to my face.

## Chapter 31. Bullock Cart Preaching Mission Feedback

*Allahabad, 1977*

A lmost four months had passed since the inauguration of the bullock cart *saṅkīrtana* party. On inaugural day, 10 September 1976, I had assured Śrīla Prabhupāda that I would regularly update him on our travelling preaching programme.

Śrīla Prabhupāda was going to be in Allahabad for just three days during the Kumbha-melā festival. I had not had the opportunity to update Śrīla Prabhupāda on our travels for a while now. Previously, I had sent Śrīla Prabhupāda updates about the activities of the bullock cart preaching mission. This would be the first opportunity I would have to give such an update personally. The bullock cart *saṅkīrtana* party would stay at the festival for a month.

We had our own tent compound where we had parked our cart. During the morning walk, Śrīla Prabhupāda had come to our compound to inspect the cart. He stood in front of the cart and looked up at the sign. When his eyes were fixed on the sign, he put his head down on his left shoulder and then moved his head slowly in a circular motion as he read the sign out loudly, “Bhaktivedānta Bullock Cart Saṅkīrtana”. As he read the last word, his head was resting on his right shoulder and there was a look of great pleasure on his face.

*When I found myself alone with Śrīla Prabhupāda in the tent, it came as no surprise when he anxiously asked, “Tell me, how is the bullock cart preaching going?”*

*Not wasting any time, I reassured Śrīla Prabhupāda, “Even though the mornings are chilly, we still manage to get up early and bathe. Śrīla Prabhupāda, you had instructed us to always park near a well and this really helps when we need to draw water so early. Sometimes while most of the villagers are fast asleep during the early morning hours we go through the village chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa and sing ‘jīva jāgo’ waking them up.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda listened as if there was nothing more important to him than hearing about how his bullock cart preaching project was making inroads into villages and towns. Using this as a prompt, I continued, “We would travel ten to fifteen kilometers every day, passing through many villages distributing books and chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa. Practically everyday we had to pass through several villages without stopping overnight. Many of those villagers would actually come all the way to the village where we had stopped for the evening programme…”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda asked, “And the book distribution?”*

*I was only too happy to report back, “Upon arrival in the village we fill the streets with hari-nāma-saṅkīrtana. The devotees also go from door to door distributing books and begging alms. These alms came in the form of grain, flour, ghee, gor and many such ingredients that we could use for cooking the prasāda. They also give fodder to feed the bulls. The villagers are gladly accepting the Hindi copies of your small books and the Hindi version of The Back to Godhead magazine.”*

*“And evening programmes?” Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted to know.*

*I answered, “The evening programmes are always well attended. It starts with an ārati and singing kīrtana for Śrī Śrī Gaura-Nitāi. This is followed by a discourse in Hindi. I encourage the Western devotees to give a talk during the evening programme. They give the talk in English and I translate. The villagers were impressed to see Western devotees following Indian culture. The villagers also sit and honour prasāda afterwards. We even managed to cook halavā in our makeshift kitchen.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda enquired about overnight stay-in places and what the general response from the public was and any difficulties we faced. This keen interest in the subject enlivened me to speak. I had spent well over thirty minutes with Śrīla Prabhupāda when he finally said, “I am very, very pleased with your preaching and you should continue this programme with full enthusiasm.”*

I have a vivid image in my mind of Śrīla Prabhupāda beaming with pleasure that day in the Kumbha-melā tent. The more I spoke, the more enlivened Śrīla Prabhupāda became. His pleasure and jubilation were plainly visible on his face. Śrīla Prabhupāda liked the simplicity and dependence on Kṛṣṇa that the bullock cart *saṅkīrtana* programme displayed. We were perfectly displaying the concept of simple living and high thinking.

If there is anything, even thirty five years later, that inspires me about the Bullock Cart Padayātrā Programme; it is the memory of how pleased Śrīla Prabhupāda was to hear my report about the beginning days. The feeling I get from remembering Śrīla Prabhupāda’s pleasure that day in the tent has stayed with me over the years and continues to keep me enlivened, giving me fresh strength and inspiration to carry on and on and on.

## Chapter 32. Book Distribution—Essence of Life Membership

*Māyāpur, 1977*

Śrīla Prabhupāda arrived for the Māyāpur Gaura Pūrṇimā, festival on 7 February. Prior to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s arrival in Māyāpur, he had been in Bhuvaneśvara for about two weeks. The cornerstone for the Bhuvaneśvara temple had been laid while he was there. Thereafter he had stopped in Calcutta for a few days. Even though Śrīla Prabhupāda was not well, the travelling, preaching and translating never stopped. Śrīla Prabhupāda was especially in high spirits because more than 300 devotees had come on a jumbo jet on its maiden journey to Calcutta the day before. This event was highly publicized by Air India, so ISKCON was also in the limelight.

Due to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s failing health, regular *Bhāgavatam* classes were not taking place, however, the evening *darśana* took place in his rooms, which were situated on the second floor of the Lotus building. On the evening of 25 February the important issues of life membership and book distribution were discussed. Jayatīrtha Mahārāja, Pañcadraviḍa Mahārāja, Rāmeśvara Mahārāja, Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Mahārāja, Jayatīrtha Mahārāja, Bhāgavata Dāsa, Ghanaśyāma Dāsa, Hari Śauri Dāsa, Upendra Dāsa and I were present.

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami started the discussion, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, generally people in India are willing to become our life members so I think that it is through life membership that more money can be gained.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda responded, “Let them become life members.”*

*I informed Śrīla Prabhupāda, “The customers are not getting their books.”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa stated, “Yes. It’s their choice.”*

*I replied, “We have to cut down the number of the books that we are giving to the members now and giving only five big books.”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa sounded surprised, “Oh. But they can’t read them all.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda had a solution, “Let them read and exchange.”*

*Bhāgavata also pointed out to Śrīla Prabhupāda, “The member needs to give two thousand rupees at one time. But there is a class of gentlemen who are not in that bracket of earning that they can pay two thousand rupees at one time, but can afford to pay, let’s say, five hundred rupees down payment and, every month, a hundred rupees or fifty rupees. For one book every month, fifty rupees a month they can pay without difficulty.”*

*Rāmeśvara reiterated, “In America and in England life members are being made, but very rarely does someone give the whole fee at once.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “So, make that.”*

*Jayatīrtha endorsed this. He said, “It is a good way of getting a steady income.”*

*I had enrolled life memberships in Bombay and therefore had firsthand experience. I suggested, “With instalment members, it is not very good in India. Very rarely have they raised the instalment. It’s very hard.”*

*To stop incurring further costs Pañcadraviḍa Mahārāja recommended, “No books should be given unless they are paid for and this way there is still no loss.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda chuckled, “Yes. You are giving only five books.”*

*Hari Śauri Dāsa, who was serving Śrīla Prabhupāda, spoke, “Initial payment should be more than one, or how many books they get.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda continued, “No... So actually, even if we give them free, there is no life membership. Let them read it. But if you give free, they will not read.”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa agreed, “Oh, yes. They’ll take it cheaply.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda had another suggestion, “At least, educated Indians can be approached.”*

*Ghanaśyāma recommended, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, since the life member programme is going on here and England and places like that, maybe it’s better to start this individualized standing order programme in places where there is not so much of a life member programme, as we’ll be in some cases asking the same people.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda advised them, “Do it conscientiously, as it is suitable.”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa proposed, “This matter must be scheduled for discussion at the GBC tomorrow.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda checked on the time and said, “So, it is time now?”*

*We all offered our obeisances to Śrīla Prabhupāda and took leave.*

Śrīla Prabhupāda always said that this movement was being supported by literature and if we wanted to see a change in the world, then we had to distribute knowledge of Kṛṣṇa in this way. Coupled with this, was the fact that people are naturally attracted to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s movement, but because of business and family commitments they are unable to come frequently or to live in the temple. However, they do want to help. So for those who want to help this movement in some way, membership programmes were formulated.

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s vision of the life membership programme was always to promote and distribute his books. He wanted to get his books across without making it seem that the books were sold. The life membership facilitated this process and both the parties benefited. The members received the books and ISKCON got the funds to print more books.

I recall that while we were enrolling life members in Bombay, we had promised them that they could receive all Śrīla Prabhupāda’s future books that would be printed, which would be part of their life membership. This included the sixty volumes of *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. I felt that due to the escalating printing costs, life membership had to be limited to five big books. The Bhaktivedanta Book Trust (BBT) individual standing order programme as suggested by Ghanaśyāma Dāsa, who later became Bhakti Tīrtha Swami, was also implemented. It also became known as the Library Travelling Party. This meant that whatever was printed was distributed to libraries and universities and the outstanding book orders were also given.

## Chapter 33. They Are Unfortunate Mostly

*Māyāpur, 1977*

The evening *darśanas* were usually intimate and profound, with Śrīla Prabhupāda sometimes sharing in depth knowledge about how the organisation should be run.

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters had two inter-connected rooms–one was for working and giving *darśana* and the other for resting. The rooms were very simply furnished. His sitting room had only a raised wooden *āsana* and a low desk, and the floors were covered with white-sheeted cotton mattresses and bolsters. In the bedroom there was an almirah, a small table with a very beautiful Deity of Lord Caitanya on it.

These evening conversations were always inspiring. Just being able to sit with Śrīla Prabhupāda and hear him speak was so remarkably fulfilling, so it was no surprise that there were many of us there. I remember Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami, Brahmānanda Mahārāja, Jayatīrtha Mahārāja, Nārāyaṇa Dāsa as well as a guest being there. In these sessions Śrīla Prabhupāda would impart his innermost sentiments about spiritual life.

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “And therefore I say that education has been so wrongly given that they are restless like animals due to the modern civilization. The beginning of spiritual education they cannot accept. What they will make further progress of spiritual life? Beginning, ABCD, they are so restless, they cannot take. This is the position. Therefore I’m speaking that it is meant for the dhīra, for the rājarṣi. Not for the men who are like animals. ‘sa eva go-kharaḥ’. Their bodily conception is so strong that they cannot hear even what is said. They are so dull, ‘mandāḥ sumanda-matayo manda-bhāgyā.”*

*Brahmānanda enquired from Śrīla Prabhupāda, “Then why is it that the people from the West show more enthusiasm?”*

*Without any hesitation Śrīla Prabhupāda answered, “Now, because they have now experience of the bodily comforts, and they have seen that ‘simply by bodily comfort we cannot be happy’. They have come to this stage.”*

*Given the response from Śrīla Prabhupāda, I made a suggestion, “It’s good fortune to take birth in the West now. Is it fortunate to take birth in America or England?” My comment amused the devotees.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “Not all.” There was laughter. “Not all. The fortunate, those who are so fortunate, they are now opposing me. The Western people, they are now opposing this movement, so they are not fortunate. So everywhere there are fortunate and unfortunate. But mostly in this age they are unfortunate. Either in the Western countries or in this country, they are unfortunate mostly. They cannot understand. In India at least those who are not very educated, mass of people, they believe transmigration of the soul... because culture is based on that, ‘pūrva-janma paro janma’. They believe that ‘If I act sinfully, then I’ll have to suffer next life, and because I did not do properly, therefore I am suffering in this life.’*

*But the so-called educated people, they say, ‘Superstition’ and the leaders say that ‘India, giving more stress on the soul, not on the body, India’s position is so degraded.’ This is the leaders’ opinion. Big, big leaders, they think. Therefore the so-called leaders or learned scholars, they write notes on the Bhagavad-gītā, but they never give any idea of spiritual life. They utilize the Bhagavad-gītā for material end... Just like our big leader, Gandhi. He was supposed to be very strict follower of the Bhagavad-gītā, and he has never instructed about spiritual life.”*

*Nārāyaṇa cited a story narrated by Satsvarūpa Mahārāja and told by Śrīla Prabhupāda, “Gandhi, who was supposed to be mahātmā, but after he won victory, independence, by his activities could not stop cow slaughter throughout India because the Muslims were still eating meat.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed, “Yes. There is heat and light. And as soon as after evening the sun is off, not from the sky, but from our sight, there is darkness. This consciousness is the rays or shining of the soul. As soon as the soul is off from this body, the shining of the soul or consciousness is completely gone. And where is our Paṇḍita?”*

*I replied, “He is in the next room.” Paṇḍita Pradyumna was Śrīla Prabhupāda’s secretary and Sanskrit translator.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda instructed, “Call him.” I immediately responded and left to get him.*

Western education equips one with the ability to acquire material assets and work. This education has proven to be incomplete. It comes as no surprise that in that part of the world, material comforts are the way of life for the majority, yet they are hankering after spiritual advancement. The people in the West are now facing God because they are receptive. In due course, they will be closer to God. They have had firsthand experience that satisfying the body only, does not bring contentment. In this way, people in the Western countries may take advantage of the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement, which had its origins in India, but is now a worldwide phenomenon**.**

The inhabitants of India are closer to God, but are turning their backs on Him and in due course, they will be away from God. This is because they want to imitate the West and aspire for the opulence and comforts, which are not necessarily a part of their way of life.

Ironically those who are close to God want to emulate the lifestyle of those who are not, while many Westerners are completely ready to face a reversal in their lives. They are willingly accepting a spiritual path in their quest to seek contentment and happiness.

## Chapter 34. Bhaktivedanta Swami Mārg

*Bombay, 1977*

It was 31 March. Due to poor health, Śrīla Prabhupāda had come directly from Māyāpur to Bombay and he had missed the Vṛndāvana festival that year. A few of us had gathered in his room on the 5th floor at ISKCON Juhu guesthouse to discuss his failing health and make better arrangements for his comfort. Among those present were Dhanañjaya Dāsa who was then the Temple President of Vṛndāvana temple, Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Dāsa, Tamāla Kṛsṇa Goswami and myself. I listened as Śrīla Prabhupāda questioned the devotees about current projects. He was especially eager to hear about the progress of our temples. The discussion then moved to Vṛndāvana in general.

*Śrīla Prabhupāda turned to me and addressed me directly. He was talking about rural living and how to approach people with little education. I put forth my opinion regarding this subject.*

*“Śrīla Prabhupāda, I believe that in such poor settings prasāda distribution would be highly successful.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda nodded vaguely as his thoughts seemed to drift off the topic.*

*Knowing how he enjoyed hearing about preaching programmes, I decided to relate to him the events of the recently concluded Vṛndāvana festival. I shared the following information about the festival with him.*

*“During the Vṛndāvana festival, we did a big programme at the Kṛṣṇa Janmasthāna temple in Mathurā: kīrtanas, discourses, dramas, cinemas, prasāda distribution, book distribution. Many, many people came, and the whole temple ground was full,” I said.*

*“Over 10,000 people came,” Gopāla Kṛṣṇa confirmed.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda became enlivened, as though he were transported to Vṛndāvana. In a low voice he said, “After all, they are Mathurā men and they are after Kṛṣṇa. They have natural love for Kṛṣṇa.”*

*Dhanañjaya continued, “Now signs are up on the old Chattikara Road, announcing the new name, Bhaktivedanta Swami Mārg. On the road, there are two big marble plaques in very nice stone fittings.”*

*There was a spelling error and an omission on the marble plaques. As usual Śrīla Prabhupāda caught it right away. “You have printed Bhaktivedanta Mārg. Why?”*

*Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Mahārāja quickly responded, “Bhaktivedanta Swami Mārg.”*

*“No,” Śrīla Prabhupāda said emphatically, “In the pamphlet, describing our Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma Mandir, I see Bhaktivedanta Road. Why?”*

*Dhanañjaya knew it was his time to take some responsibility to give Śrīla Prabhupāda the answers he wanted. “Well, you see, what happened was, Śrīla Prabhupāda, they put it in Hindi, Bhaktivedānta Swami Mārg. And the marble cutters, they made ‘mārg’ instead of road.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda looked at Dhanañjaya and firmly said, “That doesn’t matter!”*

*Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Mahārāja was trying to understand what Śrīla Prabhupāda was referring to, so he ventured with, “Mārg is now the Indian style.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda’s voice was a little louder and disturbed, “I am not speaking of that. You have printed in this pamphlet, Bhaktivedanta Mārg, not Bhaktivedanta Swami Mārg.”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa looked in the pamphlet, and verified, “Yes, you left the word Swami out.” Eventually, the situation was addressed to Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Mahārāja, who humbly said, “I follow. I understand.”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Mahārāja clarified, “Anyone can be Bhaktivedanta.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda continued to address the mistake that had been made, as his disciples didn’t seem to fully comprehend what he was referring to. “No, no, apart from that. Actual name is Bhaktivedanta Swami Mārg, but you have made Bhaktivedanta. Specific person has been omitted. This is the mistake. Bhaktivedanta could be anybody or nobody… describing two paths… the bhakti path and the jñāna path. Bhakti-yogis and the jñāni-yogīs. Why Swami is omitted? That is the mistake on your part.”*

*All the devotees in the room were quiet and grateful for the chastisement, as Śrīla Prabhupāda was continually training us for what was to come.*

He also wanted his name as Founder Ācārya visible wherever International Society for Krishna Consciousness was to be printed.

I was musing over the new name of the road and could not help ponder about how Śrīla Prabhupāda created a *mārg* (road) which could include both a physical and the philosophical perspective. Physically, this spiritual boulevard provides access to thousands of national and international guests to the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma Mandir in Vṛndāvana. If one was travelling from Delhi on the highway and did not turn left onto this *mārg*, one will end up in Agra, which offers a full dose of the illusory experience. However, if we turn onto Bhaktivedānta Swami Mārg, it leads us to Vṛndāvana and brings us to the Lotus Feet of Śrīla Prabhupāda, Nitāi-Gaura, Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma and Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Śyāmasundara.

In analyzing the concept of the *mārg* philosophically, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu’s uttterance in the Madhya [17.186](https://vedarama.com/en/books/cc-madhya/17.186), bears relevance, *mahājano yena gataḥ sa panthāḥ.* “The solid truth of religious principles is hidden in the heart of an unadulterated, self-realized person. Consequently, as the *śāstras* confirm, one should accept whatever progressive path the *mahājanas* advocate.”

Śrīla Prabhupāda represents such a *mahājana* and as his devotees we are following in his footsteps. He has shown us a bona fide *bhakti* path of hearing, chanting, remembering, worshipping in the temple and engaging oneself in the service. It is stated in *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, (*[7.5.23-24](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-7/5.23)) :

*śrī-prahrāda uvāca*  
*śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ*  
*smaraṇaṁ pāda-sevanam*  
*arcanaṁ vandanaṁ dāsyaṁ*  
*sakhyam ātma-nivedanam*

I also reflected about the devotees in Mathurā. Śrīla Prabhupāda had spoken so favourably about them, yet at the previous year’s festival at the Janmasthāna in Mathurā, I was seated at the back and had noticed much of the audience were inattentive and speaking to one another during the lecture. In spite of this, Śrīla Prabhupāda just kept speaking, oblivious to the distractions. Śrīla Prabhupāda was catering to those who were listening, eloquently delivering his message to the faithful.

## Chapter 35. Reading, Reciting And Chanting—It’s the Cure

*Bombay, 14 September—2 October 1977*

On 14 September, Śrīla Prabhupāda came to Bombay from London. His health had taken a turn for the worse and instead of heading to America, Śrīla Prabhupāda returned to Bombay. His desire was possibly to witness the opening of the Bombay temple.

Upon arrival from the airport, a frail Śrīla Prabhupāda was taken straight to his quarters on the 5th floor of the guesthouse at the Juhu temple. Since Śrīla Prabhupāda’s arrival, everyone’s mood was filled with concern and anticipation. There were no more morning walks and in order to provide around the clock care, a roster was drawn up. In this way, the devotees would get a turn to serve their Spiritual Master.

**“Keep Reading”**

*Śrīla Prabhupāda would mostly be lying down with his eyes closed. His beadbag was always next to him.*

*Prasannātmā Dāsa was with the BBT and responsible for printing the Marathi version of the book ‘The Perfection of Yoga’. Given that Marathi was my mother tongue, I accompanied Prasannātmā to see Śrīla Prabhupāda in his quarters. We approached him and handed him the first copy of the book. He was enlivened and eager to receive it.*

*After looking at it, Śrīla Prabhupāda handed the book back to me. “Please read something,” he said. I read a few pages glancing at him from time to time just to see if he wanted me to stop. “More, Śrīla Prabhupāda?” I enquired.*

*“Keep reading,” he said. In this way, he encouraged me to continue to read as he listened intently for a long time.*

After reading to Śrīla Prabhupāda, I vividly remembered one of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s temple lectures in Bombay. I recall him making a point on language and understanding. He would say: “look at these boys,” pointing to the boys who were seated, “they do not understand the language, but they sit down for the lecture. The message is transcendental. Even though they don’t understand the language, the transcendental sound vibration would act regardless of the language it was presented in.”

Most often, he also expected others to listen to transcendental discourses in languages they did not understand. I could now see his appreciation as he listened to the Marathi version of *Perfection of Yoga* with his heart. It was undoubtedly another example of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s faith in the transformational, purifying potency of his books, regardless of the language.

**“*****Ār nā koriho mane āśā...*** **”**

*On this particular day, towards the end of the kīrtana I was singing for Śrīla Prabhupāda, I asked, “Śrīla Prabhupāda is there anything we haven’t done as yet which would cure you? Please tell us. Is there something which we could still do?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda responded, “Just chanting of Hare Kṛṣṇa.” He paused for a few moments and said “ār nā koriho mane āśā... ”*

This is part of the second verse from the Śrī Guru-vandana:

*śrī guru-mukha-padma-vākya, cittete koriyā aikya*  
*ār nā koriho mane āśā, śrī-guru-caraṇe rati…*

‘*Ār nā koriho mane āśā’* can be translated as “don’t desire anything else”, but needs to be read in context with the lines preceding and following it. The words that are uttered from the lotus mouth of the Guru must be embedded in our hearts so that we do not desire anything other than what he says. Śrīla Prabhupāda did not elaborate on these words to me on that day. However, these words were uttered from his lotus mouth with so much depth and emotion that they have been etched onto my heart. When I heard these words, I let that sound vibration resonate deep within me... and it continues to do so to this day.

**“Stop and Take Rest”**

Once, I had been out the entire day preaching all over Bombay, preaching. It had been a long, busy day and I was a little tired.

*That evening it was once again my turn to chant for Śrīla Prabhupāda. However, while chanting, my fatigue was getting the better of me. I kept dozing off and when I did the singing and the playing of the kartālas would stop. I would jolt myself awake and continue chanting. I was really struggling to stay awake. Śrīla Prabhupāda noticed this and very kindly said, “Lokanāth, you must be very tired. Stop and take rest.”*

*I replied, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, I do not want to leave you. Let me just try to carry on chanting.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda suggested, “It would be better if you got some rest.”*

*Reluctantly I got up, offered obeisances and left.*

**Singing for Śrīla Prabhupāda**

The following conversation took place one afternoon. Care for Śrīla Prabhupāda was still taking place around the clock.

*The Hare Krishna Land was fairly large. There was a devotee who was searching for me all over the grounds. Upon locating me, I was told, “Śrīla Prabhupāda has summoned you to his quarters.”*

*While making our way to his quarters, I enquired, “Do you know what Śrīla Prabhupāda wants?”*

*The devotee replied, “There was another devotee singing in his quarters. Śrīla Prabhupāda stopped him and wants you to sing.” When I got to the quarters, I realised that there was no one chanting.*

*In fact, Śrīla Prabhupāda was waiting for me and as soon as he saw me, he signalled me to chant.*

During all this time Śrīla Prabhupāda rarely left his quarters. Even though he was ill, Śrīla Prabhupāda was always vigilant and attentive. I had intermittently fallen asleep while chanting and Śrīla Prabhupāda had caught me red-handed the other night, yet he still sent for me to chant for him. I personally liked the chanting of that devotee who was sent away. I wonder why Śrīla Prabhupāda preferred my chanting.

## Chapter 36. With the Blessings Of Hanumān

*Vṛndāvana, 1977*

I had gone to Allahabad in search of Dr. Ghosh on the instruction of Śrīla Prabhupāda. After enquiring, I discovered that he was in Darjeeling. On locating Dr. Ghosh, I made arrangements for our travel back to Vṛndāvana to Śrīla Prabhupāda. It was a long, tiring sixteen hour journey by bus to Calcutta, then by plane to Delhi and taxi to Vṛndāvana. On arrival at Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters on 16 October, Hari Śauri Dāsa and Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami spoke to Dr. Ghosh, providing the most recent health update for Śrīla Prabhupāda. He expressed great concern and was extremely caring toward Śrīla Prabhupāda. He requested to personally care for Śrīla Prabhupāda and prepared to perform the daily enema himself. Many of us were present, Bhakti Cāru Mahārāja, Bhakti Caitanya Mahārāja from Chandigarh and myself. The mood was somber. Śrīla Prabhupāda’s leaving his physical body was imminent. Hari Śauri Dāsa brought news of the recent death of an old friend of Śrīla Prabhupāda which also affected Śrīla Prabhupāda.

*Śrīla Prabhupāda asked, almost surprised, “He also died? He was the only person remaining. Otherwise all my contemporary friends are gone.”*

*I directed the conversation away from the depressing news of the death and toward Dr. Ghosh’s much-awaited arrival. I said to Śrīla Prabhupāda, “Dr. Ghosh likes you very much and wants to serve you.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda nodded in agreement, “Yes.”*

*I continued, “And there’s practical liking. Not just words, but he wants to render services to you. That is better.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda smiled weakly and said, “And Kṛṣṇa has made him happy in all respect.”*

*I replied, “Yes. He was mentioning that to me.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda continued, “From monetary point of view, from family life, position, he is the biggest medical practitioner in Allahabad. Everyone knows. Even in the street, Dr. Ghosh they know. So take care of him very carefully.”*

*I nodded and said, “Yes, we’ll take care of him. I promise. Actually his daughter was very much reluctant that he should or should not go immediately. But he did not hear her. He just decided to go. But I had to promise his daughter that I would take care of him. She was saying that he should not also become patient along with Śrīla Prabhupāda, as he is old and like that. We’ll give him good room and nice accommodation.”*

*“Yes,” agreed Śrīla Prabhupāda. “Attendant, whatever he wants.”*

*Dr. Gosh came in and began instructing us on how to massage Śrīla Prabhupāda. We all watched intently.*

Śrīla Prabhupāda was very weak and we were all aware that we would soon have to bid him goodbye. We were trying to ensure that he was as comfortable as possible. We wanted the best medical care for him and we all wanted to serve him in any way possible.

As usual, even though so weak and gravely ill, Śrīla Prabhupāda was still greatly concerned about his guest. He ensured that we were taking good care of Dr. Ghosh who had travelled so far to serve him.

On the way to the airport, when I had gone to fetch Dr. Ghosh in Darjeeling, in the Himālayas, I had *darśana* of the Hanumān Deity in the temple in Allahabad. I prayed to Hanumānji as I passed the temple. My thoughts and prayers were that, “just as you (Hanumānji) had flown up to the Himālayas to find medicinal herbs to heal Lakṣmaṇa who had fallen unconscious in the middle of the battle in Śrī Laṅkā, so too was I on a similar mission, flying by aircraft to get the medical doctor who was much needed to treat Śrīla Prabhupāda, the commander in chief of the Saṅkīrtana army.” At that point my only duty was to serve Śrīla Prabhupāda by bringing medical assistance. I returned with Dr. Ghosh so my mission was completed by the blessings of Hanumān.

## Chapter 37. We Showed Your Gītā to Śrīla Vyāsadeva

*Vṛndāvana, 1977*

The Nārada Muni Travelling Saṅkīrtana Party was started because Gopāla Kṛṣṇa Mahārāja who was both GBC and BBT representative in Bombay wanted me to start a BBT travelling Saṅkīrtana party which would distribute books. After finding Dr. Gosh and bringing him to Vṛndāvana, the members of this Nārada Muni Travelling Saṅkīrtana Party had travelled to the North toward Delhi and Badrikāśrama.

We had been gone for seven weeks and had not received any news from Vṛndāvana with regard to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s health. It was about midmorning on 10 November, when the Nārada Muni Travelling Saṅkīrtana Party arrived back in Vṛndāvana. We immediately went to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters to get *darśana.* At this time Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami and Bhavānanda Mahārāja were intimately serving Śrīla Prabhupāda. Svarūpa Dāmodara Mahārāja, Girirāja Mahārāja, Bhakti Cāru Mahārāja, Haṁsadūta Mahārāja, Upendra and the *kavirāja* were also present.

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was bedridden. He was very thin and weak. Śrīla Prabhupāda was just lying on his bed which had been moved down from his original bedroom.*

*We offered obeisances and sat around Śrīla Prabhupāda’s bed. I reported on behalf of the Saṅkīrtana party’s journey through Uttar Pradesh, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, we distributed many books.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was visibly moved to hear this and asked, “Which books were selling the most? How many books did you sell?”*

*Having given a detailed and interesting book distribution report, I continued, “We had also gone to Badrikāśrama”. I humorously explained to Śrīla Prabhupāda, “There we went to Śrīla Vyāsadeva’s cave to show him your translation of the Bhagavad-gītā As It Is. I had also visited Bhīma Kā Pul (Bhīma Bridge). Bhīma left his body from there. Śrīla Prabhupāda, everyone including the pūjārīs in Badrinārāyaṇa area are leaving because of the intense cold. Is that proper?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “What can be done?”*

*Having received darśana and reported back to Śrīla Prabhupāda, we decide to leave and offered our obeisances. I was the last one to leave and turned around to look at Śrīla Prabhupāda one more time. Śrīla Prabhupāda signalled me with his hand to come back. The other members of the saṅkīrtana party left and I went back to Śrīla Prabhupāda. I had to sit close to Śrīla Prabhupāda because his voice was soft and barely audible.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda spoke to me in a grave voice, “I want to see you later. What time will suit you?”*

*I replied, “Śrīla Prabhupāda. You can tell me what time suits you and I will come see you.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda asked, “Is four o’clock fine?”*

*I said, “Four o’clock is all right.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda asked, “Four?”*

*I confirmed, “Four o’clock.”*

*The time was set for four o’clock in the afternoon.*

*At 4 o’clock I made my way back to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters. Upon reaching his quarters, I was stopped by Śrīla Prabhupāda’s secretary and redirected to the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma Guesthouse Reception area. When I got there, the senior GBC members and those who were serving Śrīla Prabhupāda during this time had already assembled.*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa said, “Śrīla Prabhupāda has expressed a desire to go on bullock cart travelling.”*

*I realized that this discussion had already taken place during the day and asked, “Is that why Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted to see me at 4 o’clock?”*

*They replied in the affirmative and I then asked, “What is required of me?”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa replied on behalf of the group, “We have decided that since you have knowledge about bullock cart travelling you are the ideal person to make all the arrangements. Pañcadraviḍa Mahārāja and Trivikrama Mahārāja will also go with you.”*

*Having been given this task, the three of us immediately left to make the necessary arrangements. We firstly went looking in Vṛndāvana. Not finding a suitable bullock cart here, we went to Mathurā and located a farmer in Mathurā who agreed to let us hire his cart.*

We had been away from Vṛndāvana, but Śrīla Prabhupāda’s health was constantly on my mind. I knew that this travelling preaching mission was very close to Śrīla Prabhupāda’s heart. It was therefore rewarding to see Śrīla Prabhupāda so happy to hear about our travelling experiences. However, as I was leaving Śrīla Prabhupāda’s quarters after finalizing the time for my next *darśana*, on the way out I was stopped by Bhavānanda Mahārāja who was intimately serving Śrīla Prabhupāda. He rebuked me for telling Śrīla Prabhupāda about our visit to *Bhīma Kā Pul*. This was the spot at which one of the Pāṇḍava brothers, Bhīma had left his body. Bhavānanda felt that it was not the appropriate time to share such news. It would trigger Śrīla Prabhupāda’s desire to also leave and that was something we were trying to delay for as long as possible.

I was very enthusiastic about being asked to arrange for the bullock cart for Śrīla Prabhupāda’s pilgrimage. Śrīla Prabhupāda had given me the initial instruction for the bullock cart travelling and preaching mission directly in Delhi.

Śrīla Prabhupāda’s desire to go on bullock cart pilgrimage brought to mind a letter that he had written to me from Detroit on 12 June 1976. He had written, “When I return to India, I shall surely try to travel with your buses. It is a very nice programme and I want to see it go on very enthusiastically.”

He had not travelled with our buses then, but now he was desiring to go on bullock cart pilgrimage with us.

## Chapter 38. You Manage - Let Me Travel to All The Tīrtha-sthānas

*Vṛndāvana, 1977*

After finalizing the bullock cart arrangements, the three of us returned from Mathurā. In the evening, I had immediately gone in the Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma temple where I led *kīrtana* and then went to see Śrīla Prabhupāda. I had not seen Śrīla Prabhupāda since mid-morning. There were a few other senior disciples with Śrīla Prabhupāda keeping vigil when we arrived in his quarters. I entered and offered obeisances.

*Upon seeing me, Tamāla Kṛṣṇa announced, “Lokanāth is here. Would you like some more kīrtana, Śrīla Prabhupāda? Lokanāth can lead. Lokanāth, you lead.”*

*However Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “He must be tired.”*

*I said, “Not to chant for you, Śrīla Prabhupāda.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda softly said, “Hm.”*

*I continued, “We just had a nice kīrtana, but I could chant more. I have come a long way to see you and chant for you. So if you allow, I will chant.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda replied, “Yes.” He then asked me about the climate during my travelling. “How is the climate outside?”*

*“There’s not much cold so far. Luckily I came today. The climate is good,” was my response.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then went on to enquire about other parts of India.*

*I explained, “As we go towards the Himalayas it gets cold otherwise up to Delhi and Chandigrah same climate as it is here now. As soon as we go out into the mountains, it’s very cold. We were shivering there. As we came to the places, wherever we went the climate was the same. Same as Vṛndāvana. It’s a good climate.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda addressed his senior disciples, “I wish that you GBC manage very nicely, and consider that I am dead, and let me travel to all the tīrtha-sthānas. Bring little medicine or no medicine, little milk and travel from one place to another, and if there is death, what is the lamentation? My age is ripe. In the open air and bullock cart or during the daytime, eh? … Nowadays there is in India ample sunshine. So during the daytime I shall travel and night time you make a camp under a tree. In this way, let me travel to all the tīrthas. I am thinking this way. What is your opinion?”*

*Both Bhavānanda and Jayapatākā Mahārāja focused on assuring him, “There’s no need to be concerned. You have trained us well to manage things in the GBC.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda, with focused intellect, restated his original wish, “No, no you are managing, I know, but you are all important men and unnecessarily you are bound up. You cannot go. So Lokanāth’s party has got some experience and let me go. In India the climate is now good. If I recover, it is very good. You know. So what is the wrong? If I die, then the body will be brought either in Vṛndāvana or Māyāpur, that’s all. And if I live, it will be a great end of a life. You are all experienced.”*

*Jayapatākā Mahārāja continued to assure Śrīla Prabhupāda that he would not be burdened by material managements to which Śrīla Prabhupāda responded, “No, not from that point of view. What is the use of lying down here?”*

*Jayapatākā Mahārāja carefully explained that the kavirāja had given clear instructions, to which Śrīla Prabhupāda laughingly responded, “Don’t keep me locked up. You do your duty as I have trained you and let me be free.”*

There were many devotees in the room during this exchange and almost immediately thereafter the house was divided.

The request by Śrīla Prabhupāda remained unresolved at this point as the disciples wrestled about whether to concede to his compelling, obvious desire or to be extremely wary about his weak condition and take a decision not to allow the fulfilment thereof. The debate of what to do and what not to do, pursued. Many sided with executing Śrīla Prabhupāda’s instruction, while many more had just the opposite opinion – both for very good reasons.

I remembered however, Śrīla Prabhupāda saying to Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami on his last visit to London, “If I survive this time, I want to do *parikramā*.” Śrīla Prabhupāda also reiterated this request in Bombay, on his return from London.

## Chapter 39. You Have Got Experience

*Vṛndāvana, 10 November, 1977*

By enquiring about the climate and requesting the senior disciples and GBC members deal with all the management issues, Śrīla Prabhupāda had ingeniously set the scene to prepare to undertake the bullock cart pilgrimage.

*Śrīla Prabhupāda then asked, “So, where did you go?”*

*I explained, “I went to Mathurā. I went first to Vṛndāvana and looked for the carts. They were not of a very good kind. They were made for rough loads. So I went to Mathurā and spoke to one farmer. He had a fairly big size cart and two strong bullocks, and he asked for a hundred rupees. He wouldn’t come down. So ultimately I agreed for a hundred rupees. And he will reach tonight at eleven o’clock.”*

*I continued, “I asked him to start immediately, but they have pūjā, as we have go-pūjā, they also have oxen pūjā today, so he will only reach at eleven o’clock and he will be with us all day long tomorrow…”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda responded, “So what do you think?”*

*I asked, “Of what?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “How many carts you have?”*

*I explained, “I got just one today, one for you, and those who serve you, they could also sit beside you in the cart, and the rest could do kīrtana around the bullock cart, and the whole unit, bullock cart and saṅkīrtana group, will move together.”*

*“So what we have to pay,” Śrīla Prabhupāda questioned further.*

*I answered, “We’ll pay hundred rupees, one day. Of course, if you want to have it for the entire time, best thing is to purchase a set of bullocks, two bullocks and a cart.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was confident that I had sufficient experience to recommend whether one should purchase a bullock cart or it may be cheaper to hire it with food for the driver and the bullocks. Śrīla Prabhupāda then asked, “So, what is better?”*

*I suggested, “Well, depends on whether we like to use it for longer time. Then we should purchase. If it is just for a few days, best thing is to hire.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda probed further, “Hundred rupees and food?”*

*“No. He has his food for himself and bullocks. So that’s for the cart, two bullocks and the driver,” I replied.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was happy. “That’s nice,” he said.*

*I agreed, “Yes. This is what they earn for a living. Usually they carry different loads from place to another place. Each trip they take twenty rupees, thirty rupees, fifty rupees, according to distance and the load they carry. But it’s expensive to keep it for many days.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda said, “No. If it is inconvenient, we may not keep.”*

*I added, “If it is convenient?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda continued, “Then we shall keep.”*

*“So we’ll hire first and see how it goes, works. And if you think it is nice, or comfortable, all right with you, then we can purchase,” I concluded.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda asked, “So how many we are going?”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami informed Śrīla Prabhupāda that all the devotees would like to accompany him.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda smiled, “Oh, that’s nice. So, how many carts you require?”*

*I replied, “One for now. We also had a meeting, and we were discussing that if we go around just in Vṛndāvana, all will go. But if we keep going from holy place to holy place, then we have to decide whether everyone goes or just a few of us go.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda enquired, “So you are hiring for how many days?”*

*I answered, “This is only for one day it is hired now, but we could continue to hire the same cart for several more days by paying extra money.*”

*When Śrīla Prabhupāda asked Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami, what he thought about this experiment he replied, “It certainly is an experiment. Of course, we always like experiments to be successful.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed and asked, “Where he’ll go?”*

*Much discussion continued. Several options started to emerge seeing that the Vṛndāvana town parikramā alternative was not possible. Haṁsadūta suggested that Śrīla Prabhupāda be taken to Keśī-ghāṭa or one of the important temples like Govindajī. I confirmed, “There are at least seven temples.”*

*I also thought it would be appropriate to mention some of the details of the cart, “Śrīla Prabhupāda, this bullock cart which I hired has no cover on the top.”*

*The devotees indicated that this could be covered up.*

*Bhakti Cāru Mahārāja once again raised the question of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s condition and whether given his poor health he would be able to withstand the trip. Furthermore, bullock carts were usually very bumpy and devotees try to respond to how this could be alleviated.*

*I added, “If these roads are not bad, and the cart has tyre wheels...”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda encouraged me to continue, “Yes?”*

*“So we’re discussing bumping, so \[there\] won’t be much bumping on the cart. Also, we always could go slowly. If Śrīla Prabhupāda wants to make an experiment, we could make one day...” I said.*

*My heartfelt desire was to transport Śrīla Prabhupāda gloriously on a nice cart, a chariot. Śrīla Prabhupāda light-heartedly responded, “Hmm. You can purchase immediately.”*

*I explained how these would have to be designed, “They’re not ready- made like that. The kind which we want, the pull-on kind, with cover on top and nice arrangements, we have to design it and order it. Get it made. There’s a place called Meerut, north of Delhi from where it can be ordered…”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda interjected, “Therefore I sent you. You have got already experience.”*

*Encouraged I added, “You said you want to make an experiment, so let us make an experiment with this hired bullock cart, and in a few days time…”*

*This light-hearted banter was over. Gopāla Kṛṣṇa and Haṁsadūta again refocused on the concern as to whether Śrīla Prabhupāda would survive such an experiment. However, Śrīla Prabhupāda’s transcendental vision was that such a parikramā would actually cure him.*

*“One day experiment. It is for one day… rest assured, I will not die in one day,” Śrīla Prabhupāda had appealed.*

*Discussions continued, with reasons put forth on the difficulties which would be experienced and that the devotees would have to walk numerous hours in the sun. I then shared my village experience, “From my village there is this village called Pandharpur. This is sixty miles. And still, old men, they just travel, walk. Every Ekādaśī they go to Pandharpur, take darśana of Lord Viṭṭhala and immediately return on foot.”*

*The final arrangement was made and I informed the group, “The bullock cart will arrive at eleven o’clock tonight. Tomorrow is Govardhana-pūjā. We should depart after maṅgala-ārati.”*

*In all of this, Śrīla Prabhupāda’s only request was, “As far as possible, take me in a comfortable position. That’s all. As far as possible.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda also suggested, “Cooking could be undertaken there and a picnic enjoyed.”*

*I continued, “Or otherwise we can make real picnic. We could collect some grains there from door to door and cook some khichari there.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed, “That will be very nice.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda canvassed my opinion about the trip, “So Lokanāth, what do you say?”*

*“We should make nice experiment to please you,” was my response, “this will be ever-new experience for us also.”*

*The discussion then moved onto the return arrangements, with Svarūpa Dāmodara enquiring when we would return from Govardhana.*

*I suggested, “It would be better if that decision could be taken while we are there.”*

*However, Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami felt otherwise. A decision was taken that Śrīla Prabhupāda would not travel back the same day, and a suggestion was accepted that warm blankets be taken since the evenings were very cold, and all necessary arrangements would be made to make Śrīla Prabhupāda as comfortable as possible.*

Ultimately, Śrīla Prabhupāda never undertook this pilgrimage. Later that evening Niṣkiñcana Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī, Śrīla Prabhupāda’s godbrother from the Gauḍīya Maṭha came to visit him. Bābājī chanted, and sometimes spoke in Bengali to Śrīla Prabhupāda. While he was there, Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami, and Bhavānanda Mahārāja had also come to see Śrīla Prabhupāda. They explained that the road was in a bad condition. They understood that Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted to go on the *parikramā*, but they appealed to Śrīla Prabhupāda to wait until his health had improved. Seeing their anxiety, Śrīla Prabhupāda agreed to their proposal.

Some of us felt that we should execute the Spiritual Master’s desire. There was another group who feared that Śrīla Prabhupāda’s frail body would not survive such rough treatment on the roads. It was His Holiness Jayādvaita Mahārāja who, on the occasion of Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami Mahārāja’s disappearance festival, put the situation into perspective for all of us. He explained that originally the very devotees who were now the so called ‘opposition’ and others who were not in favour of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s travelling by bullock cart were initially also willing to go along. They were party to that decision. However, overriding all of this was their concern for Śrīla Prabhupāda. His observation was that we should not consider the others as opposing for the sake of opposition, but rather view it as healthy or loyal opposition. This opposition resulted in an intensification of both anxiety and love for Śrīla Prabhupāda in all his disciples.

## Chapter 40. Do I Come with You Śrīla Prabhupāda?

*Vṛndāvana, 13 November, 1977*

The bullock cart pilgrimage to Govardhana Hill which was meant to take place on Govardhana-pūjā, 11 November, did not happen. Now, although the idea of undertaking the pilgrimage by bullock cart had been dropped, Śrīla Prabhupāda had not given up the idea of travelling. He was persistent. Śrīla Prabhupāda considered the options available and thought about using buses to undertake this pilgrimage.

It was nearing midday on the 13 November and I had just returned from *nagar saṅkīrtana*. Śrīla Prabhupāda called for me. I was eager to see him and reported that we had *kīrtana* in a bullock cart in Vṛndāvana town.

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa said, “Oh here he is. Here’s Lokanāth, Śrīla Prabhupāda.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda called, “Lokanāth.”*

*I replied, “Śrīla Prabhupāda.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda softly asked, “You are coming back?”*

*“Yes, we came back from saṅkīrtana. We did i in the town, Vṛndāvana, Loi Bazaar and Bāṅkebihārī temple, that route. We had a bullock cart filled in with Gurukula school children, and there was a big group of devotees chanting and dancing in front of the cart. And there was also a van, making announcements, inviting people for our afternoon programme of ārati, kīrtana and prasāda distribution,” I explained to Śrīla Prabhupāda.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda softly interrupted, “So go.”*

*I explained, “No, we already did. It was very ecstatic. Thirty, forty devotees came, and it was very, very nice. Wherever we stopped, people gathered around us in big numbers, enjoyed kīrtana and heard announcements about the programme. So, I just came back.”*

*Some discussion developed about prasāda for the guests and devotees and I said, “Prasāda will be served out to the guests after this ārati. Twelve o’clock is ārati. And then we’ll be serving out prasāda to the guests first. When everyone is served, we’ll eat the Lord’s remnants.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted to know, “You have got all in your charge?”*

*I replied, “Yes, I have.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda was anxious to share with me a desire that he had had since a long time. It was his ambition to start a bus tour of all the holy places all over India. He had asked Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami, to consult with me on this issue. At this point in time Śrīla Prabhupāda was anxious about this tour.*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda called, “Lokanāth!”*

*I replied, “Yes, I’m here, Prabhupāda.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda introduced the idea of a front or advance party to facilitate his travels.*

*I understood what Śrīla Prabhupāda was saying, “Front party, advance party, yes.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda elaborated, “They’ll give us description of the land and before our going, and they’ll make a camp. Small, big, that doesn’t matter. And in the morning, the former camp will be broken, and go to the next camp with kīrtana. In the meantime the other camp is ready to receive you. Then the next camp, after taking prasāda, they’ll go to the next camp.”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa enquired if I was following what Śrīla Prabhupāda was suggesting. I confirmed, “So there is a smaller group going in advance, and bigger group with you stays back.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda affirmed this and I continued, “Yes, and they go, fix the place, and inform your party where they are and what kind of arrangement they have made. And when you are satisfied, you leave that place to join the first group, small group, advance party. Right?”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami, added, “And after Śrīla Prabhupāda joins that group, then the advance party goes again.”*

*I said, “To the next place, to make further arrangements at the next place.” Śrīla Prabhupāda was pleased that we understood the concept.*

*This was familiar to me, “Yes. This kind of programme we have done before also. Whenever we had big group travelling, we always send two devotees out or sometimes four devotees so the main party does not lose time in finding or looking for the place.”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda added, “And when you go in procession, have kīrtana.”*

*I agreed, “Yes. That would be nice.”*

*Some discussion was initiated by Tamāla Kṛṣṇa about using a few vehicles instead of bullock carts for the sake of saving time and Śrīla Prabhupāda’s comfort.*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa suggested, “I could see this Volkswagen we have. They don’t have any seats, it’s all flat. They already have mattresses, and it’s kind of a big room.”*

*I had to agree with this, “And most of the holy places are connected with good roads. During our preaching we also visit holy places. When they’re on the way, we pass through them. And if we travel not very fast, with good drivers, it won’t be much.”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa Goswami identified Bhakti Caitanya Mahārāja from Chandigarh as the fit driver for Śrīla Prabhupāda’s vehicles, as well as a good organiser. Śrīla Prabhupāda consented, “Oh, yes.”*

*Then Śrīla Prabhupāda requested the purchase of at least four vehicles and this is met with surprise by the devotees.*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa repeated, “Purchase at least four vehicles. Right. I think some scientist should also come with us, Śrīla Prabhupāda.” This was met with laughter. Tamāla Kṛṣṇa continued, “In case we meet anyone very intelligent, we need a scientific mind to defeat them. So Svarūpa Dāmodara can come with us?”*

*Śrīla Prabhupāda replied, “Oh, yes.”*

*Girirāja said, “And someone to make members, in case we meet any rich people.”*

*Tamāla Kṛṣṇa added, “Girirāja is requesting to come, Śrīla Prabhupāda. Is that all right?”*

*My humble request to Śrīla Prabhupāda was, “So, do I come with you?”*

*“You will be the leader,” was his response.*

*I was grateful. “So, I have a vehicle. They also could travel with us, and in the same town they will do nagara-saṅkīrtana all day long and distribute books.” Śrīla Prabhupāda was pleased.*

I recalled the time when the German buses were becoming a problem. Because the buses were of German origin, their permits needed to be renewed every six months and this meant that the travelling and preaching would temporarily come to a halt.

Srila Prabhupāda had astutely suggested that bullock carts were the ideal mode of transport and this was the mode our travelling party had been utilizing. When the mode of transport changed from bullock cart to vehicles for Srila Prabhupāda’s impending pilgrimage, I realised that I may not even be going with on this journey that Srila Prabhupāda was undertaking. I felt humbled when Srila Prabhupāda assigned me the role of leader. This was Srila Prabhupāda’s final wish, however it remained unfulfilled.

Since Srila Prabhupāda had asked me to be the leader of his unfulfilled *parikramā*, I took up the responsibility to execute Srila Prabhupāda’s wish and desire by planning the travelling *sankīrtana* party. This was not an easy task. Many years had passed since Srila Prabhupāda’s departure, several leaders still remembered this unfulfilled desire and the morose emotions that accompanied this period. I liaised and spoke to members of the GBC and other senior disciples as to how this could be made possible.

Luckily, 1986 marked the 500th birth anniversary of Caitanya Mahāprabhu. ISKCON leaders were thinking of ways and means to celebrate this auspicious fifth Centennial of Caitanya Mahāprabhu. A joint decision was taken that this would be the ideal opportunity to fulfil Srila Prabhupāda’s desire to undertake a pilgrimage.

This pilgrimage celebration would commence from Dvārakā and culminate in Māyāpur coinciding with the 500th Gaura Pūrṇima festival. It would span eight thousand kilometres over a period of eighteen months.

The GBC and senior leaders were looking for a leader to take charge of this event. It was at this time that they remembered Srila Prabhupāda appointing me as the leader of the unfulfilled pilgramage. Thus Srila Prabhupāda’s desire to undertake pilgrimage and my serving as its leader was therefore finally fulfilled.

## Epilogue

**Continuing the conversation**

In writing this epilogue, two quotes by Śrīla Prabhupāda come to mind that may shift the melancholic spirit of the reader from the last series of conversations. Firstly, Śrīla Prabhupāda’s citation of Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura on what a pure devotee carries in this world after his passing: “He reasons ill who tells that Vaiṣṇavas die, when thou art living still in sound! The Vaiṣṇavas die to live, and in living try to spread the holy name around!”

Secondly, in a letter to Śatadhanya Dāsa (*20 February 1972*) Śrīla Prabhupāda said “...so far as personal association with *guru* is concerned, I was only with Guru Mahārāja four or five times, but I have never left his association, not even for a moment. Because I am following his instruction, I have never felt any separation.”

This book endeavours to revivify Śrīla Prabhupāda through his spoken words and it is through his words that he is always in association with us. In reading this book we should be stirred to go back to dialogue with Śrīla Prabhupāda, connect with him and cherish his instructions.

In writing up these conversations, I am personally inspired to catch up with him with some urgency… to continue the dialogue.
