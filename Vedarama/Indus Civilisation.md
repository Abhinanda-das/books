# Indus Civilisation

## Indus Civilisation

http://www.cf.ac.uk/learn/philos/eastern/india01.html

**The early history, culture and thought of India:**

The Indus civilisation, or Harrapan culture, flourished from 2500 BC to 1500 BC; and it stretched beyond the Indus valley – from Baluchistan on the Persian border in the West to Uttar Pradesh in the central Northern plain where the tributaries of the Ganges flow. In the North it reached the Himalayas and in the South Gujurat – an area greater than ancient Egypt. The two greatest archaeological sites are Harappa and Mohendro-daro in Pakistan.

There, streets and lanes are laid out in a set plan. At Kalibangan the lane width followed a set ratio: while the lanes were 1.8 metres wide, the streets in multiples of the lane width were 3.6, 5.5, and 7.2 metres wide.

The citadel at Mohendro-daro contained imposing buildings, all made of kiln-burnt bricks: the great bath, the stupa, the priests' college, the granary and the assembly hall. The main town contained many large houses. The average comprised of four to six living rooms around a courtyard with a bathroom and a kitchen. Some great houses had up to thirty rooms with staircases indicating other stories. There were brick sewers with regular manholes. The area of the granary was 55 by 37 metres. On the lowest floor rows of brickwork podia supported the wooden floor of the storage hall and allowed air to circulate. What was probably the assembly hall covered an area of over 750 square metres. It has twenty massive brick piers in four rows of five. The working men’s quarter of the city has regular rows of living quarters with small working platforms and its own granary. It suggests that the population was extremely regulated.

At Lothal on the Gulf of Cambay, there is a huge brick-lined dock, 216 metres by 37. Probably a channel \[7 metres wide\] connected the dock to the Bhogavo River, now 2 kilometres away. A large spill-channel was also provided. Lapis lazuli, jade, turquoise, materials not native to India are also found, which, along with Indus seals found in west Asian sites indicate a flourishing sea and land trade.

Pottery was made of red slip painted with black pigments and has been found on every site. Scenes and animals were painted; especially the short-horned bull. There are sculpted stone statues and many, beautifully executed, tiny, clay figurines. They were expert workers in bronze: making knife-blades, short-swords, spears, arrow-heads, sickles, chisels, razors, pins, tweezers and fish-hooks. Beyond these were beautiful statuettes as the famous dancing girl from Mohendro-daro. The finest craftsmanship is found in the seals cut out of steatite on which frequently appeared the Brahmani bull. The seals were inscribed with a sign language of 400 signs. Portrayals show a man wearing a *dhoti* as in India today. Among ornaments the *bija*, hollow, conical earrings were found.

Artefacts found indicate the worship of a mother goddess and of nature spirits. Some are shaped like the *linga*, some like the *yoni*; and there are portrayals, on seals, of a god like Shiva, *Pashupati*, lord of the animals.

The valley cultivation was rich: wheat, barley, bananas, melons and peas. There is evidence that they ate fish and all meats. They seem to have been the first people to cultivate cotton.

The decline of the Harrapan civilisation perhaps was due to severe flooding, disease and the Aryan invasions.
