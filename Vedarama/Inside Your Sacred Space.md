# Inside Your Sacred Space

## The Sacred Space within the Heart

*There is a space in the heart*  
*Where everything meets.*  
*Come here if you want to find Me*  
*Mind, soul, God, and eternity - all are there.*  
*Are you?*

*Enter the space of vastness in the heart*  
*Listen to the song that resonates there.*  
*Give yourself to it with abandon*  
*You will find quiet ecstasy -*  
*And a steady, regal sense*  
*Of resting in a perfect place.*

*You who are the embodiment of the highest blessings*  
*Once you know the way*  
*You will always be called to return.*

*Answer that call again and again*  
*And be satisfied with knowing,*  
*"I belong here; I am at home."*  
*(the Vedas)*

**Deep in our hearts, in an inner room,** our greatest treasure lies hidden -an often unknown wealth. To help us approach this room, ancient Indian wisdom texts compare our body to a city of nine gates - two eyes, two ears, two nostrils, one mouth, an anus and a genital - through which information and energy travel constantly, like visitors. In this city the house of the heart stands out, and in that house the treasure is hidden in a special room called the *dahara akash*. This room is tiny, by material estimation, but spiritually, it’s so large that the whole universe fits inside it. This is the room where the eternal soul (*atma*) lives in the presence of the Divine. The *Upanishads* declare that those who find this room will discover their true hearts desire.

This room has been called all kinds of things, but any genuine spiritual tradition knows of it. Some call it "the sky of the heart,” others "the lotus" or "temple” of the heart. Some spiritualists prefer to describe it as a state of consciousness, or even as being in balance. Yogis call it the "yoga zone” - that place where asanas flow without conscious effort. All these descriptions refer to the same heart room.

We walk past this room regularly, but we never seem to enter it. The Upanishads say we're like someone who lives near a hidden cache of gold but who doesn’t know it's there. If we don't know about our treasure, we can never spend it.

But finding and entering the sacred space within us will make us unswervingly peaceful. No matter what happens to us in our life we’ll remain calm, happy. This is because it is in this room that we meet our innermost self, the place where we find an inexhaustible source of strength and inspiration. Experienced visitors of sacred space have divine experiences there.

Sometimes we stumble into our inner sanctum unintentionally. We can recognize these moments because everything we do suddenly seems easy, and we are filled with enthusiasm and clarity. The *Upanishads* say we enter this room during dreamless deep sleep, but we’re unconscious, and don’t remember our visit when we wake. Still, it’s why waking from a deep sleep leaves us refreshed and ready to deal with the world anew. Once we consciously know our way into our own sacred space, we’ll be able to return to it at will.

Discovering our sacred space is more and more important in these times when people are overly busy and overly stressed.

**Here's a story to help you on your way:**

*There once lived a man who felt confined by his life. He lived in a room in which inexplicably, all his basic needs were cared for - regular meals were provided, he had good sanitary facilities, fresh air, even visitors. The only thing he was missing was freedom; he could never open the only door to his room and get out.*

*Over time he grew so despondent that he began to spend all his waking hours trying to escape. But no matter what he tried, he couldn’t get the door to open. Despairing, he finally fell seriously ill.*

*When his end was near he went to try the door one last time. "The whole world is free except me,” he sighed. This time, when he tried the door, it swung effortlessly open - but to the inside! Surprised, he strode into this new i vorld of liberty and soon, health.*

*The problem had been that throughout his life, he had been trying to open the door to the outside, when the door only swung in one direction - to the inside. Only when he pulled instead of pushed was he successful - and did the world of possibilities lie before him.*

May we all find and open the door to the inside of ourselves.

## Entering Sacred Space

**Most of us are ever oriented outward.** In Sanskrit we call this *bahir mukha*: "facing the outside." We search for happiness in our environment - in good food, our achievements, our possessions. But to find sacred space we have to turn away from what's outside us and look within. Just as a turtle withdraws into its shell, we have to put outer concerns aside for some time and focus on the sacred space within us if we hope to enter it.

**Leaving other Rooms.**  
By "exterior" we don’t only mean everything outside the city of the body but everything that happens outside the hearts sacred space. Different rooms, different topics and thoughts, seem to capture us so much that we forget we were on our way into the sacred. This doesn’t mean these other rooms aren’t important; each of these other “rooms" has its own right and purpose. But if we want to search for sacred space, we’ll still need to leave them for a while. Leaving them aside is a conscious act; they are on the way to the inner room of peace and have to be passed by in order to reach the sanctum.

The four most famous of these other rooms can be compared to the various parts of an elephant.

**The Room of Evaluative Thoughts**  
This room is compared to an elephants trunk. Just as the trunk sways back and forth between the elephant's two tusks, so appraising thoughts sway back and forth between the poles of “good" and “bad.” All our judgments come from this room. How much time can we invest in life to decide between what is good and what is not, mainly to reassure ourselves of the rightness of our views? Some people choose to stay imprisoned in this room their entire lives.

**The Room of Material Intelligence**  
This is the room of analysis and problem solving. Here we ask ourselves about our duty in any given situation, and how we should react to our environment. We gather information, try to make sense of things, ponder, and plan. This room is symbolized by the elephants ears, which open themselves to as much information as possible to come to good decisions. The quest for sacred space requires setting aside our problems for the time being, so there’s no need to get ourselves stuck in this room of analysis and second-guessing. Rest assured that problems are often solved in startlingly simple ways once we have the kind of clarity that comes from meeting yourself.

**The Room of the Subconscious**  
The elephants large belly represents the vast world of the subconscious, which influences us through the constant bubbling up into the conscious mind of impulses. Think of a lake where bubbles rise to the water surface from the mud at the bottom, sometimes breaking with no odor and sometimes releasing a moldy smell. These impressions that invade from our subconscious need to be recognized for what they are. Don’t let them divert you from entering your sacred space.

**The Room of the Ego**  
The room of the ego is the skin of the elephant. Elephants have thick but sensitive skin - just like our ego, which is easily bruised and yet always relentlessly self-protec-tive. In the room of the ego we grapple with the masks we wear, always conscious of what others think of us. If others don’t treat us according to our self-assessment, we’re usually hurt.

Until we learn to walk consciously through these four rooms, understanding them for what they are, they have the strength to impede our spiritual development for a long time.

## Ways into Sacred Space

Many practices lead into sacred space - meditation, spiritual dance, ritual, scripture, pilgrimages, to name a few. But for any practice to work we have to first become aware of the self's eternal nature.

**The Eternauty of the Self**  
As the ancient Indian scriptures teach, at core we are spiritual beings. This means we are eternal and naturally blissful. Our soul is covered by two mantles, the psyche and the body. The soul continues to live on after the body dies; it trades an old body in for a new one in the same way we change our clothes.

In our seminars we teach different meditations through which you can become aware of your own eternality. Here we would like to introduce an effective way to keep your eternality in your conscious awareness during your day-to-day life:

**The Emotional Traffic Light**  
The emotional traffic light helps us maintain a spiritual perspective in everyday life. It shows us how to deal with emotions appropriately, without suppressing them.

Here’s how it works:

Whenever something happens that bothers you, switch to "red'’ and stop for a moment - don’t give in to your first reaction.

Then switch to "yellow." Remember who you are: an eternal spiritual self, unaffected by whatever is happening in the world of matter. Pull your attention away from everything happening around you and remember: "I am an eternal soul." Take the role of an observer and look at the events in your life from the soul’s perspective. This will help you see the world in a new light. Whatever your life brings you is coming from divine mercy or karma or both. Your life events are an opportunity for your growth and spiritual maturation. Ask yourself how you can respond to what's happened so everyone involved benefits.

Then switch to "green” and act.

## Mantra meditation

**The easiest way into sacred space** is the path of spiritual sound. When we chant mantras and they begin to unfold themselves within us, they carry us into sacred space. The mantra is like a bow that shoots the arrow of the self toward its destination. So as in archery, it’s important to align body, mind, and soul properly during mantra meditation. Let your body be comfortable - sit in a way that doesn't require attention, preferably with your spine straight. Make your breathing calm and regular. Direct your concentration on the mantra. If your mind wanders, gently bring it back to the sound. And especially: we want to enter the meditation, so recognize the relationship between you, the soul, and the Divine, as you chant. If you explore this relationship sincerely as you meditate, the mantra will bring you into sacred space, where you can make a connection with the Divine. Is there a greater perfection?
