# Japa meditations

## Dedication

To Śrīla Prabhupāda who taught to “sit properly!” and to chant the holy name “like a child crying for his mother”.

## Introduction

“You should compile these together in a book!” This simple “mantra” offered by Yadunātha dāsa, an enthusiastic subscriber to my online journal, struck a chord. He was referring to my “*Japa* Thoughts”, my personal realizations on how to enter the holy name, a popular entry often found in my journals during the Indian lunar month of Kārttika.

Every Kārttika since 1974, with the exception of two years, I have resided in Vṛndāvana, the sacred land of Lord Kṛṣṇa. Like many Vaiṣṇavas, during that month I take a vow to substantially increase my daily spiritual practice by increasing the time spent in *japa* meditation. Since 2000, however, I have spent Kārttika not only in Vṛndāvana, but have lived at the foot of Govardhana Hill, the most hallowed ground within Vṛndāvana. It is at this time that I decided to write my realizations on how to more deeply enter, to become absorbed in, *japa* meditation. In one fairly recent journal entry I shared the basis of such realizations:

“What is a *japa* realization? It is the recognition of the obstacles that block us from attaining our next level of meditation and the form of mercy necessary to help us reach beyond those barriers.” (November 1, 2006)

In my previous book, Greetings from Vṛndāvana, some of those realizations were listed, but the last entry was from 2003. Since then I have spent four more Kārttikas in Govardhana where each year I have recorded anywhere between 15 and 25 realizations on entering the holy name.

Half of *Japa* Meditations (Part II) is composed of these personal meditations, including the ones already printed in Greetings from Vṛndāvana. I have described the newer meditations first, the ones written after Greetings from Vṛndāvana, so the entries start in Kārttika 2007 and work their way down through the years.

Although my most inspiring *japa* realizations have been during Kārttika, good *japa* is not limited to a specific time of the year. A separate section near the end of the book, “Throughout the Years (2000-2007)”, thus lists my meditations from various times during the year. To end Part II of my book I added some “Final Thoughts” based on a lively discussion I had on the relation between humility and individuality in chanting the holy name.

I hope these meditations serve as an impetus for the reader to go deeper, to unlock, so to speak, absorption in the holy name. I also discussed this in a recent meditation:

“I want entrance into the holy name. What is the door? It is only our particular attraction to Śrī Kṛṣṇa. While chanting I thus search for my taste - the particular manifestation of the Lord that will evoke devotion and open my heart. I first pray to Śrī Guru and the Vaiṣṇavas and progressively reach the lotus feet of Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī. I think of his samādhi at Rādhā Dāmodara, his bhajana kuṭira at Ṭera Kadamba; I sing his song (Śrī Rūpa manjarī pada) and meditate on the special mercy he received from Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu. Today my taste rests there. The door opens. I gain entrance into the holy name. (October 13, 2006)

One good fortune of living in a sacred place, especially Vṛndāvana, is the association of deep sādhakas (spiritual practitioners) that often reside and visit there. It is by their association that I am guided in my own meditation. After being inspired to write this book from the simple message “You should compile these together in a book”, it dawned on me that if some were inspired by my realizations, how much more they would get from those that inspire me. I thus decided to ask these devotees if they would agree to be interviewed on how they enter the holy name. I was doubtful how successful I would be. Serious sādhakas who reside in Vṛndāvana are very busy in nāma-bhajana and other devotional practices. Not one person, however, refused my request!

For my interviews, I first visited Vṛndāvana, where my friends Śyāmapriyā dāsī and her husband Dhruva Mahārāja dāsa reside. Śyāmapriyā dāsī discusses how the instructions of Śrīla Prabhupāda are the basis of entering the holy name and can inspire one to go deeper. Based on 35 years of steady commitment to the practice of *japa*, Dhruva Mahārāja dāsa suggests a lifetime plan to gradually increase one’s dedication to the holy name.

Sacīnandana Swami discusses how to conquer the mind on the basis of Sanātana Gosvāmī’s Hari-bhakti-vilāsa and then offers his personal realizations through a metaphor describing nāma-bhajana as a sacred expedition.

Aindra dāsa talks about the spontaneous nature of nāma-bhajana, how the “sweetest of sweetest cowherd boys” strongly desires to “pasture on our tongues” in response to “our heartfelt feelings towards Him”. Pārtha-sārathī dāsa Gosvāmī gives a comprehensive recommendation on increasing the quality and quantity of chanting by understand nāma-tattva and mediating on Gaura-*līlā.*

When I visited the āśrama of Mahānidhi Swami at Śrī Rādhā-kuṇḍa for a scheduled interviewed, he humbly handed me a short hand written explanation of the essence of his *japa* - chanting in the mood of giving pleasure to Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa - which is included as his offering.

I thought it would be also helpful to add some younger and newer voices on chanting. Nityānanda dāsa, who studies and teaches at the BBT Sanskrit school in Govardhana, shares his struggles to enter the holy name and the reciprocation he experienced from Śrī Śrī Gaura-Nitāi when chanting in the mood of gratitude.

Bhūrijana dāsa wrote a short essay for *Japa* Meditations entitled “On Improving My *Japa*”. By reference to the Bhāgavatam, Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, and Śrīla Prabhupāda, he discusses chanting in the mood of helplessness and lamentation as the key to improving *japa.*

During the interviews I appreciated how each serious practitioner chose to highlight a different aspect of their practice for the benefit of others. Nirañjana Swami talks extensively of how the cultivation of humility and gratitude increases attentiveness in chanting by making *japa* more relevant to one’s life.

I specifically asked Vaiśeṣika dāsa to share his realizations on the connection between giving Kṛṣṇa consciousness to others and the quality of one’s own *japa* meditation. By discussing this connection from personal experience, Vaiśeṣika dāsa reminds us how important service to the mission of Śrī Caitanya is the cultivation of *japa* meditation.

Śrīmatī dāsī offers several key realizations to overcome lifeless chanting, most important nāma-kīrtana in the association of advanced devotees. She also stresses the inspiration one gains in *japa* by seeing nāma as a “person with deep emotions”. Keśava Bhāratī dāsa Gosvāmī also describes chanting in an intimate mood by using the simple technique of focusing on vibrating the holy name from the heart.

All of the interviews were transcribed by Sudevī dāsī, who studies and teaches at the Bhaktivedanta College in Radhadesh. Inspired by the interviews she began to transcribe lectures by her spiritual master, Rādhānātha Swami, on entering the holy name. She then sent one to me for a possible inclusion in the book. Mahārāja discusses how one’s sincere desire to purify oneself and serve humbly is not only the difference between hollow ritual and genuine devotion, but the key to pure chanting itself.

These interviews are listed in the order they were done and comprise the first half of *Japa* Meditations. Although they were an afterthought to the initial inspiration of the book, for me they are the primary subject of *Japa* Meditations. They are therefore listed as Part I of the book and followed by my own *japa* meditations, which are included as Part II of the book.

Several of the devotees who expressed their realizations about *japa* talk about the need for gratitude. Indeed this book is only the mercy of the Vaiṣṇavas. I have no taste for nāma-kīrtana outside of their continued association.

I also remain indebted to many devotees who assisted me in the completion of this book. I am very grateful to *Śāstra* dāsa, who again generously bore the full expense for the production of *Japa* Meditations as he did for Greetings from Vṛndāvana. I have a new editor, Kadamba Mālā dāsī, who made very good suggestions to improve the text of *Japa* Meditations. I am relieved that again I have the help I need to ensure a polished final text. This is the third book, after Waves of Devotion and Greetings from Vṛndāvana, designed by Jāla Keli dāsa of Bluefuse Design. His work on my first two books has been lauded for excellence. I am fully confident that there will be similar kudos for this latest design. There are numerous details in book publishing from production to distribution. For that I remain reliant on and very appreciative of the able assistance of Rathī Kṛṣṇa dāsa.

Especially I can never sufficiently express my gratitude to Śrīla Prabhupāda. He not only gave me the holy name and the practice of *japa* meditation, but continues to sustain and deepen my practice by his mercy.

The interviews are listed in the order in which they were conducted. To begin, I visited Śyāmapriyā devī dāsī and her husband, Dhruva Mahārāja, who reside in Vṛndāvana and sometimes Purī. Both have been dedicated disciples of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s mission for over 35 years and have increasingly dedicated their time to the practice of chanting and hearing about Kṛṣṇa in the holy dhamas.

## Śyāmapriyā dāsī

My main impetus for going deeper in spiritual life is Śrīla Prabhupāda’s personal instructions. He told me to chant sixteen rounds, worship Lord Caitanya in my home, read his books daily, and always stay in the fire of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Those were the four simple things that he told me to do. After thirty-five years, however, one should go deeper into those instructions. It is not that a devotee gets an instruction that keeps him on one level. Rather, one goes deeper into the instruction. That really inspires me to mediate on Śrīla Prabupada’s instructions, to really understand them, by going deeper and deeper into them. I have thus increased my rounds over the years, which was also influenced by my husband, who is very much attached to chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa.

The next thing I mediate on while chanting is that this is an opportunity to associate with Kṛṣṇa very intimately. Chanting is giving one’s time, one’s life breath, to Kṛṣṇa. Devotees have a certain amount of time and a certain amount of breaths, so as much as one dedicates himself to his relationship with Kṛṣṇa that’s as much as the relationship with Him will be. Hopefully that will become twenty-four hours a day.

Chanting is a devotee’s intimate time with Kṛṣṇa. If one chants sixteen rounds he goes somewhat deep into the relationship, and if he increases it to thirty-two, forty-eight or even sixty-four, he gets more realizations about his relationship with Kṛṣṇa because he is spending more time with Kṛṣṇa, who is non-different from His name. This is a very internal thing, but it is simple. The more time put into a relationship, the more that relationship develops. That relationship is the essence of a devotee’s being.

Certainly, until one becomes pure there is going to be some superficiality. But as one gives more and more of his life’s breath to Kṛṣṇa, the more real the relationship with Kṛṣṇa will become. It is hard to remain superficial when spending ten hours a day with someone. It’s a deep meditation that Kṛṣṇa is non-different from His name. Through His name one is really contacting Him, relating with Him and speaking to Him. He knows everything about His devotee and thus as one becomes more dependent and surrendered to chanting, He not only starts to reveal His holy name, but the devotee’s relationship with Him as well. The devotee then naturally starts taking shelter and that is absorbing.

Many wonderful things happen when one fully takes shelter of the holy name. There is just no time for *prajalpa*. A devotee doesn’t want to neglect others. At the same time one is impelled to continue the vow because he knows that when he is absorbed in the holy name and getting realizations he will have so much more to say later. I have met people whom I had previously said things to while doing my rounds who still remember what I said and they now thank me for it. When one is seriously chanting he is able to speak concisely with more realization. People appreciate that.

Another thing that helps me to become absorbed is chanting *japa* with Śrīla Prabhupāda. This is very important for me. My husband has said that when one hears a pure devotee chant Hare Kṛṣṇa, it allows one to enter into the space of his chanting and then one can just ride on it. It is very profound, very deep. Chanting with Śrīla Prabhupāda is very, very deep and very enthusing.

Another thing that is very helpful is to fix the form of the Lord within the mind. I have done this all my devotional life. I always have two huge pictures of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa and a picture of Śrīla Prabhupāda. I just get absorbed in the form of the Lord and chant. There it is before me, my relationship with the holy name. Kṛṣṇa’s form is non-different from His name and thus the holy name is before the eyes and the form of Kṛṣṇa is on the tongue. That helps to go deeper and to spend more time with the holy name.

Similarly, I chant in front of the Deities, which I don’t do so much here in Vṛndāvana because it is too crowded in my small apartment. I do, however, try to get beautiful pictures of Rādhā-Śyāmasundara and Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, the temple Deities in Vṛndāvana, which are non-different from the holy name, and I chant facing Them.

One of the biggest things that helps me enter the holy name while chanting is meditating upon what I have been reading. I am reading the Bhāgavatam daily. Prabhupāda told me to read daily, so I read the Bhāgavatam and Caitanya-caritāmṛta every day. I have to read some book that Prabhupāda has written and I do it as a daily practice. We are always reading the Bhāgavatam, my husband and I, over and over again. We are going through all the books. I have just finished reading the First Canto, the part where Śukadeva Gosvāmī is being followed by low class women and children. He enters the very exalted assembly of great saintly people, who are the most exalted people in the universe. He comes in there totally naked as dirt is being thrown upon him, but all the saints there stand up when they see him come in. He is walking up to where Mahārāja Parīkṣit is sitting, and he is offered the vyāsāsana. Parīkṣit Mahārāja and all the sages then look up and realize, “This is the person we have been waiting to hear from.” When I read this, and this is just one instance of reading the Bhāgavatam, I become happy and inspired to chant. It also reminds me of Prabhupāda. He came, he sat down, devotees would hear from him, and the result is just chant Hare Kṛṣṇa. It is very deep. One can read the Bhāgavatam, or read the books that motivate one to chant. Some of my favorite parts in the *śāstra* are the glories of the holy name.

There is a wonderful section in the back of the Prema Vivarta about the glories of the holy name. I just wanted to cite one verse that I should have memorized by now but I have a terrible memory. Lord Kṛṣṇa said to Arjuna:

“Oh Arjuna, listen attentively. When the living entity chants My name, whether out of devotion or indifference, I never forget this act. It remains always close to My heart. There is no vow like chanting the holy name, no knowledge superior to it, no meditation which comes anywhere near it, and it gives the highest result. No penance is equal to it and nothing is as potent or powerful as the holy name. Chanting is the greatest act of piety and the supreme refuge. Even the words of the Vedas do not possess sufficient power to describe its magnitude. Chanting is the highest path to liberation, peace and eternal life. It is the pinnacle of devotion, the heart’s joyous proclivity and attraction and the best form of remembrance of the Supreme Lord. The holy name has appeared solely for the benefit of the living entities as the Lord and master, their supreme worshipable object and their spiritual guide and mentor.”

So, when one reads things like this about the holy name, it inspires one to get more deeply absorbed in the holy name. And that’s just one quote among many from the Prema Vivarta.

Another great quote is from Bhaktivinoda’s commentary on the *Śikṣāṣṭaka*, called Bhajan Rahasya, which includes many wonderful glorifications of the holy name. When reading these things one might think, “Why do anything else?” For example, here’s one quote from Bhaktivinode’s Nāmāṣṭaka:

“Oh holy name, Oh Lord Keśava, you dissipate the sufferings of Your surrendered devotees caused by the offences to the holy name. You possess the transcendental form of ecstatic beauty and bliss, and You appear as the embodiment of sublime joy for the residence of Gokula. You are therefore a fully spiritual entity of the same spiritual nature as the Vaikuntha planets. I offer my repeated obeisance’s unto You.”

There are just millions of wonderful ways in which the holy name is glorified by the ācāryas, all explaining how Kṛṣṇa is non-different from His name. Hearing these things helps one become absorbed.

After preaching about the holy name for thirty-five years, I now really feel that it is about time for me to enter deep within the holy name. In other words, it has to be not only spoken about, but done. Just like Prabhupāda asked the devotees, “Is anyone reading my books?” Prabhupāda made a joke, “Oh, you are just selling these books, but have you read them?” Someone said, “No, I’m just selling,” but Śrīla Prabhupāda did not approve. So by chanting one becomes connected with Kṛṣṇa and with Prabhupāda by following his most important instruction, and it is sublime.

I have to chant. I must get deep. To represent Śrīla Prabhupāda, a devotee must become a beacon of light, to be lit up by the potency of the holy name and show others the mercy of Prabhupāda. It must be done. Devotees have to chant. And then, when devotees say something, even if it is just one or two things, they will have said it all. As one gets older, there isn’t time to say more anyway. There isn’t energy to say more. One has to choose words wisely. This is what I always say now, “I love you, chant! I have to finish my rounds.”

Devotees must have commitment, determination, duty, and ultimately taste. The sum total of it all is enthusiasm. Only then can one enter into the holy name.

## Dhruva Mahārāja dāsa

When I joined the Hare Kṛṣṇa movement in 1974 the daily program consisted of chanting rounds in the morning after maṅgala-ārati, guru-pūjā, greeting the deities, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam class, prasadam, daily activities, Bhagavad-gītā class in the early evening, then sandhya-ārati, Kṛṣṇa book reading, and rest.

By 1981, the evening part of that program had corroded and was no longer as well attended as it had been years earlier. Feeling the extra time in the evening was not being utilized to its fullest, I began chanting another 16 rounds in the late afternoon and into the evening. I’m now in my twenty-fifth year of chanting at least 32 rounds a day.

The ācāryas give the example of an unripe mango and a ripe mango; both are mangos, but one is small, green, and sour, while the other is larger, golden, soft, and sweet. In the same way that the mango receives nutrients and shelter from the tree it grows upon, if one simply chants his rounds attentively every day, he receives spiritual nourishment and shelter from the holy name, which is non-different from Kṛṣṇa.

There has been no other experience or sādhana in the 30 plus years of my devotional life that compares with the regular chanting of my rounds. I would therefore recommend a similar pattern to anyone interested in tasting its sweetness.

For those just beginning, I would recommend chanting 16 rounds for at least five years. In that way one becomes accustomed to chanting through thick and thin, and will become fixed in the practice.

After five years of steady chanting, one should be steady enough to go to the next level. One can analyze his situation and increase his rounds accordingly. If chanting can be increased by four rounds, do so; eight rounds, even better, and if *japa* can be increased another 16 rounds without “breaking the camel’s back”, then of course that’s best.

My experience was that after 20 years of practice, I began chanting even more rounds on special occasions, like Janmāṣṭamī and Gaura Pūrṇimā. Gradually, just as the mango becomes more and more ripe simply by holding onto the tree, my sweet taste increased, my passions subsided (somewhat), and I eventually added another 16 rounds in the afternoon (so now I chant 48 daily).

Text 7 from Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī’s Upadeśāmṛta, The Nectar of Instruction, sums up the process:

*syāt kṛṣṇa-nāma-caritādi-sitāpy avidyā-*  
*pittopatapta-rasanasya na rocikā nu*  
*kintv ādarād anudinaṁ khalu saiva juṣṭā*  
*svādvī kramād bhavati tad-gada-mūla-hantrī*

**“The holy name, character, pastimes, and activities of Kṛṣṇa are all transcendentally sweet like sugar candy. Although the tongue of one afflicted by jaundice of avidyā \[ignorance\] cannot taste anything sweet, it is wonderful that simply by carefully chanting these sweet names everyday, a natural relish awakens within his tongue, and his disease is gradually destroyed at the root.”**

A final word of caution: in initial enthusiasm one shouldn’t overdo it. As young boys and girls often think they’re in love, but are actually only infatuated, one may feel that he can immediately increase his chanting. Over the years I’ve witnessed many such sentiments, but most of them did not endure.

So, one can be like a mango and hold onto the tree of the Holy Name through all experiences in life, and never give up chanting at least 16 rounds every day; for just as the mango receives nourishment simply by remaining connected to the tree, it will lose nutrients the moment it separates from the branch. We receive the mercy of Kṛṣṇa through chanting His holy name, but if we ever discontinue the practice, our spiritual nourishment will dry up and be replaced by His illusory potency.

## Śrīla Sacīnandana Swami

Sacīnandana Swami takes time every year for personal intensive *japa* retreats in various sacred and secluded spots in India and Europe. He also comes every year to Govardhana to organize and lead retreats for others. He was kind enough to visit me and share his deep-rooted thoughts on entering the holy name.

I personally like to think of chanting as an exercise that develops my relationship with the Divine Couple, Śrī Śrī Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. Chanting thus means to think of the person, or the relationship with that person, whose name you are chanting. At the beginning stage of chanting, however, when a sādhaka takes his *japa*-mālā into his hands, he sits down to confront what may be his fiercest enemy - the restless mind.

Ultimately, this mind can only be subdued by the beautiful Manohara, Lord Kṛṣṇa, who can attract the mind. But how will the sādhaka receive Kṛṣṇa in the form of His holy name if his mind always runs away from Kṛṣṇa to the material world? Only a purified mind will run away from the world and throw itself at the lotus feet of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. An impure mind, however, will run away from Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa to the attractive and unattractive things of this world.

If a devotee wishes to control his mind he must be like an airplane pilot who notices when his plane deviates from the course, being pushed astray by powerful winds. Without hesitation, and fixing his destination in the mind, the pilot will bring himself back on course. To help purify the mind, and steer it back to the right course, Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī, in his Hari-bhakti-vilāsa, gives six effective rules to perfect *japa.*

The Sanskrit verse that addresses this, ‘*manaḥ saṁskaraṇaṁ śaucam*’, which can be found in my book Gāyatrī, lists qualities one must cultivate in order to perfect one’s *japa*. First there is *manaḥ saṁskaraṇam*, the ability to control the mind: Lord Kṛṣṇa has talked about this practice in the Gītā where He says: “From wherever the mind wanders due to its flickering and unsteady nature, one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back under the control of the self.” This first practice means that when the mind runs away from the divine holy name, one should be honest and humble enough to bring it back. “Dear mind! Sir! Come back! Come back!”

How is one to do this? The easiest way to do it is to just look at the syllables of the mahā-mantra while one chants. This meditation is called mantra traktaka. Gaurakiśora dāsa Bābājī once dictated a letter to a disciple where he advised him to chant the name while simultaneously looking at the syllables. This type of visual meditation is a yogic practice. It is quite mystical. It is a difficult notion to explain by the standards of Western psychology, but it is an extremely effective method to fix the mind. After some time of simply chanting and reading the mantra simultaneously, the mind will become subdued and peaceful.

Next there is *śauca*, external and internal purity. Purity is important for a devotee. One should realize that when he sits down to chant, everything comes up—all the movies he saw, all the mundane literature he read, and all the furtive glances thrown at the opposite gender. It is like watching an uncoordinated, disturbing home-cut movie in the mind. So, while it is obvious that it is easier to sit down and chant or read when one is free of dust, sweat, and mud, not everyone is sufficiently aware that the dust, sweat, and mud of the mind, are even more contaminating.

The third quality is *mauna*, silence. *Prajalpa* pollutes the mind. It should be avoided in our Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement because the effect of it is so absolutely disturbing. Sanātana Gosvāmī says that of all the forms of *prajalpa*, criticizing Vaiṣṇavas is the worst. One should learn to be materially silent, but spiritually talkative. One should talk about Kṛṣṇa, and if one wants to enjoy drama or scandal, he can talk about the amazing Lord Nityānanda , who kicked His dear devotee in the chest. Talking about spiritual subjects purifies the mind.

The next quality is *mantrārtha-cintana*: contemplating the meaning of the *mantra*. A devotee cannot simply chant mindlessly because the idle mind will go back to thinking about *māyā*. One must therefore meditate in a certain mood: “O Rādhe! O Kṛṣṇa! Please engage me in Your service.” A beginner’s service may be related to the preaching mission. More advanced service is called *bhāva-sevā*, where in a loving mood one approaches Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa according to the directives in *Prārthanā*, Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura’s guidebook to devotion. One senior devotee confidentially told me, “When I chant I decorate the Lord’s divine form with flowers. This is the service I like to do during my chanting.” The point is to contemplate the meaning of the *mantra*. A devotee should make himself acquainted with the *mantra’*s meaning. For example, we have Gopāla Guru Gosvāmī’s explanation of the *mahā-mantra*:

The first “Hare”, he says, means “Hari, steal my mind and deliver me from the bondage of this material world.” Then “Kṛṣṇa” means “O Kṛṣṇa, attract my mind.” The next “Hare” means “Oh Hari, capture my mind with the sweetness of Your name, form, and pastimes.” And the forth name, “Kṛṣṇa” means “He Kṛṣṇa, O Kṛṣṇa, send me the association of Your devotees, who will purify my mind by instructing me about Your devotional service.” Then, “Kṛṣṇa” means “O Kṛṣṇa, give me unflinching faith in Your name, form, qualities, and pastimes.” Then “Kṛṣṇa”: “O Kṛṣṇa, give me a taste for Your name, form, qualities, and pastimes.” Then “Hare”: “O Hari, make me worthy to engage in Your service.” Then again “Hare”: “O Harā, Rādhā, order me personally in the service that You wish me to perform.”

*Avyagratva*, or patience, is another quality that needs to be cultivated. Don’t rush the practice. It is so unloving, so opposed to the mood of *bhakti*, to want to “finish your rounds.” Have you ever been with someone who you feel just wants to finish his meeting with you as quickly as possible? You say, “If this is so unimportant to you, then I will leave voluntarily. No need to delay. No need for you to end the meeting; I will end the meeting. I too have no interest in this meeting any longer.” Don’t rush your chanting because Kṛṣṇa might run away from your *japa* practice when He sees that you simply want to “finish it.” He might say, “I will leave you before you leave me completely.” Then you will be left only with your dry rounds.

The last quality is *anirveda*, steadfastness. This refers to persistence. “*A*” means “not,” and “*nirveda*” means “indifference,” “disregard”, or even “depression”. One should not lose hope, in other words, if the results of chanting don’t come immediately.

These are the six qualities that are meant to convince one that chanting is life’s mission. To chant is not something that a devotee does only when he or she sits down. When a devotee wants to chant properly he has to prepare himself in his life by adopting these traits.

I would now like to say something personal. For me, chanting means a sacred expedition with the goal of discovering more and more deeply that Kṛṣṇa’s name is non-different from Kṛṣṇa. Imagine going into an ancient building that has a most confidential, most private chamber where the house owner, your most beloved friend, is waiting for you. Before going into this chamber, however, you must first pass through that house’s many rooms. You know that the room in which your friend is staying is the most secret room, with a private entrance that is heavily guarded.

For me, the chanting of Hare Kṛṣṇa is an invitation to a great discovery. Before I can come into this most private room where I will have the most direct contact with the *nāmī*, or the Divine Couple, I will have to go through three halls. The guardian of the first hall will see if I am qualified to pass. This is the hall of śraddhā, or faith that the name is non-different from the named. The Padma Purāṇa, as quoted in the Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta, describes what the holy name is and how it is non-different from Kṛṣṇa, ‘*nāma cintāmaṇiḥ kṛṣṇaś caitanya-rasa-vigrahaḥ pūrṇaḥ śuddho nitya-mukto ’bhinnatvān nāma-nāminoḥ*’. *Śraddhā* must be there—this initial faith that “Yes, I am sitting down and actually associating with Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.” That is the first hall through which I have to pass.

Then the second hall comes. In this hall I must lovingly think about the meaning of the holy name. What am I doing? What am I addressing? Whom am I addressing? “Hare Kṛṣṇa! Please engage me in Your service. Please accept me, please accept me, please accept me!” There is no better prayer, no better call.

The third hall is the teaching of Mahāprabhu, the hall of chanting with a feeling of deep separation. Those who are materially satisfied in this world, who are not interested in changing the status quo of their lives, can only offer lip service to such a conception. Their chanting will only be lip deep, not heart deep. Only those whose hearts are softened by the feeling of separation, “I need You my Lord. I need You! You are My only necessity, O my Govinda, I need you!” Only those who have this mood can associate with the Lord through feelings of separation.

Having divine feelings of separation means that although Kṛṣṇa may be externally absent, by one’s feeling of longing, Kṛṣṇa is internally present in the purified heart. One should therefore chant while expressing lamentation as expressed in the fifth sloka of the *Śikṣāṣṭaka*:

“O My Lord, somehow or other I have fallen into this ghastly material ocean. I have no business here. I am thrown around by the waves of anger and lust and attacked by the crocodiles of my material impulses. Now please kindly take me out of this dreadful ocean and place me as one of the dust particles at Your lotus feet.”

When we have gone through these halls—first *śraddhā*, then awareness of the *mantra*’s meaning, then the expression of longing—if the holy name is mercifully inclined He might open the door to the inner chamber. If He thinks we should wait before He opens the door, we will wait. We will sit before that door. Sometimes we will feel a little mercy from our master coming through, maybe a few rays of light, maybe a nice fragrance from an incense stick, and we will know, “Yes, our Lord is there, our master is there. He is present. Therefore I am satisfied to sit before this door and chant. I will not chant loudly so as not to make much noise, but He will hear me and then when He thinks it is time to open the door, He will open it.”

Hare Kṛṣṇa

## Aindra dāsa

Aindra dāsa needs little introduction as a servant of the holy name. He has dedicated his life to *nāma-saṅkīrtana* at Śrī Śrī Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma Mandir in Vṛndāvana. We were able to speak with him inside his room there.

Love is a two way street. It is important to always remember that love is never a forced affair. Love is a matter of mutual attraction. There is a saying that “birds of a feather flock together”; or one might say, “love birds of a feather flock together.” So, it is not just a matter of our approach to the holy name, but rather it is a matter of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa being inspired to come to be with us to keep us company.

We can try to chant the holy name, but generally our endeavors will manifest as *nāmābhāsa*. But as far as *kīrtana* or *śuddha-nāma*-*bhajana* is concerned, when Kṛṣṇa is attracted to the sweetness of our heartfelt feelings toward Him, or our heart’s attraction toward Him, or our determination to get His attention, then He comes to see us. He comes to keep us company. He takes up residence in our heart, and He dances on our tongue. My experience is that chanting is not a matter of simply endeavoring to articulate some syllables. Actually, such chanting is not really chanting. Chanting looks like chanting, but actually chanting is Kṛṣṇa dancing. By His own sweet will He is making His divine descent from Goloka-*dhāma*, and dancing on our tongues. The holy name is a beautiful, sweetest-of-all-sweetest cowherd boy. He is a cowherd boy, a beautiful, blue cowherd boy, handsome and completely captivating to the heart. By the mercy of Hare, Rādhā, our hearts have become undeniably, intensely enchanted by the sweetness of that little cowherd boy Kṛṣṇa, and because we have become enchanted by Him, and He is aware of that, naturally, He won’t leave us alone. Again and again and again He pastures on our tongues, by forcing His way into our consciousness, dancing on our tongues. Practically, as if by an involuntary motion, *nāma* is again and again appearing on our tongues. It is then very difficult for us to forget *nāma*, because again and again *nāma* is appearing in our hearts and dancing on our tongues. So I can’t offer any idea concerning the methodology of chanting, how to focus the mind, how to increase attraction to the holy name or any such things beyond the simple fact that because Kṛṣṇa has captivated our hearts, and our hearts go to Him, naturally He reciprocates by again and again coming and pasturing on our tongues in various ways. He comes in the form of *nāma-saṅkīrtana*, in the form of our *nikuñja-sevā*, our *nāma-bhajana.*

Pārtha-sārathī dāsa Gosvāmī lives at Govardhana for three months a year in a small *āśRāma* that he established for his personal mediation. It is an ideal environment for *bhajana*. By his grace, I am often able to use his asRāma for my own *japa* meditation when he is not in Govardhana.

## His Holiness Pārtha-sārathī dāsa Gosvāmī

If one wants to absorb himself in the holy name, he should first understand the significance of chanting. Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī explains that out of the sixty-four *aṅgas* of the practice of *bhakti*, chanting the Lord’s holy name is first and foremost. The Bṛhan-nāradīya Purāṇa similarly stresses, ‘*harer nāma harer nāma harer nāma iva kevalam kalau nāsty eva nāsty eva nāsty eva gatir anyathā*’, “In this age of Kali there is no other means, no other means, no other means for self realization than chanting the holy name, chanting the holy name, chanting the holy name of Lord Hari.” Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura a has also explained in Jaiva Dharma, his most important book, that although they may not be always clearly manifested, all the other parts of devotional service are contained within the chanting of Hare Kṛṣṇa. Therefore, to advance in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, one must first try to develop one’s chanting, both in quality and in quantity.

Sixteen rounds is the minimum one should chant daily, but one should try to chant more and increase the number of rounds. Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura a has even written in *Caitanya-śikṣāmṛta* that the definition of a *madhyama-bhakta* is that he chants more and more, gradually increasing his chanting to three lakhs every day. That is on the beads. Off the beads, one should be chanting *nirantara*, which means chanting continuously, constantly, without cessation. So his definition of *madhyama-bhakta*, not even *uttama-bhakta*, is three lakhs a day! Devotees should not be discouraged when they hear these descriptions, but should also not be satisfied with 16 rounds, which is really a basic minimum. The main point is that devotees should try to increase the quantity of their chanting. Even in the early days Prabhupāda asked the devotees to chant more rounds. I think the story is quite well known. I believe it was in the summer of 1967 in San Francisco when Prabhupāda asked his senior disciple, Mukunda, to chant 64 rounds. When Mukunda Mahārāja responded that he couldn’t possibly do that, then Prabhupāda conceded, “Ok, 32 rounds.” And then eventually Prabhupāda said at least 16 rounds. So it is Śrīla Prabhupāda’s desire for his bona fide disciples to always increase their chanting. One should thus try to increase chanting as much as possible without neglecting other responsibilities, such as managerial or preaching duties, up to a lakh, which is 58 rounds, and even more.

The quality of chanting also has to increase. One way to do this is by meditating on Gaura-*līlā*. There is a natural progression in *japa* meditation where one starts his meditation on Gaura-*līlā* and works his way up to Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa-*līlā*. This is confirmed by Śrīla Prabodhānanda Sarasvatī in his verse *yathā yathā gaura-padāravinde* - that if one starts off in Gaura-*līlā*, dives into it deeply, one comes out in Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa-*līlā*. In this respect Śrīla Prabhupāda has even written in the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, *Ādi*-*līlā*, that one who is not sufficiently spiritually advanced should not worship Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa Deities. He then even goes so far as to say that one who is not sufficiently spiritually enlightened may not even chant Hare Kṛṣṇa. But in the next paragraph Prabhupāda explains that since the name of Gaurāṅga and Kṛṣṇa are the same, non-different, we should approach the name of Lord Caitanya, which is easier. He then concludes that as we are so fallen in this age of Kali, we should worship Nitāi-Gaurāṅga. So while chanting, one should try to have some thoughts on Gaura-*līlā*. Imagine the Deities of Nitāi-Gaurāṅga on the altar, or think of Pañca-tattva in Māyāpuraa. Meditate on Lord Caitanya dancing in Ratha-yātrā or in Gambhīra, where for 18 years He was deeply entering into the mood of Rādhārāṇī. One can think of Lord Caitanya dancing in the early morning as He walks along the bank of the river Ganges, or chanting in Śrīvāsāṅgana, or enlightening the animals in the forest of Jharikhaṇḍa - whatever pastime sticks in the mind. A devotee should pray that he may have thoughts of Gaura-*līlā*, especially thoughts of Lord Caitanya and Lord Nityānanda while he is chanting. Then gradually, without jumping, carefully one can try to think of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes. It should not be forced. It should be natural. So while chanting the Lord’s holy name one should pray to spontaneously have some thoughts of some pastimes. Also it is also very helpful in the neophyte stage to think of holy places, like visits to Vṛndāvana, *parikramā* around Govardhana Hill with Śrī Guru, Dīna Bandhu Prabhu, or some other exalted Vaiṣṇava, or dancing in front of the Pañca-tattva Deities in Māyāpura. A devotee can think of all these things when chanting the Lord’s holy name as well. This will help focus the intelligence on the holy name, which is very important for concentrated chanting.

It is also essential in chanting to understand *nāma-tattva*. When Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu visited Vṛndāvana, each morning he would come to the bank of the Yamunā and sit underneath an Imlitala (a tamarind tree) enjoying the breeze that was blowing while He would chant to Himself. This is mentioned by Śrīla Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja Gosvāmī in *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*. In the afternoon, however, He would preach. What would He preach? He would preach about the most important thing. What is the most important thing? The holy name. He would give people instruction, or *śikṣā*, on how to chant the holy name. Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura a has also confirmed in *Jaiva Dharma* that the most important knowledge that one can have is about the holy name or that which encourages one to chant the holy name. A devotee should read relevant scriptures that deal with the holy name, such as *Harināma*-*cintāmaṇi* by Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura. Chapters 23, 24, and 25 in *Jaiva Dharma* deal specifically with *nāmāparādhā*, *nāmābhāsa*, and *śuddha-nāma*. One should read these sections and know them by heart so that one can then apply that understanding to chanting. In this way, by such concerted effort to increase the quality, and also the quantity of chanting, *japa* gradually becomes more concentrated and pure.

Gaura premānande – hari haribol.

## Mahānidhi Swami

Mahānidhi Swami has a beautiful *āśRāma* near the most sacred Rādhā-kuṇḍa, which is only an hour walk from my usual residence at Govardhana. He has spent years in Vṛndāvana dedicating himself to *nāma-bhajana* and sharing his thoughts with others through the several books he has written on the subject. I visited his *āśrama* for a scheduled interview, and when I arrived, he handed me a short handwritten note that he felt exemplified his mood of entering into *bhajana.*

We chant the *mahā-mantra* in the mood of giving pleasure to Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa. We try to feel Their auspicious presence before us as we chant *japa*. In our heart we pray that Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa will be happy and satisfied with our repetition of Their divine Holy Names. Along with hearing and chanting, we often meditate on the following verse from *Pañca-Pradīpa*:

“I am Yours, O Lord of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī, as are my actions, mind, and words. O Śrīmatī Rādhikā – lover of Kṛṣṇa – I belong to You. Although I am such a fallen offender, please be merciful and make me your maidservant.”

Nityānanda dāsa is a student at the Bhaktivedanta Book Trust Sanskrit School at Govardhana. Whenever I am at Govardhana for a *japa* retreat, Nityānanda stays with me to assist me and to increase his own spiritual practices. I thought it would make an interesting addition to the book to hear a younger and newer voice share his thoughts on trying the enter the holy name.

## Nityānanda dāsa

I am just starting to enter into the cultivation of *nāma*. I feel humbled being asked to share my realizations. I have been chanting for so many years, but I can’t really say that it has been any kind of cultivation. It has just been an imitation. My chanting is still not real chanting, but recently three senior devotees that I am close with helped me get a glimpse of what it means to cultivate the holy name.

The main realization I received from them is to prepare the heart before chanting, for if the heart is prepared by surrender and humility, then naturally the mind will also become absorbed. Therefore, after bath and before any other spiritual activities, I start my morning by offering prostrated obeisances to all the *ācāryas*, beginning with my *dīkṣā-guru* and *śikṣā-gurus*. I then pray to all the major devotees and *ācāryas* of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava *sampradāya* in our line: Śrīla Prabhupāda, Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta, Śrīla Gaurakiśora and Śrīla Bhativinoda up to the six Gosvāmīs, then Īśvara Purī and Mādhavendra Purī and finally Śrī Śrī Gaura-Nitāi. I then pray to all of our main *dhāmas*, Navadvīpa, Jagannātha Purī, and Vraja-*dhāma*. Finally I pray to the personalities presiding in those places like Vṛndā Devī, Govardhana, Gaṅgā devī, Yamunā, and particularly Cakhaleśvara Mahādeva. I am now staying in Govardhan, so I especially pray to Cakhaleśvara to protect my consciousness from offenses. It takes me only about fifteen minutes to complete all these obeisances, but when I am finished my heart is much better prepared to accept the holy name with the proper attitude.

Continuously falling down on the floor to offer *daṇdavat-praṇāmas* has a powerful affect on the heart. It naturally inspires the feeling that “I am servant, such a small servant.” Then “I am such a small servant, but I have such great masters.” Finally the sense of complete shelter comes. Sometimes the reciprocation and experience is so tangible that one can even feel the presence of these personalities before one as if one was standing before one’s guru today.

Many *ācāryas* have sung the glories of this practice. In one song, Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura writes that for anyone who sincerely pays obeisances to these great personalities, the obstacles on the path of pure devotion will be removed and all spiritual desires fulfilled. I therefore always pray that I can maintain this practice for it has become my wealth.

There is story about the greatness of *daṇdavat-praṇāmas*. Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura a was leaving Māyāpura one day by car. It was during the rainy season so the road was full of dirt, knee deep with mud. A *gṛhastha* disciple standing by the side of the road noticed that his spiritual master was slowly driving by. This devotee fell down in the mud, offering full obeisances and completely ruining his clothes. Bhaktisiddhānta noticed him. He stopped the car, got out, and asked his disciple, “Why did you do this?” His disciple humbly replied with folded hands, “This obeisance is my only wealth.”

I was struck when I read this. “This obeisance is my only wealth.” So I was thinking, “How little wealth I have. I should also have some wealth like this by paying obeisances to these souls.”

It is especially enlivening to pray to those devotees that are particularly relevant in one’s life now. I am now studying Jīva Gosvāmī’s grammar, so naturally I feel particularly indebted to him. To express that debt I chant the following prayer to him everyday from the beginning of the commentary to his grammar that I am studying:

“Desiring the welfare of the short-lived men of this modern age, Jīva Gosvāmī, being compassionate, bestowed upon them this *Hari-nāmāmṛta-vyākaraṇa* after churning the great ocean of Sanskrit grammar. May he become my shelter.”

A close friend of mine shared with me a simple realization that also helped me enter even further into the cultivation of *nāma*. Often one addresses the Lord and His pure devotees with a prayer for purification, but one often becomes so much possessed with his own prayers that he forgets to just plainly thank those guardians for everything they have already done for him. My friend one day suggested to me that rather than just always asking for something for myself, why not also offer my gratitude? What he said struck me. I began to especially pray to Śrī Śrī Gaura-Nitāi to thank Them for Their mercy. That simple advice very much reformed my prayers. It led to perhaps to my deepest absorption in *nāma.*

One day at the end of my morning obesiances and prayers, while chanting, I began to pray in my mind to Mahāprabhu like my friend recommended - not asking for anything for myself, but just repeatedly thanking Him for all the chances that He has given me to chant the highest names of the Lord. Meditating on and praising Mahāprabhu really affected me. It was as if for the first time the names coming from my mouth had finally reached the ears of the Lord. Naturally a personal prayer, a resignation of my being to the Lord in surrender, came into my mind:

“Dear Gaurāṅga, I am completely Yours. I am Your property. Totally Yours. I am no one else’s. I want to be only Yours!”

I thought about this. Why was I feeling so close and so connected to the Lord that I was becoming convinced that He was finally hearing my prayers? When one fully realizes that he belongs to the Lord, he does not feel any distance between himself and his eternal master - either by mind, intelligence, or ego. At the time, a favorite Vaiṣṇava song written by Locana dāsa Ṭhākura called Avatāra Sāra came to mind, which further intensified my feelings:

“Why didn’t I worship You, oh Gaura-avatāra, who is the essence of all avatāras? I was standing submerged in water, but my thirst was never gone in spite of that. Instead I just become entangled more and more in my fruitive activities.”

I just kept on thinking, “I have not worshiped You, O Lord, O Gaurāṅga.” It was not just that I didn’t worship Him before I joined this Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement or something like that, but it was stronger than that. “I have not worshiped You for many, many lifetimes.” And that sentiment was so strong, that it generated a tangible feeling of remorse like a child in front of their parents, repenting for the bad deeds he did.

Although I was feeling very guilty, I also felt the sweet quality of His love and compassion, that Gaurāṅga Mahāprabhu was accepting me again, not because He was obliged or forced to, but because of His magnanimity and pure love. It helped me understand how insignificant I am and made me face the fact that I was often not acting properly in my relationships with others due to *ahaṅkāra* (egoism) and envy. I realized that I was even manipulating and criticizing people for their apparent shortcomings, although I am lower than everyone and have no right to do so.

Such experiences while chanting are tangible jewels in my memory. Why? Because by revealing my true position I am humbled. I hope that one day, by the mercy of the Lord and the Vaiṣṇavas, I can regularly chant the holy name with such sentiment as I did that day. I now try my best to serve the Vaiṣṇavas instead of criticizing them to get their favor, and to please them so that they will be happy, because they are worthy of worship.

This is the most important thing that helps me become absorbed in the holy name – the mood of humility and remorse that comes by submission to the Vaiṣṇavas. If by their grace one gets a sense of his need for mercy, his mind will never become distracted from the holy name.

## Bhūrijana dāsa

**On Improving My** ***Japa***

Bhūrijana Prabhu has been living in Vṛndāvana for over 20 years organizing seminars, teaching *śāstra*, and deeply practicing spiritual life. When I asked him if I could interview him, he kindly wrote a short essay for my book called “On Improving My *Japa*.”

When members of the Kauravas’ assembly attempted to strip Draupadī naked, she called out to Kṛṣṇa in great distress. Kṛṣṇa immediately appeared, supplied her with unlimited sari cloth, and saved her from full humiliation.

What caused Kṛṣṇa to promptly rescue Draupadī? Why doesn’t Kṛṣṇa similarly reciprocate with me when I chant?

Of course Draupadī is a great devotee and I’m not. That clearly answers my question. Unfortunately, that answer doesn’t offer much in the way of advice on how I can improve my *japa*; Śrīla Prabhupāda’s comment on one of Queen Kuntī’s prayers fortunately does. Kuntī Devī advises:

*janmaiśvarya-śruta-śrībhir, edhamāna-madaḥ pumān*  
*naivārhaty abhidhātuṁ vai, tvām akiñcana-gocaram*

**“My Lord, Your Lordship can easily be approached, but only by those who are materially exhausted. One who is on the path of \[material\] progress, trying to improve himself with respectable parentage, great opulence, high education and bodily beauty, cannot approach You with sincere feeling.” (Bhag. 1.8.26)**

Śrīla Prabhupāda comments:

“Being materially advanced means taking birth in an aristocratic family and possessing great wealth, an education and attractive personal beauty. All materialistic men are mad after possessing all these material opulences, and this is known as the advancement of material civilization. But the result is that by possessing all these material assets one becomes artificially puffed up, intoxicated by such temporary possessions. Consequently, such materially puffed up persons are incapable of uttering the holy name of the Lord by addressing Him feelingly, ‘O Govinda, O Kṛṣṇa.’ It is said in the *śāstras* that by once uttering the holy name of the Lord, the sinner gets rid of a quantity of sins that he is unable to commit. Such is the power of uttering the holy name of the Lord. There is not the least exaggeration in this statement. Actually the Lord's holy name has such powerful potency. But there is a quality to such utterances also. It depends on the quality of feeling. A helpless man can feelingly utter the holy name of the Lord, whereas a man who utters the same holy name in great material satisfaction cannot be so sincere.”

Śrīla Prabhupāda clearly articulated the principle that caused Draupadī’s chanting to bring such dRāmatic results: Her calling out to Kṛṣṇa was without pride, focused, and enriched with her feelings of full dependence. Draupadī had fully taken shelter of Kṛṣṇa in helplessness through His holy name.

Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura in his Gītāvali prescribes similar ideals in a song he recommended be sung each day at dawn:

*jīvana anitya jānaha sār, tāhe nānā-vidha vipada-bhār,*  
*nāmāśraya kori’ jatane tumi, thākaha āpana kāje*

**“You should understand this essential fact: life is temporary and filled with various kinds of misery. Therefore, carefully take shelter of the holy name and remain always engaged in His service as your eternal occupation.”**

*jīvera kalyāna-sādhana-kām, jagate āsi’ e madhura nām,*  
*avidyā-timira-tapana-rūpe, hṛd-gagane virāje*

**“Desiring to shower benedictions upon all living entities, this sweet name of Kṛṣṇa has descended to this material universe and shines like the sun in the sky of the heart, destroying the darkness of ignorance.”**

*kṛṣṇa-nāma-sudhā koriyā pān, juḍāo bhakativinoda-prān,*  
*nāma vinā kichu nāhiko āra, caudda-bhuvana-mājhe*

**“Drink the pure nectar of the holy name of Kṛṣṇa and thus satisfy the soul of Bhaktivinoda. Except for the holy name there is nothing within all the fourteen worlds.” (Aruṇodaya Kīrtana, verses 7-9)**

Indeed, we have heard that Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura followed his own prescription. He would chant his own *japa* loudly in Godruma-dvīpa, early, long before dawn each morning. His chanting was far from mechanical, but had the quality of calling out as if from afar to a person, to Kṛṣṇa.

Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, as did Draupadī, took shelter of Kṛṣṇa through His holy names. But when I chant, my mind endlessly flickers from maintaining to securing to adjusting any of many of the aspects of my life. Why should Kṛṣṇa immediately reciprocate? Draupadī was serious in her calling out; I am distracted. Draupadī took shelter of Kṛṣṇa; I take shelter of my mind.

Or stated from a different perspective, Kṛṣṇa is perfectly reciprocating with my diffused attention, my lack of urgency in developing my relationship with Him. As I am casual about approaching Him, He similarly remains “just now coming”.

Even if I can do no better than carrying a mood of lamenting the quality of my *japa* into the arena of my distracted chanting, if I sincerely beg Kṛṣṇa to help me, He will help. Immediately. But all will depend upon my sincere desire to improve, my sincere need to develop my relationship with Kṛṣṇa, my helplessness. As Śrīla Prabhupāda had commented on Kuntī’s words, “It depends on the quality of feeling.” (Bhag. 1.8.26, Purport)

Nirañjana Swami is a very busy and respected spiritual leader in Śrīla Prabhupāda’s mission, but he always tries to make time for pilgrimage to Govardhana where he stays at the Bhaktivedanta Sādhana Āśrama to dedicate his time to the holy name. I paid a visit to Nirañjana Swami for the following interview.

## Nirañjana Swami

Especially throughout the last year, due to illness, I have given more time to chanting and meditating on the holy name. I have carefully considered the different stages of chanting. Although one should certainly aspire to purely chant the holy name of the Lord in full remembrance of Kṛṣṇa, for most that stage is only an aspiration, not a practical level of advancement. Devotees, therefore, need to adopt a reasonable means to purify the heart for the mind to be fixed in full recollection of the Lord’s pastimes. Of course, the best process is properly chanting the holy name itself.

Purification manifests to the degree one opens the heart to the holy name. I thus find it very helpful that along with endeavoring to remember the Lord’s form, qualities, and pastimes, to meditate on my exclusive dependence upon the holy name. Śrīla Prabhupāda often gave an example to show how such purification works. As an iron rod in fire makes the iron rod hotter and hotter, similarly by coming in contact with Kṛṣṇa in the form of His name, form, qualities, and pastimes, one’s mind becomes more and more purified. Therefore, for the holy name to cleanse the mind one has to open his heart fully to the holy name.

The consciousness that I have thus been trying to cultivate while chanting is the feeling of unworthiness to chant the holy name purely, the feeling of inability to be fully engaged in devotional service, and the feeling of insufficiency in love for Kṛṣṇa. It is just like the mood described by Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī in the Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta. He describes that the characteristics of a devotee are that he always feels that he doesn’t have enough of Kṛṣṇa’s mercy, that he doesn’t do enough to serve Kṛṣṇa, and that he doesn’t have enough love for Kṛṣṇa. A humble devotee is thus feeling the need more and more for Kṛṣṇa’s mercy, His service, and ultimately for devotion itself.

That feeling of inadequacy, however, should not be done in a mood of self-indulgence. It shouldn’t come to the point where one is thinking himself so unqualified and fallen that he is focused only on himself; but unless one can properly relate to the concept of being fallen, how can one feel the need for mercy?

A devotee should think that the Lord is very kind, more merciful than one can imagine and because of that mercy He is manifested in His name. One should meditate on verses like *kali-kāle nāma-rūpe kṛṣṇa-avatāra nāma haite haya sarva jagat nistāra*, “The Lord incarnates in this age of Kali in the form of His name and anyone who associates with this name is associating with Kṛṣṇa directly and certainly becomes delivered.”

In this way, the Lord is so merciful that He kindly enters the heart to purify His devotee, but first one must consider himself so much in need of that mercy, and so dependent on Him that the Lord is drawn to reciprocate those sincere feelings. As Śrīla Prabhupāda would say, one should chant as a child sincerely crying out to attract his mother’s mercy. Before meditating on different aspects of the Lord’s pastimes and features while chanting, I try to absorb myself for a period of time in meditating on how much I am in need of mercy. I look at all my inadequacies and the unwanted things that are within my heart, and realize that unless I can get mercy I have no hope.

Another very important point is that the holy name is more effective when chanted with faith. I often use the example that because the holy name has been given to us through the lips of Kṛṣṇa’s pure devotee, it is imbued with all the necessary potency to manifest *kṛṣṇa-prema*, pure love of God. I have that deep confidence, the firm faith in Śrīla Prabhupāda’s empowerment to deliver the holy name - that he is *kali kalera dharma kṛṣṇa-nāma-saṅkīrtana kṛṣṇa-śakti vinā nahe tāra pravartana*. That confidence is a most essential ingredient for the mantra to work. Without faith that the holy name is everything, that the holy name can clean my heart, that the holy name can bestow full mercy upon me, it is very difficult to focus the mind on the holy name with confidence and thus consistently. Therefore I need to not only chant carefully, but I need to hear the glories of the holy name from Śrīla Prabhupāda and other advanced Vaiṣṇavas. I need to read and hear about those glories repeatedly as described in the revealed scriptures and from the mouths of the Vaiṣṇavas and to discuss those glories with others. There is no other way to taste the sweetness of the Lord’s holy name.

This type of meditation is definitely absorbing. It instills within me humility and a genuine feeling of repentance. In turn, such contrition awakens a deep heartfelt impetus to take shelter in the holy name. It is difficult to just go on thinking about one’s inadequacies without taking refuge in the remedy, the holy name. I also have faith that not only is the holy name extremely merciful, but it can also forgive me. That hope instills further faith. Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī says that the holy name is even more merciful than the form of Kṛṣṇa because it is the shelter where one seeks forgiveness. The holy name thus gives hope and confidence and stimulates the type of emotions that inspire a strong need to chant more and more. This state of helpless dependency is the best catalyst for deeper meditation.

In other words, by first looking at my own inadequacies and realizing my utter dependence on the holy name, my chanting automatically becomes meaningful because I can see a direct connection between my chanting and my deepest needs. That deep desire in the heart for shelter is thus the main relevant feeling that is immediately accessible to me when chanting. Although I pray to the previous *ācāryas*, and Śrīla Prabhupāda, and the Vaiṣṇavas, - certainly they are all accessible by calling them - the mercy of Kṛṣṇa, and Kṛṣṇa Himself, is manifested most fully and completely in His name. I just wrote to a disciple today about this very subject. It is very short, but very relevant. Let me share it with you. My disciple hasn’t been chanting her rounds, and now she is chanting six rounds steadily. She asks me if I could push her. This is my answer:

“More than three months have passed since your request for a push. Although I did not push by letter, I hope that you might have heard that since March when I began my leave of absence I began chanting a minimum of thirty-two rounds a day. I continue to this day. Some of my disciples have told me that upon learning about this they happened to become more committed to their rounds. Either way, although it is my duty to push, my preferred way of pushing others is by first pushing myself. So now that I have done that I will encourage you that I am happy that you have committed yourself to six rounds, and I will push you by telling you that you should first increase your effort to make these six rounds, if it is still six or more, then all the better, more attentive. Chanting should be performed with some feeling, whether the emotion is hankering for Kṛṣṇa, remorse, repentance, eagerness to chant more attentively, remembrance of our favorite pastime or whatever. When there is some feeling our chanting becomes less mechanical and more spontaneous. Try to capture these moments of chanting with feeling and take note of what you were thinking about when some feeling came to you. Repeat your chanting in the same way and gradually the feeling will become more natural. And along with that will come a more natural desire to increase your chanting.”

One of the previous *ācāryas* has said that one has to feel himself to be the negative, and if he feels himself to be helpless and in need of shelter, then the positive comes to compensate and brings him up. I am inspired by that concept - that Kṛṣṇa comes down and lifts up His devotee because a devotee is not demanding, and does not say, “Kṛṣṇa you must do this.” Rather, the devotee helplessly calls, “I am totally dependent on Your mercy, although I am unqualified for it.” Kṛṣṇa then sees that his devotee is not conditional, but helpless, and being the complete positive entity, He comes down to the position of the negative, the state of inadequacy, and lifts the devotee to a higher state of consciousness. That mood of utter helplessness should always be kept at every stage of a devotee’s advancement in Kṛṣṇa consciousness for as soon as one thinks that he is in any way advanced one’s sense of dependence quickly diminishes. One also naturally loses his sense of gratitude.

Gratitude is very important. It naturally stimulates one to serve, and to feelingly chant Kṛṣṇa’s name. One should feel grateful for the smallest realization, the smallest reciprocation from the holy name. A good example of gratitude is Uddhava in his discussion with Vidura. When Vidura realized that Kṛṣṇa was thinking about him before He departed, he became completely overwhelmed with gratefulness. It was such a small thing, but not a small thing for Vidura. So in the same way, a devotee should feel so grateful that Kṛṣṇa has appeared in His name to purify him. When gratitude is awakened then the impetus to call out Kṛṣṇa’s name with love becomes natural. I am so grateful that Kṛṣṇa is accessible in His name and that any time I can call out His name, under any circumstances, He will manifest. It is a simple realization, but to me it is the most profound, that Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Lord, is so kind that He extends Himself to His devotees in such an accessible form.

It is so important to hear and discuss these types of realizations and to chant seriously. If the only reason one chants is the obligation to do it, then how long will one be able to carry on? Devotees need to go deeper than “I am obligated to chant” or it will be very difficult to keep up that commitment. There is a need to go beyond thinking that success in chanting is simply “I have finished 16 rounds.” That’s why I always tell my disciples who are having difficulty finishing their 16 rounds, “Better you chant less rounds attentively than chant 16 just for the sake of completing them.”

## Vaiśeṣika dāsa

Vaiśeṣika Prabhu is another dedicated servant of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s mission who religiously visits the Bhaktivedanta Āśrama at Govardhana during Kārttika for concentrated chanting and hearing. Because he is at the forefront of distributing Kṛṣṇa consciousness to others, I asked him to explain the relationship between giving spiritual life to others and the development of one’s own spiritual practice.

It is a principle of Kṛṣṇa consciousness that the more you give, the more you get. Anyone that has sincerely done *saṅkīrtana* has experienced this - that entering into the spirit of giving the holy name very much helps one in his own personal chanting.

Some years back we started the practice on book distribution of asking people if they were interested in learning an ancient prayer. Those interested would be shown a card with the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra and asked to repeat it. Doing that simple exercise had a profound affect on my *japa*. After awhile I just couldn’t stop thinking, “You give mercy, you get mercy.” I thought of the description of the *Pañca-tattva* in the *Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta*; how they plundered the storehouse of love of Godhead, but the more they ate and distributed, the more the supply increased hundreds of times.

Hearing people chant for the first time, when they are chanting so innocently, is the hearing of a very pure and offense-less name, one inspired by Lord Nityānanda . And when we are not only distributing that name, but also hearing from others who are doing the same, then the mantra especially echoes powerfully in our hearts. One then becomes inspired to carry it back and distribute it to others. In this way there is such a strong correlation between the distribution of the holy name and taste for the holy name.

One of the most important things we teach about effective book distribution is the importance of purity based on personal *sādhana*. *Sādhana* means chanting for no other reason than for self-purification. Therefore the most essential technique for distributing books is to keep the holy name going constantly in one’s mind. Doing so also helps one to transcend the mind, which is essential for deepening spiritual practice. The mind always wants to accept and reject. Especially it can do that intensely when we are forced out in public, meeting strangers and trying to give them books. The mind will whimsically tell us, “That person will take. That person won’t take. That person is qualified. That person is not qualified.” And there are all kinds of fears that arise from egoism like, “I might fail,” or, “They will reject me,” which only causes one to hesitate in one’s duties. The only remedy is the constant chanting of the holy name. Anyone who does that kind of yoga, who keeps chanting the holy name of the Lord, quickly realizes how much chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa brings one to the transcendental platform and deepens one’s faith in the holy name.

*Saṅkīrtana* also helps one to value devotional service more. It is a fact that when people maintain a particular lifestyle, an environment, they cherish it more when someone else appreciates their world. People tend to have a passive relationship with their own surroundings. When they see what they have, however, by way of another’s eyes, for example a visitor who expresses appreciation for their garden or interior decoration, it naturally awakens their own appreciation for what they have. Similarly for devotees, when they bring their practice outside and share it with others, and those people appreciate the holy name or our books, that devotee naturally appreciates those things more. For example, there is a newer member of our congregation who goes out and distributes books. By going out he is now becoming inspired to now read all of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s books. If you sell a book and somebody becomes excited about getting it, then you begin to think, “What is in there? Let me read it myself.” In addition, seeing Kṛṣṇa consciousness through the eyes of others also helps us to also realize ways in which we need to improve and increase our service.

Because book distribution is such a demanding and serious service, most devotees who do it for a while realize at some point that it requires some kind of *śakti* to sustain the practice, as well as do it effectively. Just as top athletes are always looking for some way in which they can empower their effort, the devotee who is dedicated to *saṅkīrtana* is always seriously looking for ways to preach more effectively. There are all kinds of sales techniques that one can use to sell things, but a *saṅkīrtana* devotee inevitably comes to the conclusion that the key to preaching more effectively is the spiritual potency born of chanting the holy name. *Saṅkīrtana* is thus a great tool and impetus in every way to take shelter of the holy name.

Śrīmatī dāsī spent years distributing the holy name around Māyāpura in West Bengal and has recently settled in Vṛndāvana for concentrated *nāma*-*bhajana*. During a visit to Govardhana, she was happy to share some of her realizations about deepening one’s *japa* meditation.

## Śrīmatī dāsī

The main obstacle to entering into *nāma*’s all-merciful, powerful and very loving association, is that we chant without taste. Our chanting has no life. Unfortunately, a primary symptom of such chanting is that we chant inattentively. We are not focused on *nāma* because we have no attraction for Him, and so the mind is distracted by so many other things. And that means we are not watering the *bhakti-lata*, but instead the weeds that will choke our devotional creeper. One may well ask, “Isn’t it better not to chant than to chant inattentively?” But perhaps a better question would be, “What do you do about inattentive chanting?” Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura gives a number of answers, but the main thing he says is, “Perform *nāma*-*saṅkīrtana*. It is the best cure.” The reason is that not only do you have wonderful Vaiṣṇavas around you who are supporting your attempt to hear and chant, but *nāma*-*saṅkīrtana* builds faith in the heart. The main reason why we can’t chant *nāma* with attention is because we don’t really have faith in Him, and so we give Him a back seat in our lives. When faith in *nāma* grows stronger through the association of others in *nāma*-saṅkīrtana, then our individual *nāma*-bhajana also becomes so much more focused and dynamic.

It is important to understand that how you live your life obviously has a lot to do with how successfully you will be able to enter the world of *nāma*. What do you do before you take rest? What is your state of consciousness? If you are in the habit of being more introspective and more prayerful before you take rest - singing *bhajanas*, *aṣṭakas*, and so on - then your consciousness will be on a higher level when you rise in the morning. And if the first thing you do upon rising is beg for the mercy of guru, the *ācāryas*, and the Vaiṣṇavas and sing the glories of *nāma*, you will easily be able to enter your *bhajana* in the mood of a humble, enthusiastic servant. Otherwise, one generally has to deal with the moods of the mind, which is so dry and frustrating.

We want to deal with the feelings of the transcendental soul, of our real heart. And that’s a whole different realm. We can only do that by entering into *nāma*’s realm on His terms, which means, “I am Your servant, I want to reestablish my relationship with You.” But not just Kṛṣṇa’s servant – also the servant of His servants. Meditation upon the fifth verse of *Śikṣāṣṭaka* makes that clear. Śrī Caitanya prays to become a speck of dust at the Lord’s lotus feet; and there are so many *śuddha*-*nāma bhaktas* already at His feet. Actually, that is where we belong; in their association at His feet. It’s not so easy to find a *śuddha*-*nāma* bhakta, but our begging to the holy name should be for that; association with souls who are dedicated to *nāma*. In this way, it is not just a matter of engaging the ear, tongue, or the mind. The heart has to also be involved in the process of *bhajana*. If the attitude is right, and the heart is yearning, then a pure greed born of intense desire will certainly fulfill our spiritual desires, by *nāma*’s grace.

It is so important to have strong faith in *nāma*. Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura describes why in a nutshell: “There are only two things of substance in this world; the *jīva* and *nāma*. Nothing else.” Everything is an expansion of *nāma* and His potencies. Nothing exists outside of *nāma* – not even us! If we have firm faith in this, entering into *nāma*’s world as His humble servant is not such a big problem.

A most essential thing to realize if we want to enter deeply into *nāma*-*bhajana* is that *nāma* is a person with deep emotions. And the realm of *nāma* can only be entered through purity and depth of heart. In the first verse of the *Śikṣāṣṭaka*, Mahāprabhu shows us how to achieve such purity - simply through the offenseless chanting of the holy name. When that verse talks of chanting, however, it is naturally also talking about carefully hearing the holy name.

It is important to remember that we have no potency to clean the heart ourselves. We are completely dependent on *nāma* to clean it. We thus have to become cooperative servants of the holy name. The first qualification of a good servant is to be a good listener; to listen to what the master wants. By absorbing ourselves in the sound vibration of the holy name, *nāma* will enter the heart and do His work. *Ceto-darpaṇa-mārjanam*. So this is the most essential thing, the first point. When we talk about *śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ smaraṇam*, *śravaṇam* is the essence. But it must be done with a genuine yearning of the heart. In other words, we have to deeply aspire to listen to what our master wants, to what He is saying. *Nāma* is speaking. He has something to say. What is He saying? Listen carefully. Then our chanting will be attentive. It will be dynamic and alive.

We also have to understand clearly that *nāma*, according to the second verse of the *Śikṣāṣṭaka*, means *nāma* and all His potencies. “All potencies” doesn’t just mean spiritual potencies. It also means His material potencies. So when we chant *nāma*, if we listen carefully to Him, we are connecting with the internal realm. If when we chant, we listen to the mind, then we are connecting with the material potencies. And naturally we will get the corresponding result. This is one of the essential things we have to remember if we really want to achieve *nāma*. If we want to link with Him in the internal realm, we have to enter into our chanting as His servants, not as mind’s servants. The mind’s service will simply entangle us – not so satisfying, not so pleasant. A very practical way to help loosen the mind’s hold on us is to always remember that one who has a taste for issues has no taste for *nāma*, and one who has a taste for *nāma* has no taste for issues.

So I have shared a few thoughts I feel are essential to understand and remember if we really want to enter into the realm of *nāma-bhajana*. But what is the heart of all this? What is the essence of the essence? In conclusion, that is what I would now like to share: Never underestimate the mercy of Patita-pavana Gaura (Gaura, who is the savior of the fallen) and Adoṣa-daraśī Nitāi (Nitāi, who does not see your faults). Accept Their Lotus Feet as your life and soul, and they will keep your heart and *bhajana* in line, and everything – both spiritual and material – will work out just fine.

## Keśava Bhāratī dāsa Gosvāmī

Over the years, what I find most important in attentive chanting is to focus on where I vibrate the name from. When I watch devotees chant, or when I watch myself chant, I sometimes chant from the mind or even sometimes from the lips. But what I have found most effective is to vibrate the name deeply within the throat and then to bring that sound vibration to where the soul resides - the heart. By vibrating the name deeply in this way, to the point where the name is coming from - the heart itself, one can feel the presence of the name throughout one’s being. From that position it’s naturally much easier to concentrate without distraction and to taste the pleasure of chanting. Just the pleasure of chanting itself is a wonderful experience and hopefully that will be the platform where I can more and more think of the form, qualities, and eventually the pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. I tend to be a heart person, so chanting for me means developing my personal relationship with the name by chanting it as intimately as possible. And this is the way I find most effective. I vibrate the name deeply in my vocal chords, feel that vibration spreading throughout my body, my being, and then draw that sound into my heart so that my chanting is centered directly from there.

By Śrīla Prabhupāda’s grace there are many dedicated servants of the holy name around the world. In order to narrow my focus, I decided to limit these interviews to those devotees who visit or reside at Govardhana or in Vṛndāvana for regular extended periods of *nāma*-*bhajana*. The interviews in this book have been kindly transcribed by Sudevī dāsī, a disciple of Rādhānātha Mahārāja, who attends the Bhaktivedanta College at Radhadesh. After reading the interviews, she became inspired to transcribe a few of her spiritual master’s lectures on *nāma*-*bhajana* and share them with me. I couldn’t resist sharing one of them here.

## Rādhānātha Swami

\[…\] As we approach Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa reciprocates and awards us accordingly. There are different types of people who offer different rituals for different purposes. And they may be doing the same thing, but they may be getting very different results. One person may be offering arati for prestige, and that person may get prestige – but that’s all. Another person may be performing a *yajña* for the purpose of getting material benedictions, and that person may get some material benedictions. Another, however, is doing religious activity with the sincere desire to cleanse and purify his heart to awaken his love for God. This is the supreme occupation for all humanity and the real essence of all spiritual practices. If our rituals are performed with that aim in mind, like a vehicle they will actually help to transport our consciousness to the supreme destination.

Religious rituals are like a package: if you give someone a gift in a nice package, but inside the package is something horrible, how will that please them? But if you offer a nice package with a fragrant rose inside, or something the person always wanted, then the package has served its purpose. It’s the content of the package which is all-important. Even when we are chanting the holy names – what is important is our motivation. The *māyāvādīs* also do *kīrtana* and chant *japa*, but their motive is to become liberated or to become one with God. But Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu teaches that the consciousness of a devotee is *na dhanam na janam* - essentially, “I do not want wealth, I do not want the pleasures of the opposite sex, I don’t want fame and followers. I don’t even want liberation from suffering. My deep desire is only to be the servant of the servant of your servant, unconditionally, birth after birth after birth.” Whatever ritual or whatever spiritual act we perform, if this is our consciousness, as Śrī Caitanya has taught, then we can please Him the most and attract His blessings.

\[…\] If we are eager and greedy to serve and please the Lord, then the Lord reciprocates with that eagerness. And that enthusiasm, that eagerness, is the difference between a hollow ritual and a genuine act of devotion, although both may have the same external form. If we are just chanting *japa*, which is the recommended process in this age for self-purification, but we are chanting the holy names of the Lord inattentively without really striving to please the Lord, then our *japa* is very much like a ritual. We are just trying to get our rounds done. The result? We get our rounds done. But Śrīla Prabhupāda says that with our lips we should be chanting, with our ears we should be hearing, and with our heart we should be crying out for the Lord like the baby crying for its mother. That’s not an empty ritual, but a very heartfelt act, as the mother is the child’s only shelter.

Whatever act of devotional service we perform, if we are attentive to it and enthusiastic about actually purifying our heart and pleasing Kṛṣṇa, then that act will have tremendous substance. And what is that substance? It is the empowerment and mercy of God.

We can recite prayers of great personalities, and that is purifying, but it is even more purifying when we actually try to enter into the mood of their prayer. There are so many beautiful stories: Gajendra, when he was on the verge of death, he was not ritualistically offering prayers, it was from the core of his heart; Prahlāda Mahārāja, when he offered his prayers; Kuntī devī, when she offered her prayers; Prabhupāda’s prayers on the Jaladuta; Also the prayers by Bhaktivinoda and Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura – We should try to understand the content and the feeling in which they are offering their prayers! – All are eager to access the mercy of the Lord and to give their hearts to the Lord through that activity. That is the content of their genuine prayers.

\[…\] When Kṛṣṇa went to Hastināpura, Duryodhana had a magnificent feast, a royal reception for Kṛṣṇa, but He was not satisfied because Duryodhana had a personal motive in receiving Him. Kṛṣṇa then went to the house of Vidura, who didn’t even expect Him. There was nothing prepared. Kṛṣṇa was offered only some bananas, but He was millions of times happier with that offering because the intent of the offering was loving devotion.

In the beginning of our spiritual path we follow *vaidhi-bhakti* - certain rituals, certain rules, certain regulations for our purification. But when there is a proper philosophy, a proper science behind it, then it is all very meaningful. \[…\]

*Bhakti* is a science based on a very deep, profound, and all encompassing philosophy of how to awaken our love of God and to express our intent to serve and to please Him. Now we may be chanting the holy names like a ritual, but if we just have the right enthusiasm, the right intent, then that ritual becomes something deeply spiritual. If it is done under the proper guidance, with the proper purpose, it purifies our heart and gradually a genuine spiritual experience awakens from within. Śrī Caitanya taught that love of God is dormant within the heart of everyone and by the nine processes of devotional service, especially hearing and chanting about the Lord in the association of those who are eager to serve the Lord, that love within our heart awakens. \[…\]

\[…\] In this age of Kali-yuga, the most powerful of all spiritual activities is the chanting of the names of God. Kali-yuga has so many impediments, so many disqualifications, but there is one benediction: simply by chanting the Lord’s name sincerely one can attain the perfection of liberation. What was possible in Satya-yuga through meditation for long durations of time, in Treta-yuga through ritualistic performances and sacrifice, in Dvāpara-yuga through worshiping the Deity in the temple, that same perfection can be achieved in Kali-yuga simply through chanting the holy names.

What makes chanting the holy names a very powerful and effective offering to the Lord is our sincerity. It is the sincerity of our intent that gives life to our spiritual activities and our worship, and brings us to the most sublime spiritual experience, the *puruṣārtha-śiromaṇi*. Śrī Caitanya has come to give us this *puruṣārtha-śiromaṇi*, the crest jewel of all goals, which is *ānandāmbudhi-vardhanam* - the highest pleasure of unmotivated love for the Supreme Lord. In that mood, all practices, even those which may appear apparently ritualistic, become purely spiritual.
