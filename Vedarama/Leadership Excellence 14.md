# Leadership Excellence 14

## Quote of the Week

If your preaching work is strong, then your management of temple affairs will also become automatically very strong. Just like if the head wills it, the hand will move. Preaching is like the head of our Krishna consciousness Society - if the head is removed, the whole body dies. Managing is the hands, which work nicely if the head is healthy. If the hands are removed, the body will not die, but it will be crippled. So preaching is more important than management, but both must be there if the whole body is to operate nicely.

\- Srila Prabhupada

## A Basic Leadership Principle, Part 2

By Isana Gauranga Dasa

*Isana Gauranga Dasa is developing Bhakti Vriksa programs in Penang, Malaysia.*

The 7 core activities mentioned in the previous article (in VE13) meant for self-discipline and sacrifice also include Srila Prabhupada's mission of bringing the mercy of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu to this needy world. One becomes a parent to 8 potential devotees (see point 6 of the core activities), and just as a parent would want to be the best example of a human being to his/her children, one, automatically, would elevate one's self spiritually to set example to the 8. How? By again going back to the core activities.

Part of self-discipline is self organization. In the early days, Srila Prabhupada demonstrated to his fresh band of disciples, even in a simple matter as cooking, that one must be organized to be effective. One couldn't get a group of a more disorganized individuals, and yet Srila Prabhupada forged them, through his example of self-discipline and organization, into a team of organized leaders that would manage a worldwide movement in a short time.

Therefore, through Srila Prabhupada's example we can see that one needs to be self-organized before one can organize his team to achieve anything. This means setting one's priorities and carrying them out in a timely manner, thus one profits from them rather than letting things become crises where one has to hurriedly cover them and not gain optimally. A simple example is chanting. I have noticed that when I finish my rounds, chanting clearly, in the morning, I feel a sense of achievement, having completed one of the 7 core activities, I am well prepared for the temptations that illusory energy intends to throw my way for the day and I feel a sense of a spiritual cleansing. I also feel free and satisfied, like a person who has properly prepared and is satisfied with his performance at an examination.

All these positive feelings allow me to face the day with the right mindset. This I had started since 1997, following the advice of Banu Swami who said that the 16 rounds should be completed by 9AM every morning. Before that I used to complete my rounds late in the night. The day was spent in anxiety that I had rounds to complete. One thing or another came and the rounds got pushed to late in the day, when I did them in a crisis mode (of course poor in quality). I used to snatch moments in the day to do one round here or there. At the end of the day I finished them with relief and guilt, knowing well that such type of chanting doesn't really qualify me for the mercy of Guru and Gauranga. Srila Prabhupada got much done because he set his priorities and executed them at the right time.

Next, one should not schedule his time in such a way that he cannot be flexible for the unexpected. This happens when one is a leader and has to interact with people. Although Srila Prabhupada had a tight schedule, he planned it so that in the eventuality of surprise visits by important dignitaries he would still be able to meet them for about half an hour without upsetting his schedule. Another example was his plans to expand were not affected by the unexpected Juhu Beach Temple crisis.

In the early days, Srila Prabhupada was the only leader and his novices were learning leadership principles from him. At that time, he took to developing and expanding one temple at a time. Only when a temple was up and going steady would he go on to another. He never gave his leaders more than one project at a time. Therefore this is another important self-discipline principle, one project at a time. There is much proof of this principle in the Temple Chowpatti Mumbai.

Developing systems that work for you is another point in self-organisation. Srila Prabhupada setup systems that were like extensions of himself, even where he could not be. One was to do his translations when everyone was retired and he did not allow anyone to disturb him at that time. He insisted that drafts of his translation be sent to him as proof that his translations were being documented and going to the printers; a feedback system that gave him visibility of what is printing and what is the backlog. The morning japa walk, regular meetings with his disciples, his trips, all these had systems that ensured an ongoing input/output of information and time management so that proper decisions could be made and nothing fell through cracks.

A leader has to understand that while doing things right is important, the more important is to do the right things. One can only do the right things when he has the vision and when he questions the relevancy of his activities in view of the vision.

In short, Srila Prabhupada has shown us that in the matter of self-discipline/self-organization one needs to:

1\) Set priorities.  
2\) Time manage the priorities.  
3\) Be flexible, time-wise, for the unexpected.  
4\) Focus on one project at a time, allowing for it to mature to an expected level before launching on to something else.  
5\) Develop systems that save you time and prevent blind-sided surprises.  
6\) Perform activities that are relevant to one's mission.

## Interview with Bhakti-tirtha Swami In Tennessee USA (Part 1)

By Bhakta Bill Duke

**Bhakta Bill:** I understand you're coming here to Middle Tennessee to provide some guidance with our ISKCON Murari farm, as you're interested in farm communities. Can you tell us a little bit about your involvement in these communities.

**Bhakti-tirtha Swami:** We started out with several farms in Africa. As a matter of fact at one point some of our projects in Africa were becoming very self-sufficient projects; we were trying to train devotees how to grow food, make our own clothes - like these shoes and things I wear - these sandals, we'd make those in Ghana.

**BB:** What part of Ghana?

**BTS:** Accra. And most of the things that devotees usually go to India for - karatals, drums, Deities, incense, oils. We train devotees how to make those things. So the idea was, as Prabhupada mentioned several times, how he would like devotees to have simple living and high thinking. And how he wanted us to move to what's called varnashrama-dharma or self-sufficiency. So that means growing our own food, making our own medicine, and being able to build our own houses, and ultimately being able to use alternative energy. Thus at the projects like at Gita Nagari, we are trying to focus in that direction. As I move about to other communities I've been talking a lot about spiritual community development. The most important thing is to establish spiritual principles, to create more of a family-like atmosphere amongst the devotees, so that they are able to assist each other, complement each other, serve each other. But service is not only a sentimental, a conceptual thing, but it has a lot to do with what you have, what you can share, and what you can give. The more devotees can be trained in those areas, the more they can have things to serve each other with and to be able to build a community. A lot of my immediate work in ISKCON is with our farm project in Pennsylvania, in a place called Port Royal. They have about three or four hundred acres of land. And then I'm one of the co-GBCs for New Vrindavan, where we have 3000 acres of land. In some of these places devotees are building their own houses. In some places at Gita Nagari devotees are not using regular electricity or water sources. They have their wells and they're using alternative energy - solar power. And the farming is organic.

**BB:** What are the main crops they grow in Gita Nagari?

**BTS:** There are so many. They are growing all types of vegetables.

**BB:** I'm thinking more of, since we're vegetarians, we eat so much dahl, dried beans. Are they doing much in that area? Learning to grow dried beans?

**BTS:** Yes. And we are also making our own seeds, so that we won't just have to always go to commercial vendors to buy seeds.

**BB:** Getting away from hybrids?

**BTS:** Yes. Exactly.

**BB:** You were saying earlier that you received a lot of early association with Satsvarupa Maharaja. Can you tell us a little about when you first became a devotee?

**BTS:**Sure. I was a student at Princeton and just graduating. My last year I wrote a dissertation on Western Psychology and Eastern Philosophy. And from that particular dissertation I did some research in hypno-therapy. I became totally convinced that there was life after death. And as I read some of Srila Prabhupada's books it helped me to understand more about life after death and the whole philosophy and lifestyle that's associated with the fact that we're more than physical beings. So the more I started reading the books, the more I became impressed about the power of Krishna Consciousness.

**BB:** There were devotees coming to Princeton? That's where you got the books?

**BTS:** Once there were some devotees that came, but I was mainly associating with devotees in the New York Temple. And what happened is that I graduated from Princeton and then I took a temporary job in New Jersey. I was working with the public defenders office, and I was in charge of all the penal reform programs for the state of New Jersey. During that time I was visiting the temple in New York, and there was a notice on the bulletin board about our school in Dallas. Satsvarupa Maharaja was the principal at that time in Dallas, and he was in need of a secretary. So some of the devotees encouraged me "Oh, this Satsvarupa Maharaja, he's a very special devotee. He's a sanyassi. He's one of the best leaders in our movement." So they said, "If you could go to assist him, this would be very wonderful." I didn't even know what a sanyassi was. I was a new devotee, a bhakta. I wasn't even living in the temple. But devotees knew I had some clerical skills, that I could do some typing. And so I started reflecting, because I had a career scheduled to go to work at the United Nations, and I had everything all arranged—my future study, my future work. But I was indecisive. After reading the books more, I took one year off, I didn't go further into education, but I just did this work in the public defenders office. And I was going to the temple every weekend. At some point I thought this Krishna Consciousness is very powerful, so I should do it full-time. And I decided I would go join Satsvarupa Maharaja in Dallas. That's how I joined the movement.

**BB:** What year would this be?

**BTS:** That was in 1971. So I went to join him. And then I stayed with him; travelled with him for six or seven years. I was his servant or secretary; his sankirtan leader; so many things.

**Srimate Matsya:** How long had you been a devotee when you joined my Guru Maharaja?

**BTS:** Only a short time. I had only been in touch with devotees for almost a year. Just reading the books. I had my own house and I was living alone. So I was reading the books and coming to visit the temple. I joined the movement by quitting my job, giving my clothes away - my house and things. And I just went and bought some cloth from the 10 cent store and made a dhoti. I shaved my head in the bathroom, and had a sikha. Then with one suitcase, mostly of lecture tapes, I went to join him. That's how I joined the movement \[laughter\].

**SM:** (unclear question)

**BTS:** No, I was sure because I had studied. As I was saying earlier, I had studied, my dissertation was in Western Psychology and Eastern Philosophy. I had studied so much about the mind and body. And I was studying comparative religion. So I read Prabhupada's books. I was convinced that this was the highest philosophy. Then I met the devotees and I started associating. And even though I had an arrangement to go to the United Nations, to be involved in international law - I was going to work with the Chief Justice of Tanzania. So many things were already arranged for me. And now I stopped all this to be a devotee. But in the last seven or eight years, now I'm doing all of those things for Krishna. Now we do programs at the United Nations. I meet presidents of countries. I've personally given that leadership book to about ten Presidents of different countries. We do workshops, lectures - to business communities, to the medical profession. So many of the things that I wanted to put away, I just wanted to be a sadhu, then I found out how to use that to preach to people.

**BB:** How do businessmen receive you as a Hare Krishna?

**BTS:** Well, two ways. There is a need for principle-centered leadership. And when they read my book, first they just know me as a Swami. Then when they meet me they find out I'm a Hare Krishna Swami. Some of them are a little paranoid about the Hare Krishna movement because they don't really know what it is about. They think it's a cult. But after they get the book, they see the material, then they're impressed that we know all these things and at the same time we are a part of an ancient tradition. It becomes even more impressive. I've been asked to write articles for major business journals around the world. Some of the top business journals.

**BB:** You've written several books. I've heard of Spiritual Warrior. And I've seen another one advertised on management that I think you just mentioned. There is one excerpt that I've read where you mention the greatest freedom was freedom from illicit sex, or something to that effect. Can you tell us a little bit about this?

**BTS:** Well, in the Krishna movement I'm in a renounced order. I'm a sanyassi; so I'm a celibate. I was explaining how celibacy is not always something of denial. But it's an expression of freedom. It's a way to serve God also. There are many ashrams, or orders, in spiritual life. Not that one order is better than another. But just like people who are from Christian backgrounds, they know that there are nuns, and there are Cardinals, and there are monks. There are bishops. So they are in a special order. But it's not that everyone is to live in that sort of lifestyle. Sometimes I go to monasteries and give lectures to Catholic priests and nuns about these topics.
