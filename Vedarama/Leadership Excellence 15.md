# Leadership Excellence 15

## Quote of the Week

If our brahmanas are properly situated, our managers will naturally be inspired and the entire movement will be more dynamic than ever before. However, as long as our brahmanas are not able to live a simple, humble and renounced life, all others in the movement will be in difficulties.

-Sadhusangananda Das, Temple President, Boston USA

## Editor's Note

We suggest that you start to develop the "culture of constant leadership improvement" in your temples. How to do it? You may start by maybe discussing principles like: caring, facilitating, empowering, delegating, motivating on a deeper level, mutual trust, constructive feedback and creative cooperation. You may have informal meetings or gatherings to begin with. Then it may evolve to seminars and workshops. You may also start your own local Newsletter - with the aim of promoting the higher principles of spiritual leadership and management. Leadership Excellence may be helpful in this regard - to give you some ideas.

## Conflict Resolution

By Srila Prabhupada

There are many cases in which a person becomes an offender to the lotus feet of a Vaisnava and later becomes repentant. Here also we find that although the king of heaven, Indra, was so powerful that he accompanied Lord Visnu, he felt himself a great offender for stealing Prthu Maharaja's horse that was meant for sacrifice. An offender at the lotus feet of a Vaisnava is never excused by the Supreme Personality of Godhead. There are many instances illustrating this fact.

Ambarisa Maharaja was offended by Durvasa Muni, a great sage and mystic yogi, and Durvasa also had to fall down at the lotus feet of Ambarisa Maharaja. Indra decided to fall down at the lotus feet of King Prthu, but the King was so magnanimous a Vaisnava that he did not want Maharaja Indra to fall down at his feet. Instead, King Prthu immediately picked him up and embraced him, and both of them forgot all the past incidents. Both King Indra and Maharaja Prthu were envious and angry with each other, but since both of them were Vaisnavas, or servants of Lord Visnu, it was their duty to adjust the cause of their envy. This is also a first-class example of cooperative behavior between Vaisnavas.

In the present days, however, because people are not Vaisnavas, they fight perpetually among one another and are vanquished without finishing the mission of human life. There is a great need to propagate the Krishna consciousness movement in the world so that even though people sometimes become angry and malicious toward one another, because of their being Krishna conscious such rivalry, competition and envy can be adjusted without difficulty.

## Interview with Bhakti-tirtha Swami In Tennessee USA (Part 2)

By Bhakta Bill Duke

**Bhakta Bill Duke:** Were you from a Christian background?

**Bhakti Tirtha Swami:** Originally Christian. But I studied Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism. And I also oversee or conduct interfaith programs, where we have leaders of different traditions sharing their ideas. And I've had my own TV programs. I've had my own TV program in Nigeria for five years. Right now I'm on TV in the Washington-Maryland-Virginia area three times a week. Some of these are the kind of eclectic programs where we deal with people of other religions and other traditions. Although I'm not officially part of the public relations department of ISKCON, a lot of my work is like communications, public relations, interfaith.

**Srimate Matsya:** Maharaja, does it make it easier for you because you are a Princeton graduate?

**BTS:** Yes. I wear three hats. I'm a scholar and a Princeton graduate, which is one of the best schools in the world. And then I'm in African chief also. So that's like a governor.

**BBD:** You're actually an African chief?

**BTS:** Yes, in Warri. About a million people are part of the area that I'm associated with. Africa and India are very similar. They have these traditional cultures. Village culture, where people respect the elders, the saintly people, they respect the tradition. So it allows me to be an adviser to many many chiefs and kings. Because I'm like an inside part of the culture. And then I'm an African-American Vaishnava swami. I'm actually the only African-American Vaishnava guru in the world.

**Srimate Matsya:** Even in ISKCON?

**BTS:** Yes. That gives me a lot of media coverage. Sometimes they will say "He's a Swami. He's an African-American. And he's an Indian also, all at the same time." \[Laughter\]

**BBD:** I didn't understand. So you were actually born as a chief?

**BTS:** No. I was born in America. But I worked in Africa for about 16 years, with developing cottage industry's, some free clinics, farms, boutique shops, and so many temples. We built so many temples and developed them. I was so popular amongst the religious people and the secular people and I was on TV constantly. Some of their leaders wanted to honour me and also to receive me as a chief. But the real reason is that one of the top kings was like my disciple. And it's kind of dangerous to be too close to these people. But by becoming a part of the traditional culture it was no longer dangerous. It's like a king, or the royalty in England, they have a lot of etiquette. Certain things you do at ceremonies. Certain ways you approach them. And so it was out of etiquette for this king to be receiving me as his spiritual master. Because to the spiritual master he offers his obeisances, he writes me letters, he gets instructions. And that was out of their culture. When I also became a member of the traditional culture, they accepted it a little easier. Otherwise it could be dangerous for his life and my life also. These kings are very powerful. It's like the royal class, the barons and the lords. A lot of them are extremely wealthy. Sometimes it's more about wealth and power, than even cultural position. People are multi-millionaires. They own so many properties. And when you enter such environments, it's a little bit delicate if you are coming from too much of an outside connection. But because I'm a spiritualist I can use that like being a spiritual adviser to these chiefs and kings.

**BBD:** Where is this?

**BTS:** This is in Nigeria. It's one of the largest countries in Africa. There is about a hundred million people in that country.

**BBD:** You're well acquainted and friends with Nelson Mandela. Is that right?

**BTS:** Yes. With him and some of the other leaders of South Africa I have a rather friendly relationship with. As a matter of fact I've given them books, they have studied the leadership book and Spiritual Warrior II.

**BBD:** How Mr. Mandela receives our philosophy?

**BTS:** He likes our devotees very much. There have been several times that he had come, not just as a politician. But he had come to participate in our programs, like major festivals. He likes the fact that devotees are very cosmopolitan and that we're into helping other people of their country. We have programs. Then we have the whites, the Africanners, the coloureds, the Asians, the blacks of all different tribes, people from all different religions also are invited to our programs. And he respects that. He likes our Food for Life program. We're distributing tons and tons of food to the people in South Africa. Millions and millions of plates of prasadam are distributed every couple of years.

**BBD:** Can you make any comment on two recent events. One within the movement, the situation with Harikesa Maharaja? And secondly the situation here in the U.S. regarding President Clinton?

**BTS:** Concerning Harikesa Maharaja I was one of those that the GBC executive committee asked to go to visit him and to evaluate the situation. What we see is someone who has worked very hard for the movement, but temporarily is in a wounded condition. He had a mental, physical and spiritual breakdown. And we were trying to give him care. And at the same time we requested that he takes a leave of absence from his major managerial activities. The devotees would see him as someone who is in a weakened state, who needs their love. In terms of President Clinton, we see it is very unfortunate that so much leadership is leadership that is not principle-centered. There's a need to have servant-leaders, where the leader sees himself as a servant of the people. There's a need for leaders to lead from the inside-out, to have a spiritual connection that allows them to maintain good ethics and morals. What we're seeing now is the opposite of that. We're seeing a leader who has been in denial, who has been manipulative, exploitive; and who has abused his position of leadership. And this is setting a tremendously bad example for the citizens in general. But unfortunately the citizens make the leader and the leader makes the citizens. So there's something about the people of America that they're ready to tolerate, such a leader whose life itself is an actual farce.

## Family for the Gita

Once a renounced spiritual master presented his neophyte disciple with a copy of the Bhagavad-gita, instructing him to study the book all the time. The disciple, eager to imitate the advanced status of his guru, entered a cave in the Vindhya hill and there he read the Gita day and night. Now, while the disciple took rest, a mouse who lived in the cave nibbled at the pages of the holy book, leaving the edges ragged. The disciple was very upset about this. He went down the hillside to a village and found a kitten, which he brought back to the cave to deal with the mouse.

But the kitten required milk. So the disciple managed to find a pious farmer who willingly parted with one cow. Then, the would-be renunciate constructed a cow-shed with materials that he begged from here and there,and with great endeavour, he dragged them up the hillside.

Even after building the shed, the disciple found it too difficult to maintain the cow alone. At last he found a young villager who was willing-simply for three meals a day-to tend the cow. But now the aspiring hermit was faced with the duty of cooking thrice daily for the cowherd as well as preparing his own meals. This meant he had to visit the village at least once a day to beg sufficient foodstuffs and firewood, and then bring it all up to the cave. The villagers were less supportive now that his possessions had increased. One of them even mocked the disciple, saying, "You've become a busy householder! Why do you persist in this charade of renunciation? Get married and work like the rest of us!"

Finding it impossible to meet his requirements, the disciple did just that. As his family grew, his needs grew also until at last he lived in a comfortable villa on a large tract of land as the owner of many animals and the employer of many servants. After the guru became aware that his disciple had abandoned the cave, he traced him to his new home and was astonished to find him amidst all the trappings of worldly existence. "Now what does all this mean?" he demanded of his disciple.

"Oh Divine Master," came the reply, "this is my family and property, they are for the Gita study that you ordered me to do."

The scriptures enjoin: *yavanirvaha pratigraha*-"accept only that which is essential for serving the Lord." This means that a devotee centers his requirements in life around Krishna and not around himself. He is concerned neither with excessive gain or excessive renunciation. Indeed, if an aspirant on the spiritual path becomes selfishly inclined even in the matter of renunciation-i.e. his vanity is displayed in his show of detachment from the world-that selfishness is very liable to mislead him into accumulating excessive material possessions in the name of maintaining his spiritual life. Another way that material attachment creeps into the life of a renunciate is through attraction to material knowledge. If pride is fostered through mastery of the scriptures and through philosophical erudition, that knowledge is only material and will result in the falldown of the student into material entanglement.

\- As told by Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati
