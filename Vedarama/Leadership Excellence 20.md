# Leadership Excellence 20

## Personal Example

By Srila Prabhupada

As you are one of the elder members of our Society and you are sannyasi, it is your duty to see that all the devotees are following strictly the regulative principles and chanting 16 rounds, attending the classes, reading our books, and always remaining enthusiastic for executing devotional service.

Everyone has got some propensity. The art of preaching is to engage that propensity in the service of Krishna. Whatever we have got, our mind, our intelligence, our possessions, our time, our energies, let them all be used in Krishna's service. So by training people in this way, this will be the success of your preaching work.

Now you continue executing your Krishna Conscious activities, chanting 16 rounds, studying our books, going on sankirtana, worshiping the deity, etc., and in this way you will become advanced more and more. Also as you are senior devotee you must set a nice example for the others to follow.

So as you are my appointed leader, you must also see that the others are following very strictly the regulative principles like chanting 16 rounds, reading my books regularly, rising early for mangala aratrika, these things will keep us purified for advancing more and more in Krishna Consciousness.

As you are temple president, that is a great responsibility. So you must try to execute it with great care. The main thing is that you must set nice example for the others by following very rigidly the regulative principles, such as chanting 16 rounds on the beads, reading the books, getting up to attend mangala aratrika, etc. In this way everyone shall be encouraged. We must be so careful to train the devotees nicely. Now they have come from the realm of maya to Krishna's place, so it is our duty to keep them there, not that they shall again fall victim of maya.

## Sannyasa

By Srila Prabhupada

"*Unless the sannyasi is freed*  
*from all cares and anxieties,*  
*like a white cloud,*  
*it is difficult for him to do*  
*anything good for society."*

The four orders of social life, as designed in the varnasrama system, are very scientific and cooperative. In student life one is taught the primary principles of the human form of life. One who enters the householder's life can execute the duties of a family man because he has already been trained for this job in the brahmacarya-asrama. And after fifty years of age the householder retires from family life and prepares for the life of sannyasa.

The householder is duty-bound to maintain the members of all three of the other asramas, namely the brahmacaris, the vanaprasthas, and sannyasis. In this way, every member of society was given a chance to retire for a higher order of spiritual culture, and the householders neglected no one. The brahmacaris, vanaprasthas, and sannyasis all curtailed their necessities to the minimum, and therefore no one would begrudge maintaining them in the bare necessities of life.

In Kali-yuga, however, the entire system has gone topsy-turvy. The student lives in luxury at the expense of the father or the father-in-law. When the educated, indulgent student becomes a householder by the strength of university degrees, he requires money by all means for all kinds of bodily comfort, and therefore he cannot spare even a penny for the so-called vanaprasthas and sannyasis. The vanaprasthas and sannyasis nowadays are those who were unsuccessful in family life. Thus the so-called sannyasis try to construct another home in the name of the sannyasa-asrama and glide down into all sorts of luxury at the expense of others. So all these varnas and asramas have now become so many transcendental frauds.

But that does not mean that there is no reality in them. One should not conclude that there is no good money simply because one has met with counterfeit coins. The sannyasa-asrama is meant for complete freedom from all anxieties, and it is meant for uplifting the fallen souls, who are merged in materialism. But unless the sannyasi is freed from all cares and anxieties, like a white cloud, it is difficult for him to do anything good for society.

## A Basic Leadership Principle, Part 3

by Isana Gauranga Dasa

*Isana Gauranga Dasa is developing Bhakti Vriksa Groups in Malaysia.*

**Self-discipline**

Self-discipline is critical to one's success as a spiritual leader/preacher. Through observation of experiences, we can see that a leader will only attract his/her own kind. A leader needs to upgrade himself to ensure that he attract followers of higher and higher quality.

**Responsibility**

We saw some years ago in Singapore, a leader sending his brahmacaris for daily book distribution following a strict schedule. This appeared wonderful until we saw that while they were gone for book distribution, the leader was playing with his toddler for hours. Because of his lack of discipline and responsibility, making the blunder that as a leader he had privileges, the leader lost these brahmacaris. Leaders never set themselves above their followers except in carrying out responsibilities. For every privilege a leader has he must accept a greater responsibility. Srila Prabhupada he took all the responsibilities for the growth of ISKCON as his. Privileges were just service by his disciples to him who was an epitome of humbleness. Being responsible also means finishing a job. Srila Prabhupada faced "insurmountable" obstacles in trying to get the BTG magazine going. He had no place to stay, no food, no finances, no assistants or friends in India and yet because his spiritual master wanted it, he took the responsibility and never gave it up until it became a reality. A leader shall be responsible for what he needs to do, for what privileges he receives and to those he leads. Sense of responsibility is a street lamp that illuminates the path to greatness.

**Accountability**

Accountability is key in an ISKCON leader's life. In the material working life, accountability without authority is easily obtained; in ISKCON, authority without accountability is easily obtained. Herein lies a great danger for the leader, if he does not volunteer to be accountable to someone. The centre/temple leader is expected to be answerable to his spiritual master and the GBC; however, it is left to the leader to set goals, report, consult, receive counsel and generally to become accountable; this is because the spiritual master is away preaching and the GBC is remote. By default of the system, the prerogative of becoming accountable, the extent to which he wants to be accountable, the decision to withdraw his accountability all falls on the shoulder of the temple/centre leader. His Holiness Radhanath Swami does not allow this situation to arise in the Chowpathy temple. Everyone of authority is answerable to him directly or indirectly. In this system, a leader is not working in a vacuum and Radhanath Swami, by giving out goals and following up, can assess the performance of his leaders and keep them in tow. Because we are not pure devotees, leaders can become drunk with power and when situations are not conducive they can withdraw from being accountable. We see leaders when approached by the GBC appointed local authorities, reject them by saying they will not work under such and such person or that they will only work under their spiritual master who is, of course, somewhere remote and sometimes almost inaccessible. This is indirect rejection of accountability. Srila Prabhupada exposed this behaviour in Hawai. Once a leader is drunk with unchecked power he will do whatever is necessary to preserve his position of "absoluteness". To not fall in this trap, the leader must accept an authority above him to whom he becomes accountable.

**Integrity**

The one quality that is difficult to achieve and yet critical to success as a leader is integrity. The dictionary defines integrity as "wholeness". When a leader does not have different sets of rules for different people or is not deceitful or is not hypocritical, who follows his own advice, does what he says, is honest and holds the spiritual interests of others as foremost - is said to have integrity. The leader must keep re-assessing his character and work at improving himself. If a centre/temple has, regularly, people coming for a short while and then leaving, and therefore not growing, the leader may want to look within himself. A great flaw of character is betrayal of trust, not keeping one's commitment. The betrayed person, usually a follower, will not bring the matter up, the follower will just fade away. The leader may use his charisma to attract followers but he requires character to retain them. For the leader, ISKCON's interest and the spiritual interests of the devotees must be put before his self-interest.

Responsibility, accountability and integrity are three points discussed above which, in addition to the points discussed in the two previous articles, assist the leader to develop a character for success. Good character in the age of Kali is almost impossible to develop. However, by the grace of Srila Prabhupada, we can do it by the use of the 7 core activities (see the first article on this topic in VE12), become examplary and assist our spiritual master to push forward the mission of Srila Prabhupada.

## Editor's Note

As we enter the year 2000, before us is a great leadership challenge. Karmic account of this planet is increasing and reactions are coming more and more. To be a leader today, and more so a spiritual leader, requires a great amount of courage, compassion and personal integrity. The whole show of the material nature is here to test our character. Shall we (re)act as a devotee or as a selfish materialistic person - in difficult situations that time, nature and society bring us.

We may set a goal to become more responsible and develop higher consciousness leadership based on the principle that the leader is the servant first. We wish you all success in your sublime leadership improvement endeavours.
