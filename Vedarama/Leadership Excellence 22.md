# Leadership Excellence 22

## Quote of the Week

I am more of a learner than a leader.

\- Manu Dasa, Temple Vice President, Northern Ireland

## Srila Prabhupada's Pearls

**Prime Responsibility**

Every state and its administrators, regardless of the nature of the administration—monarchy or democracy, oligarchy or dictatorship or autocracy—have the prime responsibility to lead the citizens toward God realization. This is essential for all human beings, and it is the duty of the father, spiritual master, and ultimately the state to take up the responsibility of leading the citizens towards this end.

**Bodily**

There are many leaders to lead the ignorant public, but because every one of them is bewildered by the bodily conception of life, there is no peace and prosperity in human society.

**Don't Give Up**

Failures may not be detrimental; they may be the pillars of success.

**Senses**

All leaders should ponder how they can discharge their duties by satisfying the transcendental senses of Visnu, for what the leaders do will be imitated by their followers.

**Walk Your Talk**

President should be the first to come forward and lead the battle.

**Model**

Their ideal behaviour, character, will lead other people to appreciate: "Yes. We should be like this."

## Perseverance

By Srila Prabhupada

"*Let the curse of the brahmana's son*  
*fall upon me - I do not mind.*"

When a person is undisturbed even in the presence of various causes of disturbance, he is called reserved and perseverant. An example of this perseverance and reservation is found in the behavior of King Pariksit, as described in the First Canto, Nineteenth Chapter, verse 15, of Srimad-Bhagavatam. The King says there to all the sages present before him at the time of his death, "My dear brahmanas, you should always accept me as your surrendered servant. I have come to the bank of the Ganges just to devote my heart and soul unto the lotus feet of Lord Krishna. So please bless me, that mother Ganges may also be pleased with me. Let the curse of the brahmana's son fall upon me - I do not mind. I only request that at the last moment of my life all of you will kindly chant the holy name of Visnu, so that I may realize His transcendental qualities."

This example of Maharaja Pariksit's behavior, his remaining patient even at the last point of his life, his undisturbed condition of mind, is an example of reservation. This is one of the characteristics of a devotee who has developed ecstatic love for Krishna.

## Effective Preaching

By Vijaya-venugopala Dasa

Effective preaching is - to inspire (by example, by convincing, by caring) someone to take to Krishna consciousness and render devotional service to Krishna, and to remain in Krishna consciousness and continue to render devotional service.

We can inspire devotees and people to serve Krishna by:

1. Our Krishna consciousness.
2. The example of our behaviour.
3. Our genuine love and caring for them.
4. Setting the example of serving Krishna ourselves - tirelessly and without motivation.

How can we purify our motives in preaching?

By only thinking of the spiritual welfare of those to whom we preach. Become humble by understanding that, even though we are totally unqualified, by the mercy of the pure devotees in the guru parampara, we have been given this wonderful opportunity to go back home back to Godhead. It is therefore our compulsory duty to give this knowledge to everyone else, so that they can also go back home, back to Godhead. I think there is one motivation which is 'permissible' - if even one of those to whom we preach becomes successful in going back home, by virtue of being his/her vartma pradarsaka guru(one who shows the path), we too will go back!

## Phone

By Akrura das

One way to improve the quality of service in the Temples is to make it a priority to allow a phone in the Temple to ring maximum 2-3 times before answering and to have standard way of answering like: "Hare Krishna Temple. Can I help you?!"

Devotee who is answering may be enthusiastically ready to do anything needed to help the person who is calling: to find the person asked for, to write down and convey the message, to ask the phone number for calling back, etc. Good impression about phoning the Temple can make a big difference in how people view our movement and our service to humanity.
