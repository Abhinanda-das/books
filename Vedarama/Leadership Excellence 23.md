# Leadership Excellence 23

## Protection

Do your mind and heart find pleasure in justice? Are you and your people so happy that your minds are never frustrated? O noble Bharata, do you see that the citizens are not severely disturbed by cruel punishment, and do your ministers faithfully govern the kingdom? Do you give the army their proper food and wages, at the time they are due, without holding back or dragging out the time? Do you avoid exceeding the limits of power, and is your rule thus free of selfish, whimsical absolutism in the many affairs of life and death?

O noble Bharata, do you maintain the wives of those men who gave their lives for you or who suffered calamity for your sake? O Partha, do you fully protect, like a son, an enemy who has submitted in fear or who is impotent or defeated in battle? Are you fair and equal to all the earth, O earthly lord, as worthy of trust as one's own mother and father? When men are experienced in their work and care for you and wish you well, do you refrain from removing them from office unless they have first committed an offense? Are the farmers nourished and prospering? Are the reservoirs of water large and full, and have they been laid out systematically so that agriculture does not depend exclusively on rains from the gods? When seed and food are scarce, do you show mercy to the farmer and grant him a loan?

Are the perennial thieves and gang leaders who dwell in your kingdom chased by the troops over rough as well as level land? Do you comfort and carefully protect the women? O lord of the earth, do you never, under any circumstances, out of arrogance, illusion, or selfish desire, drive away or disregard plaintiffs and defendants who have come to you \[for justice\]. Religious principles are based on the three Vedas, and your ancestors followed them. Do you abide by these same principles, and do you thus carry out those same sacred duties? \[In your kingdom,\] is a respectable and purified soul, clean in his habits, never falsely accused of theft by greedy men, who are blind to the scriptures and the common weal and who would put that pious man to death? When a thief commits a crime and is questioned and captured with the evidence by those who witnessed his crime, is he never then set free for a bribe, O best of men, out of greed for his wealth? When a dispute arises among the rich and poor, do your ministers never see the issues falsely, under the influence of wealth?

Do you always provide all the craftsmen their tools and materials, and do you always give them proper shelter during the four months of the rainy season? Do you fully protect your kingdom against the dangers of fire, and from snakes, beasts of prey, disease, and demons? As a knower of God's law, do you, like a loving father, care for the blind, the mute, and the crippled, those who have lost a limb or a loved one, and those who have left their worldly life in search of God?

\- Mahabharata

## Respect and Appreciation

By Karnamrta Dasa

I want to honour and respect you, dear devotee. You may call me sentimental, or whatever, yet I see that this consciousness of respect and compassion for others has saved and nurtured my spiritual life. To me this is what being a devotee is all about. I don't want to criticize any devotee's sincerity. How can I really know another's heart? I strongly assert that we must believe in the integrity and sincerity of every devotee. Certainly there are examples to the contrary, yet I want to avoid the critical mentality which can develop through focusing on the negative that practically everyone is suspect, and that I must constantly judge everyone.

If that becomes the standard of devotee interaction, then we are doomed as a Society. Love and trust have to be the basis of our dealings-not blindly of course, but let us practice compassion and understanding, and give everyone the benefit of the doubt. Let us put love and compassion first, not fall into Kali-yuga quarrel. I like what Steven Covey says, "Seek first to understand, then to be understood." Are we doing that in all our disputes?

It seems that the official understanding of our Krishna consciousness philosophy lends itself well to fanaticism or "black and white" thinking, where there is "only one way"-our way, though we may say it is Srila Prabhupada's way. Usually both sides are claiming to represent our beloved founder-acharya. This makes finding a workable solution next to impossible. Once we condemn or harshly criticize any devotee or group of devotees and not honor and respect them, all devotees are smeared, and we are hindering our own advancement (through offences) and the spreading of Krishna consciousness. We must all learn to respect everyone, what to speak of devotees. I am rather amazed at how attacking so many devotees are-as if differing devotees are a personal threat. Devotees are not our enemies! (Although people might consider this true by reading our dealings with each other) Lack of Krishna consciousness and our lack of Vaisnava qualities are the real enemies.

We ourselves are the problem, not others. How are we contributing to the problems? What is our mentality and motivation in these disputes? Do we want to be right and see other's put in their place, or do want a spiritual society that can accommodate Unity in Diversity? I realize that there are many issues that are very emotionally charged. Never the less, our attitude of condemnation and character assassination-now so prevalent in many of our dealings over differences-is only adding to the problem facing all devotees. How we react to problems is really a statement of how Krishna conscious we are-or aren't.

This doesn't mean that we have to endorse views we consider wrong, but that we respect the integrity of other devotees to follow their convictions and understanding-and that we sincerely try to understand their position. This lack or respect and appreciation for other devotees is to me the single greatest problem facing all devotees everywhere. We must learn HOW to disagree as Vaisnavas, or as ladies and gentlemen. We must live with differences now, and for eternity, without envy or hatred!

## Open the Door to Get Light on Your Floor

\- As told by Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati

When a spoiled young man who was pampered by his parents once overslept in his bolted and shuttered room, his family gathered outside to awaken him. Inside his room it was dark, so he yelled out, "Why are you waking me in the middle of the night? It's time to sleep!"

The family called back, "Night has passed long ago. It's almost mid-day! The sun is shining high in the sky."

The young man replied, "If the sun is so bright, let it shine into this room. Then I'll know it is day."

The family called back, "Open the door to get light on your floor!"

This story is used to answer those who say that only foolish people say God is merciful. God shows no mercy on the world, because it is such a terrible place. But the answer is, the suffering materialists live in darkness by their own refusal to open their lives up to the sun-like mercy of Krishna.
