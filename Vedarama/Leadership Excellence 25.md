## Srila Prabhupada on Prasadam

By Satsvarupa Dasa Goswami

In India, Prabhupada instructed the devotees how a gentlemen would eat and how to feed guests who came to the temple. During the meal the host should be very attentive to his guest's needs, supplying him servings of hot puris, fresh water, and more of everything. He should also engage his guest in light, relaxing conversation, and not heavy, anxiety producing topics. Prabhupada said rice was useless unless served hot. He also said reheating rice must never be done, since it causes a poisonous effect. "So far as offering to Krishna apple cider, this can be done only if it is prepared by the devotees. These food manufacturers do not take proper precautions in cleanliness nor do they have devotion for Krishna in their labours, so it is not very acceptable offering. If you can make this preparation yourself, then it would be all right."

## Adhere to the True Path

Once there were the men of a large caravan of 1000 wagons, on realizing their party was too large to be manageable, held a meeting and picked two leaders to each lead 500 wagons. Though both leaders were skilful handlers of animals, only one had real management foresight. So the intelligent one said to the other, "It is impossible for us to travel together—will you go first, or should I?" The other leader thought, "If I can go first, the roads will be smooth; if I follow him, they will be rutted. If I go first, my oxen will eat untouched grass, the water will be untouched, and I can set my goods at any price I choose to set."

So he chose to go first. The intelligent leader though, "Very good. If he goes first, he will smooth the rough spots of the road for his wagons to pass; I'll be spared that work. His oxen will eat the old tough grass that has grown wild for a long time; mine will eat the fresh young grass that will grow after his oxen have moved on. He will dig wells where he cannot find water, and we'll be spared that work. And he and his men will have to haggle for a good price; whereas I will sell my goods for whatever price his party was able to establish." The foolish caravan leader entered a "demon-wilderness" (there are five kinds of wilderness: robber-wilderness, wild beast-wilderness, waterless wilderness, famine-wilderness and demon-wilderness). Before entering that wilderness, the leader filled big clay jars with emergency water provisions and had the carts loaded with extra leaves and grass for the animals.

In the middle of the wilderness, they were met by a black person riding an ass who was soaking wet and garlanded with fresh blue and white lilies. The black man hailed the foolish leader in a friendly manner. The leader surmised that there must be plentiful water and plants up ahead. The black man heartily agreed, telling the leader that he should dump out the water and throw off the grass and leaves from the wagons to lighten the load so that the caravan could more quickly reach the oasis up ahead. The leader ordered this to be done. But there was no oasis, and the entire caravan met its end in the desert. When the intelligent leader approached the demon-wilderness, he had made the same preparations and was met by the same black man on the ass. But he told the man on the ass, "Begone with you. You appear to me to be the bearer of all inauspicity. We shall not lighten our load one bit because of your suspicious statements." And so the caravan crossed the wilderness, and found the remnants of the first caravan on the way.

## Vision: An Essential Requirement for a Leader

By Isana Gauranga Dasa

*Isana Gauranga Das is developing Bhakti Vriksa programs in Penang, Malaysia.*

Vision for a leader is the ability to see in the future the desired reality that is not visible at the present. This quality is indispensable to a leader without which the leader is so in name only. He may have his own vision or it may be borrowed, it does not matter. But he needs to be able to see what most others can't. The tourist guide knows where he is going because he has seen the destination and he knows it is the appropriate goal. Likewise, a leader is a guide because he has been there in his vision, in fact he visits there, at the pictorial final goal, regularly. We can see Srila Prabhupada was living in an envisioned higher plane which his disciples could not access. For example, some of Srila Prabhupada's disciples thought that the Krishna Consciousness movement was not to go beyond the Lower East Side of New York whereas he was planning something far beyond, seeing in his vision temples all over the world. Srila Prabhupada, on several occasions, mentioned that he could see the then unbuilt temples, unseen by his disciples, and it was only time that separated them from him.

In Prabhupada-lila, Srila Prabhupada talks of thousands of his future disciples waiting for him even before he had met them. Such grand vision is the sign of a world class leader.

**Loss of Vision**

A leader who has no vision or loses his vision stops leading and starts reacting. He will soon lose his followers or organisation. Sometimes it is seen that a leader has a limited vision with which he leads and grows. Somewhere along the way, he gives up building on or expanding his vision. His rise, however, which follows the expansion of his vision continues for sometime after his vision has stagnated. This shoots the leader from his level of competence to incompetence. Since he is operating in the level of incompetence, the leader in order to hold on to his position will resort to political and unfair means and stifle those with larger vision in the organisation.

**Vision Sharing**

A shared vision means an organisation where everyone puts aside his differences and works towards the common goal. The sharing of the organisation's vision, in a systematic manner, is essential for its survival. It prevents the people within an organisation working in opposing directions. It is not enough that all are working hard but the vectors of such hard work should be unidirectional, for the organisation to progress, otherwise the vectors will cancel each other and we will have a hard working society without much progress. The constant sharing of vision by the leader will also generate synergy. Synergy creates a result that is far bigger and better than what can be logically expected of a given group. For example, a team of 3 men, with different understanding of their mission doing their own things, will produce a combined effect that does not justify the existence of the team of 3. Next a team of three, with different understanding of their mission but working together, will justify the team. A team of three whose understandings are united by a shared vision and working together will produce a resonating effect that is beyond logical explanation. Therefore a shared vision is crucial to the growth of a leader, his followers and the organisation as a whole. Srila Prabhupada, a leader par excellence, has given us a framework of a vision to last 10,000 years and has left enough space within the framework for subsequent great leaders to exercise their ability to cast visions.

**Force and Focus**

The vision provides the motive force in all endeavours. Srila Prabhupada was relentless in his drive to achieve his vision the seed of which was the instruction of his guru. In the initial chapters in the Prabhupada Lilamrta, we see this drive along the highway to his destination where he did not allow anything to sidetrack him. When the governor's wife wanted the building he was occupying in Jhansi and was using some underhand methods, Srila Prabhupada's friends advised him to sue the offending parties. Srila Prabhupada declined saying that it would distract him from his mission. Visionaries do not get sidetracked by problems. This focus and drive when communicated to the followers surcharges the whole group with the motive force required to realise the vision.

**Levels of Vision**

A person with a vision, who is not able to convince others about the vision but pursues it, nevertheless, is an achiever. A person with a vision who convinces others about it is a leader. One who has no vision should attach himself to a leader with a vision that he wants to be part of. Srila Prabhupada has given us a vision, a huge framework for 10,000 years. Those of us with visions should fit ours within that framework and those without vision, who can see the vision if shown, should attach with the leaders whose visions are within Srila Prabhupada's framework. There are those who either don't have a vision and can't see one or have visions that are detrimental to the organisation or refuse to the share the vision, these are the disrupters. They should be retrained, reassigned or removed.
