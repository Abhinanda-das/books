## Lord Ramacandra's Advice to Bharata (Part 1)

I hope the citizens do not become agitated by the stern rule in the kingdom and therefore insult Your ministers. I hope the sacrificial priests do not refuse to officiate at Your sacrifices, as they would to a degraded person or as a wife would to a husband who cohabited with a vulgar woman. One who does not get rid of a doctor who aggravates maladies, a servant intent on disgracing the master or a warrior seeking sovereignty, will be killed by these. I hope You have appointed a person who is satisfied, resolute, valorous, intelligent, of spotless character, well-born, dedicated and clever as the commander-in-chief. Have You properly honoured Your foremost warriors who are mighty and skilled in fighting and whose prowess has been shown on the battlefield?

I hope you supply them with provisions properly and pay their salaries on time and without delay. Servants become extremely angry with their masters when their provisions and salaries are delayed, and that in itself is highly unnecessary. I hope all the principal members of Your family are devoted to You. Do they purposefully lay down their lives for You? I hope You have appointed as ambassador someone from the kingdom who is learned, skilful, witty and eloquent.

Keep an eye on the eighteen functionaries of the enemies and your own fifteen functionaries , using three secret spies for each. I hope You remain vigilant of enemies who, after being expelled, have returned, even though they are weak. I hope You do not patronize materialistic brahmanas, foolish and conceited as they are, they are expert in useless pursuits. Basing themselves in logic and reason, these fellows of depraved intelligence interpret the main scriptures in a nonsensical way.

I hope You are kind to those who cultivate the land and protect their cows. Are those people, dependent as they are on their occupation, happy at present? I hope You have protected them and supplied them with their necessities, for all the inhabitants of the kingdom should be dutifully protected by the king. Are You pacifying the womenfolk, giving them proper protection? I hope You do not put too much faith in them and do not confide Your secrets in them..

## GBC Trains Temple Presidents in Caring

Bhakti-tirtha Swami described in his class in Ireland how he instructed Temple Presidents in some African Temples about how to improve their service to the devotees:

"We suggested to all of the leaders that they will do a journal, like a diary, where they could reflect on ways that they are going out of their way to try to facilitate devotees under their care. We did this so they would really see how much they are going out of their way, with the idea that they will see what they are not doing and they can grasp and be in a better position to do what needs to be done.

Externally everything was going well, but people were coming and leaving.

So, when they started writing they began to see in which way they were supposed to put in some interest. From that, some of them began to realize that they were being rather mechanical, and were taking excellent care of themselves. When they got sick, they'd try to get best doctors. When they needed something they would make arrangements. But when devotees under their care needed..."

Swami also said that it had a very positive effect on Temple Presidents' service to devotees in the Temples.

## Inner Faculites

By Bhakti-tirtha Swami

Fear and love do not go together. Fear is constricting, self-centered and self-conscious, whereas love is expansive, selfless and directed toward service. To become effective spiritual warriors, we must learn to cultivate genuine love, courage, and compassion and come to depend on our inner faculties rather than externals. This allows us to understand our own true nature more deeply, and to behave more like the children and servants of God that we are. Then, firmly established in a higher state of consciousness, we can serve others - and the world - from the deepest, most aware and loving aspect of ourselves during these challenging times.
