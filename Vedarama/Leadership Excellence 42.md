## Pearls of Wisdom

By Srila Prabhupada

**A Thief**

If one gets a good manager, his estate is very nicely managed, but if the manager is a thief, his estate is spoiled. Similarly, in his material, conditional existence, the living entity gives power of attorney to his mind. As such, he is liable to be misdirected by his mind into enjoying sense objects.

**Kowtow**

All of them are therefore kowtowing to the so-called leaders or "big guns" in order to achieve temporary boons, and they indeed achieve such things. Such foolish men are not interested in Krishna consciousness for the permanent solution to the hardships of material existence. They are all after sense enjoyment.

**The Good of the World**

We simply request the leaders of all nations to pick up this science of Krishna for their own good, for the good of society and for the good of all the world's people.

**For Peace**

The Lord inaugurated this system of mass sankirtana, and leaders of all countries can take advantage of this spiritual movement in order to keep the mass of people in a pure state of peace and friendship with one another. This is now the demand of the present human society all over the world.

## Interview with Narayani dasi

*Narayani Dasi is serving as an educator in Vaisnava Institute for Higher Education. She is presently writing curriculum for Bhakti Sastri Course as well as helping in research for Bhakti Vaibhava Course.*

**LE:** How does the present Bhakti Sastri course which you teach in Vrndavana help to develop the character of the students?

**ND:** Often devotees think that if we spend too much time reading we will simply become armchair philosophers. Our aim is to train brahmanas who will be able to make a substantial contribution to society as theologians, advisors, counsellors and teachers. We use a variety of types of interactive learning which enable students to be more introspective about their own level of advancement and be able to take the necessary steps to progress in spiritual life. We use introspection, for example, where in Bg 3.6. Lord Krishna says, "One who restrains the senses of action but whose mind dwells on sense objects certainly deludes himself and is called a pretender." We then ask the students the questions, "What are the symptoms of being a pretender in spiritual life? What are the symptoms of false renunciation?" By understanding how Krishna's words are meant for personal application the students can change their attitudes and behaviour.

**LE:** How is this training relevant to ISKCON's present needs?

**ND:** Among the many aims of sastric study which form the basis of our course are:

**Moral and Academic integrity**

This means training the students to spot misuse of scripture, philosophical misconceptions and being able to understand the proper conclusion according to the disciplic succession. One example of misuse of scripture is to quote part of a verse or purport which supports your particular view and neglecting other relevant sections of the scriptures. Another example of misuse is to quote scripture with some personal motivation.

**Preaching Application**

In our classes we simulate actual experiences that devotees might meet in their daily preaching activities, while at the same time showing how to apply Krishna's words in Bhagavad-gita as a basis for problem-solving.

**Personal Application**

We can analyse Krishna's words to find many principles which form the basis of our ongoing spiritual lives and which form the basis of our world view and which shape our behaviour. For example, Krishna says in Bg [3.21](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/3.21) "Whatever a great man does, common men will follow." If we accept this statement as a principle to follow, then as leaders we must first set an example for others to follow. In other words, our behaviour will be shaped by the principles we find in the scriptures.

**Appreciation of Srila Prabhupada's Mood and Mission**

By including many quotes from Srila Prabhupada's lectures we show how he dealt with a variety of people and how he preached.

## Humility Test

By Sacinandana Swami

1. How eager am I to serve, or do I just want to be served?
2. How eager am I to respect others, or do I rather strive to be respected?
3. How much do I care for others, or do I mainly care for myself?
4. Am I glad upon seeing the advancement of others, or do I envy them?
5. Am I fully dependent on guru and Krishna, or rather on my own arrangement?
