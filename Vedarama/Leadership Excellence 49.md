## The Need for Real Leadership

By Bhakti-tirtha Swami

Our contemporary governments and other institutions are sorely lacking powerful, responsible, selfless leaders. Do not mistakenly believe that the media is playing games when they expose leaders and unearth scandals. Their message is a serious one: we cannot trust most of our leaders. Why not? Because many of them are engaged in selfish sense gratification and manipulation to gain power. The leaders of most countries are increasingly guiding people away from spiritual life, and many of their policies are officially encouraging massive immorality. For instance, many states are exploring how to legalize gambling because it is an effective way to build revenue. However, is this constructive for the people who are gambling? What about economic policies that further exacerbate the divisions between the world's rich and poor?

A serious situation arises when material illusions become so strong that sin is committed boldly and collectively, becoming a prominent part of the culture. I have personally observed the effects of this global delusion in my travels around the world. Meanwhile, our other guardians are not proving themselves to be any better. For example, America has been uncovering many instances of disrespectful treatment, rape and abuse in mental health institutions and nursing homes. There have been numerous expos'e9s in the media reporting the stories of helpless, bedridden, paralyzed patients who were raped in their wards by so-called health practitioners.

## Loving Coalition

By Akrura Das

"*It requires more spiritual understanding,*  
*selflessness, commitment and sacrifice.*"

The idea of collective care may be found in the Srimad-Bhagavatam, ancient extremely potent wisdom literature, in connection with the sages of Naimisaranya attempting to understand what is the highest good for humanity. This very idea may be largely applied to our modern ISKCON situation in leading/managing our Temples and projects. It's not the same as mere teamwork, councils, boards, etc. because it implies highly people-oriented approach. By some, this is considered touchy-feely or sentimental, but it is not, because it requires more spiritual understanding, selflessness, commitment and sacrifice.

Collective care means that we have mature adults discussing very seriously how to fully benefit the people they lead, by engaging them according to their needs and talents and protecting them from dangers. It also means that several hearts and minds can understand more fully the individual which is under their care. Just imagine if someone is in crisis or in trouble for some reason, and then 4-5 leaders/managers are genuinely concerned how to help him. Mature caring leaders are thinking: "Let us help this devotee. We cannot allow him to sink deeper into suffering and troubles. Let's help him become stronger and more reasonable."

If one is a parent, then with one's spouse, child's welfare can be extensively discussed. Child will feel it and feel more protected and loved. If you do like that, how do you think people will feel in your Temple, project, or family? It might make a big difference.

Action: Make a Loving Coalition with a few like-minded devotees, colleagues in a workplace or a spouse and supply collective care to few individuals in your Temple, project or family.
