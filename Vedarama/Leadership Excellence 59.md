## Vedic Commander

By Satsvarupa Dasa Goswami

(Srila Prabhupada wrote:) I think we are becoming like a gigantic guerilla warfare movement fighting with maya. This travelling in buses is the best means to drive away maya and establish Krishna Consciousness all over the world. Prabhupada was glad to hear that temple presidents and zonal leaders were also going out with the travelling sankirtana parties. The Vedic concept of the commander, he said, is that he must fight in the front lines, not sit behind the scenes, sheltered. Having already witnessed how some of his leading disciples had become bogged down by managing many things, he advised them all to travel and preach, distributing literature wherever they went.

## Respect for Men and Women

By Ravindra Svarupa Dasa

In the Vedic culture...women were highly respected. If womanhood were offended, then all doom would be impending. ... They were regarded as mother and dealt with accordingly. Still, they had their place and a man was regarded as being more naturally equipped to deal with the tough world of business, commerce, and politics. Men and women are equipped for different kinds of austerities, but a man is especially suited to developing spiritual strength and performing hard labour. A man's psycho-physical nature makes him better suited to bearing worldly dealings.

A woman's nature is equally important for a properly balanced society. It should be protected and nurtured. The feminine touch is naturally more soft and gentle, more caring and nurturing-perfect for making the home a pleasant haven for the husband to return to and for the children to grow up in. Therefore, the feminine nature should be protected and allowed to grow in an environment that is safe. The degree to which a woman can participate in realm to which men are best suited is limited. And so is the degree to which a man may interfere in the realm to which women are best suited.

So, basically, the Vedic culture has a high regard for both masculine and feminine natures in society. It recognizes their necessity and their requirements. It recognizes that they are different and thus have different places to play out their functions. It allows for those different sets of qualities to specialize and flourish in their natural environments and to complement each other rather than compete.

## Mutual Trust

By Akrura dasa

Srila Prabhupada gave us the principle of love and trust. Trust starts with us. Transforming a temple, department, or family begins at the personal level. Other devotee's trust in us grows out of our personal trustworthiness - our character and competence. When mutual trust exists, devotees feel motivated to go out of their way to help and support one another. We may ask: What's it like being in a relationship with me? How well do I:

1. Keep promises and honor commitments?
2. Acknowledge and apologize for my mistakes?
3. Remain loyal to the absent?
4. Share information, both positive and negative, with people who need it?
5. Involve people in decisions that affect them?
6. Give credit where credit is due?
7. Communicate consistently, regardless of the situation or the person's authority or influence?
8. Honor confidential and sensitive information?
9. Discover the Benefits of Increased Trust.

The effects of increased trust reach deep into the heart and soul of devotees and temples. Why? Because trust lies at the very core of spiritual leadership and effective devotee interaction. Here are just a few of the benefits of building trust:

1. Increased personal trust and trustworthiness
2. Strengthened relationships and improved communication in all areas of missionary activities.
3. Sustained positive change, resulting in more effective and cohesive devotee teams.
4. Decreased frustrations caused by dysfunctional relationships.
5. Increased commitment to ISKCON's strategy and vision.
6. Drastic reduction or elimination of politics and infighting.
7. Improved quality of services and interactions in temples and projects.
