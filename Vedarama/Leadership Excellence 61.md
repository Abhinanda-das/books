# Leadership Excellence 61

## Excellent Quote

Good leadership means you must learn how to lead people. Yad yad acarati sresthas tat tad evetaro janah. If the srestha, the leader is ideal, then others will follow. But if the leader is not ideal, if he is not in the awareness of things, then people will be misguided.

\- Srila Prabhupada

## Interview with Pance Pandava dasa, Temple Manager in the Bhaktivedanta Manor

**LE:** What is your professional background? You joined Bhaktivedanta Manor management team as a quite new devotee.

**PPD:** I qualified as a Marine Engineer in the 1970's and then took further qualifications, similar to degree standard in the early 1980's. I was a Chief Engineer for about five years before I left the sea in 1987. During this time I developed some management skills, as I was responsible for the operation and management of all technical aspects of the ship I was on, these were mainly tankers carrying chemicals and petroleum products. When I left the sea I found my way into the technical aspects of Leisure Management. This was first at the Ice Rink in Oxford, and then later in larger complexes, ending at Guildford Spectrum, which is the largest Leisure Complex of it's kind in Europe. There I was directly responsible for a 31m budget and 15 staff. I was part of the senior management team responsible for the operation of the whole complex, employing over 150 staff and a turnover of over a 34.5m. I further developed my management skills during this time by practical experience and attending various management courses. When I joined Bhaktivedanta Manor in 1998 I started the Bhakta course and ended up being made Temple Commander half way through the course and then became Temple Manager in January 2000, which is the position I now hold.

**LE:** What is your experience in ISKCON as a manager?

**PPD:**I became Temple Commander about halfway through my Bhakta course, this was to facilitate the previous TC to move to Book Distribution on a full time basis. The TC post does not really carry great responsibilities, but it is probably the first time a devotee would have to deal with other devotees as an authority. As a new devotee this was difficult at times as I had to deal with more senior devotees, while all the time trying not to make offences. Again as Temple Manager it has been sometimes difficult to command the authority of the position being such a new devotee. However over time I have managed to earn the respect from senior devotees in the Manor community. The main part of my management in ISKCON has been dealing with people, most of the management principles I learnt in the commercial world still apply to dealing with the devotees.

**LE:** What one advice would you give to all new (and old) managers in ISKCON? What would be the most important principle to base one's management service upon?

**PPD:**Your question is in two parts but I feel the answer to both is the same. Truthfulness is one of the four pillars of religion, but I also believe it is one of the main principles of management. If one is honest with people, slowly they will begin to trust you and from that trust develops respect. One cannot command respect, it has to be earned and the only way to do that is to be honest and truthful in your dealings with people. When one has the respect of people they will then be more willing to do what you want them to do because they will feel that they want to do it for you, not that they are being commanded to do it. Remaining humble is also a prerequisite for someone in a position of authority, this does not mean he should allow himself to be downtrodden. One can be firm in ones position while still maintaining humility.
