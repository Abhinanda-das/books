It is said that the intelligent man should pick up nectar from a stock of poison, should accept gold even from a filthy place, should accept a good and qualified wife even from an obscure family and should accept a good lesson even from a man or from a teacher who comes from the untouchables. So for knowledge we can take it from anywhere. There should not be sectarianism, Oh, why shall I take knowledge from here and there? Wherever knowledge is available, we should take it. That is the real position of seeker of knowledge.

## Srila Prabhupada on Excellence

Just see, although you cursed the King, he was not at all afraid or sorry. Rather, he offered respect to you, called you mother and accepted your curse, thinking himself faulty. He did not say anything in retaliation. This is the excellence of a devotee.

As stated by Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, taror api sahisnuna. Just like a tree, a devotee can tolerate all kinds of curses and reversals in life. This is the excellence of a devotee.

Parvati might naturally have inquired how devotees become so exalted. Therefore this verse explains that they are narayana-para, simply dependent on Narayana. They do not mind reverses in life because in the service of Narayana they have learned to tolerate whatever hardships there may be. They do not care whether they are in heaven or in hell: they simply engage in the service of the Lord. This is their excellence.

## Interview with Bhakta Vatsala dasa

*Bhakta Vatsala Dasa is a seminar facilitator of Vaisnava Training and Education*

**LE:** Is there any 'paradigm shift' in ISKCON leadership today?

**BVD:** In my limited experience, I see a variety of attitudes in ISKCON leaders. There are those firmly situated in Jurassic Park, who oppose anything they perceive as new or a challenge to the way they've always done things. They may see any suggestions of reform as potentially dangerous for their position and status. On some level they may even realise incapacity to make the sort of adjustments that reform demands, and therefore oppose reforms. Others (who have possibly had leadership thrust upon them) are unable to understand the fundamental differences in various leadership approaches, and are consequently satisfied with the way things are, under the motto "if it's not broken, don't fix it."

Some appreciate reform ideas, but are too set in their ways to break out of reactionary habits. From such leaders we receive support - moral and/or practical. Many that I have come in contact appreciate that there is a great need for reform and are capable of adopting progressive methods that align with a principle centred approach. They support us in many ways and appreciate that education is a major, proactive step toward empowering ISKCON leadership.

**LE:** Are there senior leaders in ISKCON interested in training or you mainly get new leaders on your seminars?

**BVD:** We get a good mixture. In the most recent seminars (in New Vrindavana and Los Angeles) we had Radhanath Swami, Malati dd, Svavasa d., Candra-sekara Swami, Candra-mali Swami, Jai Sri dd., Nityodita Swami and Radha dd (all senior ISKCON leaders) as well as department heads and other lower-key leaders. An interactive educational seminar is always an exciting context for leaders of various levels of experience and expertise to associate together.

**LE:** What do you expect to accomplish by your workshops?

**BVD:** By the end of the course the students should have a clear understanding of the difference between leadership and management. They should be convinced of the value of proactive, principle centred leadership, and they should have gained the knowledge, skills and values necessary for such leadership. We hope to convince current leaders and especially provide a progressive alternative to future leaders in our society.

## The Exploded Frog

The little son of a fat mother frog came hopping home to the pond in great excitement.

"Mother, today I saw something wonderful!" he announced excitedly.

Mother, who had been dozing in the sun on a big lily leaf, opened her eyes and with only faint interest asked, "What was that, son?"

"Oh, it was such a huge animal - so big it is difficult to describe!"

Mother chuckled indulgently. "And how big would you say it was, little fellow?"

The child-frog's eyes widened with innocent wonder. "Till today, Mother, you were the biggest creature I'd ever seen. But this animal was much, much bigger than you!"

Mother became a little indignant at this comparison. She puffed up her throat and asked, "Was the creature you saw as big as I am now?"

"Mother, much bigger!"

She puffed herself up even more. "What it so big?"

"Mother, much, much bigger!"

"This big?"

And so the mother frog kept inflating her body more and more, while the baby continued to cry, "Bigger, Mother, bigger!" Finally the poor mother frog burst with a bang.

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Gosvami used to say in connection to this story, "It is better to be a good one than a big one." Thinking oneself to be big - even in spiritual pursuits - is the cause of falldown. "I am Brahman", "I am siddha",

"I am self-realized", "I am a pundit", even "I am a Vaisnava" are all egotistical assertions. Rather, the serious aspirant for spiritual advancement should realize himself as an insignificant particle of dust under the lotus feet of his spiritual master and the Vaisnavas.

## Leadership Tips

By Bhakti-tirtha Swami

As a leader, you can help create an atmosphere where people begin to experience the higher pleasures.

Seek positive lessons inherent in apparently negative events.

As you seek the common denominator, look for how a higher good can come out of each situation, and take steps to make it happen.

Everything is available to you if you connect properly.

Always examine each situation carefully in order to gain the maximum benefit from each interaction.

If people do not move to a deeper level, they will remain imprisoned in suffering.
