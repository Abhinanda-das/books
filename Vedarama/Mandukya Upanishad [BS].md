# Mandukya Upanishad \[BS\]

## Santi sukta

*Oṁ bhadraṁ karnebhiḥ śṛṇuyāma deva bhadraṁ pasyemākṣabhir yajatrāḥ*  
*Sthirair aṅgas tuṣṭuram sastanūbhir vyaśema deva-hitam yad ayuḥ*  
*Om svasti na indro vṛddhaśravāḥ*  
*Svasti nah puṣā viśvavedāḥ*  
*Svasti nas tārkṣyā’ riṣṭanemiḥ*  
*Svasti no bṛhaspatir dadhātu*

**Перевод:**

**Devas of the senses,**  
**By your mercy with our ears we hear the auspicious message of the upaniṣads and see with our eyes auspicious objects. With firm limbs and healthy body, engaged in praising the Lord, may we live a long spiritual life.**  
**May the lord of unequalled glories bestow blessings on us**  
**May the Lord, the nourishing sun, giving all knowledge, and help us finish our studies without obstacle.**  
**May Garuḍa, carrier of Viṣṇu, who bears the cakra, take us to our goal.**  
**May the Lord of words, lord of intelligence, bestow power to learn.**

### Verse 1

*Om ity etad akṣaram idaṁ sarvam tasyopa-vyakhyanam*  
*Bhūtam bhavad bhaviṣyad iti sarvam oṁkāra eva*  
*Yac cānyat tri-kālātitam tad apy oṁkāra eva*

**Перевод:**

**Om indicates the akṣara brahma, the embodiment of all truth. Its explanation of om as akṣara is as follows.**

**Everything in the past, present and future is oṁkāra.**

**What is beyond the three measures of time is also oṁkāra.**

**Комментарий:**

For the qualified person (adhikārī), aspiring for liberation, for the attainment of supreme bliss while extinguishing all suffering (payojana), the Upaniṣad speaks of worship of the Lord (viṣaya), indicated by oṁkāra, in its whole form and its components. First the whole is described.

Brahman is called om as the entire universe of conscious and unconscious entities is contained within it (otam). Brahman is called akṣara because it is not destroyed (ksara) and remains in one form in past, present or future. Sarva indicates the whole of the conscious and unconscious worlds. It is called oṁkāra because it is indicated (akriyate) by om. Anything beyond the changes of time is also oṁkāra. There is nothing else like Brahman.

### Verse 2

*sarvam hy etad brahma ayam ātmā brahma*  
*so’yam ātmā catuṣpāt*

**Перевод:**

**Brahman is perfect and whole. Brahman is ātmā, the controller of everything. It has four forms.**

**Комментарий:**

This Brahman is perfect, complete.

*paramam yo mahad brahma*

**That great Brahman is supreme. Viṣṇu-sahasra-nāma**

*tad eva brahma paramam kavīnām*

**This Brahman is supreme for the wise. Taittirīya Āraṇyaka**

*Pūrṇaḥ adaḥ pūrṇam idam etc.*

**Brahmanis complete. Though the world emanates from him, he remains complete. Īśopaniṣad**

Who is the controler of all the acitivies of the jīvas? Ayam ātmā. Ātmā mean he who controls (adan) everything from lord Brahmā downwards. This completes the explanation of the whole word oṁ.

Having described the whole word, now the four syllables (a u m nada) are explained for worship of the forms of Bhagavān as viśva etc. In the waking state this form as vaiśvanara has seven limbs and nineteen faces. This is described in the succeeding verse. The Brahma designated by om is situated as the controller of all entities as ātmā in four parts.

### Verse 3

*jagārita- sthāno bahis prajñaḥ saptāṅga ekona-viṁśati mukhah sthūla bhug vaiśvānaraḥ prathamaḥ pādaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The first form called Vaiśvānara is situated in the eye. His function is comprehension of the outer world during waking states. He has seven limbs and nineteen faces. He enjoys the gross objects.**

**Комментарий:**

Now the places, functions, forms and objects of enjoyment are respectively described.

Jagārita-sthāna means residing in the eye. Bahis prajña means being aware of external objects. Seven limbs means four hands, two feet and the face of an elephant. The nineteen mouths consists of one central elephant face and nine human faces on each side. Sthūla-bhuk means he who enjoys gross objects. Viśva means “known to all, or gross objects.” Vaiś

va is the enjoyer of gross objects. Nara means one who does not decrease (na riyate). He resides in all bodies as the controller (ātmā). He is the first form of ātmā, the lord.

### Verse 4

*svapna-sthāno ‘ntaḥ prajñaḥ saptāṅga ekona-viṁśati mukhah*  
*pravivikta-bhuk taijaso dvitīya pādaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The second form is situated in the place of sleep, where the jīva sees dream objects. Its function is internal knowledge. It has seven limbs and nineteen faces. It enjoys subtle objects. It is called taijasa. It is the second form of the Lord.**

**Комментарий:**

The place where the jīva perceives dream objects is the throat. Anta-prajña means one who makes the jīva perceives internal objects in the form of impressions, or mental objects. The limbs and the faces are the same as in the previous part. It enjoys the external objects in the form of subtle impressions. It is called taijasa become it is consciousness filled with tejas or brightness. This is the second form of God, ātmā.

### Verse 5

*yatra supto na kañcana kāmaṁ kāmayate*  
*na kañcana svapnam paśyati tat susuptam*  
*susupta-sthāna-ekībhutaḥ prajñāna-ghana evānanda-*  
*mayo hy ānanda-bhuk ceto mukhaḥ prajñas tritīya-pādaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The third form resides in the place where the sleeping person has no desires nor sees dreams, in the centre of the heart. It unites with vaiśvānara and taijasa. It gives perception to the jīva. It enjoys complete bliss. Its face is knowledge. It is called prajña. It is the third form.**

**Комментарий:**

Supta refers to being covered by ignorance or attaining the Lord endowed with joyful form. By saying that he does not have desires or see dreams, it implies that he sees only himself. He is situated in the susupta-sthāna, on top of the lotus of the heart. This form merges with the taijasa and vaśsvānara forms and therefore called ekībhūta. Ghana refrs to the jīva, covered by the śakti of ignorance. He who gives susupti consciousness to the jīva is called prajñāna- ghana. Ānanda-maya means having deep bliss. He enjoys bliss without material objects. This form experiences complete, eternal bliss (ānanda-bhuk). Face indicates the other parts also. All the parts and limbs are knowledge (ceto mukha). Prajña means the one who does not give explicit knowledge (pra ajña), because prajña does not give awareness of either external or dream objects--only the form of the jīva and time. This is the third form or pāda of the Lord.

### Verse 6

*Eṣa sarveśvara esa sarva-jña eṣo’ ntaryāmy eṣa yoniḥ sarvasya prabhavāpyayau hi bhūtānām*

**Перевод:**

**This ātmā with four forms is the Lord of all, all knowing, the indwelling spirit, the source , the creation and destruction of all entities.**

**Комментарий:**

The fourth form will be described in verse 7. Now the glories of all four forms is presented. Eṣa means the one ātmā with four forms now being described. Antaryāmī means one who controls within. Sarvasya yoniḥ means the cause of all. To elaborate the meaning he is described as the place of creation and destruction of all entities. To emphasize this, some mantras are now given.

### Verse 7

*nāntaḥ prajñam na bahiḥ prajñam nobhayataḥ prajñam*  
*na prajñāna-ghanaṁ na prajñam nāprajñam*  
*adṛśyam avyavahāryam agrahyam alakṣaṇam acintyam*  
*avyapadeśyam ekātmā pratyaya-sāraṁ prapañcopaśamam*  
*śāntmā śivam advaitam caturtham manyante*  
*sa ātmā sa vijyeyah*

**Перевод:**

**He does not give internal awareness nor external awareness, nor a mixture of both. Nor does he give the awareness in deep sleep, the knowledge of the jīva. He does not give awareness. He is none of these forms of awareness, but it is not that he does not give awareness.**

**He is imperceptible to the other states. He is indescribable, indefinable, inconceivable, and unnamable. He is complete, full of attributes, the most blissful. He removes bondage, is peaceful, auspicious, and without false ego. This is the fourth form of the Lord. The ātmā in four forms should be understood.**

**Комментарий:**

The fourth form of the Lord is here described.

Antah prajña refers to the person who gives internal awareness or dream objects to the jīva. The fourth form does not do this. Nor does he reveal external objects of the waking state (bahya prajña) to the jīva. Concsiousness of both (ubhaya prajna) means to become aware of objects in the waking state and then perceive them in the dreaming state. The fourth form does not carry out this function either. Though the words describe functions, they refer to the forms of vaiśvānara and taijasa also.

Prajñāna-ghana should read ghana-prajña. This refers to the person who gives consciousness to the sleeping jīva covered in ignorance. The fourth form is not this.

Prajña refers to one who gives clear knowledge of objects conceived by the mind. The fourth form is not this. This action is carried out by the taijasa form of the Lord, being equivalent to the dream functions. These are not functions of the fourth form. This is the intended meaning.

In the waking state there may be a stunned condition where there is awareness but of no particular object. This is equivalent to the deep sleep state, ruled by the prajña form of the Lord. One should infer that these also are not functions of the fourth form. It is not under the jursidiction of either vaiśvānara, taijasa or prajña, nor functions for awareness of external objects, dream objects or awareness during sleep, or a mixture of objects from waking and dream states, nor to the experience during deep concentration (samādhi), nor to the state of inertia. If it is not any state of awareness , what is it? Is it without function at all? No, thus it is said: it is na aprajñam—it is not without awareness. There is denial of its not causing awareness. This means that this form causes the function of all types of knowledge for the liberated soul alone.

He is described as adṛṣṭa because he is not perceivable by any except the liberated person. He is agrahya except to the liberated soul. Alakṣaṇam: he cannot be measured by any of the other states of consciousness. Avyapadeśyam: because he is inconceivable, he cannot be designated. Eka means perfect. He is the complete, perfect ātmā. Pratyaya means form of knowledge and sāra means form of bliss. Upasama means he removes bondage. Or he is all pervading and blissful. Śāntam means devoid of the six afflictions. Śiva means without material happiness or distress. Advaita means without false knowledge, without the false conceptions of I and mine. These four forms are to be meditated on by the aspirant for liberation.

### Verse 8

*so’ yam ātmā ‘dhyakṣaram oṁkaro ’dhimatram pāda mātrā mātrāś ca pādā a-kāra u-kāra ma-kāra iti*

**Перевод:**

**The Paramātmā designated as oṁ is supreme, and imperishable. He has four forms designated by four divisions of the sound om: a, u m and nāda.**

**Комментарий:**

Having described the forms of the Lord within oṁkāra, now the syllables representing the forms are described. The Lord with four forms (ātmā), designated by om (oṁkāra), is above all (adhi) and indestructible (akṣara). Oṁ has many parts (adhi mātrā). What are these parts? That is described next. It has parts having four forms (pāda-mātrā) . These are represented by the sounds *a* for viśva, *u* for taijasa, *m* for prajña and nāda for prajñāna. The fourth (prajñāna) is not mentioned however.

### Verse 9

*jāgarita-sthāno vaiśvānaro’kārah prathama-mātrā*  
*āpter adimattvāt va āpnoti ha vai sarvān kāmān*  
*Adiś ca bhavati ya evaṁ veda*

**Перевод:**

**The first form, residing in the place of waking, the deity named vaiśvānara, is represented by the syllable a, because he provides enjoyment to the jīvas, and comes before the other forms. He who knows him as such, attains all desires and is the first among knowers.**

**Комментарий:**

First is described the form and place of the syllable “a”. “a-kāra” means “a “representing, the first form, prathama-mātrā. This syllable is supervised by vaiśvānara. Next is described the causes why vaiśvānara is represented by “a”. Because of āpti and adimattva. Āpti means attainment. Because Vaiśvānara helps the jīva to attain enjoyment of external objects (āpti) he is designated by the syllable “a”. Or because he comes before taijasa and prajña, being first (adiman), he is designated by the syllable “a”. As viśva combines both the prajña and taijasa forms, and has the power to arouse a person from the deep sleep and dreaming states, it is regarded as first (ādi) . Knowing that vaiśvānara is represented by “a” which has two reasons behind it, next the results of knowing is given. To the person who knows this—that vaiśvānara is represented by “a” (evam), -- vaiśvārana lets him attain all desires. “According to his capacity” should be understood. Ādi refers to viśva. Vaiśvānara is the cause of the jīva perceiving this, by supplying the jīva suitable body in liberation. Or the meaning can be: the knower of vaiśvānara is first.

(Madhva: for the jīva “āpti” means he gest all enjoyment and ādi means he is first to get liberated.)

### Verse 10

*svapna-sthānas taijasa ukāro dvitīya-mātrā*  
*utkarsād ubhayatvād votkarṣati ha vai jñana -santatiṁ*  
*samanaś ca bhavati nāsyābrahmavit-kule bhavati ya evaṁ veda*

**Перевод:**

**The second form, taijasa, supervising the dream state is represented by u, because he draws the jīva (utkarṣa) from the waking state and provides ubhayatva or dual nature--absence of external consciousess and awareness of dream objects. One who knows this attains knowledge, draws himself out of bondage and is counted among the liberated. His descendents will be knowers of Brahman.**

**Комментарий:**

This verse explains the second form represented by “u”. The second explansion of the Lord, in charge of the dream state, is represented by “u”. The reason for this is explained: because of utkarṣa and ubhayatva. This form makes the jīva give up his bodily identity in the waking state and pulls (utkarṣa) the jīva into sleep. And he gives sleep, in the form of absence of external consciousness as well as experience of subtle objects (ubhayatva). Therefore taijasa is called “u”. Next, the results of knowing this is described. He who knows taijasa and the reasons for his being represented by “u” attains jñāna-santatim, permanent knowledge. He draws himself from bodily bondage (utkarṣati) . He becomes respected or counted among the liberated souls (samāna). It is understood that this is done out of affection because he is without fault. Such a knower does not have offspring who are ignorant of Brahman.

(Madhva: for the jīva utkarṣati means he gets out of bondage and ubhayatva means he gets knowledge and liberation.)

### Verse 11

*susupta-sthānah prajño ma-kāras tritīya-mātrā miter apīter va minoti ha va idaṁ sarvam apītiś ca bhavati, ya evaṁ veda*

**Перевод:**

**The third form of Paramātmā, prajña, superintending the deep sleep state is conveyed by m, because he takes the jīva unto himself during this state and eliminates both waking and dream states. One who knows this knows the world according to his capacity and eliminates all distress.**

**Комментарий:**

*paramam yo mahad brahma*

He now speaks of the third syllable “ m,” the reasons and the results of knowing the reasons. Tritīya-mātrā means that this is the third manifestation of the Lord, represented by ma (ma-kāra). Why ma? Because of miti and apiti. Miti: the Lord causes the jīva to enter him; apīti: he causes the dissolution of vṛtti-jñāna. For one who knows prajña as the “ma’” along with the reasons for this designation, the Lord causes him to know the world (idam sarvam minoti) according to his capacity, or causes the world to enter himself. Though the jīva is minute, he shares the qualities of the Lord to some degree, like the sun ray and the sun. The meaning is that the jīva becomes liberated. The jīva becomes apīti, or causes dissolution of his sufferings.

Having described the three forms with their respective syllables, and having described the results, now verses are given to illustrate realization of Brahman, the fourth form.

### Verse 12

*a mātraś caturtho’ vyavahāryaḥ prapañcopaśamaḥ*  
*śivo ’dvaita evam oṁkāra ātmāiva*  
*saṁvisaty ātmānātmānam ya evaṁ veda ya evaṁ veda*

**Перевод:**

**The fourth form, who is not conveyed by a syllable, who is not describable, who removes bondage, who is auspicious, removing wrong knowledge, is conveyed by the nāda. One who knows this enters the fourth form of the Lord by his grace, experiencing bliss, entering or exiting by his free will.**

**Комментарий:**

After describing the fourth form previously, now the fourth form as represented by nāda is described. The fourth form represented by nāda (amātrā) is described again as imcomprehesible by the material senses, releasing one from matter, and as all auspiscious. The means are the same as before. They are repeated to show that the same qualities are the object of meditation by the practitioner and also the liberated soul. Then the results for the worshiper are described. Oṁkāra indicates the nāda aspect. One (ātmā), being completely purified, giving up attachment to body and house etc. who knows the fourth form represented by nāda, by the mercy of Paramātmā (ātmanā) enters (saṁviśati) the fourth form of the Lord (ātmānam) represented by nāda. Entering implies that he enjoys bliss according to his capacity, entering and exiting by his free will.
