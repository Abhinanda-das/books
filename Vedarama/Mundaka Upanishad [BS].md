# Mundaka Upanishad \[BS\]

## Śānti-pāṭaḥ

*Oṁ bhadraṁ karnebhiḥ śṛṇuyāma deva bhadraṁ pasyemākṣabhir yajatrāḥ*  
*Sthirair aṅgas tuṣṭuram sastanūbhir vyaśema deva-hitam yad ayuḥ*  
*Om svasti na indro vṛddhaśravāḥ*  
*Svasti nah puṣā viśvavedāḥ*  
*Svasti nas tārkṣyā’ riṣṭanemiḥ*  
*Svasti no bṛhaspatir dadhātu*  
*Om śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ*

**Перевод:**

**Devas of the senses,**  
**By your mercy with our ears we hear the auspicious message of the Upaniṣads and see with our eyes auspicious objects. With firm limbs and healthy body, engaged in praising the Lord, may we live a long spiritual life.**  
**May the lord of unequalled glories bestow blessings on us**  
**May the Lord, the nourishing sun, giving all knowledge, and help us finish our studies without obstacle.**  
**May Garuḍa, carrier of Viṣṇu, who bears the cakra, take us to our goal.**  
**May the Lord of words, lord of intelligence, bestow power to learn!**

## First Muṇḍaka

## Part 1

### 1

*oṁ brahmā devānāṁ prathamaḥ sambabhuva*  
*viśvasya kartā bhuvanasya goptā*  
*sa brahma-vidyāṁ sarva-vidyā pratiṣṭhām*  
*atharvāya jyeṣṭa-putrāya prāha*

**Перевод:**

**Brahmā, creator of the universe and protector of the world, manifested before the devatās. Brahmā spoke to his eldest son Atharvā the basis of all knowledge—knowledge of Brahman.**

**Комментарий:**

A short narrative is introduced to create interest in knowledge.

Four headed Brahmā appeared first, before the devatās like Indra. He is described as the creator and protector of all the worlds (goptā). This Brahmā spoke brahma-vidyā, the shelter of all knowledge, to his eldest son name Atharvā. This knowledge is the shelter of all knowledge since all knowledge is included in brahma-jñāna. If Brahman is known all things are known. This is stated by Vyāsa and others.

### 2

*atharvaṇe yāṁ pravadeta brahmā*  
*‘tharvā tāṁ purovācāṅgire brahma-vidyām*  
*sa bhāradvājāya satyavahāya prāha*  
*bharadvājo ‘ṅgirase parāvarām*

**Перевод:**

**Long ago, Atharvā taught to the sage Aṅgir that brahma-vidyā which Brahmā had spoken to him. Aṅgri spoke that knowledge to Satyavāha of Bhāravāja lineage. Satyavāha taught that parampara knowledge to Aṅgirā.**

**Комментарий:**

Brahmā spoke to Atharvā. Atharvā spoke to the sage names Aṅgir, his disciple. Aṅgir spoke it to Satyavāha of the Bharadvāja gotra. Bharadvāja spoke to Aṅgiras that knowledge received in succession (paravarām) or which produced all knowledge.

### 3

*śaunako ha vai mahāśālo ‘ṅgirasaṁ vidhivad upasannaḥ prapraccha*  
*kasmin nu bhagavo vijñate sarvam idaṁ vijñātam bhavatīti*

**Перевод:**

**Śaunaka, a great householder, approached Aṅgirasa and asked, “Dear sage! Knowing what does all become known?”**

**Комментарий:**

The son of Śunaka, a great householder, approached Aṅgirasa according to the rules—holding firewood in his hands etc. and asked “O bhagava!”

*utpattiṁ pralayaṁ caiva bhūtānām āgatiṁ gatim*  
*vetti vidyām avidyāṁ ca sa vācyo bhagavan iti*

**He is called bhagavān who knows creation and destruction, the coming and going of all beings, as well as knowledge and ignorance. Viṣṇu Purāṇa**

This stands for Bhagavān, but the “n” is omitted for metrical reasons.

Just as one knows all things make of clay by knowing a piece of clay, one can know all effects by knowing the special spiritual object (kasmin). I have heard in general this from persons like you. What is the nature of that spiritual object? What is the efficient and material cause of everything?

### 4

*tasmai sa hovāca dve diye veditavyo*  
*iti ha sma yad brahma-vido vadanti—parā caivāparā ca*

**Перевод:**

**Two types of excellent knowledge should be known in order to attain this object. The knowers of Brahman such as Parāśara say this.**

**Комментарий:**

*tat-prāpti-hetur vijñānaṁ karma coktaṁ mahāmune*  
*Āgamotthaṁ vivekāc ca dvidhā jñānaṁ tathocyate*

**O sage! O great sage! The cause of attaining him is knowledge and actions which purify existence Knowledge is of two types: from scriptures and by discrimination. Viṣnu Purāṇa**

Āgama means śabda-brahma: the Lord in the form of scriptures. The Lord is known through scriptures and discrimination. The knowers of brahman speak about that object, knowing which everything becomes known. The two types of knowledge are parakṣa and aparokṣa.

### 5

*tatrāparā ṛg vedo yajur vedaḥ*  
*sāma-vedo ‘tharva-vedaḥ, śikṣā kalpo vyākaraṇaṁ*  
*niruktaṁ chando jyotiṣam iti*  
*atha parā—yayā tad akṣaraṁ adhigamyate*

**Перевод:**

**The inferior knowledge consists of the Ṛg, Yajur, Sāma and Atharva Vedas, as well as śikṣā, kalpa, vyākaraṇa, niruka, jyotiṣa and chandas. The superior knowledge is that by which Parabrahman is achieved.**

**Комментарий:**

Starting with the insignificant, first, inferior knowledge is explained in order to promote hearing the Vedas with their primary aṅgas. This is knowledge of things beyond the senses (theoretical knowledge). Then the superior knowledge (realized knowledge) is described. *Tat* in tad-akṣaram refers to yad in verse three. Adhigamyate means that it is realized. This akṣara (the Lord) is realized by para-jñāna, seeing him after attaining the first type of knowledge generated from hearing, which produces seven types of sādhana such as discrimination.1 Adhigamyate means understood or attained.

### 6

*yat tad adreśyam agrāhyam agotram avarṇam acakṣuḥ-*  
*śtrotraṁ tad apāṇipādam*  
*nityaṁ vibhuṁ sarva-gataṁ susūkṣmaṁ*  
*tad avyayaṁ yad bhuta-yoniṁ paripaśyati dhīrāḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The wise man sees the indestructible entity, the cause of all beings, who cannot be seen, who is beyond the action senses, who belongs to no family, who is without varṇa, who is without eyes, without ears, without hands and without feet, who is eternal, the greatest, all pervading and the smallest.**

**Комментарий:**

He is beyond the knowledge gathering senses (adreśyam) and acceptance and rejection by the action senses (agrāhyam). He is without a family lineage (agotram). He does not have a varṇa such as brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya etc. He is devoid of knowledge gathering senses. He is devoid of action senses. He is not divided by time (nityam). He is not divided by space (vibhum). He enters into everything (sarva-gatam) since he is the smallest. These are the qualities of the avyaya entity. This refers to akṣaram of the previous verse. Those who have wisdom see the Lord as material cause of everything (bhūta-yonim). Yonim means material causes (upādāna) as is explained in the next verse with yathorṇanābhiḥ smṛjate.

### 7

*yathorṇanābhiḥ sṛjate gṛnate ca*  
*yathā pṛtḥivyām oṣadhayaḥ sambhavanti*  
*yathā sataḥ puruṣāt keśa-lomāni*  
*tathā ‘ksarāt sambhavatīha viśvam*

**Перевод:**

**As a spider produces a web from his body and then withdraws it, as plants grow from the earth, and as body hairs arise from a man, so this universe arises from the akṣara.**

**Комментарий:**

“In this world one sees that there is an efficient cause different from the material cause. But it is now said that knowing this, everything becomes known. It is not possible for the one Brahman to be the material cause of the unlimited material world which is very different from the Lord. When a pot arises from a lump of clay, the clay lump is dissipated. When a wall is made, the rectangular shape of the bricks disappears. Aksāra, being without form, cannot be the material cause, since it impossible for Brahman to dissipate or disappear.”

Just as the spider produces a web and withdraws it, the Lord is the independent creator. The earth is one entity and does not decrease or disappear but rather, it produces unlimited plants which are quite different from it.

A man, a conscious being, produces unconscious hairs. Similarly, the Lord produces the universe. The whole world composed of conscious and unconscious entities, which are very different from each other, arise from the Lord who is unchanging, who is very different, who is most excellent, and who is independent of other efficient causes.

*Yasmāt paraṁ nāparam asti kiñcid*  
*Yasmān nāṇiyo na jyāyo ‘sti kaścit*  
*Vṛṣkā iva stabdho divi tiṣṭhaty ekas*  
*Tenedaṁ pūrṇaṁ puruṣeṇa sarvam*

Not included in most versions.

There is nothing superior or inferior to Brahman. There is nothing junior or senior to Brahman. The one Brahman stands in the sky, solid like a tree. By this person the whole world is complete.

### 8

*tapasā cīyate brahman tato ‘nnam abhijāyate*  
*annāt prāno manaḥ satyaṁ lokāḥ karmasu cāmṛtam*

**Перевод:**

**Brahman, inclined to create, manifested as the world of names and forms. From this arose, prāṇa, from prāṇa arose mind, the enjoyers, the planets and happiness that from action.**

**Комментарий:**

The method of producing the universe from Brahman is described. Tapasā means “from knowledge,” since it will be said *yasya jñānamayaṁ tapaḥ*. By knowledge (tapasā) Brahman increased (cīyate). By his decision, Brahman becomes inclined to create the universe. Bahu syāma: may I become many.

From Brahman the unmanifested conglomeration of conscious and unconscious elements or enjoyed and enjoyed (annam) arose.

From that conglomeration, unmanifested, arose the chief prāṇa, the antaḥkaraṇa (manaḥ), the enjoyers (satyam), Svarga and other lokas, and happiness (amṛtam) depending on karma. Actions produce enjoyment. Or among actions, actions which produce liberation (amṛtam) arose.

### 9

*yaḥ sarva-jñaḥ sarva-vid yasya jñāna-mayaṁ tapaḥ*  
*tasmād etad brahma nāma rūpam annaṁ ca jāyate*

**Перевод:**

**From Brahman which is omniscient, who knows all, who is full of knowledge, arose unmanifested Brahman and then the names, forms and food.**

**Комментарий:**

Tapaḥ of the previous verse is defined as jñāna-mayam. Brahman who was called bhūta-yonim, is shown to have all knowledge to assist in creation.

He knows all topics (sarva-jñaḥ) (general knowledge) and has all types of knowledge concerning individual objects (sarva-vit). This verse mentions that he has knowledge of everything because of his nature and in detail, since that is necessary for creating the universe (mentioned in the previous verse). The word tapaḥ of the previous verse is defined. Without the decision of the Lord, no action suitable for creation will take place.

Then the succession of elements produces described in the previous verse are mentioned again.

From Brahman inclined to create the universe arises the unmanifested (avyākṛta or prakṛti). This produces the enjoyer and enjoyed (annam) with names and forms. Adyate ‘tti ca bhūtānī: food is eaten and eats all beings. (Taittirīya Upaniṣad). This mantra shows who the Lord created the unmanifest--through his omniscience. The previous mantra did not mention this.

“To avoid repetition, the word sarva-jñāḥ should mean Śiva. In this way the material cause of the universe can be explained to be different from the efficient cause Brahman.” That is not so. There is no difference in the akṣara brahman described in the previous verse and the Brahman who is sarva-jñaḥ and sarva-vit of this verse. If the efficient and material causes were different persons, then the statement that by one knowledge everything is known would be useless. The whole discourse would be meaningless.

## Part 2

### 1

*tad etat satyam*  
*mantreṣu karmāṇi kavayo yāny apaśyaṁs*  
*tāni tretāyāṁ bahudhā santatāni*  
*tāny ācaratha niyataṁ satya-kāmā*  
*eṣa vaḥ panthāḥ sukṛtasya loke*

**Перевод:**

**Brahman is eternal. Perform regularly many types of sacrifices in the three fires which the sages saw in the Vedas. O persons who desire Brahman! This is also the path for you to attain the pious planets.**

**Комментарий:**

This is eternal (satyam), devoid of six changes such as creation and destruction.

Those who were capable of directly seeing what is beyond the material senses (kavayaḥ) saw sacrifices in the Vedas (mantreṣu).

The sacrifices were endowed with many types (bahudhā santatāni) according to results desired, according to mantras and according to qualification, which should be performed throughout life, with three types of fires (tretāyām). O persons who desire parabrahma alone, with no material desires (satya-kāmāḥ)! Perform these sacrifices. They should be seen as means to attain Brahman, by jñāna. For those who do not desire liberation, this is the path to planets based on pious results (sukṛtaysa loke).

### 2

*yadā lelāyate hy arciḥ samiddhe havya-vāhane*  
*tadājya-bhāgāvantereṇāhutīḥ pratipādayet*

**Перевод:**

**When the fire flames up because of the oblations, one should throw offerings of ghee in the middle of the fire, between Agni (right side) and Soma (left side).**

**Комментарий:**

One should perform these karmas without material desire as an aṅga of brahma-vidyā. If one performs the karmas with material desires, one will not attain the promised result. Rather one get opposite results. Even performing the karmas properly, one cannot only attain up to Satya-loka.

When the fire flickers brightly from being kindled with oblations (samiddhe), one should throw offerings of ghee (āhutīḥ) into the middle of the fire between (antareṇa) Agni and Soma (ājya-bhāgau).

### 3

*yasyāgni-hotram adarśam apaurṇamāsam*  
*acāturmāsyam anāgrayaṇam atithi-varjitam ca*  
*ahutam avaiśvadevam avidhinā hutam*  
*āsaptamāṁs tasya lokān hinasti*

**Перевод:**

**If the fire sacrifice is devoid of darṣa, paurṇamāsa, cāturmāsya, āgrayana, or is without guests, not performed at the proper time, devoid of Vaiśvadeva offerings, and not performed according to the rules, the person cannot attain the seven planets.**

**Комментарий:**

One should perform sacrifices on the dark and full moons (darśa, paurṇamāsa). One should perform sacrifice in the autumn at the time of accepting new grains (āgrayaṇa). One should welcome guests and worship the Vaiśvadevas. It should be performed according to scriptures, with faith. If the sacrifice is devoid of these items, all pious results are destroyed for up to the seven generation of the sacrificer. Even though they are not aṅgas of the agni-hotra, since they give separate results, one should perform all nitya and naimittika karmas. That is the conclusion. If one karma is omitted among the karmas prescribed by the Lord in the śrutis and smṛtis, all other karmas performed bear no result. Performing karmas improperly is equivalent of not performing them at all.

### 4

*kalī karālī ca manojavā ca*  
*sulohitā yā ca sudhumra-varnā*  
*sphuliṅginī viśvarucī ca devī*  
*lelāyamānā iti sapta-jihvāḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The seven flaming tongues of fire are Kalī, Karālī, Manojavā, Sulohitā, Sudhumra-varnā, Sphuliṅginī and bright Viśvarucī.**

**Комментарий:**

Devī modifies Viśvarucī. Lelāyamānā modifies sapta-jihvāḥ.

### 5

*eteśu yaś carate bhrājamāneśu*  
*yathā-kālaṁ cāhutayo hy ādadāyan*  
*taṁ nayanty etāḥ sūryasya raśmayo*  
*yatra devānāṁ patir eko ‘dhivāsaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The oblations becoming rays of the sun and take the person who offers oblations to the seven fires at the proper time to the place where Hiraṇyagarbha resides.**

**Комментарий:**

The oblations, becoming rays of the sun take the person who performs (carate) these karmas, offering (ādadāyan) oblations to the seven blazing tongues of fire, not overlooking performance at the proper time (yathā kālam), lead the person to the place (adhivāsaḥ) where the Brahmā (devānām patiḥ) lives.

### 6

*ehy ehīti tam āhutayaḥ suvarcasaḥ*  
*sūryasya raśmibhir yajamānaṁ vahanti*  
*priyāṁ vacam abhivadantyo ‘rcayantya*  
*eṣa vaḥ puṇyaḥ sukṛto brahmalokaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The oblations, resplendent with the sun’s rays, carry the performer of sacrifices, respecting him and uttering pleasing words, saying, “Come, come! Pure Brahmaloka, earned by pious acts is yours.”**

**Комментарий:**

The oblations, shining because of being endowed with the rays of the sun, say pleasing words such as “Brahma-loka, pure (puṇyaḥ) and attained by pious acts (sukrṭaḥ), is yours.” Calling “Come, come!” they take the performer of sacrifices to Brahmaloka.

### 7

*plavā hy ete adṛḍhā yajña-rūpā*  
*aṣṭa-daśoktam avaraṁ yeṣu karma*  
*etac creyo ye ‘bhinandanti mūḍhā*  
*jarāmṛtyuṁ te punar evāpi yanti*

**Перевод:**

**Those who perform sacrifices in which there is inferior karma of eighteen parts are unable to cross saṁsāra. These fools rejoice, thinking that these are the best actions, but they go repeatedly to old age and death.**

**Комментарий:**

Acts leading to Brahmaloka are criticizes since the results are impermanent. Sixteen priests, the sacrifice, and the wife are the eighteen constituents of sacrifice. But this is an inferior (avaram) karma. The sacrificers who perform such rites are like living boats (plavāḥ), who cannot cross saṁsāra. Or the śrauta-karmas, in which there are eighteen parts to qualify the person for inferior karma, cannot take one across the material ocean of suffering.

Those who rejoice, thinking that these are is the best actions, repeatedly go to old age and death.

### 8

*avidyāyām antare vartamānāḥ*  
*svayaṁ dhīrāḥ paṇḍitam manyamānāḥ*  
*jaṅghanyamānāh paryanti mūḍhā*  
*andhenaiva nīyamānā yathāndhāḥ*

**Перевод:**

**Dwelling in ignorance, thinking themselves most intelligent, the fools, attacked by suffering, wander about, like blind persons led by another blind person.**

**Комментарий:**

Though without discrimination, they think they are very intelligent (dhīrāḥ). The fools wander about, attacked by various obstacles such as old age and disease.

### 9

*avidyāyāṁ bahudhā vartamānā*  
*vayaṁ kṛtārthā ity abhimanyanti bālāḥ*  
*yat karmiṇo na pravedayanti rāgāt*  
*tenāturāḥ kṣīṇa-lokāś cyavante*

**Перевод:**

**Existing in many types of ignorance, the fools think that they are successful. Because of attachment, they do not know. When their pious results are exhausted, suffering, they fall from Svarga.**

**Комментарий:**

This is further explained. The ignorant exist with many conceptions (bahudhā) of human, devatā etc. in the material world. They think they are successful. Because (yat) they do not know the truth because of attachment to Svarga (rāgāt), because of that lack of knowledge (tena) they suffer and fall. Kṣīṇe puṇye martya-kokaṁ viśaṇti: when their pious acts are exhausted they fall to earth.

### 10

*īṣṭāpūṛtaṁ manyamānā variṣṭhaṁ*  
*nānyac creyo vedayante pramūḍhāḥ*  
*nākasya pṛṣṭhe te sukṛte ‘nubhutve*  
*māṁ lokaṁ hīnataraṁ vā viśanti*

**Перевод:**

**Thinking that sacrifices and digging canals are the best, not knowing anything better, the fools, after experiencing the results of pious acts on Svarga, enter the earth or lower planets.**

**Комментарий:**

Īṣṭa means sacrifices. Pūrta means digging canals etc. They think these are the best actions for perfecting human life. They do not know anything else as the best. Having experienced the results of pious acts on Svarga, they enter earth again, or lower places such as hell.

### 11

*tapaḥ śraddhā ye hy apavasanty araṇye*  
*śānta vidvāṁso bhaikṣya-caryāṁ carantaḥ*  
*sūrya-dvāreṇa te virajāḥ prayanti*  
*yatrāmṛtaḥ sa puruṣo hy avyayātmā*

**Перевод:**

**The sannyāsīs, after performing śravana and manana, situated in the forest and controlling their senses, faithfully worship Brahman (tapaḥ śraddhāḥ). Having destroyed all sins, they pierce the sun planet, rejecting the inferior as mentioned in paryāṅka-vidyā of Kauṣitakī Upaniṣad.**

**Комментарий:**

*Sahasra-sthūṇe vimite dṛḍha ugre yatra devānām adhideva āste:*

**There, in a place with a thousand firm, huge pillars the lord of the devatās resides. Sama Veda, Jaiminīya-brāhmaṇa**

They go to the place where the person with a form exists (puruṣaḥ). Sadaika-rūpa-rūpāya namaḥ: I offer respects to the form of the Lord, always in one form. (Viṣṇu-sahasra-nāma-stotra) The word tapaḥ refers to Brahman as in the following. Ye ceme ‘raṇy śraddhā tapa ity upāsate: the faithful worship tapaḥ in the forest (Chāndogya Upaniṣad) śraddhā satyam upāsate: the faithful worship satyam. (Bṛhad-ārāṇyaka Upaniṣad) Here, satyam means brahman and this is then equated with tapaḥ in the previous quote. Thus tapaḥ means Brahman.

### 12

*parīkṣya lokān karma-citān brāhmaṇo*  
*nirvedam āyān nasty akṛtaḥ kṛtena*  
*tad vijñānārthaṁ sa gurum evābhigacchet*  
*samit pāṇiḥ śrotriyaṁ brahma-niṣṭham*

**Перевод:**

**The brāhmaṇa, seeing the planets attained by karmas, and becoming indifferent to them, understanding that the eternal Lord cannot be attained by karmas, with firewood in his hands, approaches a guru fixed in Brahman and skillful in scriptures, in order to know Brahman.**

**Комментарий:**

Confirming the completion of the standard method, surrender to guru is established. Analyzing by conclusive logic the planets attained by karmas (karma-citān), the person who (yaḥ to be supplied) has studied the principle Vedas and their aṅgas (brāhmaṇaḥ), understanding that the eternal person (akṛtaḥ) cannot be attained by karmas (kṛtena), becomes disinterested in karmas. He approaches a guru in order to gain knowledge of the Lord. In the next verse akṛtaḥ will be identified as the akṣaram-puruṣam. The word eva (only) indicates that approaching a guru is a rule.

He should not approach the guru empty-handed. Rikta-pāṇis tu nopeyād rājānaṁ daivataṁ gurum: one should not approach a king, devatā or guru empty-handed.2 The guru should have heard Vedānta (śrotriyam) and should have directly met the Lord (brahma-niṣṭham). Even though he has heard Vedānta, if he has not realized the Lord, because he has other tastes (impersonal), he should not be approached.

### 13

*tasmai sa vidvān upasannāya samyak*  
*praśānta-cittāya śamānvitāya*  
*yenākṣaraṁ puruṣaṁ veda satyaṁ*  
*provāca tāṁ tattvato brahma-vidyām*

**Перевод:**

**The guru will teach brahma-vidyā in truth to the student who has completely surrendered, who has controlled his mind and has controlled his senses. By that knowledge, he will know the eternal indestructible Lord.**

**Комментарий:**

Ṣamānvityāya means the disciple has controlled his senses. Praśānta-cittāya means he has controlled his antaḥkaraṇa. These qualifications are suitable for hearing. However, they are not enough for meditation. The word yena refers to vijñāna, which was mentioned previously: tad-vijñānārtham. This indicates the reason for approaching guru. Or it can be a change of gender (masculine), in reference to brahma-vidyām (feminine). Akṣaram means the Lord is unchanging in his svarūpa. Satyam means he is unchanging in his qualities. By these two features, matter and jīvas are excluded as the topic. Provāca means “the guru should speak.” This is explained in Vedānta-sāra. Placing the meaning of this statement in his heart, the Lord Vyāsa then said athāto brham-jijñāsā: now you should inquire about Brahman. (Vedānta-sūtra).

## Second Muṇḍaka

## Part 1

### 1

*tad etat satyam*  
*yathā sudīptāt pāvakād visphuliṅgāḥ*  
*sahasraśaḥ prabhavante sarūpāḥ*  
*tathā’ ksārād vividhāḥ saumya bhāvāḥ*  
*prajāyante tatra caivāpiyanti*

**Перевод:**

**O gentle one! Just as thousands of sparks of similar form arise from a blazing fire, so various entities arise from the Lord, akṣara, and then merge into him.**

**Комментарий:**

Many similar sparks arise from a blazing fire in a house. Similarly from akṣara-brahman, having a find body of cit and acit arise various gross cit and acit forms. They then merge into him (apiyanti).

### 2

*divyo hy amūrtaḥ purusaḥ sabāhyābyantaro hy ajaḥ*  
*aprāṇo hy amanāḥ śubhro hy akṣarāt parataḥ paraḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord is in the sky. He is the puruṣa without material form. He is inside and outside and is unborn. He has no material senses or material mind. He is without change. He is superior to the totality of jīvas who are superior to prakṛti.**

**Комментарий:**

Divyah can indicate that the Lord is related to the sky as in vṛkṣa iva stabdho divi tiṣthati ekaḥ: the one Lord is situated like a solid tree in the sky. (Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad)

He is without form as in tad apāṇi-pādam: the Lord without hands and feet (1.1.6)

He is the ātmā inside and outside of everything. Tenedaṁ pūrṇām puruṣeṇa sarvam: this whole universe is pervaded by the Lord. (Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad)

He is without senses, prāṇa and mind. Acakṣuḥ ṣrotam: he has no eyes or ears (Muṇḍaka 1.1.6)

He does not have a changing form (śubhraḥ). Yenāksāraṁ puruṣa veda satyam: by this knowledge a person knows the unchanging, eternal Lord. (Muṇḍaka 1.2.13)

He is superior (paraḥ) to the samasṭi puruṣa (parataḥ—all the jīvas) who is superior to the unmanifest, prakṛti (akṣarāt). He produces pradhāna and the jīva. Aksāra can also mean that which is enjoyed, or that which does not decay. It is not subject to change like its transformations. Thus it refers to the undeveloped or avyākṛta (prakṛti).

Akṣara here does not refer to he who is the source of all beings (bhūta-yoni), who is inside and outside of everything. He is superior (paraḥ) to this akṣara (akṣarāt parataḥ). It is impossible that anything is superior to him. Nor should parataḥ modify akṣarāt, since prakrṭi is not the jīva. Nor should parataḥ mean that the unmanifest is superior (parataḥ) to its transformations such as the samaṣṭi jīvas. Parataḥ should have something to be superior to. Nor does parataḥ means that the unmanifest arose accidently.

### 3

*etasmāj jāyate prāṇo mahaḥ sarvendriyāṇi ca*  
*khaṁ vāyur jyotir āpaḥ pṛthivī viśvasya dhāriṇī*

**Перевод:**

**From the Lord arises prāṇa, the mind, all the senses, ether, air, fire, water, and earth which supports everything**

**Комментарий:**

This describes the creation. The meaning is clear. Kham means ether, jyotiḥ means fire. The earth supports everything (viśvasya).

### 4

*agnir mūrddhā cakṣuṣī candra-sūryo*  
*diśaḥ śrotre vāg vivṛtāś ca vedāḥ*  
*vāyuḥ prāṇo hṛdayaṁ viśvam asya*  
*padbhyām pṛthivī hy eṣa sarva-bhūtāntarātmā*

**Перевод:**

**The sky is his head. The sun and moon are his two eyes. The directions are his ears. The Vedas are his function of speech. Air is his prāṇa. The universe is his heart. The earth is his two feet. He is soul of all beings.**

**Комментарий:**

Agniḥ here refers to the heavenly planets for it is said asau vai loko’ agniḥ (Bṛhad-ārāṇyaka Upaniṣad) The sages also say:

*dyāṁ mūrdhānaṁ yasya viprā vadanti*  
*khaṁ vai nābhiś candra-sūryau ca netre*  
*diśaḥ śrotre viddhi pādau kṣitaṁ*  
*So ‘cintyātmā sarva-bhūta-pranetā*

**The brāhmaṇas say that the sky is his head. Ether is his navel. The sun and moon are his eyes. The directions are his ears. Know that the earth is his feet. He is the inconceivable Lord, the creator of all beings. Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa**

The function of the voice, one of the senses is the Vedas (vāg-vivṛtā). Vāyu is his prāṇa. The great vāyu or prāṇa maintains his body. The whole universe is his heart, special flesh. The earth is his two feet (pādbhyām). He is the soul of all beings, since he maintains all their bodies.

### 5

*tasmād agniḥ samidho yasya sūryaḥ*  
*somāt parjanya oṣadhayaḥ pṛthivyām*  
*pumān retaḥ siñcati yositāyāṁ*  
*bahvīḥ prajāh puruṣāt samprasūtāḥ*

**Перевод:**

**From the Lord arose the heavens for which sun is the fuel. From the heavens arose the moon. From the moon arose plants on earth. The man impregnates the woman. From the supreme Lord many offspring were born.**

**Комментарий:**

From akṣara, the Lord, arose the heavens (agni, the first fire), of which the sun is the fuel (samidhaḥ). Asau vai loko ‘gnir gautama tasyādity eva samit: O Gautama, the heavens is thefire and the sun is its fuel. (Bṛhad-ārāṇyaka Upaniṣad) From the heavens arose the moon, from which arose rain, a second fire. From rain arose the plants on earth, which is the third fire. From plants arose man, the fourth fire. Man impregnates woman, the fifth fire, through oblations of semen. From the supreme person many progeny were born. This is according to pañcāgni-vidyā, knowledge of five fires.

### 6

*tasmād ṛcaḥ sāma yajūṁṣi dīkṣā*  
*yajñāś ca sarve kratavo dakṣiṇāś ca*  
*saṁvatsaraś ca yajamānaś ca lokāḥ*  
*somo yatra pavate yatra sūryaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**From the Lord arose the Ṛg, Yajur and Sāma Vedas, dīkṣā, yajñas, kratus, donations, the year, the sponsor and the planets which are purified by the sun and moon.**

**Комментарий:**

From the akṣara-puruṣa arose the Rg, Yajus and Sāma Vedas, the yajñas like agni-hotra, transformations of soma, kratus ( animal sacrifice), donations to the priests, the times such as the year, the sponsor and the results such as Svarga (lokāḥ), which are purified by the rays of the sun and moon.

### 7

*tasmāc ca devā bahudhā saṁprasūtāḥ*  
*sādhya manuṣyāḥ paśavo vayāṁsi*  
*prāṇāpānau brīhi-yavau tapās ca*  
*śraddhā satyaṁ brahmacaryaṁ vidhiś ca*

**Перевод:**

**From the Lord arose many types of devaṭās, sādhyas, humans, animals and birds, prāṇa and apāna, grains, austerity, faith, brahmacārya and nitya and naimittika karmas.**

**Комментарий:**

From the Lord arose many types of devatās, because of being from karma, jñāna and other principles. Vayāṁsi means birds. The rest is clear. Yava is barley. Brīhi-yavau indicates all types of grains. Tapaḥ means austerities. Śraddhā is faint in scriptures. Satyam is telling the truth. Brahmacaryṁ is avoiding association with women. Vidhīḥ refers to that which is prescribed--nitya and naimittika karmas.

### 8

*sapta-prāṇāḥ prabhavanti tasmāt*  
*saptāriciṣaḥ samidhaḥ sapta homāḥ*  
*sapta ime lokā yeṣu caranti prāṇā*  
*guhāśayā nihitāḥ sapta sapta*

**Перевод:**

**From the Lord arose the seven senses, the seven fires, fuel, the seven tongues of fire and the seven planets, where the seven senses, arranged by the Lord, wander about in the heart to the various organs during deep sleep.**

**Комментарий:**

From the akṣara-puruṣa arose the seven senses (prāṇāḥ) – tongue, two eyes, two ears and two nostrils. The seven fires starting with gārhapatyā arose from the Lord. Samidhāḥ means fuel wood. The seven tongues of fire (sapta-homāḥ) arose from the Lord. These (ime) seven planets arose from the Lord. In the cavity of the heart at the time of deep sleep, the seven senses, arranged by the Lord (nihitāḥ) wander about to the places of the physical senses such as the eye. Sapta sapta means the seven senses wander about in various persons.

### 9

*ataḥ sumudrā girayaś ca sarve*  
*‘smāt syandante sindhavaḥ sarva-rūpāḥ*  
*ataś ca sarvā oṣadhayo rasaś ca*  
*yenaiṣa bhūtais tiṣṭhate hy antarātmā*

**Перевод:**

**From him, all the oceans, mountains and rivers flowed. From him arose all the plants and juices. Because of this, the Lord, the ātmā within, remains with all things.**

**Комментарий:**

The rivers have many forms such as Gaṅgā (sarva-rūpāḥ). Plants arise from earth and rasas from plants like sugar cane. How does everything arise from the Lord? Since the akṣara puruṣa is surrounded by all the elements and remains as the ātmā of everything, even though plants arise from earth, one can say that they ultimately arise from the Lord (who is in the plants).

### 10

*puruṣa evedaṁ viśvaṁ karma*  
*tapo brahma parāmṛtam*  
*tad yo veda nihitaṁ guhāyāṁ*  
*so ‘vidyā-granthiṁ vikiratīha saumya*

**Перевод:**

**O gentle one! The supreme Lord is the universe. He is action to create it and deliberation upon it. He is Brahman, from whom one experiences the highest bliss. He who knows the Lord situated in the heart rejects the knot of ignorance.**

**Комментарий:**

Since the Lord is the inner ātmā of everything, he is everything (idam). Since he is the material cause, by knowing him everything becomes known. Karma means all dealings favorable for creating the universe. Tapaḥ means deliberation on what has been created. The Lord is experienced as the highest bliss for those persons who have become freed from the control of prakṛti. O gentle one! He who knows this akṣara-brahman situated in the heart rejects ignorance, which is like a knot, difficult to get free from (avidyā-granthim).

## Part 2

### 1

*āviḥ sannihitaṁ guhācaraṁ nāma*  
*mahat-padam atraitat samarpitam*  
*ejat prāṇan nimiṣac ca yat*  
*Eeaj jānatha sad-asad vareñyaṁ*  
*paraṁ vijñānād yad variṣṭham prajānām*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord resides near the jīva, dwelling in his heart. He is famous. He is the great goal. Living beings in waking and deep sleep are offered to him. Know this Lord, object of prayer for gross and subtle beings, who is worshipped by all people and is superior to the jīva.**

**Комментарий:**

Avīḥ means that the Lord manifests himself directly to yogīs. He is very near, since he resides in the cavity of the heart. He is famous for being difficult to understand (nāma). He is the great goal to be attained.

Ejat means trembling, or the waking state. Nimiṣat means deep sleep. Pranat means living beings. In waking or deep sleep they are offered to the Lord. He is worshipped (vareṇyam) by all people (tad ajānām) by all subtle and gross entities (sad-asat). Know this Lord who is the best, superior to the jīva (vijñānāt).

### 2

*yad arcimat yad aṇubhyo ‘ṇu ca*  
*tasmiṇ lokā nihitā lokinaś ca*  
*tad etad akṣaraṁ brahma sa prāṇas tad u vāg manaḥ*  
*tad etat satyaṁ tad amṛtaṁ tad veddhavvvyaṁ saumya viddhi*

**Перевод:**

**The aksāra brahman is effulgent, smaller than the smallest. In him reside all the planets and their residents. This akṣara brahma is prāṇa, speech and mind. Know this real Lord, beyond death by the concentrated mind, O gentle one!**

**Комментарий:**

The Lord is effulgent (arcimat), to be seen with a particular form. He is smaller than the small. He can thus enter within everything. In him are placed all the planets and their residents. This is the aksāra-brahma. He is prāṇa, speech and mind, since they are part of him. Know this Lord who is beyond death or is enjoyed by persons beyond saṁsāra (amṛtam), by the concentrated mind.

### 3

*dhanur gṛhītvaupaniṣadaṁ mahāstraṁ*  
*śaraṁ hy upasānisitaṁ sandadhīta*  
*āyamya tad bhāvagatena cetasā*  
*lakṣyaṁ tad evākṣaraṁ saumya viddhi*

**Перевод:**

**O gentle one! Taking up the bow (omkāra) and fixing on it the arrow known as the ātmā made sharp by worship, drawing back the bow, with mind concentrated on the Lord, know that one can attain the Lord.**

**Комментарий:**

Taking up the bow in the form of oṁ, famous in the Upaniṣads, one should string the arrow--the ātmā, distinguished from the gross and subtle bodies, and sharpened by worship of the Lord, along with the great weapon of the eight syllable mantra. Drawing back the bow with a mind inclined to the Lord, know that the akṣara is to be attained. Drawing the bow means “remembering Paramātmā as the whole and jīva as the part”. One should place the arrow on the bow with a desire to attain the Lord (tad-bhāva-gatena). One should aim at the Lord while withdrawing the sense and mind from other objects. O gentle one! Know that the Lord can be attained.

### 4

*Praṇavo dhanuḥ śaro hy ātmā brahma tal-laksyam ucyate*  
*Apramattena vedhavyam śaravat tanmayo bhavet*

**Перевод:**

**Praṇava is the bow and ātmā is the arrow, aimed at the Lord. With concentration on him, one should attain the Lord, and should become similar to Paramātmā just as an arrow merges with the target.**

**Комментарий:**

The previous verse is explained. This is a metaphor. Om is the bow since it is the cause of the ātmā offering itself to the akṣara. The Lord is to be attained by a concentrated mind, devoid of other thoughts (apramattena). The arrow merges with the target. By praṇava, the jīva offers himself to Paramātmā, where a different type of form (material) disappears. Having realization, the jīva attains liberation, with similarity to the Lord. The jīva becomes a form of consciousness (spiritual form), without a form as a devatā and human. That is the meaning of becoming one (tan-mayaḥ).

### 5

*yasmin dyauḥ pṛthivī cāntarikṣam*  
*otaṁ manaḥ saha prāṇaiś ca sarvaiḥ*  
*tam evaikaṁjānatha ātmānam*  
*anyā vāco vimuñcathāmrṭasyaiṣa setuḥ*

**Перевод:**

**Know that supreme ātmā, in whom the heavens, earth, sky, and mind with all senses are interwoven. Give up other talks. He is the bridge to immortality.**

**Комментарий:**

Know that one Lord (ātmānam) in whom heaven, earth, sky, mind and senses are brought together (otam). The Lord is spread everywhere and controls everything. Give up talks other that the Lord.

What is the reason? The Lord is the bridge to immortality. The bridge allows one to cross over a river to the other bank. Similarly the Lord allows one to attain immortality across the ocean of saṁsāra.

### 6

*arā iva ratha-nābhau saṁhatā yatra nāḍyaḥ*  
*sa eṣo ‘ntaś carate bahudhājāyamānaḥ*  
*om ity evaṁ dhyāyatha ātmānaṁ*  
*svasti vaḥ pārāya tamasaḥ parastāt*

**Перевод:**

**In the heart, the nerves are fixed like spokes to a chariot hub. The Lord, manifesting in many ways, moves about within. Meditate on the Lord using oṁ. May you have auspiciousness to meditate, for crossing beyond ignorance.**

**Комментарий:**

There is a similar description in Nārāyaṇa-sukta. Santataṁ śilābhistu lambatyā kośa-sannibham: the lotus bud heart is surrounded by nerves.

In the heart the nerves are fixed like spokes to a chariot hub. The Lord, manfesting in many ways, moves about within. One should meditate on the Lord using oṁ, for crossing beyond ignorance which is present at the moment (pārāya), for attaining the Lord. May there be auspiciousness for you, engaged in meditation.

### 7

*yaḥ sarva-jñaḥ sarva-vid yasyaiṣa mahimā bhuvi*  
*divye brahma-pure hy eṣa vyomany ātmā pratiṣṭitaḥ*  
*manomayaḥ prāṇa-śarīra-netā*  
*pratiṣṭhito ‘nne hrḍayaṁ sannidhāya*  
*tad vijñānena paripaśyanti dhīrā*  
*ānanda-rūpam amṛtaṁ yad vibhati*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord who is omniscient, who knows all individuals, and whose glory is present on earth, resides in the spiritual sky in Vaikuṇṭha.**

**The Lord is understood by the pure mind, and is the supporter of the jīva’s senses and body. He is situated within the body, in the heart. The wise see this indestructible form of bliss by visualizing his form.**

**Комментарий:**

The Lord’s glory is to start the cycle of saṁsāra on earth (mahimā bhuvi). He is also glorious for performing pastimes on earth. In the spiritual sky (divye vyomani)), in Vaikuṇṭha (brahmam-pure), the Lord is established. This is the tripada-vibhūti mentioned by Vyāsa.

The Lord is understood by the pure mind (manomayaḥ). He is the leader of the senses and body. He supports the jīva’s senses and body. Or he has prāṇa as his body and is the master (netā). Manomayaḥ prāṇa-śārīro bhā-rūpaḥ: the effulgent Lord, grasped by the pure mind, has prāṇa as his body. (Chāndogya Upaniṣad) He is established in the jīva’s body which is a transformation of food (anne). He is situated in the heart within the body. The wise directly seen (paripaśyanti) the Lord whose form is bliss, untouched by saṁsāra (amṛtam), through worship (vijñānena) in which one visualizes the Lord as if he were present before one’s eyes.

### 8

*bhidyate hṛdaya-granthiś chidyante sarva-saṁśayaḥ*  
*kṣīyante cāsya karmāṇi tasmin dṛsṭe parāvare*

**Перевод:**

**The knot in the heart is cut. All doubts are destroyed. Karmas are destroyed on seeing the Lord.**

**Комментарий:**

The knots in the heart refers to attachment and hatred etc. residing in the antaḥkaraṇa, which are difficult to escape, like knots. Hṛdaya can mean the location of the heart, or the jīva. All doubts on all subjects are destroyed by becoming knowledgeable of everything through realizing the Lord. With the exception of prārabdha karmas, all karmas accrued over many lifetimes are destroyed. Destruction means destruction of the power to produce results.

*tad-adhigama uttara-pūrvārdhayor aśleṣa-vināśau tad-vyapadeśāt \|\|*

**With attainment of Brahman, present sinful actions have no effect and previous karmas are destroyed since that is indicated the śruti. Vedānta-sūtra**

The sātra says that the karmas have no effect, or no power to give their results.

The Lord is superior (pare) to all beings, including Brahmā, who are inferior (avare). Or the Lord is the ātmā of all beings high and low. Seeing the Lord means that one meditates on the Lord in such a way that it is if the Lord were present before one’s eyes.

*aśva iva romāṇi vidhūya pāpam:*

**He is like a horse, shaking off all sin.**

*dhūtvā śarīram akrtaṁ kṛtātmā brahma-lokam abhisambhavāmi*

**Freeing myself from the dead body, having done what is to be done, I attain the world of the Lord.** ***Chāndogya Upaniṣad***  [**8.13.1**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/chandogya/8.13.1)

One should not think that merely giving up the body frees one from papa and puṇya.

*sāmparāye tartavyābhāvāt tathā hy anye*

**The scripture says that when one has attained prema, this type of meditation and deliberation is not necessary since there is no bondage to overcome. Brahma-sūtra**

When one sees the Lord, all karmas are destroyed. However, how does this take place? When one worships the Lord who is affectionate to the person who just beings the worship of his form, karmas will be destroyed. This should be one’s determination. However, it is not wrong to think that one should tolerate karmas till one gives up the body in the future.

### 9

*hiraṇmaye pare kośe vijrajaṁ brahma niṣkalam*  
*tac chubhram jyotasāṁ jyotis tad yad ātmā-vido viduḥ*

**Перевод:**

**Those who know the Lord realize him as the person without parts, beyond the guṇas, without faults, the revealer of the jīva’s senses, in the attractive place, which protects the Lord like a casket.**

**Комментарий:**

The supreme place is self-manifesting or most attractive (hiraṇmaye), like a casket (kośe), where one keeps a valuable treasure. The Lord is beyond sattva, rajas and tamas (virajam) and without parts. He is without faults (śubhram). He is the revealer of the senses which illuminate (jyotisāṁ jyotiḥ). The Lord emanates light from his form. Those who know the Lord realize this.

### 10

*na tatra sūryo bhāti na candra-tārakaṁ*  
*nemā vidyuto bhānti kuto ‘yam agihḥ*  
*tam eva bhāntam anubhāti sarvaṁ*  
*tasya bhāsā sarva idaṁ vibhāti.*

**Перевод:**

**In that place the sun does not shine, nor the moon or stars. Lightning does not flash what to speak of fire burning. They shine following the Lord who shines. By his light everything shines.**

**Комментарий:**

This verse explains how the Lord is the light of all lights. The sun and moon do not shine unless the Lord shines. “But we directly perceive the sun shining, illuminating the world. This statement seems contradictory.” This is then explained. The illuminous objects of the world such as sun have light because it is given by the Lord. It is not their own light.

### 11

*yad āditya-gataṁ tejo jagad bhāsayate ’khilam \|*  
*yac candramasi yac cāgnau tat tejo viddhi māmakam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Know that the light of the sun, moon and fire, which illuminates this universe, is My light. BG** [**15.12**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/15.12)

**Комментарий:**

The luminaries do not have independent light. They are like persons who wear borrowed ornaments. By his light, everything shines. Since all light is given by the Lord, they do not have their own light. One should also see the explanation given in Kaṭha Upaniṣad.

### 12

*brahmaivedam amṛtaṁ purastād brahma*  
*paścād brahma dakṣiṇata cottareṇa*  
*adhaś corddhaṁ ca prasṛtaṁ brahmaivedaṁ viśvam idaṁ variṣṭham*

**Перевод:**

**Brahman is spread in front. Brahman is spread behind. Brahman is spread to the right and to the left. Brahman is spread above and below. The whole world is excellent Brahman.**

**Комментарий:**

Everything seen in all directions is Brahman.

## Third Muṇḍaka

## Part 1

### 1

*dvā suparṇā sayujā sakhāyā*  
*samānaṁ vṛkṣaṁ parisāsvajāte*  
*tayor anyaḥ pippalaṁ svādvatti*  
*anaśnan anyo abhicākaśīti*

**Перевод:**

**Two birds, friends, of similar qualities, reside in the same tree. One bird eats the ripe fruit and the other bird does not eat, and remains glorious.**

**Комментарий:**

When the Lord enters all bodies, does he experience the pain and happiness of the jīva?

Sayujā means having similar qualities. The jīva and the Lord are like two birds, having similar qualities, sitting in the same body, which may be cut down at any time like a tree. The jīva enjoys the ripe fruit, the results of karma. The Lord, not eating the fruit, shines.

### 2

*samāne vṛkṣe puruṣo nimagno*  
*‘nīśayā śocati muhyamānaḥ*  
*juṣṭaṁ yaḍa paśyaty anyam īśam*  
*asya mahimānam iti vīta-śokaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The jīva absorbed in the tree, bewildered, laments because of prakṛti. When he sees the pleased Lord and recognizes the Lord’s greatness, he gives up lamentation.**

**Комментарий:**

The jīva is bewildered by prakṛti (anīśayā), which is to be enjoyed. Or anīśayā can mean “because he is incapable.” He loses awareness that he is the servant of the Lord and becomes full of bliss because of prakṛti. In one body thinks he is fat or thin. Identifying with a body made of earth and water, he thus experiences suffering caused by contact with matter.

When the jīva, giving up absorption in the fruits of the tree and sees the Lord who controls the world, who is pleased (juṣṭam) by the jīva’s actions, who is different from the jīva, since the Lord is his maintainer, regulator and chief being, when he sees the Lord’s glory, the jīva gives up lamentation.

A person who sinks in mud becomes bewildered about the mud and, because he cannot escape, laments. A person who has not sunk in the mud can rescue that person out of friendship. Just seeing that, the person in the mud gives up lamentation.

### 3

*yadā paśyaḥ paśyate rukma-varṇaṁ*  
*kartāram īśaṁ puruṣaṁ braham-yonim*  
*tadā vidvān puṇya-pāpe vidhuya*  
*nirañjanaḥ paramaṁ sāmyam upaiti*

**Перевод:**

**When the seer sees the Lord of resplendent complexion, who is the creator of the world, the ruler of the world, and who is the material cause, that learned person is free of all sin and piety, and being pure, attains a form similar to the Lord’s.**

**Комментарий:**

Paśyaḥ means “one who sees.” When the seer sees the Lord endowed with a shining, auspicious form (rukma-varṇam), who is the controller and creator of the world (kartāram), who is the material cause of the world, though not being transformed (brahma-yonim), attains a similar form.

*bhagavān iti śabdo ‘yaṁ tathā puruṣa ity api*  
*nirupādhī ca vartete vāsudeve sanātane*

**The word Bhagavān means puruṣa. These two, beyond matter, reside in eternal Vāsudeva.**

The puruṣa is Vāsudeva. When one sees him then sin and piety are destroyed. Free of all contamination, the jīva attains a form similar to the Lord’s, which has eight qualities. Brahma yonim can also mean the Lord is Brahman who is the source of everything.

### 4

*prāṇo hi eṣa yaḥ sarva-bhūtair vibhāti*  
*vijānan vidvān bhava tenātivādī*  
*ātmā-kṛiḍa ātmam-ratiḥ kriyāvān*  
*eṣa braham-vidāṁ vairṣṭhaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord is the shelter of all beings. The wise person knows the Lord through hearing and contemplation. Become a person who only speaks of the Lord. Become a person who plays with the Lord, who delights in the Lord and performs selfless actions. He is the best knower of Brahman.**

**Комментарий:**

Prāṇa refers to the supreme Lord as in sarvāṇi ha vā imāni bhūtāni prāṇam evābhisaṁviśanti: all beings enter into the Lord. (Chāndogya Upaniṣad) The supreme Lord (prāṇaḥ) is taken shelter of (vibhāti) by all beings. Knowing him by hearing and contemplation, the man in knowledge should become an ativādī, one who talks to everyone about the Lord. One who speaks of the supremacy of the Lord who is to be worshipped by all is an ativādī.

The ātmā-krīḍa is a person who plays with the Lord, not in gardens etc. He takes pleasure in the Lord (ātmā-ratiḥ), not in garlands or sandalwood. Vyāsa speaks of this enjoyment in the bhūmā adhikāraṇa of Vedānta-sūtra. He performs actions no seeking results (kriyāvān). Become an ātmā-krīḍa and ātmā-rati.

Why does he perform actions? Developing a pure antaḥkaraṇa by actions, he becomes the best knower of Brahman by the production of brahma-vidyā.

### 5

*satyena labhyas tapasā hy eṣa ātmā*  
*samyag jñānena brahmacaryeṇa nityam*  
*antaḥśarīre jyotir mayo hi śubhro*  
*yaṁ paśyanti yatayaḥ kṣīṇa-doṣāḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord is to be attained by truth, by control of the senses and mind, by knowledge, and constant brahmacārya. The wise, free of all faults, see this faultless Lord who is knowledge within the body.**

**Комментарий:**

Those free of attachment and other faults (kṣina-doṣaḥ) see the faultless (śubhraḥ) Lord, full of knowledge (jyotir-mayaḥ) within the body. The supreme tapas is concentration on the Lord by the mind and senses. By concentration of mind and senses, by knowledge arising from that, by constant avoidance of women (brahmacāryena), by words which give benefit to all beings (satyena), the Lord is directly visible or attainable (labhyaḥ). He is attained by worship.

### 6

*satyam eva jayati nānṛtaṁ*  
*satyena panthā vitato devayānaḥ*  
*yenākramanty ṛsayo hy āptakāmā*  
*yatra tat satyasya paramaṁ nidhānam*

**Перевод:**

**Truth, not untruth, is victorious. By speaking truth, one attains the progressive path leading to liberation. The sages, without material desire, went by this path. There, they attained the supreme goal, by speaking truth.**

**Комментарий:**

The last verse mentioned satyam. That is praised in this verse. The speaker of untruth is defeated by the speaker of truth in this world. The path of the devatās, spread out as progression through the sun etc. is attained by truth. The speaker of truth attains this path. The path is described. The sages who were devoid of material thirst (āptakāmāḥ) travelled by this path. What did they attain? At that place the speaker of truth attains Brahman personified, the highest goal.

### 7

*bṛhac ca tad divyam acintya-rūpaṁ*  
*sūkṣmāc ca tat sūkṣmataraṁ vibhāti*  
*dūrāt sudūre tad ihāntike ca*  
*paśyatsv ihaiva nihitaṁ guhāyām*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord is large and dwells in the highest sky. He has an inconceivable form. He is finer than the jīva. He is far away and dwells in his abode. But he is in the heart within the body, of those who see Brahman.**

**Комментарий:**

What is attained by following the arcādi path is explained. The Lord is great (bṛhat) in form and qualities. He is in his abode of the highest sky (divyam). Possessing the most attractive form, beyond mind and words, being able to enter the jīva, he is most subtle (sūkṣmataram). He is more subtle than the jīva who can enter unconscious matter. He is very far from matter and is in his supreme abode (antike) and is within the sun which is within the universe. He dwells in the space of the heart (guyāyām) of persons who see Brahman.

### 8

*na cakṣuṣā gṛhyate nāpi vācā*  
*nānyair devais tapasā karmanā vā*  
*jñāna-prasādena viśuddha-sattvas*  
*tatas tu taṁ paśyate niṣkalaṁ dhyāyamānaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**He is not attained by the eye or words, by the senses, by austerity or karmas. Meditating on the Lord who is undivided, becoming pure hearted by the mercy of the Lord, one sees the Lord.**

**Комментарий:**

He is not seen by the material senses (devaiḥ). Jñāna means Paramātmā since he is the cause of giving knowledge. Jñāna means that by which something is known. Prajñā ca tasmāt prasṛtā purāṇī: the ancient wisdom emanated from the Lord. (Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad). Meditating on the Lord, who is undivided (niṣkalam), one becomes pure in heart (viśuddha-sattvaḥ) by the mercy of the Lord. Then one makes him the object of thought by awareness of him as if actually seeing him.

### 9

*eṣo ‘ṇur ātmā cetasā veditavyo*  
*yasmin prāṇaḥ pañcadhā saṁviveśa*  
*prānaiś cittaṁ sarvam otaṁ prajānāṁ*  
*yasmin viśuddhe vibhavaty eṣa ātmā*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord is difficult to understand, but should be realized by the mind. The five prāṇas take shelter of him. The mind along with the senses of all beings take shelter of him. Then the Lord is pleased, the jīva becomes endowed with eight spiritual qualities.**

**Комментарий:**

The prāṇa, divided into five parts, takes shelter in the Lord (yasmin). The senses of all beings (prajānām) and the mind (cittam) along with the senses take shelter of him. When the Lord is pleased (viśuddhe) the jīva becomes endowed with the eight qualities. Or the Lord, difficult to understand (aṇuḥ) is understood by the pure mind.

### 10

*yaṁ yaṁ lokaṁ manasā saṁvibhāti*  
*viśuddha-sattvaḥ kāmayate yāṁś ca kāmān*  
*taṁ taṁ lokaṁ jayate tāṁś ca kāmāṁs*  
*tasmād ātmā-jñaṁ hy arcayed bhuti-kāmaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The pure person attains whatever planet he desires to attain, and whatever attractive object he desires. Desiring wealth, he should worship the Lord who knows him.**

**Комментарий:**

Whatever planet a man is determined to achieve (saṁvibhavati) by his mind, whatever objects (kāmān) (such as women) he desires, he gains control (jayate) of that planet and those objects. Since the Lord can have his devotees attain any planet or object of desire, since all planets and objects are under his control, the person desiring wealth should worship the Lord, thinking, “The Lord (ātmā-jñaḥ), if he pleased, will bestow to me this boon.”

## Part 2

### 1

*sa vedaitat parama brahma dhāma*  
*yatra viśvaṁ nihitaṁ bhāti śubhram*  
*upāsate puruṣaṁ ye hy akamās*  
*te śukram etad ativartanti dhīraḥ*

**Перевод:**

**He knows the self-manifesting, supreme Brahman, in whom all jīvas reside. The wise, giving up all desires, worship the Lord and surpass birth.**

**Комментарий:**

The knower of the Lord attains liberation by worship. The knower of ātmā knows the supreme Brahman, called dhāma, where all desires are fulfilled, in whom all jīvas manifest (viśvam) and who is self-manifesting (śubhram). The wise who desire liberation and have no desire except the Lord (akāmāḥ) worship the Lord (puruṣam) as the supreme soul. They surpass the last dhātu, semen. This means they surpass birth.

### 2

*kāmān yaḥ kāmayate manyamānaḥ*  
*sa kāmabhir jāyate tatra tatra*  
*paryāpta-kāmasya kṛtātmanas tu*  
*ihaiva sarve pravilīyanti kāmāḥ*

**Перевод:**

**He who, thinking of desirable objects, develops desires for them, is born on various planets because of those desires. But for the person who desires only the Lord, who knows ātmā, all desires for birth in this world are destroyed.**

**Комментарий:**

The person who, thinking that being a human or devatā is enjoyable, desires those objects, is born as human or devatā because of those desires (kāmabhiḥ). For the person who desires only the Lord (paryāpta-kāmasya), who has understood about ātmā (kṛtāmanaḥ), all desires to be born (iha) are destroyed.

### 3

*nāyam ātmā pravacanena labhyo*  
*na medhayā na bahunā śrutena*  
*yam evaiṣa vṛnute tena labhyas*  
*yasyaiṣa ātmā vivṛnute tanuṁ svām*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord cannot be attained by contemplation, by meditation or by hearing many scriptures. He is attained by the person whom the Lord chooses. For that person, the Lord reveals his form.**

**Комментарий:**

The Lord cannot be attained by contemplation (pravacanena), by meditation (medhayā) or by hearing many scriptures. He is attained by the person whom the Lord accepts (vṛnute). The person for whom the Lord has great affection is accepted by him. The Lord has great affection for the person who has great affection for the Lord. He who has great affection for the Lord attains the Lord. For that person, the Lord (ātmā) reveals himself (svām tanum). He gives realization of himself to that person.

### 4

*nāyam ātmā bala-hīnena labhyo*  
*na ca pramādāt tapaso vāpy aliṅgāt*  
*etair upāyair yatate yas tu vidvāṁs*  
*tasyaiṣa ātmā viśate brahma dhāma*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord is not attained by a weak mind, by an inattentive mind or by an āśrama devoid of proper markings. The learned man who endeavors by proper means attains para brahman.**

**Комментарий:**

The Lord is not attained by an absence of strength (bala-hīnena). Strength means absence of misery which is produced by time, place or bad qualities of the person. The Lord cannot be attained by an inattentive mind (pramādāt). He cannot be attained by the sannyāṣa āśrama, in which austerity is prominent. He cannot be attained even by sannyāsa devoid of the marks (aliṅgāt) of sikhā, thread, cloth bag and cloth for purifying water.This indicates other āśramas as well. All āśramas are qualified for brahma-vidyā (if they are conducted according to the rules). The meaning is: the Lord is not attained by a person of any āśrama devoid of its characteristics. The āśramas depend on their particular markings. The learned person, who endeavors for attaining Brahman by a strong mind, attention and āśrama with proper markings, attains param brahma or ātmā-svarūpa.

### 5

*saṁprāpyainam ṛsayo jñāna-trptāḥ*  
*kṛtātmāno vīta-rāgāḥ praśāntāḥ*  
*te sarvagaṁ sarvataḥ prāpya dhīrā*  
*yuktātmānaḥ sarvam evāviśanti*

**Перевод:**

**The seers of truth, attaining the Lord, become satisfied, free of material desire and in control of their senses. Attaining in all places the Lord who pervades everything, endowed with spiritual forms, they experience everything.**

**Комментарий:**

The seers of truth, realizing Paramātmā (enam) while living in their bodies, becoming satisfied (jñāṇa-tṛptāḥ) with realizing him (kṛtātmānaḥ), free of material desire (vīta-rāgāḥ), having controlled their senses (praśāntāḥ), attain Paramātmā who is spread in all things inside and outside, through all places, but who is also confined to a particular place. Manifesting spiritual forms (yuktātmānaḥ), they experience all things by a full developed spiritual consciousness.

### 6

*vedānta-vijñāna-suniścitārthāḥ*  
*sannyāsa-yogād yatayaḥ śuddha-sattvāḥ*  
*te brahma-loke parānta-kāle*  
*parāmrtāt parimucyanti sarve*

**Перевод:**

**Those sannyāsīs who are have gained control of their senses, who have renounced kāmya-karmas, who have understood Paramātmā by hearing Vedānta, who are fixed in the Lord, become liberated by the mercy of the Lord at the time of leaving their bodies.**

**Комментарий:**

Those who have understood Paramātmā by knowledge generated from hearing Vedanta (vedānta-vijñāna), who have purified their antaḥkaraṇas by rejecting kāmya-karmas (sannyāsa-yogāt) and thus control their senses (śuddha-sattvāḥ), those fixed in Brahman (brahma-lokeṣu), become liberated because of the Lord who is pleased (parāmṛtāt), at the time of death (parānta-kale).

*anta-kāle ca mām eva smaran muktvā kalevaram \|*  
*yaḥ prayāti sa mad-bhāvaṁ yāti nāsty atra saṁśayaḥ \|\|*

**At the point of death he who leaves the body remembering Me attains a nature like Mine. Of this there is no doubt*****. BG*** [***9.5***](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/9.5)***.***

*viśeṣaṁ da darśayati*

**In exceptional cases, the Lord takes the devotee. This is shown in scripture. Vedānta-sūtra** [**4.3.16**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vs/4.3.16)

Another version has brahma-loke paranta-kāle parāmṛtāḥ: having attained the Lord (parāmṛṭaḥ) in the Lord’s abodes, they become liberated.

*evam evaiṣa samprasādo 'smāt śarīrāt samutthāya paraṁ jyotir upasampadya svena rūpeṇābhiniṣpadyate sa uttamaḥ puruṣaḥ.*

**Receiving mercy, rising from his body, the jīva attains the supreme light and manifests with his own form. The Lord is the supreme person. Chāndogya Upaniṣad**

One should not think that the Lord has only one planet. Does the Lord who is full in all respects, who is all-pervading, whose every desire is fulfilled, not have innumerable, most astonishing spiritual planets manifested by his desire? This is proven by śruti, smṛti and itihāsa and stated by Vyāsa himself.

### 7

*gatāḥ kalāḥ pañca-daśa-pratiṣṭhā*  
*devāś ca sarve prati-devatāsu*  
*karmāṇi vijñāna-mayaś ca ātmā*  
*pare ‘vyaye sarva ekī bhavanti*

**Перевод:**

**The fifteen factors including the senses merge in the sense devatās. Remaining karmas and the jīva become one with the Lord, who is different from all else.**

**Комментарий:**

The sixteen elements are prāṇa, śraddhā ether, air, fire, water, earth, the senses, mind, food, vīrya, austerity, mantra, karma, lokas and name as mentioned in Praśna Upaniṣad. Excluding karma, there are fifteen elements mentioned in this verse. The senses (devāḥ) join with the sense devatās but do not disappear since they remain for the day of Brahmā.

*apye yatrāsya puruṣasya mṛtasyāgniṁ vāg apy eti, vātaṁ prāṇaś, cakṣur ādityaṁ, manaś candraṁ, diśaḥśrotraṁ, pṛthivīśarīram, ākāśam ātmauṣadhīr lomāni, nvamaspatīn keśā, apsu lohitam; ca retaś ca nidhīyate kvāyaṁ tadā puruṣo bhavati*

**When the speech of the dead person enters fire, when** ***prāṇa*** **enters air, when the eye enters the sun, when the mind enters the moon, when the ear enters the directions, when the body enters earth, when the self enters ether, when the body hairs enter herbs, when the head hairs enter the trees, when blood and semen enter water, where is the man? Bṛhad-āraṇyaka Upaniṣad** [**3.2.13**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bau/3.2.13)

*\|\| agny-ādi-gati-śruter iti cen, na, bhāktatvāt \|\|*

**If one argues that the śruti says that the senses merge into various elements, and thus do not accompany the jīva, it is not so, since those statements are figurative only. Vedānta-sūtra 3.1**

At the time of seeing the Lord, the karmas which have not given their results are destroyed. Those karmas and the ātmā with vijñāna become one with the Lord (avyaye) who is different from them. Those karmas whether favorable or unfavorable to the Lord become one with the Lord’s consciousness. Oneness of the jīva with the Lord means that the (previous) names and forms of the jīva are destroyed. Because the jīva’s karmas are destroyed, the fifteen items are of no use to the liberated jīva.

### 8

*yathā nadyāḥ syandamānāḥ samudre*  
*‘stam gacchanti nāma-rūpe vihāya*  
*tathā vidvān nāma-rūpād vimuktaḥ*  
*parāt-paraṁ puruṣam upaiti divyam*

**Перевод:**

**Just as rivers flowing into the ocean, giving up names and forms (colors), disappear, so the man in knowledge, liberated from names and forms attains the attractive supreme Lord.**

**Комментарий:**

This explains the last verse. Rivers like Gaṅgā, Yamunā and Sarasvatī, flowing from their sources, giving up their names and colors like white, black and red, seem to become one as they merge in the ocean. The wise person becomes free from names and forms which distinguish him in this world and attains the supreme person (divyam puruṣa). This supreme person was described previously as divyo hy amūrtaḥ purusaḥ. (2.2) Just as the water of the river and ocean are actually not one entity, but they give up distinguishable forms, so there is no absolute oneness of the Lord with the previously liberated jīvas. The liberated jīvas develop similarity to the Lord.

### 9

*sa yo ha vai tat paramaṁ brahma veda*  
*brahmaiva bhavati nāsyābrahmavit kule bhavati*  
*tarati śokaṁ tarati pāpmānaṁ*  
*guhā-granthibhyo vimukto ‘mṛto bhavati*

**Перевод:**

**He who knows the supreme Brahman is able to see the Lord. Those born in his family become knowers of Brahman. He surpasses suffering and sin. Free from the knots in the heart, he becomes endowed with spiritual qualities.**

**Комментарий:**

Knowledge progresses to interrupted meditation, uninterrupted meditation and continual meditation on the Lord in which one sees the Lord. This becomes filled with bhakti. He who knows (veda) the supreme Brahman, who has affection endowed with worship which is equivalent to seeing the Lord, becomes a person to whom the Lord manifests (brahma bhavati). Accompanying results are mentioned. In his family no one is born who is not a knower of Brahman. Freed from attraction and repulsion caused by three guṇas of prakṛti, he becomes endowed with the eight spiritual qualities (amṛtaḥ).

### 10

*tad etad ṛcā ‘bhyuktam*  
*kriyāvantaḥ śrotriyā brahma-niṣṭhāḥ*  
*svayaṁ juhvata ekarṣiṁ śraddhayantaḥ*  
*teṣāṁ evaitāṁ brahma-vidyāṁ vadeta*  
*śiro vrataṁ vidhivad yais tu cīrṇam*

**Перевод:**

**Giving the knowledge is described in the mantra.**

**Комментарий:**

The person who performs (cīrṇaṁ) the śiro-vrata5 should speak the knowledge which explain Brahman (etām brahma-vidyām), to those who perform nitya and naimittika karmas (kriyāvantaḥ), who have studied the Vedas (śrotriya), who desires to know Brahman (brahma-niṣṭhāḥ), who offer oblations in agni-hotra (ekarṣim) or who worships Paramātmā. Ekarṣim can mean the main ṛṣi. The term ṛṣi is applied to the Lord since he is omniscient.

### 11

*tad etat saytam ṛṣir aṅgirāḥ purovāca*  
*aitad acīrṇa-vrato ‘dhīte*  
*namaḥ parama-ṛṣibho namah parama-ṛṣibhaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The sage Āṅgirā spoke this knowledge of akṣara long ago. A person who is not a śiro-vrata should not learn it. All respects to the great sages. All respect to the great sages.**

**Комментарий:**

The sage Aṅgīrā spoke this knowledge about akṣara, the Lord, to Ṣaunaka.6 This should not be learned by a person who has not performed the śiro-vrata (acīṛṇa-vrataḥ). Respects are offered twice to show great respect or to indicate the end of the text.
