**The Story Before My Abrahamic Journey**

**The Discovery of Pagan Religions**

I don’t like telling personal stories, but in this case, I’ll make an exception. Almost by serendipity, I came to study the broader history of Abrahamic religions, and what I found stunned me. This is the story of what sent me in that direction, what I learned about Abrahamic faiths, and how that helped me understand the modern landscape of religions.

For most outsiders, the story of Abrahamic faiths is very simple. It involves a Prophet who arrived among the impious masses, and gave visions of the divine, which became a religious scripture, and thus arose a new religion. Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad are the prophets, and the Torah, the Bible, and the Koran are the scriptures. That is the extent of knowledge about Abrahamic faiths in most Indians, and I was not significantly better.

About a decade ago, I chanced upon a video that talked about a predecessor religion called Mithraism prevalent in Europe before Christianity. The name Mithra sounded familiar to me because he is one of the 12 Aditya in the Vedic demigod pantheon. Adityas are one of three groups of demigods, the other two being Rudra and Vasu. Mithraism immediately piqued my interest. Watching the video, I learned that Mithraism worshipped the sun god like Egyptians, and Christianity borrowed many Mithraic practices and weaved them into Christianity. One such practice, the video claimed, was the celebration of the birthday of the sun god on December 25th, which became Christmas. I don’t remember all the details, but there were at least a dozen important parallels between Mithraism and Christianity.

This was my first encounter with what I came to know later as “pagan religions”. The word “pagan” denotes a religious belief different from that held by the mainstream. More specifically, the term (Lat. paganus) was used by the early Roman Christians to designate the rural non-Christians living in the countryside. As I dug into the details, I realized that they were descendants and relatives of Greek, Roman, Babylonian, and Egyptian systems of demigods, collectively called “polytheism” by the Abrahamic religions. Today, paganism and polytheism are used interchangeably and nearly synonymously by most people.

In all pagan religions, different aspects of nature were personalized. There were gods of water, air, and fire. There were gods and goddesses of love and war. Planets like Sun, Moon, Jupiter, Mars, Venus, Mercury, and Saturn were personalized. Each of these gods had their own rituals. However, fundamentally, there was no animosity among them. They all existed in harmony with people having the freedom to worship their chosen deity. Today, we would call this “religious freedom”. I have recently learned that early Jews had pagan friends. They would visit the rituals and ceremonies of pagans. Even when they did not perform the rituals and ceremonies themselves, there was friendship between them.

**A Childhood of Religious Pluralism**

Growing up in India, I have seen the freedom to practice religion. In my school, I had Hindu, Muslim, Christian, Zoroastrian, Sikh, Jain, and Buddhist friends. We could identify them by their different names. But there was no animosity between us. Religion was never discussed and we studied and played together like we were one people. My parents never said anything adverse about any religion. My mother would sometimes go to a Church and get books about Christianity and Jesus. We all lovingly called Jesus “Yesuh Masih”. I attended many Church services because I loved to listen to the organ being played followed by the choir singing. I did not know the word secularism or what it meant. But we were living and growing up like there was no difference between any of the religions.

I was ignorant about the religious violence in all parts of the world, the destruction of native cultures, and their overtake by Abrahamic religions. Thanks to the Indian education system that washed away all religious crimes from history books, India’s colonization by the British was a political event, not a religious one. Even Islamic invasions were political rather than religious projects. Since I had mentally separated religion from society, politics, economics, and science, I thought everyone was doing the same. Parents all over the world tell their children about their past. But my parents never said anything about the past. It never occurred to me that I should ask them about it because there was no reason to.

**Writing of the Book Cosmic Theogony**

Therefore, in all innocence, I wrote the book Cosmic Theogony where I talked about the pagan histories of Abrahamic religions and subtitled it “The Personalization of Nature”. Theogony is the name of a poem written by the Greek poet Hesiod describing the genealogies of Greek gods and goddesses around the 7th century BCE. I saw that the pagan religions personalized nature by talking about demigods controlling various aspects of nature but Abrahamic religions did not. Overtly, they were quite different.

However, due to the pagan antecedents of Abrahamic faiths, there were pagan traits left over in all Abrahamic faiths. Jews symbolized their religion by stars because there was a preceding religion of Vasus who are symbolized by stars in the Vedic tradition. Christians drew a halo around Jesus because there was a preceding religion of Adityas that worshipped the Sun with halos around his deities. Muslims symbolized their religion with the moon because there was a preceding religion of Rudras that performed Tantric practices, worshipped Shiva-Shakti in many forms, and saw the moon as a heavenly place.

The connections that seem obscure to most people become crystal clear if we know the Vedic system of demigod worship. I discussed in Cosmic Theogony all world religions coming from a pantheon of demigods summarized into three groups called Vasu, Aditya, and Rudra, emanating from Brahma, Viṣṇu, and Shiva, respectively. This trinity in turn represents the three features of a person called sat-chit-ānanda. The worship of one form of divinity, therefore, uplifts the soul by one of its features, like lifting a person by the head, the torso, or the legs. A person can be lifted in many ways so they are valid methods of lifting. I talked of a religious unity based on the history of many religions coming out of one system.

This unity has been recognized in the West too. Carl Jung saw the similarities between Roman, Greek, Egyptian, and Babylonian religious systems. He created the theory of Jungian Archetypes to explain the recurrence of similar religious systems in geographically disconnected lands by claiming that they were all part of the collective unconscious. By embedding these deities in the human mind as forms of purity and perfection, he hoped to explain religious diversity through latent structures of the human mind.

I had not imagined that I would go any deeper into any religion. But I was hoping someone else would. The Dead Sea Scrolls include reincarnation and feminine divinities. I was hoping that someone would find out why these were excluded in later-day religious formulations and I would learn from them.
