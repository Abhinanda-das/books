# Mādhurya-kādambinī

## First Rain of Nectar - prathamāmṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ

### Verse 1

*hṛd-vapre nava-bhakti-śasya-vitateḥ sañjīvanī svāgamā-*  
*rambhe kāma-tapartu-dāha-damanī viśvāpagollāsinī*  
*dūrān me maru-śākhino ’pi sarasī-bhāvāya bhūyāt prabhu-*  
*śrī-caitanya-kṛpā-niraṅkuśa-mahā-mādhurya-kādambinī*

**Пословный перевод:**

*hṛt*—of the heart; *vapre*—in the agricultural field; *nava*—nine; *bhakti*—of *bhakti; śasya*—of the grains; *vitateḥ*—the strewn collection; *sañjīvanī*—bringing back to life; *sva*—its own; *āgama*—of the coming; *ārambhe*—at the very beginning; *kāma*—of lust; *tapaḥ-*ṛtu—of the summer season; *dāha*—the burning heat; *damanī*—which restrains; *viśva*—of the entire world; *āpaga*—the rivers; *ullāsinī*—which makes swell; *dūrāt*—from far away; *me*—of me; *maru*—in the desert; *śākhinaḥ*—a tree; *api*—also; *sarasī-bhāvāya*—for the restoring of the sap; *bhūyāt*—may it be; *prabhu*—of the Supreme Lord; *śrī-caitanya*—Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu; *kṛpā*—of the mercy; *niraṅkuśa*—unrestricted; *mahā*—great; *mādhurya*—of the sweetness; *kādambinī*—the cloudbank.

**Перевод:**

**It is the gift of life for the nine devotional crops strewn in the field of the heart. At the very onset of its approach it subdues the burning heat of the summer season of lust. It causes all the down-flowing rivers of the world to swell with delight. And even from a distance, I hope, that great cloudbank of sweetness which is the unfettered mercy of Lord Śrī Caitanya may also vitalize me, a dry tree in the desert.**

**Комментарий:**

The geography of the Indian subcontinent is such that the preservation of water has always been a major problem for the maintenance of normal life. In most places significant rain comes only during a single season. For the rest of the year water may become scarce, making especially the hot summer difficult to tolerate. It is a cause for great celebration, therefore, when the monsoon clouds finally move in from Arabian Sea to begin the rainy season.

Taking this idea as the source for an extended metaphor, Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī has written this first verse of his book to invoke auspiciousness. After many seasons of His absence, measuring an entire day of Brahmā, the most merciful Supreme Lord, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, has once again appeared on the horizon of this suffering world.

*anarpita-carīṁ cirāt karuṇayāvatīrṇaḥ kalau*  
*samarpayitum unnatojjvala-rasāṁ sva-bhakti-śriyam*  
*hariḥ puraṭa-sundara-dyuti-kadamba-sandīpitaḥ*  
*sadā hṛdaya-kandare sphuratu vaḥ śacī-nandanaḥ*

**“May the Supreme Lord who is known as the son of Śrīmatī Śacīdevī be transcendentally situated in the innermost chambers of your heart. Resplendent with the radiance of molten gold, He has appeared in the Age of Kali by His causeless mercy to bestow what has not been made available in a very long time: the most sublime and radiant mellow of devotional service, the mellow of conjugal love.” (Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī’s** ***Vidagdha-mādhava*** **1.2)**

The mercy of Lord Caitanya is unbounded and universal. Like a range of clouds expanding from one end of the sky to the other, it dispenses its rain indiscriminately, on fertile land and also in the desert. When a soul’s heart has been well prepared like a plowed and fertilized agricultural field, it is ready to respond to the Lord’s mercy and to bear through all the stages of gestation the crops of loving devotion that His mercy potency sows. One most important part of this preparation of the heart is the devotional education imparted in the scriptures *Bhagavad-gītā, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam,* and works of Lord Caitanya’s empowered devotees such as *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, Bhakti-sandarbha,* and this *Mādhurya-kādambinī.* Having been primed in this education by the tutelage of his spiritual masters and by practical service to his *gurus,* to the community of devotees, and to the needy populace of Kali-yuga, a soul’s heart is then well prepared to receive the monsoon rain of Lord Śrī Caitanya’s mercy.

This verse of invocation shows the author’s exceptional powers as a poet. Lord Caitanya’s sweet mercy is described as a range of pregnant rainclouds (*kādambinī*). This is what the theoreticians of Sanskrit poetry call *rūpaka,* or metaphor. Not just the mercy of the Lord is said to be *like* a bank of clouds (by the device of *upamā* or simile), but in the poet’s mind, which can create its own supernatural reality, it *is* that bank of clouds. The definition supplied by Śrīla Kavi-karṇapūra in his *Alaṅkāra-kaustubha* (8.16) is *rūpakaṁ tu tat* / *yat tādātmyaṁ dvayoḥ.* (“Describing the compared and what it is compared to as nondifferent is called *rūpaka.*”) The Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas prize *Alaṅkāra-kaustubha* as a definitive textbook on poetics, and Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī himself wrote a commentary on it.

When a poet uses the device *rūpaka* he is imagining two things to be the same that are actually different. What is needed to justify linking the two is some shared quality, like the radiant beauty common to someone’s face and the moon. This quality, the *sāmānya-dharma,* is sometimes expressed and other times left unspoken. In this invocation at the start of *Mādhurya-kādambinī,* Śrīla Viśvanātha has offered not only one but four such explicit *sāmānya-dharmas,* each enveloped in a vivid image of its own sub-metaphor. According to *Alaṅkāra-kaustubha* (8.51), this amounts to another ornament of meaning, called *parikara: viśeṣoktiḥ parikaraḥ syāt sākūtair viśeṣaṇaiḥ.* (“When something exceptional is expressed by using a number of insightful modifiers, that is called *parikara.*”)

The sweet rain of Lord Caitanya’s undeserved kindness revives the thirsty crops of devotion sown in the field of heart, left parched by long neglect. The first meaning of the word *vapra* is a plot surrounded by an embankment for retaining water, as is needed for water-intensive crops like rice. As rice needs a great deal of water to grow, *bhakti* to thrive requires continuous mercy from its bestower and attentive care from its beneficiary. The single word *vapra* here suggests this subtle idea, an example of expert use of a word’s meaning (*vastu*) to evoke the reverberation of a second thought (*vastunā vastu-vyañjanā*). Yet another reverberation is produced by the ornament (*alaṅkāra*) of *śleṣa* or double meaning in the word *nava. Nava* means “new,” and it also means “nine.” Newly sprouted crops need special protection. In the same way *bhakti* in its beginning, before the attainment of firm convinction (*niṣṭhā*), is very vulnerable to hostile influences. Real devotion, moreover, the pure devotion promoted by Caitanya Mahāprabhu, has nine significant varieties, the first of which are hearing and chanting about Kṛṣṇa and remembering Him. The sprouts of any other but these nine should be considered weeds and removed from the field; this is the responsibility of the field’s owner, and if he neglects it he cannot blame the rain for indiscriminately falling on weeds also. The suggestion of these additional thoughts by the ornament *śleṣa* is an instance of what is technically labeled *alaṅkāreṇa vastu-vyañjanā*.

More ideas are suggested by *vastu* and *alaṅkāra* in the further descriptions. As soon as the monsoon rains begin, immediate relief is felt from the oppressive summer heat, even though for the full nourishment of the crops the rain will have to continue for some time. Similarly, souls tormented by material life can expect quick mitigation of their suffering from the very start of their practicing devotional service. Although complete satisfaction, absolute understanding, and transcendental bliss take more time to accomplish, the initial release new devotees are granted from anxiety and confusion should encourage them to willingly make the efforts requested of them. Within the compound word *kāma-tapartu-damanī,* the lust which is the chief burden of suffering conditioned souls is equated by *rūpaka-alaṅkāra* with *tapa-ṛtu,* the season of excessive heat from the sun. Lust is a problem solvable only by *damana,* restraint, not by indulgence, just as there is no escape from the summer heat, day or night, until the rains come.

The coming of this mercy rain makes all the rivers swell with delight. The word *viśva* in the compound phrase *viśvāpagollāsinī* also has two meanings, “all” and “universe.” Normally rain is a local phenomena; a huge mass of clouds may extend for hundreds of miles, but to say that a rainfall is making rivers swell all over the universe is an obvious exaggeration for dramatic effect. The transcendental rain of Lord Caitanya’s mercy, however, does flood the entire creation, and the conditioned souls throughout the universe who are benefited by it can be also called *apaga*. The word *āpa-ga* (literally “movement of water”) means “river,” but by a coincidence of sound combination, the compound can be alternatively split as *viśva-apaga*. *Apa-ga* (starting with a short *a*) means “diverging” or “going away,” by which *viśvāpaga* can be understood as referring to the wayward souls wandering all over the material universe. They can all be eventually enlivened by the rain of Lord Caitanya’s mercy as the flood it creates expands to its inconceivable limits. And if we take the liberty of merging these two images, souls living in the material world, like rivers flowing from higher land to lower by the force of gravity, are constantly subject to the degrading pull of their selfish desires. This again is a novel idea suggested by the ornament *śleṣa,* where the double entendre is made possible by splitting up the components of a compound in two different ways (*sa-bhaṅga*), and it depends on the actual word being used (*śabda-alaṅkāra*) without allowing the substitution of a synonym like *nadī* for *āpaga.* The poet is thus imagining a wonderous situation, in which all the rivers in the universe are overflowing and have come to life, experiencing delight.

As for himself, Śrīla Viśvanātha draws the picture of a barely alive tree in a desert far away from the fertile fields of *bhakti.* This tree is praying to be restored to his natural condition, flowing with vital sap. Maybe, he hopes, the rain of mercy will reach even his desert home, which he shares with other sapless plants, the materialists and impersonalists who have no taste for the pleasure of Kṛṣṇa’s devotional service. By Lord Caitanya’s special mercy, may the *nīrasa* become *sarasa* and become able to enjoy life. This is the ornament *tad-guṇa:*

*sva-guṇaṁ tyaktvā*  
*praguṇasya samīpa-gam*  
*tasyaiva guṇam ādatte*  
*yad vastu syāt sa tad-guṇaḥ*

**“When something gives up its own quality to assume the quality of another, superior object it has come close to, that is called** ***tad-guṇa*****.” (*****Alaṅkāra-kaustubha*** **8.297)**

Simultaneously there is another ornament here, since the author is not actually disqualified for Lord Caitanya’s mercy as he describes himself: *virodhaḥ sa virodhābhaḥ* (“Apparent contradiction when there actually is none is called *virodha*.”) These ornaments suggest at least two more sublime conclusions. One is that *rasa,* the nectar of devotion, is the essence of all existence, the real difference between material and spiritual life, and that life without *rasa* is hardly being alive at all. Another is that Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī possesses the rarest saintly quality *dainya,* utter humility without any trace of selfishness or dishonesty, the actual key to obtaining the Supreme Lord’s full mercy.

### Verse 2

*bhaktiḥ pūrvaiḥ śritā tāṁ tu*  
*rasaṁ paśyed yad-ātta-dhīḥ*  
*taṁ naumi satataṁ rūpa-*  
*nāma-priya-janaṁ hareḥ*

**Пословный перевод:**

*bhaktiḥ*—devotional service to the Supreme Lord; *pūrvaiḥ*—by previous authorities; *śritā*—taken shelter of; *tām*—it; *tu*—but; *rasam*—as the ecstatic taste of loving service; *paśyet*—one can see; *yat*—by whom; *ātta*—granted; *dhīḥ*—intelligence; *tam*—him; *naumi*—I praise; *satatam*—constantly; *rūpa-nāma*—named Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī; *priya-janam*—the dear servant; *hareḥ*—of Lord Hari.

**Перевод:**

**Others in the past took shelter of devotional service, but it is by the insight provided by the beloved of Hari named Śrī Rūpa that one can recognize devotional service as** ***rasa.*** **I offer him my homage constantly.**

**Комментарий:**

This second verse of invocation reiterates the theme of *rasa.* Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī was directly authorized and empowered by Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu to be the teacher of the whole science of *rasa* to the followers of Lord Caitanya. Other, more ancient, schools of Vaiṣṇavism also know more or less in their own ways about the varieties of experience of personal exchange with the Supreme, but only Lord Caitanya and his followers headed by Rūpa Gosvāmī have fully revealed this realm of transcendence.

On the order of Lord Caitanya, Śrīla Rūpa wrote *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu,* which is known as the “grammar book of *rasa.*” What he explained in this book is the foundation of all knowledge in the Gauḍīya *sampradāya* of loving relationships with Kṛṣṇa and how to develop them. He also wrote plays and poetry expressing the intricacies of these ecstatic dealings. Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī is a faithful representative of the tradition of *rasa-śāstra* founded by Rūpa Gosvāmī, and has rendered Śrī Rūpa the services of composing a commentary on *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* and his short summary study *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu-bindu.*

### Verse 3

*iha khalu paramānanda-mayād api puruṣād brahma pucchaṁ pratiṣṭhā iti brahmato ’pi parāt-paro raso vai saḥ, rasaṁ hy evāyaṁ labdhvānandī bhavati iti śrutyā sūcyamāno mallānām aśanir nṛṇāṁ nara-varaḥ strīṇāṁ smaro mūrtimān iti sarva-vedānta-sāreṇa nikhila-pramāṇa-cakravartinā śrīmad-bhāgavatena rasatvena vivriyamāṇaḥ, brahmaṇo hi pratiṣṭhāham iti śrī-gītopaniṣadā ca evāyam iti sammanyamānaḥ śrī-vraja-rāja-nandana eva śuddha-sattva-maya-nija-nāma-rūpa-guṇa-līlāḍhyo ’nādi-vapur eva kam api hetum anapekṣamāṇa eva svecchayaiva jana-śravaṇa-nayana-mano-buddhy-ādīndriya-vṛttiṣv avatarate, yathaiva yadu-raghv-ādi-vaṁśeṣu svecchayaiva kṛṣṇa-rāmādi-rūpeṇa. tasya bhagavata iva tad-rūpāyā bhakter api sva-prakāśatā-siddhy-artham eva hetutvānapekṣatā.*

**Перевод:**

**He who is the Absolute Supreme—superior to even the all-blissful soul and, according to the words “Brahman is the tail, the foundation” (*****Taittirīya Upaniṣad*** **2.29), also superior to the impersonal Supreme—the** ***Vedas*** **allude by saying “He is** ***rasa*** **itself, and when the living being obtains this** ***rasa*** **he becomes ecstatic.” (*****Taittirīya Up.*** **3.31)** ***Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam,*** **the essence of the most advanced Vedic texts and ruling lord of all authoritative evidence, describes Him as** ***rasa:*** **“For the wrestlers He was a lightning bolt, for the men in general He was the best of men, and for the women He was Cupid in person.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.43.17)** ***Śrī Gītopaniṣad*** **confirms His identity in the statement “I am indeed the foundation of Brahman.” (*****Bhagavad-gītā*** **14.27) That darling son of the King of Vraja descends to this world in His eternal body and endowed with His unique names, forms, qualities, and pastimes, all composed of pure goodness. Without regard for any prior cause, He descends by His sweet will into the active functions of His devotees’ ears, eyes, mind, intelligence, and other faculties, similarly to how He appears by His own will in the dynasties of Yadu, Raghu, and others, as Kṛṣṇa, Rāma, and so on. And just like that Supreme Lord, His devotional service, whose nature is equal to His, also pays no regard to any cause to enable her self-made appearance.**

**Комментарий:**

One of the main purposes of *Śrī Mādhurya-kādambinī* is to depict what a devotee can expect to experience as he progresses through the stages of devotional practice to the perfection of unconditional love. The glories of the object of this love, the Supreme Lord in His original form of Kṛṣṇa, are not discussed much because it is assumed that the readers of this book will already be familiar with them. Those who need to be informed about why Kṛṣṇa should be the focus of their love can consult His own testimony in the *Bhagavad-gītā,* chapters 7–11, and the descriptions of His superexcellence in the Tenth Canto of *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (or its summary study *Kṛṣṇa, The Supreme Personality of Godhead*) and in Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī’s *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* (2.1, or chapters 21–24 of *The Nectar of Devotion*) and *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta.*

To establish beyond doubt the unique position of Kṛṣṇa, Śrīla Viśvanātha appeals first to the primary authority of the *Vedas,* specifically to the unquestionable teachings of the *Upaniṣads,* and then to the perfect distillation of Vedic revelation spoken by Kṛṣṇa Himself in the *Bhagavad-gītā* and by His devotees in the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam.* The *Taittirīya Upaniṣad* of the Black *Yajur Veda* describes in its second chapter five progressive concepts of the living person, the *puruṣa*. The most primitive idea is that the person is simply the visible body of flesh and vital fluids. More advanced thinking may discover the inner person within this physical person who is composed of the vital airs, then the yet more internal mental person, then the most internal spirit soul, and ultimately the Supreme Soul of all souls. The individual living being is the soul of his physical, prāṇic, and mental bodies, but the infinite Supreme Soul has the finite soul also as part of His cosmic body. All five of these are concepts of embodied personality, poetically imagined by the *Upaniṣad* as a bird’s body with its head, two wings, torso, heart, and tail. As a bird’s tail supports and balances its whole body, so the figurative tail of each of the five *puruṣas* serve as its support (*pratiṣṭhā*). The Personality of Godhead’s impersonal feature Brahman appears from Him as a person’s nonliving hair and nails appear from his living body. Brahman supports the Lord’s personal existence and that of everything created from Him. As the Supreme Personality says in His own words, *mayā tatam idaṁ sarvaṁ / jagad avyakta-mūrtinā* (*Bhagavad-gītā* 9.4: “By Me, in My unmanifested form, this entire universe is pervaded.”). And as the creator Brahmā prays,

*yasya prabhā prabhavato jagad-aṇḍa-koṭi-*  
*koṭiṣv aśeṣa-vasudhādi-vibhūti-bhinnam*  
*tad brahma niṣkalam anantam aśeṣa-bhūtaṁ*  
*govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi*

**“I serve the Supreme Personality of Godhead Govinda, the primeval Lord, whose transcendental bodily effulgence, known as the** ***brahma-jyotir,*** **which is unlimited, unfathomed and all-pervasive, is the cause of the creation of unlimited numbers of planets, etc., with varieties of climates and specific conditions of life.” (*****Brahma-saṁhitā*** **5.40)**

Soon after explaining the five *puruṣas,* the *Taittirīya Upaniṣad* goes on to describe the final *puruṣa* as the reservoir of all pleasure. The finite souls and the Supreme Soul share the same spiritual nature of eternity, full knowledge, and bliss. There is still a great difference between one and the other, however. The Supreme Soul is self-sufficiently omniscient and full of joy, and He bestows knowledge and bliss on others according to His pleasure. The finite souls, in contrast, are completely dependent on him for everything. Souls conditioned in material life struggle to fulfill their needs and desires, not knowing that all the assets they acquire come ultimately from the Supreme Lord alone. And souls liberated from the bondage of matter, who are ecstatically attracted to the Lord in pure love, enjoy intimate personal relationships with Him. The various flavors of these blissful exchanges are called *rasas*—passive appreciation, servitude, friendship, parental affection, conjugal love, and several other secondary moods. The object of love in all *rasas* is the Supreme Lord, especially in His original form of Kṛṣṇa, whom Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī has called *akhila-rasāmṛta-mūrti,* the embodiment of nectar in all the different *rasas.* Kṛṣṇa simultaneously attracts all kinds of devotees:

*mallānām aśanir nṛṇāṁ nara-varaḥ strīṇāṁ smaro mūrtimān*  
*gopānāṁ sva-jano ’satāṁ kṣiti-bhujāṁ śāstā sva-pitroḥ śiśuḥ*  
*mṛtyur bhoja-pater virāḍ aviduṣāṁ tattvaṁ paraṁ yogināṁ*  
*vṛṣṇīnāṁ para-devateti vidito raṅgaṁ gataḥ sāgrajaḥ*

**“The various groups of people in the arena regarded Kṛṣṇa in different ways when He entered it with His elder brother. The wrestlers saw Kṛṣṇa as a lightning bolt, the men of Mathurā as the best of males, the women as Cupid in person, the cowherd men as their relative, the impious rulers as a chastiser, His parents as their child, the King of the Bhojas as death, the unintelligent as the Supreme Lord’s universal form, the** ***yogīs*** **as the Absolute Truth, and the Vṛṣṇis as their supreme worshipable Deity.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.43.17)**

Kṛṣṇa Himself speaking in the *Bhagavad-gītā* echoes the terminology of the *Taittirīya Upaniṣad* while claiming that He is the foundation of that Brahman which the *Upaniṣad* refers to as the foundation of all existence (*brahma pucchaṁ pratiṣṭhā*). From this statement of Kṛṣṇa’s it can be concluded that mere impersonal realization of the Supreme is incomplete, because it misses the actual aim of life everyone strives for, the *rasa* of loving reciprocation with one who will never fail His beloved’s expectations.

When He appears in this world in His various *avatāras,* the Supreme Lord is not impelled by any force other than the love of His devotees. He is completely independent, and so also is His devotional service. As Kṛṣṇa makes His appearance wherever and whenever He likes, *bhakti* also show herself in someone’s heart by her own sweet will, unrestricted by any law of nature.

### Verse 4

*yathā hi, yato bhaktir adhokṣaje / ahaituky apratihatā ity-ādau hetuṁ vinaivāvirbhavatīti tatrārthaḥ. tathaiva, yadṛcchayā mat-kathādau, mad-bhaktiṁ vā yadṛcchayā, yadṛcchayaivopacitā ity-ādāv api yadṛcchayety asya svācchandyenety arthaḥ, yadṛcchā svairitety abhidhānāt. yadṛcchayā kenāpi bhāgyeneti vyākhyāne bhāgyaṁ nāma kiṁ śubha-karma-janyaṁ tad-ajanyaṁ vā. ādye bhakteḥ karma-janya-bhāgya-janyatve karma-pāratantrye sva-prakāśatāpagamaḥ. dvitīye bhāgyasyānirvācyatvenājñeyatvād asiddheḥ kathaṁ hetutvam.*

**Перевод:**

**Thus in words like “which leads to causeless and unimpeded devotion for the transcendental Lord” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **1.2.6), the idea is that** ***bhakti*** **makes her appearance without any cause. In the same way such phrases as “when \[faith develops\] spontaneously for My topics and so on” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.20.8), “or spontaneously \[one attains\] devotion for Me” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.20.11), and “which develops spontaneously,” the idea of the word “spontaneously” is “by her own free choice,” since the word** ***yadṛcchā*** **has the dictionary meaning “liberty.” If “spontaneously” is explained as indicating “by some good fortune,” we have to ask whether that good fortune originates from auspicious work or not. If the first were the case, devotional service would be produced by good fortune, which itself is produced by material work, and by becoming dependent on** ***karma*** **devotional service would fail to be self-manifesting. In the second case, the good fortune would be undefineable, thus unknowable, and so would not even necessarily be anything that actually exists. How, then, could it be said to be the cause?**

**Комментарий:**

*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* says that devotional service does not pay regard to any cause, expressing this sometimes by the term *ahaitukī* and sometimes by the synoymous term *yadṛcchayā.* Since there are various kinds of “causes,” there may be some doubt about which kind is disregarded by *bhakti.* A *hetu* or cause may be a causal factor that is necessary for producing some result (*kāraka-hetu*). Or it may be the cause for some fact becoming known (*jñāpaka-hetu*). Or it may be the purpose for which some action is performed (*prayojana*), what in Western philosophy is called “final cause.”

The reference in the Second Canto of the *Bhāgavatam’s* First Canto to “causeless” devotional service (*ahaitukī bhakti*) has been explained by Śrīla Śrīdhara Svāmī in his *Bhāvārtha-dīpikā* commentary by taking *hetu* in the sense *phalānusandhānam* (“expectation of a desirable result”) and then defining *ahaitukī* as “devoid of such an expectation.” Śrīla Viśvanātha offers as relevant to the current question of “what causes *bhakti* to appear?” an alternative, equally valid, interpretation: *Ahaitukī bhakti* means “devotional service who makes her appearance without any cause,” or in other words, without being necessarily preceeded by something else that makes or allows her to manifest. In Śrīdhara Svāmī’s version it is the devotee who has no *hetu,* while in Śrīla Viśvanātha’s it is devotional service. Here *yadṛcchayā* (“spontaneously”) is explained as *svācchandyena* (“by her own free will”), which means the same as the word *svecchayā* used above to describe Kṛṣṇa’s descent to this world.

In the modern study of physical science, the movement of the invisible atoms in a gaseous substance is said to be “random.” Ultimately there must be some particular forces impelling the bouncing about of each and every atom, but these forces are two many and complex for any scientist to calculate. “Random” therefore really means not “without any cause” but “whose cause is unknowable.” Science, however cannot predict anything definite from unknowable causes, but can only measure their results after the fact. As long as “random chance” isn’t redefined in terms of specific physical forces, it can’t be cited scientifically as the cause of anything. Similarly, “good fortune” may be proposed as the cause of devotional service, but what does “good fortune” consist of? That it consists of the results of pious activity is ruled out as inconsistent with the independence of *bhakti.* Then unless the speculator who proposed *bhāgya* as the cause can define it some other way, the term remains indefinite, not referring to anything in particular that could be the cause of something else.

### Verse 5

*bhagavat-kṛpaiva hetur ity ukte tasyā asārvatrikatvena tasmin bhagavati vaiṣamyaṁ prasajjate. duṣṭa-nigraheṇa sva-bhakta-pālana-rūpaṁ tu vaiṣamyaṁ tatra na dūṣaṇāvahaṁ praty uta bhūṣaṇāvaham eva, bhakta-vātsalya-guṇasya sarva-cakravartitvena sarvopamardakatvenopaviṣṭād aṣṭamyāmṛta-vṛṣṭau vyākhyāsyamānatvāt. nirupādhikāyās tad-bhakta-kṛpāyā hetutve vastuto bhaktānām api vaiṣamyānucitatve ’pi prema-maitrī-kṛpopekṣā yaḥ karoti sa madhyamaḥ iti madhyama-bhakta-vaiṣamyasya vidyamānatvād bhagavataś ca sva-bhakta-vaśyatvena tat-kṛpānugāmi-kṛpatve na kiñcid asāmañjasam, yato bhakta-kṛpāyā hetur bhaktasyaiva tasya hṛdaya-vartinī bhaktir eva. tāṁ vinā kṛpodaya-sambhavābhāvād iti bhakteḥ sva-prakāśatvam eva siddham.*

**Перевод:**

**If it is said that the mercy of the Supreme Lord is the cause, then since that mercy is not shown everywhere it would appear that the Lord is unfair. Actually, however, His partiality of protecting His devotees by subduing the wicked does not bring blame on Him but rather glorifies Him; this will be explained in the Eighth Rain of Nectar in terms of His virtue of compassion for His devotees being the emperor of all His qualities, capable of overruling all others. There is nothing wrong in the idea that the mercy of His devotees is the cause of devotion, since even though it is also inappropriate in general for devotees of the Lord to be partial, still intermediate-level devotees do show partiality—as is stated in the words, “One who differently shows love, friendship, mercy, and indifference is intermediate” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.2.46)—and also since the Lord, being under the control of His devotees, displays His mercy in pursuance of their mercy. After all, the cause of a devotee’s mercy is devotion herself present in the devotee’s heart, since without her influence mercy can never arise. In this way it is proven that devotion is self-manifest.**

### Verse 6

*ato yaḥ kenāpy ati-bhāgyena jāta-śraddho ’sya sevane ity atra ati-bhāgyena śubha-karma-janya-bhāgyam atikrāntena kenāpi bhakta-kāruṇyeneti tattvārtho jñeyaḥ. na ca bhaktānāṁ kṛpāyāḥ prāthamyāsambhavas teṣām apīśvara-preryatvād iti vācyam, īśvareṇaiva sva-bhakta-vaśyatāṁ svī-kurvatā sva-kṛpā-śakti-sampradānī-kṛta-sva-bhaktena tādṛśasya bhaktotkarṣasya dānāt. antar-yāminaś ca īśitavyānāṁ svādṛṣṭopārjita-bahir-indriya-vyāpāreṣu niyamana-mātra-kāritve ’pi sva-bhakteṣu sva-prasāda eva dṛśyate. yad uktaṁ śrī-gītāsu, mat-prasādāt parāṁ śāntiṁ mat-saṁsthānam adhigacchati iti. prasādaś ca sva-kṛpā-śakti-dānātmakaḥ pūrvam ukta eva. kiṁ ca svecchāvatāra-caritaiḥ iti, svecchā-maysaya ity-ādi-pramāṇa-śatair avagatena svācchandyenāvatarato ’pi tasya bhū-bhāra-haraṇādeḥ sthūla-dṛṣṭyā hetutve iva niṣkāma-karmādeḥ kvāpi dvāratve ’pi na kṣatiḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Therefore in the statement “One who by some exceptional good fortune has developed faith in serving Him” the words “by exceptional good fortune” should be understood as meaning “by some compassion of a devotee of the Lord, above and beyond the good fortune generated by auspicious work.” Nor should it be said that the devotee’s mercy cannot be primary on the grounds that the devotees are themselves impelled by the supreme controller; rather it is the Lord Himself who, by agreeing to become subordinate to His devotees and by making them the recipients of His mercy potency, bestows this exalted position on the devotees. Even though the inner controller, the Supersoul, does no more than regulate of the functions of the external senses of those whom He rules, according to what they have earned as the karmic reactions of their previous activities, nonetheless it is seen that He shows His own special favor to His devotees. This is as stated in** ***Śrī Gītā,*** **“By My grace he attains supreme peace and My abode.” It has already been said that this grace consists of the Lord’s bestowing His mercy potency. And there is nothing amiss if sometimes selfless work and so on are presented as the means of obtaining devotion, similarly to how the Lord’s purposes of incarnating are seen by gross vision to be such goals as removing the burden of the earth, even though He in fact descends simply by His own will, as attested to by hundreds of authoritative statements like “By His activities in His incarnations, which He enacts by His own sweet will” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **4.8.57) and “whose appearances occur only by His will.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.14.2)**

### Verse 7

*kiṁ ca,*  
*yaṁ na yogena sāṅkhyena*  
*dāna-vrata-tapo-’dhvaraiḥ*  
*vyākhyā-svādhyāya-sannyāsaiḥ*  
*prāpnuyād yatnavān api*  
*ity-ādinā dāna-vratādīnāṁ spaṣṭam eva hetu-khaṇḍane ’pi,*  
*dāna-vrata-tapo-homa-*  
*japa-svādhyāya-saṁyamaiḥ*  
*śreyobhir vividhaiś cānyaiḥ*  
*kṛṣṇe bhaktir hi sādhyate*

*iti yad dhetutvaṁ śrūyate tat khalu jñānāṅga-bhūtāyāḥ sāttvikyā eva bhakter na tu nirguṇāyāṁ premāṅga-bhūtāyāḥ. kecit tu dānaṁ viṣṇu-vaiṣṇava-sampradānakaṁ vratāny ekādaśy-ādīni tapas tat-prāpti-hetuko bhogādi-tyāga iti sādhana-bhakty-aṅgāny evāhuḥ. tat-sādhyatve bhakteḥ bhaktyā sañjātayā bhaktyā iti-vat nirhetukatvam eva siddham iti sarvaṁ samañjasam.*

**Перевод:**

**Furthermore, even though the possibility of charity, austere vows, and so on being the cause of devotion is explicitly denied in such statements as “Even though one engages with great endeavor in the mystic** ***yoga*** **system, philosophical speculation, charity, vows, penances, ritualistic sacrifices, teaching of Vedic** ***mantras*** **to others, personal study of the** ***Vedas,*** **or the renounced order of life, still one cannot achieve Me” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.2.9), we still hear that such activites are the cause of devotion: “Devotional service unto Lord Kṛṣṇa is attained by charity, strict vows, austerities, and fire sacrifices, by** ***japa,*** **study of Vedic texts, observance of regulative principles, and, indeed, by the performance of many other auspicious practices.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.47.24) These are causes, however, only of devotion in the material mode of goodness which is a subsidiary aspect of the cultivation of knowledge, not causes of the devotion transcendental to the material modes which is an aspect of pure love of God. Or, as others explain, these are components of the process of devotional service in practice: this charity is given to Viṣṇu and the Vaiṣṇavas, the vows are those of Ekādaśī and so on, and the penances are such renunciation of sense gratification and other things which leads to obtaining pure devotion.** ***Bhakti’s*** **being achieved by these proves that she has no cause, similarly to as stated in the words “by devotion which is created by devotion.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.3.31) Thus everything is reconciled.**

### Verse 8

*śreyaḥ-sṛtiṁ bhaktim udasya te vibho, ko vārtha āpto ’bhajatāṁ sva-dharmataḥ iti, pureha bhūman bahavo ’pi yoginaḥ ity-ādibhyo jñāna-karma-yogādīnāṁ prati-sva-phala-siddhyai bhaktim avaśyam apekṣamāṇānām iva bhakteḥ svīya-phala-prema-siddhyai svapne ’pi na tat-tat-sāpekṣatvam. praty uta, na jñānaṁ na ca vairāgyaṁ prāyaḥ śreyo bhaved iha iti, dharmān santyajya yaḥ sarvān māṁ bhajeta sa tu sattamaḥ ity-ādibhyas tasyāḥ sarvathānanyāpekṣitvaṁ kiṁ vaktavyaṁ teṣām eva jñāna-karma-yogādīnāṁ prati-svakeṣu phaleṣv api kadācid ātmanā sādhyamāneṣu na tat-tad-gandhāpekṣatvam api, yad uktam, yat karmabhir yat tapasā jñāna-vairāgyataś ca yat ity-ādau, sarvaṁ mad-bhakti-yogena mad-bhakto labhate ’ñjasā iti.*

**Перевод:**

**We learn from such statements as “Those, O almighty one, who give up Your devotional service, the path of perfection” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.14.4), “And what is achieved by executing one’s duties if one fails to worship the Lord?” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **1.5.17), and “O infinite one, many** ***yogīs*** **suceeded in the past” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.14.5), that** ***jñāna, karma, yoga,*** **and other processes depend necesarrily on devotional service for achieving their respective fruits.** ***Bhakti,*** **however, does not depend on any of them, even in her dreams, for the achievement of her own fruit, love of God. On the other hand, we have such statements as “The cultivation of knowledge and renunciation is generally not the means of achieving the highest perfection within this world” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.20.31) and “He is the most saintly who gives up all forms of religiosity and just worships Me.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.11.32) These statements indicate that what to speak of devotional service’s being altogether independent of all other processes, even when devotional service engages herself in accomplishing the various objectives proper to** ***jñāna, karma, yoga,*** **and so, she still is not at all dependent on any of the other processes; this is stated in the passage beginning “Everything that can be achieved by fruitive activities, penance, knowledge, detachment, mystic** ***yoga,*** **charity, religious duties, and all other means of perfecting life” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.20.32), in the words “All this is easily achieved by My devotee through loving service unto Me.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.20.33)**

### Verse 9

*taṁ vinā tu teṣām,*  
*bhagavad-bhakti-hīnasya*  
*jātiḥ śāstraṁ japas tapaḥ*  
*aprāṇasyaiva dehasya*  
*maṇḍanaṁ loka-rañjanam*

*ity-āder vaiphalyāyaiva syād iti. tasyāḥ parama-mahatyā adhīnatvaṁ teṣāṁ samprāṇāyaivāstām, api tu karma-yogasya kāla-deśa-pātra-dravyānuṣṭhāna-śuddhy-ādy-apekṣā ca tat-tat-smṛti-prasiddhaiva.*

**Перевод:**

**But without devotion these other processes bear no fruit, as expressed in such statements as “The good birth, scriptural knowledge, chanting, and austerities of one who has no devotion for the Supreme Lord is just like the decoration of a dead body, meant just to please the public.” (*****Hari-bhakti-sudhodaya*** **3.11) The supremely exalted process of devotion may seem dependent on the other processes just to give them some life, but we know very well from the various** ***smṛti*** **scriptures describing it that** ***karma-yoga*** **depends on the sanctity of the time, the place, the candidate, the paraphernalia used, and the specific rituals.**

### Verse 10

*asyās tu na tathā,*  
*na deśa-niyamas tatra*  
*na kāla-niyamas tathā*  
*nocchiṣṭādau niṣedho ’sti*  
*harer nāmani lubdhaka*  
*ity-ādeḥ. kiṁ cāsyāḥ prasiddha-sāpekṣatvam api na,*  
*sakṛd api parigītaṁ śraddhayā helayā vā*  
*bhṛgu-vara nara-mātraṁ tārayet kṛṣṇa-nāma*  
*ity-ādeḥ. karma-yogasya tathā-bhūtatve mahānartha-kāritvam eva.*  
*mantra-hīnaḥ svarato varṇato vā*  
*mithaḥ prayukto na tam artham āha*  
*yathendra-śatruḥ svarato ’parādhāt*  
*sa vāg-vajro yajamānaṁ hi hinasti*  
*ity-ādeḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**It is not like this for devotional service, however, as is stated, “O hunter, in chanting the name of Hari there are no regulations about time or place and no probhibitions concerning such matters as eating other’s remnants.” Even what may be commonly understood to be a dependence of devotion on something else is not actual fact; this we hear from such statements as “If the name of Kṛṣṇa is chanted even once, whether with faith or neglegently, it will deliver any human, O best of the Bhṛgus.” If** ***karma-yoga*** **is performed in the same way, it produces severe undesirable reactions, as we here in such words as “If a** ***mantra*** **is chanted incorrectly, with a wrong intonation or syllable, the combined effect of the words chanted will not express the intended meaning. Indeed, the** ***mantra*** **will become a lightning bolt of speech and destroy the sacrificer, just as the enemy of Indra was born from a transgression in a single intonation.”**

### Verse 11

*evaṁ jñānasyāntaḥ-karaṇa-śuddhy-adhīnatvaṁ prasiddham eva. niṣphala-karma-yogenāntaḥ-karaṇasya śuddhau niṣpāditāyām eva tatra tasya praveśāt karmādhīnatvaṁ ca. tad-adhikṛtasya daivāt durācāratva-lave ’pi, sa vai vāntāsy apatrapaḥ iti nindā. kaṁsa-hiraṇyakaśipu-rāvaṇādīnāṁ tat-tat-prakaraṇa-dṛṣṭyā jñānābhyāsavatām api na tattvena vyapadeśa-lavo ’pi.*

**Перевод:**

**Similarly it is well known that the process of cultivating knowledge depends on purification of the mind. Since only when the mind has been properly purified by the** ***yoga*** **of selfless work can knowledge enter the mind,** ***jñāna*** **is thus dependent on** ***karma.*** **If by a quirk of fate one who has earned the privilege to engage in the** ***jñāna*** **process performs even the slightest improper act, he is condemned in the words “He is a shameless eater of his own vomit.” We can see from consulting the relevant texts that Kaṁsa, Hiraṇyakaśipu, Rāvaṇa, and others, even though they practiced the process of** ***jñāna,*** **did not in fact deserve at all the title of its practitioner.**

### Verse 12

*bhaktes tu vikrīḍitaṁ vraja-vadhūbhir ity-ādau,*  
*bhaktiṁ parāṁ bhagavati parilabhya kāmaṁ*  
*hṛd-rogam āśv apahinoty acireṇa dhīraḥ*

*ity atra ktvā-pratyayena hṛd-rogavaty evādhikāriṇi paramāyā api tasyāḥ prathamam eva praveśas tatas tayaiva parama-svatantrayā kāmādīnām apagamaś ca. teṣāṁ kadācit sattve ’pi, api cet su-durācāro bhajate mām iti, bādhyamāno ’pi mad-bhakta ity-ādibhyaś ca tadvatāṁ na kvāpi śāstreṣu nindā-leśo ’pi. ajāmilasya bhaktatvaṁ viṣṇu-dūtair nirūpitam. saṅketaṁ bhagavan-nāma putra-snehānuṣaṅgajam ity-ādi-dṛṣṭyā tad-ābhāsatām apy ajāmilādīnāṁ bhaktatvaṁ sarvaiḥ saṅgītam eva.*

**Перевод:**

**We hear about devotional service, however, in the verse beginning “The sports with the young girls of Vraja,” that “Attaining the Lord’s pure devotional service, he will quickly become sober and conquer lust, the disease of the heart.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.33.39) In this statement, by the gerund suffix \[on the word “attaining”\], it is indicated that devotion service, being supreme, first of all enters a candidate suffering from disease of the heart and then, being supremely independent, effects the removal of lust and other evils. Even if these evils remain for sometime, we hear no trace of condemnation of those who have them; this we are told in such statements as “Even if one is most wicked in his behavior, if he worships Me,” (*****Bhagavad-gīta*** **9.30), “My devotee, even if he encounters obstacles.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.14.18) The Viṣṇu-dūtas described how Ajāmila was actually a devotee. It is universally acknowledged that Ajāmila and others like him were devotees of the Lord, even though Ajāmila chanted only a shadow of of the Lord’s name, as we see from his chanting the name with reference to something else, as a concidental effect of his affection for his son.**

### Verse 13

*tad evaṁ karma-yogādīnām antaḥ-karaṇa-śuddhi-dravya-deśa-śuddhy-ādayaḥ sādhakās tad-vaiguṇyādayo bādhakā bhaktis tu prāṇa-dāyiny eveti. sarvathā pāratantryam eva teṣām. na hi svatantrāḥ kenāpi sādhyante bādhyante veti. kiṁ ca jñānaika-sādhana-mātratvaṁ bhakter ity ajñair evocyate yato jñāna-sādhyān mokṣād api tasyāḥ paramotkarṣaḥ evālocyate, muktiṁ dadāti karhicit sma na bhakti-yogam iti,*

*muktānām api siddhānāṁ*  
*nārāyaṇa-parāyaṇaḥ*  
*su-durlabhaḥ praśāntātmā*  
*koṭiṣv api mahāmune*  
*ity-ādibhyaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**It should thus be concluded that purification of the mind and purification of the items for offering and the place bring about the sucess of** ***karma, yoga,*** **and the other processes, while discrepancies in these obstruct them, but devotional service is the very giver of life to these processes. The other processes are altogether dependent. Whatever is independent is not accomplished or obstructed by anything else. Moreover, only fools say that** ***bhakti*** **is just a means of achieving** ***jñāna;*** **after all, we see that** ***bhakti*** **is by far superior even to the liberation achieved by** ***jñāna,*** **in such statements as “He grants liberation sometimes, but not connection with Him in devotion” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **5.6.18) and “O great sage, among many millions who are liberated and perfect in knowledge of liberation, one may be a devotee of Lord Nārāyaṇa, or Kṛṣṇa. Such devotees, who are fully peaceful, are extremely rare.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **6.14.5)**

### Verse 14

*indram eva pradhānī-kṛtya svayaṁ guṇī-bhavatopendreṇa taṁ sarvathā puṣṇatā sva-kṛpālutvam eva yathābhijña-janeṣu pratyāyate na tu svāpakarṣas tathaiva jñānaṁ puṣṇantyās tat-tat-prakaraṇa-vākyeṣu tasyā bhakter anugraha eva su-dhībhir anugamyata iti. bhaktyā sañjātayā bhaktyā iti bhakteḥ phalaṁ prema-rūpā saiveti svayaṁ puruṣārtha-mauli-rūpatvaṁ tasyāḥ. tad evaṁ bhagavata iva svarūpa-bhūtāyā mahā-śakteḥ sarva-vyāpakatvaṁ sarva-vaśī-kāritvaṁ sarva-sañjīvakatvaṁ sarvotkarṣa-parama-svātantryaṁ sva-prakāśatvaṁ ca kiñcid uṭṭaṅkitaṁ tad api tāṁ vinā anyatra pravṛttau prekṣā-vattvasyābhāva iti kiṁ vaktavyam. naratvasyāpi ko vai na seveta vinā naretaram ity-ādibhir avagamo dṛṣṭaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**In the same way as knowledgable persons can discern Lord Upendra’s kindness rather than His inferiority when He gives all kinds of support to Indra, making Indra prominent and Himself less important, so also those who are intelligent can understand that when in various contexts of scripture devotional service gives support to the process of knowledge, that is simply devotion’s kindness. According to the words “by devotion which is created by devotion” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.3.31), the fruit of** ***bhakti*** **is love of God, and love of God is none other than the ultimate goal of life. So it is that just like the Personality of Godhead Himself, His supreme energy, which belongs to His essential self, is all pervasive, brings everything else under her control, gives life to everything else, and is superexcellent, absolutely independent, and self-manifesting. If a person tries to find in any other process these and whatever other special qualities are ascribed to** ***bhakti,*** **it goes without saying that he has no power of discrimination. In fact, he is not even a human being, which we learn such statements as “Who will not serve Him, other than one who is not human?”**

*iti mahā-mahopadhyāya-śrīmad-viśvanātha-cakravarti-viracitāyāṁ mādhurya-kādambinyāṁ bhakteḥ sarvotkarṣa-nāma prathamāmṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the First Rain of Nectar, named “The superexcellence of devotional service,” of** ***Mādhurya-kādambinī,*** **composed by Mahā-mahopadhyāya Śrīmān Viśvanātha Cakravartī.**

## Second Rain of Nectar - dvitīyāmṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ

### Verse 1

*athātra mādhurya-kādambinyāṁ dvaitādvaita-vāda-vivādayor nāvakāśo labhanta iti kaiścid apekṣaṇīyāś ced aiśvarya-kādambinyāṁ dṛśyatāṁ nāma. (1)*

**Перевод:**

**In this Mādhurya-kādambinī there is no opportunity to discuss the disagreements between dualism and monism; if some readers want information on these, he should consult the book Aiśvarya-kādambinī.**

### Verse 2

*idānīṁ karaṇa-kedārikāsu prādurbhavantyās tasyā eva bhakter jñāna-karmādy-amiśritatvena śuddhāyāḥ kalpa-vallyā api nirastānya-phalābhisandhitayaiva dhṛta-vratair madhu-vratair iva bhavya-janair āśriyamāṇāyāḥ sva-viṣayaikānukūlya-mūla-prāṇāyāḥ sva-sparśena sparśa-maṇir iva karaṇa-vṛttīr api prākṛtatva-lohatāṁ śanais tyājayitvā cinmayatva-śuddha-jāmbunadatāṁ prāpayantyāḥ kandalī-bhāvānte samudgacchantyāḥ sādhanābhikhye dve patrike vivriyete. tayoḥ prathamā kleśa-ghnī dvitīyā śubha-deti. dvayor api tayor antas tu lobha-pravartakatva-lakṣaṇa-caikkaṇyena yeṣām ahaṁ priya ātmā sutaś ca ity-ādi-śuddha-sambandha-snigdhatayā ca prāptotkarṣe deśe rāga-nāmno rājña evādhikāraḥ. bahis tu tasmād bhārata sarvātmā ity-ādi-śāstra-pravartakatva-lakṣaṇa-pāruṣyābhāsena priyādi-śuddha-sambandhābhāvāt svata evāti-snigdhatānudayena pūrvataḥ kiñcid apakṛṣṭe deśe vaidha-nāmno ’parasya rājñaḥ. kleśa-ghantva-śubha-datvābhyāṁ tu prāyayor na ko ’pi viśeṣaḥ. (2)*

**Перевод:**

**Devotional service is a desire creeper appearing in the paddy fields of the senses, pure because it is not mixed with** ***jñāna, karma,*** **and so on. Because those who are fortunate avoid interest in other fruits, they are firm in their intentions like bees determined to obtain honey, and take full shelter of this creeper of devotion. The basic drive of this creeper’s life is to do whatever helps focus itself exclusively on its object. Just like the mystic touchstone, when this creeper touches the functions of the senses it frees them gradually from their material conditioning, like that of dull iron, turning them into the pure gold of living spirit. After the creeper has passes through the stage of initial blossoming, two leaves appear, known as the methods of practice. Now we will describe these two methods. The first “destroys distress” and the second “bestows good fortune.” Of these two methods, the second is ruled by the king named Attraction in the land of achieved excellence, because it has the effortless spontenaity of being impelled by intense hankering and because it involves all the intimate affection of pure relationships, as described in the words “for whom I am their dear friend, their self, and their son.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **3.25.38) The other, external, method is ruled by another king named Injunction in a land somewhat inferior to one described first, because it appears superficially to be harsh on account of being impelled by scriptural regulation, as indicated in such statements as “Therefore, O Bhārata, the Supreme Soul...” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **2.1.5), and because in it the same feelings of great intimacy have not spontaneously arised, the pure relationships of dear friend and so on not being present. Practically speaking, however, there is no real difference between destroying distress and bestowing good fortune.**

### Verse 3

*tatrāvidyāsmitā-rāga-dveṣābhiniveśāḥ pañca kleśāḥ. prārabdhāprārabdha-rūḍha-bīja-pāpādayas tan-mayā eva. śubhāni durviṣaya-vaitṛṣṇya-bhagavad-viṣaya-satṛṣṇyānukūlya-kṛpā-kṣamā-satya-sāralya-sāmya-dhairya-gāmbhīrya-mānadatvāmānitva-sarva-subhagatvādayo guṇāś ca, sarvair guṇais tatra samāsate surāḥ ity-ādi-dṛṣṭyā jñeyāḥ. (3)*

**Перевод:**

**In this regard, the five distresses are delusion, selfishness, material attraction, enmity, and addiction. Comprised of these are manifest karmic reactions, not yet manifest ripened reactions, ripening reactions, reactions lying in seed, and sinful activities. Forms of auspciousness include such qualities as distaste for inappropriate sense objects, a taste for sensory engagement with the Personality of Godhead, a favorable attitude toward service, mercy, forgiveness, truth, simplicity, fairness, thoughtfulness, sobriety, respect for others, freedom from expectation of respect, and gracefulness in all dealings; this we can understand from what is said in statements like “The demigods reside in such a person along with all their good qualities.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **5.18.12)**

### Verse 4

*bhaktiḥ pareśānubhavo viraktir anyatra caiṣa trika eka-kālaḥ ity ukta-prakāreṇa yugapad api pravṛttayor api tayoḥ patrikayor udgama-tāratamyenaiva tat-tad-aśubha-nivṛtti-śubha-pravṛtti-tāratamyād asty eva kramaḥ. sa cāti-sūkṣmo durlakṣyo ’pi tat-tat-kārya-darśana-liṅgena su-dhībhir avasīyate. (4)*

**Перевод:**

**In the manner indicated by the words “Devotion, realization of the Supreme Lord, and detachment from other things all three develop simultaneously” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.2.42), these two leaves develop simultaneously. But there is a certain sequence of appearance due to the priority of one manifesting before the other, which results in a graded priority of the varieties of cessation from what is bad and engagement in what is good. And even though this sequence is extremely subtle and difficult to perceive, those who are sufficiently intelligent can ascertain it from the logical signs of its various visible effects.**

### Verse 5

*tatra bhakty-adhikāriṇaḥ prathamaṁ śraddhā. sā ca tat-tac-chāstrārthe dṛḍha-pratyaya-mayī, prakramyamāṇa-yatnaika-nidāna-rūpa-tad-viṣayakatvaika-nirvāha-rūpa-sādara-spṛhā ca. sā ca sā ca svābhāvikī kenāpi balād utpāditā ca. tataś cāśrita-guru-caraṇasya tasya jijñāsyamāna-sad-ācārasya tac-chikṣayaiva sajātīyāśaya-snigdha-bhakty-abhijña-sādhu-saṅga-bhāgyodayaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Specifically, a candidate for devotional service first develops faith, which consists of firm belief in the ideas presented in various authoritative texts and a sincere desire to simply execute the objectives taught by the texts, which are achieved only by making an effort to follow the methods the texts present. Both these factors can either arise naturally or be induced in one by someone else. And then, when one takes shelter at the feet of a guru and is eager to enquire from him about the right way to act, just by following his instructions one experiences the good fortune of coming into the association of saintly persons whose mentalities are compatible with one’s own, who are sympathetic, and who know well the science of devotion.**

### Verse 6

*tato bhajana-kriyā. sā ca dvi-vidhā aniṣṭhitā niṣṭhitā ca. tatra prathamam aniṣṭhitā kramenotsāha-mayī ghana-taralā vyūḍha-vikalpā viṣaya-saṅgarā niyamākṣamā taraṅga-raṅgiṇīti ṣaḍ-vidhā bhavantīti svādhāraṁ vilakṣayati.*

**Перевод:**

**Then follows the practice of worship, which is of two kinds, without conviction and with conviction. The first of these, practice without conviction, distinguishes those who take it up by six varieties one after another—“full of enthusiasm,” “thick and thin,” “with splayed intentions,” “at war with enjoyment,” “unable to control oneself,” and “enjoying in the waves.”**

### Verse 7

*tatra utsāha-mayī. prathamam eva śāstram adhyetum ārabhamāṇasya sarva-loka-ślokyamāna-pāṇḍityam upapannam iva svasmin manyamānasya baṭor iva utsāha-svādhikaraṇasya pracurayatīty utsāha-mayī.*

**Перевод:**

**Among these, “full of enthusiasm”: This comes first, and is called such because it fills one with enthusiasm like a young student just beginning to study a subject, who thinks himself already endowed with wisdom and deserving praise from everyone.**

### Verse 8

*atha ghana-taralā. prakramyamāṇāni bhakty-aṅgāni kadācin nirvahanti kadācic ca na veti ghanatvaṁ taralatvaṁ cāsyāḥ yathā baṭoḥ śāstrābhyāsaḥ kadācit sāndraḥ kadācit tad-artha-praveśāsamarthatayā sārasyānudayena śithilaś ca.*

**Перевод:**

**Next, “thick and thin”: The activities of devotional practice one attempts sometimes succeed and sometimes not, thus making one’s devotion sometimes thick and sometimes thin, just as a student’s study of his subject is sometimes intense and sometimes slackens, when he cannot penetrate its meaning and grasp the essence.**

### Verse 9

*atha vyūḍha-vikalpā. kim ahaṁ sa-parigraha eva putra-kalatrādīn vaiṣṇavī-kṛtya bhagavat-paricaryāyāṁ niyojya gṛha eva sukhaṁ taṁ bhaje, kiṁ vā sarvān eva parityajya nivikṣepaḥ śrī-vṛndāvanaṁ dhyeya-sthānam evāsīnaḥ kīrtana-śravaṇādibhiḥ kṛtārthī-bhaveyam. sa ca tyāgaḥ kiṁ bhukta-bhogasyāvagata-viṣama-viṣaya-dāva-davathor mama carama-daśāyām eva kiṁ vādhunaiva samucita iti. kiṁ ca, tām īkṣed ātmano mṛtyuṁ tṛṇaiḥ kūpam ivāvṛtam iti dṛṣṭyā āśramasyāsyāviśvāsasyatayā yo dustyajān dāra-sutān ity atra jahau yuvaiva mala-vat ity ādi-dṛṣṭyā tyakta-vilambas tatrāpi aho me pitarau vṛddhau ity atra atṛptas tān anudhyāyan mṛto ’ndhaṁ viśate tamaḥ iti bhagavad-vākyena tyāge ’labdha-balaś ca sampraty eva prāṇa-dhāraṇa-mātra-vṛttir vanaṁ tadaiva praviśyāṣṭāv eva ca yāmān abhyarthayānīti. na jñānaṁ na ca vairāgyaṁ prāyaḥ śreyo bhaved iha ity atra tu vairāgyasya bhakti-janakatve eva doṣo na tu bhakti-janitatve iti tad-anubhāva-rūpatayā tad-adhīnatvam iti. yad yad āśramam agāt sa bhikṣukas tat-tad-anna-paripūrṇam aikṣata iti nyāyena kadācid vairāgyaṁ tāvad rāgādayaḥ stenāḥ tāvat kārāgṛhaṁ gṛham iti kadācid gārhasthyaṁ ca niścinvan kim ahaṁ kīrtanam eva kiṁ vā kathā-śravaṇam api uta sevām eva utāho tāvad ambarīṣādi-vad anekāṅgām eva bhaktiṁ karavai ity-ādi vividhā eva prāptā vikalpā yatra bhavantīti vyūḍha-viklapā.*

**Перевод:**

**Next, “with splayed intentions”: “Should I remain with my family, make my children, wife, and others into Vaiṣṇavas, and engage them in the service of the Supreme Lord, worshiping the Lord happily at home? Or should I renounce them all, and free from disturbance sit down in Śrī Vṛndāvana, the place which is the object of my meditation, and perfect my life by chanting, hearing, and so on? And should this renunciation be done at the last stage of my life, when I have had enough of material enjoyment and have properly understood that sense gratification is a terrible forest fire? Or would it be more appropriate to renounce right now? Furthermore, isn’t this family stage of life undeserving of my trust, according to the view of statements like ‘One should view his wife as the cause of his death, like a well covered with grass’? (*****Bhāgavatam*** **3.31.40) Shouldn’t I stop hesistating, following the ideas presented in the verse beginning ‘Although one’s wife and children are very difficult to give up,’ in the words ‘Even though still young, he left them aside like feces.’ (*****Bhāgavatam*** **5.14.43) And even more to the point, the Supreme Lord Himself says, in the passage including the words ‘Oh my elderly parents,’ that ‘If he dies unsatisfied, thinking about them, he will enter the darkness of ignorance’ (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.17.57–58); thus I may at present lack the strength to renounce, but I should for now work only to barely maintain my existence and later enter the forest to spend twenty-four hours a day there. But on the other hand, the statement ‘Neither knowledge nor renunciation practically ever lead to perfection in this life’ (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.20.31) indicates that it is wrong to try to make renunciation a cause of devotion but not wrong to let it be caused by devotion. So, because renunciation is an effect of devotion, it is subordinate to devotion.” In this way sometimes he decides for renunciation, following the logic of the words “Whatever hermitage the mendicant visited he saw it filled with all kinds of food,” and sometimes he decides to remain a householder, following the idea of the words “Only for that long are attachment and so on thieves and one’s home is a prison” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.14.36). And all along he has to confront various alternatives: “Should I do chanting, or listening to topics of the Lord, or else perform personal service? Or should I do many kinds of service as Ambarīṣa and others did?” Such devotion in which many alternatives are confronted is called “with splayed intentions.”**

### Verse 10

*atha viṣaya-saṅgarā.*  
*viṣayāviṣṭa-cittānāṁ*  
*viṣṇv-āveśaḥ su-dūrataḥ*  
*vāruṇī-dig-gataṁ vastu*  
*vrajann aindrīṁ kim āpnuyāt*

*iti bhogā eva balāt svasminn abhiniveśya māṁ bhajane śithilayantīti tad amī tyaktvā nāma-grāhaṁ kāṁścana kāṁścana tyaktavato ’pi bhuñjānasya, juṣamāṇaś ca tān kāmān parityāge ’py anīśvaraḥ iti bhagavad-vākyasyodāharaṇatvaṁ prāptavatas tasya pūrvābhyastair viṣayais taiḥ saha saṅgaro yuddhaṁ kadācit tat-parājayaḥ kadācit sva-parājaya iti viṣaya-saṅgarā.*

**Перевод:**

**Next, “at war with enjoyment”: It is said, “For those whose minds are absorbed in material enjoyment it is practically impossible to become absorbed in thought of Lord Viṣṇu. How can one obtain an object located in the west by going to the east?” In this way thinking “Sense enjoyments are forcing themselves upon me and weakening me in my worship,” one therefore tries to give them up and resort to the Lord’s name. But even though he repeatedly rejects these enjoyments, sometimes he indulges in them, becoming an example of what is described in the words “Indulging in these sense gratifications, being unable to completely give them up.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.20.28) Thus he is at war with the gratifications he was previously accustomed to. Sometimes they lose the battle and sometimes he does. This is devotional practice “at war with enjoyment.”**

### Verse 11

*atha niyamākṣamā. adyārabhya imāni nāmāni gṛhītavyāni etāvatyaś ca praṇatayaḥ kāryā ittham eva tad-bhaktā api sevanīyā bhagavad-asambandha-vāco ’pi noccāraṇīyā grāmya-vārtāvatāṁ sannidhis tyaktavatyaḥ ity-ādi prati-dinam api pratijānato ’pi samaye tathā na kṣamatvam iti niyamākṣamā. viṣaya-saṅgarāyāṁ viṣaya-tyāgākṣamatvam atra tu bhakty-utkarṣākṣamatvam iti bhedaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Next, “unable to control oneself”: “Beginning today, I should chant these names, offer these many obeisances, and serve the Lord’s devotees in these certain ways. I should not utter any words not connected to the Lord, and should avoid the company of persons who engage in mundane talks.” But even though one makes resolutions like this day after day, he soon proves incapable of carrying them out. In practice which is “at war with enjoyment” one is incapable of giving up sense gratification, but in this kind of practice one is unable to raise his devotional service to a high standard; that is the difference.**

### Verse 12

*atha taraṅga-raṅgiṇī. bhakteḥ svabhāva evāyaṁ yat tadvati sarve ’pi janā anurajyantīti janānurāga-prabhavā hi sampada iti prācāṁ vāco ’pi. bhakty-utthāsu vibhūtiṣu lābha-pūjā-pratiṣṭhādiṣu vallī-valitāsūpaśākhāsu taraṅgeṣv ivācarantyā asyā raṅga iti taraṅga-raṅgiṇī.*

**Перевод:**

**Next, “enjoying in the waves”: It is the nature of devotional service that everyone becomes attracted to one who possesses it, and as the ancients have said, when one becomes popular he obtains various assets. The sporting of devotional practice when it moves about in the waves which are the opulences generated by** ***bhakti—*****material gain, worship, position and so, growing like a tree’s minor branches embraced by creepers—is called “enjoying in the waves.”**

*iti mahā-mahopādhyāya-śrīmad-viśvanātha-cakravarti-viracitāyāṁ mādhurya-kādambinyāṁ bhakteḥ śraddhādi-krama-traya-kathana-pūrvakaṁ bhajana-kriyā-bheda-kathanaṁ nāma dvitīyāmṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the Second Rain of Nectar, named “Description of the triple sequence of conviction and so on, followed by description of the difference ways of practicing worship,” of** ***Mādhurya-kādambinī,*** **composed by Mahā-mahopadhyāya Śrīmān Viśvanātha Cakravartī.**

## Third Rain of Nectar - tṛtīyāmṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ

### Verse 1

*atha anarthānāṁ nivṛttiḥ. te cānarthāś catur-vidhāḥ, duṣkṛtotthāḥ sukṛtotthā aparādhotthā bhakty-utthāś ceti.*

**Перевод:**

**Now cessation of undesirable things. These undesirable things are of four kinds: rising from bad deeds, from good deeds, from offenses, and from devotional service.**

### Verse 2

*tatra duṣkṛtotthāḥ durabhiniveśa-dveṣa-rāgādyāḥ pūrvoktāḥ kleśā eva. sukṛtotthā bhogābhiniveśā vividhā eva. te ca kleśāntaḥ-pātina iti kecit. aparādhotthā ity atra nāmāparādhā eva gṛhyante, sevāparādhānāṁ tu nāmabhis tat-tan-nivartaka-stotra-pāṭhaiḥ sevā-sātatyena ca bhavyasya vivekinaḥ prāyaḥ prati-dinam evopaśamenāṅkurī- bhāvānupalabdheḥ. kintu tat-tad-upaśama-sambhava-balena tatra sāvadhānatā-śaithilye sevāparādhā api nāmāparādhā eva syuḥ. tathā hy uktam, nāmno balād yasya hi pāpa buddhiḥ iti. tatra nāma ity upalakṣaṇaṁ bhakti-mātrasyaivopaśamakasya. dharma-śāstre ’pi prāyaścitta-balena pāpa-caraṇe na tasya pāpasya kṣayaḥ praty uta gāḍhataiva.*

**Перевод:**

**Among these, the ones rising from bad deeds are the same distresses described earlier—harmful obsessions, emnity, material attraction, and so on. Those rising from good deeds are obsessions with enjoyment, and are of various kinds. Some say that they also fall in the category of distresses. In the present context “rising from offenses” refers only to offenses against the Supreme Lord’s names, since we see that one who is fortunate and discriminating can usually counteract offenses in service to the Lord by engaging every day in chanting the Lord’s names, reciting prayers for undoing various offenses, and remaining constant in service, with the result that these offenses do not sprout into growing plants. But if on the strength of being able to resort to these counteractions one’s attention becomes lax toward offenses in serving the Lord, they can change into offenses against the Lord’s names. This is described in the words “one who develops a sinful attitude on the strength of the Lord’s name,” where “name” is only a partial indication implying all kinds of devotional service, any one of which has the power to counteract offenses. It is also said in the** ***dharma-śāstra*** **that if one engages in sinful activity on the strength of rituals of atonement his sin is not erradicated but rather becomes more firmly rooted.**

### Verse 3

*nanv evam na hy aṅgopakrame dhvaṁso mad-dharmasyoddhavāṇv api iti, viśeṣato daśārṇo ’yaṁ japa-mātreṇa siddhi-da ity-ādi-vākya-balena tat-tad-aṅgānām ananuṣṭhāne vaikalyādāv api vā jāte nāmāparādhaḥ prasajjate. maivam, nāmno balād yasyety atra pāpe buddhiś cikīrṣādi. tad eva hi pāpaṁ yatra sati nindā-prāyaścittādi-śravaṇam.*

**Перевод:**

**“But if this is so, then when someone neglects to execute every one of the elements of practice by taking advantage of such statements as ‘Dear Uddhava, if one tries to perform duties related to Me, there is not the slightest chance of his failing’ (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.29.20) and ‘This ten-syllable mantra has the special potency of giving perfection just by being chanted,’ or when something is done incorrectly or there is some other discrepancy, offense against the holy name will result.” No, in the statement “on the strength of the Lord’s name” a sinful attitude is indicated, meaning an intention to sin. That alone is sin about which when it occurs we hear condemnation, prescriptions of atonement, and so on.**

### Verse 4

*na ca karma-mārga iva bhakti-mārge ’pi aṅga-vaikalyādau kvāpi nindā-śravaṇam iti na tatrāparādha-śaṅkā. yad uktam, ye vai bhagavatā proktā upāyā hy ātma-labdhaye añjaḥ puṁsām aviduṣāṁ viddhi bhāgavatān hi tān yān āsthāya naro rājan na pramādyeta karhicit dhāvan nimīlya vā netre na skhalen na pated iha iti.*

**Перевод:**

**We furthermore never hear condemnation for failure to perform all details and other discrepancies on the path of devotional service, as we hear for the same on the path of karma; thus we need not worry that these discrepancies are offenses. After all, it has been said, “Even ignorant living entities can very easily come to know the Supreme Lord if they adopt those means prescribed by the Supreme Lord Himself. The process recommended by the Lord is to be known as bhāgavata-dharma, or devotional service to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. O King, one who accepts this process of devotional service to the Supreme Personality of Godhead will never blunder on his path in this world. Even while running with eyes closed, he will never trip or fall.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.2.34–35)**

### Verse 5

*atra nimīlyeti kartṛ-vyāpāra-liṅgena vidyamāne eva netre mudrayitvā tatrāpi dhāvan pāda-nyāsa-sthalam atikramyāpi vrajan na skhaled ity akṣarārtha-labdher bhagavad-dharmam āśritya tad-aṅgāni sarvāṇi jñātvāpy ajña iva kānicid ullaṅghyāpi anutiṣṭhan na pratyavāyī syāt, nāpi phalād bhraśyed ity eva vyākhyā upapadyate. nimīlanaṁ nāmājñānaṁ tasyāpi śruti-smṛti-viṣayāv ity eṣā tu na saṅgacchate mukhyārtha-bādhāyogāt.*

**Перевод:**

**According to the literal indication of this statement, derived from the word “closing” with its indication of an intentional action by a conscious agent—that even though one has eyes he closes them—and furthermore from the word “running”—that even while jumping over the proper places for putting his feet he does not trip in moving along, we obtain the suitable interpretation that when one takes shelter of the religious principles of service to the Supreme Lord, if while in full knowledge of its subsidiary practices one executes service while neglecting some of them like a person ignorant of them, he still does not suffer any loss, nor does he fail to achieve the goal. The idea that “closing one’s eyes” means “ignorance,” specifically ignorance in the realm of śruti and smṛti scriptures, is not a consistent interpretation because there is no good reason to put aside the literal meaning.**

### Verse 6

*na ca dhāvan nimīlyety etad eva dvātriṁśad-aparādhābhāvam api kroḍī-karotv iti vācyam, yān bhagavatā proktān upāyān āśrityety uktatvāt. yānair vā pādukair vāpi gamanaṁ bhagavad-gṛhe ity-ādayas tu tatra niṣiddhā eva. sevāparādhe tu harer apy aparādhān yaḥ kuryād dvipada-pāṁśanaḥ ity-ādiṣu śrūyanta eva nindāḥ. kiṁ ca te nāmāparādhāḥ prācīnā arvācīnā vā yadi samyag-anabhijñāta-prakārāḥ syuḥ kintu tat-phala-liṅgenānumīyamānā eva tadā teṣāṁ nāmabhir evāśrānta-prayuktair bhakti-niṣṭhām utpadyamānāyāṁ krameṇopaśamaḥ. yadi te jñāyanta eva tadā tv asti kvacit kaścid viśeṣaḥ. (1)*

**Перевод:**

**Nor should it be said that the words “running with one’s eyes closed” also give one a license to indulge in the thirty-two offenses, since what was said is “having taken shelter of those means described by the Supreme Lord.” In the immediate context, such offenses as “going into the Lord’s house on a conveyance or with shoes” are merely prohibited, but under the topic of offenses in service we hear condemnations in such statements as “One who commits offenses against Lord Hari is the worst of two-legged creatures.” (*****Padma Purāṇa*****) And moreover if one is not fully aware of what particular offenses, old or new, one has commited against the Lord’s names, but can only infer they have been commited from the signs of their results, then one can gradually counteract them, as strong faith in devotional service develops, by constantly uttering the same names. And if on the other hand one knows which offenses have been commited, there may sometimes be special prescriptions.**

### Verse 7

*yathā satāṁ nindā iti daśasu nāmnaḥ prathamo ’parādhaḥ. tatra nindety anena dveṣa-drohādayo ’py upalakṣyante. tataś ca daivāt tasminn aparādhe jāte, hanta pāmareṇa mayā sādhuṣu aparāddham iti anutapto janaḥ kṛśānau śāmyati taptaḥ kṛśānunā evāyam iti nyāyena tat-padāgra eva nipatya prasādayāmīti viṣaṇṇa-cetasā praṇati-stuti-sammānādibhis tasyopaśamaḥ kāryaḥ. kadācit kasyacana kair api duṣprasādanīyatve bahu-dinam api tan-mano-’bhirociny anuvṛttiḥ kāryā. aparādhasyāti-mahattvāt kathañcit tayāpy anivartya-kopatve dhiṅ mām akṣīṇa-bhaktāparādhaṁ niraya-koṭiṣu patantam iti nirvidya sarvaṁ parityajya samāśrayaṇīyā nāma-saṅkīrtana-santatis tayā ca mahā-śaktimatyāvaśyam eva kāle tataḥ syād evoddhāraḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**For example, “denigration of saintly persons” is the first of the ten offenses against the Lord’s name. There the word “denigration” implies also hatred, quarrel, and so on. Thus if by chance this offense happens to occur, the offender should feel remorse, thinking “Oh, I am so degraded that I have offended saintly persons.” Following the principle that “If someone has been burned by fire, the burn can be remedied by using fire again,” he should counteract the offense by falling at the saintly person’ feet and with a sad heart ask “I beg for you forgiveness,” offering obeisances, prayers, glorification, and so on. If in some case someone cannot by any way he tries satisfy the person he has offended, he should follow and serve that person so as to please him, even if it takes many days. And if the offense is so serious that the offended person’s anger cannot be mitigated even in this way, the offender should lament “Damn me, whose offense against devotees remains undiminished, and who am going to fall into millions of hells.” He should renounce everything and resort to constant chanting of the Lord’s names, by which most potent means he will for sure be eventually delivered.**

### Verse 8

*kiṁ me muhur muhur eva pāda-patanādibhiḥ svāpakarṣa-svīkāreṇa nāmāparādha-yuktānāṁ nāmāny eva haranty agham ity asyaiva paramopāyaḥ sa eva samāśrayaṇīyaḥ iti bhāvanāyāṁ pūrva-vad eva punar api nāmāparādhaḥ. na ca kṛpālur akṛta-drohas titikṣuḥ sarva-dehinām ity-ādi sampūrṇa-dharmakā eva santas teṣām eva nindā aparādha iti vācyam, sarvācāra-vivarjitāḥ śaṭha-dhiyo vrātyā jagad-vañcakāḥ iti tat-prakaraṇa-vartinā vacanena tādṛśa-duścaritānām api bhagavantaṁ bhajatāṁ kaimutika-nyāyena sac-chabda-vācyatvena sūcitatvāt. kiṁ ca kaścin mahā-bhāgavatatvāt mahāparādhiny api yady api na kupyati tad api tatrāparādhavatā sva-śuddhy-arthaṁ praṇaty-ādibhir anuvartanīya eva saḥ, serṣaṁ mahā-puruṣa-pāda-pāṁśubhir nirasta-tejaḥsu tad eva śobhanam iti satāṁ vākyena tac-caraṇa-reṇūnām asahiṣṇutayā tat-phala-pradatvāvagamāt.*

**Перевод:**

**One might think “What is the use of my falling at his feet over and over again to acknowledge my inferiority? Since it is said ‘The sins of those who are implicated in offenses against the Lord’s names can only be removed by the names themselves,’ one should seek shelter in this alone, the ultimate means of atoning.” This train of thought, however, will only result in a perpetuation of even more offenses against the Lord’s names. Nor should it be said that the offense only involves criticism of saintly persons who are perfectly religious, as described in such words as “One who is merciful, never inimical, and forgiving to all living beings,” because according to another statement in the same text—“Those who are devoid of all good behavior, whose minds are duplicitous, and who try to cheat the whole world”—by the logic of “what to speak of” it is indicated that even such evildoers should be called saintly if they worship the Supreme Lord.**

**And furthermore, if someone is such a saintly devotee of the Lord that he does not become angry even at the greatest offender, nonetheless the one who offends him is obliged to honor him with obeisances and so on for his own purification. As we learn from the statement “Those who are envious have their potency destroyed by the dust from the feet of saintly persons, and this is quite fitting,” the dust of a saint’s feet itself cannot tolerate and so, we understand, that dust bestows the reaction.**

### Verse 9

*kiṁ ca, duravagama-niṣkāraṇake kvacit kṛpā-dṛṣṭau prabhaviṣṇau svacchanda-carite kvacin mahā-bhāgavata-maulau tu na kāpi maryādā paryāpnoti, yathā śibikāṁ vāhayati kaṭūkti-viṣa-varṣiṇy api rahūgaṇe śrī-jaḍa-bharatasya kṛpā, yathā ca pāṣaṇḍa-dharmāvalambini sva-hiṁsārtham upaseduṣi daitya-samūhe uparicarasya vasoś cedi-rājasya. yathā vā mahā-pāpini sva-lalāṭe rudhira-pātiny api mādhave prabhu-varasya nityānandasyeti. evam eva guror avajñā ity atrāpi jñeyam. śivasya śrī-viṣṇor ity atraivaṁ vivecanīyam. (2)*

**Перевод:**

**Sometimes, moreover, a most excellent pure devotee of the Lord, acting freely according to his own will, exerts the power of his merciful glance, inconceivable and causeless, in which case no force is capable of regulating what occurs. For example, when King Raghūgaṇa made Śrī Jaḍa Bharata carry his palanquin and showered him with the poison of harsh words, Jaḍa Bharata was nevertheless merciful to him. The king of Cedi Uparicara Vasu showed his mercy to a crowd of demons even though they were proponents of atheistic religious principles and approach him to do violence. And the best of Lords, Nityānanda, showed His mercy even to Mādhava, the greatest of sinners, who made blood flow from His forehead. From these same examples can be similarly understood the offense of “neglecting one’s spiritual master.” The offense of “\[equating\] the all-auspicious \[name\] of Śrī Viṣṇu \[with those of the demigods\]” can be analyzed as follows.**  
**Uparicara** ***itihāsa*** **described in** ***Bhakti-sandarbha*** **179, from** ***Viṣṇu-dharma.*** **Mercy on Rahūgaṇa also described there.**

### Verse 10

*caitanyaṁ hi dvi-vidhaṁ bhavati, svatantram asvatantraṁ ca. tatra prathamaṁ sarva-vyāpakam īśvarākhyaṁ dvitīyaṁ deha-mātra-vyāpi-śaktikaṁ jīvākhyam īśitavyam. īśvara-caitanyaṁ dvi-vidhaṁ māyā-sparśa-rahitaṁ līlayā svīkṛta-māyā-sparśaṁ ca. tatra prathamaṁ nārāyaṇādy-abhidham. yad uktaṁ, harir hi nirguṇaḥ sākṣat puruṣaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ dvitīyaṁ śivādy-abhidham, yad uktam, śivaḥ śakti-yutaḥ śaśvat tri-liṅgo guṇa-saṁvṛtaḥ iti. atra guṇa-saṁvṛta-liṅgenāpi tasya jīvatvaṁ nāśaṅkanīyam,*

*kṣīraṁ yathā dadhi vikāra viśeṣa yogāt*  
*sañjāyate na hi tataḥ pṛthag asti hetoḥ*  
*yaḥ śambhutām api tathā samupaiti kāryād*  
*govindam ādi puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi*

*iti brahma-saṁhitokteḥ, anyatra ca purāṇāgamādiṣu bahutra īśvaratvena prasiddheś ca.*

**Перевод:**

**Conscious life is of two kinds, independent and dependent. The first is all-pervading and is known as īśvara. The second pervades with energy only his own body and is the called the jīva. He is meant to be controlled. The conscious being īśvara is also of two kinds, one devoid of any touch of material illusion and the other accepting the touch of illusion as his sport. The first goes by the names Nārāyaṇa and so on, as described in the words “Lord Hari is unaffected by the material modes. He directly the enjoying Lord, beyond material nature.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.88.5) The second has the names Śiva and so on, as stated, “Lord Śiva is always united with his personal energy, the material nature. He manifests himself in three features in response to the entreaties of nature’s three modes.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.88.3) One should not conjecture that Lord Śiva is a jīva even though he is characterized in this statement as being covered by the modes of nature. According to** ***Brahma-saṁhitā*** **(5.45), “Just as milk is transformed into curd by the action of acids, but yet the effect curd is neither same as, nor different from, its cause, viz., milk, so I adore the primeval Lord Govinda of whom the state of Śambhu is a transformation for the performance of the work of destruction.” And it is well known from other sources—many statements of the Purāṇas, Āgamas, and other scriptures—that Lord Śiva belongs to the category of īśvara.**

### Verse 11

*yat tu sattvaṁ rajas tama iti prakṛter guṇāḥ ity atra sthity-ādaye hari-viriñci-harā ity anena tat-sādhāraṇyāt brahmaṇy apīśvaratvam avagamyate tad-īśvarāveśād eveti jñeyam,*  
*bhāsvān yathāśma śakaleṣu nijeṣu tejaḥ*  
*svīyam kiyat prakaṭayaty api tadvad atra*  
*brahmā ya eṣa jagad aṇḍa vidhāna kartā*  
*iti brahma-saṁhitokteḥ, tathā,*  
*pārthivād dāruṇo dhūmas*  
*tasmād agnis trayī-mayaḥ*  
*tamasas tu rajas tasmāt*  
*sattvaṁ yad brahma-darśanam*

*ity atra tamasaḥ sakāśāt rajasaḥ śraiṣṭhye ’pi vastuto rajasi dhūma-sthānīye śuddha-tejaḥ-sthānīyasyeśvarasyānupalabdheś ca. sattve sañjvalanāgnau śuddha-tejasaḥ sākṣād iva pārthive dāru-sthānīye tamasy api tasyāntarhitatayopalabdhir asty eva, tat-kārya-suṣuptau nirbheda-jñāna-sukhānubhava ivety-ādi vicārya tattvam avaseyam.*

**Перевод:**

**In the verse beginning “Goodness, passion, and ignorance, are the modes of nature” (Bhāgavatam 1.2.23), we learn from the words “as Hari, Viriñci, and Harā for maintenance and so on” that because of being grouped together with the others Brahmā is also in the category of īśvara. That, however, should be understood in terms of his being empowered by the Supreme Lord, as is corroborated by the Brahma-saṁhitā (5.49), “As the sun exhibits certain parts of its radiance in small stones, similarly this Brahmā acts as the creator of the universe.” We also evidence of this from the statement “Firewood is a transformation of earth, but smoke is better than the raw wood. And fire is still better, for by fire we can derive the benefits of superior knowledge \[through Vedic sacrifices\]. Similarly, passion is better than ignorance, but goodness is best because by goodness one can come to realize the Absolute Truth.” (Bhāgavatam 1.2.24) By considering this statement the actual situation can be ascertained, as follows: Even though passion is superior to ignorance, actually in passion, represented by smoke, the Supreme Lord, represented by pure light, cannot be perceived. Similarly to how the pure light is perceived visibily in the blazing fire of goodness, so also it is perceived as being hidden in the earthy matter of ignorance. In other words, with its effects completely dormant the Supreme appears as the experience of undifferentiated knowledge.**

### Verse 12

*atheśitavyaṁ caitanyaṁ ca sva-daśā-bhedena dvi-vidham, avidyayāvṛtam anāvṛtaṁ ca. tatrāvṛtaṁ deva-manuṣya-tiryag-ādi. anāvṛtaṁ dvi-vidhaṁ, īśvareṇaiśvarya-śaktyānāviṣṭam āviṣṭaṁ ca. anāviṣṭaṁ sthūlato dvi-vidham, jñāna-bhakti-sādhana-vaśāt īśvare līnam alīnaṁ ca. prathamaṁ śocyam, dvitīyaṁ tan-mādhuryāsvādy aśocyam. āviṣṭaṁ ca dvi-vidham, cid-aṁśa-bhūta-jñānādibhir māyāṁśa-bhūta-sṛṣṭy-ādibhiś ceti. prathamaṁ catuḥ-sanādi. dvitīyaṁ brahmādīti. evaṁ ca viṣṇu-śivayor abheda eva prasaktaś caitanyaika-rūpyāt. niṣkāmair upāsyatvānupāsyatve tu nirguṇatva-saguṇatvābhyām evety avagantavyam. viṣṇu-brahmādyos tu bheda eva caitanya-pārthakyād eva. kvacit tu sūryasya tad-āviṣṭa-sūrya-kānta-maṇer abheda iva viṣṇu-brahmaṇor abhedaś ca purāṇa-vacaneṣu dṛṣṭaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Next we should consider that the controlled conscious being is also of two kinds, according to his different conditions of being covered by ignorance or not covered. The covered living beings are demigods, humans, animals, and so on. The uncovered are of two further kinds, empowered by the Supreme Lord through his energies of lordship or not empowered. The unempowered are generally of two kinds, by virtue of their executing one or the other practice of knowledge or devotion; the first are merged into the Lord and the second not merged. The first is pitiable and the second is not pitiable, being a relisher of the Lord’s sweetness. The empowered are also of two kinds, empowered by the knowledge and so on which belong to the Lord’s spiritual aspect, and by the energies of creation and so on which belong to His material aspect. The first of these include the four Kumāras and the second includes Brahmā. In this view it is concluded that Viṣṇu and Śiva are nondifferent, being one and the same living being. We should only understand that those who have no material desires worship one of them and not the other because one is beyond the material modes and the other is connected with the modes. The difference between Viṣṇu and Brahmā, however, is on the basis of their being separate kinds of living beings. Only sometimes, in like manner to how the sun is nondifferent from the sun-stone empowered by the sun, we find statements in the Purāṇas that Viṣṇu and Brahmā are nondifferent.**

### Verse 13

*kiṁ ca kvacin mahā-kalpe śivo ’pi brahmeva īśvarāviṣṭā jīva eva bhavet. yad uktam, kvacij jīva-viśeṣatvaṁ harasyoktaṁ vidher iveti. ata eva,*  
*yas tu nārāyaṇaṁ devaṁ*  
*brahma-rudrādi-daivataiḥ*  
*samatvenaiva vīkṣeta*  
*sa pāṣaṇḍī bhaved dhruvam*

*iti vacanam api brahma-sāhacaryeṇa saṅgacchate iti. evam aparyālocayatāṁ viṣṇur eveśvaro na śivaḥ śiva eveśvaro na viṣṇur vayam ananyā naiva paśyāmaḥ śivaṁ vayaṁ ca na viṣnum ity-ādi-vivāda-grasta-matīnām aparādhe jāte kālena kadācit tat-tātparyālocana-vijña-sādhu-jana-prabodhitatve teṣām eva śivasya bhagavat-svarūpād abhinnatvena labdha-pratītīnāṁ nāma-kīrtanenaivāparādha-kṣayaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**In addition, in some periods of universal creation Śiva is also like Brahmā only an empowered jīva. This is stated: “Sometimes Lord Hara is a particular jīva as is Brahmā.” (Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta 2.27) Thus it is valid for the statement “One who sees Lord Nārāyaṇa as equal to Brahmā, Rudra, and other demigods is certainly an atheist” (Hari-bhakti-vilāsa 1.73) to group Śiva along with Brahmā. Along this line the minds of persons who do not consider the facts carefully become caught in the grips of such controversies as “Viṣṇu alone is God, not Śiva,” “Śiva alone is God, not Viṣṇu.” “We are exclusive devotees and will not look at Śiva.” “And we will not look at Viṣṇu.” In this way they commit offenses, but they may sometime happen to be informed by wise saints who have correctly studied the question; realizing that Śiva is nondifferent from the Supreme Lord in His original form, they may be relieved of their offenses by chanting the Lord’s names.**

### Verse 14

*evaṁ ca naitā bhagavad-bhaktiṁ spṛśanti bahir-mukhyo vigītā iti jñāna-karma-pratipādikāḥ śrutīr yenaiva mukhenānindaṁs tenaiva mukhena tās tad-anuṣṭhātṝṁś ca janān muhur abhinandya nāmabhir uccaiḥ saṅkīrtitaiḥ śruti-śāstra-nindana-rūpāc caturthāparādhān nistareyuḥ. yatas tāḥ śrutayo bhakti-mārgeṣv anadhikāriṇaḥ svacchanda-vartinaḥ parama-rāgāndhān api vartma-mātram adhyārohayitum udyatāḥ parama-kāruṇikā eveti tat-tātparya-vijña-jana-prabodhitā yadi bhāgya-vaśād bhaveyus tadaiveti. evam evānyeṣām api ṣaṇṇām aparādhānām udbhava-nivṛtti-nidānāni avagantavyāni. (3)*

**Перевод:**

**Similarly, using the same tongues by which they condemned the revealed scriptures propounding jñāna and karma—“These scriptures don’t even touch the Supreme Lord’s devotional service. They are atheistic and contemptible.”—to instead praise them and those who carry out their teachings, offenders can deliver themselves by loud chanting of the Lord’s names from the fourth offense of criticizing the Vedic literatures. After all, these revealed scriptures endeavor to take persons unfit for the paths of devotional service, who are whimsical in their behavior and altogether blinded by material attractions, and elevate them to some sort of proper path. These scriptures are thus very compassionate. If by some good fortune offenders are awakened to this understanding by those who are learned in the purports of the revealed scriptures, then they can be freed from this offense. In similar fashion we can ascertain the causes of the other six offenses’ developing and ceasing.**

### Verse 15

*atha bhakty-utthās te ca mūla-śākhāta upaśākhā iva bhaktyaiva dhanādi-lābha-pūjā-pratiṣṭhādyāḥ sva-vṛttibhiḥ sādhaka-cittam apy uparajya sva-vṛddhyā mūla-śākhām iva bhaktim api kuṇṭhayituṁ prabhavantīti.*

*teṣāṁ caturṇām anarthānāṁ nivṛttir api pañca-vidhā, eka-deśa-vartinī bahu-deśa-vartinī prāyikī pūrṇā ātyantikī ceti. tatra grāmo dagdhaḥ paṭo bhagna iti nyāyenāparādhotthānām anarthānāṁ nivṛttir bhajana-kriyānantaram ekadeśa-vartinī niṣṭhāyām utpannāyāṁ bahula-deśa-vartinī ratāv utpadyamānāyāṁ prāyikī premṇi pūrṇā śrī-bhagavat-pada-prāptāv ātyantikī. yas tu tatrāpi citraketau kādācitko mahad-aparādhaḥ sa prātītika eva na vāstavaḥ, satyāṁ prema-sampattau pārṣadatva-vṛtratvayor vaiśiṣṭyābhāva-siddhāntāt.*

**Перевод:**

**As for the undesirable things derived from devotional service, they act like secondary branches coming from a tree’s main branch. In the same way as excessive growth of a tree’s secondary branches may restrict the growth of the main branch, so these byproducts of devotional service—money and other forms of wealth, honor, position, and so on—can attract the practitioner’s mind and restrict the growth of** ***bhakti*****. The elimination of these four kinds of undesirable things can be in five different degrees: to a minor extent, to a major extent, almost complete, complete, and ultimate. Following the logical pattern of “The village has burned down and I ripped my cloth,” the elimination of undesirable things derived from offenses is to a minor extent in the stage of praticing worship, to a major extent when firm faith has developed, almost complete when attachment has appeared, complete in pure love, and ultimate when the divine Lord’s feet have been achieved. That Citraketu in one instance committed a great offense even at that stage of having attained the Lord’s feet was only apparent, not factual; since he had already attained pure love, it is concluded beyond a doubt that there was no difference between his being a personal associate of the Lord and being the demon Vṛtra.**

### Verse 16

*jaya-vijayayos tv aparādha-kāraṇaṁ prema-vijṛmbhitā svecchaiva. sā ca, he prabhu-vara devādi-deva nārāyaṇa, anyatrālpa-balatvād asmāsu tu prātikūlyābhāvād yadi tatra-bhavato yuyutsā na sampādyate tadā āvām eva kenāpi prakāreṇa pratikūlī-kṛtya tad yuddha-sukham anubhūyatām ity āvayoḥ svataḥ-paripūrṇatāyām aṇu-mātram api nyūnatvam asahamānayoḥ kiṅkarayoḥ prārthanā-haṭhaḥ sva-bhakta-vātsyalya-guṇam api laghū-kṛtya niṣpādyatām ity-ākārā kādācitka-prasaṅga-bhavā mānasā manasaiva jñeyā. tathā duṣkṛtotthānāṁ bhajana-kriyānantaram eva prāyikī niṣṭhāyāṁ jātāyāṁ pūrṇā āsaktāv evātyantikī. tathā bhakty-utthānāṁ bhajana-kriyānantaram eka-deśa-vartinī niṣṭhāyāṁ pūrṇā rucāv ātyantikīti anubhavinā bahu-dṛśvanā samyag vivicyānumantavyam. (4)*

**Перевод:**

**The reason for the offense of Jaya and Vijaya, however, was their own desire, a manifestation of their pure love: “O best of masters, Nārāyaṇa, Lord of the demigods and others, other people lack enough strength, while we are not inimical. So if Your desire to fight cannot be fulfilled, then please somehow make the two of us inimical and experience that joy of fighting. May the force of this prayer by us who cannot tolerate the tiniest trace of incompleteness in Your natural perfection, diminish even Your quality of compassion for Your devotees, and make our prayer a reality.” We can mentally reconstruct that something like this was the train of thought that arose on this exceptional occasion. Regarding the elimination of undesirable things dervied from bad deeds, after the practice of worship is begun it is almost complete, when firm conviction appears it is complete, and in attachment it is ultimate. And the elimination of those which are derived from devotional service is to a minor extent after beginning the practice of worship, complete in firm conviction, and ultimate in attraction. The discriminating reader who examines the several different cases can properly discriminate them and infer all this for himself.**

### Verse 17

*nanu, aṁhaḥ saṁharad akhilaṁ sakṛd udayād eva \[nāma-kaumudī, padyāvalī 16\] iti, yan-nāma sakṛc-chravaṇāt pukkaśo ’pi vimucyate saṁsārāt ity api pramāṇa-śatād ajāmilādy-upākhyāneṣv ekasyaiva nāmābhāsasyāvidyā-paryanta-sarvānartha-nivṛtti-pūrvaka-bhagavat-prāpakatvānubhavād bhagavad-bhaktānāṁ duritādi-nivṛttāv uktaḥ kramo na saṅgacchate. satyam, nāmna etāvaty eva śaktir nātra sandehaḥ, paraṁ tu svāparādhiṣv aprasannena tena yat sva-śaktiḥ samyak na prakāśyate tad eva duṣṭatādīnāṁ jīvātur ity avagantavyam. kintu yama-dūtānāṁ tad-ākramaṇe na śaktiḥ. na vidyate tasya yamair hi śuddhir ity atra yamer yogāṅgair iti vyākhyeyam. yathā samarthena paramāḍhyenāpi svāminā kṛtāparādhaḥ sva-jano yadi na pālyate kintu tatrodāsyate tadaiva duḥkha-dāridrya-mālinya-śokādayaḥ krameṇa labdhāvasarā bhavanti na tv anyadīyā janāḥ ke ’pi kadāpīti jñeyam. tathā ca punaḥ sva-svāmino mano-’bhirocinyām anuvṛttau satyāṁ śanais tat-prasādād duḥkha-dāridryādayaḥ śanair apayānti. tathā bhagavad-bhakta-śāstra-guru-prabhṛtibhir amāyayā muhuḥ sevitaiḥ śanair eva tasya nāmnaḥ prasāde duritādīnām api śanair eva nāśaḥ, iti nāsti vivādaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**“But the sequence described above of the the elimination of difficulties and so on for devotees of the Supreme Lord does not agree with the facts, since there are hundreds of authoritative statements saying, for example, ‘The Lord’s name removes all sin by being uttered even once’ (*****Nāma-kaumudī*****) and ‘By hearing whose name just once even a Pukkaśa is delivered from the material existence,’ and we see in the narrations of Ajāmila and others that the shadow of the Lord’s name chanted just once removes all undesirable things, including one’s underlying material delusion, and then leads one to attain the Personality of Godhead.” Yes, there is no doubt that the Lord’s name possesses this much potency. But we should understand that the name’s being dissatisfied with those who offend it and therefore not revealing its potency fully to them is the reason their wickedness and so on hold on to life. Still, however, the servants of Yama have no power to attack such a person, as we hear from such statements as “Even in dreams, such surrendered souls do not see Yamarāja or his order carriers, who are equipped with ropes to bind the sinful.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **6.1.19) In the words “There is no purification for this by yamas” the phrase “by yamas” should be explained as meaning “by practices of the yoga system.” For example, we can understand that if a capable and extremely rich master does not give his protection to a servant of his who has been offensive and instead treats him with neglect, then discomfort, poverty, squalor, and sorrow will gradually find their opportunities. Servants belonging to other masters, however, will never have any chance to harm him. And if the servant begins once more to behave in a way pleasing to his master, he will gradually get his favor again and his discomfort, poverty, and so on will gradually disappear. Similarly it is incontrovertible that when the Supreme Lord, His devotees, the scriptures, the spiritual masters, and so on, are served repeatedly without deception, then by the Lord’s name becoming satisfied one’s sinful reactions and other obstacles will gradually be destroyed.**

### Verse 18

*na ca mama ko ’pi nāsti nāmāparādha iti vaktavyaṁ phalenaiva phala-kāraṇasyāparādhasya prācīnasyārvācīnasya vānumānāt. phalaṁ ca bahu-nāma-kīrtane ’pi prema-liṅgānudaya iti. yad uktaṁ,*

*tad aśma sāraṁ hṛdayaṁ batedaṁ*  
*yad gṛhyamāṇair hari nāmadheyaiḥ*  
*na vikriyetātha yadā vikāro*  
*netre jalaṁ gātra ruheṣu harṣaḥ*  
*iti. tathā hi nāmāparādha-prasaṅge,*  
*ke te ’parādhā viprendra*  
*nāmno bhagavataḥ kṛtāḥ*  
*vinighnanti nṛṇāṁ kṛtyaṁ*  
*prākṛtaṁ hy ānayanti hi*

*iti. tadīya-guṇa-nāmādīni sadyaḥ prema-pradāny api śrutāni kīrtitāni ca tat-tīrthādikaṁ sadyaḥ siddhi-dam api cirāt sevitaṁ tan-niveditāni ghṛta-dugdha-tāmbūlādīni sadyaḥ sarvendriya-taraṅga-nivartakāni muhur āsvādya upayuktāny eva svataḥ parama-cin-mayāny apy etāni yasmāt prākṛtānīva bhavanti te ’parādhāḥ ke bhagavan-nāma iti sotkampa-savismayaḥ praśnaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**One should not say “I haven’t commited any offenses against the Lord’s name,” since we can infer the cause—some offense, either ancient or recent—from its result. This result is that even after chanting the Lord’s name many times the symptoms of pure love do not appear. As it has been said, “Certainly that heart is steel-framed which, in spite of one’s chanting the holy name of the Lord with concentration, does not change when ecstasy takes place, tears fill the eyes, and the hairs stand on end.” (Bhāgavatam 2.3.24) It is also said in regards to offenses against the Lord’s name, “O learned brāhmaṇa, what are these offenses people commit against the name of the Supreme Lord, that the offenses destroy the good they have done and turn them into materialists?” This is a question asked with compassion and amazement: The Lord’s qualities, names, and so on at once give pure love, and they have been heard and chanted about. The holy places and so on connected with Him at one give perfection, and they have been served for a long time. The ghee, milk, betel nut, and so on offered to Him at once cause waves of pleasure for all the senses, and they have been repeatedly partaken of and relished. If all these seem to become material just because of offenses against the Lord’s names, what could these offenses be?**

### Verse 19

*nanv evaṁ sati nāmāparādhavato janasya bhagavad-vaimukhyasyaivocityāt tad uktaṁ guru-pādāśraya-bhajana-kriyādikam api na sambhavet. satyam, pravartamāne mahā-jvara iva odanāder arocakatvād evānupādānam iva nāmāparādhasya gāḍhatve sati tatra puṁsi śravaṇa-kīrtanādi-bhajana-kriyāyā avakāśa eva na syād ity atra kaḥ sandehaḥ. kintu jvarasya mṛdutve cirantanatve odanāder api kiñcid rocakatvam iva. bahu-dinato bhogenāparādhasya kṣīṇa-vegatve mṛdutve ca bhagavad-bhaktau kiñcin-mātra-ruciḥ syād iti puṁsaḥ prasajjati bhakty-adhikāraḥ. tataś ca yathā pauṣṭikāny api dugdhaudanādīni jīrṇa-jvaravantaṁ pumāṁsaḥ na puṣyanti kiñcit puṣyanti ca kintu glāni-kārśyena nivartayituṁ śaknuvanti kālenauṣadha-pathyayoḥ sevitayoḥ śaknuvanti ca. tathaiva tādṛśasya bhakty-adhikāriṇaḥ śravaṇa-kīrtanādīni kālenaiva krameṇaiva sakalaṁ prakāśayantīti sādhūktam,*

*ādau śraddhā tataḥ sādhu-*  
*saṅgo ’tha bhajana-kriyā*  
*tato ’nartha-nivṛttiḥ syāt*  
*tato niṣṭhā…*  
*ity-ādi.*

**Перевод:**

**“But if this is so, most likely a person who has commited offense against the Personality of Godhead’s name will become inimical to the Lord. How then, can he possibly take shelter of the feet of a guru, engage in the practice of worship, and so on, as described above?” Yes, in the same was as when one is suffering from a serious fever he does not take rice and other food because it doesn’t appeal to him, so also there is no doubt that when one’s offenses against the Lord become very severe there is no chance that he will engage in hearing, chanting, and the other practices of worship. But as when the fever becomes milder and grows old, the rice and other food become a little attractive. so when one’s offenses lose their impetus and become milder by their reactions being suffered for a long time, a person may develop a little attraction to the Lord’s devotional service and he may become a fit candidate for bhakti. Even then, the most nourishing milk, rice, and other food fail to nourish a person debilitated by fever, or else nourish him only a little, but these foods are able to remedy his fatigue and weakness, and after some time of his taking medicine and a proper diet they are then able to nourish him. Similarly, these activities of hearing, chanting, and so on over time gradually reveal everything for such a candidate for devotional service. Thus it has appropriately been said, “First one has faith, then there is association with saintly persons, then he practices worship, then undesirable things cease, and then there is firm conviction…” (Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu 1.4.15) and so on.**

### Verse 20

*kaiścit tu nāma-kīrtanādivatāṁ bhaktānāṁ prema-liṅgādarśanena pāpa-pravṛttyā ca na kevalam aparādhaḥ kalpyate vyavahārika-bahu-duḥkha-darśanena cāpi prārabdha-nāśābhāvaś ca, niraparādhatvena nirdhāritasyājāmilasyāpi sva-putra-nāma-karaṇa-pratidina-bahudhā-tan-nāmāhvāna-samayeṣv api premābhāva-dāsī-saṅgādi-pāpa-pravṛtti-darśanāt, prārabdhābhāve ’pi yudhiṣṭhirāder vyavahārika-bahu-duḥkha-darśanāc ca. tasmāt phalann api vṛkṣaḥ prāyaśaḥ kāla eva phalati iti-vat niraparādheṣu prasīdad api nāma sva-prasādaṁ kāla eva prakāśayet. pūrvābhyāsāt kriyamāṇā pāpa-rāśir api utkhāta-daṁṣṭroraga-daṁśa ivākiñcit-karā eva. roga-śokādi-duḥkham api na prārabdha-phalam,*

*yasyāham anugṛhṇāmi*  
*hariṣye tad-dhanaṁ śanaiḥ*  
*tato ’dhanaṁ tyajanty asya*  
*sva-janā duḥkha-duḥkhitam*

*iti, nirdhanatva-mahā-rogo mad-anugraha-lakṣaṇam ity-ādi-vacanāt. sva-bhakta-hita-kāriṇā tadīya-dainyotkaṇṭhādi-vardhana-catureṇa bhagavataiva duḥkhasya dīyamānatvāt karma-phalatvābhāvena na prārabdhatvam ity āhuḥ. (5)*

**Перевод:**

**Some presume, from not seeing the signs of pure love in devotees engaged in chanting and so on of the Lord’s names and from seeing them engage in sinful activities, that not only have offeness been commited but also, from observing them suffer considerably in their worldly dealings, that their manifest sinful reactions have not been destroyed. Indeed it is seen that even Ajāmila, who is understood to have not committed offense \[against the Lord’s name\], still had no pure love and engaged in such sins as consorting with a low-class woman, even at the same times that he was calling out the name of the Lord many times daily by speaking the name of his own son. It is also seen that Yudhiṣṭhira and others who had no manifest sinful reactions suffered considerably in their worldly dealings. The conclusion therefore is that just as even a fruit-bearing tree bears its fruit only in due course of time, so the name of the Lord, even when satisfied with those who are free from offenses, reveals its grace only at the proper time. The heaps of sins a devotee may perform because of past habit are ineffective, like the bite of serpent whose fangs have been extracted. His miseries of disease, sorrow, and so on are not results of manifest karma, as affirmed in such statements as “If I especially favor someone, I gradually deprive him of his wealth. Then the relatives and friends of such a poverty-stricken man abandon him. In this way he suffers one distress after another” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.88.8) and “The terrible disease of poverty is a sign of My favor.” It is explained by authorities that since this misery is being given by the Supreme Lord, who acts for the benefit of His devotees and is expert in promoting their humility, eagerness, and so on, thus it is not a result of karma and so is not a manifest reaction of sin.**

*iti mahā-mahopādhyāya-śrīmad-viśvanātha-cakravarti-viracitāyāṁ mādhurya-kādambinyāṁ bhakteḥ sarva-graha-praśaminī nāma tṛtīyāmṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the Third Rain of Nectar, named “Dispelling all evil influences,” of** ***Mādhurya-kādambinī,*** **composed by Mahā-mahopadhyāya Śrīmān Viśvanātha Cakravartī.**

## Fourth Rain of Nectar - caturthy amṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ

### Verse 1

*atha pūrvaṁ yā aniṣṭhitā niṣṭhiteti dvi-vidhoktā bhajana-kriyā tasyāḥ prathamā ṣaḍ-vidhā lakṣitā. tato dvitīyām alakṣayitvaivānartha-nivṛttiḥ prakrāntā. yad uktam,*

*śṛṇvatāṁ sva-kathāḥ kṛṣṇaḥ*  
*puṇya-śravaṇa-kīrtanaḥ*  
*hṛdy antaḥ-stho hy abhadrāṇi*  
*vidhunoti suhṛt-satām*  
*naṣṭa-prāyeṣv abhadreṣu*  
*nityaṁ bhāgavata-sevayā*  
*bhagavaty uttama-śloke*  
*bhaktir bhavati naiṣṭhikī*

*tatra śṛṇvatāṁ sva-kathāḥ kṛṣṇaḥ puṇya-śravaṇa-kīrtanaḥ ity aniṣṭhitaiva bhaktir avagamyate naiṣṭhikīty agre vakṣyamāṇatvāt. abhadrāṇi vidhunoti iti tayor madhye evānarthānāṁ nivṛttir uktā. naṣṭa-prāyeṣv abhadreṣv ity atra teṣāṁ kaścana bhāgo nāpi nivartata ity api sūcita iti. ata eva krama-prāptatayā niṣṭhitā bhaktir idānīṁ vivriyate. (1)*

**Перевод:**

**Now, of the two varieties of practice of worship spoken of—without and with conviction—the first has first been described in six divisions. Then, instead of describing the characteristics of the second variety, the topic of eliminating undesired things was taken up. This accords with the description, “Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Personality of Godhead, who is the Paramātmā in everyone’s heart and the benefactor of the truthful devotee, cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages, which are in themselves virtuous when properly heard and chanted. By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed, and loving service unto the Personality of Godhead, who is praised with transcendental songs, is established as an irrevocable fact.” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **1.2.17–18) In these verses the words “Śrī Kṛṣṇa, whose topics are themselves virtuous when properly heard and chanted, for those who are hearing His topics…” should be understood as referring to devotional service without firm conviction, since the term “with firm faith” is spoken after this. And in between the two stages, the elimination of undesired things is spoken of in the words “He cleanses unwanted things.” By the words “When inauspicious things have been almost completely destroyed” implies that sometimes some portion of them does not desist. Therefore it is appropriate to the sequence that we now discuss devotional service with firm conviction.**

### Verse 2

*niṣṭhā naiścalyam utpannā yasyā iti niṣṭhitā. naiścalyaṁ bhakteḥ praty-ahaṁ vidhitsitam apy anartha-daśāyāṁ laya-vikṣepāpratipatti-kaṣāya-rasāsvādānāṁ pañcānām antarīyāṇāṁ durvāratvān na siddham āsīt. anartha-nivṛtty-anantaraṁ teṣāṁ tadīyānāṁ nivṛtta-prāyatvāt naiścalyam sampadyate iti layādy-abhāva eva niṣṭhā-liṅgam. tatra layaḥ kīrtana-śravaṇa-smaraṇeṣv uttareṣv ādhikyena nidrodgamaḥ. vikṣepas teṣu vyavahārika-vārtā-samparkaḥ. apratipattiḥ kadācil laya-vikṣepayor abhāve kīrtanādy-asāmarthyam. kaṣāyaḥ krodha-lobha-garvādi-saṁskāraḥ. rasāsvādaḥ viṣaya-sukhodaya-kāle kīrtanādiṣu mano- ’nabhiniveśa iti.*

**Перевод:**

**Niṣṭhitā bhakti is that devotional service in which firm conviction, steadiness, has developed. Steadiness in devotional service was not achieved in the stage of still being burdened by unwanted things even though daily attempts were made to achieve it because it was difficult to avoid the five obstacles of “dissolution,” “distraction,” “incapacity,” “contamination,” and “indulgence in pleasure.” But once undersirable things have been eliminated, these obstacles and everything related to them are almost completely erradicated, and then steadiness is attained. Thus absence of “dissolution” and so on is the sign of firm conviction. Among these obstacles, “dissolution” is when the more one engages in chanting, hearing, and remembering, the more one is overcome by drowsiness. “Distraction” is involving oneself in mundane topics. “Incapacity” is when even in the absence of “dissolution” and “distraction” one cannot perform chanting and the other processes. “Contamination” is the residue of past anger, greed, pride, and so on. “Indulgence in pleasure” is the mind’s inability to stay absorbed in chanting and so on when opportunities for sense gratification arise.**

### Verse 3

*bhaktir bhavati naiṣṭhikī*  
*tadā rajas-tamo-bhāvāḥ*  
*kāma-lobhādayaś ca ye*  
*ceta etair anāviddhaṁ*  
*sthitaṁ sattve prasīdati*

*ity atra ca-kārasya samuccayārthatvād rajas-tamo-bhāvā eva labhyante. kiṁ ca, etair anāviddham ity ukte bhāva-paryantaṁ teṣāṁ sthitir apy asti bhakty-abādhakatayaiva.*

**Перевод:**

**“Devotion becomes fixed. Then the effects of nature’s modes of passion and ignorance, such as lust, desire and hankering, disappear from the heart. The devotee is established in goodness, and he becomes completely happy.” (Bhāgavatam 1.2.18–19) Since the word “and” in this text signifies completed combination, it is only the effects of passion and ignorance that are indicated. Furthermore, since it said “not obstructed by them,” these effects remain even up to the stage of ecstasy, but without obstructing devotional service.**

### Verse 4

*sā ca niṣṭhā sākṣād-bhakti-vartinī tad-anukūla-vastu-vartinīti dvi-vidhā. tatra sākṣād-bhaktir ananta-prakārāpi sthūlatayā tri-vidhā, kāyikī vācikī mānasī ceti. tatra prathamaṁ kāyikyās tato vācikyās tata eva mānasyā bhakter niṣṭhā sambhaved iti kecit. bhakteṣu tāratamyena sthitānām api saha-ojo-balānāṁ madhye kvacana bhakte vilakṣaṇa-tādṛśa-saṁskāra-vaśāt kasyacid eva bhagavad-unmukhatvādhikyaṁ syād iti nāyaṁ krama ity anye.*

**Перевод:**

**This firm conviction is moreover of two kinds, residing in direct devotional service and residing in things favorable to devotional service. Specifically, direct devotional service, although it has infinite varieties, is generally of three categories—done with the body, words, and the mind. Some say in this regard that first devotional service done with the body becomes fixed in conviction, then devotional service with one’s words, and then devotional service with the mind. Others, however, say that this is not necessarily the order because although within devotees there is a normal hierarchy of mental strength, sensory strength, and mental strength, sometimes in a particular devotee one of them becomes especially inclined toward the Supreme Lord because of the special conditioning of that kind of strength in the devotee.**

### Verse 5

*tad-anukūla-vastuni amānitva-mānadatva-maitrī-dayādīni. teṣāṁ niṣṭhā ca kutracana śama-prakṛtau bhakte bhakter aniṣṭhitatve dṛśyate kutracana tasminn uddhate bhakte niṣṭhitatve ’pi na dṛśyate yady api tad api bhakti-niṣṭhaiva sva-sattvāsattvābhyāṁ tan-niṣṭhā-sattvāsattve su-dhiyam avagamayati na tu bāla-pratītir eva vāstavī-kartuṁ śakyeti. yad uktam,*

*bhaktir bhavati naiṣṭhikī*  
*tadā rajas-tamo-bhāvāḥ*  
*kāma-lobhādayaś ca ye*  
*ceta etair anāviddhaṁ*  
*sthitaṁ sattve prasīdati*

*iti. śravaṇa-kīrtanādiṣu yatnasya śaithilya-prābalya eva dustyajye sambhavantī niṣṭhitāniṣṭhite bhaktī pradarśayetām iti saṅkṣepato vivekaḥ. \[2\]*

**Перевод:**

**Things favorable to devotional service include freedom from expectation of respect, giving respect to all, friendliness, and compassion. Although these are sometimes seen in a devotee of self-controlled nature even when his devotion is not yet fixed, and sometimes are not seen in a devotee of an excitable nature even when he is fixed in conviction, nonetheless it is fixed conviction in devotional service alone which informs a wise person by its own presence or absence about the presence or absence of these related qualities. Immature perception, on the other hand, is not capable of ascertaining the factual situation. As it is said, “Devotion becomes fixed. Then the effects of nature’s modes of passion and ignorance, such as lust, desire and hankering, disappear from the heart. The devotee is established in goodness, and he becomes completely happy.” (Bhāgavatam 1.2.18–19) This can be analyzed in brief, that when laxity or firmness in one’s endeavor in hearing, chanting, and so on, are irrevocable, they indicate devotional service that is fixed or not fixed in conviction.**

*iti mādhurya-kādambinyāṁ niṣyanda-bandhurā nāma caturthy amṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the Fourth Rain of Nectar, named “Preparing to pour down,” of** ***Mādhurya-kādambinī,*** **composed by Mahā-mahopadhyāya Śrīmān Viśvanātha Cakravartī.**

## The Fifth Rain of Nectar - pañcamy amṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ

### Verse 1

*athābhyāsa-kṛṣṇa-vartma-dīpitāṁ bhakti-kāñcana-mudrāṁ sva-tejasā vahantīṁ dadhāne bhakta-hṛdi tasyāṁ rucir utpadyate. śravaṇa-kīrtanādīnām anyato vailakṣaṇyena rocakatvaṁ ruciḥ. yasyām utpadyamānāyāṁ pūrva-daśāyām iva tair muhur apy anuśīlitair na śramopalabdhi-gandho ’pi. yā hi teṣu vyasanitvam acirād evotpādayati. (1)*

**Перевод:**

**Next, when a devotee’s heart is carrying the golden coin of devotion, and that coin has been heated by the fire of practice to the point that by its own energy it begins to melt, then the devotee’s heart develops a taste for it. “Taste” is a relish for hearing, chanting, and so on due to their exceptional nature. When one has developed this taste, he no longer experiences even a trace of fatigue, as he did in earlier stage of his practice, in repeatedly engaging in these activities. The taste soon makes him intensely interested in them.**

### Verse 2

*yathā nityaṁ śāstram adhīyānasya baṭoḥ kāle śāstrārtha-praveśe sati śāstrasya rocakatvam utpādyamānam eva taṁ tatra śramaṁ nopanayaty āsañjayati ca. vastutaḥ siddhānte tu paittika-vaiguṇyena dūṣitāyāṁ rasanāyāṁ sitāyā arocakatve ’pi sitaiva tad-vaiguṇya-nirāsakam auṣadham iti vivekinaḥ tasyā eva yathā muhur upasevane kālena svādvīyaṁ svādvīyam ābhātīti tasyā eva rocakatvaṁ tathaivāvidyādi-vidūṣitasya jīvāntaḥ-karaṇasya śravaṇādi-bhaktyā tad-doṣa-praśame tasyāṁ rucir udbhavatīti. (2)*

**Перевод:**

**This is similar to how a young student constantly studying some subject eventually catches on to gist of the subject and develops a relish for it. He is no longer overcome by fatigue in his study and he becomes attached to it. The actual situation is that although when one’s sense of taste is spoiled by a bilious disorder, one experiences no relish for the taste of sugar candy, nonetheless it is the same sugar candy which is the medicine to remedy that disorder. Those who have good discrimination therefore will take repeated doses of that candy, and it will gradually seem more and more palatable. Thus they regain their taste for it. And in the same way, a living entity’s mind contaminated by material illusion and other factors will experience the curing of that faultly condition by the devotional practices of hearing and so on, and will develop a taste for devotional service.**

### Verse 3

*sā ca rucir dvi-vidhā, vastu-vaiśiṣṭyāpekṣiṇī tad-anapekṣiṇī ca. vastūnāṁ bhagavan-nāma-rūpa-guṇa-līlādīnāṁ vaiśiṣṭyaṁ kīrtanasya sausvaryādimattvaṁ varṇita-bhagavac-caritāder guṇālaṅkāra-dhvany-ādimattvaṁ paricaryādīnāṁ tādṛśa-svābhīṣṭa-deśa-pātra-dravyādi-sad-bhāvavattvaṁ yad apekṣate tad vastu-vaiśiṣṭyāpekṣiṇī, kiṁ kiṁ kīdṛśaṁ vyañjanam astīti pṛcchatāṁ manda-kṣudvatām iva. prathamā seyaṁ yato ’ntaḥ-karaṇasya yat kiñcid doṣa-lava eva kīrtanādīnāṁ vaiśiṣṭyam apekṣate ato ’sty antaḥkaraṇa-doṣābhāsā jñeyā. dvitīyā tu yathā tan-nāma-rūpādy-upakrama eva balavatī bhavantī vaiśiṣṭye tv ati-prauḍhatvam āpadyamāneyaṁ nāsti mano-vaiguṇya-gandhā eva jñeyā. (3)*

**Перевод:**

**This taste, moreover, is of two kinds, paying regard and not paying regard to the special qualities of the object. Taste is said to pay regard to speciality when it is concerned about the special qualities of features of the Lord like His names, forms, qualities, and pastimes: whether chanting is done with good melody and so on, whether stories and other topics narrated about the Lord have good poetic style, figures of speech, suggestive use of words, and so on, whether personal service and other activities involve one’s own favorite places, participants, paraphernalia, and so on. This is similar to the taste of persons who are not very hungry and ask, “What kind of vegetables are there, and how have they been prepared?” This is the rudimentary kind of taste, since due to some slight contamination of the mind one is concerned about the special qualities of chanting and so on. Thus it is to be understood that there remains a shadow of contamination in the mind. The second variety of taste is such, however, that as soon as the Lord’s names, forms, and so on are perceived the taste manifests with full strength, and if there are especially good qualities in what is perceived it becomes a peak experience. Then it can be understood that there is no trace of contamination left in the mind.**

### Verse 4

*tataś cāho sakhe, kṛṣṇa-nāmāmṛtāni vihāya kim iti duṣparigraha-yoga-kṣema-vārtā-viṣayeṣu nimajjayasi tvāṁ vā kiṁ bravīmi dhiṅ māṁ yad aham api pāmaraḥ śrī-guru-caraṇa-prasāda-labdham apy etad vastu sva-granthi-nibaddhaṁ mahā-ratnam ivānupalabhya parito bhramann etāvantaṁ kālam anya-vyāpāra-pārāvāra-madhye mithyā-sukha-leśa-sphuṭita-kapardaka-mātram anviṣyāyūṁsi vṛthaivānayam. bhakteḥ kam apy anaṅgī-kurvan śakter abhāvam evādyotayam. hanta sa evāhaṁ saiveyaṁ me rasanā yā hy anṛta-kaṭu-grāmya-pralāpam amṛtam iva lihyatī bhagavan-nāma-guṇa-vārtāsu sālasaivāsīt. hanta hanta tat-kathā-śravaṇārambha eva svāpaṁ bhajaṁs tadaiva kadācit prastutāyāṁ grāmya-vārtāyām utkarṇatayā labdha-jāgaraṁ sādhūnāṁ sad eva taṁ sakalam akalaṅkayan. asya ca duṣpūrasya jaṭharasya kṛte jaraṭho ’pi kāṁs kān duṣkṛtodyamān nākaravam.*

**Перевод:**

**It is like this: “My dear friend, why have you abandoned the nectarean names of Kṛṣṇa to immerse yourself in topics of acquiring and securing useless property?” “What can I tell you? I should be condemned, being such a wretch that I have failed to recognize what I obtained by the mercy of my divine spiritual master’s feet, which is like a priceless jewel tied up in the knot of my own garment. Instead all this time I have wandered about searching for drops of imaginary pleasure, for mere broken pieces of shell, and have wasted my whole life. I have not taken advantage of anything from devotional service and have only displayed my impotence. Oh, I am so pitiable, and so is my tongue, which has licked up the bitter dregs of mundane gossip as if it were nectar and has been lazy about the topics of the names and qualities of the Supreme Lord. Oh, as soon as I begin to hear discussions about the Lord, I fall asleep, but if by chance someone introduces some mundane topic my ears perk up and I am suddenly awake. In this way I have poluted everything the pure saints hold sacred. And for the sake of my insatiable belly what wicked attempts have I not made, even in my old age!**

### Verse 5

*tad ahaṁ na jāne kasmin vā niraye sva-kṛta-phalam upabhuñjānaḥ sthāsyāmīti nirvidyamānas tadaiva kvacid aho raho bhuvi mahopaniṣat-kalpa-vallī-phala-sāraṁ sāraṅga iva prabhoś caritāmṛtaṁ svādayann abhivādayan muhur muhur api sādhūn avyādhūta-saṁlāpas tiṣṭhann upaviśan praviśann api bhagavad-dhāma-baddhāmala-sevā-niṣṭhas tan-manā unmanā ivānabhijña-lokair ālakṣyamāṇo bhakta-jana-bhajanānanda-nṛtyādhyāyam adhyetum upakramamāṇa iva ruci-nartakyā pāṇibhyāṁ gṛhītveva tat tat śikṣyamāṇa iva kāñcana mudam ananubhūta-carīm upalabhe na jāne kuśīlavācāryābhyāṁ bhāva-premabhyāṁ kālena praviśya nartayiṣyamāṇaḥ kasyāṁ vā nirvṛti-nīvṛti virājayiṣyatīti.*

**Перевод:**

**“Therefore I do not know in what hell I will end up, suffering the fruits of my deeds.” Lamenting like this, he then finds some secluded place on the face of the earth to relish like a bumblebee the nectar of narrations about the Lord, the essential fruit of the desire tree of the great Upaniṣads, and to offer respectful greetings over and over again to saintly devotees. Undistracted by mundane talks, whether standing, sitting, or entering a place, his mind is fixed on the pure service found always in the Lord’s personal abode. When less intelligent people see him absorbed like this, they take him to be insane. He seems to want to start studying from the Lord’s devotees the dancing lessons of their blissful worship, but then it seems the dancing girl of transcendental taste grabs him by the hands. He then seems to learn everything from her, and he obtains a special joy which he has not experienced in a long time. And after some more time, when the two dancing masters ecstasy and love will make their entrance, it is hard to say what splendorous state of delight the devotee will attain in his dancing.**

*iti mahā-mahopadhyāya-śrīmad-viśvanātha-cakravarti-viracitāyāṁ mādhurya-kādambinyāṁ upalabdhāsvāda-nāma pañcamy amṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the Fifth Rain of Nectar, named “Experiencing the taste,” of** ***Mādhurya-kādambinī,*** **composed by Mahā-mahopadhyāya Śrīmān Viśvanātha Cakravartī.**

## Sixth Rain of Nectar - ṣaṣṭhy amṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ

### Verse 1

*atha saiva bhajana-viṣayā ruciḥ parama-prauḍhitamā satī yadā bhajanīyaṁ bhagavantaṁ viṣayī-karoti tadeyam āsaktir ity ākhyāyate, yaiva bhakti-kalpa-vallyāḥ stavakī-bhāvam āsādayantī bhāva-premaṇī puṣpa-phale acirād eva bhāvinī dyotayati. rucir bhajana-viṣayā āsaktir bhajanīya-viṣayeti bhūmnaiva vyāpadeśaḥ. vastutas tūbhe apy ubhayaṁ viṣayī-karoty eva. aprauḍhatva-prauḍhatvābhyām eva bhedaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Next, when this taste for the process of worship becomes perfectly mature and makes the worshipful Lord its focus, it then becomes known as “attachment.” Developing as the budding sprout of the desire creeper of devotion, it signifies that the flower and fruit of ecstasy and love are going to soon appear. The two stages of taste and attachment are differently designated according to what is prominent in them, taste focusing on the worship and attachment focusing on the object of worship. In fact, however, both of them focus on both aspects; the real difference is simply that one is immature and the other is mature.**

### Verse 2

*āsaktir evāntaḥ-karaṇa-mukuraṁ tathā mārjayati yathā tatra sahasā pratibimbito bhagavān avalokyamāna iva bhavati. hanta viṣayair ākramyate madīyaṁ cetas tad idaṁ bhagavati nidadhāmīti bhaktasya vidhitsānantaram eva prāyo viṣayebhyo niṣkramya, tad-rūpa-guṇādau yat praveśa-śīlaṁ pūrva-daśāyām āsīt tad eva cittam āsaktau jātāyāṁ vidhitsātaḥ pūrvam eva svayam eva tathā-bhūtaṁ bhavet. yathā bhagavad-rūpa-guṇādibhyo niṣkramya vārtāntare cetaḥ kadā praviṣṭam iti prāpta-niṣṭhenāpi bhaktena nānusandhātuṁ śakyate tathaiva vārtāntarato niṣkramya bhagavad-rūpa-guṇādiṣu kadā praviṣṭaḥ sva-ceta ity āsaktir anāsaktena na lakṣyate. āsaktimatā bhaktena tu tal lakṣyate. (1)*

**Перевод:**

**Attachment cleans the mirror of the heart so that the Lord can without warning appear there in His reflection. In his previous condition the devotee was able to extract his mind from sensory gratifications and absorb it in the Lord’s form, qualities, and so on usually only after a concerted effort: “Oh, my mind has been attacked by objects of gratification! Let me concentrate it now on the Supreme Lord.” But when he has achieved attachment, his mind becomes thus absorbed even before he makes any effort. Even a devotee who has achieved the stage of fixed determination cannot recognize when his mind might have become distracted from the forms, qualities, and so on of the Supreme Lord and entered into contemplation of other matters. Similarly, attachment means that one cannot recognize when his mind became distracted from other matters to enter contemplation of the Lord’s forms, qualities, and so on; one who has not achieved the stage of** ***āsakti*** **cannot experience this attachment but one who has achieved it can.**

### Verse 3

*tataś ca prātaḥ kutastyo ’pi bhoḥ kaṇṭha-lambita-śrī-śālagrāma-śilā-sundara-sampuṭo laghu-laghūccārita-śrī-kṛṣṇa-nāmāmṛtāsvāda-pratikṣaṇa-lolita-rasanaḥ prekṣyamāṇa eva durbhagaṁ mām ullāsayasi kasmiṁścid arthe tat kathaya kutra kutra vā tīrthe bhramaṇaṁ keṣāṁ dṛṣṭyā keṣāṁ vā bhagavad-anubhavānām āsadī-bhavann ātmānam anyaṁ cākṛtārthayaḥ. ity udbhāvita-saṁlāpāmṛta-pāna-yāpita-katipaya-kṣaṇaḥ punar anyato gatvā bhoḥ kakṣa-nikṣipta-manohara-pustaka-vilakṣaṇayā śriyā vidvān evānumīyase tad vyācakṣva daśama-skandhīyaṁ padyam ekaṁ jīvaya śruti-cātakīṁ tad-arthāmṛta-vṛṣṭyā.*

**Перевод:**

**It is like this: Early in the morning one says “Dear sir coming from somewhere with the divine Śālagrāma-śilā hanging from your neck in a handsome box, I see that your tongue is in a constant flurry of relishing the nectar of Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s names as you recite them very softly. This sight encourages me, who am so unfortunate, to ask you something. Please tell me, what holy places have you visited in your wanderings? What things have you seen and what realization of the Supreme Lord have you had that have made you able to perfect your own life and those of others?” Spending a few minutes drinking the nectar of the words spoken in response, he then goes somewhere else and says, “Dear sir with the exceptional opulence of these attractive books arranged in your room, I can deduce that you are a learned man. So please explain to me this one verse of the Tenth Canto and bring the sparrow of my ears back to life by raining down the nectar of its purport.”**

### Verse 4

*iti tad-vyākhyayā romāñcita-gātraḥ punar anyato gatvā hantādhunaivāhaṁ kṛtārthī-bhaviṣyāmi yad iyaṁ sabhaiva sadya eva mama samasta-duṣkṛta-dhvaṁsinīti viracita-daṇḍavad-avani-praṇipāta-puraḥsara-praṇati-vinatikaḥ tat-sabhā-mukuṭa-maṇinā mahā-bhāgavata-varyeṇa parama-viduṣā sa-rasam ādriyamāṇaḥ saṅkucita-tanus tad-antika-kṛtopaveśa eva bhos tribhuvana-jīva-bhavana-mahā-bhava-roga-bhiṣak-śiromaṇe dhṛtvaiva dhamanīm adhamasyāpi me mahā-dīnasya nirūpaya rujaṁ samādiśasva pathyauṣadhe kenāpi prayuktena mahā-rasāyanena mad-abhīpsitāṁ puṣṭim api sampādayeti sāsraṁ yācamānas tat-kṛpāvaloka-madhura-vāṅ-mayāmṛta-niḥsyanda-nanditas tac-caraṇa-paricaraṇa-nīta-pañca-ṣaḍ-vāsaraḥ sa-rasam aṭann api kadācid aṭavīṁ yadi mayi vartate kṛṣṇasya kṛpāvalokas tadāyaṁ dūrataḥ puro ’valokyamānaḥ kṛṣṇasāras tri-caturāṇi padāni mad-abhimukham āyātu na cen māṁ pṛṣṭhī-karotv iti naisargikīr api mṛga-paśu-pakṣi-ceṣṭās tad-anugraha-nigraha-liṅgatayaiva jānan grāmopaśalye ’pi khelato vipra-bālakān sanakādīn iva kim ahaṁ vrajendra-kumāraṁ prāpsyāmi iti spṛṣṭvā tad-dattam uttaraṁ meti mugdhākṣaraṁ durbodhārthatayā subhodārthatayā vā parāmṛṣya sva-gṛha-madhyam adhyāsyāpi mahā-dhana-gṛdhnuḥ kṛpaṇa-vaṇig iva kvāhaṁ yāmi, kiṁ karomi kena vyāpāreṇa me tad abhīṣṭa-vastu-jātaṁ hasta-gataṁ syād iti parimlāna-vadanaś cintayan svapan uttiṣṭhan upaviśan parijanaiḥ kāraṇaṁ pṛcchyamāno ’pi kadācin mūka iva kadācid avahitthām ālambamānaḥ sāmpratam abhūd ayaṁ channa-buddhir iti bandhubhiḥ svabhāvata evāyaṁ jaḍa iti vedāntibhiḥ bhraṣṭa iti karmibhir aho mahā-sāraṁ vastu samadhigatam iti abhaktair dāmbhika iti tatrāparādhibhiḥ parāmṛṣyamāṇo mānāpamāna-vicāra-vidhuro bhagavad-āsakti-svardhunī-pravāha-patita eva ceṣṭate bhakta iti. (2)*

**Перевод:**

**His body becomes covered with horripilation by hearing this explanation, and then he goes somewhere else where he says, “This very moment my life will become successful, because I see this assembly which can all at once counteract everything bad I have ever done.” Thus, after falling to the ground like a stick, he shows signs of respect and humility. Greeted with honor in a transcendentally affectionate way by the crest jewel of the assembly, a greatly learned pure devotee of the Lord, he sits down nearby that leader of the assembly, his body shrinking in humility, and says, “Dear sir, O most eminent of physicians, who can cure the grave disease of material existence present in all the living beings of the three worlds, please feel my pulse and determine the disease of me, this lowly and wretched person. Please prescribe my diet and medicine. Please use some potent tonic to effect the strong, healthy condition I desire.” Thus pleading with tears in his eyes, he is then pleased by the nectar flowing from that person’s merciful glance and sweet words. He spends five or six days serving his feet and then moves on, spiritually enlivened, and eventually reaches a forest. “If I have actually received Kṛṣṇa’s merciful glance, then this black dear looking at me from a distance should make three or four steps toward me. If I haven’t received it, he should turn his back to me.” Thus understanding even the instinctive behavior of the deer, other animals, and birds as signs of the Kṛṣṇa’s favor and displeasure, he comes to the outskirts of a village where some playing brahmaṇa boys seem to him to be Sanaka Kumāra and his brothers. He asks them, “Am I going to obtain the young son of the king of Vraja?” They reply to him with a childish broken sylable, “mā,” which he alternatively considers impossible to understand or else obvious. Then, sitting once more in his house, he thinks, like a miserly merchant greedy for more and more wealth, “Where can I go, and what can I do? By what means can all these things I want come into my hands?” He ponders thus with a languid face, sometimes going to sleep, sometimes standing up, and sometimes sitting down. When his family members ask him the cause of this behavior, he sometimes keeps quite like a dumb man and sometimes says something evasive. His relatives think “Now his intelligence has become covered over.” The scholars of Vedānta think “He is just by nature a fool.” The ritualists think “He is fallen.” The nondevotees think “Oh, he must have obtained some very rare thing.” And the offenders think “He is am impostor.” But whatever they think, he has no concern at all for honor or dishonor. This is how a devotee acts who has fallen into the Gaṅgā stream of attachment to the Supreme Lord.**

*iti mahā-mahopadhyāya-śrīmad-viśvanātha-cakravarti-viracitāyāṁ mādhurya-kādambinyāṁ mano-hāriṇī nāma ṣaṣṭhy amṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the Sixth Rain of Nectar, named “Stealing the mind,” of** ***Mādhurya-kādambinī,*** **composed by Mahā-mahopadhyāya Śrīmān Viśvanātha Cakravartī.**

## Seventh Rain of Nectar - saptamy amṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ

### Verse 1

*atha saivāsaktiḥ parama-pariṇāmaṁ prāptavatī raty-apara-paryāyo bhāva ity ākhyāṁ labhate. ya eva hi sac-cid-ānanda iti śakti-trikasya svarūpa-bhūtasya kandalī-bhāvaṁ bhajate. yam eva khalu bhakti-kalpa-vallyā utphullaṁ prasūnam ācakṣate. yasya ca bāhyaiva prabhā sarvaiḥ su-durlabhā ābhyantarī tu mokṣam api laghū-karoti. yasya ca paramāṇur eka eva tamaḥ samastam unmūlayati. yasya parimalaiḥ prasṛmaraiḥ madhusūdanaṁ nimantryānīya tatra prakaṭī-kartuṁ prabhūyate. kiṁ bahunā yair eva vāsitāś citta-vṛtti-tila-vitatayo dravī-bhāvam āsādya sadya eva bhagavad-aṅgam akhilam snehayituṁ yogyatāṁ dadhate. yaḥ khalv āvirbhavann eva svādhāraṁ śva-pacam api brahmāder api namasyatvam āpādayati.*

**Перевод:**

**Next, when that same attachment reaches its ultimate evolution, it recieves the name “ecstasy,” or, alternatively, “delight.” This is in effect the nascent sprout of the three energies of the Lord’s inner self—eternity, knowledge, and bliss. It is also described as the blooming flower of the desire creeper of devotion. Its external effulgence is very difficult to everyone to achieve, and its internal effulgence minimizes even liberation. Even one atom of it dispells all darkness. The fragrances it emanates have the power to invite Lord Madhusūdana to come and show Himself. What more need be said? By its influence, the various activities of the mind, like a heap of seasame seeds, become liquified and at once fit to anoint the Supreme Lord’s whole body. Its makes the person in whom it appears, even a dog eater, worshipable even by Brahmā and the other demigods.**

### Verse 2

*udyotamāne cāsmin śyāmalimānaṁ vraja-mahendra-nandanasyāṅgānām eva āruṇyaṁ tadīyādhara-netrāntāder eva dhavalimānaṁ tadīya-vadana-smita-candrikāder eva pītimānaṁ tad-ambara-bhūṣaṇāder eva leḍhuṁ labdhvāsanna-samayam iva balitotkaṇṭhaṁ bhaktasya nayana-dvandvam aśrubhir ajasram ātmānam abhiṣiñcet. gītaṁ tadīyaṁ muralyā eva śiñjitaṁ tadīya-nūpurāder eva saundaryaṁ tadīya-kaṇṭhasyaiva nideśaṁ tac-caraṇa-paricaraṇasyaiva tat-kṛtaṁ kam api svasyāvataṁsī-kartuṁ mṛgyad iva sthāne sthāne kṣaṇe kṣaṇe śravaṇa-dvayaṁ niścalī-bhavad unnamet.*

**Перевод:**

**As this ecstasy develops into its fullness, it makes the two eyes of the devotee extremely eager, as if they now had the opportunity to lick the dark-blue of the great king of Vraja’s son’s limbs, the dawn-red of His lips, the corners of His eyes, and so on, the dazzling white of the moonbeams coming from the smile on His face, and so on, and the yellow of His dress, ornaments, and so on. The devotee’s eyes then drench his own body incessantly with tears. In various places and at various times the devotee’s two ears stand still and perk themselves up, trying to make into their ornaments the song of His flute, the jingling of His anklebells, the beautiful sound coming from His throat, and the instructions received to perform services at His feet.**

### Verse 3

*evam eva kīdṛśo vā tad-ubhaya-kara-kiśalaya-sparśa iti tadaiva tam anubhavad iva gātraṁ romāñcitaṁ bhavet. tat-saurabhyaṁ labhyamānam iva viduṣyau nāse praphulle kṣaṇe kṣaṇe śvāsaṁ gṛhītvā paricicīṣetām. hanta sā phenā kiṁ me svādanīyā iti tadaiva tām upalabhamāneva rasanāpy ullāsaṁ dadhānaivoṣṭhādharau lihyāt. kadāpi tadīya-sphūrtau taṁ sākṣāt prāptavad iva ceto hṛṣyet tan-mādhuryāsvāda-sampattyā mādyet tadaiva tat-tirobhāve viṣīdet glāyed ity evaṁ sañcāri-bhāvair ātmānam alaṅkurvad iva śobheta. buddhir apatantam evārtham avadhārayantī jāgrat-svapna-suṣuptiṣu tadīya-smṛti-vartmany eva pānthatvam adhyavasyet. ahantā ca prāpsyamāne sevopayogini siddha-dehe praviśantīva sādhaka-śarīraṁ prāyo jahātīva virājeta. mamatā ca tac-caraṇāravinda-makaranda eva madhukarī-bhavitum upakrameteti. sa ca bhaktaḥ prāptaṁ mahā-ratnaṁ kṛpaṇa iva janebhyo bhāvaṁ gopayann api kṣānti-vairāgyādīnām āspadī-bhavan lasal-lalāṭam evāntar-dhanaṁ kathayatīti nyāyena tad-vijña-sādhu-goṣṭhyāṁ vidito bhaved anyatra tu vikṣipta ity unmatta iti sajjata iti durlakṣyatāṁ gacchet.*

**Перевод:**

**He thinks like this: “I wonder what the touch of both of His creeper-like hands must be like,” and his body, as if experiencing this directly, becomes covered with horripilation. “My wise nostrils seem to be perceiving His fragrance,” he thinks as his nostrils expand to try to gather that fragrance in each breath moment after moment. “Oh, will I get a chance to taste that foam?” he thinks as he tongue seems to obtain that taste and with eagerness licks his lips. “When will my mind see Him in person?” he thinks as his mind thrills. In these ways the devotee becomes maddened by obtaining the treasure of being allowed to relish His sweetness, but then the Lord disappears and he becomes sad and discouraged. Thus the devotee is beautified, decorating himself with these transitory ecstasies. His intelligence is focused on an object that will not fall away from his view, in all the three states of waking, sleep, and deep sleep persisting as a traveller on the path of remembering the Lord. His ego, on the verge of attaining his perfect spiritual body, suitable for serving the Lord, seems to have already entered that body and to have practically given up his former body of a practitioner. His sense of possession is preparing to become a bee in the honey of the Lord’s lotus feet. The devotee hides his ecstasy from the public like a miser who has acquired a rare jewel; he assumes the moods of tolerance and renunciation, but by the logic that the glow on one’s forehead discloses one’s inner wealth he is recognized in the assembly of learned saints. Elsewhere, however, he is misidentified as distracted, insane, or obsessed.**

### Verse 4

*sa ca bhāvo rāga-bhakty-uttho vaidha-bhakty-uttha iti dvi-vidhaḥ. ādyo jāti-pramāṇābhyām ādhikyena mahima-jñānānādareṇa bhagavati sāmānyādhikyāc ca sāndraḥ. dvitīyaḥ tābhyāṁ prathamataḥ kiñcin-nyūnatvena aiśvarya-jñāna-viddha-mamatāvattvāc cāsāndraḥ. prāyo dvi-vidha evāyaṁ bhāvo dvi-vidhānāṁ bhaktānāṁ dvi-vidha-cid-vāsanā-sanātheṣu hṛdayeṣu sphuran dvi-vidhāsvādyatvaṁ bhajate. ghana-rasa iva rasāla-panasekṣu-drākṣādiṣu praviṣṭaḥ pṛthak pṛthaṅ mādhuryavattvaṁ bhajate. te ca bhaktāḥ śānta-dāsa-sakhi-pitṛ-preyasī-bhāvavantaḥ pañca-vidhāḥ syuḥ. tatra śānteṣu śāntir iti dāseṣu prītir iti sakhiṣu sakhyam iti pitṛ-bhāvavatsu vātsalyam iti preyasī-bhāvavatsu priyateti nāmatedam api.*

**Перевод:**

**This ecstasy, moreover, is of two kinds, arising from spontaneous and regulated devotional service. The first of these is greater in quality and quantity, and because it disregards thought of the Lord’s majesty it is more intense, since in it one considers oneself equal to or greater than the Lord. The second is somewhat inferior to the first both qualitatively and quantitatively, and so is less intense, since in it one’s sense of possessing the Lord is vitiated by awareness of His majesty. These two kinds of ecstasy for the most part assumes two different kinds of tastes, manifesting in the hearts of the two kinds of devotees endowed with two kinds of spiritual sentiment. It is like sugarcane juice assuming different kinds of sweetness when mixed with such different items as spiced curds, breadfruit, and grapes. These devotees are furthemore of five kinds, according to their having the ecstasy of a peaceful sage, servant, friend, parent, or lover. Their respective ecstasies are thus named as peace in the peaceful, affection in the servants, friendship in the friends, fond concern in those who have the mood of parents, and intimate love in those who are in the mood of girlfriends.**

### Verse 5

*punaś cāyaṁ sva-śaktair āvirbhāvitair vibhāvānubhāva-vyabhicāribhir ātmeva rājeva vā prakṛtibhir udbhūtaiśvaryaḥ sthāyīti nāmnā vaiśiṣṭyaṁ gacchan tair militaḥ śānta iti dāsyam iti sakhyam iti vātsalyam iti ujjvala iti labdha-vibhedo raso bhavati. yo hi raso vai saḥ, rasaṁ hy evāyaṁ labdhvānandī bhavati iti śrutyābhidhīyate. ayam anyatrāvatāre ’vatāriṇi vā sambhavann api svayaṁ sampūrti-mānaṁ tatra tatrālabhamāno vrajendra-nandana eva sva-kāṣṭhāṁ labhate nada-nadī-taḍāgādiṣu sambhavad api yathā samudra eva jala-nidhitvam. yo hi bhāvasya prathama-pariṇatāv eva utpadyamāna eva premaṇi mūrta eva rasaḥ sākṣād eva tadvatā bhaktenānubhūyata iti. (2)*

**Перевод:**

**Furthermore this ecstasy manifests and empowers vibhāva, anubhāva, and vyabhicāri-bhāvas, along with which it fully displays its opulence, like the soul or like a king with his ministers. At that time it becomes specially termed as sthāyī. Joined by these components it becomes rasa, differentiated as neutrality, servitude, friendship, parenthood, and conjugal love. It is this rasa which is described in the revealed scriptures: “He is rasa itself, and when the living being obtains this rasa he becomes ecstatic.” (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 3.31) This rasa is also present in other incarnations and other sources of incarnations, but in these other appearances of the Lord it fails to show all its glory. Only in the darling son of the king of Vraja does it reach its full limit, just as small and big rivers and lakes are also storehouses of water, but only the ocean is so in the fullest sense. Rasa begins to appear in the very first transformations of ecstasy, and in mature love the devotee who has it can experience it directly in its personal form.**

*iti mahā-mahopadhyāya-śrīmad-viśvanātha-cakravarti-viracitāyāṁ mādhurya-kādambinyāṁ paramānanda-niṣyandiṇī nāma saptamy amṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the Seventh of Nectar, named “Pouring down supreme bliss,” of** ***Mādhurya-kādambinī,*** **composed by Mahā-mahopadhyāya Śrīmān Viśvanātha Cakravartī.**

## Eighth Rain of Nectar - aṣṭamy amṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ

### Verse 1

*atha tasyā eva bhakti-kalpa-vallyāḥ sādhanābhikhye ye pūrvaṁ dve patrike lakṣite, idānīṁ tato ’ti-cikkaṇāni tādṛśa-śravaṇa-kīrtanādi-mayāni bhāva-kusuma-saṁlagnāni*

*anubhāvābhidhānāni bahūni patrāṇi sahasaivāvirbhūya kṣaṇe kṣaṇe dyotayanti. yāny eva bhāva-kusumaṁ pariṇāmaṁ prāpayya punas tadaiva premābhidhāna-phalatvam ānayanti. kiṁ ca, āścarya-caryeyaṁ bhakti-kalpa-vallī yasyāḥ patra-stavaka-puṣpa-phalāni prāpta-pariṇatīny api sva-svarūpam atyajanty eva nava-navāny eva sahaiva sarvāṇi vibhrājante. tataś cāsya bhakta-janasyātmātmīya-gṛha-vittādiṣu śata-sahasraśo bhavatyo yāś citta-vṛttayo mamatā-rajjubhis teṣu teṣu nibaddhā eva pūrvam āsan tā eva citta-vṛttīḥ sarvā eva tatas tato ’vahelayaivonmocya sva-śaktyā māyikīr api tā mahā-rasa-kūpa-spṛśyamāna-padārtha-mātrāṇīva sākāra-cid-ānanda-jyotirmayī-kṛtya tābhir eva mamatābhiḥ sarvābhis tatas tato vicitābhiḥ sva-śaktyaiva tathā-bhūtī-kṛtābhiḥ śrī-bhagavad-rūpa-nāma-guṇa-mādhuryeṣu yo nibadhnāti so ’yaṁ prema-mahā-kiraṇa-mālīva udayiṣyamāṇa eva nikhila-puruṣārtha-nakṣatra-maṇḍalīḥ sahasaiva vilāpayati.*

**Перевод:**

**Next, from the two small leaves of the desire tree of devotion, both called sādhana, which were previously described, there now suddenly appear many other leaves, very smooth in texture, comprised of the substance of hearing, chanting, and so on, attached to the flower of ecstasy, and known by names of anubhāvas. These leaves cause transformations in the flower by which it becomes the fruit called prema. This desire tree of devotion, moreover, acts so amazingly: even as its leaves, buds, flowers, and fruits undergo changes, they do not give up their original forms, but rather all these forms appear together with newer and newer brilliance. Previously there had been hundreds and thousands of activities of the devotee’s mind in relation to his body, relations, home, wealth, and so on which kept him bound up with the ropes of possessiveness. But now prema effortlessly frees him from all this bondage and by its potency turns every one of these materialistic identifications, like objects being dipped into a cistern of mercury, into transcendental objects, manifestations of pure, blissful consciousness. Prema then takes these transformed tendencies, organizes them, and with its potency renders them such that they form attachment to the varieties of sweetness found in the forms, names, and qualities of the divine Personality of Godhead. Thus rising like the mighty sun, prema all at once eclipses the entire zodiac of the constellations of goals of life.**

### Verse 2

*phala-bhūtasyāsya yaḥ svādyamāno rasaḥ sa sāndrānanda-viśeṣātmā, rasasya parama-pauṣṭikī śaktiḥ śrī-kṛṣṇākarṣiṇīty ucyate. yasmin āsvādayitum ārabhyamāṇa eva vighnān na gaṇayatīti kiṁ vaktavyam. mahā-śūro bhaṭa iva mahā-dhana-gṛdhnur aty-āveśa-lupta-vicāras taskara iva svātmānam api nāvekṣate. kiṁ ca rātrin-divam eva prati-kṣaṇam abhyavahriyamāṇaiś catur-vidhaiḥ parama-svādubhir aparimitair annair api durupaśamanīyā yadi kācit kṣudhā sambhavet, tat-sadṛśyā utkaṇṭhayā sūrya iva tāpayan tat-kāla eva sphūrtair āvirbhāvitāni bhagavad-guṇa-rūpa-mādhuryāṇy apārāṇy āsvāda-viṣayī-kārayan koṭi-candra iva śiśirāyati.*

**Перевод:**

**This rasa, appearing as a fruit, has a sweet-tasting juice which is “of the special nature of intense bliss,” and the supremely nourishing potency of this juice is said to “have the power to attract Śrī Kṛṣṇa.” What to speak, then, of the fact that one who has begun to taste this rasa pays no regard to disturbances. Like a valiant warrior intent on winning great wealth, or like a thief who has lost his discrimination because of his obsession with stealing, the devotee does not even notice his own body. If there might be a kind of insatiable hunger that could not be quenced even by all four kinds of extremely palatable foods taken in unlimited quantity at every moment day and night, it might be comparable to the eagerness with which this rasa burns like the sun, but at the same moment presents as objects for the devotee to relish the unlimited varities of the Lord’s qualities, forms, and sweetness, thus cooling the devotee like millions of moons.**

### Verse 3

*yugapad eva svādhāra adbhuto ’yaṁ premā uditya ca yasminn īṣad eva vardhamāne bhagavat-sākṣāt-kāram eva prati-kṣaṇam ākāṅkṣato bhaktasya utkaṇṭhā-śalyasya mahā-dāhakasyevāti-prābalyodayāt sphūrti-prāpta-tad-rūpa-līlā-mādhuryair api atṛptasya tasya bāndhavo ’pi nirudakāndha-kūpa eva bhavanam api kaṇṭaka-vanam eva yat kiñcanābhyavahāro ’pi prahāro mahān eva saj-jana-kṛta-praśaṁsā api sarpa-daṁśā eva prātyahika-kṛtya-kartavyam api martavyam eva aṅga-pratyaṅgāni api mahā-bhāra eva suhṛd-gaṇa-sāntvanam api viṣa-dṛṣṭa eva sadā jāgaro ’pi sāgaro ’nutāpasyaiva kadācit nidrāpi vidrāviṇī jīvanasyaiva sva-vigraho ’pi bhagavan-nigraho mūrta eva prāṇā api dhānāḥ punaḥ punar bhṛṣṭā eva. kiṁ bahunā, prāk sadaivābhīṣṭam āsīd yat tac ca raho mahopadrava eva bhagavac-cintanam evātma-nikṛntanam eva.*

**Перевод:**

**As soon as this amazing love has arisen in the devotee who is its recepticle and it has grown a little, the devotee becomes anxious to see the Lord directly at every moment. This eagerness pains the devotee like a wound. It burns him greatly, and as it becomes extremely powerful the devotee can no longer be satisfied even when the Lord’s forms, pastimes, and sweetness become visible to him. His family becomes for him like a dry, dark well, his home becomes like a forest full of thorns, whatever he eats feels like being hit by a powerful blow, praise given by respectable people becomes like the bites of snakes, the obligations of his daily rituals become like having to die, his body and its limbs become like a terrible burden, the consolation of his friends becomes like poison, the time he remains awake at night becomes like an ocean of lament, the sleep he sometimes experiences becomes a threat to his very existence, his own body becomes like the embodiment of the Lord’s disfavor, and his life airs become like grains that have been roasted over and over again. What more need be said? Whatever was desirable to him before he now experiences in the depths of his heart as a great disturbance. Even thinking about the Personality of Godhead tears his soul to pieces.**

### Verse 4

*tataś ca premaiva cumbakī-bhāvam āpadya kārṣṇāyasī-bhūtaṁ kṛṣṇam ākṛṣyānīya kasmiṁścana kṣaṇe bhaktasyāsya nayana-gocarī-karoti. tatra ca saundarya-saurabhya-sausvarya-saukumārya-saurasyaudārya-kāruṇyānīti svīyāḥ svarūpa-bhūtāḥ parama-kalyāṇa-guṇāḥ bhagavatā sva-bhaktasya tasya nayanādiṣv indriyeṣu nidhīyante. teṣāṁ ca parama-madhuratve nitya-navatve ca bhaktasyāsya ca tad-āsvādayituḥ premṇaiva pravartamāne pratikṣaṇa-vardhiṣṇau mahotkaṇṭhāyāṁ ca ko ’py ānanda-mahodadhir āvirbhavan nārhati kavi-sarasvatī-lakuṭyā parimeyatām. yathā hy ati-niviḍatara-viṭapa-dala-kula-pravalita-mahā-nyagrodha-talasya sura-dīrghikā-hima-salila-sambhṛta-ghaṭa-śata-valayita-taṭasyāti-śiśiratve tad-āśrayitur janasya ca tapartu-taraṇi-kiraṇa-tapta-maru-saraṇi-mahā-pānthatve ca, tathā kādambinī-ghanāsārasyāpāratva iva tad-abhiṣicyamānasya vana-mataṅgajasya cirantana-dava-davathu-dūnatvena ca, tathā sudhā-kiraṇasyāti-madhuratve svāda-lopupatve ca yas tādātmika ānandaḥ, sa eva dig-darśanārthaṁ tayopamānī-kriyate. (1)*

**Перевод:**

**Then prema becomes like a magnet, attracting Kṛṣṇa like a piece of iron, and at a certain moment it reveals Kṛṣṇa before the devotee’s eyes. In this process, the natural, supremely auspicious qualities of the Lord’s personality—His beauty, fragrance, sweet sound, tender touch, fine taste, generosity, and kindness—are deposited by the Lord in the eyes and other senses of His devotee. By the pure love of the devotee who is relishing these qualities, they remain supremely sweet and ever-fresh, and as the devotee’s great eagerness increases moment after moment, the vast ocean of bliss experienced cannot be measured by all the inspired devices of speech at the disposal of poets. As just a few examples: It might be compared to the situation of a person who has taken shelter at the base of a huge banyan tree, with masses of leaves covering its very dense network of branches and surrouded by hundreds of pots filled with the icy water of the celestial Gaṅgā. But at the same time as the person under this tree enjoys this very cool location, he is also standing as a traveller with a long way to go on the desert-like path of the summer sun. Or, a wild elephant is being drenched by a seemingly endless downpour from a bank of rainclouds, but at the same time he is being tormented by a forest fire that has been raging for a long time. Or, it is like when the moon with its nectarean rays appears extremely charming and one is greedy to taste its sweetness. The ecstasy the devotee experiences at this time can be just hinted at by these analogies.**

### Verse 5

*tatra prathamaṁ labdhāpāra-camatkārasya bhaktasya locanayoḥ sva-saundaryaṁ prakāśyate prabhuṇā. tatas tan-mādhuryeṇa sarvendriyāṇāṁ manasaś ca locana-mayī-bhāve pravartite stambha-kampa-bāṣpādibhiḥ kṛta-vighnaś ca tasyānanda-kṛta-mūrcchāyāṁ jātāyāṁ prabodhayitum iva dvitīyaṁ saurabhyaṁ tadīya-ghrāṇendriyeṣu prakāśyate. tenāpi teṣāṁ ghrāṇa-mayī-bhāve dvitīya-mūrcchārambhe, are mad-bhakta tavāham eva sampadyamāno ’smi mā vihvalī-bhūr nikāmaṁ mām anubhaveti tṛtīyaṁ saundaryaṁ śravaṇendriya-grāhyam āvirbhāvyate. punas tenāpi teṣāṁ śravaṇa-mayī-bhāve tṛtīya-mūrcchopakrame kṛpayā caraṇāravindena pāṇibhyām urasā ca sva-sparśaṁ dattvā caturthaṁ sva-saukumāryam asāv anubhāvyate. tatra dāsya-bhāvavatas tasya mūrdhni caraṇena sparśaḥ sakhya-bhāvavataḥ pāṇyoḥ pāṇibhyāṁ vātsalya-bhāvavataḥ sva-kara-talenāśru-mārjanaṁ preyasī-bhāvavatas tu urasi sva-vakṣasā bāhubhyām āśleṣaḥ kriyate iti bhedo bodhyaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**In this experience, the Lord first reveals His beauty before the eyes of His devotee who has attained the mood of unbounded wonder. When by the sweetness of this beauty all the devotee’s senses and his mind become like eyes, the devotee becomes disturbed by the symptoms of paralysis, trembling, tears, and so on, and his ecstasy makes him about to faint. At that time to reawaken him the Lord reveals a second charm, His sweet fragrance, to the smelling faculties of the devotee. This makes all the devotee’s senses become like noses, and he begins to faint a second time. As if saying, “My dear devotee, I am trying to give you your perfection. Please don’t become overwhelmed. Just experience Me at your pleasure,” the Lord then exhibits a third kind of beauty, perceivable by the sense of hearing. Then again all the devotee’s senses become like ears, and he starts to faint a third time, but the Lord bestows His touch with His lotus feet, His hands, and His chest, so that the devotee is made to experience a fourth charm, the Lord’s tender touch. In this regard one difference should be recognized, that the Lord touches a devotee who has the ecstaties of servitude with His feet on the devotee’s head, He touches with His hands the hands of a devotee with the ecstasies of friendship, He wipes away with the palm of His hand the tears of a devotee with the ecstasies of parenthood, and He embraces with His chest and arms the breast of a devotee with the ecstasies of a beloved girlfriend.**

### Verse 6

*punaś ca tenāpi tathā tathaiva caturtha-mahā-mūrcchārambhe pañcamaṁ svādhara-sambandhi saurabhyaṁ tadīya-rasanendriya-grāhyaṁ preyasī-bhāvavatyeva tat-kāla-prādurbhūta-tad-abhīṣṭākāra-rati-bhajana eva prakāśyate nānyatra. tataś ca pūrva-vad eva tathā tathā-bhāve ’pi tadātanyās tv ānanda-mūrcchāyās tv ati-naiviḍye jāte tataḥ prabodhayitum asamartheneva bhagavatā ṣaṣṭham audāryaṁ vitanyate. tac ca teṣām eva saundaryādīnāṁ sarveṣām eva tan-nayanādi-sarvendriyeṣv eva yugapad eva balād vitaraṇam. tadaiva bhagavad-iṅgita-jñeneva premṇāpy ati-vardhamānena satā tad-anurūpa-tṛṣṇātirekaṁ saṁvardhyāpi tatra bhakte svayaṁ candratvam upeyuṣā yugapad evānanda-samudra-śata-laharī-vyatisammarda-bhara-jarjaritatvam iva tasya antar nirmimāṇena svayam eva sākāra-tan-mano-’dhidaivatī-bhavateva tathā sva-śaktir vitīryate yathā yaugapadyenaiva te te svādā nirvivādā eva bhavanti.*

**Перевод:**

**And then again, when the fourth serious threat of fainting occurs, He reveals a fifth charm, the sweet taste of His lips, perceivable by a devotee’s sense of taste, but only of a devotee who possesses the ecstasies of a beloved, not of anyone else, and only in the devotee’s worship in the conjugal mood as it appears at the time when the devotee’s hankering is fulfilled. And when the same kind of changes occur as before, and in the same way the devotee then is dragged deep into a swoon of ecstasy, the Personality of Godhead, unable to awaken the devotee, expands a sixth charm, His magninimity. This forcibly distributes to all the senses at once, beginning with the devotee’s eyes, all His various charms, beginning with His beauty. And then prema, as if aware of what the Lord wants to do, increases itself to an extreme peak, inspires an appropriate exceess of thirst in the devotee, assumes the form of the moon itself within the devotee’s heart and creates therein the shattering force of the waves of hundreds of ecstasies clashed against one another. Prema then appears in person as the ruling god of the devotee’s mind, and its potency becomes spread so that all the different kinds of tastes can be relished at the same time without conflict.**

### Verse 7

*na caivaṁ manaso ’nekāgratvena tat-tad-āsvādasyāsāndrateti vācyam. praty uta saundarya-sausvaryādīn prati sarvendriyāṇām eva nayanī-bhāva-śravaṇī-bhāvādyā ekadaiva bobhūyamānā alaukikācintyādbhuta-camatkāram evātanvataḥ svādasyāti-sāndratvam eva kurvanti. naivāsti tatra laukikānubhava-tarka-dāva-davathor avakāśo ’pi, acintyāḥ khalu ye bhāvā na tāṁs tarkeṇa yojayed ity-ādeḥ. (2)*

**Перевод:**

**It is not right to suggest, moreover, that because the mind is thus distracted in various directions, these various tastings cannot therefore be very intense. Rather, all the senses become at one and the same time free to assume the functions of eyes, ears, and so on to experience the Lord’s beauty, sweet voice, and other charms; expanding an supermundane, inconceivable sense of great wonder, they make the relishment extremely intense. In this matter there is no scope for the blazing forest fires of mundane experience and logic, as we hear in such statements as “Things which are inconceivable cannot be fathomed by speculative logic.” (Mahābhārata, Bhīṣma-parva 6.11)**

### Verse 8

*tataś ca saundaryādīnāṁ yāvanti mādhuryāṇi teṣāṁ sāmastyenānububhūṣāv api asmin bhakta-cātaka-cañcu-puṭe jalada-bindv-āvalīva na mānti tāni vimṛśyāho tarhi mayaitāni saundaryādīny etāvanti kim arthaṁ dhṛtānīti teṣāṁ sambhojanāyaiva saptamaṁ sarva-śakti-kadamba-paramādhyakṣāyā āgamādāv api vimalotkarṣiṇy-ādīnām aṣṭa-dig-daleṣu vartamānānāṁ svarūpa-śaktīnāṁ madhya eva karṇikāyāṁ mahārāja-cakravartinyā iva sthitāyā hy anugrahābhidhānatvenoktāyā bhagavato nayanāravinda eva ātmānaṁ vyañjayantyāḥ kṛpā-śakter vilasitaṁ kvacit dāsādau vātsalyam iti kvacit kāruṇyam iti priyādau ceto-drava iti kvacid anu kati nāmnābhidhīyamānam udayate. yayaiva kṛpā-śaktyā sarva-vyāpiny api tadīyecchā-śaktiḥ sādhuṣu sādhv evaṁ rañjitā paramātmārāmān api mahā-camatkṛti-bhūmīr adhyārohayati. yayaiva bhagavato bhakta-vātsalyaṁ nāma eka eva guṇaḥ samrāḍ iva prathama-skandhe pṛthivyoktān svarūpa-bhūtān satya-śaucādīn kalyāṇa-guṇān śāsti.*

**Перевод:**

**And then, when the desire arises to experience in total all these varieties of beauty and so on, there cannot be found room for them, like a flood of raindrops from a cloud, in the beak of the** ***cātaka*** **bird who is the devotee. Wondering what to do about them, he thinks, “Why have I picked up so many of these qualities of beauty and so on?” But to make it possible for him to enjoy them, a seventh event occurs, the playful expansion of the Lord’s potency of mercy. She is the ultimate superintendent of the entire corps of His energies. In the** ***āgamas*** **and other scriptures the internal energies of the Lord, Vimalā and so on, are found present on a flower’s leaves in the eight directions, and she is in their midst, in the whorl of the flower, like their imperial ruler, known by the name Anugrahā. She reveals herself subtly within the Lord’s lotus eyes. Her playful expansion manifests sometimes, in the moods of servitude and so on, as concern or as compassion, and sometimes, in the moods of friendship and so on, as a melting of the heart, and correspondingly it assumes any number of designations. By this potency of mercy, the Lord’s all-pervading desire potency appears with especial vividness in His saintly devotees, raising even those who are satisfied within themselves to levels of extreme astonishment. And by it also Lord’s one particular quality called concern for His devotees becomes like an independent king presiding over the auspicious qualities of truth, cleanliness, and so on, which belong to the Lord’s inner nature and are described by the goddess Earth in the First Canto of the Bhāgavatam.**

### Verse 9

*mohas tandrā bhramo rukṣa- rasatā kāma ulbaṇaḥ lolatā mada-mātsarye hiṁsā kheda-pariśramau asatyaṁ krodha ākāṅkṣā āśaṅkā viśva-vibhramaḥ viṣamatvaṁ parāpekṣā doṣā aṣṭādaśoditāḥ aṣṭādaśa-mahā-doṣai rahitā bhagavat-tanuḥ*

*bhagavati sarvathā niṣiddhā apy ete doṣā yad-anurodhena rāma-kṛṣṇādy-avatāreṣu kvacit kvacid vidyamānā eva santo bhaktair anubhūyamānā mahā-guṇāyante. tataś ca sarvāṇy eva tad-vitīrṇāni saundaryādīny āsvādayituṁ labdhaujasi bhakte āsvādyāsvādya ca tāṁ camatkṛti-parama-kāṣṭhām adhiruhyādhiruhya cāśruta-caraṁ bhagavato bhakta-vātsalyam idam iveti manasā muhur muhur evānubhūya dravī-bhāvam āseduṣi. tasminn are tad-bhakta-varya bahūni janmāni mad-arthaṁ dārāgāra-dhanādikaṁ parityajya mat-paricaryānurodhena śīta-vāta-kṣudhā-tṛṣṇā-vyathā-mayādīn bahūn eva kleśān soḍhavate janāvamānādīn apy avagaṇitavate bhikṣu-caryāṁ gṛhītavate bhavate kim api dātum aśaknuvan ṛṇī kevalam abhūvam.*

**Перевод:**

**“Bewilderment, sloth, misunderstanding, dry taste, ferocious lust, unsteadiness, pride, envy, violence, lamentation, fatigue, untruth, anger, hankering, apprehension, delusion of the general populace, partiality, and dependence on others—these are said to be the eighteen kinds of faults.” (*****Viṣṇu-yāmala*** **cited in** ***Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu,*** **2.1.247–8)**

**“The eighteen great faults are lacking in the body of the Personality of Godhead.” (*****Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*** **2.1.246)**

**By the specially sanction of this mercy potency even these faults, which are altogether disallowed in the Personality of Godhead, from time to time appear in the descents of Rāma, Kṛṣṇa, and so on, and in the way they are experienced by the Lord’s devotees they become great virtues. And then, when the devotee obtains the power to relish all the qualities of beauty and so on expanded by this mercy potency, he goes on relishing them continuously and rises higher and higher up to the final extreme of astonishment. He experiences again and again in his mind the sense that “This concern of the Lord for His devotees is unprecedented,” and his heart melts. The Lord then tells Him, “O best of My devotees, in many lifetimes you have abandoned wife, home, wealth, and so on for My sake, you have tolerated the many pains of cold, wind, hunger, thirst, physical agony, and so on, you have discounted the scorn and other abuse of people, and you have resorted to begging. Since I have nothing to give you in return, I have become simply your debtor.**

### Verse 10

*sārvabhaumatva-pārameṣṭhya-yoga-siddhy-ādikaṁ ca na bhavad-anurūpam iti tat tat kathaṁ vitariṣyāmi. na hi na hi paśubhyo rocamānaṁ ghāṣa-tuṣa-buṣādikaṁ kasmaicin manuṣyāya dīyate. tad aham ajito ’pi bhavatādhunā jita eva varte. narte bhavat-sauśīlya-vallīṁ samyag avalambanam iti bhagavato vāṅ-mādhurīṁ parama-snigdha-varṇāṁ karṇāvataṁsī-kṛtya prabho bhagavan kṛpā-pārāvāra ghora-saṁsāra-pravāha-prāpita-kleśa-cakra-nakra-vyūha-caryamāṇaṁ māṁ vilokya kāruṇyodyota-drava-ceto-navanīto ’khila-lokātīto bhagavan śrī-guru-rūpa-dhārī madanādy-avidyā-vidārī sva-darśanena sudarśanenaiva tan nirbhidya tad-daṁṣṭrā-taṭād evonmocya nija-caraṇa-kamala-yugala-dāsī-cikīrṣayā sva-mantra-varṇa-vīthīṁ mat-karṇa-vīthīṁ praveśya nirvyathī-kṛtya muhur muhur api sva-guṇa-nāma-śravaṇa-kīrtana-smaraṇādibhir māṁ yad aśūśudhan nija-bhaktair api saṅgamitaiḥ sva-sevām apy abūbudhat tad api durmedho ’ham adhama-tamo divasam ekam api prabhuṁ na paryacaraṁ kadarya-caryas tad ayaṁ jano daṇḍayitum evārhaḥ praty utaitāvad darśana-mādhurīṁ pāyitaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**“How can I grant you things that don’t suit you, like universal sovereignty, the position of Lord Brahmā, and the mystic perfections of** ***yoga*****? No, it is not proper to give a human being the grass, hay, and so on which please cattle. So even though I am unconquerable, today I have been conquered by you. You shouldn’t expect to find support anywhere else but from the creeper of your own good character.” Accepting these sweet words of the Lord, in which every syllable is spoken with supreme affection, as the ornament of his ears, the devotee says, “My Lord, O Personality of Godhead, O reservoir of infinite mercy, please glance upon Me, who have fallen into the fearsome flood of birth and death and am being devoured by the swarms of crocodiles who are the countless miseries of material life. O Personality of Godhead, Your compassion has melted my heart into butter. You transcend all the worlds. By assuming the form of my divine spiritual master, you have shattered my illusions, including lust. And by showing Yourself to Me You have destroyed these forms of illusion as if with Your Sudarśana disc and You have delivered me from the edge of their sharp teeth. Wanting to make me a servant at Your two lotus feet, You have caused the syllables arranged in a stanza that make up Your** ***mantra*** **to enter the path of my ears, thus relieving me of all anxiety. Even though You have repeatedly purified Me through and through with the hearing, chanting, remembrance, and so on of Your qualities and names, and even though you have brought me into the association of Your devotees and made me understand thoroughly Your service, still I am so unintelligent! I am the worst of men. I have not done proper service of You, my Lord and master, for even one day. Therefore this wicked person deserves to be punished, but nonetheless You have caused him to drink the sweetness of seeing You.**

### Verse 11

*kiṁ ca, ṛṇī-bhavāmīti śrī-mukha-vāṇyā prabhu-vareṇa viḍambito ’smīti manye ’haṁ tat kiṁ karomi pañca vā saptāṣṭātha vā lakṣa-koṭayo ’pi yady aparādhā bhayeyus tad api tān samprati kṣamayituṁ dhārṣṭyam ālambeta mām. parārdhato ’py adhikāṁs tān avadhārayāmi kiṁ ca te te ’ti-prabalāś cirantanā bhukta-bhoktavya-phalā vartantāṁ nāma. samprati pūrve-dyur eva nīra-dena nīla-nīrajena nīla-maṇinā śrīmad-aṅgasya candramasā śrī-mukhasya nava-pallavena śrī-caraṇasya dyutim upamimānena mayā dagdha-sarṣapārdhena kanaka-śikhariṇam iva caṇaka-kaṇena cintā-maṇim iva pheruṇā keśariṇam iva maśakena garuttvantam iva samī-kurvatā durbuddhinā spaṣṭam aparāddham evety adhunaivāvagatam. tadā tu prabhum ahaṁ staumīti svīyam avidvattvam api kavitvam etad iti janeṣv api prakhyāpitam. ataḥ paraṁ tu mad-īkṣaṇena kṣaṇena samīkṣita-śrī-mūrti-rūpeṇa vaibhavena javena tarjyamānā dhairya-rahitā gaur iva me gauḥ śrīmat-saundarya-kalpa-vallīm upamāna-radanair dūṣayituṁ na prabhaviṣyatīty evaṁ bahu-vidhaṁ śaṁsati tasminn ati-prasannena bhagavatā punar api preyasy-ādi-bhāvatas tasya yathā-sambhavam abhīpsitaṁ tādātmika-tat-sva-vilāsa-vilakṣitaṁ śrī-vṛndāvanaṁ kalpa-śākhinaṁ mahā-yoga-pīṭhaṁ sva-preyasī-vṛnda-mukhyāṁ śrī-vṛṣabhānu-nandinīṁ tat-sakhīḥ śrī-lalitādyās tat-kiṅkarīr api sva-vayasyān śrī-subalādīn sva-pālyamānā naicikīś ca śrī-yamunāṁ śrī-govardhanaṁ bhāṇḍīraṁ ca nandīśvara-giriṁ tatratya-janaka-jananī-bhrātṛ-bandhu-dāsādīn sarvān eva vrajaukaso rasotkarṣeṇa darśayitvā tat-tad-ānanda-mahā-moha-taraṅginyāṁ taṁ nimagnī-kṛtya svayaṁ parikareṇāntardhīyate.*

**Перевод:**

**“And what is more, I presume I have been deceived by my exalted Lord with the statement from His divine mouth, ‘I have become a debtor.’ What, then, can I do? Even my offences might number five, seven, eight, or hundreds of thousands and tens of millions, still the audacity takes hold of me to now ask for them all to be forgiven. I actually consider them more numerous than the largest possible number, and moreover very serious offences which have persisted for a very, very long time. Let them, therefore, remain until their consequences have been suffered. As for now, just yesterday I was comparing the effulgence of His divine body to that of a raincloud, a blue lotus, and a sapphire, that of His divine face to that of the moon, and that of His divine feet to that of new buds, in effect thus equating a golden mountain with a burnt half of a mustard seed, a** ***cintāmaṇi*** **stone with a speck of dried chickpea, a lion with a jackal, Garuḍa with a mosquito. So today I have to conclude that I was most unintelligent and have obviously commited offence. At that time I thought I was actually praising the Supreme Lord and so I was advertising to the general public this ignorance of mine as great scholarship. But from now on my faculty of speech, scolded sharply by the splendor of His divine form, which my eyes have glanced at for just a moment, will no longer be able to act like a silly cow, defiling with its teeth of analogies the desire-granting vine of His opulent beauty.” As the devotee thus goes on speaking in various ways, the Personality of Godhead becomes very satisfied with him, and then reveals to the devotee in all the glory of transcendental enjoyment, as appropriate to the devotee’s particular ecstasy of a girlfriend or other type of loving servant, whatever the devotee most desires, the special features of the Lord’s pastimes in the devotee’s own mood—Śrī Vṛndāvana; its desire trees; the supreme center of pastimes; the chief of all the Lord’s beloveds, the darling daughter of Śrī Vṛṣābhānu; her companions, headed by Śrī Lalitā; their maidservants; His boyfriends, headed by Śrī Subala; the servants He maintains; Śrī Yamunā; Śrī Govardhana, the Bhāṇḍīra forest; Nandīśvara Hill; His father, mother, brother, friends, and servants who reside there; and all the residents of Vraja. Thus submerging the devotee in the turbulent river of total bewilderment which is the bliss found in each of these, the Lord along with His entourage then disappears.**

### Verse 12

*tataś ca kiyadbhiḥ kṣaṇair labdha-prabodhaḥ punar api prabhuṁ didṛkṣur locana-mudrām unmocya, taṁ nāvalokayann ātmānam aśrubhir abhiṣiñcan, kim ayaṁ svapna ālokitaḥ. na hi na hi śayyālasya-nayana-kāluṣyādy-abhāvāt. kim iyaṁ kasyacin māyā. na hi na hi, etādṛśānandasya māyikatvāsambhavāt. kiṁ vā, cittasyaiva bhrama-mayī kāpi vṛttiḥ. na hi na hi, laya-vikṣepādy-ananubhavāt. kiṁ vā, manoratha-paripāka-prāpto ’yaṁ vastu-viśeṣaḥ. na hi na hi, īdṛśa-padārthasya sīmno ’pi kadāpi manorathenādhiroḍhum aśakyatvāt. sphūrti-labdho ’yaṁ bhagavat-sākṣāt-kāro vā. na hi na hi, samprati smaryamāṇābhyaḥ pūrva-pūrvodbhutābhyaḥ sphūrtibhyo ’syāti-vailakṣaṇyāt. ity evaṁ vividham eva saṁśayānaḥ, śayāna eva dhūli-dhoraṇi-dhūsarāyāṁ dharaṇau, yathā tathāstu punar api tad-darśanaṁ me bhūyād iti muhur āśāsāno ’pi tad-anupalabhamānaḥ khidyan luṭhan rudan gātrāṇi vraṇayan mūrcchayan prabudhyamāna uttiṣṭhann upaviśan abhidravan krośann unmatta iva kṣaṇaṁ tūṣṇīm āsīno manīṣīva kṣaṇaṁ lupta-nitya-kriyo bhraṣṭācāra iva kṣaṇam asambaddhaṁ pralapan graha-grasta iva kṣaṇaṁ kasmaicid āśvāsakāya nibhṛtaṁ pṛcchate bhakta-janāya sva-bandhave svānubhūtam arthaṁ bruvāṇaḥ, kṣaṇaṁ prakṛti-stha iva sakhe bhūri-bhāga bhagavat-sākṣāt-kāra evāyaṁ tavābhavad iti tena yuktyā pratoṣyamāṇo hṛṣyann eva, hanta tarhi katham eva punar na bhavatīti tadaiva viṣīdan, hanta kasyacin mahānubhāva-cūḍāmaṇer mahā-bhāgavatasya kāpi kṛpā-vitāna-pariṇatir vā durbhāgasyāpi me bhagavat-paricaryāyā ghūṇākṣara-nyāyena vā kasmiṁścid divase kathañcid utpannāyā niṣkaitavatāyāḥ phalam idaṁ vā. kiṁ vā, vaiguṇya-samudre ’pi kṣudre mayi bhagavad-anukampāyā nirupādhitvam eva mūrtaṁ prakaṭī-babhūva, hanta hanta kena vā anirvacanīya-bhāgyena svayaṁ hasta-prāpto nidhir ajani, kena vā mahāparādhena tataś cyutam iti niścetuṁ niścetano ’haṁ na prabhavāmi tad-bādhā-bādhita-dhīḥ. kva yāmi kiṁ vā karomi kam upāyam atra kam u ha vā pṛcchāmi. mahā-śūnyam iva nirātmakam iva niḥśaraṇam iva dāva-pluṣṭam iva māṁ nigilad iva tri-bhuvanam avaloke.*

**Перевод:**

**After a few moments the devotee then comes back to his senses. Eager to again see the Lord, he opens his eyes, but cannot see Him, and drenches himself with tears. “Is what I have just seen a dream? No, no, because I’m not feeling the drowsiness of someone who is getting up from sleep, nor are there the other symptoms of dirty eyes and so on. Was this instead an illusion created by someone? No, no, because this kind of ecstasy cannot be illusory. Or else was this a confused act of my own mind? No, no, I’m not perceiving any dizziness, distraction, or other such symptoms. Or was this some real object obtained as the consequence of some fanciful ambition? No, no, it is not possible for a mere fancy to ascend to even the outer edge of such a thing. Then maybe this was a direct vision before my eyes of the Personality of Godhead? No, no, this was very much different from the visions I had at various times in the past, as I remember them now.” The devotee thus expresses a variety of doubts, and he lies down on the bare ground, even though it is covered with heaps of dust. He hopes again and again, “However it may be possible, may I again have the sight of Him!” But not able to perceive Him, he laments, rolls on the ground, cries, lets his limbs become cut, faints, again gains consciousness, stands up, sits down, runs about, shouts for a moment like a madman, sits silently for a moment like a wise man, forgets his regular business like someone who has fallen from his standards of behavior, chatters incoherently for a moment like a person haunted by a ghost, asks questions for a moment from a nearby giver of consolation, speaks about what he is experiencing to a devotee who is his friend, is joyful for a moment as if restored to a normal condition after being pleased by hearing the reasoning of this friend that “My friend, O most fortunate one, what has happened to you is a direct revelation of the Personality of Godhead,” and just then laments, “But oh, why is it that this vision does not happen again?” He further laments, “Oh, is this some consequence of mercy from the crest-jewel among great souls, a pure devotee of the Supreme Lord? Or else, even though I am so unfortunate, is this the fruit of my service to the Supreme Lord, which has somehow become on some certain day free from deceit, by the random of logic events like the letters accidently cut in wood by worms? Or is this the unconditionality of the Supreme Lord’s compasion appearing in person before me, an insignificant creature who resides in the ocean of bad qualities? Oh, oh, by what indescribable good fortune has this treasure come of its own into my hands, and by what grievous offence has it fallen from those hands? I have no good sense to be able to discern this, since all these disturbances are blocking my intelligence. Where will I go, and what will I do? What is the solution for this? Who can I even inquire from? The three worlds appear to me as a total void, lifeless, without any shelter, destroyed by conflagration, and about to swallow me whole.”**

### Verse 13

*lokebhyo niḥsṛtya tad ebhyaḥ kṣaṇaṁ vivikte praṇidadhāmīti. tathā kurvan hā prabho sundara-mukhāravinda-mādhurīka-sudhā-dhārā-dhurīṇa bhāvita-vāsita-nikhila-vipina-śrī-vigraha-vara-parimala vana-māla-caṭulitāli-jāla punar api kṣaṇam api tatra-bhavantaṁ dṛśyāsam. sakṛd eva ca svādita eva, svādita-tan-mādhurīko na punar evam abhyarthayiṣye iti vilapan luṭhan śvasan mūrcchann unmādyan prati-diśam eva taṁ paśyan hṛṣyan śliṣyan hasann aṭan gāyan punar apy anīkṣamāṇo ’nutapan rudan alaukika-ceṣṭita evāyūṁṣi nayan sva-deho ’py asti nāsti vā nānusandadhate.*

**Перевод:**

**“Let me go away from all these people and collect my thoughts for a moment in a secluded place.” He does this, and then wails, “O master, Your beautiful lotus mouth is overflowing with a flood of nectarean sap. The excellent fragrance of Your divine form perfumes and enlivens the entire forest. A network of bees trembles around Your flower garland. Please allow Me to see Your good self again for just a moment. Even though I have only once tasted the honey from Your mouth, I have tasted it, and I dare not ask to taste it again.” The devotee thus laments, rolls on the ground, sighs, faints, becomes wild, sees Him in every direction, rejoices, holds on to Him, laughs, walks about, sings, and then once more not seeing Him is remorseful and cries. Passing the course of his life engaged in such unwordly behavior, he cannot even notice whether his own body is present or not.**

### Verse 14

*tataś ca samaye pañcatāṁ gacchataṁ sva-dehaṁ na jānan mayābhyarthitaḥ sa eva karuṇā-varuṇālayas tathaiva pratyakṣī-bhūya sākṣāt sevāyāṁ māṁ niyuñjānaḥ sva-bhavanaṁ nayatīti jānan kṛta-kṛtyo bhakto bhavatīti.*

*ādau śraddhā tataḥ sādhu- saṅgo ’tha bhajana-kriyā tato ’nartha-nivṛttiḥ syāt tato niṣṭhā rucis tataḥ athāsaktis tato bhāvas tataḥ premābhyudañcati ity arthaḥ sādhu vivṛtaḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**And then the time comes when his body is approaching its end, but the devotee is not even aware of it, and is rather aware that “Entreated by me, He, the ocean of mercy, is becoming visible to me in the same way as before to engage me directly in His service, and He is taking me to His abode.” In this way the devotee perfects his life. And so we have properly explained the meaning of the words “In the beginning there is faith, then association with saintly persons, then the activities of worship, then cessation of unwanted things, then firm conviction, then attraction, then attachment, then ecstasy, and then pure love arises.” (*****Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*** **1.4.15–16)**

### Verse 15

*ato ’pi yathottara-svādu-vaiśiṣṭya-bhājita-sneha-māna-praṇaya-rāgānurāga-mahā-bhāvākhyāni bhakti-kalpa-vallyā ūrdhvordhva-pallava-gāmīni phalāni santi. na teṣām āsvāda-sampad-auṣṇa-śaitya-sammarda-sahaḥ sādhakasya deho bhaved iti na teṣāṁ tatra prākaṭya-sambhava iti na tāny atra vivṛtāni. kiṁ ceha rucy-āsakti-bhāva-premasu lakṣayitvā sākṣād-anubhava-gocaratāṁ prāpiteṣu tatra santy api bhūrīṇi pramāṇāni nopanyastāni, pramāṇāpekṣayā hy anubhava-vartma-pāruṣyāpādakatvāt. kiṁ ca tāny apekṣāṇi cet, tasmiṁs tadā labdha-rucer mahā-mate iti rucau, guṇeṣu saktaṁ bandhāya rataṁ vā puṁsi muktaye iti āsaktau, priya-śravasy aṅga mamābhavad ratiḥ iti ratau, premāti-bhara-nirbhinna-pulakāṅgo ’ti-nirvṛta iti premaṇi, tā ye pibanty avitṛṣo nṛpa gāḍha-karṇais tān na spṛśanty aśana-tṛḍ-bhaya-śoka-mohā iti rucy-anubhāve, gāyan vilajjo vicared asaṅga iti āsakty-anubhāve,*

*yathā bhrāmyaty ayo brahman*  
*svayam ākarṣa-sannidhau*  
*tathā me bhrāmyate cetaś*  
*cakra-pāṇer yadṛcchayā*

*iti rucy-anubhāve, evaṁ-vrata ity atra hasaty atho roditi rauti gāyati iti premṇo ’nubhāve, āhūta iva me śīghraṁ darśanaṁ yāti cetasi iti tatra sphūrtau, paśyanti te me rucirāṇy amba santa iti sākṣād-darśane, tair darśanīyāvayavair udāra-vilāsa-hāsekṣita-vāma-sūktaiḥ iti labdha-darśanasya svabhāve, vāso yathā parikṛtaṁ madirā-madāndha iti ceṣṭāyāṁ pramāṇāny anusandhāya vicārayitavyāni. atredaṁ tattvam, ahaṅkārasya dve vṛttī ahantā mamatā ca iti. tayor jñānena layo mokṣaḥ deha-gehādi-viṣayatve bandhaḥ. ahaṁ prabhor janaḥ sevako ’smi sevyo me prabhur bhagavān sa-parikara eva rūpa-guṇa-mādhurī-mahodadhir iti pārṣada-rūpa-vigraha-bhagavad-vigrahādi-viṣayatve premā sa hi bandha-mokṣābhyāṁ vilakṣaṇa eva puruṣārtha-cūḍāmaṇir ity ucyate.*

**Перевод:**

**Beyond even this are the fruits of the desire creeper of devotion which appear on its upper and more upper twigs, assuming one after another increasingly special sweetness, and known as** ***sneha, māna, praṇaya, rāga, anurāga,*** **and** ***mahā-bhāva.*** **Since the body of a devotee who is still just practicing is not able to tolerate the hot-and-cold pressure of their rich taste, these cannot actually appear in his body; therefore we do not elaborate on them here.**

**Furthermore, we have given definitions of attraction, attachment, ecstasy, and pure love, and the direct experience of them has been depicted. But even though there are many evidences that could be cited in this regard we have not presented them, because turning our attention to evidence wonder render harsh the accounts of personal experience. If there is interest in such evidences, however, the following authoritative statements can be considered and analyzed: About taste, “O great sage, at that time for me who had attained attraction to Him” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **1.5.27); about attachment, “The mind attached to the material modes tends to bondage, but attached to the Supreme Person tends to liberation” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **3.5.15); about ecstatic affection, “I then developed ecstatic affection, dear Vyāsa, for Him who is so pleasing to hear about” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **1.5.26); about pure love, “The extreme burden of love made my body break out in horripilations, and I was thoroughly delighted” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **1.6.18); about the** ***anubhāva*** **symptoms of taste, “O king, those who drink through their deep ears without losing their thirst these topics are never touched by hunger, thirst, fear, sorrow, or confusion” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **4.29.40); about the** ***anubhāvas*** **of attachment, “Singing without shame, he moves about free from material attachment” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **10.2.39); about the** ***anubhāvas*** **of ecstatic affection, “O** ***brāhmaṇa,*** **in the same way as iron moves about on its own in the proximity of a magnet, so my mind moves about by the whims of the Lord who holds a disc in His hand” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **7.5.14); about the** ***anubhāvas*** **of pure love, “He laughs and then cries, shouts, and sings” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.2.40); about the Lord’s revealing Himself, “As if beckoned by a call, He quickly shows Himself in my mind” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **1.6.33); about direct vision of the Lord, “Dear mother, the saintly devotees see My charming forms”; about the character of one who has gotten this vision, “By those most attractive-to-see limbs of the Lord and by His magnanimous pastimes, smiling glances, and enchanting words” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **3.25.36); and about the behavior of this devotee, “As a person blinded by the intoxication of wine does not notice that his clothing is in disarray” (*****Bhāgavatam*** **11.13.36). The correct way to understand all this is as follows: Ego has two functions, the sense of “I” and the sense of “mine.” When both are dissolved by knowledge there is liberation, and when the two are directed toward body, home, and so on, there is bondage. But when they are directed toward the personal forms and other features of the Supreme Lord and with His associates, with the idea “I belong to the Lord as His servant, and He, the Personality of Godhead, the vast ocean of beauty, excellence, and sweetness, is the master I must serve, along with His associates,” then there is pure love. This pure love is distinct from both bondage and liberation. It is called the supreme aim of life.**

### Verse 16

*tatra kramaḥ. ahantā-mamatayor vyavahārikyām eva vṛttāv ati-sāndrāyāṁ satyāṁ saṁsāra eva. ahaṁ vaiṣṇavo bhūyāsaṁ prabhur me bhagavān sevyo bhavatv iti yādṛcchikyāṁ śraddhā-kaṇikāyāṁ satyāṁ tad-vṛtteḥ pāramārthikatva-gandhe bhaktāv adhikāraḥ. tataḥ sādhu-saṅge sati pāramārthikatva-gandhasya sāndratvaṁ vyavahāre ātyantikī. tato bhajana-kriyāyām aniṣṭhitāyāṁ satyāṁ tayoḥ paramārthe vastuny ekadeśa-vyāpinī vṛttiḥ vyavahāre pūrṇaiva. tasyāṁ niṣṭhitāyāṁ paramārthe bahula-deśa-vyāpinī vyavahāre prāyiky eva. rucāv utpannāyāṁ paramārthe prāyiky eva vṛttir vyavahāre tu bahu-deśa-vyāpinī. āsaktau jātāyāṁ paramārthe pūrṇā vyavahāre tu gandha-mātrī. bhāve tu paramārtha evātyantikī vṛttir vyavahāre tu bādhitānuvṛtti-nyāyenābhāsa-mayī. premaṇi tayor ahantā-mamatayor vṛttiḥ paramārthe paramātyantikī vyavahāre tu naikāpīti.*

**Перевод:**

**There is a sequence followed in this: When one’s sense “I” and “mine” are totally fixed in expressing themselves in a mundane way, one is simple in material existence. When by chance there develops a slight particle of faith—“Let the Supreme Lord become my master, the object of my service”—then the expression of these two senses acquire a slight aroma of spirituality, and one becomes a candidate for devotional service. Then, when there is association with devotees this aroma of spirituality becomes intense, but one remains completely dedicated to material activity. Then, when one is engaged in the practice of worship but without firm conviction, the two senses extend in some part to the absolute entity and are still completely involved in material activity. When this practice gains firm conviction, they extend to a great extent to the supreme truth and are still almost completely are involved in material activity. When taste arises, they extend almost completely to the supreme truth, and only to a great extent remain in material activity. When attachment develops, the connection with the supreme truth is complete and with material activity only a trace. When there is ecstasy, the connection with the supreme truth is absolute, and to material activity is only like a shadow, following the pattern of the persistence of an illusion even after it is disproven. When there is pure love, the two senses of “I” and “mine” act to the most extreme absolute extent in connection with the supreme truth, and not at all in connection with material affairs.**

### Verse 17

*evaṁ ca bhajana-kriyāyāṁ bhagavad-dhyānaṁ vārtāntara-gandhi kṣaṇikam eva. niṣṭhāyāṁ tad-dhyāne vārtāntarābhāsaḥ. rucau vārtāntara-rahitam eva tad-dhyānaṁ bahula-kāla-vyāpī. āsaktau tad-dhyānam ati-sāndram. bhāve dhyāna-mātram eva bhagavataḥ sphūrtiḥ. premaṇi sphūrter vailakṣaṇyaṁ tad-darśanaṁ ceti.*

**Перевод:**

**So it is that while engaged in the practice of worship one’s meditation on the Personality of Godhead is only momentary and is tainted by other interests. When firm conviction has developed there remains in one’s meditation on Him only a shadow of other interests. When there is taste, that meditation on Him is devoid of other interests and continues for long periods of time. When there is attachment, that meditation on Him is very intense. When there is ecstasy there is nothing but this meditation, and the Lord reveals Himself. When there is pure love, this revelation becomes very special and one sees Him face to face.**

### Verse 18

*mādhurya-vāridheḥ kṛṣṇa-*  
*caitanyād uddhṛtaiḥ rasaiḥ*  
*iyaṁ dhinotu mādhurya-*  
*mayī kādambinī jagat*

**Перевод:**

**May this cloud bank of full of sweetness, with its** ***rasas*** **extracted from the ocean of sweetness, Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, give satisfaction to the whole world.**

*iti mahā-mahopadhyāya-śrīmad-viśvanātha-cakravarti-viracitāyāṁ mādhurya-kādambinyāṁ pūrṇa-manoratho nāma aṣṭamy amṛta-vṛṣṭiḥ.*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the Eight of Nectar, named “Fulfilled in all desires,” of** ***Mādhurya-kādambinī,*** **composed by Mahā-mahopadhyāya Śrīmān Viśvanātha Cakravartī.**
