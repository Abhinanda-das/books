# Nava-vraja-mahima. Vol. 9. Map book

## Introduction

This map book is the companion to Nava-vraja-mahima and a guide to Radha-Syamasundara’s transcendental playground. Those who want to circumambulate either New Vraja-dhama or one of its pastime places will find this book invaluable.

Devotees show respect to the dhama by circumambulating all or part of it, but the essence of parikrama is to hear and recite Krsna’s glorious pastimes at those holy places where the pastimes actually take place. There are over nine hundred pastime places mapped in this book, each accompanied by a synopsis of the corresponding pastime. While visiting a pastime place, pilgrims can use these brief descriptions to stimulate more detailed remembrances, as those pastimes have been described in the preceding eight volumes. In this way one enters into a full absorption in Radha-Syamasundara, Their abode, pastimes, names, and qualities, and thus tastes transcendental bliss.

A map of Bhauma Vrndavana on page 16 alongside a map of New Vraja-dhama shows all major pastime places, allowing readers to compare locations and parikrama routes among the two dhamas. Since Nava-vraja-mahima is primarily a glorification of New Vraja-dhama, this map book charts only the circumambulation of Radha-Syamasundara’s pastime places. And while New Vraja-dhama and Bhauma Vrndavana are transcendentally one, the differences in geography, the order of forests, and the location and configuration of holy places make this book only generally applicable to a parikrama of Krsna’s original pastime place on earth. Moreover, in Bhauma Vrndavana names may be in one or more of several languages—Sanskrit, Hindi, Prakrit, or Bengali — while in New Vraja-dhama, for the sake of consistency all the names are in Sanskrit, save a few exceptions.

**About Performing Parikrama**

There are many rituals one can practise at the start, for the duration, and at the end of parikrama; the essence of all rituals, however, is to remember the divine couple with devotion.

When undertaking the full circumambulation of New Vraja-dhama pilgrims should begin at Visrama-ghata, offering heartfelt prayers to Radha-Syamasundara and the other deities of New Vraja-dhama and requesting protection for one’s journey from Sri Hanumanji. Of the many vows like celibacy, silence, and fasting, the most important is finishing one’s parikrama. And vows should be taken in the presence of one’s guru, a senior Vaisnava, or the dhama-panda. With strong faith in the presence of the transcendental abode, devotees should bow down at every pastime place, bathe physically or mentally in sacred waters, offer respects to the deities presiding over lila-sthanas, and constantly chant Hare Krsna when not hearing of Krsna’s pastimes. Showing great respect to all the residents of the dhama — from brahmanas to insects—one should avoid all frivolous or offensive talk and any activity that would offend the dhama. This same conduct should be followed if one circumambulates even a part of the dhama or visits a single holy place. Thus one will get the full benefit of one’s worship.

Devotees whose intention is to perform the complete parikrama of New Vraja-dhama will find the route with all possible pastime places in this book. They can either physically visit the places or meditate on them from major sites. Another purpose of this book is to enable devotees who cannot physically circumambulate New Vraja-dhama to do so in their minds. In addition to the full circumambulation, devotees may also follow the perimeter parikrama that encircles the entire dhama, or circumambulate major places like Mathura, Nandagrama, Varsana, Vrndavana village, and Gokula. Whether these parikramas take days or just an hour, are done alone or in a group, during summer or winter, they each reveal a unique fragrance of Radha-Syama’s sports, qualities, and forms, that arc all part of the divine bouquet of Their transcendental abode.

**How to Use This Book**

Preceding the parikrama maps of the two dhamas is a map of New Vraja-dhama showing major landmarks, buildings, and pavilions. In addition to these principal maps there are twenty-four component maps in this book, which altogether encompass the lotus of New Vraja-dhama. The title page of each map highlights the part of the lotus being detailed. The maps themselves show the New Vraja-dhama parikrama path and other parikrama paths that are parts of the dhama, and identify the locations of major holy places and landmarks—kundas, temples, rasa-sthalis, pavilions, forests, presiding deities, sacred banyan trees, wells, and existing buildings. Readers should note that the New Vraja-dhama and Vrndavana parikrama paths depicted in the maps are for the purpose of circumambulating the entire dhama and Vrndavana town respectively. They do not trace every pastime place in the two dhamas. Devotees wanting to visit every lila-sthana in the list of pastime places can do so by leaving the parikrama path at the appropriate places indicated in the list and then returning to the parikrama path and continuing their circumambulation.

Following each component map is the New Vraja-dhama parikrama path listing the pastimes places, along with the vertical and horizontal coordinates so they can be located in the map. These places are then described in brief in the pages entitled “Pastime Places.” In some descriptions readers will find places’ names in bold. This is to denote nearby sites that have not been separately numbered. At the back of the book, on page 215, is a glossary explaining the meaning of place names, followed by a table with the GPS co-ordinates of specific sites to make pastime places all the easier to locate for both readers and visitors. The index on page 225 shows the locations of the pastime places not only in the map book but also in the other eight volumes of the text.

And so I hope that pilgrims, with the help of this map book, can confidently navigate their way through the dhama, visiting Radha-Syama’s pastime places at their leisure.

New Vraja-dhama parikrama ki jaya! Radha- Syamasundara ki jaya!

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Mathura** While eternally manifest in Vaikuntha, on earth this city was established during the reign of the demon Madhu. Well known as the birthplace of Krsna and the site where, after leaving Vrndavana, He performed many heroic deeds, culminating in killing King Kamsa.

2\. **Visrama-ghata (Adi-varaha-tirtha / Visranti-tirtha)** This is where Lord Varaha recited the glories of Mathura, and is the starting point of both the New Vraja-dhama and the Mathura parikramas. Here Krsna washed His hands after killing Kamsa, and rested after killing Dantavakra.

3\. **Yamaraja and Yamuna temple** King Vajra established this temple at Visrama-ghata. On the festival of Bhratr-dvitiya people bathe here to avoid repeated birth.

4\. **Ghata** Here is where Krsna felt relief after killing Kamsa.

5\. **Kamsa-khara** This gully was created when Krsna dragged Kamsa’s body through Mathura from the wrestling arena to Visrama-ghata.

6\. **Main road of Mathura** Krsna was greeted by brahmanas as He approached the royal road of Mathura, where He beheaded the washerman and blessed the weaver. The residents of Mathura felt bliss when they saw Krsna walking through the city.

7\. **Sudama’s home** Krsna went in search of His friend Sudama in Mathura to bless his house and to request a special garland from him.

8\. **Yoga-ghata** Here stands the large rock upon which Kamsa tried to dash the eighth child of Devaki, Yogamaya.

9\. **Small market** The place where the hunchback Kubja offered Krsna the ointments she was carrying to Kamsa. Krsna straightened her in return and promised to visit her later.

10\. **Main market** The place where Krsna met the merchants, their wives, and some local children who showed Him the way to the sacrificial arena.

11\. **Ranga-bhumi (Ranga-ksetra)** Kamsa’s wrestling arena, where Krsna first broke the sacrificial bow and later killed Kamsa. The memorials of these two most important heroic deeds are Dhanus-tirtha and Kamsa-tila. Kamsa’s throne, Rangesvara Mahadeva, and the site of Kuvalayapida’s death are also here.

12\. **Rangesvara Mahadeva** Kamsa installed this deity to gain victory over Krsna and Balarama, but the presence of the Lords brought more pleasure to Lord Siva than Kamsa’s worship.

13\. **Kuvalayapida** This giant elephant was killed when he blocked the way of Krsna and Balarama at the entrance of Ranga-bhumi.

14\. **Dhanus-tirtha** A central stage at Ranga- bhumi where Siva’s celestial bow was kept. After snapping the weapon into two pieces, Krsna and Balarama killed thousands of soldiers with Their half-bow weapons.

15\. **Kamsa-tila** The place in Ranga-bhumi where Kamsa was slain.

16\. **Kamsa-kupa** Kamsa dug this well to drown Krsna in, if somehow or other the Lord survived the wrestling match in Ranga-bhumi.

17\. **Sapta-samudrika-kupa** A well containing water from the seven oceans. Beside the well stands the stone against which Kamsa dashed Devaki’s six sons.

18\. **Kubja-tirtha** The home Visvakarma built for Kubja on Krsna’s order after she served Him by offering ointments. Krsna visited Kubja’s home one year after their first meeting.

19\. **Kubja-kupa** A short distance past Kubja’s house is a well from which she drew water.

20\. **Ugrasena’s palace** Among the exquisite residences of Mathura is the palace of Maharaja Ugrasena, which was built by Lord Brahma.

21\. **Akrura’s home** Krsna met with His uncle a year after Kamsa's death and requested him to visit the Pandavas in Hastinapura and return with a report on their welfare.

22\. **Uddhava’s home** Krsna and Balarama visited Uddhava after conducting the funeral rites of Kamsa. When the three youths met, they lost awareness of time and place while talking of Vrndavana.

23\. **Vasudeva’s palace** This is where Krsna and Balarama met the members of the Yadu dynasty and re-established Ugrasena on the throne. This is also where They cried in loneliness after the departure of the Vraja-vasis.

24\. **Kankali-tila** When Kamsa tried to kill the eighth child of Devaki, the baby manifested herself as this deity of Durga-devi. Kamsa personally worshipped the eight-armed form of Kali found on this hill.

25\. **Devi-khara** The forested area near Baladeva- kunda where long ago donkeys grazed around a temple of Durga.

26\. **Baladeva-kunda** A lake on the outskirts of Mathura where Nanda Maharaja and the cowherd men set up camp. Krsna also remained here as long as His Vraja-vasi kinsmen stayed in Mathura.

27\. **Bhutesvara Mahadeva** One of Mathura’s protectors. Kamsa foolishly tried to please Lord Siva by offering him animals in ritual slaughter. Kamsa also worshipped Patala-devi in a nearby subterranean temple in order to be victorious over Krsna.

28\. **Varaha temple (Kapila-Varaha temple)** The ancient Deity who manifested Himself from the mind of the sage Kapila is worshipped here as the presiding Deity of Mathura. The Deity was installed here by Satrughna after His victory over Lavana.

29\. **Janmasthana** A cluster of pastime places associated with Krsna’s birthplace, such as Jnana-babara, Potara-kunda, Narada-baithaka, Malla- pura, the temple of Kesavaji, and Krsna-kupa.

30\. **Jnana-babara** A small well at Janmasthana where Yudhisthira Maharaja and Krsna would meet to discuss different topics.

31\. **Potara-kunda (Pavitra-kunda)** A tank in which Devaki washed Krsna’s diapers. Devaki and Vasudeva would bathe here while they were imprisoned.

32\. **Kamsa’s palace** Next to Potara-kunda stood this great building. In that palace was the prison where Lord Krsna made His transcendental appearance on earth. A solitary grove at Kamsa's palace called Narada-baithaka is where Narada Muni incited Kamsa to act in heinous ways to hasten Krsna’s appearance.

33\. **Mallapura** The actual room where Krsna took birth, situated behind Kamsa’s palace in the wrestlers’ residence. A Pratibhu of Kesavaji is worshipped here. The four-armed Deity of Krsna was installed by Vajra as Mathura’s presiding Deity. Caitanya Mahaprabhu danced in ecstasy when, on His visit to Mathura, He saw Kesavadeva.

34\. **Krsna-kupa** A well at Janmasthana from which Vasudeva and Devaki drew their drinking water during their imprisonment.

35\. **Vasudeva’s path** This is the path Vasudeva took with baby Krsna. From Janmasthana devotees on Mathura parikrama can follow either this path or the arc of the city.

36\. **Viththala’s house** On the pretext of escaping from Muslim soldiers, the Deity Gopala fled to this house where the Gosvamis of Vrndavana had His darsana for one month.

37\. **Mutra-sthana** Vasudeva carried baby Krsna from Kamsa’s prison to Gokula. When Krsna started to urinate on the way, His father placed Him on this large rock.

38\. **Balarama-tirtha** The gopas played here while staying in Mathura. Lord Nityananda also blessed this place with His presence while visiting the city.

39\. **Ambikavana** The deities of Mahavidya, Ambika, and Siddhesvari are found in this forest. Ambika was installed by Devaki for the protection of her unborn child, Siddhesvari by Krsna for success in killing Kamsa. The Vraja-vasis stayed here when they came to celebrate Siva-ratri.

40\. **Mahavidya-devi temple** Krsna and the gopas visited this temple in Ambikavana before the events leading to the death of Kamsa. Nearby they saw the deity of Ambika, who inspired them to instal Siddhesvari for success in their plan to kill Kamsa.

41\. **Sudarsana-kunda** The place where Krsna saved Nanda Maharaja from a large serpent and liberated Sudarsana, a Vidyadhara, who had been cursed to become a snake.

42\. **Sarasvati-kunda** Balarama accompanied the Vraja-vasis as far as Sarasvati-kunda when they left Mathura for Vrndavana. The Sarasvati River flows nearby.

43\. **Srila Prabhupada Memorial** This monument was erected in New Vraja-dhama for the centennial appearance day of Srila Prabhupada to honour the memory of His Divine Grace.

44\. **Koti-ghata** Downstream from Akrura-ghata, this is the first of the twenty-four Yamuna ghatas of Mathura. By bathing here one can attain great piety.

45\. **Vighnaraja-ghata (Ganesa-ghata)** At this bathing place Ganesa removes all obstacles to pure devotion and bestows freedom from sins and miseries.

46\. **Sarasvati-tirtha** The confluence of the Sarasvati and Yamuna rivers, where merely touching the water blesses one with the detachment of the renounced order.

47\. **Gokarna-ghata (Gokarna-tirtha)** This is where Gokarnesvara Mahadeva stays with Sati and Parvati. One of Vraja’s great places ofMathurapilgrimage, the Vraja-vasis also came here to worship Lord Siva on Siva-ratri.

48\. **Dasasvamedha-tirtha** Self-controlled persons come here to easily attain the pleasures of the heavenly planets, as one bath here yields the fruit of ten horse sacrifices.

49\. **Cakra-tirtha** When Durvasa Muni cursed Ambarisa Maharaja to die, Krsna’s Sudarsana disc protected the king. This is where Durvasa apologised and where the cakra resides. Ambarisa-tila is the hill nearby where King Ambarisa observed the Dvadasi vow for one year.

50\. **Soma-tirtha (Krsna-ganga-ghata)** One can gain access to the moon planet by bathing here. It is also said that one bath here is more auspicious than the world’s five most sacred baths, since Krsna made the Ganges appear here in the middle of the Yamuna. Vyasa’s island is here, where the great saint lived in penance for twelve years before composing Srimad-Bhagavatam in the Himalayas.

51\. **Brahma-tirtha** This ghata on Yamuna’s banks is named after the creator because he performed austerities here for one thousand years.

52\. **Ghantabharana-ghata** Bathing here leads one to the planet of the sun god. A bell is rung here to wake Lord Visnu, who rests at Visrama-ghata.

53\. **Naga-tirtha** The immortal snake Ananta protects the dhama from here. When Vasudeva carried baby Krsna to Gokula, Ananta appeared to shelter them.

54\. **Dharapatana-tirtha** A bath taken here assures one of heavenly happiness or, if one desires it, eternal life in Vaikuntha. This is where Pivari drowned before taking birth as a princess in ??si.

55\. **Krsna-baithaka** A banyan tree that was Krsna’s sitting place, and where Uddhava met Krsna after returning from Vrndavana.

56\. **Samyamana-tirtha (Svami-ghata)** A hunter once drowned here and, by the power of the tirtha, was reborn as the king of Saurastra. He returned here to worship the Lord before ascending to Goloka. Krsna also bathed here on the day of Kamsa’s death.

57\. **Asi-kunda (Nava-ghata)** When the father of the demon Vimati left his body on pilgrimage of Mathura, Vimati decided to destroy the city’s holy places. Lord Varaha rushed here and slew the demon.

58\. **Avimukta-tirtha** Mahadeva never leaves this place where he is absorbed in meditation on the pastimes of Krsna. He blesses devotees who bathe here to never leave the path of bhakti.

59\. **Guhya-tirtha** Bathing here reveals the secret of the Vedas: how to know Krsna.

60\. **Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s place of ecstasy** After entering Vraja, Lord Caitanya lay in the dust for three days, overwhelmed by ecstasy. A brahmana named Krsnadasa brought Him back to external consciousness and became His guide on parikrama.

61\. **Prayaga-tirtha** The king of all holy places, Prayaga performed austerities here to atone for his offences to Krsna’s eternal home. Worship of this tirtha bestows residence in either heaven or Goloka, depending on one’s desire.

62\. **Pippalesvara Mahadeva** The sage Pippa-layana was attacked by a demon at Prayaga- tirtha, but Lord Siva saved the sage from danger. Since then Siva has been worshipped here as Pippalesvara.

63\. **Veni-madhava-ghata** Vajra established the Deity of Veni-madhava, whose temple stands here adjacent to Prayaga-ghata.

64\. **Kachala-tirtha** This ghata corresponds to holy Ramesvaram and to the place of the same name in Haridvara, where Sari gave up her body. The Deity of Ramesvara is worshipped here.

65\. **Tinduka-tirtha** A barber named Tinduka regularly bathed here and eventually, upon leaving his body, was reborn as a brahmana.

66\. **Dandi-ghata (Bengali-ghata)** The ghata received its name when Caitanya Mahaprabhu — a dandi (sannyasi)— bathed here.

67\. **Surya-tirtha** Both Bali Maharaja and Samba attained the blessings of the sun god here: the former conquered the entire universe, the latter recovered from leprosy. Bathing at this ghata yields more benefit than performing a Rajasuya sacrifice.

68\. **Bali-tila** A raised mound where Bali Maharaja performed the Visvajit sacrifice in order to gain complete mastery over the universe. Devotees pray here to Vamanadeva and Bali Maharaja for pure devotional service.

69\. **Vatasvami-tirtha** This holy place is dedicated to the sun god, whose deity is worshipped here and dates back to Satya-yuga. One can attain good health, wealth, and also the supreme destination at this tirtha.

70\. **Dhruva-tirtha** Where little Dhruva stood on one leg, concentrating fully on the Lord. His footprints are visible here even today. One can gain unwavering determination in devotional service by bathing at this ghata, and deliverance for ancestors by performing the sraddha ceremony.

71\. **Narada-tila** The hillock from which Narada Muni watched Dhruva’s obstinate determination to acquire an abode that excelled all others.

72\. **Saptarsi-ghata (Rsi-tirtha)** The place where the seven sages performed austerities. The five-thousand-year-old deities of these rsis are still worshipped here.

73\. **Moksa-tirtha** This holy place can bestow liberation in the form of bhakti upon those who bathe in its waters.

74\. **Koti-tirtha** Millions of demigods seek to reside here and receive the blessings that accrue to one who offers charity or bathes in this place.

75\. **Bodhi-tirtha** This place at the Yamuna is said to be equal to Bodhgaya, the place where Lord Buddha attained perfection under a bodhi tree. The parikrama of Mathura ends at Bodhi-tirtha. Here one should remember the neighbouring pastime places.

76\. **Ravana-tila** In Treta-yuga Ravana came to Vraja and performed austerities at this hill to conquer the three worlds.

77\. **Agravana** This is where Caitanya Mahaprabhu first entered Vraja. He cried and trembled in ecstasy and called out to the trees and the forest animals, “Chant Krsna!” Devotees come here to perform nama-sankirtana.

78\. **Renuka-asrama** The birthplace of Parasurama, where the Lord performed a sacrifice to punish the wicked. It is named after the wife of Jamadagni, Renuka.

79\. **Yajna-sala** The sacrificial arena of Bharata Maharaja, who offered seven spotted deer in a yajna.

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Madhuvana** The forest where Krsna frolicked as a cowherd boy, so named because it teems with bees whose hives drip honey. It is situated on the banks of the Yamuna and encompasses both present-day and ancient Mathura, called Maholi. Krsna is famous as Madhusudana for having killed the demon Madhu here.

2\. **Dhruva-tila** The hill where five-year-old Dhruva met the Lord face-to-face. As a result of his devotion, the Lord fulfilled the child’s desire to obtain a kingdom that surpassed Lord Brahma’s.

3\. **Lavanasura’s cave** Once the entrance to a subterranean city beautified by palaces, lakes, and parks and illuminated by an artificial sun. In the field just outside the cave, Satrughna killed Lavanasura and brought peace to the region.

4\. **Maholi** The original site of Mathura. It was the capital of Madhuvana and the residence of the demons Madhu and Lavana.

5\. **Madhu-kunda (Krsna-kunda)** The lake sits in the area that was once the ancient city of Maholi. The gopas would come here to water the cows, drink honey, and play ball.

6\. **Balarama temple** This temple has a blackish Deity of Balarama. Once, when Lord Balarama left Dvaraka and visited Vrndavana for two months, He met the Vraja-vasis at Madhu-kunda. As He narrated Krsna’s adventures to them, His body turned cloud-blue.

7\. **Satrughna temple** This temple stands on the bank of Madhu-kunda, and the Deity of Satrughna, said to date back to Treta-yuga, commemorates the Lord’s killing of the demon Lavana.

8\. **Rasa-sthali** Here Krsna enjoyed a dance festival with Radha and the gopis in the month of Vaisakha. Krsna desired to showcase Radha’s preeminence as the queen of the rasa dance, and so He placed Her at the centre of the circle of gopis.

9\. **Talavana** The palm-tree forest situated next to Madhuvana. After Balarama killed Dhenukasura here, Talavana became a place where everyone could enjoy tala fruit, either by eating it or throwing it, as the gopas did.

10\. **Balarama-kunda (Vetra-ganga)** The gopis had requested Krsna to fetch some drinking water as they were exhausted from rasa-dancing. It is said that Ganges water bubbled up and formed a lake here when Krsna struck the ground with His dancing stick. A temple of Lord Balarama stands on the bank of the lake.

11\. **Balarama temple** A beautiful Deity of Lord Balarama is worshipped on the bank of Balarama-kunda, which is close to where He killed Dhenukasura.

12\. **Dhana-grama** A charming village at the extreme tip of Talavana that marks the border of Nanda Maharaja’s domain. Lord Balarama is worshipped here.

13\. **Kumudavana (Kondaliya)** The smallest of Vraja’s twelve forests derives its name from its abundant night-blossoming lotuses. The gopas like playing here. Once a dispute broke out over the rules and the games turned into a quarrel (kondala).

14\. **Kumuda-kunda** A delightful playground for Krsna and the gopas who engage in sports of valour or imitate the sounds and movements of the animals. Balarama and the gopas once held a dance performance here. It is also the site of the rasa dance in the month of Vaisakha.

15\. **Sansa** The place where Radha decorated Krsna with flower ornaments when He and the gopis gathered for rasa-dancing at charming Kumuda-kunda.

16\. **Kapila-baithaka** During Treta-yuga, Lord Kapila lived on the bank of Kumuda-kunda and worshipped Krsna’s Kumudavana pastimes with the gopas and gopis. A Deity of Lord Kapila is still served here in a temple.

17\. **Madhuri-kunda** In the area around this lake is the residence of Radha’s girlfriend Madhuri. A Vaisnava poet named Madhuri Dasa also resided here, and on the bank of the lake is a temple of Madhuri-mohana.

18\. **Basai** The village where each gopi thought, “If I bring Krsna under my control, then He will love me more than He does the others.”

19\. **Sai-pura** A contest of skill took place between the gopas and the gopis here. Krsna thought He was the winner, but everyone else knew that Srimati Radharani had been victorious.

20\. **Santanu-sthana (Satoha / Santanu-kunda)** Maharaja Santanu worshipped the deity of the sun god here to get an extraordinary son. Near his lake is the cottage where Radha was hiding when Kamsa made plans to abduct Her.

21\. **Kotika** A great circle of blossoming trees. While Krsna was walking through here on a visit to Mathura, a girl shyly smiled at Him, uncovering part of her shoulder.

22\. **Gandhesvara-sthana (Ganesra/ Gandharva-kunda)** The place where Krsna tested a variety of perfumes to find one that would be most attractive to Radha. Vrndavana’s perfume makers kept flower gardens around the lake here.

23\. **Tosa (Tosa-kunda / Tarasa-kunda)** This place is named after Tosa, Krsna’s confidential friend, an accomplished dancer who helped Him hone His dancing skills as other gopas watched.

24\. **Sri Prahlada Gurukula** The children’s school of New Vraja-dhama stands at Tosa, reminding the students of the place where Krsna would play with His friends.

25\. **Dati-upavana (Dantavakra-ha / Datiha)** In this village Krsna killed Dantavakra, the last significant demon. The Lord then crossed the Yamuna near Visrama-ghata to finally meet the Vraja-vasis who were waiting for Him in Gaura vai.

26\. **Mayuravana (Mayura)** Thousands of peacocks were attracted here by the beauty of the divine couple. At first they simply gazed at Radha and Krsna, but after some time they began to dance in concentric circles, inspiring the divine couple to join them.

27\. **Mayura-kunda** The lake in which the divine couple refreshed Themselves after dancing with the peacocks of ???ur?. On the bank of the lake is a temple dedicated to the Pandavas.

28\. **Aring (Adin / Arista-tila)** From the small hill found here the demon Aristasura emerged to kill Krsna. The gopis, gopas, and cows fled in fear of the bull, but Hari quickly killed him.

29\. **Keli-kunda** At this lake Krsna enjoyed water sports with His friends and with the gopis. There is also a tree from where Krsna called the gopis with His flute, and a platform on the bank of the kunda commemorating the rasa-lila of Krsna and the gopis.

30\. **Kamala-kunda (Kumuda-kunda)** A lake full of red lotuses next to Keli-kunda. Around the two lakes are many temples dedicated to Krsna, Balarama, Ganga-devi, and others. A siva-linga is also found here.

31\. **Daksina (Jakhina)** A village where Radha became completely submissive to Krsna. It is also said that a yaksini (witch) lived here and disturbed the area until Balarama killed her. Many lakes grace the area: Balarama-kunda, Revati-kunda, Jamadagni-kunda, and Renuka-kunda are amongst them.

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Nandagrama** Nanda Maharaja moved here when Krsna was seven years old. Nanda’s hilltop capital—with its wealth of palaces, houses, barns, and gates—was named after the king. At the summit of the hill stood his palace, Nanda-bhavana.

2\. **Nandisvara Hill** The hill upon which Nandagrama rests. Nandisvara Hill is a form of Lord Siva descended to earth in order to relish Krsna’s pastimes of youth.

3\. **Nanda-bhavana (Amodavardhana)** The palace of Nanda Maharaja where he lives with Yas'oda, Rohini, Krsna, Balarama, and other members of the family. Krsna leaves the palace for the pastures in the morning and returns in the evening, only to sneak out again at night for His nocturnal adventures with the gopis. The palace is sweetened by Yasoda’s motherly affection and Radha’s daily cooking. Nanda-bhavana temple stands here today. Krsna and Balarama are in the middle of the altar, and Yasoda and Radha are to the left of Krsna. Nanda, Sudama, and Madhumangala stand to the right of Balarama. The throne for Krsna and Balarama and the garden where Radha and Krsna meet, Radha-bag, are nearby.

4\. **Nandisvara Mahadeva** Once, when Lord Siva visited Vraja to see baby Krsna, he requested the remnants of the Lord, and so here Mahadeva receives the food offered to the Deity of Krsna in Nanda-bhavana every day. The deity was installed by Vajra as one of the five protectors of Vraja. Pilgrims usually start the parikrama of Nandagrama here after offering obeisances to Mahadeva.

5\. **Dhoyani-kunda** A small lake where, while singing of Krsna, Mother Yasoda’s servants regularly wash the pots used for making or storing yogurt.

6\. **Vidyudvari (Vidyutkari)** A place where, on two occasions, the Vraja-vasis felt separation from Krsna as the pain of being struck by thunderbolts: first when Akrura’s chariot stood here ready to take Krsna and Balarama to Mathura, and another time when Nanda arrived with his chariot from Mathura without Krsna.

7\. **Parasu (Paraso-kunda / Sati-sthana)** Here the Vraja-vasis cried for Krsna and chanted, “Govinda! Damodara! Madhava!” as He sat on Akrura’s chariot and repeatedly promised them He would return the day after tomorrow (parasu). It is also the place where the flames of the funeral pyre did not burn a gopi who was about to observe the sati rite after her husband’s death.

8\. **Sahasi-kunda (Sarasi-kunda)** A lake in a grove of banyan trees close to the palace of Nanda Maharaja. One day the gopis managed to bring the divine couple here. While Radha and Krsna were swinging, the gopis sang of Krsna’s heroic pastimes. On another occasion Yasoda named her playing sons “the swans (sarasa) of my life” at this lake.

9\. **Leovata-kunda** Mother Yasoda and her gopi assistants would make butter by churning yogurt, and they would feed the cowherd boys the leftover buttermilk. The gopas enjoyed drinking the buttermilk in the shade of a banyan tree near this small lake.

10\. **Mukta-tirtha** According to one pastime Krsna grew pearls on this field to protect His father from shame, as Nanda was not able to match the gift sent by King Vrsabhanu. Another pastime tells how Krsna wanted pearls for decorating His cows, but because the gopis refused to give Him any, He grew pearl creepers here.

11\. **Mukta-kunda (Moti-kunda)** Krsna would water His cows at this clear lake in the centre of Mukta-tirtha. The field around the kunda is where Krsna cultivated creepers that yielded pearls.

12\. **Phulvari-kunda** In a nearby flower garden Radha was picking flowers to string a garland for Krsna when He appeared on the scene, only to immediately disappear, causing Her to faint. Only the voice and touch of Krsna could make Her regain consciousness.

13\. **Pavana-sarovara** The foremost lake of Nandagrama built by Pavana-gopa. Krsna comes here in the morning and in the afternoon to water His cows. He would sometimes miss the afternoon milking to enjoy pastimes with the gopis, who came to the lake on the pretext of fetching water. Pavana-sarovara was also a social hub: the Vraja-vasis gathered here on summer evenings to wash themselves and share stories of Krsna.

14\. **Pavana-bihari** The presiding Deity of Pavana-sarovara.

15\. **Tadaga-tirtha (Sunyahara-kunda/Sunyahara-tirtha)** The kadamba forest where Krsna’s grandfather, Parjanya Maharaja, worshipped Lord Narayana to obtain the most worthy descendant of his line. It is also a favourite place of Lord Balarama, who plays here with His friends.

16\. **Sanatana Gosvami’s bhajana-kutira** Sanatana Gosvami was absorbed in his bhajana near Pavana-sarovara, fully exposed to the elements. First Krsna appeared before him in the guise of a local cowherd boy and gave him milk to drink, then the villagers came to construct a bhajana-kutira for the saint. Akincana Krsnadasa Babaji’s samadhi is nearby.

17\. **Radha’s tower** Maharaja Vrsabhanu constructed this tower-palace so that his daughter would have a place at the lake to change and rest. Radha sometimes came to the tower to spy on Krsna because from here She could spot Him wherever He was in Nandagrama.

18\. **Radha’s kitchen** Radha came here in the summer months to cook Krsna’s breakfast, which was served and eaten on the banks of Pavana- sarovara. The nearby watchtower also served as storage for Radha’s cooking provisions.

19\. **Rama-kunda** This was a convenient place to hold part of Krsna’s birthday festival because Radha’s kitchen was nearby. It is the same summer kitchen where She cooks when Krsna eats His breakfast at Pavana-sarovara.

20\. **Uddhava-khyari (Uddhava-kriya-sthana / Yogya-sthana)** One of the places where Uddhava conversed with the Vraja-vasis during his ten-month visit in Vrndavana. The most prominent pastimes of Uddhava-khyari include Uddhava’sNandagramadelivering Krsna’s message to the gopis and Radha’s mad talk with a bumblebee.

21\. **Visakha-kunda** Radha and Krsna would meet in Visakha’s grove at the kunda, bathe in the lake, and enjoy rasa-dancing at a place now marked by a rasa-vedi. The gopis chose the same kadamba grove to listen to Uddhava’s narrations when he visited Vrndavana.

22\. **Gosala-sthana** The barns of Nanda Maharaja are situated here, where the cows are fed and milked and where Krsna and Balarama daily come to take the cows out to the pasture. Nanda-baithaka, Vatsa-tirtha, and Ranga-sthala are also found in this area.

23\. **Barns of Nanda Maharaja** Krsna milks the cows morning and evening in these barns, whose opulence rivals the palaces in the heavenly kingdoms.

24\. **Nanda-baithaka** Here stands a throne upon which Nanda Maharaja sits and counts the full churns of milk placed before him. When Krsna and Balarama arrive, Nanda has Them sit either side of him on the throne and teaches Them arithmetic by counting the churns.

25\. **Vatsa-tirtha** There are stakes at this part of Gosala-sthana to which Krsna once tied unruly calves. Simply remembering or bowing before these stakes helps tic down the restless mind.

26\. **Ranga-sthala** This is the wrestling arena of the boys in Gosala-sthana. Some gopas take the part of contestants, others surround the arena as spectators, choosing sides and cheering on their respective heroes.

27\. **Kadambavana (Kadamba-kanana)** Near Gosala-sthana is Kadambavana, a forest of kadamba trees. Krsna and the gopas wander here to seek out forest animals and birds. When Balarama feels fatigued, He lies down and allows Krsna to massage His legs, to joke with Him, or bring Him water.

28\. **Akrura-tirtha (Akrura-kunda)** The place where Akrura dismounted his chariot after entering Nandagrama, embraced Krsna’s footprints in the dust, and got his first glimpse of his nephews Krsna and Balarama.

29\. **Khadiravana** This forest takes its name both from the acacia trees (khadira) that grow here in abundance and the pastime in which Krsna chased (khaira) the demon Bakasura before killing him. The presiding Deities of the rasa dance constantly enjoy loving pastimes here.

30\. **Campakavana (Campa-hati)** In one part of Khadiravana, always cooled by pleasant breezes, is this garden of campaka trees where Campakalata- sakhi eternally collects flowers and strings garlands for Radha and Krsna.

31\. **Madana-gopala-tirtha** When the gopis arrived at this place where Krsna stood, the gopas made a tumultuous sound like that of an approaching monster. Radha was so startled that She threw Herself at Krsna, appealing to Him for protection.

32\. **Sangama-kunja** After Radha threw Herself at Krsna, startled by the tumultuous noise the cowherd boys made, They enjoyed many amorous pastimes here, concluding with the rasa dance.

33\. **Sangama-kunda** A favourite lake of gopas and gopis alike. The hungry cowherd boys often came here to collect dates and to play in the lake. Bhugarbha Gosvami’s and Lokanatha Gosvami’s bhajana-kutiras are here.

34\. **Khayara** Khayare means, “It ate Him!” The cowherd boys cried out these words when the demon Baka swallowed Krsna. The place was home to a large lake formed by a tributary of the Yamuna.

35\. **Baka-sthala** Bakasura swallowed Krsna at Khayara, but Krsna forced the demon to cough Him up, grabbed the two halves of the demon’s beak, and tore him in two at this place.

36\. **Neochaka-sthana** A favourite picnic spot of the gopas. This is where they sat and ate, and praised Krsna’s gallantry after He had torn Bakasura in two.

37\. **Gupta-kunda** A hidden lake upon whose shores Radha and Krsna secretly meet in the early morning. It is one of Vraja’s three special meeting places (yoga-pithas), the other two being Radha-kunda and Govinda-sthali.

38\. **Vrnda-kunda (Vrnda-kunja)** Vrnda-devi, Paurnamasi, and Nandimukhi meet here to plan Krsna’s daily itinerary. The tranquil grove at the lake is the residence of both Vrnda-devi and the parrot Daksa.

39\. **Yasoda-kunda** Mother Yas'oda comes to bathe here early in the morning. She sometimes brings Krsna and Balarama along and bathes Them while They play in the water. The cave at Yasoda-kunda has been a place of bhajana for many Vaisnavas.

40\. **Hau-bilau-kunda** Mother Yasoda concocted a story to make Krsna and Balarama give up Their games and come home. The Hau monsters she pretended to see became real a few days later when Krsna decided to trick His mother.

41\. **Nanda-kunda** The King of Vraja bathes here and then chants the sacred Gayatri mantra every morning. If Krsna and Balarama are awake he brings Them to his lake to bathe.

42\. **Nanda-baithaka** In a shady grove beside Nanda-kunda is the place where Nanda and his assembly of elders sit and discuss matters of interest and concern regarding Vraja and Krsna.

43\. **Nrsimha temple** Nanda Maharaja installed a Deity of Lord Nrsimha for the protection of Krsna and Balarama and his subjects. He also worshipped Deities of Lord Varaha and Lord Narayana in the same temple.

44\. **Dadhi-manthana** A large pot sunken in the earth that Mother Yasoda would use to churn yogurt.

45\. **Lalita-kunda** This is where clever Lalita arranged a meeting of the divine couple on Krsna’s birthday, and where she and Krsna were swinging at the request of Narada Muni. A temple of Radha, Krsna, and Lalita stands at the lake.

46\. **Surya-kunda** The place where the sun god almost fell to earth, overwhelmed by the beautiful sight of Krsna herding cows with His jolly friends.

47\. **Domilavana (Domanavana)** A forest where the two (dva) lovers, Radha and Krsna meet (milana), and Their minds (mana) merge in pastimes of transcendental love.

48\. **Runaki-kunda and Jhunaki-kunda** Two hidden lakes in Domilavana bearing the names of two gopis who arranged for Radha and Krsna to meet here.

49\. **Paurnamasi-kunda** The cottages of two brahmanis, Paurnamasi-devi and her granddaughter Nandimukhi, are situated at this lake.

50\. **Caves of Paurnamasi and Nandimukhi** Two caves in Nandisvara Hill are also said to be residences of the two brahmanis.

51\. **Gendokhara** A level field for playing ball. Once, from within a nearby bush, Radha prayed to the gopas that She too might someday play with Krsna as freely as they could.

52\. **Madhusudanavana (Madhusudana-kunda)** This garden is famous for its humming bees. Krsna sits here alone in the shade of a bakula tree overlooking Madhusudana-kunda, and listens to the drone of bees.

53\. **Carana-pahari** Once, crouching on a flat rock, Krsna admired the beauty of Vraja which reminded Him of Sri Radha. His body became hot and melted the stone, leaving an imprint of His lotus feet.

54\. **Gaura-sthana** The cave on Nandisvara Hill in which Caitanya Mahaprabhu found the deitiesNandagramaof Krsna, Nanda, and Yasoda that were later installed in Nanda-bhavana temple.

55\. **Nanda-bagicha** The garden where Mother Yasoda had the best vegetables, fruits, and spices grown for her precocious son.

56\. **Krsna-kunda** In a grove of kadamba trees is this lovely lake where Krsna and the gopas enjoy water sports and the cows quench their thirst. Uddhava bathed in this lake on his first morning in Vraja.

57\. **Chacha-kunda** Here the Vraja-vasis either discard their excess buttermilk (chacha), or the cowherd boys consume it.

58\. **Panihari-kunda** The Vraja-vasis come here to fetch drinking water. Radha and Rohini cook under the surrounding bakula trees in summer; Krsna and the boys eat their breakfast at the adjoining corner of the lake. Radha and Krsna secretly meet each other after eating.

59\. **Sarasika-yudi** This is where a tearful Yasoda entrusts Krsna to Balarama every morning as the Vraja-vasis leave the boys who are heading out to the pastures with Their cows. Devotees complete their parikrama by returning to the top of Nandisvara Hill where they offer obeisances to Mahadeva and Krsna-Balarama.

7\. **Barifa** On the advice of Krsna the cowherd boys sat to eat their lunches and their tiffins never became empty.

8\. **Vatsavana (Bacchavana)** On the request of the hungry gopas Krsna conjured up pots of yogurt, rabri, and butter hy playing His flute. It was also here that the cows and older gopas were overcome by affection for their offspring, which were actually Krsna’s expansions.

9\. **Rabri-kunda** The place where the rabri appeared when Krsna conjured up food for His hungry friends.

10\. **Sankara-kunda** Various lakes decorate Vatsa- vana: one is Sankara-kunda, with a temple of Radha and Krsna on its bank. Bathing here washes away one’s sins.

11\. **Sonkh (Sonkh-kunda)** The gopis cooked a feast from forest vegetables, using utensils and spices that miraculously appeared in this clearing amidst kadamba trees. The smell of the gopis’ chaunce attracted Krsna and Madhumangala.

12\. **Malapura** When Krsna boasted of being the supreme controller, the gopas asked Him to create many enjoyable dishes which reappeared after they were eaten. As their desire was fulfilled, the gopas admitted that Krsna could actually be Govinda the Lord.

13\. **Kothara (Kothara-kunda)** The lake here provides fresh drinking water for Krsna’s cows and a place for them to rest before returning home. A temple of Lord Narayana stands here.

14\. **Malipura** The flowers that gardeners (mali) once cultivated here became garlands for all of Vraja. The florists would always keep their best garlands aside for Krsna and Balarama.

15\. **Devasirsa (Devasirsa-kunda)** The demigods observed the carefree Lords herding Their cows and playing with Their friends, and here they offered exalted prayers to Krsna and Balarama. The flowers they showered upon the divine brothers became Devasirsa-kunda.

16\. **Munisirsa-sthana** The place where some sages achieved the highest realisation, having taken a vow to stand on their heads until Krsna, the goal of their austerities, appeared.

17\. **Ghata** The landscape of Vrndavana awakened in Krsna such a surge of affection for Radha that Her absence made His life feel empty (gata). Subala understood the heart of His friend and convinced Radha to meet Krsna here.

18\. **Pramodavana (Pramodana)** The gopis started to cry when they couldn’t find Krsna. Their tears formed a rivulet flowing to the forest where He happened to be. Krsna immediately realised the situation, appeared before the girls, and pacified each of them after expanding Himself into as many forms as there were gopis.

19\. **Krsna-kunda in Pramodavana** When the gopis collected water from this lake, the reflection of dark clouds in the water reminded them of Krsna and they fainted. Krsna rushed to the place to revive the girls. A deity of Sudama is worshipped here.

20\. **Carana-kunda** A lake blessed by Krsna’s feet (carana).

21\. **Kadambavana** A small forest of kadamba trees.

22\. **Kanoyaro** This is where Kanva Muni set up his hermitage and worshipped Krsna. Kanoyaro is famous of its sweet-water lakes.

23\. **Panihari-kunda** It is a sweet-water lake in the area where Kanva Muni resided.

24\. **Krsna-kunda at Kanoyaro** As the lake is close to the asrama of Kanva Muni, he would sit upon its banks and blissfully recite his favourite names of Krsna, like Deva and Cakrapani.

25\. **Indroli** The birthplace of Indulekha. In her village and the neighbouring area, she is the leader of her friends.

26\. **Indra-kunda** King Indra came here feeling humbled after Krsna punished him for attacking the Vraja-vasis, and absorbed himself in the life of a Vaisnava. It is from here that pilgrims enter Kamyavana and visit the pastime places first in and then around Kaman.

27\. **Dhulauda-grama** There are three locations outside the Kamyavana parikrama path but near to Kamyavana: Dhulauda-grama, Udha-grama, and Atora. Dhulauda-grama is an especially arid patch of land suited neither for agriculture nor grazing. As the cows and gopas cross through it, Vraja-vasis and demigods alike can see the dust rising.

28\. **Udha-grama** A tranquil garden where Krsna’s cousin Uddhava rested on his journey to Nandagrama to deliver the messages Krsna had entrusted him with.

29\. **Atora** A secluded forest where Krsna and His friends were so absorbed in play one day that they failed to notice that twenty-four hours had passed.

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Kamyavana (Kamavana)** The fulfilment of every kind of desire is perfected in this forest of many charming groves and lakes. The forest has given shelter to many renunciants and is famous as the playground of Krsna, the residence of the Pandavas, and the hiding place of the fleeing Vrndavana deities.

2\. **Vimala-kunda** The largest lake in Kamyavana. Krsna waters His cows here and sports in its pure water with His friends. This is where Durvasa Muni demanded that Draupadi feed him and his sixty thousand disciples, and where Krsna housed ten million wives, the daughters of King Vimala. Madana-mohana, Vimala-devi, Nilakanthesvara Mahadeva, and many other deities reside at Vimala-kunda. The Kamyavana parikrama starts and ends at this lake.

3\. **Go-ghata** The western side of Vimala-kunda where Krsna’s cows drink.

4\. **Dharma-kunda** Here Yamaraja, disguised as a Yaksa, questioned Yudhisthira Maharaja about the principles of dharma. Pleased with the answers of the king, Yamaraja revived the other Pandavas, who had all died after drinking from the lake.

5\. **Kaman** The village of Yasoda’s parents in the heart of Kamyavana, which was surrounded by a wall with seven gates. The Pandavas lived here for a while during their time in exile.

6\. **Seven gates** The seven gates of Kaman are called Dig, Lanka, Amera, Devi, Delhi, Ramaji, and Mathura. After entering the village one visits the pastime places of Kaman.

7\. **Panca-pandava-kunda** The lake where the pastime of the Pandavas and Yamaraja took place. It is so close to Dharma-kunda that the lakes are almost indistinguishable from each other.

8\. **Panca-pandava-tirtha** In a brahmana’s house deities of Yudhisthira Maharaja and two of his brothers are served. The Pandavas are also said to have lived here for part of their stay in Kamyavana.

9\. **Pandava-kupa** A well from which the sons of Pandu would draw water and where Draupadi would do her washing.

10\. **Kamesvara Mahadeva** The deity here was originally worshipped by the Pandavas and later installed by Vajra as the guardian of Kamyavana. One of the five main Mahadevas of Vraja.

11\. **Manikarnika-ghata (Manikarnika-kunda)** Mahadeva invested this place with his potencies. King Vrsabhanu and his wife, desiring a special offspring, bathed here when they were on pilgrimage.

12\. **Radha-vallabha temple** A few of Vrndavana’s Deities — removed from Vrndavana to avoid Aurangzeb’s atrocities—stayed in Kaman before reaching Their final destinations. Amongst the temples constructed here for the pratibhiis is the temple of Radha-vallabha.

13\. **Radha-Govinda temple** Here, beside the pratibhiis of Radha-Govinda, Vrnda-devi resides. The ancient deity was originally installed by Vajra and rediscovered by Rupa Gosvami in Brahma-kunda. When the Vrndavana deities were about to move on from Kaman, Vrnda-devi declared that she would never leave Vrndavana.

14\. **Carana-kunda** Once while herding cows in Kamyavana, Krsna and Balarama decided to visit Their in-laws in Kaman to taste their delicious cooking. Before doing so They washed Their hands and feet in this lake.

15\. **Visnu-simhasana** A throne which is the sitting place of Lord Krsna. The Vraja-vasis also come here to celebrate the marriage of Lord Narayana and Laksmi in the month of Vaisakha.

16\. **Caurasi-stambh** Atop a large hill in Kaman stands the Kamyavana residence of Nanda Maharaja, an eighty-four-pillar palace. It is said that the Pandavas also lived here: Yudhisthira’s throne and Draupadi’s kitchen are still visible.

17\. **Radha-Gopinatha temple** Behind Caurasi-stambh Hill is the temple where the pratibhus of Radha-Gopinatha are worshipped.

18\. **Kamana-kunda** The water of this lake is composed of kama, the liquid essence of Kamyavana. By bathing here, those pure in heart have their spiritual desires fulfilled. When the gopis bathe here, Krsna appears and reciprocates their pure kama.

19\. **Narada-kunda** Narada Muni fell into an ecstatic swoon while singing of Krsna’s pastimes. His tears created this pond.

20\. **Nanda-kunda** Nanda Maharaja bathes here while visiting relatives in Kaman. Krsna joins His father when He is awake and willing.

21\. **Yasoda-kunda** While visiting relatives in Kaman, Mother Yasoda bathes in this lake and comes here to make butter for Krsna. It is also a place where Krsna and Balarama rest when herding cows, and where Prabodhananda Sarasvati and Gopala Bhatta Gosvami performed bhajana.

22\. **Lanka-kunda (Setubandha-kunda)** This lake represents the point of the ocean at Ramesvara from where Lord Ramacandra crossed to Lanka over a bridge (setu) of joined (bandha) boulders. Responding to Lalita’s challenge, Krsna and the monkeys re-enacted the pastime here.

23\. **Luklukivana** A place of hide-and-seek. Krsna sometimes hides in the forest or even in the caves of the hill nearby, and the gopis fan out in all directions to find Him.

24\. **Luklukana-micali-kunda** The lake is found in a dense woods where Krsna and the gopis play hide-and-seek. On one occasion they played a new version of the game by going underwater to see who could hold their breath the longest.

25\. **Lakes of Kasi, Prayaga, Puskara, and Gomati** The presiding deities of these holy places reside at the lakes named after them. Residence in Kamyavana blesses them with the rarely attained treasure of contact with Krsna’s devotees.

26\. **Gaya-kunda** Gaya personified and Agastya Muni reside here. Krsna manifested this lake to convince Nanda that he does not have to leave Vraja to visit Gaya. Many people come here to offer water or pinda for their ancestors.

27\. **Candrasena Hill** When Krsna played His flute after climbing this hill, the sound melted both the hearts of the gopis and the stones of the hill.

28\. **Carana-pahari** Once, when Krsna and the gopis were dancing in Kamyavana, the girls became tired. As they rested, Krsna climbed Candrasena Hill where He played His flute so that even the hill melted. This is where His footprint is visible.

29\. **Dvaraka-kunda** A lake near Candrasena Hill where Krsna brought His wives from Dvaraka, stayed with them in a city of tents, and showed them His footprints.

30\. **Dhyana-kunda** Intoxicated Krsna sat down here and meditated (dhyana) on Srimati Radharani’s features and witty talks. His thoughtsKamyavanacreated a pathway that starts here and leads to Radha’s lotus feet.

31\. **Vihvala-kunda** When the gopis became tired from dancing, Radha retired at a lakeside garden while Krsna climbed Candrasena Hill. At this lake She became totally bewildered (vihvala) by hearing Krsna playing the flute from the top of the hill.

32\. **Syama-kunda** On the advice of Lalita, Krsna once came to this lake to wait for Radharani who was in divine trance at Vihvala-kunda.

33\. **Lalita-kunda and Visakha-kunda** At their respective lakes, Radha’s two dearmost friends divulged to Krsna the cause of their mistress’s anger and advised Him how to pacify Her.

34\. **Mana-sarovara** The place where Krsna’s clever words dispelled Radha’s anger (mana).

35\. **Mohini-kunda** To prove that Sri Radhika was indeed the ultimate beauty—more beautiful than even His Mohini form—Krsna revealed the pastime incarnation of His beauty at this lake.

36\. **Indrasena Hill** The gopas’ playground with a stone slide and a musical stone, and also a battlefield where Krsna killed Vyomasura who pretended to be a friend of Krsna.

37\. **Picchalini-sila** A smooth, slippery depression in this stone extends down the slope of the hill, forming a perfect slide upon which the cowherd boys repeatedly ride.

38\. **Bhajana-sila** When tapped, this stone makes musical sounds like a drum. The gopas happily use it to accompany their dancing.

39\. **Vyomasura’s cave** At the end and near the top of Indrasena-parvata is this cave, where the demon Vyomasura, disguised as a gopa, entered Krsna’s play in the valley below to kidnap the cowherd boys.

40\. **Caurya-khela-sthana** The cowherd boys were stolen by Vyomasura while playing “stealing and hiding” here at the foot of Indrasena-parvata. Krsna knew what was happening and easily killed the demon.

41\. **Carana-cihna** Krsna killed Vyomasura by throwing him to the ground and strangling him. Balarama had to steady the shaking earth and so He pushed down a large rock, leaving an imprint of His feet there.

42\. **Bhojana-sthali (Bhojana-thali)** After killing Vyomasura, Krsna sat with His famished friends to share a meal. He manifested a huge variety and quantity of food and the gopas joyfully dived into their feast. The impression and marks of Krsna’s plates (thali), Balarama’s footprint, and His spilled milk are still visible here on a stone.

43\. **Ksira-sagara (Krsna-kunda)** When Krsna and the gopas ate together at Bhojana-sthali after the death of Vyomasura, Balarama spilled His condensed milk which flowed down the rock and into a lake known since as Ksira-sagara.

44\. **Kalavata** In a clearing within this forest the gopis danced and sang with such artistic grace that the sound of their voices and ankle bells forcibly drew Krsna away from His friends.

45\. **Kama-sarovara** This lake resembles shimmering liquid gold. Radha and Krsna enjoy untold pastimes in its waters and surrounding gardens. Once, Malati-gopi saw Krsna kiss her rival Lalita on the bank of this lake.

46\. **Santanu-kunda** Where the great King Santanu performed austerities before moving to Satoha.

47\. **Alta-pahari** This lotus-shaped grove is the exclusive resting place of Srimati Radharani. Once, as She rested here, Radha requested Her friends to apply fresh lac (alta) to the soles of Her feet, giving this place its name.

48\. **Citravicitra-sila** After Srimati Radharani requested Her friends to refresh the red lac on Her feet, She walked over some stones, painting (citra) them with different (vicitra) markings. After visiting the pastime places in Kamyavana, devotees return to Vimala-kunda where the parikrama ends.

9\. **Rupa Gosvami’s bhajana-kutira** Rupa Gosvami sat here beneath a kadamba tree writing books on devotional service. Once, when he was at a loss how to celebrate Sanatana’s appearance day, Radha appeared to him in the guise of a local girl carrying ingredients fit for a feast.

10\. **Asesa** Siva resides in this forest as Asesvara. When Mother Yasoda refused to let Lord Siva have Krsna’s darsana, he came here to lament. Nanda Maharaja realised what had happened and wanted to pacify the guest, but Siva’s only request was to taste Krsna’s remnants.

11\. **Asa-kunda** The lake in Asesa where Asesvara Mahadeva resides. While performing austerities at Pavana-sarovara, Maharaja Parjanya regularly came to bathe here, and prayed to Lord Siva for his grace.

12\. **Gendokhara-kunda** Krsna played ball with Radha and Her girlfriends here at a lakeside meadow.

13\. **Kokilavana** The divine couple strolled through the forests of Vraja hiding from the gopis who followed Their distinctive footprints. In Kokilavana, Radha became somewhat proud, and so Krsna disappeared. On another occasion the joined concert of Krsna and the cuckoos provided Radha with an excuse to meet Her beloved here. A lake called Krsna-kunda and a temple of Kokila-bihari are found in this forest.

14\. **Yavata-grama** The village where Radharani moved after marrying Abhimanyu. With its wide alleys and palatial dwellings, Yavata was a wedding gift for Radha but She saw it as prison.

15\. **Yavavata** Under this banyan tree Krsna painted Radha’s feet with red lac (yava). The village Yavata’s name is derived from this tree.

16\. **Kisori-kunda** One of the fifteen sacred lakes of Yavata where the sounds of Krsna’s and the gopis’ nocturnal rasa dance echo across the waters. This is also the lake where Kamsa transformed into a gopi.

17\. **Kandarpa-gudhali** A hidden (gudhali) garden that serves as the playground of Cupid (Kandarpa). Here Krsna lovingly serves Radha when She feels fatigued at the end of night.

18\. **Pibana-kunda (Padar-ganga / Piyala-kunda)** Here Lalita urged Krsna to kiss Radha. Hari happily obliged, but when He drank the nectar of Her lips He became completely intoxicated. Radha planted a parijata tree at this lake, and used its flowers to make garlands for Hari.

19\. **Vatsa-khora** A small wooded area where, on Gopastami, Sri Radha, in the clothes of Subala and holding a calf in Her arms, met Krsna in the middle of the day.

20\. **Candra-salika** A multi-storey moon-tower in the home of Sri Radha’s husband, Abhimanyu, from where Radha watches Krsna when He leads His cows through Yavata. Krsna once entered the house in a gift box sent by Yasoda.

21\. **Pilu tree** In the courtyard of Abhimanyu’s house there is a pilu tree that Krsna climbs and calls Radha with His cuckoo-like voice. When Jatila decides to guard the house on suspicion of Krsna’s presence, He has to spend the whole night in the tree.

22\. **Larili-kunda** Here Lalita arranged Radha’s meeting with Krsna after He had to spend two nights hiding in trees.

23\. **Mukta-kunda** The place where the gopis dressed Radha with pearl (mukta) necklaces, bracelets, and a headdress before She went to meet Krsna and liberated (mukta) Him from loneliness.

24\. **Daharavana** The forest near Abhimanyu’s house.

25\. **Sunthera** The birthplace of Campakalata-devi.

The name Sanketa also denotes a meeting place set up by mirror signals (sanketa). The first meeting of the divine couple also took place here on a night when Radha’s loving passion forcibly carried Her into the dark forest. Syamananda Pandita displayed the symptoms of gopi-bhava when he visited here. The temples of Sanketa-devi and Sanketa-bihari are found in this forest.

4\. **Sanketa-kunja** This is where, after leaving the rasa dance, Krsna decorated Radha with flowers, and Radha made curls in Krsna’s black hair and painted pictures on His moon-like face.

5\. **Gopala Bhatta Gosvami’s cave** The Gosvami lived in Sanketa for many years in an underground chamber next to the deity of Sanketa-devi, deeply absorbed in the pastimes of this place.

6\. **Vihvalavana** A forest where Krsna was overwhelmed (vihvala) by impatience to meet Radharani.

7\. **Vihvala-kunda** Formed by Krsna’s tears, this small pond induces overpowering feelings of separation in whoever drinks from or bathes in its waters. The temple of Vihvala-devi stands at the lake.

8\. **Vasati** Radha enjoyed Her girlhood here before Her family moved to Varsana.

9\. **Candravalivana (Candravali-kunda)** As a young girl, Candravali and her friends would play and bathe here and rest within the shade of a kadamba tree on the bank of the lake.

10\. **Rithora** The birthplace of Candravali-devi, Radharani’s competitor expansion. Gaudiya Vaisnavas offer her respect from a distance.

11\. **Siha (Si / Sri)** Since the day Akrura took Krsna to Mathura this roadside garden has been called Siha. As the crying gopis followed the chariot, Krsna turned to them with joined palms and said, “Go home. I will return quickly (sighra)."

12\. **Dhanasimha** The birthplace of Dhanistha-devi.

13\. **Premavana** The love-saturated forest where Prema-sarovara, Prema-bihari, a rasa-mandala, and a swing commemorate the pastimes of the divine couple.

14\. **Prema-sarovara** In a display of pr???- vaicittya, though sitting side by side, Radha thought that Krsna had left Her and Krsna thought that She had left Him. The tears of the divine couple and the gopis flowed into a nearby lake, now known as the “lake of transcendental love.”

15\. **Viharavana** Krsna expanded Himself to be with each and every gopi here and satisfied them individually. A lake called Vihara-kunda is found in the forest.

16\. **Bahulavana** The forest animals gathered to greet Lord Caitanya while He was walking through this forest. This is where Krsna invoked clouds by the music of His flute when the gopis became tired from rasa-dancing through the twelve forests.

17\. **Krsna-kunda (Bahula-kunda / Mana-sarovara)** Where Krsna was so pleased by the truthfulness of the cow Bahula that He saved her from a tiger and blessed her to become His consort in her next life. It is also the lake where once Radha disappeared to sulk. In a nearby temple deities of a cow, a calf, a tiger, a brahmana, and Krsna are worshipped.

18\. **Sankarsana-kunda** One who bathes in the lakes of Bahulavana during the month of Caitra can see the forms of Laksmi-Narayana that eternally reside in this forest.

19\. **Chatikara (Sattikara)** Where Nanda Maharaja’s clan camped after leaving Gokula and before moving to Nandagrama. This is where Krsna first tended the calves and where Garuda surrendered to Him.

20\. **Garuda-Govinda temple** Garuda became enlightened about the truths of the Lord’s humanlike pastimes, seeing Krsna both kill invincible demons and lose in wrestling against His friends. Here Krsna also manifested His four-armed form and sat on Sridama, who transformed himself into Garuda.

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Varsana area** Radharani and Her friends grew up and spent their carefree youth in this region before they were married. Next to Varsana, the eternal home of Radharani, one can visit Unchagaon, where Lalita-devi lived as a child; Ciksoli, the birthplace of Citra-gopi; Kamai, the village of Visakha-devi; Dabhararo, where Rangadevi grew up, and other holy places where the young gopis played.

2\. **Pipasavana (Piyaso)** The jungle behind Yogamaya’s temple where one visits Pyasa-kunda and Jhariwale-baba-kunda when entering the area of Varsana.

3\. **Yogamaya temple** The deity form of the Supreme Lord’s internal potency stands here in New Vraja-dhama. The sight of her can drive away all inauspicious thoughts and make one’s heart swell with joy.

4\. **Pyasa-kunda** When Krsna became thirsty (pipasa) while herding cows, Balarama brought Him water from this lake.

5\. **Jhariwale-baba-kunda** Asvatthama lives on the bank of this lake. He constantly chants Krsna’s names to regain the Lord’s mercy, which he lost when he offended Draupadi by killing her sons. The lake also has a rasa-vedi on its banks. From here one goes clockwise all the way to Gendokhara.

6\. **Gendokhara** In their childhood, Radha and the gopis would come here to play ball. It was also here that Krsna once made Radharani close Her eyes in shame by telling Her friends the details of Their intimate pastimes. Gendokhara is the starting point of the Varsana parikrama. Before taking the parikrama path, devotees can offer their respects to the holy places of nearby Unchagaon and Sakhigiri.

7\. **Unchagaon (Atora-parvata / Sri Lalita-grama)** The settlement close to Varsana where Lalita-devi grew up. Two hills, the village itself, and nearby Sakhigiri were the playgrounds of little Lalita and her friends.

8\. **Lalita-vivaha-sthala (Lalita-vivaha-mandapa)** The marriage platform where Radharani arranged a wedding festival for Krsna and Lalita to broadcast Lalita’s unique place in the divine couple’s heart. Deities of Radha-Krsna and Lalita are still served here today.

9\. **Sakhigiri** The playground of Lalita-devi and her friends. On this hill there is a stone slide and many other sacred stones with the marks of the gopis—citra-silas, citravicitra-silas, and alta-silas.

10\. **Picchalini-sila** The stone on which Lalita and her girlfriends would slide down the hill.

11\. **Carana-pahari** The footprints on this hill are not Krsna’s. They belong to the young gopis, most notably Sri Radha, whose footprints can be seen by careful scrutiny.

12\. **Gopi-puskarmi** The lake where Lalita and her friends played water sports.

13\. **Sakhi-kupa** Lalita and her friends dug this well, and when thirsty they drew water from it.

14\. **Triveni-kupa** Krsna dug this well with the vibration of His flute in response to the gopis' desire to bathe at the confluence of the Ganges, Yamuna, and Sarasvati rivers. Lalita bathes here every day after dawn. Balarama once bathed here, too. Both a temple of Dauji and Narayana Bhatta Gosvami’s samadhi are nearby.

15\. **Deha-kunda** A lake where the gopis gave themselves body (deha) and soul to Krsna. In this place a poor brahmana once approached Krsna and the gopis and asked for daksina. Krsna gave him a sack filled with gold equal in weight to Sri Radha.

16\. **Venisankara Mahadeva** On the bank of Deha-kunda is the deity of Mahadeva that the gopis installed with their own hands. Bathing here and seeing Lord Siva surpasses bathing at the confluence at Prayaga. After remembering the pastimes of the young gopis in Unchagaon, one starts the parikrama of Varsana in Gendokhara at the foot of Brahma-giri.

17\. **Varsana (Mukhyeravali)** This village is the parental home of Radharani on the four-peaked hill of Brahma. Because Radha is the cause of the downpour (varsa) of bliss from Krsna’s cloud, She is called Varsana, and the place is so named after Her.

18\. **Brahma-giri (Brahma-sarin / Brhatsanu)** The four peaks of Brahma-giri are the heads of Lord Brahma, who performed austerities with a desire to become a hill so that he could receive the touch of Radha’s and the gopis’ feet.

19\. **Pili-pokhara** A lake named after the pilu trees on its banks. It once turned yellow from the turmeric on Radha’s hands. When Krsna decided to bathe in the golden pond, He experienced the sentiments of Gaura Krsna: separation from Himself.

20\. **Bhadavali** One of Nanda Maharaja’s granaries. It is surrounded by lush pastures where the cows of King Vrsabhanu graze.

21\. **Kirti-kunda** The bathing place of Radha’s mother, Kirtida. Anyone who dies here returns to Goloka.

22\. **Bhanu-sarovara (Vrsabhanu-kunda)** Maharaja Vrsabhanu takes his morning bath here. In a previous day of Brahma, Radha appeared from a lotus within the waters of this lake. Radha and Krsna enjoy a variety of water sports in Bhanu-sarovara.

23\. **Vrajesvara Mahadeva** King Vrsabhanu installed this Mahadeva on the bank of Bhanu-sarovara. Although there have been efforts made to move the deity, he refuses to budge.

24\. **Karala (Karahala / Karhela)** The village known as Lalita-devi’s birthplace. Here Krsna repeatedly enjoys rasa-dancing, during which the gopis make graceful gestures with their hands. A rasa-mandala commemorates these pastimes.

25\. **Lalita-kunda** On the banks of this tranquil lake sits a temple devoted to the worship of Lalita and Krsna.

26\. **Sankari-khor** A narrow pass at the mountains of Varsana. Radha and the gopis often take this route to give Krsna a chance to collect tax from them. Once, after capturing and ridiculing Krsna, the gopis forced Him to take a vow of never taking tax again.

27\. **Mayuravana** The forest covering the hill whose peak is called Mora-garh or Mayura-kutira.

28\. **Mora-garh (Mayura-kutira / Mora-kutira)** Where Radha and Krsna danced surrounded on all sides by dancing peacocks. It was here that the eyesight of a blind Vaisnava was restored after he had fulfilled Radha’s desire by painting the peacock dance of the divine couple. On another occasion, Krsna made baskets of laddus appear here in response to His friends’ requests.

29\. **Visnu-parvata** The small mountain at the famous dana-lila passage, Sankari-khor. Vilasa- garh is on its slopes.

30\. **Vilasa-garh** A flower garden on Visnu-parvata where, just prior to Her youth, Radha and Her girlfriends would frolic (vilasa). Once the gopis stood blocking Krsna’s path to Radha, but a strong wind stirred up a dust storm, giving Krsna an ideal opportunity to get to Radha and embrace Her.

31\. **Tamala-kunja** Krsna would sometimes appear among the frolicking girls and on some pretext lead Radha away into this flower-filled garden at Vilasa-garh.

32\. **Krsna-baithaka** On some occasions Krsna waits here all day for Radha to come but She never arrives. Because of these daytime vigils, the place is know as one of Krsna’s sitting places.

33\. **Ciksoll (Citrasala / Citrasali)** The birthplace of Citra-gopi. It was in a vetasi grove nearby that Radha disclosed to Her friends that She loved three persons simultaneously. In a tamala-kunja at Ciksoli, Radha once waited for Krsna while He mistakenly waited for Her at Visnu-parvata. When the gopis finally brought Him to the right place He was amazed by the beauty of Radha. From here one can offer respects to the neighbouring holy places of the Varsana area.

34\. **Kamai** The birthplace of Visakha-devi. Simply by remembering this village or by saying its name, a devotee receives the mercy of Radha.

35\. **Ravar-kunda** On the banks of this lake Visakha and her friends would make statues of Krsna from mud and decorate them with flowers and twigs. A temple of Gopala and a temple of Dauji stand here.

36\. **Dabhararo** The birthplace of either Rangadevi or Tungavidya. Radha comes here to play with Her friends’ dolls. Once, when Krsna saw Radha playing ball here with the gopis, His eyes flooded (dubu) with tears. From then on the locals called their village Dabhararo.

37\. **Surya-kunda** A lake where Radha and Her friends regularly come to worship the sun god with offerings of flowers and incense.

38\. **Mukta-kunda (Ratna-kunda)** The gopis, following in Krsna’s footsteps, planted their families’ pearls. When they realised that only thorn bushes grew on the field, they had no other option than to beg pearls from Krsna.

39\. **Dohani-kunda** King Vrsabhanu keeps his vast herd of cows in barns and corrals at this place. Once, while Radha was milking a cow, Krsna appeared and teased Her, saying She wasn’t doing it the right way. He then sat down beside Her and showed Her how to do it properly.

40\. **Rankoli (Rasoli)** On the nights when the gopis visit their parents in and around Varsana, Krsna calls them to this place with His flute to enjoy rasa-dancing.

41\. **Rasa-kunda** After rasa-dancing in Rankoli Krsna and the gopis come here to bathe and enjoy a picnic underneath a banyan tree on the banks of the lake.

42\. **Gahvaravana** It is a dense, conch-shaped forest at Varsana, including Dana-, Mana-, and Mora-garh. Gahvaravana-bihari is the presiding Deity of the area.

43\. **Sitala-kunda (Gahvara-kunda / Krsna-kunda)** Here Lalita-devi once fed laddus to Madhumangala in order to compensate him for the humiliation he was previously forced to endure. At another time, an old Vraja-vasi woman roasted chickpeas for Krsna at this lake.

44\. **Mana-garh** Here Radha and Krsna blessed Brahma by dancing upon his peak-like head. It is also the place where Krsna likes to swing with Radha, and where gopi-disguised Krsna successfully pacified sulking Radha, who came to know that Her beloved had visited Candravali. A rasa-mandala and a swing commemorate the pastimes the divine couple enjoyed here.

45\. **Dana-garh** One of Varsana’s peaks that overlooks Sankari-khor and where Krsna regularly demands a toll (dana) from the gopis. There are beautiful gardens atop the hill and a temple nearby called Dana-mandira where the Deities Radha-Dana-bihari were installed six thousand years ago.

46\. **Vrsabhanu-pura** Vrsabhanu Maharaja’s palace in Varsana atop the four-peaked hill of Brahma where Srimati Radharani spent Her carefreeVarsana to Umraoyouth. The Deities of Larili-Lal are served here in the main temple called Sriji. This is where one completes the parikrama of Varsana. Before starting the circumambulation of Govardhana Hill at Rahela, devotees can visit the nearby sites relating to the coronation of Radha and Krsna.

47\. **Chattravana (Chata)** The forest where the gopas crowned Krsna and where thousands of gopis attacked the kingdom of the new monarch for offending the real authority of Vrndavana.

48\. **Umrao (Radha-sthali / Umraya)** Krsna desired to establish Radha’s sovereignty over Himself as well as over Vraja and the Vraja-vasis, and so in this place, by the arrangement of Yogamaya, Sri Radha was crowned the undisputed queen of Vrndavana.

49\. **Kisori-kunda** A lake that commemorates Radha’s coronation bath. At the bank of this kunda is Lokanatha Gosvami’s bhajana-sthana where Krsna gifted Radha-vinoda to the great saint.

The deities of Radha, Kirtida, and Mukhara are served in the village and a bhajana-sila is found here.

3\. **Deravali** In this clearing Nanda Maharaja’s clan camped on their way from Chatikara to Nandagrama.

4\. **Rahela** This is where one enters the path around Govardhana, but devotees who do parikrama solely of Govardhana Hill can start and end their trek at Manasa-gariga or Radha-kunda. Rahela is the place where Uddhava entered Nanda Maharaja’s kingdom and where Krsna once played His flute while the gopis were still bathing and dressing in the evening. In their haste to join Krsna, they all neglected their appearance.

5\. **Yamuna-mata** A tributary of the Yamuna once flowed here.

6\. **Naradavana** Narada Muni resides in this forest eternally. Devotees who desire his blessings sit here, offer prayers, chant Krsna’s names, and recite the sutras written by the great sage.

7\. **Narada-kunda** This is where Narada Muni performed austerities and became able to see the astakaliya-lila of Radha and Krsna. At Krsna’s request he remained at this lake and penned the Narada-bhakti-sutra.

8\. **Khelanavana** The caravan of the Vraja-vasis’ carts stretched from here to Deravali when they set up a camp on their way from Chatikara to Nandagrama.

9\. **KiIala-kunda** Within the waters of this lake, Krsna sometimes enjoys splashing and swimming with the gopis.

10\. **Kanduka-tirtha (Kanduka-ksetra)** The field at the banks of Kilala-kunda where Krsna and the gopas play ball.

11\. **Yugala-kunda** Where the Vraja-vasis stayed on their way from Chatikara to Nandagrama.

12\. **Panca-tirtha-kunda (Harideva-kunda)** It contains the purifying waters of five sacred rivers: Gomati, Narmada, Sarayu, Vetra, and Kanci. Five holy places also reside on the bathing ghatas of the lake: Ganga, Puskara, Prayaga, Kuruksetra, and Gaya.

13\. **Puspavana** The gopis come here to collect flowers. On one such occasion Krsna pretended to be the gardener of the king and accused them of stealing flowers. But since His sins proved to be greater, Radha sentenced Him to dress Her hair.GovardhanaOn another occasion Krsna lifted Radha up in the air with the branch of a tree. There are sub-forests in Puspavana such as Asokavana, Kadambavana, and Kusumavana.

14\. **Kusuma-sarovara (Sumanah-sarovara)** The lake of flowers in the heart of Puspavana was created by Krsna after being sentenced to decorate Radha’s hair. This is where Astavakra Muni was liberated, Narada attained a gopi body, and Uddhava narrated Srimad-Bhagavatam for the Dvaraka queens. The Radha-Vanabihari temple stands on the bank of the lake.

15\. **Uddhava-baithaka** After witnessing the depth of the gopis' love, Uddhava prayed to take birth as grass in Vraja in order to obtain the dust of the cowherd damsels’ lotus feet. Since his desire was fulfilled, he eternally resides as creepers around Kusuma-sarovara.

16\. **Syamavana** The forest is named Syama not only due to its darkness but also because here Radha once disguised Herself as Syama, giving an opportunity to Krsna, who was disguised as Radha, to take all the gopis to a kunja one by one. In the same forest, Krsna and Balarama once played Holi with Their gopis on the full-moon night of Phalguna.

17\. **Ratna-kunda** A rasa-vedi at the lake marks the place where Krsna would dance with the gopis. Here, too, is the divine couple’s famous Ratna- simhasana upon which They sat after playing Holi, when Sankhacuda appeared to kidnap Radha. The footprints of Krsna are visible in a stone which melted at the sound of the flute.

18\. **Gvalavana (Gopala-kunda / Gvala-kunda)** A resting place of the gopas. Sometimes, before eating, the cowherd boys steal Krsna’s tiffin and throw it around playing “keep-away” until it gets back to Krsna. Once Madhumangala tried to hide some prasada in his scarf, but the gopas stole it from him.

19\. **Manasa-gariga** This original form of Ganga was invoked by Krsna’s mind when the gopas insisted that He should purify Himself by bathing in the sacred river after killing Vatsasura. When the Vraja-vasis wanted to go on pilgrimage to Mother Ganga, Krsna convinced them to visit Manasa-gariga to meet her. This is where they celebrated Dipavali on the following night.

20\. **Cakra-tirtha** After Govardhana was set back in place, the Sudarsana cakra requested Krsna that he may stay somewhere in the area. Krsna gave him Cakra-tirtha as a place of bhajana. This is also the place of Cakresvara Mahadeva and Sanatana Gosvami.

21\. **Sanatana Gosvami’s bhajana-kutira** This is where old Sanatana worshipped the sila he received from Krsna personally during one of his Govardhana parikramas. Once, when Sanatana Gosvami became disturbed by the mosquitoes at Cakra-tirtha, Cakresvara Mahadeva ordered them to leave the area. This is where Sanatana left this world.

22\. **Paranga** Krsna, disguised as a boatman, was ferrying Radha and the gopis from this boat landing of Cakra-tirtha to the other side of Manasa-gariga.

23\. **Saubhari Muni’s palace** On the bank of Manasa-gariga stands the palace where Saubhari Muni lived with his fifty wives. Mandhata Maharaja’s residence is nearby.

24\. **Mukharavinda temple** This temple is home to a sila considered to be the lotus mouth of Govardhana and the Mukuta-sila, the stone with the impression of Krsna’s crown.

25\. **Harideva temple** Once Krsna heard His names, “O Hari! ? Deva!” and so appeared before the gopis. When Radha begged Him to stay, He left a Deity of Himself behind that the gopis could serve daily. Other residents of Vraja thought Him to be an incarnation of Lord Narayana.

26\. **Brahma-kunda** This lake was created from the water Brahma used to bathe Krsna in order to atone for stealing the gopas and the calves. On the four sides of Brahma-kunda are the lakes of Indra, Yamaraja, Varuna, and Kuvera, formed when each of these demigods bathed the Lord. Manasi-devi, one of the four goddesses of Vraja, resides at this lake.

27\. **Indra-dhvaja-tirtha** A small hill where Nandaraja and the Vraja-vasis would annually worship Indra by raising a flag in his honour. Krsna brings His cows here to graze.

28\. **Papamocana-kunda and Rnamocana-kunda** These two lakes are famed for their sanctity, and devotees who bathe in them are freed from all vices.

29\. **Laksmi-Narayana** The festival Deities of Govardhana Hill. Some say that they are actually Krsna and Satyabhama who came to Govardhana to attract worshippers in the regulated devotion of Vaikuntha to the spontaneous devotion of Vrndavana.

30\. **Krsna-vedi (Dana-ghati / Syama-vedi)** This is the highest point on a path that traverses Govardhana Hill. Here Krsna collects a toll from the cowherd girls by setting up toll stations. The footmarks of Radha and Krsna and the Dani- raya temple are nearby.

31\. **Dana-nivartana-vana** The gopis promised to return (nivartana) to pay the toll (dana) they owed Krsna, but instead they took revenge on Him in this forest, thus repaying (nivartana) for His pranks at Krsna-vedi. This is where Krsna placed His head upon Radha’s lotus toes, offering obeisances to His beloved.

32\. **Dana-nivartana-kunda (Dana-sarovara)** This lake is so called because Krsna enjoyed the dana-keli pastime on its shores at Dana-nivartana- vana with His cowherd boyfriends and girlfriends.

33\. **Parasauli** This is where Krsna enacted the spring rasa dance with the nitya-siddha gopis on a full-moon night by the banks of the Yamuna. Other holy places at the village are Sankarsana-kunda, Baladeva’s pastime place; Gandharva-kunda, where the Gandharvas marvelled at Krsna’s divine singing; and Indra’s drums, two cylindrical stones upon which Indra declared the supremacy of Krsna.

34\. **Vamsivata** Krsna stood beneath this expansive banyan tree and played His flute to call the eternally liberated gopis for a rasa dance.

35\. **Mohavana** The forest where the demigods were bewildered at the sight of the spring rasa dance.

36\. **Candra-sarovara** The lake over which Candra hung motionless and into which he cried tears of ecstasy, moved by the wonders of the spring rasa dance. The divine couple also come here to enjoy rasa-dancing and honey-drinking during Their midday pastimes. The temple of Radha- Candrabihari and Suradasa’s samadhi are nearby.

37\. **Srngara-mandira** A resting place of Radha and Krsna on the bank of Candra-sarovara.

38\. **Paitha** Krsna assumed a four-armed Narayana form to conceal Himself from the gopis, but Radha’s superior love forced Him to revert to His original two-armed form. On another occasion Krsna revealed His plan against Indra to the gopas at a nearby kadamba tree and entered a cave in Govardhana’s body to lift him.

39\. **Aimtha-kadamba** Krsna gathered His friends beneath this giant kadamba tree and revealed His plan to lift Govardhana like a giant umbrella. Then He tied the branches of the tree in a knot to prove His ability to accomplish the plan.

40\. **Narayana-sarovara** The lake at Paitha. The Caturbhuja temple with a four-armed Deity of Krsna stands nearby.

41\. **Annakuta-ksetra** The field between Dana-nivartana-kunda and Aniyora where Govardhana-puja took place. It is known as the “table of Govardhana” because it was filled with thousands of hills of food offered to the gigantic personal form of the hill. This is also where the Vraja-vasis ran to escape the clouds of Indra, and where Krsna lifted Govardhana. Stones with the impressions of plates, bowls, and pots that held the offerings to Giriraja can still be seen here.

42\. **Sankarsana-kunda** Here Lord Balarama and His friends frolic while their cows drink the lake’s clear waters or rest beneath the trees on its banks. The Deity of Dauji in nearby Balarama-mandira was installed by King Vajranabha five thousand years ago.

43\. **Aniyora** During the Vraja-vasis’ Govardhana- puja, the gigantic deity form of the hill ate the offerings with his right hand while motioning with his left and saying, “Bring me more!” (Ani aur!)

44\. **Srngara-sthali (Gopala-prakata-sthali)** Here the gopis insisted that Radha rearrange Her clothing, ornaments, and makeup (srngara) before entering the rasa dance. Once Krsna Himself ornamented Radha here. This is also where Madhavendra Puri discovered the Deity of Gopala.

45\. **Stones with coloured marks** Red finger marks from the lac used to repaint Radha’s feet, Vermillion stains from the kunkuma in Her hair part, and a black spot from the collyrium used to paint Her eyes mark the stones here.

46\. **Gopala-mandira** Where Madhavendra Puri performed the installation ceremony of the Gopala Deity and where the Deity’s first temple was constructed.

47\. **Gauri-tirtha (Gauri-kunda)** Candravali favours this place the same way Radha favours Radha-kunda. At this lake in the temple of Gauri-devi, Sri Radha met Krsna, disguised as goddess Nikunjavidya. When Radha’s husband arrived to catch his unchaste wife, he found Her praying to the goddess of the temple.

48\. **Surabhivana** Surabhi and Indra bathed Krsna in this forest. Madana-mohana with the Asta-sakhis are worshipped here in a temple.

49\. **Govinda-kunda** When Surabhi and Indra held an abhiseka for Krsna, the bath water of the Lord formed a pool now called Govinda-kunda. Indra-tila is a small hill where Krsna sat on a stone throne while He was crowned the indisputable king of the cows, Govinda. A temple of Gopala and another of Radha-Govinda are located here. The Jhulana-sila with a picture of Radha and Krsna on a swing, the footprints and handprints of Krsna, and a footprint of Surabhi can also be seen here.

50\. **Madhavendra Puri’s bhajana-kutira** After circumambulating Govardhana Hill, Madhavendra Puri sat down to rest here at Govinda-kunda. A beautiful cowherd boy, Gopala, brought him a pot of milk and appeared in his dream the following night describing His hiding place in the nearby bushes.

51\. **Nipa-kunda** As the gopas lay on the banks of this lake, repeatedly wishing aloud for yogurt, Radha and some gopis appeared with pots on their heads. The gopas requested a toll and luckily the pots were full of yogurt.

52\. **Drona-ksetra (Drona-tirtha)** This sacred place is so named because the gopas would use the cup-shaped leaves of the kadamba trees nearby as cups (dona) from which they drank yogurt or ate their food.

53\. **Mukuta-sila** The stone with the impression of Balarama’s crown. The crown toppled from Balarama’s head when He marvelled at the rasa dance of Radha and Krsna from a hiding place on Govardhana Hill.

54\. **Apsaravana** In this forest Krsna revealed His Varaha-rupa while playing with the gopas. On another occasion the gopas came here to reenact Lord Rama’s battle with Ravana; Krsna and Balarama transformed into Rama and Laksmana, and the gopas became Their monkey army.

55\. **Nrsimha-tila** A hill from which Lord Nrsimha, protector of the devotees, surveys the surrounding forest. It is said that the black stone Deity of Nrsimha here is over one thousand years old.

56\. **Puncari-kunda (Navala-kunda)** During the Puncari rasa dance the over-churned pr???- milk liquefied. Liquid Krsna entered the water of Puncari-kunda, and liquid Radha and the gopis melted into Apsara-kunda.

57\. **Apsara-kunda** The lake was created from the water the Apsaras used to bathe Krsna. Twenty-four incarnations of the Lord are said to reside on its banks. When the Puncari rasa dance took place Apsara-kunda and Puncari-kunda transformed into liquid pr???. Apsara-bihari and Apsaresvara Mahadeva reside here.

58\. **Puncari** The tail end of Govardhana, one of the scenes of the spring rasa dance of Krsna and the nitya-siddha gopis.

59\. **Dauji-mandira (Dimka Dauji / Luk-luk Dauji)** At this lookout atop Govardhana Hill Balarama witnessed a unique rasa dance taking place at nearby Candra-sarovara: Radha and Krsna expanding themselves into many couples. The footprint of Krsna here marks the spot below where Krsna stood when He lifted Govardhana.

60\. **Indra-vedi** The place where Indra offered prayers to Krsna to nullify the offences he committed after Govardhana-puja. The footprint of Airavata, Indra’s gigantic elephant, is still visible here. Once the cowherd boys entertained themselves here by dancing and playing music, but they had no drum. Krsna used a large rock, the Vadani-sila, as a drum to keep the beat.

61\. **Lautha** Here Lautha-gopa sits beneath a tree and waits for Krsna until He returns from Mathura. Krsna blessed His friend to remain staunch—refraining even from food—while he waits for Him.

62\. **Rasa-sthali** The area of Sutalavana and Apsaravana where Krsna likes to bring His girlfriends to stage the rasa dance.

63\. **Sutalavana** A small forest famous for Sutala- kunda and the cave called Manikandali.

64\. **Sutala-kunda** A small lake where Radha and Krsna would sometimes rest after enjoying the rasa dance.

65\. **Manikandali** This cave is famous as the scene of Radha’s and Krsna’s intimate pastimes and as Raghava Gosvami’s place of bhajana.

66\. **Syama-dhak** The gopas would rest in the shade and eat their lunches from the leaf-cups of the surrounding trees, while the cows graze in the pastures on the forests edge.

67\. **Samai** Krsna approached pouting Radha at this place in gopi disguise as Syama-sakhi and was able to capture Her attention long enough to transform Her indifference into dire longing.

68\. **Reheja** A clearing in Syama-dhak, one of the places where Indra tried to pacify Krsna. At Reheja, Indra fell to the ground at Krsna’s feet.

69\. **Go-kunda** Whenever Krsna and the cowherd boys come to this small lake with their cows, they all drink deeply from its clear, sweet waters.

70\. **Suryapatanavana** A small forest where the sun god, Surya, fell to the ground (patana) while fleeing in battle from the mighty Ravana.

71\. **Kadambavana (Kadamba-khandi)** A beautiful forest of kadamba trees with four important lakes: Indra-kunda, Airavata-kunda, Surabhi-kunda, and Rudra-kunda. Krsna enjoys rasa dances with the gopis here.

72\. **Indra-kunda (Carana-kunda)** Indra’s tears of remorse for having offended Krsna flew down from Indra-vedi and collected in a depression in the earth. This lake is also called Carana-kunda, because Indra’s tears washed Krsna’s lotus feet (carana). A resting place of Lord Caitanya is nearby.

73\. **Surabhi-kunda** The lake marks the place where Mother Surabhi interceded on King Indra’s behalf as he tried to pacify Krsna. Once Krsna waited in vain for Radha in a nearby grove, then Radha waited for Him at the same place. When finally a gopi took Krsna back to the meeting place, Radha backed away.

74\. **Airavata-kunda** This is where Indra’s elephant deposited the waters of the celestial Ganges he had brought for Krsna’s coronation abhiseka.

75\. **Rudravana** This forest in Kadambavana is famous for secluded Rudra-kunda.

76\. **Rudra-kunda (Rudan-kunda / Haraji-kunda)** The lake was formed by Lord Siva’s tears of compassion for the fallen souls. The gopis also cried here once when they did not find Krsna, and the Vraja-vasis did so too when Krsna and Balarama left for Mathura. Radha and Krsna would come here to enjoy water sports. The lake is also known as the embodiment of Giriraja’s anger. Gendoka-ksetra, where Hara-gopa played ball with Krsna, is nearby.

77\. **Yatipura (Gopalapura / Srngara-sthali)** This is the place where the sannyasi (yati) Madhavendra Puri stored Gopala’s grains and kept His cows after installing the Deity on Govardhana Hill. Madhavendra Puri also lived here along with Gopala’s servants. Viththalesvara’s tomb and the Dandavat-sila are found here.

78\. **Surya-kunda** Where the Vraja-vasis once worshipped the sun god. One of the resting places of Lord Caitanya is at the lake.

79\. **Mara-kunda** Where Gopala’s grains were husked, cleansed, and sacked. The husking of wheat and the thrashing of rice made a depression in the ground, a basin the rain soon filled to form this lake.

80\. **Vilasavana** This forest is a favourite arena for the charming pastimes (vilasa) of Radha and Krsna. Radha once lost Her favourite ankle bell here and was busy with searching for it. Krsna eventually presented several other beautiful anklets to Radha.

81\. **Vilasa-kunda** A deep lake where Radha and Krsna bathe. Long ago, the Deity of Harideva was immersed here to hide Him from Aurangzeb’s soldiers.

82\. **Jnana’s and Ajnana’s garden** Where Krsna likes to play hide-and-seek with His friends and where two trees, Jnana and Ajnana, stand. They are two girls who transformed themselves into trees in order not to disturb the spontaneity of the divine couple’s rendezvous.

83\. **Ganthuli** One spring day a great Holi battle raged in Ganthuli. Intoxicated by the fun, some girls decided to tie the divine couple’s garments together with a knot (gantha) so that They could not escape. This is also the place where Caitanya Mahaprabhu could see Gopala when He left His hilltop temple.

84\. **Gulala-kunda** This lake at Ganthuli was an ideal place for the girls and the boys to play Holi. It is said that in spring coloured dyes are visible in the waters, the sounds of children playing Holi permeate the air, and the trees are dusted with gulala.

85\. **Govardhana town** First Haryasva built his palace in the valley between two peaks of Govardhana, later Saubhari Muni lived here with his fifty wives in great opulence. Another famous resident of the town was Artha Vanta, whose desire to see Lord Balarama was fulfilled when Nityananda Prabhu came to Vraja. Moksa-kunda and one of the resting places of Lord Caitanya are nearby.

86\. **Sakhivana** The forest is famous for its large-leafed palasa trees. Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami once rejected the buttermilk served in a leaf-cup from this forest, for it is known as the residence of Candravali. The village where Govardhana Malla and his wife Candravali live is called Sakhi-sthali.

87\. **Sakhi-kunda (Lilavati-kunda)** This lake was excavated by Candravali and her girlfriends in much the same way as Radha-kunda was by Radha and Her gopi friends. During Uddhava’s visit, he would sometimes talk about Krsna’s pastimes to the gopis on the banks of this lake.

88\. **Uddhava-kunda** The lake that is strongly related to Kusuma-sarovara. This is where Uddhava narrated Srimad-Bhagavatam to the queens of Dvaraka and where he lives eternally as grass.

89\. **Sakaroya** After Giriraja was put to its place, Indra came to Vraja and realised what terrible damage he could have done. Indra - also known as Sakra - was overcome by fear at this place.

90\. **Saunkarai** At this place the gopis extracted an oath from Krsna. He swore on all the cows of Nanda Maharaja that He would never know any girl but Radha.

91\. **Nimavana** Nimbarka acarya, known as Niyamananda, used to live in this forest. He once had a debate with a Jain sannyasi who was forbidden to eat in the evening. Some say that Nimbarka climbed a nima tree to hold the sun (arka) above the horizon so that the sannyasi could complete his meal without breaking his vow; others say that he invoked the Sudarsana cakra, which blazed as brightly as the sun, so that his guest could eat.

92\. **Nima-grama (Nirmancana-grama)** After Krsna replaced Govardhana Hill and the danger was over, at this very spot His mother and the elderly gopis embraced Him, kissed His face, and worshipped Him. One offers obeisances to sacred Yoga-pitha from here before starting the parikrama of Radha-kunda.

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Yoga-pitha** The favourite abode of Lord Govinda that is not different from His transcendental body. It stands upon the summit of Vrndavana, rising out of the waters of the Yamuna. In the centre of its thousand-petalled lotus is the throne on which Radha and Krsna sit in the circle of the asta-sakhis. In New Vraja-dhama, the general area of Radha-Syamasundara’s temple covers the inner petals of the Yoga-pitha lotus with Radha-Syama in its centre.

2\. **Radha-kunda area (Srivana)** Radha-kunda and Syama-kunda in the temple courtyard, the temple of Radha-Syamasundara itself, the terraces surrounding it, and the area encompassing the inner petals of Yoga-pitha are combinedly called the Radha-kunda area or Radha-kunda of New Vraja-dhama.

3\. **Ramesvara Mahadeva** The guardian of Siva-khor stands at the entrance of the Radha-kunda area. When approaching Radha-kunda on Govardhana parikrama one passes by here. After offering obeisances to Ramesvara, one should offer prayers here to the nearby primary guardian of Radha-kunda, Kundesvara Mahadeva, begging his permission to enter the area.

4\. **Siva-khor** Once Radha sent Lalita-devi to Siva-khor to save a female jackal from mischievous children. By Lalita’s touch, the jackal realised its nature as Krsna’s servant and soon assumed a gopi form.

5\. **Patala (Patalavana)** A beautiful garden with pink roses on the eastern petal of Yoga-pitha. This is where Radha and the gopis come to collect flowers and string garlands.

6\. **Malyahari-kunda** The gopis come here to make garlands for their worship of the sun god. This is also where Krsna once begged the gopis for a few pearls but they refused, inspiring Him to plant the pearls of Nanda’s treasury.

7\. **Mukta-ksetra** “The place of pearls” at Malyahari-kunda where the gopis once strung pearl necklaces for the cows as part of the Dipavali and Govardhana-puja celebrations.

8\. **Tulasi greenhouse** Where the deity of Vrnda-devi and Radha-Syama’s tulasi plants live in New Vraja-dhama. During the winter months the deities of Gopisvara Mahadeva and his consort Yogamaya also reside here.

9\. **Navagrama (Kunjera)** The place of a special elephant. This elephant was made of gopis who acrobatically arranged themselves into its different parts, and which Krsna mounted and drove hither and thither using Cupid’s goad.

10\. **Sankarsana-Balarama’s grove** A serene white grove full of sandalwood trees. Lord Sankarsana sits on a moonstone throne within a moonstone palace, eyes rolling, intoxicated from drinking varuni.

11\. **Svananda-sukhada-kunja** The cottage where Bhaktivinoda Thakura performed his bhajana and eternally resides as Kamala-manjari. Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura also used it as his bhajana-kutira.

12\. **Jagannatha temple** Where Lord Jagannatha, Lord Balarama, and Subhadra Maharani reside.

13\. **Vrsabhanu-kunda** When Maharaja Vrsabhanu and his townspeople came to observe the worship of Govardhana Hill they camped by the banks of this lake.

14\. **Lalita-kunda** Once Krsna and Lalita called upon eighty-four holy places to excavate and fill a lake that could wash one clean of the sinful reactions of committing six kinds of murder. Lalita-bihari temple is nearby the lake.

15\. **Mohana-kunda** Lalita’s lake containing eighty-four holy places overflowed and formed another lake, which she named Mohana-kunda after Krsna. After killing Aristasura, Krsna bathed in these two lakes to purify Himself.

16\. **Balarama-kunda** In the centre of Sankarsana-Balarama’s and Revati’s grove sits refreshing Balarama-kunda.

17\. **Seven sakhis’ lakes** Close to the famous Mohana-kunda and Lalita-kunda are the lakes of Radha’s seven other girlfriends.

18\. **Gopi-kupa** The gopis come here to fill their pitchers; Krsna comes to beg the gopis for water. When the well was excavated by the order of Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami, the workers found a bleeding stone: the injured tongue of Govardhana Hill. The temples of Radha-Vinoda and Radha- Govinda of Manipur’s king are nearby.

19\. **Madhavendra Puri-baithaka** The spot nearby Syama-kunda where Madhavendra Puri rested on his parikrama around Govardhana Hill.

20\. **Narayana-sarovara** Once, to surprise the gopis, Krsna playfully manifested the magnificent four-armed form of Lord Narayana at this place.

21\. **Bankhandi Mahadeva** One of the guards of the Radha-kunda area.

22\. **Lagmohana-kunda** Krsna killed the demon Sankhacuda here, at the end of a long battle that began in Syamavana after the demon tried to abduct Srimati Radharani. The lake is also a meeting place of Radha and Krsna.

23\. **Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami’s bhajana-sthana** Dasa Gosvami lived at Lagmohana-kunda until Sanatana Gosvami convinced him to move to Radha-kunda.

24\. **Brahma’s dwelling place** The creator’s residence is situated on the tip of Yoga-pitha’s western petal.

25\. **Anjanavana (Anjanaka)** Here Krsna personally applied anjana to Radha’s eyes when Sri Radha had rushed so much to find Her lover after hearing His flute that She had left Her grove without decorating Her right eye.

26\. **Radha-Syamasundara temple building** The building stands in the centre of New Vraja-dhama. Its three main parts are Radha-kunda and Syama-kunda in the temple courtyard, and the temple of Radha-Syamasundara itself. One enters the building via the main entrance at Anjanavana and leaves the same way after visiting the pastime places inside, ending with Haridasa Svami’s bhajana-sthana.

27\. **Radha-kunda (Kancana-kunda / Sri-kunda)** Radha-kunda was dug out by the gopis and filled by the holy waters, who appeared in person and requested residency in Radha’s lake. It is the most sacred of all places and a favourite playground of the divine couple. The lake is surrounded by the groves of the asta-sakhis and the asta-manjaris. The island grove at its centre is the residence of Ananga-manjari, Salila-kamala-kunja.

28\. **Rasavedi-ghata** The place of the rasa dance at Radha’s lake. In unmanifest forms the groves of Indulekha, Rangadevi, and Campakalata respectively decorate its two corners and centre. Ratyambuja-kunja, Kasturyanandada-kunja, and Gunanandaprada-kunja, the groves of three manjaris are connected to the residences of these sakhis.

29\. **Jhulana-ghata** At this side of Radha- kunda there was once a banyan tree between Tungavidyanandada-kunja and Sudevi-sukhada- kunja where Radha and Krsna would enjoy swing pastimes. The groves of Manjulali-manjari and Lavanga-manjari are also situated at this part of the lake.

30\. **Jhulana-sthali** The spot where the divine couple once enjoyed Their pastimes on a swing with eighteen seats—two for Radha and Krsna and sixteen for the eight sakhis and the eight manjaris. The forest here is called Varsa-harsa, “the happiness of the rainy season,” because whenever Radha and Krsna want to swing, Vraja provides clear skies and summer warmth, even in the rainy season.

31\. **Radha-kanta temple** The temple and Vakresvara Pandita’s puspa-samadhi are near the grove of Tungavidya.

32\. **Jahnava-ghata** When Jahnava-devi visited Vrndavana she stayed at Radha-kunda for four days to meet and inspire the resident Vaisnavas including Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami. This was her bathing place at Radha-kunda.

33\. **Jahnava-baithaka** The sitting place of Lord Nityananda’s consort at Jahnava-ghata. One day, as Jahnava-devi sat on the shore of the lake, Krsna appeared before her in His threefold-bending form.

34\. **Radha-Gopinatha temple** The temple was built to commemorate Jahnava-devi’s darsana of Gopinatha at the banks of Radha-kunda.

35\. **Dasa Gosvami-ghata** This ghata at Radha-kunda is so called because the samadhi of Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami—the first resident of the lake — is situated here.

36\. **Govinda-ghata** The shoreline of Radha-kunda between Visakhanandada-kunja and Citranandada-kunja with the relating groves of Vilasa-manjari and Rasa-manjari. Once, while bathing here, Sanatana Gosvami had a vision of Radharani and the gopis in which the Gosvami saw that Radha’s braid did indeed resemble a black serpent, just as Sri Rupa Gosvami had said in his Catu-puspanjali.

37\. **Radha-Syamasundara temple** The temple with Radha-Syama in the heart of the Yoga- pitha of New Vraja-dhama corresponds to Govinda-sthali and the Radha-Govinda temple of Vrndavana. After offering obeisances to the Deities, one visits the pastime places of Radha- kunda and Yoga-pitha located inside the temple.

38\. **Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami’s samadhi** Dasa Gosvami considered Radha-kunda his eternal home and only residence. His samadhi was placed near the groves of Lalita, Visakha, and Rupa-manjari to fulfil his desire to live forever on the banks of the lake.

39\. **Radha-Syamasundara’s altar** Radha-Syamasundara are the root of the lotus of New Vraja-dhama, the nucleus of the Yoga-pitha, wherein Syamasundara is also known by names of Madana-mohana, Govindaji, and Gopinatha. Govardhana Lala and Gaura Nataraja also stand on Radha-Syamasundara’s altar.

40\. **Gaurakisora Dasa Babaji’s samadhi** The Babaji was famous for his austere life. His puspa-samadhi was established by his only disciple, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura. The Radha-Kunjabihari temple is nearby.

41\. **Nrsimhadeva temple** Laksmi-Nrsimha and Sudarsana reside here. Laksmi-Nrsimha arrived at New Vraja-dhama with Indradyumna Swami and in a dream declared His desire to stay and protect Krsna Valley. Sudarsana also has a small form of a seated Nrsimha on his back.

42\. **Srinivasa Acarya-baithaka** Srinivasa Acarya did not live directly on the banks of Radha-kunda as his predecessors did, but considering himself their servant, he stayed a respectful distance behind. The temple of Radha-Madhava is at the same place.

43\. **Narahari Sarakara’s samadhi** When Lord Caitanya departed from the world, His intimate associate — the spiritual master of Locana Dasa Thakura—retired to Radha-kunda.

44\. **Prabhupada deity** Srila Prabhupada sits at the rear of the temple room facing the altar so he always has darsana of Radha-Syamasundara.

45\. **Radha-Govinda temple** Radha and Govinda were installed after the original Deities of Vrndavana had briefly stayed at Radha-kunda on Their way to the safety of Jaipur. The temple marks the divine couple’s daily meeting and is known as one of the Yoga-pithas of Radha-kunda.

46\. **Jihva-sila temple** The tongue of Govardhana Hill is worshipped in this small temple.

47\. **Gaura-Gadadhara temple** The temple was the place of service and residence of two saintly ladies, Gatiga-mata Gosvamini and Laksmipriya-devi.

48\. **Krsnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami’s bhajana-kutira** The composer of Caitanya-caritamrta came to Radha-kunda at an early age on the order of Lord Nityananda and lived there at Manasa-pavana-ghata in the vicinity of Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami and Gopala Bhatta Gosvami.

49\. **Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami’s bhajana-kutira** The first resident of Radha-kunda, Dasa Gosvami performed his bliajana in the open air, but after two special incidents Sanatana Gosvami convinced him to accept a kutira. Once Krsna appeared to protect the meditating saint from tigers; on another occasion Radha held Her cloth above his head to shield him from the sun’s rays.

50\. **Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura’s bhajana-kutira** The acarya composed many books at Radha-kunda. Once in a dream Sri Radha helped him understand the Kama-gayatri mantra and inspired him to write Mantrartha-dipika.

51\. **Gopala Bhatta Gosvami’s bhajana-kutira** Gopala Bhatta often visited the other Gosvamis also living at Manasa-pavana-ghata. When Krsnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami began to write Caitanya- caritdmrta, he begged the author not to mention him in the book.

52\. **Syama-kunda (Krsna-kunda / Vajra-kunda)** Radha insisted that Krsna should atone for the sin of killing a bull, after He had slain Aristasura. Krsna called all holy waters of the universe and bathed in the pond they formed to purify Himself.

53\. **Manasa-pavana-ghata** The bathing place at Subalanandada-kunja. Radha and the gopis take their midday bath at this spot while Krsna hides on the branches of a nearby tree to spy on the girls.

54\. **Pilu bush** As a result of his austerities, a Vaisnava brahmana became a pilu bush by the bank of Syama-kunda at Subalanandada-kunja, from where he eternally witnesses Krsna’s pastimes and serves the gopis by holding their clothes as they bathe.

55\. **Tin-gosvami-samadhi** The place where three (tin) Gosvamis—Raghunatha Bhatta, Raghunatha Dasa, and Krsnadasa Kaviraja — were cremated and a portion of their ashes interred. Jiva Gosvami had the balance of their remains individually entombed at other places in Vrndavana.

56\. **Panca-pandava-ghata** During the excavation of Syama-kunda, Dasa Gosvami decided to cut a few trees, but Maharaja Yudhisthira appeared in his dream and revealed that he and his brothers resided in the bodies of those trees. Today only Arjuna’s tree is still manifest. Madhumangala’s kunja corresponds to this part of Syama-kunda.

57\. **Jiva Gosvami-ghata** Jiva Gosvami played an important role in the excavation of Radha-kunda and Syama-kunda. The ghata of Syama-kunda named after him stretches from Ujjvala’s grove to the grove of Arjuna-gopa.

58\. **Jiva Gosvami’s bhajana-kutira** A short distance from the bank of Jiva Gosvami-ghata is a small cluster of bakula trees, where Jiva Gosvami would stay on his visits to Radha-kunda.

59\. **Nandini-devi’s samadhi** Nandini-devi’s was a maidservant to the wife of Advaita Acarya, Sita Thakurani, and a great devotee in her own right.

60\. **Gaya-ghata** The bathing place between Jiva Gosvami-ghata and Asta-sakhi-ghata.

61\. **Asta-sakhi-ghata** The bathing place next to Gaya-ghata where a temple dedicated to Radha- Krsna and the asta-sakhis stands.

62\. **Radha-Madhava temple** When Jayadeva Gosvami visited Vrndavana he brought Radha- Madhava with him and installed Them at Kesi- ghata. This temple is home to the pratibhu-murtis of Jayadeva Gosvami’s Deities. The temple dedicated to Gaura-Nitai with Advaita is nearby.

63\. **Jayadeva Gosvami’s samadhi** The author of Gita-govinda who, while being a devotee of Jagannatha, worshipped Deities of Radha-Madhava. His puspa-samadhi was established at the Radha-Madhava temple to honour the owner of the Deities.

64\. **Tamala-tala-ghata** After discovering the two divine lakes of Radha and Syama, Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu rested beneath a large tamala tree at the grove of Gandharva-sakha and remembered the pastimes Krsna and the gopis once enjoyed here.

65\. **Mahaprabhu-baithaka** The sitting place of Caitanya Mahaprabhu at Tamala-tala-ghata.

66\. **Pasa-khela-ghata (Vallabha-ghata)** The divine couple usually play the dice game of pasa-khela in Sudevi’s grove, but once, expecting victory by choosing a favourable place, Krsna challenged Srimati Radharani to play in Vidagdha-sakha’s grove at Syama-kunda.

67\. **Madana-mohana-ghata** The bathing place of Syama-kunda where the Madana-mohana temple and the sitting place of Sanatana Gosvami stand.

68\. **Sanatana Gosvami-baithaka** Sanatana Gosvami daily circumambulated Govardhana Hill and visited Radha-kunda. During these visits he sat here beneath a tree meditating upon the divine couple or advising the local devotees. Kokila-gopa tends his grove nearby, at the corner of Syama-kunda.

69\. **Gaura-Nitai temple** Every year the temple custodians hold a swing festival for the pleasure of the Deities and the residents.

70\. **Yugala-sangama-ghata** On either side of the walkway separating Radha-kunda from Syama- kunda is Yugala-sangama-ghata. The groves of Citra-gopi and Sananda-gopa correspond to the central part of the ghata.

71\. **Giriraja Maharaja temple** A large number of Govardhana-Silas are piled up here. Every evening one of the silas is dressed and decorated.

72\. **Ratna-vedi (Sangama)** At the centre of Yugala-sangama-ghata is the Yoga-pitha of Radha- kunda, the meeting place (sangama) where the liquid forms of the divine couple, Radha-kunda and Syama-kunda, embrace. It is also called “the jewelled altar,” Ratna-vedi.

73\. **Carana-cihna** In the very heart of Ratna-vedi the lotus footprints of Radha and Syamasundara mark the place where the divine couple stand and marvel at the beauty of Their lakes and each other.

74\. **Banke-bihari temple** Banke-bihari stayed here after fleeing from Vrndavana, returning to Vrndavana when a new temple was constructed for Him.

75\. **Haridasa Svami’s bhajana-sthana** The place where Haridasa Svami performed his bhajana was between Carana-cihna and Govinda-ghata where the temple of Banke-bihari stands.

76\. **Imli-tala (Amli-tala)** The ancient tamarind tree at Radha-kunda where Riipa Gosvami once sat to chant Hare Krsna and witnessed the swing pastimes of Radha and Krsna.

77\. **Radha-Gopinatha temple** The pratibhiis of Madhu Pandita’s Deities are worshipped here.

78\. **Nityananda-baithaka** Lord Nityananda visited Vrndavana during His twenty-year pilgrimage of Bharata-varsa. His sitting place is found in the courtyard of Radha-Gopinatha’s temple.

79\. **Mayura-kunda** Hoping to expose Krsna as a libertine, Indra ordered many Apsaras to entice Him, but Krsna showed complete indifference and transformed Himself into a peacock.

80\. **Anantadeva’s abode** On the tip of the northern petal of Yoga-pitha sits Lord Ananta, who with His thousand mouths constantly recites the glories of Sri Krsna.

81\. **Radha-Gokulananda temple** The pratibhiis of Radha-Gokulananda are worshipped here.

82\. **Lokanatha Gosvami’s bhajana-kutira** An underground cave at Radha-Gokulananda temple where Lokanatha Gosvami performed his bhajana.

83\. **Purana Radha-Krsna temple** The first temple constructed at Radha-kunda for the Deities Dasa Gosvami found while excavating the lake. In the temple courtyard stand two entwined trees, a banyan and a nima, representing Radha and Syama.

84\. **Kundesvara Mahadeva** The primary guardian of Radha-kunda; the divine gatekeeper from whom one must beg permission to enter the area. Those who perform parikrama solely of the Radha-kunda area begin and end their trek here.

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Phakarapura** Leaving Vrndavana town at Damodaravana, one can climb to Kamaravana and Carana-pahari and then take the path leading to the pastime places on the eastern petals of New Vraja-dhama. One of the places on the way is Phakarapura, where the cowherd boys often play. Once, simply by playing His flute, Krsna made a small lake in order to quench the thirst of His cowherd friends.

2\. **Kamaravana** The forest in which Kamara, Durvasa-kunda, and an asrama of Durvasa Muni are found.

3\. **Kamara (Kamera)** This is where once, during winter, Krsna placed His folded blanket (kamara) on a rock, sat on it, and waited for Radha to come. Krsna’s kama was so intense that it can still be felt here today.

4\. **Durvasa-kunda** Durvasa Muni’s asrama is here. It was nearby at Kamara where Krsna gifted His blanket to Durvasa. The sage knew that by wearing it he would be freed from the cycle of birth and death.

5\. **Carana-pahari** Krsna climbed this hill and sounded His flute in such a way that not only the boys and cows but also the villagers and forest animals flocked to Him. Their hearts melted, as did the stones, which sank into the softened ground of the mountainside, and thus the imprints of all those who stood there can be seen.

6\. **Carana-ganga** After causing a divine trance with His flute-song for the living entities surrounding Him, Krsna called for the Ganges, which burst through stone and filled a large depression to form a pond where Krsna and the gopas could cleanse their feet of stone particles.

7\. **Khitavita** Seeing the cowherd boys deeply absorbed in their games, Krsna was apprehensive that there would be a quarrel about who was actually victorious.

8\. **Maharana** The birthplace and parental home of Yasoda. The barns of Krsna’s elder uncle, Abhinandana, are also here.

9\. **Bhadokhara** This is where Krsna’s maternal grandfather, Mahananda, was born.

10\. **Dig (Dirghapura / Lauhavana)** The northernmost location from where one walks towards south on the eastern petals of New Vraja-dhama. This place has no special religious significance. Once a king of Dig decreed that pilgrims who did not pass through the town would be forcibly brought there by soldiers armed with canes.

11\. **Didavali** The birthplace of Nanda Maharaja’s mother, Variyasi-devi, where Krsna worships this best (variyasi) of gopis, His grandmother (didima).

12\. **Maharaniya** The gopis sat Radharani upon a golden throne and decorated Her profusely with floral ornaments. Appearing as the queen of spring, the girls called Her Maharani and named the place where She was adorned “Maharaniya.”

13\. **Gohana** The birthplace of Sudama. In childhood Krsna and Balarama would herd Their cows here, and Sudama would happily join the two brothers, who were his very life.

14\. **Kalyana** A pasture with sweet grasses favoured by Krsna’s cows. When Krsna and Balarama once rested here the gopas jokingly offered Them dandavats and asked the brothers for blessings. The cows bowed down as well.

15\. **Adi-badri (Setukandara)** When the Vrajavasls decided to go on pilgrimage in the Himalayas, Krsna explained that all holy places are present in Vraja. He took the Vraja-vasis to Adi-badri, the original form of the Himalayan Badarinatha, where they saw the Deity of Badarlnatha. At the same place Krsna once showed His four-armed Nara-Narayana Rsi form to the gopis. Adi-badri is home to many sacred places: the forest where Pandu and Madri performed austerities; where the Pandavas passed by on their way to heaven; where Bhima met Hanuman; where Srila Vyasadeva compiled the Vedic literature; and where Krsna sent Uddhava to teach the secrets of vraja-bhakti.

16\. **Tapovana** The forest where Nara-Narayana Rsi performs austerities even today. Once Indra sent Cupid with beautiful damsels to bewilder the Lord, but He could not be tempted by the celestial enticements. The forest is surrounded by the mountains Gandhamadana, Kesava, Nisadha, and Sankhakuta.

17\. **Gauri-kunda** The Vraja-vasis took bath in this lake of Mother Durga when Krsna took them on pilgrimage to Adi-badri.

18\. **Tapta-kunda** Though of moderate temperature and not hot, as its name suggests, this lake is still more purifying than its Himalayan counterpart.

19\. **Alipura** Below the hills of Adi-badri is a village that was previously also named Adi-badri.

20\. **Pahalabada** Here the cowherd boys, altogether, challenged Krsna to a wrestling match. Unsure who should lead the charge, the boys called out, "You attack first (pahila bada).” During the match, Krsna expanded Himself to fight with each boy individually.

21\. **Bhayari** One day the gopis were so deeply absorbed in their play that they lost track of time, and although they set off on their way home during daylight, the winter night fell early. This is the place where they stood in fear (bhaya) in the darkness.

22\. **Kayarika** The gopis became stuck in the dark forest and lost their way at Bhayari, and so they called out for Krsna. When their hero appeared they convinced Him to light up the forest with the effulgence of His body (haya) by saying, “We know that You are the source of the Brahman effulgence.”

23\. **Pasupavana (Pasupa)** Where Krsna and Balarama once hid from Their friends, but the clever cows discovered Their hiding place by following the two brothers’ bodily aroma.

24\. **Virara** A resting place of Krsna that was carfully prepared for Him by the loving hands of the gopis. The girls made a thick mattress of aromatic flowers and covered it with a spotless white cotton sheet.

25\. **Badali** The cowherd boys named this place Badali, because it was here that Krsna called for clouds and a cooling rainfall (badala) with His flute-song to ease the unbearable heat at midday.

26\. **Karamuka** One of the many places where Radha and Krsna enact Their pastime of meeting.

27\. **Kanabadi** In this village Krsna went door to door looking for a missing gopa. He asked everyone, “Is this the house of My friend?” (kena badi).

28\. **Papadivana (Papadi)** Where the gopis decorated Radha and Krsna with petal ornaments and made a throne, umbrella, and festoons from flower petals.

29\. **Kedaranatha** The place where the Pandavas came to free themselves of sins incurred during the Battle of Kuruksetra. Sripada Sankaracarya’s samadhi'mandira is here, where he left this world.

30\. **Kedaranatha temple** The Pandavas wanted to take shelter of Lord Siva after the Battle of Kuruksetra, but Siva, afraid of the sinful reactions, assumed the form of a bull and ran away. Where the temple stands he dived into the ground, leaving only his hump exposed.

31\. **Satavasa** Srimati Radharani comes here to worship the sun. One Surya-kunda is located at Satavasa.

32\. **Surya-kunda** Satrajit constructed a temple for the sun god on the bank of this lake where he worshipped the deity to attain immeasurable wealth and an incomparable child.

33\. **Sita-Rama garden (Pancavati forest)** A garden where Radha and Krsna enacted pastimes of the exiled king and queen of Ayodhya, Lord Rama and Sita-devi.

34\. **Vanacariya** A garden where, in Krsna’s absence, the cowherd boys once discussed His varna, wondering whether Krsna was actually a cowherd or a forest dweller (vana-cari).

35\. **Pai** In this area Krsna and the gopis once played a game called pai, or what we know as hide-and-seek. When Radha was the seeker the sound of Her voice singing Hare Krsna helped Her catch Krsna.

36\. **Calana-sila** Krsna stood mesmerised by the voice of Sri Radha at this place while She was approaching Him singing Hare Krsna. This is how Radha could catch Her beloved and was thus victorious in the game they played with the gopis at Pai.

37\. **Parehi** In this grove Krsna told the gopis that He was fed up with their taunting and threatened that if they joked with Him again they would not see Him afterwards (pare). Although it is a beautiful place, they never returned.

38\. **Nai** While Krsna meets the gopis in this part of the dhama, Balarama and the cowherd boys tend their cows around Nai, playing and resting on the banks of beautiful Sankarsana-kunda.

39\. **Sankarsana-kunda** When Balarama comes to Nai with the gopas, He takes a short nap at this lake on a jewelled platform beneath a tree whose arching branches sweep the ground. The boys search for fruits and play various games here.

40\. **Srngaravana** The forest famous for three locations of one pastime: Niganya, Tiloyara, and Sringara-vata.

41\. **Niganya** Once the gopis were on their way to Kosi when Krsna appeared and convinced them that they needed a chaperone. When the gopis realised that Krsna had taken them on a detour they forcibly stopped their guide and cross- examined Him here.

42\. **Tiloyara** In this grove Radha and Krsna once engaged in an intense romantic exchange without the slightest (tila) interruption or disturbance. This took place after Krsna had misguided the gopis, who were about to visit Kosikala.

43\. **Srngara-vata (Srngara)** Where Krsna decorated Radharani with flower ornaments while She was sitting on a swing hanging from a majestic banyan tree. Fully ornamented, Radha appeared like a floral goddess — truly the queen of Vrndavana.

44\. **Vichora** One night, while the gopis were totally immersed in dancing and sankirtana of Krsna’s names and glories, He confidently snuck away to the grove where Sri Radha was waiting.

45\. **Vasosi** This place is so named because it bears the fragrance (vasa) of Krsna’s body, which perfected Vaisnavas can still perceive today.

46\. **Rasoli** A rasa dance was held beneath a large banyan tree here; the dancers twisted and wove in a zigzag pattern around the tangled hanging roots of the gigantic tree.

47\. **Rasa-kunda** After the rasa dance at Rasoli, Krsna encouraged the gopis to refresh themselves in the cool waters of this lake, where they played various water games. They relished a picnic afterwards and rested in banyan cottages.

48\. **Patharali (Prana-vallabha)** The gopis once came here with Krsna to fill their pitchers with mineral water. While the girls were busy at the spring, Krsna left for a walk. After returning He saw the gopis standing in a circle around an imaginary Krsna whom they envisioned in their ecstasy while waiting for Him.

49\. **Letari** Here the gopas, exhausted from herding cows, decided to stop. They sat to eat in concentric circles, drank from a nearby pond, then rested; some of the boys fed Krsna betel, massaged His feet, and cooled Him with a peacock fan. From here one returns to Kamaravana. Those who follow the parikrama route of New Vraja-dhama walk further to Nidhuvana where one starts the parikrama of Vrndavana town.

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Vrndavana forest** One of the twelve main forests of Vrndavana, but so special that it lends its name to all of Vraja. Its name is from two sources: the daughter of Kedara, called Vrnda, who attained perfection in this forest, and Tulasi who performed austerities here. Its origin can also be one of Radha’s names as She is known as one who is always surrounded by a multitude (vrnda) of gopis. After constructing Vrndavana town, Visvakarma decorated Vrndavana forest, as he knew that Radha and Krsna would enjoy Their love-sports in its groves.

2\. **Vrndavana town** One part of Vrndavana forest. The original town was constructed by Visvakarma during the night the Vraja-vasis arrived here from Gokula. The opulences of Nandagrama, the Lord’s eternal residence, appeared here in a different form, with Chatikara at its centre. Vrndavana town today is the area in Vraja which was most frequented by our acaryas.

3\. **Nidhuvana** The first pastime place on the parikrama of Vrndavana town. At the beginning of the saradiya-rasa dance the gopis became a little proud, therefore Krsna disappeared with Radharani. While the gopis searched after Him, the divine couple enjoyed Their loving pastimes in the groves of this forest. Radha and Krsna regularly meet here at night. Sri Radha once dreamt of Lord Caitanya here as the combined form of Her and Krsna.

4\. **Rang-mahal** This jewelled pavilion is a favourite resting place of Radha and Krsna where They come after Their nocturnal rasa dance. As dawn approaches, the gopis dress the divine couple and offer Them mangala-arati, but sometimes Krsna decorates Radharani during this morning period.

5\. **Visakha-kunda** Once, when the divine couple and the gopis were in Nidhuvana, Visakha complained of being thirsty. Krsna poked the ground with His flute and created a pond of drinking water.

6\. **Svami Haridasa’s bhajana-sthana** The Vaisnava saint lived in Nidhuvana where he worshipped the divine couple by composing devotional songs. One day Krsna appeared to Him in the form of a Deity whom he named Kunja-bihari, today known as Banke-bihari.

7\. **Radharani temple** The temple commemorates the pastime when Radharani taught Krsna flute lessons in a grove of Nidhuvana. Svami Haridasa’s samadhi is nearby.

8\. **Galis** The path the gopis take to come from Yavata to Seva-kunja is called Kunja-gali. Dana-gali is the path where Krsna once asked Radha for a toll to pass through here. Radha completely ignored Krsna’s demands and avoided Him on Gumana-gali. Mana-gali is the path Radha once used when, in a huff, She left Seva-kunja for Mana-sarovara.

9\. **Nikunjavana** This forest, more popularly known as Seva-kunja, is most famous as the site of the saradiya-rasa dance.

10\. **Seva-kunja** Also known as Nikunjavana, where the saradiya-rasa dance began when the gopis made Krsna re-appear by their devotional songs. Originally the grove was named so because, at the end of the festival, Krsna personally served Radha by dressing and ornamenting Her and massaging Her feet. A small Rang-mahal temple commemorates this pastime.

11\. **Lalita-kunda** During a lull in the saradrya-rasa dance, Lalita-devi told Krsna that she was thirsty. To satisfy her, Krsna dug His heel into the ground, where a fountain of nectar bubbled up and formed a little pond for drinking.

12\. **Rasa-mandala** One of the sites of the saradiya-rasa dance. The path of the dance festival snakes through the entire area.

13\. **Radha-ramana temple** Radha-ramana appeared from one of the salagrama-silas of Gopala Bhatta Gosvami. The Gosvami desired a Deity with a face like Govindaji’s, a chest like Gopinatha’s, and feet like Madana-mohana’s.

14\. **Sapta-samudrika-kupa** This well was established when Indra’s charioteer brought water from the seven seas to bathe Krsna after He lifted Govardhana.

15\. **Paurnamasi's residence** Paurnamasi always resides here at Seva-kunja. Annapurna-devi, the goddess of grains or nourishment—as she is known in Varanasi—is also present here.

16\. **Kisoravana** The forest at Seva-kunja where Advaita Acarya had his residence below the Advaita-vata tree.

17\. **Advaita-vata** Advaita Acarya’s residence was below this tree. This is where Madana-gopala jumped off the altar and hid in the bushes when the Muslim soldiers came to destroy Him. The sitting place of the Acarya, Advaita-baithaka, is at the tree.

18\. **Damodaravana** In New Vraja-dhama, at the rear of the Radha-Damodara temple is a rose garden called Gula-camana, and a small lake called Gula-kunda. The landscape here resembles the enchanting scriptural descriptions of the subforests of Vraja.

19\. **Radha-Damodara temple** Rupa Gosvami personally designed, carved, and then offered the Deity of Damodara to Jiva Gosvami, who later erected a temple for Him. The temple is famous as Rupa’s and Jiva’s headquarters and as Srila Prabhupada’s bhajana-sthana. The Radha- Damodara Deities in New Vraja-dhama were the object of Svarupa Damodara Gosvami’s personal worship. Damodara holds the flute to His left side as if offering it to Sri Radha. A Govardhana-sila called “Little Giri” is also worshipped here in New Vraja-dhama, just as Sanatana Gosvami’s Govardhana-sila is worshipped in the Radha- Damodara temple of Vrndavana.

20\. **Meeting place of the Six Gosvamis** The Gosvamis would meet at the Radha-Damodara temple, discuss how to spread Krsna consciousness, and honour prasada.

21\. **Bada-bathain (Balabhadra-kunda)** The place where young boys and girls would come on the day after Holi. The girls would carry bamboo sticks, hoping to strike the boys for their teasing during the year. It is also a place where Nanda Maharaja would meet the senior cowherd men to discuss community matters.

22\. **Rupa Gosvami’s samadhi** Rupa Gosvami came to Vrndavana on the order of Lord Caitanya to discover the lost pastime places and to write books about devotional service to Radha and Krsna. He established the worship of Govindaji. When Rupa Gosvami left this world his only disciple, Jiva Gosvami, oversaw the construction of his samadhi, which is near the Radha-Damodara temple.

23\. **Krsna-kunda** On the bank of this lake there is a garden called Harayola where the divine couple play a variety of table games, such as dice.

24\. **Nikunja-mandira** At the bank of Krsna-kunda is this grove which serves as a resting place for the divine couple. After being defeated in dice Krsna was invited here for a contest of love.

25\. **Nipavana** The forest where a secluded lake, Krsna-kunda, is located.

26\. **Krsna-kunda in Nipavana** The lake provides a place for water play for Radha and Krsna in summer.

27\. **Jiva Gosvami’s samadhi** After completing his studies in Varanasi, Jiva joined Rupa and Sanatana in Vrndavana. He was selected as the acarya of all Gaudiya Vaisnavas after his uncles’ disappearance. Known as the foremost scholar, devotees came to him to learn philosophy and theology. According to his will, Jiva Gosvami’s sacred body was interred close to Radha and Damodara.

28\. **Krsnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami’s samadhi** The samadhi of the author of Sri Caitanya-caritamrta is near the temple of Radha-Damodara.

29\. **Bedokhora-kunja** Radha and Krsna keep the doors of this grove closed, and its walls are difficult to see through. Thus the pastimes They perform here are known only to Them and Their most intimate devotees.

30\. **Chota-baithana** Krsna and Balarama rested here while tending Their cows. Then, after regaining Their strength, They played games as Their herd grazed peacefully.

31\. **Kuntala-kunda** The gopas came here to bathe after playing in the dust, then decorated Krsna’s hair (kuntala) with feathers and flowers. On another occasion Radha’s hair was decorated here by Her maidservants.

32\. **Kanaka-kunja** Syamananda Prabhu serves the divine couple as Kanaka-manjari in this sacred grove.

33\. **Tilaka-sthana** While sweeping Seva-kunja, Syamananda Prabhu found the ankle bells of Sri Radha that he personally delivered to Her by the intercession of Lalita-devi. Radha, pleased by his service, gave him a tilaka stamp, a Deity of Krsna, and a new name.

34\. **Syamananda Pandita’s samadhi** After studying under Jiva Gosvami, Syamananda left Vrndavana for Orissa to preach, as Sri Radha ordered. Even the most inimical Sher Khan requested initiation from him. The Radha-Syamasundara temple in Vrndavana, where many years later Baladeva Vidyabhusana became a steward, was established by Syamananda Pandita.

35\. **Ramana-reti** Upon arriving at their favourite playground on their cow-herding tours the gopas would burst into the open space dancing, singing, and rolling on the ground. They would enjoy various diversions here like wrestling or catch.

36\. **Prabhupadavana** This forest in New Vraja-dhama is so named because the Krsna-Balarama Mandira and Srila Prabhupada’s samadhi are located here.

37\. **Krsna-Balarama Mandira** The ISKCON temple founded by Srila Prabhupada in Ramana-reti. As Krsna and Balarama would play and tend cows in this area, Prabhupada established Them on the central altar, with Gaura-Nitai on the left, and Radha-Syamasundara on the right. The tamalaVrndavcnatree in the courtyard of the temple is said to be a meeting place of Radha and Krsna.

38\. **Prabhupada’s samadhi** His Divine Grace A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada made Krsna and Vrndavana known throughout the world. His samadhi is in the Krsna-Balarama Mandira.

39\. **Gajendra-moksana-kunda** The lake at Rangaji temple where the deliverance of Gajendra the elephant is enacted yearly. This pastime instructs us to always place the service of the Supreme Lord before our own needs.

40\. **Krsna-Balarama tree** A white eucalyptus growing from the trunk of a black tamala symbolising the two divine brothers. It is said to be a place where Radha and Krsna meet at night.

41\. **Paksi-sthana** A bird sanctuary where Srimati Radharani once ordered a devotee called Krsna Dasa to daily feed all the birds of Sri Vrndavana-dhama.

42\. **Rasa-mandala** One of the many places that represent the arenas where the divine couple enjoy the exhilaration of the rasa dance.

43\. **Venu-kupa** When Radha became thirsty, Krsna pierced the ground with the sound of His flute. The fountain created became famous amongst the gopis as Krsna’s liquefied flute-song and they would drink from it to savour the nectar of Krsna’s lotus lips.

44\. **Visrama-kunja** One girl compared the radiant faces of Radha and Krsna to full moons—one dark and one fair — when the divine couple and the gopis rested here after strolling through the forest of Vrndavana.

45\. **Mohana-terh** One day Cupid decided that he would incite the fire of passion in Radha and Krsna while They were in the rasa dance, but the divine couple not only resisted his machinations, but made him faint in ecstasy by experiencing the attractiveness of the Lord.

46\. **Jharu-mandala (Viraha-sthana)** Devotees come here to hear hari-katha, therefore the locals keep this area clean by sweeping it with a broom (jharu). It is also called the place of separation (viraha), for it was here that Krsna disappeared when Radha was too tired to walk any further and had tried to climb up on Krsna’s shoulder. Viraha-taru, the “tree of separation,” is found here.

47\. **Footprint of Krsna** An old lady made too much noise with grinding and disturbed the devotees visiting Jharu-mandala. Krsna appeared before her and offered another way of living for the woman by pressing His foot into the grinding stone.

48\. **Asokavana** One of Vrnda-devi’s residences. It is full of birds, forest creatures, and sylvan goddesses, all ready to serve Krsna according to Vrnda’s order. Radha and Krsna sometimes come here to collect flowers for Their Sanji decorations.

49\. **Brahma-kunda** When Brahma returned after stealing the gopas and the calves, he was shocked to see two sets of them: one playing with Krsna and the other still lying in mystic slumber. The tears of joy Lord Brahma shed after he was humbled formed this lake. Rupa Gosvami discovered the deity of Vrnda-devi in this lake. A temple dedicated to Brahma stands here.

50\. **Bilvamangala Thakura’s residence** The author of Krsna-karnamrta lived at Brahma-kunda in Asokavana for seven hundred years, which makes him a contemporary of the Six Gosvamis.

51\. **Gahvaravana** The forest where renounced saints live. These ascetics are the followers of Svami Haridasa. They live an austere life, covering their bodies in the dust of the dhama and wearing only loincloths.

52\. **Rupa Gosvami’s bhajana-kutira** Rupa Gosvami’s main residence was at Seva-kunja, in the area that would later be home to the Radha-Damodara temple.

53\. **Jnana-gudari** Marks the place where Uddhava and Vidura met beneath a banyan tree and recalled the Lord’s pastimes after Krsna’s disappearance. Prayaga-tirtha once came here in the form of a black horse, but he turned white in the purifying water of the Yamuna.

54\. **Katyayani-pitha (Bhadrakali-tirtha)** A clump of Kali’s hair fell here, where her deity was later established. Dhanya and the young gopis regularly worshipped Kali as Katyayani in this temple to get Krsna as their husband.

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Krsna-kunda-tila** This hill is so named due to the attractive lake called Krsna-kunda in one of its gardens. It is part of Vrndavana forest and lies just outside of Vrndavana town. Those who follow the path of the New Vraja-dhama parikrama visit the pastime places on Krsna-kunda-tila; devotees on Vrndavana town parikrama can turn towards Jhulanavana and continue form there to Gopakrta.

2\. **Holivana** Amongst the many pastimes in which Krsna enjoys the love of His devotees is the springtime festival of Holi. One of the sites of this loving exchange is this forest where Alabai and Ranavari are found.

3\. **Ranavari** Once, at the appearance of spring, the gopas and gopis clashed in a fierce Holi battle (rana), which started with Madhu’s challenging words and ended with his complaint that he was hungry.

4\. **Nari-samari (Syamari-Kinnari)** The residence of Syamala-sakhi where Krsna tried to pacify Radha in gopi disguise. Dressed as Syama-sakhi playing a vina, Krsna appeared to be a Kinnari.

5\. **Sankarsana-kunda** Lord Balarama once had a barn and cow-pen for His herd in this place.

6\. **Balarama and Kinnari temple** The temple at Sankarsana-kunda is dedicated to Lord Balarama and to the Kinnari deity of Devi.

7\. **Alabai (Arabadi)** One of the places in Holi-vana where Radha and Krsna play Holi.

8\. **Nagendra-bhoga** The cows of this village provide abundant milk that gives off such a sweet fragrance that it rivals the aroma of the milk ocean. Krsna once lay here and happily drifted off to sleep as Radha massaged His lotus feet.

9\. **Soyano (Sehona / Sihana)** The residents of this village, who were the first to learn that Krsna had killed Agha by expanding Himself within the snake, praised the Lord by saying, “Clever! Clever!” (seyana). A temple dedicated to the Kumaras is found here.

10\. **Roadside at Soyano** As Krsna and Balarama rode Akrura’s chariot along this road to Mathura, the girls of Vrndavana followed behind and tried to stop Them, sometimes by crying and sometimes by smiling.

11\. **Ajhai** After the bewilderment of Brahma, the cowherd boys gathered together here and said amongst themselves, “Today (dja) Krsna killed the evil snake, Aghasura.” Actually, one year had passed since Krsna killed the demon.

12\. **Syamabagh and Ramabagh** The gardens on Krsna-kunda-tila where the gopas loitered eating fruits and nuts after spreading the news that Krsna had killed Aghasura.

13\. **Krsna-kunda** This attractive lake gives its name to the hill Krsna-kunda-tila.

14\. **Rama-kunda** At this place Krsna gave the Syamantaka jewel to Balarama after killing Sankhacuda. Krsna knew that His brother would find the right way to give the jewel to Radharani.

15\. **Tamalavana** The small forest at the foot of Krsna-kunda-tila is known as a meeting place of the divine couple.

16\. **Maghera (Magahera)** The gopis who had followed Akrura’s chariot came this far before losing all hope of catching up with Krsna. It was here they all fainted.

17\. **Rai (Raola)** The village bordered Nanda Maharaja’s kingdom when he ruled from Chatikara and also bordered King Vrsabhanu’s kingdom, which he ruled from nearby Vasati. The presiding Deity of Rai is Balarama.

18\. **Balabhadra-kunda** The area was a great favourite of Krsna and Balarama, who would regularly come here with Their friends and cows and pass time playing games and enjoying nature’s bounty.

19\. **Balarama temple** The Deity of Balarama installed by King Vajranabha is still worshipped in His original form on the bank of Balabhadra-kunda.

20\. **Bhadayara-grama** The residence of the gopi Bhadra, a close friend of Candravali. After Krsna had left Vrndavana for Mathura, Bhadra continuously lamented His absence. Her expression of grief still echoes throughout the woods of Bhadayara.

21\. **Konaivana** The forest that is home to Konai and Konai-kunda.

22\. **Konai (Kohi)** Krsna left His cowherding duties to meet Radha, but She was nowhere to be found. After a while a gopi appeared and offered Him a garland made by Radha. Krsna asked the girl, kyo n’ai: "Why has She not come?”

23\. **Konai-kunda** The lapping waves of this lake seem to make a sound kyo n’ai, kyo nai, as if repeating Krsna’s question of Radha’s whereabouts. A Syamasundara and Gopala temple is nearby.

24\. **Gopisvara Mahadeva** The gatekeeper of New Vraja-dhama. Every form of Lord Siva present in the dhama can be approached through Gopisvara Mahadeva, who is worshipped even by the gopis.

25\. **Madana-rana-vatika** “The garden of Cupid’s battle,” which is extremely dear to Sri Radha. Krsna also comes here to make garlands for Her.

26\. **Surya-kunda** A beautiful lake in Madana-rana-vatika, a secluded part of Vrndavana forest where Radha comes to worship the sun god. It is a famous meeting place of the divine couple.

27\. **Surya-narayana temple** A Deity of Lord Narayana, the sun and the moon in His hands, appeared here to serve and witness the divine couple’s pastimes.

28\. **Jagannatha Dasa Babaji’s bhajana-kutira** Babaji Maharaja would not sleep at night; rather, he would constantly chant the maha-mantra. Bhaktivinoda Thakura often visited him here at Surya-kunda.

29\. **Madhusudana Dasa Babaji’s bhajana-kutira** Radharani directed Madhusudana to recover a large stone from Surya-kunda, upon which She had once placed Her crown, jewellery, and veil. He installed it in the wall of his cottage.

30\. **Salmala** The residents of this village kept innumerable buffaloes. The children here were all enamoured with Krsna’s heroic deeds and acted them out always.

31\. **Sahara (Sahar)** The residence of Upananda and his family. Nanda Maharaja would bring his entire clan here for lunch every year on Bhratr-dvitiya. Krsna stopped here to eat butter on His way to Mathura.

32\. **Kundalata’s garden** A beautiful garden in Sahara where Radha and Krsna would share intimate moments after the Bhratr-dvitiya feast while everyone else was resting.

33\. **Sankhi** Krsna killed Sankhacuda here after catching up to him when he was about to kidnap Radha.

4\. **Tapana Misra’s samadhi** One night after Tapana Misra had been constantly crying for Krsna, Nimai Pandita appeared to His devotee in a dream. The dream was followed by a meeting when the Lord instructed him to chant the maha-mantra day and night. Later they met again in Varanasi, where Tapana Misra’s young son, Raghunatha Bhatta, had a chance to serve the Lord.

5\. **Candrasekhara Acarya’s samadhi** Lord Caitanya stayed in Candrasekhara’s house in Varanasi for two months while He instructed Sanatana Gosvami.

7\. **Sanatana Gosvami’s bhajana-kutira** Where Lord Caitanya appeared to the Gosvami in a dream while he was waiting for the Lord’s arrival in Vrndavana. This is also the place where at first he served Madana-gopala beneath a tree, and then later in a hut beside his kutira.

8\. **Praskandana-kunda (Praskandana-sarovara)** When the twelve sun gods warmed Krsna, His perspiration flowed down Dvadasaditya-tila and formed this small lake known as Praskandana-kunda.

9\. **Ganga-kunda** Where for the sake of His companions Krsna invoked the Ganges. A bath here is equivalent to one hundred baths in the Ganges River itself.

10\. **Radha—Madana-mohana temple** Originally known as Madana-gopala, the Deity is said to be worshipped by Ambarisa Maharaja and Kubja before Vajra discovered Him. Advaita Acarya rediscovered it and asked the Chauhe family to look after him. Sanatana Gosvami took Him to Dvasasaditya-tila where a temple and deities of Radharani and Lalita were arranged for Him.

11\. **Kaliyadamana-vana** The forest commemorating Krsna’s triumph over the hundred-headed snake.

12\. **Nanda-bhavana** Not connected to Nanda Maharaja. It marks the place of forgiveness in Vraja, where pilgrims get relief from reactions to sinful deeds they performed since their last pilgrimage.

13\. **Prabodhananda Sarasvati Thakura’s samadhi** The author of Sri Vrndavana-mahimamrta, the uncle and guru of Gopala Bhatta Gosvami. His samadhi marks the place where he regularly engaged in bhajana in Vrndavana.

14\. **Gendokhara-ksetra** The boys made a wide circle here and threw a ball to one another. Whoever dropped the ball had to leave the game.

15\. **Gendokhara-kundas** The two lakes mark where Krsna and Balarama stood when the gopas played ball on Gendokhara-ksetra.

16\. **Astasakhi-vana** This forest is named after the temple found here.

17\. **Asta-sakhi temple** Raja Ramranjan built this temple after his wife dreamt that Krsna had requested them to build a temple for the glorification of the eight main gopi friends of Radha.

18\. **Biharivana** The area from Gopakrta to the samadhi of Gopala Bhatta Gosvami marks Biharivana, where amongst other holy places the temple of Banke-bihari is found.

19\. **Gopala Bhatta Gosvami’s samadhi** The Gosvami served Caitanya Mahaprabhu as a young boy when the Lord stayed with his family for four months. As soon as his parents died he left for Vrndavana, where he accepted many disciples. His samadhi is near the site where he performed his bhajana and where Radha-ramana appeared from one of his silas.

20\. **Raghunatha Bhatta Gosvami’s samadhi** The ashes of the Gosvami are in the sixty-four samadhis area in Vrndavana. This was also the area where he performed his bhajana close to Govindajl’s early temple. It is the ideal place to recite Srimad-Bhagavatam.

21\. **Banke-bihari temple** The most popular Deity of Vrndavana was discovered by Svami Haridasa in Nidhuvana during the time of the Six Gosvamis.

22\. **Gopakrta** A playground for Krsna and the cowherd boys, who indulged in all kinds of games (krta) here.

**Pastime Places**

2\. **Samadhivana (Causatti-mahanta-samadhi-pitha)** The samadhis of the sixty-four primary associates of Lord Caitanya are found here.

Narottama became the student of Jiva Gosvami and the sole initiated disciple of Lokanatha Gosvami. The famous Kheturi festival was organised by him. His samadhi is found in the Radha-Gokulananda temple.

12\. **Lokanatha Gosvami’s samadhi** Caitanya Mahaprabhu sent His friend and Bhugarbha Gosvami to Vraja to reclaim Krsna’s lost pastime places. His only disciple was Narottama Dasa Thakura. The samadhi is found in the courtyard of the Radha-Gokulananda temple.

The body of the Thakura was first placed in samadhi at Visvanatha-kunja, where he had performed his bhajana, but was later moved to the compound of Radha-Gokulananda temple.

14\. **Vamsi-gopalaji temple** The small temple close to Radha-Gokulananda is where Srila Prabhupada lived in Vrndavana before moving to the Radha-Damodara temple.

15\. **Ganga-narayana Cakravarti’s samadhi** One of the foremost disciples of Narottama Dasa Thakura, Ganga-narayana was born into a family critical of Narottama. His samadhi is found in the courtyard of the Radha-Gokulananda temple.

16\. **Kevarivana** The Vraja-vasis spent the night following the triumph over Kaliya in the open air, giving a chance for the fire demon to attack them. When Krsna swallowed the fire, the Vraja-vasis looked around, asking each other, “Who put it out?” (ke nivari).

17\. **Ramananda Raya’s samadhi** One of Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s most intimate associates. After their meeting and conversation on the subjects of bhakti, the Lord requested Ramananda Raya to live with Him in Puri. His samadhi is found in the sixty-four samadhis area in Vrndavana.

18\. **Bilvamangala Thakura’s samadhi** Cintamani, a prostitute, inspired him to choose the path of bhakti. When his own eyes became an obstruction to his spiritual advancement he blinded himself.

19\. **Davanala-kunda** The lake marks the place where the Vraja-vasis were surrounded by the ring of fire created by the fire demon.

20\. **Visrama-kunda** After Krsna swallowed the fire demon, the Vraja-vasis, exhausted from the harrowing events, came to this place to rest (visrama).

21\. **Four samadhis** The four holy places that lie side by side in New Vraja-dhama are Srinivasa Acarya’s samadhi, Ramacandra Kaviraja’s samadhi, Hemalata Thakurani’s samadhi, and Gauridasa Pandita’s samadhi.

22\. **Srinivasa Acarya’s samadhi** One of the three famous students of Jiva Gosvami who converted Virahamvira and his kingdom to Vaisnavism. His samadhi is found in Srinivasa-kunja.

23\. **Ramacandra Kaviraja’s samadhi** The foremost disciple of Srinivasa Acarya and a close friend of Narottama Dasa Thakura. His samadhi is found in the courtyard of Srinivasa-kunja.

24\. **Hemalata Thakurani’s samadhi** The daughter of Srinivasa Acarya, the Thakurani was a great Vaisnavi who became a spiritual master and initiated many devotees.

25\. **Gauridasa Pandita’s samadhi** Lord Caitanya once gifted him with an oar, at another time he instructed him to carve Deities of Gaura and Nitai. When Gauridasa took up residence in Vrndavana, he chose the banks of the Yamuna by Dhira-samira, where he worshipped the Deity of Syama-raya.

26\. **Baladeva Vidyabhusana’s samadhi** The student of Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura who proved the authenticity of Lord Caitanya’s followers by writing a commentary of the Vedanta-sutras under the guidance of Govindaji Himself. His samadhi is in the compound of Radha-Syamasundara’s temple in Vrndavana.

27\. **Gadadhara Pandita’s samadhi** The inseparable friend of Caitanya Mahaprabhu who, after accepting ksetra-sannyasa, never left Puri where he served Tota-gopinatha. One of his teeth is placed in the samadhi near Vamsi-gopalaji temple.

28\. **Davanala-bihari temple** This is where Krsna stood as He swallowed the forest fire.

29\. **Bhugarbha Gosvami’s samadhi** The inseparable friend of Lokanatha Gosvami and one of Lord Caitanya’s first missioners in Vrndavana. He would often live in an underground (bhu) cave (garbha) to avoid distractions in his bhajana.

30\. **Sivananda Sena’s samadhi** The father of Kavi-karnapura. Every year he would organise and finance a trip for the Navadvipa devotees to Puri for the Ratha-yatra festival.

31\. **Pe** Those who follow the parikrama path of New Vraja-dhama visit the many neighbouring pastime places of Vrndavana forest from Pe to Cauki. However, devotees on Vrndavana town parikrama just go straight to Randhera and then Gopavana. In Pe the cowherd boys became thirsty while herding cows when a gopi appeared and offered them yogurt to drink. The pot remained full while the gopi urged the boys, “Aur piyo! Aur piyo!” (Drink more!), which sounded like “Pe.”

32\. **Viharavana** The forest of pleasure where Krsna would meet Srimati Radharani while the gopas herded their cows nearby.

33\. **Vihara-kunda** Once, when Radha was not with Him, Krsna sat on the bank of Vihara-kunda and played His flute until the gopis were drawn to Him. Radha’s reluctance melted into the waters of Vihara-kunda in the presence of Krsna.

34\. **Nityananda-baithaka** Lord Nityananda stayed here in Viharavana when He visited Vrndavana. He remembered His pastimes as Balarama herding cows with Krsna nearjby, and Krsna’s pastimes with the gopis.

35\. **Radha-kupa** Devotees on parikrama meditate on their spiritual aspiration and loudly shout the holy names “Radhe-Syama!” into this well.

36\. **Jagadananda Pandita’s samadhi** Jagadananda Pandita, a childhood friend of Caitanya Mahaprabhu, would quarrel with the Lord every day, earning for himself the nickname Kandali Jagai, “quarrelsome Jagai.”

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Phalain** Hiranyakasipu wanted to burn Prahlada to ashes, so he constructed a gigantic pyre and asked his sister, Holika, to take Prahlada to the fire. Though Holika had received a boon from Brahma that she could never be burnt, she was the one consumed by the flames. This pastime is re-enacted here every year.

2\. **Prahlada-kunda** The lake named after Prahlada Maharaja is found at Phalain. Even Krsna remembered Prahlada and celebrated His devotee’s strong faith; we should also remember him and aspire for his level of devotion.

3\. **Kholaveca Sridhara’s samadhi** A poor brahmana who lived in Navadvipa. He eked out a living selling cups and plates made from banana leaves, but he spent fifty percent of his profit on the worship of Mother Ganges.

4\. **Kosivana** Radha once locked Herself in a grove of this forest. When Krsna knocked on the door, She asked, “Who are you?” (k? ‘si). This meeting was followed by a humorous conversation between Radha and Krsna.

5\. **Kosikala (Kosi / Kusasthali)** One of Nanda Maharaja’s principal granaries is here. It is also Dvaraka manifest in Vraja. Once, when Nanda Maharaja desired to visit sacred Kusasthali and the Gomati River, Krsna convinced him that they are not different from Kosikala and Gomati-kunda in Vraja.

6\. **Gomati-kunda (Gomati-ghata)** The lake representing sacred Gomati River in Vrndavana. Instead of the pilgrimage they planned, the Vrajavasls bathed in the waters of Gomati-kunda and saw the wonders of the holy place.

7\. **Kotaravana** Krsna would bring his cows here to graze, and the boys would hide from each other in the hollows (kotara) of the trees. They also played Holi here.

8\. **Paya-grama** Once, after playing all morning in Kotaravana, the gopas begged for milk (payah) in this village as their lunch was late to arrive.

9\. **Dadhi-grama** This place is well known because here Krsna once stole yogurt (dadhi) from the gopis when they were about to offer it to Dadhi-devi, a deity of Parvati. He then distributed it to His friends who happily ate. Dadhi-kunda is nearby.

10\. **Kharero (Khayero)** Lord Balarama, returning to Vraja from Dvaraka, stopped His chariot here and asked some cowherds, “What is the good news?” (khaira).

11\. **Bhulanvana** Once, when Krsna was lamenting alone beneath a tree, Subala asked Him why He did not play. After understanding the problem, Subala promised Krsna to arrange a meeting between Him and Radha, and Krsna warned His friend not to forget (bhulano) his promise.

12\. **Karamana** Krsna wandered from forest to forest absorbed in thoughts of Radha and looking for His beloved. By the time He arrived here His feelings of separation were so intense that He fainted.

13\. **Hodal** An ancient dwelling place where the Pandavas lived incognito during their last year of exile, near what is now called Pandava-kunda.

14\. **Sesasayanavana** Krsna assumed the form of Sesasayi Visnu on the request of Radharani. In His Visnu form He laid back against the hoods of the serpent and Radha, in the form of Laksmi, massaged His feet.

15\. **Ksira-sagara (Maha-dadhi-kunda)** When Radha asked Krsna to show Her His Visnu form resting on Sesa-naga in the ocean of milk, Krsna made the cows soak the ground with milk, creating a lake where the serpent appeared.

16\. **Kadambavana** This is where Krsna roams in the shade of the trees and delight in the heady fragrance of kadamba blossoms that permeates the forest in flower-bearing spring.

17\. **Nandavana** The forest where Nanda Maharaja would come to absorb himself in bhajana, but his thoughts of Narayana would always transform into thoughts of Krsna.

18\. **Kavi-karnapura’s samadhi** A great Vaisnava poet who disclosed the identities of Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s associates in Krsna’s pastimes.

19\. **Candanavana** This forest is abundant with sandalwood trees.

20\. **Lotana-kunda** One day the cows, feeling the heat of the sun, began to playfully roll (lot) in the cool sand. The gopas first gathered around to watch the fun, then joined the cows, laughing and making lowing sounds.

21\. **Phalena** Once the gopis sat Radha and Krsna on a throne, played musical instruments, and danced. While doing so they threw different colours on the divine couple until They became indistinguishable from the throne and from each other.

22\. **Kerala** One of the many places where Krsna herds His cows.

23\. **Cauki** Krsna was once tired from walking and used the exposed root of a tree as a seat (cauki). Leaning against the tree’s trunk, He closed His eyes and dreamt of meeting Radha later that night.

24\. **Randhera** In this place thirsty Krsna begged water from a group of gopis who were carrying pitchers of water drawn from a lake called Sri Radharani. Krsna declared the water to be saturated with Radha’s transcendental qualities (radhera).

25\. **Govinda Ghosa Thakura’s samadhi** A famous kirtaniya who lived in Puri and Bengal. The Thakura’s Gopinatha Deity promised to perform the annual sraddha ceremony after the Thakura’s death as a proxy for his son, who had died earlier.

26\. **Gopavana** To celebrate Krsna’s victory over Kaliya and to avert future demonic attacks, Nanda distributed much wealth, grains, and cows to the brahmanas at this place.

27\. **Paramesvara Dasa’s samadhi** In vraja-lila Paramesvara Dasa is Krsna’s cowherd friend Arjuna.

28\. **Sundarananda Dasa’s samadhi** Sundara-ananda’s eternal identity is Sudama, the friend of Krsna. The book Preyo-bhakti-rasar-nava is written about him.

29\. **Ramananda Vasu’s samadhi** The son of Satyaraja Khan and the devotee of Lord Nityananda. Haridasa Thakura often visited their home in Kulina-grama.

30\. **Raghava Pandita’s samadhi** The Pandita always offered the best ingredients to the Deity of the Lord, who sometimes completely consumed what Raghava offered Him. Every year he carried various preparations, made by his sister Damayanti, to Puri to satisfy Lord Caitanya.

31\. **Gopakrta in Gopavana** One of the playgrounds of the gopas. This one has two hills, Taroli and Varoli. When playing here, the gopas split into two groups, each claiming one of the hills as their base.

32\. **Na-grama** Once the gopis were feeling extreme separation from Krsna, and so they went out in search of Him, but they were unsuccesful. At this place the gopis lost all hope and cried out, “No (na) Krsna!”

33\. **Atalavana (Atala-tirtha)** This is where Krsna declared to the cowherd boys that He had eaten so much of the brahmanis’ cooking in Bhatrola that He was now as immovable (atala) as the food in His overfull belly. The boys stopped here to rest.

34\. **Gauravana** Named after Caitanya Mahaprabhu, for this was the forest in which the Lord would rest beneath a large banyan tree when He visited this part of Vrndavana.

35\. **Gauranga-sthana** The resting place of Lord Caitanya in the forest named after Him.

36\. **Vakresvara Pandita’s samadhi** One of Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s lifelong companions. Lord Caitanya said that Krsna personally danced in Vakresvara’s heart. He lived in Puri close to the residence of the Lord.

37\. **Subuddhi Raya’s samadhi** The once-wealthy landlord became a wood collector in Vrndavana, where he would provide visiting Bengali devotees with yogurt and rice. He greeted Rupa and Sanatana upon their arrival to Mathura. His samadhi is near the resting place of his friend Raghunatha Bhatta.

38\. **Ramadasa Abhirama Dasa’s samadhi** A close friend of Lord Nityananda who could tell which black stone was a salagrama-sila and which son of Lord Nityananda was a pure devotee simply by offering obeisances to them.

39\. **Sikhi Mahiti’s samadhi** One of the constant companions of Lord Caitanya. Once in a dream he saw Lord Caitanya entering into and coming out of the body of Lord Jagannatha.

40\. **Four samadhis** The four samadhis that lie side by side in New Vraja-dhama are Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura’s samadhi, Virahamvira’s samadhi, Satyaraja Khan’s samadhi, and Gopala- guru Gosvami’s samadhi.

41\. **Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura’s samadhi** The son of Bhaktivinoda Thakura and the spiritual master of Srila Prabhupada. He preached throughout India, establishing the Gaudiya Matha and sixty-four temples.

42\. **Virahamvira’s samadhi** The king of Visnupura. His country became a land of Krsna and he himself a great Vaisnava under the guidance of Srinivasa Acarya.

43\. **Satyaraja Khan’s samadhi** The father of Ramananda Vasu.

44\. **Gopala-guru Gosvami’s samadhi** Servant and disciple of Vakresvara Pandita. When he once explained that the holy name should be chanted always, even while answering nature’s call, Lord Caitanya called him “guru.” Dhyanacandra Gosvami was his disciple.

45\. **Radhavana (Radhabagh)** This forest at the Yamuna is as dear to Sri Radha as Her lake. Radha once became angry when Krsna scared Her while She was swimming. Krsna once disguised Himself as a gopi to gain entrance to the grove here where Radha was dressed by Her friends.

46\. **Govinda-kunda (Govinda-tirtha / Govinda-svami-tirtha)** The lake is surrounded by beautiful gardens that resound with humming bees and chirping birds. Rupa Gosvami and Sanatana Gosvami engaged in their bhajana here.

47\. **Govindananda’s samadhi** A singer in Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s sankirtana party. Lord Caitanya would relish the singing of Govindananda when His seven kirtana groups all joined together and He danced for Jagannatha’s pleasure.

48\. **Madhava Ghosa’s samadhi** The three brothers Madhava, Vasudeva, and Govinda Ghosa were all famous singers. In Panihati, Madhava Ghosa once sang about Krsna’s dana-keli pastime in a way that inspired Lord Nityananda and Gadadhara Dasa to dance in wild ecstasy.

49\. **Vasudeva Ghosa’s samadhi** A great singer whose songs about Lord Caitanya and Nityananda would melt even wood and stone.

50\. **Kasisvara Pandita’s samadhi** Although a godbrother of Caitanya Mahaprabhu, he spent his life rendering menial service to Lord Caitanya as a bodyguard in Jagannatha Purl. Later he left for Vrndavana where his Gaura-Govinda Deity is still worshipped alongside Govindaji. Devotees on Vrndavana town parikrama will walk to Varaha- ghata from here, however pilgrims on New Vraja- dhama parikrama can visit the neighbouring pastime places of Vrndavana forest on the southern petals of the dhama and along the Yamuna.

51\. **Camelivana** One can either offer respects to the pastime places on the southern petals from Kerala or visit them individually, starting at Camelivana. The trees in this forest blossom when Krsna is there. The scent of the cameli flowers induces amorous feelings, making personal dealings very pleasant. Thus Krsna likes to meet Sri Radha here.

52\. **Vanacari** This place received its name because Krsna often wanders here, herding His cows, playing with His friends, or strolling with Radha. The gopis like to bring Radha and Krsna here, seat Them on a crystal throne, and decorate Them with flower ornaments.

53\. **Sondha** Sannanda, Nanda Maharaja’s younger brother and trusted wise counsellor, lives here.

54\. **Khambi** Once, at the border of Vrndavana, the gopas played leaving the spriritual world for Maya’s land. Balarama then cut a pillar (stamba) from the mountainside and set it on the peak of the mountain, so marking the boundary between Krsna’s realm and Maya’s realm.

55\. **Hasyavana** The forest home to sacred Hasanapura, the capital city of the secondary mellow of humour (hasya-rasa).

56\. **Hasanapura** Krsna appeared to be sitting beside each and every gopa simultaneously when He was asked about His superhuman deeds. When He explained that He was the Supreme Lord, the boys’ pure love made them dive and surface in waves of smiles and laughter.

57\. **Mahali** A place known for the Krsna conscious standard of its residents. They always had the names of Krsna upon their lips, His pastimes within their minds, and attachment to Him within their hearts.

58\. **Marava-grama** Markandeya Rsi came here to engage in bhajana under the protection of Sri Vrndavana-dhama after understanding that Krsna is the Supreme Lord.

59\. **Tilakagadhi** Everyone in this village has worn tilaka since a babaji living nearby impressed upon them the importance of decorating their bodies with the Gaudiya Vaisnava marks.

60\. **Manigadhi** The village was established at the site where a powerful babaji, living nearby, once found a cintamani gem (mani). He immediately threw the treasure into the water in order to instruct his followers of the inestimable value of the holy name.

61\. **Chinna-pahari** The village where Krsna and Balarama stole (chinna) milk products on the request of Their friends, who were hungrily waiting for Them in the nearby forest.

62\. **Basau** The grove where Krsna and Balarama returned after stealing yogurt in the nearby village. They sat (basa) with Their friends on the roots of an expansive banyan tree to enjoy the special taste that stealing lends to milk products.

4\. **Balarama-kunda** The lake is dedicated to Krsna’s and Balarama’s sports with Their friends in Khelanavana. The Radha-Krsna temple near the lake commemorates the pastimes the divine couple enjoy here.

5\. **Rama-ghata** When Lord Balarama and His gopis arrived near the Yamuna during their rasa-lila, Balarama felt too tired to walk any further and so He called the river to come to Him. Yamuna ignored His request, so He dragged the river closer with His plough, thus creating many irrigation ditches. A powerful black Deity of Balarama, originally installed by Vajra, was discovered in a nearby lake and is still worshipped here.

6\. **Rasa-sthali** When Lord Balarama returned to Vrndavana, He fulfilled the desire of the gopis who were attached to Him. They enjoyed the rasa dance here in a meadow surrounding a banyan tree.

7\. **Viharavana** The forest is so called because it witnessed exchanges of divine pleasure (vihara). At the centre of Viharavana are Banke-bihari’s temple and Vihara-kunda.

8\. **Vihara-kunda** On the banks of this lake Krsna once played a mystical melody on His flute that captured Radharani and Her cowherd girlfriends and dragged them to Krsna. This special sound vibration also changed Radha’s contrary nature.

9\. **Nivaranavana** When Radha and Krsna were sitting beside each other, Krsna’s effulgence, like a mirror, showed Radha Her reflection, while Radha’s effulgence reflected Krsna. They both thought They were seeing each other with another girl and boy. Their divine pride and anger were finally swept away by the loving ecstasy of laughter.

10\. **Nivarana-ghata** The stairs on the Yamuna’s bank at Nivaranavana have come to be known as Nivarana-ghata.

11\. **Kacchapa-tirtha (Kacchapavana)** The gopas here imitated the movements and behaviour of the turtles they saw on a sandbank of the Yamuna. They scampered along the sand and splashed in the water, and the air was filled with their laughter.

12\. **Gunjavana** Radha and Krsna would come to this forest and decorate each other with crowns, necklaces, and bracelets made solely from gunja beads.

13\. **Bhandiravana** Krsna and Balarama would regularly herd the cows in this forest and play various games with Their friends. Once, the demon Pralamba — assuming the form of a gopa—joined Their games and met his death at Balarama’s hands.

14\. **Bhandira-vata (Aksaya-vata / Bhandiraka)** A huge banyan tree in Bhandiravana that serves as the playground of the gopas. They sometimes decorate the tree, on other occasions entertain themselves with dance performance, wrestling, or swinging. They play kings and subjects, pelt each other with fruits, and play harina. Once, the gopis challenged Krsna to a wrestling match at Bhandiraka.

15\. **Munjatavi forest** When the gopas returned to their base camp after witnessing Pralamba’s death, they realised that the cows had wandered off in search of fresh grass. Munjatavi is the forest where they found the cows and where Krsna swallowed the fire that threatened the gopas.

19\. **Bhramara-ghata** Here Radha and Krsna once met in the shade of a desire tree, expressing Their affection for each other with honey-laden words that attracted many bees (bhramara).

20\. **Kesi-ghata (Kesi-tirtha)** The place where Krsna killed the horse demon Kesi, and then bathed in the Yamuna. A kadamba tree marking the place of the fight and a temple called Yugala- kisora are nearby.

## Kuruksetra to Naimisaranya

Let us offer our respects to the help places Kuruksetra, Sukratal, and Naimisaranya, whose glories are ornaments in Radha-Syama's divine playground.

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Kuruksetra (Brahmaksetra / Bhrguksetra)** The place where Brahma created the earth, where Bali Maharaja gave the universe to Vamanadeva, and where Parasurama killed twenty-one generations of ksatriyas. Here, too, King Kuru performed austerities, the Vraja-vasis met Krsna during a solar eclipse, and the fratricidal war between the Pandavas and the Kurus took place.

2\. **Jyotisar** The most significant event of the Kuruksetra war was Krsna’s sermon to Arjuna, a discourse given at Jyotisar beneath a banyan tree. The Bhagavad-gita contains the essence (sara) of the truth (jyoti) spoken by Krsna at this place.

3\. **Bhisma-kunda (Bana-ganga)** Where Bhisma fell on the tenth day of the battle. Here Arjuna shot an arrow into the earth and invoked the Ganga to quench Bhisma’s thirst.

4\. **Bana-ganga** Here Arjuna pierced the earth once again, this time creating a lake from Ganges water for his horses as he battled the Kauravas in his effort to kill Jayadratha before sunset.

5\. **Samanta-pancaka** When Parasurama killed the twenty-one generations of ksatriyas, he collected their blood here in five lakes. This is also where Jayadratha’s father was killed while performing penance during the battle of Kuruksetra.

6\. **Karnavadha** This is a trough where Kama's chariot wheel got stuck in the ground. The great Kuru hero was then killed by Arjuna as he was trying to free his chariot.

7\. **Cakravyuha** Here Drona arranged his army in the disc (cakra) formation (vyuha). Abhimanyu broke the formation, but once inside, besieged by many of the Kuru generals, he lost his life.

8\. **Sthanesvara** This wooded area was the encampment of Krsna, where He and Arjuna set up their tents. The two friends would eat and rest here in the evenings after the fighting ended.

9\. **Brahma-sarovara** Tradition says that Brahma created the earth in all its diverse features as he sat in the centre of this lake. King Kuru excavated and beautified the area for the comfort of pilgrims.

10\. **Sannihita-sarovara** A small lake where pilgrims offer oblations for departed souls. This was the secluded area where Krsna met the Vraja-vasis during the solar eclipse.

11\. **Bhagavatavana** This forest in New Vraja- dhama is so named because Sukratal and Naimisaranya, the two famous places where Srimad-Bhagavatam was recited, are here.

12\. **Sukratal** The shoreline where Sukadeva Gosvami spoke the Bhagavatam to King Pariksit in the midst of eighty-thousand sages.

13\. **Suka-tirtha** The specific place in Sukratal where Sukadeva sat beneath a banyan tree while narrating Srimad-Bhagavatam.

14\. **Beraheri** While Pariksit Maharaja was listening to the narration of Srimad-Bhagavatam, the snake-bird Taksaka and Kasyapa were on their way to Sukratal—Taksaka to bite the king, Kasyapa to counteract the snakebite. Beraheri is the place where Kasyapa revived a tree burnt to ashes by the bite of Taksaka.

15\. **Modna** The place where Taksaka offered a fortune to Kasyapa to not save Pariksit Maharaja’s life. The Muni accepted the bribe.

16\. **Naimisaranya** The forest best known amongst devotees as the place where Suta Gosvami recited Srimad-Bhagavatam after he had heard it from Sukadeva Gosvami.

17\. **Suta-gadi** The most important sacred place for Gaudiya Vaisnavas in Naimisaranya. This is where Suta Gosvami sat to recite Srimad'Bhagavatam.

18\. **Cakra-tirtha** Lord Brahma once planned for a wheel that would enclose the entire universe. The hub of the cakra was to be fixed in Naimisaranya, but the wheel shattered and its rim created this lake.

19\. **Dadhici-kunda** Dadhlci, who performed austerities here, donated his invincible body to the demigods so they could transform it into a weapon to kill the demon Vrtra.

20\. **Hanuman temple** A self-manifest deity of Hanuman carrying Lord Rama and Laksmana on each shoulder stands at Dadhici-kunda. The deity is said to date back to the Ramayana war.

21\. **Vyasa-gadi** Where Vyasadeva dictated the Mahabharata to Ganesa, who accepted the task on the condition that the sage dictate at such a pace that Ganesa’s pen need not stop. Vyasa’s stipulation was that Ganesa would not record the verses mechanically.

## Map Twenty-Three Western Petals

## Kinarai to Devanagara

Once angry with Krsna, Radha fumed, “What a cheater! A man who spends his life stealing the butter, yogurt, clothes, hearts, and lives of ethers ultimately ends up cheating himself.”

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Vatsa-krida** The place where, on their way from Gokula to Vrndavana forest, the Vraja- vasis disembarked from their boats and gathered their carts and animals together after crossing the Yamuna. It is called Vatsa-krida because Krsna’s first interest was playing (krida) with His boyfriends (vatsa).

2\. **Kinarai** After crossing the Yamuna at Vatsa-krida one visits Kinarai, the first pastime place on the parikrama of the western petals. Kinarai is where the Yamuna is quite deep. Krsna and Balarama once sneaked off from Gokula to play here with Their friends.

3\. **Jayapura** The place where Krsna was soundly defeated in some childish game and obliged by His friends to reward them with sweets. Krsna wanted to play one last game and was so determined to win that He even ignored Her mother when she came to collect Him.

4\. **Pani-grama** The residence of Durvasa Muni is here. After crossing the river at Pani-ghata, the gopis fed the Muni with food brought from home, hoping to win his blessings. They also asked him about Krsna’s brahmacarya and the Muni’s vow to live solely on the juice of durva grass.

5\. **Parasara Muni’s asrama** The Muni is well known as the authority of Rg Veda, the composer of the Visnu Purana, and the father of Srila Vyasadeva.

6\. **Satyavati’s home** Not far from the asrama of Parasara Muni lived a fisherman and his daughter, Satyavati. Once, as Satyavati was ferrying people across the river, she met the Muni, who was attracted to the girl and begot a son by her, Srila Vyasadeva.

7\. **Dattatreya’s asrama** The sage living on the shore of the Yamuna was the combined incarnation of the three presiding deities of the universe and the embodiment of the Lord’s potency of knowledge.

8\. **Mana-sarovara (Hrdaya-sarovara)** On the occasion of a rasa dance Srimati Radharani felt slighted. After leaving the scene She sat on a stone and shed tears of loving anger (mana), thus creating a lake around Her. Krsna attempted to pacify Her by placing His flute at Radha’s lotus feet. The lake is also known as the heart of both Radha and Krsna. This is also the place where Lord Siva and Asuri Muni attained gopi bodies.

9\. **Bilvavana (Belvana / Srivana)** This forest is full of roseapple (bel) trees whose fruits are the favourite of the cowherd boys and are also ideal balls for playing catch. This is also the place where Laksmi performed austerities to enter the rasa dance.

10\. **Krsna-kunda** Once, during winter, the gopas insisted that Krsna provide them with ripe bel fruits. Ever ready to please His friends, Krsna played a beautiful melody on His flute that caused the trees to sprout, flower, and grow fruits, all within minutes.

11\. **Dagauli (Dag)** This place is named after the stick (dag) that Krsna once lost here while herding Nanda’s cows. That stick has yet to be found, though many devotees have searched for it.

12\. **Bhandiravana** A favourite pastime place not only for the gopas and the gopis but also for the yogis. This is where Narada saw parrots debating the truth of the Vedanta-sutra, where Rati-manjari gifted a picture of Krsna to Srimati Radharani, where the divine couple were saved from Jatila by Syama-sakhi’s made-up story, and where the marriage of Radha and Krsna took place.

13\. **Chaheri** A shady forest at the edge of a large meadow of Bhandiravana, where the cowherd boys sometimes rest while the cows graze nearby.

14\. **Bhandiraka** Vrndavana manifested its eternal form in this hidden grove of Bhandiravana, when Radharani brought baby Krsna here after saving Him from the stormy weather. This was the time when Brahma appeared to perform the divine couple’s wedding ceremony before Their earthly pastimes could take place. The newlyweds played games and performed a rasa dance suited for a single couple. Years later Krsna enjoyed rasa- dancing with the cowherd girls at the same place.

15\. **Bhandira-vata (Vamsivata)** The wedding ceremony of the divine couple took place under this banyan tree in Bhandiraka. Years later, Krsna would call the gopis to this spot with His flute-song.

16\. **Venu-kupa (Dauji-kupa)** The gopis insisted that Krsna should bathe in all the holy places of the universe to purify Himself from the sin of killing Vatsasura, who had assumed the form of a calf. Playing His flute, Krsna called all holy waters in this well and cheerfully bathed with its water. Once Balarama felt so much ecstasy as He bathed here that the stones melted. Beside a temple of Balarama, a stone with the imprint of His turban is still visible.

17\. **Sridama temple** The place where Krsna’s dear friend sat in sorrow. One pastime says that Sridama was neglected by the gopas for he had let Krsna carry him on His back; another pastime explains that the reason of his lamentation was Krsna’s departure from Vrndavana.

18\. **Mathavana (Matha)** A small forest where Krsna and Balarama played with Their friends while the cows grazed. The name matha is either derived from the clay-like soil used to manufacture pots, or from the pots the villagers make here. It is said that a drop of Mahadeva’s semen fell at Mathavana when Mohini-murti bewildered him.

19\. **Harasali** Krsna and the gopis enjoyed rasa- dancing here. In the rasa dance, Krsna, who is also called Hari, captures the minds of all gopis but one, Sri Radha, who captures His mind. For this feat She is called ??r?, and this village is known as Harasali.

20\. **Varahara** Krsna again revealed the opulence and majesty of His boar incarnation to His wonderstruck friends here, as He had done for them at Apsaravana and for the gopis at Varaha-ghata.

21\. **Bhusanavana** The place where the gopas ornamented (bhusana) Krsna with fresh sprouts, leaves, and flowers, all beautified by His touch. The cowherd boys were wondering how this was possible. They concluded that Krsna was the ornament of all ornaments.

22\. **Bhajana** A market town where the gopas would buy and sell cows, even though the market in Kosi was better. In a nearby forest the gopas and the gopis once came together to worship (bhajana) the divine couple with songs. Once Radha and Krsna rested here after playing Holi and accepted the loving service of Their intimate gopi and gopa friends.

23\. **Parasauli** After Krsna’s departure, Radha and Visakha entered the sun planet through the waters of the Yamuna. Another 16,100 gopis worshipped Katyayani here on the riverbank, praying that Krsna would reunite with them. The girls were kidnapped by Bhaumasura, just to give a chance for Krsna to rescue and marry them.

24\. **Nohajhila** The land here was swampy, so the gopas would avoid the area. But while their cows grazed in nearby pastures, from a distance they would watch the flocks of waterfowl nesting there.

25\. **Baghari (Vyaghari)** Once, while walking along the forest path, the boys came face-to-face with a tiger. Like an ordinary child, Krsna yelled, “Vyaghra!” and ran for dear life, followed by His terrified friends. The large beast was a devotee of Krsna, who just wanted to have a closer look at the Lord of his heart.

26\. **Raya** One of Nanda Maharaja’s many storehouses in Vraja.

27\. **Venuya** The hill where Krsna played the flute to convey messages individually suited to the Vraja-vasis, especially to the gopis. The cowherd girls heard the flute say, “Sometime soon, fate will arrange for us to meet.”

28\. **Bhadravana** The favourite forest of Balarama named after Him. Krsna’s elder brother is called Balabhadra because His extensive strength is the cause of auspiciousness (bhadra). He makes both Vraja and the life of the devotees auspicious by empowering them with spiritual strength and subduing demons.

29\. **Bhadra-sarovara** While residing on the bank of this lake, Rupa Gosvami completed his enchanting drama Lalita-madhava, which he began many years earlier under the direction of Queen Satyabhama and Caitanya Mahaprabhu.

30\. **Vaikuntha-pura** This is where the gopas requested Krsna to manifest His eternal abode and prove to them that He is truly Narayana. When Krsna revealed Vaikuntha, the feelings of reverence in the gopas' hearts conflicted with their spontaneous loving mood and they all thought, “We don’t like it here.”

31\. **Sarakoriya** In this place, by clapping His hands, Krsna conjured up pots of milks at the request of His hungry friends. After finishing one pitcher, each boy skimmed the cream (sara) from a second. When the servants arrived with the lunch, they found all the boys asleep amidst milk pitchers scattered here and there.

32\. **Lohavana (Lohajanghavana)** Krsna and His friends enjoyed herding cows in this forest, once the residence of the demon Lohajangha. The forest also served as the battlefield upon which Krsna defeated Jarasandha and his enormous armies.

33\. **Krsna-kunda** At this lake Lohajanghasura lived in a hillside cave. Some says that Krsna killed the demon near the well called Krsna-kupa, a second account attests that it was Satrughna who slew him.

34\. **Boat landing** Krsna, disguised as a boatman, was sleeping in a boat here when the gopis came with their milk products and asked Him to take them across to Mathura. As the gopis climbed in the boat it began to sink and they had to throw away all the pots and bail the water out with their hands.

35\. **Ayore (Ayara)** When Krsna finally returned to Vrndavana after killing Dantavakra, the Vraja-vasis stood here on the road, and at the first glimpse of Krsna’s chariot they all made a great tumult by calling out, “Welcome! Welcome!” (aya, aya). This place is still covered with the many flowers and flower petals that the demigods showered upon Krsna and His kin.

36\. **Gauravai (Gaurai)** Upon returning from Kuruksetra, Nanda Maharaja decided to stay in Lohavana with His friend, Dhana, until Krsna’s return. When Krsna’s divine father entered the town, he was received with so much honour (gaurava) that the place was renamed Gauravai. This is where Krsna married the gopis who had walked through a fire to prove their unwavering chastity to Krsna. Gauravai is also the place where Krsna concluded His Vraja pastimes—the eternal features of Goloka disappeared from view.

37\. **Kheriya** Where the gopas fearfully called out, “Khayare! Khayare!” which means “he will eat us.” Lohajanghasura is said to be the cause of the gopas’ cries, but no one knows if it were in play or for real.

38\. **Vandi** Krsna’s hair-cutting ceremony took place here in the presence of the deities of Durga: Vandi and Anandi. After the ceremony, the two deities—eager to continue seeing the beauty of the Lord — took human forms. They made cow- dung patties, and by regularly delivering the fuel to the palace they could see baby Krsna and play with Him. The temples and lakes of Vandi and Anandi are still visible here.

39\. **Cow cemetery** Where the deceased cows and oxen of Syamasundara’s herd are ceremonially buried in New Vraja-dhama.

40\. **Saukara-tirtha** Where Lord Varaha appeared to deliver the earth. It is said that fasting while residing here purifies one of all offences.

41\. **Vidrumavana** This forest is so named because of the coral (vidruma) that paves the ground and the coral-coloured leaves on the trees.

42\. **Baladeva (Dauji / Ridha)** The pastime place of Krsna’s elder brother. Whenever the gopas trek all the way out to Lohavana or Mahavana, Lord Balarama likes to graze the cows in the open meadows here while He and His friends rest together in the shade of the trees. Baladeva is also known as the birthplace of Revati.

43\. **Sankarsana-kunda (Dugdha-kunda / Ksira-sagara)** It is said that long ago villagers dug the lake and filled it with the milk of 100,000 cows as an offering to Lord Balarama. The Deity in the Balarama temple here never left Vraja, but sometimes hid from the Muslim soldiers. On one such occasion the priests threw Him in this lake where He remained for many years.

44\. **Devanagara** This area is so called because Nanda Maharaja would often come here in the company of his friend Devaspati. The Govardhana-gila that belonged to Devaspati is still worshipped here.

45\. **Nanda-baithaka** The king of the gopas rests here while herding his cows. To obtain a son, Nanda worshipped a deity of Lord Siva nearby, who was called Gopala-lala Mahadeva after Krsna’s birth.

## Gokula to Raval

With all my heart J worship an incredibly beautiful queen of cowherds, the antics of whose naughty son bring divine joy to the hearts of all those who hear of them.

**Pastime Places**

1\. **Mahavana (Brhadvana / Maharanya)** Krsna’s grandfather, Maharaja Parjanya, moved to Mahavana because demons harassed the area of Nandagrama where he used to live with his clan. This forest is sweetened by Krsna’s early childhood pastimes, conluding with the toppling of the yamala-arjuna trees, which prompted Nanda Maharaja to move to Vrndavana.

2\. **Gokula (Gokula-mahavana)** Nanda’s opulent capital at the edge of Mahavana was full of beautiful palaces, regal gates, and floral gardens, whose combined beauty and grandeur eclipsed the kingdom of heaven. The village here is also called Gokula-mahavana for it is so much linked to the forest Mahavana.

3\. **Nanda-bhavana (Assi-stambh)** The palace of Nanda Maharaja is mostly known for Krsna’s butter-stealing childhood pranks, for He lived here from His birth until the age of three years and four months. It is said that Balarama took birth from Rohini’s womb in the palace one year prior to Krsna’s appearance. It was also the scene of Krsna’s first birthday festival, Krsna Janmastami. Deities of Krsna, Balarama, Nanda, and Yasoda stand on the altar here today. One can also see the balcony where Yasoda churned butter and a stone pillar that is still marked with stolen butter.

4\. **Dadhi-manthana-sthala** The veranda of Nanda-bhavana where Mother Yasoda would churn butter. Once, when Krsna woke up, she breastfed the hungry child, but alarmed by the sound of boiling milk she put Him down and ran into the kitchen. Angry Krsna broke the churning pot and left for the storeroom, leaving buttery footprints everywhere. He then sat on a wooden mortar in the courtyard near the veranda and fed the monkeys handfuls of butter. After catching her errant son, Mother Yasoda tried to tie the naughty child to the mortar, but the rope was always two fingers short.

5\. **Nanda-bhavana’s gate** Once a Pulinda woman gave Krsna fruits and in exchange He filled her basket with jewels.

6\. **Danta-dhavana-tila** A small hill close to the palace, where Nanda Maharaja would brush (dhavana) his teeth (danta) every morning.

7\. **Malla-tirtha** The makeshift wrestling arena in the palace courtyard, where Krsna and Balarama would lock arms as Their friends cheered and the elder gopis watched, amused and fully satisfied. Gopisvara Mahadeva, the elder gopis’ worshipable deity, presides over this area. Nearby is another deity, Maha-malla-raya, who is the child wrestler of Gokula.

8\. **Sakata-bhanjana-sthana** After Krsna turned over in His crib, Yasoda happily arranged the utthana ceremony for her son in the courtyard of the palace. When the ceremony was over, Krsna was placed in the shade of a cart that was actually Utkaca, also known as Sakatasura, “the cart demon.” When the cart bore down on Krsna, He casually kicked it, lifting it into the air and breaking it to pieces. A few days later Krsna and Balarama were ceremonially bound together in the same courtyard.

9\. **Trnavarta-badha-sthala** The demon Trnavarta assumed the form of a whirlwind and lifted one-year-old Krsna into the air. Krsna enjoyed the flight for a while before forcing the demon to hurtle to earth. The place where Trnavarta hit the ground and where the Vraja-vasis found baby Krsna crawling on the demon’s chest is called Trnavarta-badha-sthala.

10\. **Putana-khadda (Putana-uddhara-sthala)** Putana entered Gokula in the disguise of a heavenly lady and offered her poisoned breast to Nanda’s son. Krsna sucked the life air from the demoness, who flew out the window with Him hanging on her breast. The spot where she fell is called Putana-khadda.

11\. **Ramana-reti (Ramaka)** Krsna and His cowherd boyfriends enjoyed their childhood play in this area. Once, seeing the boys, Durvasa Muni doubted that the Supreme Lord would roll on the ground with children. Krsna approached the sage, sat in his lap, and inhaled him through His lotus mouth, where Durvasa could see the entire universe. It is said that Sanatana Gosvami also saw a beautiful boy playing here and entering the temple of Madana-gopala, where He disappeared.

12\. **Madana-gopala’s old temple** Caitanya Mahaprabhu was overwhelmed by ecstatic love when He visited the place of Krsna’s birth festival, where the temple of Madana-gopala stood. Sanatana Gosvami would come to the temple daily to admire the beauty of the Lord.

13\. **Brahmanda-ghata** The place where Krsna ate mud, but instead of confessing the mischief, He decided to show His lotus mouth to Yasoda-devi. There she saw the unlimited cosmic manifestation (brahmanda) in detail and forgot all about Krsna’s having eaten dirt.

14\. **Gauranga-baithaka** When Caitanya Mahaprabhu visited Mahavana, He sat on the steps of Brahmanda-ghata and in ecstasy recalled the loving exchanges that took place here between Krsna and Yasoda-devi.

15\. **Cintaharana-ghata** The ghata is named after the anxiety (cinta) Mother Yas'oda felt seeing the universal form in the mouth of her tiny son. Cintaharana Mahadeva is worshipped here.

16\. **Cintaharana Mahadeva** Yasoda petitioned this deity of Lord Siva to free (harana) Krsna from the dangerous situation that had caused her vision. Because Lord Siva freed Yasoda from her anxiety, he is called Cintaharana Mahadeva.

17\. **Koila** When Vasudeva carried baby Krsna from Mathura to Gokula, he reached the Yamuna at Koila where the river parted to give way to the Lord. Halfway across the water rose so high that Vasudeva was forced to lift Krsna over his head and call out, “Someone take Him!” (koi le \[h\]u). Yamuna-devi took Him and after playing with the beautiful child for a while, she gave Him back to His terrified father. This is also the place where the ash from Putana’s burning body settled, blackening the bank of the river.

18\. **Yamalarjuna-bhanjana-tirtha** Tied to a mortar, Krsna passed between the two arjuna trees, who were actually Nalakuvara and Manigriva waiting for their liberation. When the mortar behind Him became stuck, Krsna pulled on the rope and easily uprooted the trees.

19\. **Syama-mandira** The maternity house where Vasudeva placed baby Krsna beside Yasoda while taking the baby girl Yogamaya with him back to Mathura. This is where Yasoda breastfed Krsna for the first time, where Nanda and the brahmanas performed His birth ceremony, and where Krsna’s umbilical cord was cut. Nanda-haveli, a mansionlike structure with many columns, stood at the foot of the hill.

20\. **Gosala** Vasudeva sent Garga Muni to Vraja to perform the name-giving ceremony of Krsna and Balarama. The ritual took place in the barn of Nanda Maharaja in secrecy. The sage declared to the king that his son is as good as Narayana, gave the astrological reading of Krsna’s birth, and swore the king to secrecy.

21\. **Krsna-kupa (Nanda-kupa / Sapta-samudrika-kupa)** It is said that demigods created it for Krsna, blessing its water to be warm in winter and cool in summer. Nanda Maharaja regularly drew water from here for his bath. It is also the site of Krsna’s ear-piercing ceremony.

22\. **Sanatana’s guha** The Gosvami’s Mahavana residence is a deep, cave-like hollow in the ground at Krsna-kupa. This is where Jagadananda Pandita visited Sanatana and lived with him while staying in Vrndavana. Here one concludes the parikrama of Gokula. Devotees on New Vraja-dhama parikrama visit Raval before ending their circumambulation of the dhama at Visrama-ghata.

23\. **Raval (Mukhyeravali)** The birthplace of Srimati Radharani, the ancestral home of Maharaja Vrsabhanu and Kirtida-devi. Scripture and oral tradition give three reports of Radha’s appearance, saying that She took birth from the ground, a lotus, and the womb. It is said that Radharani opened Her eyes for the first time when Her eternal consort was nearby, and that She took Her first grain from Krsna’s bowl. Once Durvasa Muni visited the village and blessed Radharani that whatever She cooked would have the taste of nectar. A temple of Lariliji stands here today.

24\. **Durvasa Muni’s asrama** The sage, having heard that the Supreme Lord Krsna had appeared on earth, came to the Yamuna River and took up residence near Raval.

25\. **Gauranga-baithaka** From Gokula, Lord Caitanya visited Raval and exhibited symptoms of ecstasy so uniquely wonderful that the villagers concluded that He was Krsna. Pilgrims should return to Mathura from here and finish the New Vraja-dhama parikrama at Visrama-ghata.
