## Foreword

For millennia, drama has been a universal, powerful means of communicating the beliefs, truths, and experiences shared within a culture. It has been a channel through which the depths of the religious heart could find expression. The ancient Sanskrit dramatic tradition of India preserves, to this day, a very rich and sophisticated art form, wherein the religious subject continues to be illuminated.

The author of the present work, Tamal Krishna Goswami, who is a voice of the Vaiṣṇava tradition to which he belongs, is the first to write dramas in the English language that conform, in every detail, to the conventions of Sanskrit dramaturgy. In his first drama, *Jagannātha-priya Nāṭakam: The Drama of Lord Jagannātha* (with a Foreword by Gary Tubb, Harvard University), Tamal Krishna Goswami supplements his text with a thorough analysis of the application of Sanskrit dramatic theory. *Prabhupāda Antya-līlā: The Final Pastimes of Śrīla Prabhupāda* represents the author's second work which adheres to the laws and conventions of Sanskrit drama. By aligning himself with the Sanskrit tradition, Tamal Krishna Goswami has made the contribution of bringing this ancient art form to the West.

Vaiṣṇavism, originating in ancient India, is one of the most highly theistic and devotional traditions in the history of religion wherein the mystical intimacy between the Supreme Lord and the devotee has been realized and extensively developed. The Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava tradition, founded in medieval India by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, produced many skilled dramatists, of whom Rūpa Goswami is especially notable, because he developed the use of drama for revealing the deeper mysteries of the intimacy between Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa and Their devotee associates.

Religions of the world tell of persons in whom features of persons in whom features of the ultimate being are manifested. The priest, the saint, the incarnation, the Christ, the *guru*, the *avatāra*, the *lama*, the *buddha*, etc., are different channels through which aspects of the divinity make themselves known to humans in different cultures. In the Vaiṣṇava tradition one essential way in which the divinity manifests itself is the *guru*. It is through the *guru* that one attains connection to the Supreme; it is also in him that we find certain divine attributes manifested directly from the Supreme. The appearance of the *guru* in one's life is considered most fortunate because it constitutes the presence of the Supreme and access to Him.

While the process of obtaining a spiritual teacher, or *guru*, is explicated by the tradition, and the instructions regarding the proper behavior toward the *guru* are very extensive, this drama explores the more spontaneous dedication between the *guru* and disciple. In the relationship between the guru and disciple intense personal affections for one another exist, much in the way affections are experienced between parents and children. The drama presented herein is testimony to this fact.

*The Final Pastimes of Śrīla Prabhupāda* is the story of one great, elderly Vaiṣṇava *guru* known as Prabhupāda, just prior to his departure from this world, and the intense devotion and love of the disciples in relationship to him. The author is himself and intimate disciple of Prabhupāda who was closely serving him during his last days, and thus he is one of the key figures in the drama. The present work, which grew out of experiences recorded in the author's diary of Prabhupāda's final activities and words, captures the feelings and thoughts that went through the hearts and minds of many disciples as they struggled to take care of their spiritual master during the moments that led up to his departure. Through the colorful medium of drama, written to be staged upon the mind of the reader, a sense of the love and devotion the Vaiṣṇava disciples have for their *guru* is powerfully conveyed even to one for whom the Vaiṣṇava tradition is foreign.

Although it is primarily a dramatic and devotional work, *The Final Pastimes of Śrīla Prabhupāda* has historical value. With the exception of the supernatural personalities, all of the characters in the drama are historical, with their actual names used. Most of the events are portrayed as they occurred, with only minor chronological adjustments to suit the aesthetic movement of the play. Most of the words spoken by Prabhupāda in the drama are words that he indeed did speak during his lifetime. This realism transmitted by the author's careful presentation of these last days, combined with the devotional idealism as seen in the behavior of the disciples, and further heightened by a vision of transhistorical figures, allows the reader to be swept up into the drama's power and poignancy.

The author introduces "supernatural" personalities in the fifth and tenth acts of the drama. These "mythic" figures are a common feature of the Sanskrit dramatic tradition and personify aspects of a world "behind the scenes," allowing the audience to enter into the fullness of meaning signified by these personalities. When one reads the drama, however, one quickly learns that these transhistorical figures are not "mythical" at all; they conform to the logic of Vaiṣṇava theology, which explains the power behind all events. No matter how small and insignificant or how big and magnificent, all things, events, and existences have their origin in and are constantly sustained by a greater personal being in whom the Supreme

Person Himself has invested His power. Put in non-metaphysical aesthetic terms, without the inclusion of these transhistorical figures, this drama would become incomplete.

The two main supernatural personalities in the drama are Yogamāyā and Śraddhā. Yogamāyā, whose name means "the mystical energy of the Lord," is the spiritual personality responsible for arranging and directing the activities of the Lord and the devotees in order to facilitate exchanges of love and affection. Śraddhā, whose name means "faith," personifies the pure devotion of the disciples in serving their *guru*. These two personalities, in conversation with each other and witnessing the dealings among the disciples serving their master, allow the author to reveal to the audience the spiritual significance of the drama. The devotees' faith and spontaneous devotion (*śraddhā*) to their *guru* as it was confronted with the mysterious plan of the Lord (*yogamāyā*) is the underlying theme of the drama. Thus these divine personifications are themselves participants in the drama of Prabhupāda's departure, especially in the tenth act, they reveal a poignant and glorious conclusion.

For Vaiṣṇava devotees and particularly for those devotees in the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (the movement that Prabhupāda began in 1966, which spread to all parts of the globe), this work is a valuable gift. Once a year, the members of the Krishna consciousness movement honor the day on which Prabhupāda departed. Because the author re-creates the pain and joy that a devotee feels while contemplating this event, this work will be especially important on that day. This drama could constitute a study, indeed, a meditation, on the pain of being separated from such a beloved teacher and leader; by his example, during his final days, Prabhupāda taught how to die from this world while being absorbed in love of God.

The Institute for Vaishnava Studies presents *Prabhupāda Antya-līlā: The Final Pastimes of Śrīla Prabhupāda* as the first book in its division of cultural publications. The purpose of this division is to publish works by authors from the various Vaiṣṇava sects in India and the West in order to make the living Vaiṣṇava traditions accessible to persons in both the academic and devotional communities. The publication of this drama is an auspicious beginning toward that end.

Graham M. Schweig

## Prologue

(*The director hurriedly enters the gateway courtyard of the Krishna-Balaram temple complex in Vṛndāvana, searching for his assistant. He is carrying a copy of* The Nectar of Devotion.)

**Director:** (*to himself, annoyed*) These playwrights! They think they can take *unlimited* liberties. We are about to begin, and suddenly I'm handed a *new* introduction. (*Sarcastically*) The author is worried the audience won't be prepared for the drama's striking theme. Why didn't he think of that earlier? Besides, they are *already* prepared! Why else have they come to Vṛndāvana at this *particular* time? Anyway, I'd better rehearse. (*He calls his assistant, but there is no response*.) When I need him the most, he disappears. Now where did I put it? (*He searches his pockets, then thumbs through the book* *and lifts out a sheet, which he unfolds.*) Ah, here it is. (*He reads* *out loud*.) "To be read dramatically, with intense emotion." (*He studies the lines and then delivers them with feeling*.)

"Release him! Release him! (*Mistaking their beloved master to be in death's embrace, the devotees fell unconscious. Reviving, they implored:*) Death! Has evil Kali engaged you where he himself has failed? Then the presence of the *bhāgavata* has made Kali hide in fear, exactly like a thief taking shelter of the night."

(*To himself, in normal voice, appreciating*) Hmmm. A very effective simile. Prabhupada's preaching is as illuminating as the sun and has driven the personality of Kali into hiding. Using the cover of night, Kali tries to steal Prabhupāda's life away. (*He again repeats the lines, beginning from "Death! Has evil Kali engaged you," etc.*) I like it. Now what else? (*After studying the script further, he resumes his dramatic recitation.*)

"Ah, Fate! We thought you were justice personified, awarding men their due, yet today we see religion crushed in your coils.

"Impetuous Time, God's eerie specter! You have entered Vraja with your own nefarious plan. Now leave at once! Be gone! This is the Lord's eternal abode."

(*To himself*) Good! Then—? (*After reading, he recites.*)

"Let our cry of despair reach the limits of the universe, that all may now hear this anguished prayer, 'Dear Kṛṣṇa, if You so desire, please spare Śrīla Prabhupāda.'"

(To himself, resuming his normal voice) It's definitely an improvement. Prabhupāda's disappearance pastimes are difficult both for the actors and the audience, but those who make the effort will certainly develop unalloyed devotion and transcend all fear of death.

**Assistant:** (*entering, alarmed*) Sir . . .

**Director:** (*demanding*) Where have you been?

**Assistant:** (*alarmed*) Sir, I must inform you of an impending misfortune.

**Director:** Misfortune? (*Reassuring*) My boy, we are residing in Vṛndāvana during the month of Kārttika and taking a daily bath in the sacred Yamunā. Fortune herself holds us in her arms, especially when this auspicious new drama, *Prabhupāda Antya-līlā*, is about to be performed.

**Assistant:** (*still perturbed*) Sir, that's just it. I did as you asked and invited all the devotees to attend. But when I explained the theme, they became morose. One devotee shouted and chased me away. What good is this drama if its theme is so disturbing?

**Director:** My boy, as a gardener knows his trees, so you must know your dramas. Each drama, like a tree, has its special quality. Some—like the mango—produce sweet fruits, while others—like the *nīm*—are bitter . . .

**Assistant:** I'm afraid this drama's subject is *very* bitter.

**Director:** . . . But the *nīm* is medicinal, and its bitterness stimulates our appetite. Still, I would compare our drama to the *tamāla* tree. Just as in Kṛṣṇa's absence the *gopīs* could feel His presence only be embracing the beautiful black *tamāla* tree, devotees pained by separation from Prabhupāda will feel his presence once again when they hear this drama.

**Assistant:** But sir—

**Director:** (*firmly*) Listen! The devotees' response proves that they are experiencing devotional ecstasy when hearing about Kṛṣṇa's pure devotee, Śrīla Prabhupāda. Just calm yourself. Let me explain something to you. In *The Nectar of Devotion* (*He holds the book before his assistant to emphasize his point*.) it says that ecstatic love can develop when one sees Lord Kṛṣṇa, His pure devotee, or an object which reminds one of Kṛṣṇa.

**Assistant:** But when I announced that Śrīla Prabhupāda would be the hero of our drama, they stared aghast. I had reminded them of that most painful experience: Śrīla Prabhupāda's disappearance.

**Director:** Don't be deceived by their reaction. They are actually absorbed in the ecstasy of *karuṇa-bhakti-rasa*. Just be patient and everything will become clear to you. In this ecstasy of devotional service there are sometimes symptoms like regret, heavy breathing, crying, falling on the ground, and beating upon one's chest. When a devotee expects some mishap to Kṛṣṇa or to His pure devotee, it is called devotional service in bereavement.

Even though the very mention of Prabhupāda's disappearance does indeed bring pain to a disciple's heart, hearing the subject is like drinking hot sugarcane juice: painful yet irresistibly sweet. (*Pointing to the audience*) Look out there; the audience is beginning to swarm like maddened bees, searching for that sweet taste.

**Assistant:** Then with your permission, sir, I shall go at once to Śrīla Prabhupāda's residence, where I'm sure the pain of separation from him will be softened by the sweet nectar of his inspiration.

**Director:** Excellent! (*They walk about.*) As a true *parama-haṁsa*, His Divine Grace roved the planet like a second Garuḍa. Only after he made sure that Śrī Kṛṣṇa's message had penetrated all seven continents did Prabhupāda finally take rest in this most sacred land of Vṛndāvana. (*They have reached Prabhupada's residence.*)

**Assistant:** (*pointing*) Look, the lights are on! And in the left window two people are silhouetted against the curtain.

**Director:** Let's move closer; we might make out who they are. (*They move closer to listen. From offstage comes the indistinct murmur of the voices of Tamal Krishna Goswami and Bhakti Charu Swami. Then, clearly—* )

**Bhakti Charu:** (*warmly*) . . . It is by your mercy only that I have this opportunity to serve Śrīla Prabhupāda.

**Tamal Krishna:** No, no . . . not by my mercy, but by your wonderful devotion. And Śrīla Prabhupāda's divine grace.

**Director:** (*softly*) We're in luck. Prabhupāda's secretary, Tamal Krishna Goswami, and his servant, Bhakti Charu Mahārāja.

**Assistant:** (*eagerly*) Let's join them at once.

**Director:** Wait! Don't intrude. When Prabhupāda's servants meet, it is like the deliciously sweet blending of milk with sugar; if heated further, the thick nectar of *rabrī* forms. Relishing spiritual emotions and mastery in the kitchen have this in common: You must have patience and the correct ingredients. Let's enter unnoticed. We can hear everything from the reception room. (*They exit.*)

## Act I

(*Tamal Krishna Goswami and Bhakti Charu Swami enter*. *The scene is the main sitting room of Prabhupāda's residence, late evening, toward the end of July*.)

**Bhakti Charu:** Like a child born to elderly parents, I feel that I've missed the joy of our father's younger days. Please describe them to me.

**Tamal Krishna:** These photos speak far more eloquently than I ever could. (*They walk from photo to photo*.) Ah, Prabhupāda's laughter is like the sun rising brilliantly on the horizon. (*Walking*)

And here, with his head touching the petals of a rose. How similar—Prabhupāda and the rose . . . both fragrant and soft. But watch for the thorns! There are tests after all one must pass to qualify for his grace. (*Walking*)

(*Chuckles*) I remember one morning during a walk through Golden Gate Park in San Francisco, Prabhupāda stopped to praise the humility of the grass and the kindness of a boy feeding some pigeons. He talked about the voices of the woods—the groan of an old redwood here, the mating call of a thrush there—deftly pointing out nature's simple yet wise lessons to us all. (*Walking*)

On another morning walk, this time at Venice Beach, he exposed the scientists' bluff with simple common sense, as easily as Kṛṣṇa could crush the pride of Kaṁsa's big wrestlers. (*Wistfully*) How different things seem now . . . (*Pause*)

**Bhakti Charu:** Prabhupāda hasn't taken his morning walk for at least six months; even riding in a car is too exhausting.

**Tamal Krishna:** Ah, but his consciousness is completely unaffected, though the illness has certainly taken its toll in other ways.

**Bhakti Charu:** Look here how briskly he moved through the airport lounges, mobbed by hundreds of ecstatic devotees.

**Tamal Krishna:** He is three times our age, yet none of us kept up with him. He challenged us in so many ways and always came out victorious. (*Pause; smiling*) He said that it was our youth that kept him from growing old. (*Walking*)

**Bhakti Charu:** This looks like an early fire sacrifice; isn't that Brahmānanda helping Prabhupāda?

**Tamal Krishna:** (*agreeing*) Hmmm . . . New York City, probably '68 or '69.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*quietly laughing*) Devotees say my getting first, second and then *sannyāsa* initiation all within two months was extraordinary.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*jokingly*) You're a demigod, Bhakti Charu. They also take birth without pregnancy. (*Bhakti Charu laughs*. *After a pause, Tamal Krishna continues seriously*.) Others are apparently not so fortunate. We've got more than two hundred devotees asking for initiation. I haven't told Prabhupāda, because he told Bhagatjī that his illness is the result of accepting *so* many disciples. (*Walking*)

Prabhupāda lecturing in Juhu beside Rādhā-Rāsavihārī. We should title this "The Loyal Soldier"!

**Bhakti Charu:** Girirāj told me that Śrīla Prabhupāda considered fighting for Rādhā-Rāsavihārī his greatest victory of all.

**Tamal Krishna:** Yes, he did, and it was a long battle . . . And it's *still* going on, only now it has taken a new turn.

**Bhakti Charu:** What do you mean?

**Tamal Krishna:** When he first installed these Deities, he promised Them he would build Them the most magnificent temple of all if only They would stand in this tin shed to keep everything secure while he negotiated for the land. Rādhā-Rāsavihārī kept Their end of the bargain, and now Śrīla Prabhupāda is pressuring us to complete the temple. Sometimes I think that his promise to Rādhā-Rāsavihārī, more than anything else, keeps Prabhupāda struggling to survive. (*Walking*)

**Bhakti Charu:** What a contrast between that simple shed and this great palace.

**Tamal Krishna:** The chateau in France. Prabhupāda, surrounded by the French devotees.

**Bhakti Charu:** He looks so noble.

**Tamal Krishna:** Yet no more so than when sitting in a thatched hut in Māyāpur, or huddled together with hundreds of *karmīs* on a 747. (*Walking*) That photo was taken while walking through the streets of Moscow.

**Bhakti Charu:** It seems that he alone had the courage and inspiration to travel to foreign lands and humbly beg *mlecchas* and *yavanas* to please accept the mercy of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu. (*Walking*) Whenever I see this early picture of Prabhupāda standing in Tompkins Square Park, I'm struck by his compassion and quiet determination.

**Tamal Krishna:** The hardships he has gone through . . . Everyone is indebted to him, although most people know nothing of him.

**Bhakti Charu:** This is the real cause of Prabhupāda's illness . . .

**Tamal Krishna:** . . . not the failure of his kidneys, or mere age . . .

**Bhakti Charu:** . . . but the sins of the world.

**Tamal Krishna:** *Our* sins, Bhakti Charu. It is *our* sins that bear most heavily.

**Bhakti Charu:** I feel guilty. Do you?

**Tamal Krishna:** Guilty and indebted, eternally indebted.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*helplessly*) What can we do? When I see Prabhupāda (*tapping on a photo*) being offered a plate of *prasādam*, I feel even worse. He asks me to cook for him but takes nothing more than a little juice, and that very rarely. Seeing him as he was before, healthy, vigorous, I feel hopeless.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*reprimanding*) Never say that!

**Bhakti Charu:** (*blurring out*) Mahārāja, do you think Prabhupāda will *ever* recover?

**Tamal Krishna:** Will the sun rise? Can you imagine that it won't? Well, in my mind, it's the same with Prabhupāda. Without the sun this world would be finished. It's like that. I can't think of living without him. Prabhupāda seems to be living on love alone, and if our love grows faint from selfishness or neglect . . .

**Bhakti Charu:** (*interrupting, a bit ashamed*) Forgive me! I've become somewhat feverish during these months of his ordeal. Śrīla Prabhupāda binds me to him with his affection, and then shatters my hopes by saying there's no chance he'll recover. Love is a cruel prison-keeper. It brings me to the point of death and then suddenly grants me a reprieve.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*turning and listening*) Who's that coming down the stairs?

**Bhakti Charu:** Maybe it's Upendra. Bhagatjī and he were alone with Prabhupāda.

(*Prahhupāda's servant, Upendra, enters*.)

**Upendra:** (*to Tamal Krishna*) Good, you're still up. Prabhupāda wants to see you. Wait a moment while I bring him in from the veranda. (*He exits*.)

**Bhakti Charu:** What could Śrīla Prabhupāda want?

**Tamal Krishna:** Hmmm . . . at this hour I'm not sure. (*He pauses to think*.) You go ahead and take some rest. If it's important I'll wake you up.

**Bhakti Charu:** Good night, Mahārāja. (*He exits*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** (*to himself, as he ascends the stairs*) Bhagatjī will know. He is a Brijabāsī, and Prabhupāda speaks freely with him.

(*Bhagatjī enters and sits down*.)

**Bhagatjī:** (*Seeing Tamal Krishna, he nods his head in greeting and smiles*) *Daṇḍavat*, Mahārāja, *daṇḍavat*.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*half-jokingly*) Bhagatjī, I thought you were too old to climb these stairs.

**Bhagatjī:** (*proud of his loyalty*) Mahārāja, for Prabhupāda I'd climb a mountain.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*serious*) What has Prabhupāda been speaking about?

**Bhagatjī:** Prabhupāda is not talking tonight. He gave me his hand to hold.

**Tamal Krishna:** Prabhupāda says your massage is very good.

**Bhagatjī:** (*gravely*) Tonight there was no massage. I think Prabhupāda is feeling lonely.

(*Upendra enters, escorting Śrīla Prabhupāda in*. *Tamal Krislma offers obeisances while Bhagatjī bows his head and hands to the floor*. *Śrīla Prabhupāda moves with the greatest difficulty*. *His body is frail after months of illness*. *Upendra grips Prabhupāda beneath the arms and eases him down to a seat*. *The pain of seeing Prabhupāda's physical condition keeps everyone silent*.)

**Prabhupāda:** (*finally breaking the silence*) I think no one recovers from this disease. It is called dropsy. Look it up in the dictionary. (*He indicates a dictionary on the desk*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** (*reading from the dictionary*) "Dropsy: abnormal amount of fluid in the body tissues, causing swelling." (*He looks up for a response, then adds*.) Your eyes seem cloudy.

**Prabhupāda:** 1 can no longer see properly.

**Tamal Krishna:** Upendra and I were saying that you haven't done any translation for days.

**Prabhupāda:** (*shaking his head*) My brain also is not working. (*He pauses*) According to medical science the only cure is cutting or dialysis. (*He pauses*. *Then, conclusively*) My present health is so weak that death can take place at any time.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*encouraging*) But Āyur Vedic medicine says there's a cure. According to the *kavirāja*, the fluid that has collected in your body is actually transformed blood. That is why you are feeling so weak. And you haven't eaten anything for more than a month.

**Prabhupāda:** (*discouraged*) How shall I eat when I have no taste? In my opinion all doctors and their medicines are failures.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*keeping up the encouragement*) But you have always said that there must be some doctor, just as a woman must have a husband. What will happen if you reject the help of all doctors?

**Prabhupāda:** (*wittily*) Then I'll become a widow. (*He pauses, acknowledging their serious concern*.) One thing you *can* do. In your daily routine you can pray to Kṛṣṇa, "lf You want him to stay, please cure him, and if not, please take him away. We are fully surrendered to You. Now it depends on Your desire to keep him alive or let him leave this world."

But so far I am concerned, I am praying, "*kṛṣṇa tvadīya-pada-paṅkaja-pañjarāntam*: My dear Kṛṣṇa, please help me die immediately so that the swan of my mind may be encircled by the stem of Your lotus feet. Otherwise, at the time of my final breath, when my throat is choked up, how will it be possible for me to think of You?"

**Bhagatjī:** (*a loving protest*) Śrī Prabhupāda, you will never forget Kṛṣṇa. You are the greatest *yogī*, and not even death can disturb your meditation. We are not so advanced that we know how to meditate on Kṛṣṇa. All we can see is your two lotus feet. Your young children still cling to you. lt is not proper for a father to abandon such small children.

**Prabhupāda:** (*acknowledging*) It is only due to their prayers that I am still living. My Guru Mahārāja left very dissatisfied. But I like their association. (*Submissively*) I am surrendered to Kṛṣṇa. Whatever He desires.

**Bhagatjī:** ISKCON must first be made stronger.

**Prabhupāda:** That I am also thinking. Such a big society . . . The aims and objects may not be dismantled . . . I am thinking from that angle.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*energetically*) And Bombay is still unfinished. Rādhā-Rāsavihārīstand waiting. And the *Bhāgavatam* is not complete.

**Prabhupāda:** (*despondently*) What can I do? I am trying my best, but I see no indication of improvement. Therefore I think it is not Kṛṣṇa's desire. Perhaps Kṛṣṇa does not want me to recover. (*There is silence*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** (*with sudden inspiration*) Śrīla Prabhupāda, why not travel to the West! (*There is a long silence*.)

**Prabhupāda:** (*determined otherwise*) Let me die gloriously—in Vṛndāvana.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*persisting*) That's just it. Devotees come here to die. Once before, you left Vṛndāvana against all others' advice. By turning your back on old age and not caring for death, you gave everyone in this world a chance for eternal life. Now you must do it again.

**Prabhupāda:** There is a Bengali proverb, "Even if you go to heaven, your forehead will go with you." This disease is in the kidneys. So wherever I go, the kidneys will go as well.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*undeterred*) If you were to tour your temples and see all the devotees serving so sincerely, their love would sustain your life. And you could visit the farms, eat homegrown food, drink fresh water. Surely your appetite and taste would return.

**Prabhupāda:** (*responding somewhat*) Actually, I have a further ambition. People are suffering from agnosticism. I want to drive agnosticism from the face of this world.

**Upendra:** (*supportively*) Tamal Krishna's right. With you personally present among them, the devotees would work so hard that agnosticism *would* be annihilated.

**Prabhupāda:** Someone once told me that at eighty-one there would be some change. What is 1896 subtracted from the present?

**Upendra:** Eighty-one exactly!

**Prabhupāda:** (*ready to consider*) Let us consult an astrologer: whether I shall go, whether I shall be cured, how long I shall live. I was born in the evening at 4 P.M. It was *nanda-utsava*. You can consult an old *pañcikā* to see the day. It was a Tuesday. Bhagatjī, what is your opinion?

**Bhagatjī:** (*morosely*) No one should recommend traveling from Vṛndāvana in this condition. I think Tamal Krishna Mahārāja is more cruel than Akrūra.

**Prabhupāda:** (*taking up the argument himself*) To remain in Vṛndāvana is a sentiment. Wherever our centers are, that is Vaikuṇṭha. New York, Los Angeles, Paris, London, Melbourne. I am not so limited to think a certain place is my country. Everything is Kṛṣṇa's. Why should I limit Him? Once I had a dream that Vaikuṇṭha men came to take me. They were all white men, with shaven heads.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*full-steam*) Kṛṣṇa told Arjuna, "Either you will be killed on the battlefield and attain the heavenly planets, or you will conquer and enjoy the earthly kingdom. Therefore, get up with determination and fight."

**Prabhupāda:** (*fully responsive*)

*hato vā prāpsyasi svargaṁ*  
*jitvā vā bhokṣyase mahīm*  
*tasmād uttiṣṭha kaunteya*  
*yuddhāya kṛta-niścayaḥ*

At least my disciples will know I came at the risk of my life. They are the future hope, and it is my duty to enthuse them. (*Determined*) Kṛṣṇa ordered Arjuna, and I am Arjuna's servant. A soldier should die fighting on the battlefield. What is this slow death? Let me die a glorious death, fulfilling the order of my Guru Mahārāja. And if it is in New York, entomb me on the roof—there is no other place. So long I die among you, that is Vaikuṇṭha.

**Tamal Krishna:** Do not worry, Śrīla Prabhupāda, we shall not let you die. (*Envisioning*) First we can go to London, then New York and the Pennsylvania farm. From there to Los Angeles, and then on to Hawaii and Australia. In each place devotees can come from nearby temples to be with you. That lawn at Bhaktivedanta Manor. We'll help you walk a little each day. Rādhā-Londonīśvara and Gokulānanda are so beautiful.

**Prabhupāda:** (*to Upendra*) His words are making me feel different. Just hearing, I become enthused. (*To Tamal Krishna*) Yes, make a big party!

**Tamal Krishna:** If you go to the West, I know you will recover.

**Prabhupāda:** May Kṛṣṇa make your words fulfilled. I am prepared to go. (*All exit*.)

## Act II

(*A British devotee enters chanting* japa*, shortly followed by Vicitravīrya, the temple president of Bhaktivedanta Manor, near London, England*. *It is an early morning in the beginning of September*.)

**Vicitravīrya:** (*upon seeing the devotee*) Haribol, Prabhu!

How many books did you distribute yesterday?

**Devotee:** Eighty-nine, *Gītās* and *Bhāgavatams*.

**Vicitravīrya:** *Jaya*!

**Devotee:** I worked along Oxford Street until the shops closed, and then I distributed at the pubs.

**Vicitravīrya:** What time did you return?

**Devotee:** Just before *maṅgala-ārati*.

**Vicitravīrya:** (*surprised*) You haven't taken rest yet?

**Devotee:** No. I'm going to finish my rounds, take a short nap, and get ready to greet Śrīla Prabhupāda.

**Vicitravīrya:** What kept you out so late?

**Devotee:** When I first began distributing, a copper nicked me at Portabello Road and took me to be charged. But I preached to the sergeant. He remembered Prabhupāda from one Ratha-yātrā. I explained how Śrīla Prabhupāda has risked his life to come back to London, so the British devotees are on a marathon, hoping to keep him here by distributing more books than any other temple in the world. When the sergeant heard of Prabhupāda's condition, he became sympathetic and told me I could go. How is Prabhupāda feeling?

**Vicitravīrya:** Prabhupāda honors *prasādam*, but only a teaspoon-tip's worth. He speaks only rarely, but often makes a long deep sound like "hmmm." He says he feels no pain or disturbance, yet tears glide from his eyes, forming rivulets on his golden cheeks.

**Devotee:** What do these symptoms mean?

**Vicitravīrya:** It's hard to understand his thoughts and emotions.

**Devotee:** At least we *know* that Prabhupāda's tears don't come from ordinary suffering.

**Vicitravīrya:** No, certainly not. Why does the moon release its cooling rays? Or the monsoon cloud its soothing rains? Or a munificent man his wealth? Each delivers relief. So Prabhupāda cries tears of love of God to nourish our dry hearts. He used to conceal his emotions so they wouldn't be misunderstood. But now his tears flow at the slightest opportunity when he sits before Gokulānanda, or when he hears a *kīrtana*, or when he sees a beloved disciple.

**Devotee:** How do you feel seeing Prabhupāda in this way?

**Vicitravīrya:** (*sorrowfully*) As if each meeting may be the last.

**Devotee:** (*pointing upward*) Look! The light has just gone on in Śrīla Prabhupāda's bedroom.

**Vicitravīrya:** Finish your *japa* and pray that Prabhupāda comes down. (*They exit*.)

\[end of prelude\]

(*Śrīla Prabhupāda enters, lying down, with Tamal Krishna Goswami standing by his side*.)

**Prabhupāda:** (*opening his eyes*) Tamal Krishna?

**Tamal Krishna:** Yes, Śrīla Prabhupāda. Have you rested well?

**Prabhupāda:** Lift me up. (*Śrīla Prabhupāda places his arms around Tamal Krishna's neck and thus allows Tamal Krishna to raise him to a sitting position*. *Sitting behind him, Tamal Krishna proceeds to scratch Śrīla Prabhupāda's back*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** Śrīla Prabhupāda, Upendra must have put your shirt on backwards.

**Prabhupāda:** (*smiling while remembering*) My mother used to do that purposely so I wouldn't remove it. I think I'm becoming like a child again. I was my mother's pet child. Once I swallowed a watermelon pit, and my friends told me it would grow into a tree in my stomach. When I ran to my mother, she told me not to worry. She would chant a *mantra* to prevent any harm. (*He laughs*.) I was so dependent on her, I would run to her even for asking to go to the privy. "Who is stopping you?" she would ask. "Yes, you can go." Only then would I go. Now I have so many fathers and mothers, and once again I have become so dependent that I take help, even to sit up, to dress, or to bathe.

**Tamal Krishna:** Kṛṣṇa plays the same way, saying, "Mother, please feed Me. Please give Me protection!" He sustains the universe with His breath, but He shows dependence to evoke His mother's love.

**Prabhupāda:** Yaśodā's love is so strong that Kṛṣṇa is actually dependent. He is so bound by her loving affection that He is afraid to displease her. And I am the same. I am living only due to my disciples' love. Otherwise, in this condition, no one can maintain his life.

Kṛṣṇa has always surrounded me with well-wishers. Not only was I my mother's favorite, but my father's as well. When I was still a youth, one of my father's friends said to him that I was very intelligent so he should send me to England to become a lawyer. But my father said he would never send me to become a *mleccha*. His only wish was that I become a devotee of Rādhārāṇī. Still, when I grew up, I liked that friend's idea and thought, "Why not go to England and get a white wife and white children?" (*They both laugh*.)

In recent years, when I would take my morning walks here near the law colleges, I would think, "Now I've got thousands of white children, without the help of a white wife." (*Again laughter*)

**Tamal Krishna:** Like Lord Viṣṇu conceiving Brahmāwithout Lakṣmī's help.

**Prabhupāda:** The other day I was thinking how fortunate I was to always be surrounded by devotees. One old friend of mine, a pleader named Nandulal Pine, was such a nice devotee. He remarried when his wife died, though he was seventy years old. When offering obeisances, sometimes, because of his age, he would stay asleep like that for two or three hours. Yesterday, just by thinking of him, I felt purified.

Whatever I have introduced throughout the world—*prasādam* distribution, Deity worship, Ratha-yātrā—I was trained for in my childhood. I have simply imitated what I learned then. All except for writing books. That I have done on the order of my Guru Mahārāja. (*Pausing*) The fact is that in those days everyone was a devotee.

**Tamal Krishna:** Even now in India one can still feel the presence of Vedic culture.

**Prabhupāda:** (*distastefully*) But at present religion is being discouraged, and therefore conditions are becoming hellish.

(*Upendra enters, offering obeisances*.)

**Upendra:** Śrīla Prabhupāda, Harikeśa Swami has arrived with the German and Swiss devotees.

**Prabhupāda:** (*pleased and excited*) Oh! Let him come at once! (*Upendra offers obeisances and exits*.) Seeing all of you gives me life.

**Tamal Krishna:** Seeing your lotus feet, all of Europe has gotten life.

**Prabhupāda:** That you could transplant a half-dead lotus without losing its life is a great credit.

**Tamal Krishna:** I think you are the first lotus to thrive in this dreary climate.

**Prabhupāda:** I used to wonder, if the sun never sets on the British Empire, why it never rises in London? But so many devotees have come means so many suns. Therefore I am feeling better.

**Tamal Krishna:** Night makes the lotus wilt, but the sun gives life to its drooping face.

**Prabhupāda:** Yes, for me, India was a long, dark night. I was rotting in Vṛndāvana, thinking, "What is this slow death?"

Coming to London was the right decision. Kṛṣṇa-Balarāma have placed me in the care of Rādhā-Gokulānanda. Bhagavān has come and told me of the preaching in France and other countries. When I get reports like that my chest swells with pride to hear how our movement is expanding. Now help me up. It is a little cold, so only half-bath.

(*Tamal Krishna helps Prabhupāda rise and they slowly exit*. *After a short interval, Prabhupāda, nicely dressed, enters assisted by Tamal Krishna and moves to a seat*. *He is wearing dark sunglasses*. *Harikeśa Swami, Ghanaśyāma, Abhirāma, and a few others enter*, *offering full* daṇḍavats.)

**Prabhupāda:** Harikeśa!

**Harikeśa:** Yes, Prabhupāda?

**Prabhupāda:** (*with intense emotion and tears*) Come here! When I sent you away, you thought I was degrading you. But I thought, "Here is a very intelligent boy. I already have ten servants. Why should he be typewriting when he can do so much more?"

(*To the others*) He was typing so furiously, pushing for more dictation. And cooking all varieties of foodstuffs, even when I asked for simple *khicarī*. (*To Harikesa*) Now do you understand why I sent you away?

**Harikeśa:** (*also crying*) Now I understand, Śrīla Prabhupāda.

**Prabhupāda:** (*giving inspiration*) The whole world is simply waiting for Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Let us give them something. Let us make them happy. Open temples! Print books! (*Seeing him still crying*) Why are you crying?

**Harikeśa:** (*between tears*) Since you sent me away, my constant meditation has been to join you again. The new temples, the books, the devotees made—all just to attract you to look my way again.

**Prabhupāda:** (*apologetically*) I know that. The *gopīs* also meditated like this.

**Harikeśa:** (*fighting back the tears*) But I am not as advanced as the *gopīs*. I am just a conditioned soul. (*Beseechingly*) Please, Śrīla Prabhupāda, get better, more healthy.

**Prabhupāda:** (*with a detached calmness*) Healthy? I have nothing to do with this body. I have to do with the spiritual world.

**Harikeśa:** When I see you in this condition, I feel ill, too. And with your loss of appetite, who can think of eating? Seeing you wear those dark glasses makes me want to cry.

**Prabhupāda:** You are a young man; you have the right to live.

**Harikeśa:** Then I offer you my youth, as Puru offered his.

**Prabhupāda:** But I have no Devayānī. How shall I use your youth?

**Harikeśa:** For preaching! To deliver the world!

**Prabhupāda:** (*with calm reassurance*) That I shall do through all of you. At my age, death is not surprising. Still, if it is possible, I want to see you all a little stronger; a father is always concerned to see his estate nicely managed. (*There is silence*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** Śrīla Prabhupāda, Ghanaśyāma has come from Eastern Europe.

**Prabhupāda:** (*He looks and, seeing him, smiles*) Come closer. (*Ghanaśyāma* *moves to Harikeśa's side, and Prabhupāda strokes his head affectionately*.) This is the *paramparā* system. My Guru Mahārāja pushed me. I am pushing you, and you are pushing others. (*Appreciatively*) You are risking so much!

**Ghanaśyāma:** That is my only hope. Living behind the Iron Curtain. I know that Your Divine Grace and Lord Kṛṣṇa are my constant protectors.

**Prabhupāda:** I never expected standing orders from the Communists. In selling a thing, these points are important: the appearance of the salesman, the sweet language he uses, and the familiarity with the product. But in your case . . . they have never seen a black man, they do not know the language, and the subject is very difficult. Yet despite all obstacles, *Śrīmad*-*Bhāgavatam* is going through. It is wonderful.

**Ghanaśyāma:** The whole world feels indebted to you, Śrīla Prabhupāda.

**Prabhupāda:** (*proudly*) They must! I am giving real civilization. There is no hope for this Western so-called civilization. Hari Śauri's grandmother advised his mother to kill him while he was still in the womb. There is no culture, no religion. For woman—no husband; for father—no son.

**Ghanaśyāma:** And in many places—no food.

**Prabhupāda:** That I have also experienced. In some countries we could not get sugar, wheat, rice, milk, or fruit. Soon it shall be the same everywhere. As they make their materialistic programs, these things will finish. It is stated in the *Śrīmad*-*Bhāgavatam*. (*Indignantly*) You can tell them frankly, "If you don't take to Kṛṣṇa consciousness, these things will not be available. Then you will have to eat your philosophy. You will have to eat your own child's fetus." (*He pauses, reflecting on the injustices perpetrated by a godless society*.) Such a nasty civilization. (*There is silence*. *Tamal Krishna seeks to divert Prabhupāda's thoughts to a more pleasant subject*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** Śrīla Prabhupāda, Rāmeśvara Mahārāja has sent something very special for your pleasure. (*Handing him a new publication*) Śrīla Prabhupāda, Tenth Canto, Part One.

**Prabhupāda:** (*respectfully touching the* Bhāgavatam *to his forehead*) Ah, this is my life! lf book distribution increases, I shall never die. I will live for centuries. (*His strength has noticeably increased just by his holding the new book*.) Life is not within these bones, or manufactured in the chemist's lab. The living force, the spirit soul, is superior to these bones and flesh. There is a science by which you can sustain life only in bones. Hiraṇyakaśipu did it.

**Abhirāma:** You are also doing it, Śrīla Prabhupāda.

**Prabhupāda:** So take care of the bones as long as possible. But always remember what real life is. (*He turns the pages, enlivened*) Print more and more books. All that you collect, use half for printing, and half you may spend as you like. Whatever English books we have, translate them into all languages.

**Ghanaśyāma:** One Communist professor remarked that all intellectuals and scholars should read your books.

**Prabhupāda:** If they don't, their learning is imperfect. *Asampūrṇam*.

**Harikeśa:** Your books teach ideal Communism, *bhāgavata* Communism. They are the only solution for the world's problems because they contain directions for every section of society. No one has ever done a greater service.

**Prabhupāda:** (*smiling*) These books have got a standing appreciation. Wherever they go, we shall conquer.

**Harikeśa:** Everyone else speculates.

**Prabhupāda:** But our philosophy is realistic and practical. There is no imagination. Everything is based upon authoritative statements of the *ācāryas*. That is the difference between the materialists and us. They only take interest if something is different and new.

D. L. Roy has written a song: (*singing*)

Put your head down,  
Your legs up.  
You must do something new,  
No matter how odd or ugly  
Or rubbish it is!

(*They all laugh*.) But we say, "Stick to the old." Childish means you cannot stick to one principle. Therefore I say the Western civilization is childish. One car design finished, and next year a different one. (*His delivery quickens as he becomes absorbed in preaching*.) A devil's workshop. Entanglement in useless work. Chewing the chewed. Topless, bottomless. Naked dancing. This is material civilization. This is their happiness. Am I right about this? So remain Kṛṣṇa conscious. Stick to the principles. You are making definite progress. I can see you feel it. (*Looking to each for confirmation*) Harikeśa Mahārāja? Tamal Krishna? Ghanaśyāma Prabhu? (*Each nods in agreement*.) This *Bhāgavatam* is sufficient to nourish us all.

**Harikeśa:** So you must go on feeding us. Give us more and more books.

**Prabhupāda:** (*humbly*) I am only a child. (*Crying*) But I have tried to please my Guru Mahārāja. I have not written. It is Kṛṣṇa. Kṛṣṇa and my Guru Mahārāja. They have sent all of you to help me. Otherwise, what could I do, all alone, an old man with no friends in America? America is so good to me, to give me money, men, everything—I see no difference between one country and another, but because the Americans have given me so much facility to serve Kṛṣṇa, I cannot forget my obligations to them. I want to make them happy, and through them the whole world.

**Abhirāma:** Your feelings for America are sometimes misunderstood. Especially in India.

**Prabhupāda:** (*disappointed*) Such a mistake they have made in India! From the members of Parliament to the members of the street, so many suspect that I have brought the CIA. (*Pained*) What do they want? That I should have remained in India? I tried for two decades without the slightest response. I offered them diamonds, but they wanted only broken glass. They will do anything to resist Kṛṣṇa. I did not go as a beggar of Western technology. I went to distribute India's greatest wealth, her spiritual culture, kept hidden for centuries.

And what a reception I got! All over the world, even in Russia, I am thanked, even praised. But on returning, where is the hero's reception? Where is the joy that our son has represented us well? Instead, condemnation and suspicion, mistrust and abuse. (*Regretfully*) How unfortunate they have become!

Of course, there are exceptions. Our life members and friends have supported us in many ways. I'm not speaking of them. The majority of people would naturally appreciate what I have done, but they are being misled.

And when I try to help them, they do everything to prevent. The government wishes to know why I have built such a large *prasādam* pavilion in Māyāpur. Should I have grown some vegetables to feed a few hundred instead of feeding hundreds of thousands, as we are now doing? Their answer: CIA, harass the old rascal.

During the recent shooting incident, the newspapers reported, "The founder-*ācārya* was not present." Their intent was clear: to place me under arrest and humiliate me as far as possible. If I had been locked up in a small jail cell, I would have died. (*After pausing to reflect*) Kṛṣṇa has personally saved my honor, prestige, and life.

(*Fatigued and bitter*) Frankly, I must tell you I am disgusted. I do not wish to return there. My best energy has been spoiled, my health lost. They put me into so much uncomfortable position, I sometimes cry. (*Anguished and choked with tears*) They do not appreciate me. It's hard to find such nice devotees as there are here. In India they think everyone is God, everyone is a transcendentalist. Cent percent I do not want to return. I would rather die in the battlefield preaching than such an inglorious death.

(*The sound of a roaring* kīrtana *comes from offstage, attracting everyone's attention*. *Vicitravīrya enters*.)

**Vicitravīrya:**

Śrīla Prabhupāda—(*oratorically*)  
This *kīrtan* fills the world with boundless joy.  
The European Vaiṣṇav voices call.  
Gaurāṅga's army, yours now to deploy  
Awaits your presence in the *kīrtan* hall.  
Devotees shout like elephants in rut,  
In madness drinking deep of *kṛṣṇa*-*prem,*  
With graceful dance and leap and prancing strut,  
Declaring loud Śrī Kṛṣṇa's holy name.  
The *kartāls* jingle, jangle, clang, and strike;  
The drums and conches add to the rhythmic din.  
The beat grows faster, faster, till, boltlike,  
The sound destroys the heart of every sin.  
This splendid sound—none can its charm avoid—  
Makes friends embrace their foes without a fear.  
Nor thirst, nor hunger knows one who's enjoyed  
The mellows of the holy name, most dear.  
May Hare Kṛṣṇa *mantra*, so sublime,  
Free Your Divine Grace from the grip of time.

**Prabhupāda:** (*happily to others*) How nicely composed! *Bhavauṣadhāc chrotra-mano-'bhirāmāt, ka uttama-śloka-guṇānuvādāt*. *Kīrtana* is the remedy for all diseases. It pleases the ear and mind. With so many qualified doctors administering such potent medicine, can there be any doubt of the cure? (*To Vicitravīrya*) I have heard that the British devotees are expert at playing dramas.

**Upendra:** I have watched them perform at the Sunday Feast. They're very talented.

**Prabhupāda:** What subject were you performing?

**Vicitravīrya:** "Bhīṣma's Perfection!"

**Prabhupāda:** Does it concern Bhīṣma's passing away?

**Vicitravīrya:** Yes, Śrīla Prabhupāda.

**Prabhupāda:** (*interested*) Oh? That is most important. I want to see this drama. Can you perform it just now?

**Vicitravīrya:** (*a bit unprepared*) Well . . . ah . . .

**Tamal Krishna:** (*alarmed, to Abhirāma*) This could have a contrary effect.

**Vicitravīrya:** . . . yes, I think so.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*intervening*) Śrīla Prabhupāda, won't watching a play be too exhausting?

**Prabhupāda:** (*to Tamal Krishna*) No. Not at all. (*To the others*) Let us see it. Being with all the devotees will wipe away the painful thoughts of India.

**Vicitravīrya:** (*calling out*) Amita! Tīrthakara! Bring the palanquin!

(*Two* brahmacarīs*, Amita and Tīrthakara, enter carrying a palanquin*. *Abhirāma and Upendra help Prabhupāda sit on it*. *The bearers lift it*.)

**Ghanaśyāma:** Śrīla Prabhupāda, you look very regal.

(*They move around in procession, all following* *Śrīla* *Prabhupada's palanquin, as the* kīrtana *from offstage begins to chant "*Jaya *Prabhupāda*! Jaya *Prabhupāda*!*" When the palanquin is finally placed down, Prabhupāda remains sitting, while all others sit around him to watch the drama*.)

## Act III

(*While Śrīla* *Prabhupāda and his disciples sit attentively, a stage director enters to introduce the play,* *"Bhīṣma's Perfection," which is accompanied by music appropriate to a military theme, with drums especially prominent*. *The actors are spotlighted*. *Prabhupāda and others are to the side, with faint lighting*.)

Director:

The sky of Kurukṣetra was ablaze  
As Bhīṣma faced Arjuna's angry rays.  
May you be blessed with Bhīṣma's strength of life,  
To ward off death as Bhīṣma did a wife.  
Please hear how Bhīṣma met his doom,  
Eclipsing Kṛṣṇa and Arjun'  
As Rāhu hid the sun and moon.

**Voice Offstage:** (*angrily*)

Who dares compare great Bhīṣma to that cheat?

**Director:** (*He looks apprehensively in the direction of the voice*) It's Droṇācārya. My exit must be fleet.

(*He exits*.)

**Droṇācārya:** (*entering*)

Who dares compare great Bhīṣma to that rogue  
Speaks not of truth but suits poetic vogue.  
I'll call my son, this battle to survey.  
Dread omens loom, portending our doomsday.  
(*Shouting*) Aśvatthāmā! Aśvatthāmā! (*Aśvatthāmāenters*.)  
See how the sons of Pāṇḍu all arrange  
To place Śikhaṇḍī first; is it not strange?  
Arjun' and Bhīma carefully guard his sides.  
All our worth is naught if Bhīṣma dies.

Aśvatthāmā:

Many's the time they've tried this trick before,  
Yet Bhīṣma reigns with no competitor.  
(*Bhīṣma enters dancing in a chivalrous mood and hurling weapons in all directions*.)  
Behold him now, grandsire of all who fight:  
Śrī Bhīṣma, Gaṅgā's son, the gods' delight.  
Behold his battle dance unleash a scourge  
Of weapons wielded to a deadly dirge.  
He smiles while show'ring death-tipped arrows down,  
A brilliant sun becloaked in rainy gown.  
Like *yajñic* chants, the *mantras* Bhīṣma roars  
Promote his rivals up through heaven's doors.  
Libations offered use their blood for ghee.  
The sacrificial fire: hostility.  
Beheaded victims rush down streams of blood.  
Their heads float bloated, fishlike, in the flood.  
Proud Pāṇḍavas, now gasp your final breath:  
May Bhīṣma's blessings hurl you to your death!

(*Śikhaṇḍī* *enters in a chivalrous mood, stalking Bhīṣma, who repeatedly turns to avoid confrontation*.)

Droṇācārya:

Not so! Śikhaṇḍī's bowstring mocks your word,  
Mocks wooingly like a ladybird.

(*Śikhaṇḍī* *stalks Bhīṣma, who continues to avoid him*.)

**Prabhupāda:** (*explaining to the devotees*) Śikhaṇḍī was previously a woman named Ambā who was granted the boon of killing Bhīṣma.

**Śikhaṇḍī:** (*haughtily to Bhisma*) Coward! Ashamed to face your destiny.

**Bhīṣma:** (*nobly*) A woman faced, effaces dignity.

**Śikhaṇḍī:** (*stung by these words*) Ambā's death has not decreased my hate.

**Bhīṣma:** Nor my resolve as lifetime celibate.

**Aśvatthāmā:** (*to Droṇācārya*)

Look how Śikhaṇḍī's arrows meet their mark  
As Arjuna rushes toward our patriarch.  
A meteor wreaking havoc in his trail . . .

**Droṇācārya:** (*fearing for Bhisma's safety*)

Away! Make haste! Arjuna's wrath assail!

(*Droṇācārya and Aśvatthāmāboth exit*.)

**Bhīṣma:** (*eagerly to himself*)

Come to me, Death, my life to consecrate.  
Oh, hear me now, for I will fight no more;  
Make haste, Arjun'. The day is growing late.  
Do slay me now and end this damned war.  
(*Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna now enter*. *They dance menacingly about Bhīṣma, Śikhaṇḍī* *always in front of them*. *Bhīṣma looks at them but makes no attempt at defense*.)  
(*To Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna*)  
Come, welcome Death, my soul to liberate.  
Come, Arjun', help me to my dying bed.  
Last night I promised you I'd abdicate.  
No tears of pity should for me be shed.  
The Pāṇḍavas should be the heads of state.  
But that can only be when I lie dead,  
A hero's couch of arrows set for me,  
With Kṛṣṇa 'fore me all eternity.

**Voices Offstage:** (*as if from the sky, a long and drawn-out tribute*) Bhīṣma! Bhīṣma! Bhīṣma!

**Kṛṣṇa:** (*impellingly*) Now, Arjuna! Shoot!

**Arjuna:** (*with much aversion, no longer brandishing weapons*)

O Lord!  
I can't kill him, more dear to me than life,  
For life is death if I outlive this strife;  
If this be *dharma*, surely this is hell;  
Where *dharma* serves *adharma* 'tis death's knell.  
Death cannot purchase one alone of us,  
For killing him I kill myself as well.

**Kṛṣṇa:** (*In a berating tone, He exhorts Arjuna to action*)

Arjuna!  
What selfish, ill-judged words do you now utter,  
Heart hardened, mind yet soft as melted butter?  
Now when his valor's fury grows tranquil,  
This final act, enact it to My will!  
For love of Bhīṣma, though 'tis agony,  
Slay Bhīṣma now and thus set Bhīṣma free.

**Arjuna:** (*with a heavy heart*)

O Lord, the world shall never be the same  
Without her Kuru hero. What is fame?

(*Guiltily, as he raises his bow to shoot*)

O grandfather, forgive me!

(*He carefully aims an arrow and releases it*. *Bhīṣma* *makes no attempt to defend himself having dropped his weapons*. *Now the drums have stopped beating as Arjuna continues to shoot arrow after arrow, his lips tightly pressed together though tears stream from his eyes*. *Bhīṣma* *is repeatedly wounded*.)

**Voices Offstage:** (*sounded from the heavens in glorious tribute*) Bhīṣma! Bhīṣma! Bhīṣma!

(*Bhīṣma falls from his chariot, the arrows shot through his body forming a bed*. *There is silence*.)

**Prabhupāda:** (*calling in the dark*) Tamal?

**Tamal Krishna:** Yes, Prabhupāda?

**Prabhupāda:** I am feeling exhausted. Take me upstairs.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*calling out to the others*) Turn on the lights. (*The lights come on*.) Prabhupāda will go to his quarters now. Have *kīrtana*.

(*Everyone rises and accompanies Prabhupāda upstairs with "*Jaya *Prabhupāda*!*"* kīrtana. *Afier reaching the sitting room, Upendra helps Prabhupāda move from the palanquin to a seat*. *Tamal Krishna asks everyone to leave*. *All exit, except Prabhupāda, Tamal Krishna, Upendra*, *and Abhirāma*.)

**Abhirāma:** (*breaking the silence, concerned*) Are you feeling all right, Śrīla Prabhupāda? (*His eyes closed in a thoughtful mood, Prabhupāda indicates he is, with a slight nod of his head*.)

**Upendra:** Did you enjoy the play? (*Again, Prabhupāda nods concordantly, his eyes still shut*. *Upendra continues enthusiastically*.) The second act depicts Bhīṣma's instructions to Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira, and in Act Three . . .

**Prabhupāda:** (*interrupting*, *as if coming out of deep thought and not having heard the previous words*) Bhīṣma was so powerful that no one could kill him.

**Tamal Krishna:** But Arjuna was able to—with Kṛṣṇa's help.

**Prabhupāda:** (*smiling*) No. He continued to live for more than two months, though thousands of arrows were lodged in his body. Even Kṛṣṇa was amazed at Bhīṣma's endurance and his power to hold off death.

**Upendra:** Bhīṣma refused to fight with Śikhaṇḍībecause Śikhaṇḍīwas actually a woman. But why didn't Bhīṣma continue to fight Arjuna?

**Prabhupāda:** Due to his love for the Pāṇḍavas, his grandsons. When they saw that Bhīṣma alone was powerful enough to destroy their entire army, they approached him to ask how they might defeat him. Bhīṣma then told them to keep Śikhaṇḍīalways in front.

**Upendra:** It seems he was partial to the Pāṇḍavas.

**Prabhupāda:** He knew that Kṛṣṇa wanted the Pandavas to win. He was fully surrendered to Kṛṣṇa's will.

**Abhirāma:** And Kṛṣṇa reciprocated by empowering to assuage Yudhiṣṭhira's grief through instructions on *dharma*. Vyāsadeva, and even Kṛṣṇa Himself, could not console Yudhiṣṭhira, but Bhīṣma did despite his agonizing condition.

**Prabhupāda:** That is Kṛṣṇa's kindness. He would rather see His devotee glorified. When Bhīṣma was lying on the bed of arrows, Kṛṣṇa gave a special benediction: Bhīṣma would feel neither pain nor weakness, and his intelligence would be as keen as a sword. And at last he would die with Kṛṣṇa standing before him.

(*There is silence as Prabhupāda is again deep in thought, his eyes closed*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** (*a little hesitant*) Śrīla Prabhupāda? What are you thinking?

**Prabhupāda:** (*his eyes open to reveal a look of fixed resolve*) That my death may be equally glorious. I want to die like a soldier, preaching in the field. Even if I become unconscious, do not put me in a hospital to die, plugged up with tubes. That would be like Bhīṣma dying at the hands of Śikhaṇḍī. Bhīṣma was pleased that the arrows which did the worst harm were Arjuna's and not Śikhaṇḍī's. All of you, give me the benediction to die a glorious death, in the presence of devotees and the holy name of Kṛṣṇa.

**Tamal Krishna:** Whatever our power may be, we bless you to live, not die, and to continue to instruct us.

**Prabhupāda:** There are some instructions only death can give.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*to Abhir*ā*ma*) See what this play has done!

**Prabhupāda:** To cushion Bhīṣma's head they brought silk pillows, but he rejected them. Only Arjuna could understand Bhīṣma's mind, so he at once shot three arrows, and their feathery ends formed a suitable cushion. And no one could find a drink to quench Bhīṣma's thirst. So Arjuna invoked a *mantra* he alone knew and brought the sacred Ganges from the ground.

These are my final days. Be attentive like Arjuna and make it possible for me to remember Kṛṣṇa until the very end. Now let me take some rest. (*All exit*.)

## Act IV

\[Prelude\]

(*From offstage comes the voice of Abhirāma, straining to communicate via the Indian telephone system*.)

**Abhirāma:** (*loudly*) Hello? Hello, operator? Yes, this is Abhirāma speaking. You're placing my call to America? . . . What did you say? . . . There's no response? Operator, I've been trying this call for three days. Let it ring longer. Someone is definitely there. (*Suddenly, with excitement*) Hello, Śrutirūpā! This is Abhirāma. l'm calling from Vṛndāvana. (*Shouting*) Yes, Abhirāma . . . What? Yes, Prabhupāda's here . . . (*Shouting*) Vṛndāvana. Why did he return? It was an emergency. His health has gotten . . . Śrutirūpā, now calm down. (*To himself*) God! She's hysterical. (*Firmly*) Śrutirūpā, listen! (*Shouting*) What? I can't hear you. Speak louder . . . How's Prabhupāda now'? No improvement. No. Even if you come, you won't be able to cook. He's not eating at all . . . What? Who's here? All the GBC and many other devotees. It's very serious. Śrutirūpā? Hello, Śrutirūpā? (*Shouting, his words drawn out*) Can . . . you . . . hear . . . me? (*Pause*) Damn! We've been cut off.

\[End Of Prelude\]

(*Śrīla Prabhupāda, lying down, enters*. *Then Haṁsadūta Swami enters with two other devotees, They sit to the side and begin singing the* Brahma*-*saṁhitā *prayers softly, accompanied by harmonium and a small set of* karatālas. *The room is dimly lit*. *After one or two minutes Kīrtanānanda Swami enters, followed by Brahmānanda Swami, Tamal Krishna Goswami, Bhav*ā*nanda Goswami, Harikeśa Swami, Satsvarūpa dāsa Goswami, Bhakti Charu Swami, Hari Śauri, Upendra*. *Abhirāma, and Viśāla*. *They gather around Prabhupāda's bed*. *It is just past noon, early in the month of October*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** (*Prabhupāda does not acknowledge their presence, so he leans forward to touch Prabhupāda's arm softly*) Śrīla Prabhupāda, we're here.

**Prabhupāda:** Hmmm. Who else? (*His speech throughout will be slow and weak*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** Kīrtanānanda Mahārāja, Harikeśa, Brahmānanda, Satsvarūpa, Bhavānanda . . .

**Prabhupāda:** Hmmm. Kīrtanānanda? (*He clears his throat and then coughs up deep mucus and spits into a spittoon held by Upendra*. *The devotees performing* kīrtana *have stopped and moved closer*.) The *Āyur Veda* describes a disease where one drowns in his own mucus. (*He says this to settle any doubts as to the deterioration of his health*.) You see, whatever I take turns to mucus. Therefore I have stopped taking everything. Only a little *caraṇāmṛta*. And the holy name. *Amala* *harinām* *amiya*-*vilāsā*.

**Hari Śauri:** That is the sweetest nectar.

**Abhirāma:** (*anticipating a negative response, yet dutiful as well as loving*) Śrīla Prabhupāda, should we call for a doctor?

**Prabhupāda:** (*tired of trying so many doctors*) These doctors will come to give something to try and save. (*Conclusively*) I don't want to be saved.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*encouraging*) It has been more than three days since you took anything to eat. But you seem more peaceful than in many months. To depend on *kīrtana* is best, because then at least we can pray to Kṛṣṇa.

**Prabhupāda:** (*dampening any hope*) Better you don't pray to Kṛṣṇa to save me. When I was in London, I thought recovery was possible. But I have tried everything, and it is all a failure. If I want to survive, of course I must take something. But my survival means so many complications. My lungs will fill with mucus, the food remain undigested, no strength to move. Simply sleep. (*Appalled at the thought*) What kind of living is that'? It is worse than death. (*After a pause, conclusively*) Now I have finally made up my mind: I want to die peacefully here in Vṛndāvana.

(*There is total silence except for an occasional "hmmm " from Prabhupāda*. *It is so still, one can hear the thoughts of each devotee who sits there*.)

**Abhirāma:** (*to himself while looking at the others*) How lifeless everyone looks: like statues staring silently, each absorbed in his thoughts.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*lamenting, to himself*) O Prabhupāda! I wish we hadn't come back to India, when America was so eager to receive you. Now how can I possibly take you again?

**Kīrtanānanda:** (*lamenting, to himself*) How unfortunate that I wasn't in London to persuade Prabhupāda to take help from a proper doctor.

**Upendra:** (*doubtfully, to himself*) Did I worry too much about my family and neglect him? Did I forget his medicine or overlook some other service?

**Harikeśa:** (*disappointed, to himself*) So near, but I can offer no help. I'm just making Prabhupāda suffer. My mind is numb, but my body goes on breathing. If only I could die for my sinfulness.

**Satsvarūpa:** (*disappointed, to himself*) If Prabhupāda leaves this world, the devotees will be close behind, just like bees leaving a flower as soon as the honey is gone. These thoughts would not please Prabhupāda: He'd want us to be selfless, to carry on the preaching.

**Bhavānanda:** (*to himself*) My mind is feverish!

**Upendra:** (*lamenting to himself*) How can Prabhupāda leave? Where will I go? Who will give us shelter now?

(*Hari Śauri is heard sobbing*.)

**Prabhupāda:** (*Knowing their thoughts, he protests*) Why do you want me to survive? If I want to die, this is the way of peaceful death. A little *caraṇāmṛta* and the holy name.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*thoughtfully, to himself*) I must stop this before it goes any further. (*Then, out loud*) Śrīla Prabhupāda, may I go out for a few minutes?

**Prabhupāda:** Why?

**Tamal Krishna:** I want to discuss with the others what should be done.

**Prabhupāda:** (*contemptuously*) For a discussion? They want me to survive, and I want to die peacefully. I cannot make miracles. The physical body has to be maintained if I am to survive. But without taking food, how the physical body will go on? You are indulging in fanaticism. (*There is a long silence*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** (*seeing Prabhupāda's determination*) Everything is in Kṛṣṇa's hands.

**Prabhupāda:** (*opening his eyes*) Kṛṣṇa wants me to do as *I* like. The *choice* is *mine*. Kṛṣṇa has given me full freedom.

**Kīrtanānanda:** (*to himself, suddenly alert*) What? Śrīla Prabhupāda has actually thrown us a rope to save us from despair!

**Brahmānanda:** (*reassuring*) It doesn't matter, Śrila Prabhupāda. Whether you live or die, you will always be with Kṛṣṇa. And by following your instructions, we will always be with you.

**Kīrtanānanda:** (*to himself, upset*) Kṛṣṇa! What has he said!

**Prabhupāda:** (*taking his cue from Brahmānanda's words*) Yes, whether I live or die, I will always be Kṛṣṇa's servant. If Brahmānanda assures me that this movement will go on, then better let me die peacefully.

(*After a pause, the* kīrtana *party resumes its soft singing while others exit from Prabhupāda's sitting room to the reception room in another section of the stage*. *Upendra alone remains sitting beside Prabhupāda*.)

**Bhavānanda:** (*afier reaching the next room, excitedly*) Did you hear that?! Prabhupāda said that Kṛṣṇa has given him full freedom to do as he likes.

**Brahmānanda:** (*full of guilt*) And I could kill myself for my reply. My pledge of faith turned into an indifferent prophecy of death. Kṛṣṇa! My foolish uncontrolled tongue!

**Abhirāma:** For days we've all seen Prabhupāda slipping away, no longer even bothering to continue the struggle. Yet these torturous months have forced me to wish Prabhupāda serene . . .

**Harikeśa:** (*Due to the tension, he interrupts*) But not *this* kind of serenity. We wish him peace for translating and preaching.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*helplessly*) But what can we do? I feel so *helpless*! On the one side is death, ominous, all-devouring, and on the other Śrīla Prabhupāda, frail and unresisting. What good is all our devotion if we can't save Prabhupāda!

**Satsvarūpa:** (*suggestively*) The real point is how we can encourage Prabhupāda to stay.

**Kīrtanānanda:** (*with conviction*) Yes, the choice is ours, in a way. Kṛṣṇa has given the crucial decision over to Śrīla Prabhupāda, but hasn't He given us a choice as well? So, let's assert *our* will by pleading with Prabhupāda to stay.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*regaining hope*) That's it! Śrīla Prabhupāda is a pure devotee, and if he wishes to stay here, would the Lord refuse him?

**Abhirāma:** But do you think Prabhupāda would petition Kṛṣṇa for his own sake?

**Kīrtanānanda:** (*arguing with conviction*) Uttarā was not selfish when she prayed for Kṛṣṇa to protect her unborn child. She knew that in the future her child would protect the world. Well, Prabhupāda's disappearance will be more powerful than any *brahmāstra*. It could easily destroy our movement.

**Brahmānanda:** (*agreeing*) Even if ISKCON survived, it would never be the same.

**Satsvarūpa:** We'd be like the devotees in Vṛndāvana after Kṛṣṇa left. Even during festive times they cried in separation.

**Brahmānanda:** We need Prabhupāda! That should be our understanding. There's no question of allowing Prabhupāda to leave us, is there?!

**Kīrtanānanda:** (*driving home the point*) Why should we think that there is no hope for him to recover? Jesus could bring people back to life from the dead, and even mundane *yogīs* can do it. So Śrīla Prabhupāda can certainly recover, if he wants.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*wishing to remove any lingering considerations*) Prabhupāda used the word "survive." Do we merely want him to "survive" as he has been doing these past months?

**Brahmānanda:** (*absolute*) No! He has to fully recover and then finish the entire *Śrīmad*-*Bhāgavatam*!

**Hari Śauri:** We could ask Śrīla Prabhupāda to stay at least five or ten *years*, why just a while?

**Harikeśa:** (*buoyantly*) Five or ten? We should ask him to stay for one hundred years!

**Tamal Krishna:** Who wants to present all this to Śrīla Prabhupāda?

**Brahmānanda:** (*certain*) It should be Kīrtanānanda. (*Others confirm with "Yes"and "*Jaya*" and "Let's go in now," as Kīrtanānanda accepts their vote with a nod*. *They all enter Prabhupāda's room and surround his bed*. *As they sit silently, the* kīrtana *party stops and comes over also*. *Though Prabhupāda is motionless, with eyes closed, he is alert to their presence*. *At last Kīrtanānanda attempts to speak, but his lips quiver, his eyes fill with tears, and he breaks down sobbing, his head resting on the side of the bed*. *Prabhupāda, his eyes still closed*, *tries to reach out and touch Kīrtanānanda's head*. *but he cannot find it*.)

**Prabhupāda:** Who?

**Hari Śauri:** Kīrtanānanda.

**Prabhupāda:** (*Finding Kīrtanānanda now, he rubs his head gently*) Hmmm? So, what do you want?

(*There is silence as Kīrtanānanda still cries*. *Brahmānanda rubs Kīrtanānanda's back encouragingly*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** (*whispering*) Kīrtanānanda, say *something*.

**Kīrtanānanda:** (*Afier a long pause he raises his head and looks at Śrīla Prabhupāda with great emotion, his voice quivering*) If Kṛṣṇa gives you the choice, don't go! (*Blurts out*) We need you!

**Prabhupāda:** (*After a pause he waves his hand to indicate everyone*) So this is your joint opinion? You have all discussed?

**Brahmānanda:** (*trembling, but strong*) Yes, we have all met together, Śrīla Prabhupāda. We want you to stay and lead this movement and finish *Śrīmad*-*Bhāgavatam*. You should stay at least another ten years. You've only done fifty percent of your work.

**Prabhupāda:** (*shaking his head in disagreement with the last remark*) No. (*There is a very long, intense silence*. *It is broken only by an occasional, slow, thoughtful "hmmm" uttered by Śrīla* *Prabhupāda*. *The atmosphere is surcharged with suspense*. *Suddenly, his eyes still closed, Śrīla* *Prabhupāda yawns, and then he immediately states his decision nonchalantly*.) All right.

**Harikeśa:** (*laughing briefly*) *Jaya*, Śrīla Prabhupāda!

(*Others also laugh nervously, not sure whether to laugh or cry and still not fully convinced that Prabhupāda will stay*. *Again there is silence, to see what Prabhupāda will do next*.)

**Prabhupāda:** So, give me something to drink.

**Brahmānanda:** Śrīla Prabhupāda, *ki*—

**All:** (*with great relief, loudly*) *Jaya!*

**Kīrtanānanda:** (*crying happily*) All glories to Śrīla Prabhupāda! (*Others happily shout,* "Jaya!" *and* "Jaya, *Prabhupāda!*")

**Prabhupāda:** (*when they have quieted down*) Now help me sit up. (*The devotees lift Prabhupāda*. *Upendra hands him a glass of grape juice*. *Slowly and deliberately, Prabhupāda drinks the entire glass as the devotees watch breathlessly*. *Again they cheer,* "Jaya*, Śrīla Prabhupāda!"*) This is real affection. Simply seeing all of you gives me vital force. I am living simply due to your love. All you Vaiṣṇavas, be merciful to me.

**Bhakti Charu:** If you had ordered us to drown ourselves in the Yamunā, we would have, but we never could tolerate your leaving us.

**Prabhupāda:** Thank you very much. Hare Kṛṣṇa.

**Tamal Krishna:** Actually, Śrīla Prabhupāda, you gave your word to Kṛṣṇa in Bombay that you would see Him sitting in His big new temple. You haven't fully kept your word to Him. (*Prabhupāda smiles broadly*.)

**Brahmānanda:** We have fixed a date for the Bombay opening—first of January.

**Prabhupāda:** Oh?

**Brahmānanda:** So we would like to invite you to come, Śrīla Prabhupāda. It is your temple. You have asked Kṛṣṇa to come there. When we all gave up, you carried on the fight.

**Prabhupāda:** Yes. (*Smiling broadly*) That was a great fight. And after fighting, to construct such an important temple is a great triumph.

**Tamal Krishna:** I don't think Kṛṣṇa will come into the temple unless you are personally there to open the doors.

**Prabhupāda:** (*He smiles*. *After a pause, to Kīrtanānanda*) And in America? Kṛṣṇa's palace? When will it be ready?

**Kīrtanānanda:** Early spring, as soon as the weather is a little warmer. It gives you just enough time to recuperate after going to Bombay. By then your palace will be ready. I have nearly seventy-five letters from the devotees of New Vrindaban begging that you come. (*Taking a small box from his pocket*) They have made this gold ring with valuable jewels for you.

**Prabhupāda:** (*accepting the ring and allowing it to be put on*) So, where is the bride? (*Everyone laughs*.)

**Viśāla:** (*loudly*) Śrīla Prabhupāda? (*Everyone looks at Viśāla, surprised that he has spoken out*.)

**Prabhupāda:** Who?

**Viśāla:** It's me, Viśāla. (*Zealously and unabashedly*) Śrīla Prabhupāda, anyone who has had even a moment of your association has become a recipient of Kṛṣṇa's special favor. There's no end to your glories. It would be easier to count the stars in the sky, the grains of sand on the earth, the waves in the sea, for in time your presence will have liberated every living entity. Let the sun stop shining, the heavens become submerged, and the entire universe cease to exist—even then your glories will continue to be chanted beyond the limits of time and space. Śrīla Prabhupāda, *ki*-

ALL (*loudly*) *Jaya*!

**Viśāla:** Śrīla Prabhupāda, *ki*—

ALL (*still louder*) *Jaya!!*

**Viśāla:** Śrīla Prabhupāda, *ki*—

ALL (*joyously*) *Jaya!!!*

**Prabhupāda:** (*after the excitement has subsided*) So now let me take a little rest. If Haṁsadūta is not too tired, he can continue singing.

(*Haṁsadūta softly leads the* kīrtana *party in* Brahma*-*saṁhitā *prayers as everyone exits*, *after offering obeisances*.)

## Act V

\[Prelude\]

(*Upendra, his wife Citralekhā, his five-year-old son Saumya, and his three-year-old daughter Śāntāya enter the courtyard in front of Prabhupāda's residence*. *It is an afternoon late in the month of October*.)

**Upendra:** Śāntāya! Stop teasing your brother. Citralekhā, if you can't control them, they're not going to see Prabhupāda.

**Citralekhā:** (*shaking them both by the arms*) Both of you, stop it! (*To Upendra*) They'll be good. (*The children play quietly off to the side, and Citralekhā occasionally straightens them out during the ensuing conversation*.) I still can't believe that nearly all the senior devotees have left. We heard in America that Śrīla Prabhupāda had fully submitted himself to a strict regimen under their care.

**Upendra:** That's true. They induced Prabhupāda to accept the help of an allopathic doctor, which normally he would have refused. Kīrtanānanda Mahārāja compared himself to mother Yaśodā, who had to force Kṛṣṇa to take His bath, dress nicely, and eat. But mother Yaśodācould forget that Kṛṣṇa was God. Kīrtanānanda couldn't forget that Śrīla Prabhupāda was his spiritual master, who should not be pressured, what to speak of chastised.

**Citralekhā:** ls that why he and the others went back to the West?

**Upendra:** Well, sort of. Though he seemed to be dependent on our care, Prabhupāda was actually independent. When his sister Pisīmāarrived, he decided she should cook for him.

**Citralekhā:** So, his health was improving?

**Upendra:** Not really. Kīrtanānanda Mahārāja said that such rich cooking was like poison, from a medical point of view. But Prabhupāda countered that his body was already dead, so what was the question of being poisoned? Then Kīrtanānanda argued that Prabhupāda's body was not dead but very much alive. When Prabhupāda heard this he laughed and said that Kīrtanānanda had given up his scientific reasoning and was now talking of miracles. Prabhupāda told him, "Since you believe in miracles, you should give up your dependence on medical science and instead pray to Kṛṣṇa."

**Citralekhā:** Did Prabhupāda eat Pisīmā's cooking?

**Upendra:** Yes. And afterwards he announced, "Medical science finished."

**Citralekhā:** No treatment of any kind?

**Upendra:** I think Prabhupāda was worried that bringing in an allopathic doctor would lead to being hospitalized. Recently an old Delhi friend of his was slightly ill, and by mistake his diagnosis was exchanged with another, and he was treated for tuberculosis. Prabhupāda said it was safer to take a little *bhāgavata* treatment than depend on such medical help.

**Citralekhā:** But I just saw a gentleman with Rāmānuja *tilaka* leaving Prabhupāda's house. Isn't he a doctor?

**Upendra:** His name is Damodara Prasada Sastri. He's an Āyur Vedic *kavirāja* from Calcutta. Prabhupāda saw him in a dream.

**Citralekhā:** In a *dream*?

**Upendra:** He saw a Vaiṣṇava with Rāmānuja *tilaka* preparing medicine for him. Over the last month a whole string of Rāmānuja *kavirājas* have come, answering this description, but all have been rejected as either incompetent or greedy or both. But Damodara Prasada is different.

**Citralekhā:** He looked very serious.

**Upendra:** (*appreciatively*) He's expert. He personally goes to the forest to get herbs and then prepares the medicine while chanting *mantras* over it. He has many respectable patients, and none of them will see any other *kavirāja*. We've been begging him not to return to Calcutta.

**Citralekhā:** Well, is his medicine helping?

**Upendra:** (*slightly enthusiastic*) Prabhupāda is drinking more liquids now—even milk—and they don't turn to mucus. (*Conceding*) But still, he can't even turn over by himself. Even lifting his arm is a great effort.

(*The children have again become overactive, drawing their parents' attention*.)

**Citralekhā:** Shouldn't we prepare them a little? They're expecting Prabhupāda to look the same as always. Saumya's asked me a dozen times if he can sit on Prabhupāda's lap again like last time.

**Upendra:** You're right. They'll be shocked. (*He kneels down on one knee to talk to the children*.) Prabhupāda has been fasting for . . .

**Saumya:** (*interrupting*) Ekādaśī?

**Upendra:** (*smiling*) No. He's been fasting for a very long time and has become very skinny.

**Śāntāya:** (*wide-eyed*) You mean like King Rantideva? Has Prabhupāda also given his *prasādam* to feed a hungry poor man? Is that why he's so skinny?

**Upendra:** Yes, to so many hungry poor people all over the world. Because he hasn't eaten for a long time, he's feeling very weak and won't be walking or even sitting.

**Śāntāya:** (*to her brother; knowingly*) He's sleeping.

**Saumya:** (*to his sister*) Prabhupāda's dreaming about Kṛṣṇa.

**Upendra:** And sometimes Prabhupāda cries.

**Saumya:** (*startled*) Why, *pitā*? Did someone hurt Śrīla Prabhupāda?

**Upendra:** No. He's crying because he's very happy.

**Śāntāya:** Like Akrura when he saw Kṛṣṇa's footprints?

**Upendra:** (*to Citralekhā*) These children understand better than we do. (*Getting up*) Okay. Come on. (*Each takes the hand of a child, and they approach Prabhupāda's house*.) Now wait here while I check inside. (*He exits and then returns*.) There are many devotees in the room. I think we can see Prabhupāda by looking through the outside windows.

(*They lead the children to the two windows through which they look, Upendra with Saumya in front of him and Citralekhāwith Śāntāya in front of her*. *They all face the audience, as if Prabhupāda's* *room is where the audience is*. *Upon seeing Prabhupāda, Upendra and Citralekhā offer obeisances while the children continue to look with much curiosity*. *The parents again look on from behind the children*.)

**Śāntāya:** (*loudly*) *Mātā*, that's not Prabhupāda!

**Voice Offstage:** Shhh!

**Citralekhā:** Shhh! Yes, it is.

**Śāntāya:** Ohhh! (*She offers obeisances, as does Saumya, encouraged by Upendra*.)

**Saumya:** (*standing again, in full voice*) Why has Prabhupāda become so thin?

**Voice Offstage:** Shhhh! (*Citralekha looks anxiously at Upendra, who's equally anxious*.)

**Upendra:** If you're not quiet, we'll have to leave.

**Citralekhā:** (*while looking through the window, astonished*) Upendra, I can't believe it! Prabhupāda's . . . *translating*! Lying on his bed, surrounded by devotees, he looks exactly like Grandfather Bhīṣma teaching King Yudhiṣṭhira!

**Upendra:** Pradyumna is reading the verses aloud, and Śrīla Prabhupāda is commenting into a microphone which Jayādvaita holds inches from his mouth.

**Citralekhā:** (*Amazed, she turns her head to Upendra*) How is Prabhupāda able to do it?

**Upendra:** (*in equal disbelief while continuing to observe*) It's Kṛṣṇa's special mercy. Prabhupāda prayed to Kṛṣṇa for this special blessing, and now we're seeing it actually come true. Not caring for hunger or pain, he's instructing his spiritual children on the highest duty of life, exactly like Grandfather Bhīṣma.

**Citralekhā:** (*with great appreciation*) Upendra, I *knew* that Prabhupāda was not of this world. No ordinary human could ever dream of doing such a thing! All these years we just didn't realize how *great* Prabhupāda is, or how *fortunate* we are.

**Upendra:** And now, just as we are beginning to believe it, it might be too late.

**Citralekhā:** (*nervously, looking to the side*) Who's that coming?

**Upendra:** (*looking*) It's Tamal Krishna and Bhakti Charu. And Girirāj is with them. Maybe we'd better go.

(*Citralekha looks at Prabhupāda one last time and offers abeisances*. *Then they all exit*.)

\[End Of Prelude\]

(*Tamal Krishna Goswami, Bhakti Charu Swami, and Girirāj enter*.)

**Girirāj:** (*guiltily and slightly accusingly*) Mahārāja, I feel it's wrong to leave while Prabhupāda is translating.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*defending*) Don't you think I also want to hear? But look at Prabhupāda's condition! We've tried everything else. Please, let's just keep to the plan. We'll go to all the temples and pray to the Deities for Prabhupāda's protection. (*Looking about*) I told Bhagatjī we'd meet him at exactly three o'clock. Look! Here he comes now. (*Calling out*) Bhagatjī! Wait there! (*They walk quickly to meet Bhagatjī, who enters slowly, walking with a cane*.)

**Bhagatjī:** (*as he enters*) Am I late? I was busy doing your work only—pacifying the neighboring *sādhus* that the drainage will soon be repaired. How is ŚrīPrabhupāda?

**Bhakti Charu:** (*pressing Bhagatjī's hand enthusiastically*) You won't believe it! Prabhupāda is translating again.

**Bhagatjī:** (*in disbelief*) *Translating?*

**Bhakti Charu:** (*excitedly*) Yes, with Pradyumna reading the *ślokas* and portions of the *ṭīkās*, he's been speaking into the dictaphone for nearly half an hour. He is translating the part where Lord Brahmā steals the calves and cowherdboys.

**Bhagatjī:** (*still incredulous, to Tamal Krishna*) Prabhupāda sitting up translating?

**Tamal Krishna:** (*solemnly shaking his head*) Not sitting. Lying down.

**Bhagatjī:** (*pleased*) Then the *kavirāja's* medicine is working. But Mahārāja, why do you look so unhappy?

**Tamal Krishna:** This latest miracle is not due to any medicine.

**Bhakti Charu:** It's Kṛṣṇa's special mercy.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*somberly*) That is what I'm afraid of.

(*Bhagatjīand Bhakti Charu look at each other, not comprehending, and then to Tamal Krishna for an explanation*.) When Prabhupāda was in London, the devotees performed a drama called "Bhīṣma's Perfection." Afterwards Prabhupāda prayed to Kṛṣṇa for the strength to die following Bhīṣma's glorious example. I'm afraid these may be Prabhupāda's final days.

**Bhagatjī:** (*at last understanding*) I see . . . (*Resolutely*) Then it is good that we have planned this *parikrama*. But what excuse have you given for your absence? Prabhupāda is sure to call for one of you.

**Tamal Krishna:** I've left word that we'rc going to the Rādhā-Dāmodara temple to see how his rooms are being maintained.

**Bhagatjī:** Then let us go without further delay. (*They walk about*.) Walking barefoot in the cool sand of Ramaṇa-retīis a great relief from the heat of the day.

**Tamal Krishna:** Not cool enough for our anxiety.

**Bhagatjī:** The *gopī* Vṛndādevī, the goddess of Vṛndāvana's forests, would make all arrangements for Kṛṣṇa to enjoy the beauty of Vṛndāvana. She would decorate the land with fragrant trees and forest flowers, fill the woods with deer and other gentle creatures. The sky would be filled with the sounds of lovebirds.

**Girirāj:** (*stopping*) Listen! (*He gestures for them to be still*.) The sound of peacocks. (*They listen*.)

**Bhagatjī:** That is why there are so few snakes. The peacocks are their deadly enemies. (*Again, they listen to the sound of peacocks*.)

**Bhakti Charu:** (*calling out*) O Vṛndā, goddess of Vṛndāvana's forests, won't you please command these peacocks to protect Prabhupāda from the snake of death? (*As if in response, the cry of a peacock calls from the distance*. *They walk further*.)

**Girirāj:** These trees seem very old.

**Bhagatjī:** There are trees here that are still standing from Kṛṣṇa's time. Soon I will show you the *keli*-*kadamba*, from which Kṛṣṇa leaped into the Yamunā to chastise Kāliya.

**Girirāj:** Is this where Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma played with Their friends?

**Bhagatjī:** They would wander throughout Ramaṇa-retī. Now, by Prabhupāda's arrangement, Kṛṣṇa and Balarama are again in Ramaṇa-retī.

**Girirāj:** When Kṛṣṇa wanted to praise His elder brother, Balarāma, He said these trees, heavy with fruits and flowers, were bowing to worship Balarāma's lotus feet.

**Tamal Krishna:** But where are their fruits and flowers now?

**Bhakti Charu:** I think these trees are bent in lamentation, their sagging, twisted limbs heavy with despair over ŚrIla Prabhupāda's condition.

**Girirāj:** Perhaps Vṛndādevīhas also become sad and has allowed Vṛndāvana to become so dry.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*They stop*. *Anguished* ). Why does everything seem so lifeless? Or is it our devotion that has actually dried up? What has happened to our faith? (*Calling out beseechingly*) O Śraddhā, Faith, can you hear us? (*He pauses as if expecting a reply*.) Or have you deserted us? (*Śraddhā enters unseen by the others and listens silently*. *Tamal Krishna continues supplicatingly*.) O Śraddhā, without your help how will we prove our sincerity to Kṛṣṇa? We have come to Vṛndāvana to relish the Lord's eternal pastimes, but instead each *tīrtha* brings another wave of grief. I know we are strange pilgrims, morose and distracted, but with each holy place we visit our faith should increase. Instead, it is desiccated by our overwhelming grief.

O Kṛṣṇa! In Goloka, You are surrounded and served by countless devotees. You have unlimited pure devotees, but we have only one. How can You take him away from us? O Balarāma, You are the most powerful Personality of Godhead. But we are weak and unprotected, so we appeal to You: *Please* spare our only shelter! Please allow ŚrIla Prabhupāda to remain with us!

**Bhakti Charu:** (*entreatingly*) O Śraddhā, if you can hear us, please make our faith steady. Vṛndāvana is full of wish-fulfilling trees. We know that a devotee must be careful, lest his material desires be fulfilled. But you should know that for months such desires have never crossed our minds. Even spiritual advancement is not our main concern. We often miss our meals, we snatch the time to chant our rounds, and our sleep is restless. We've lost our sense of time, our peace of mind, our health. And perhaps our sanity too!

Our future is tied to such a taut and delicate thread; at any moment it could snap. Our beloved spiritual master hovers at the precipice between life and death. O Śraddhā, you know very well how to help fulfill the devotees' desires. Please, we beg you, be merciful to us. Fulfill our only desire—let Prabhupāda stay with us.

**Bhagatjī:** (*after a moment of silent prayer*) Mahārājas, we must move along if we are to cover all the important places. (*Pointing*) See, there is the Yamunā and further on the *keli-kadamba* tree, from which Kṛṣṇa leaped to chastise the serpent Kāliya. Ah, my legs are getting tired. Let us rest there while one of you tells this pastime. (*They exit*.)

**Śraddhā:** (*moving forward as they exit*) These blessed followers of Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda! The divine presence of the spiritual master is more pleasing than a thousand moons, his word more brilliant than a million suns, his mercy worth more than a billion precious gems. It is natural that these devotees fear separation from their beloved spiritual father.

Their restless wandering, their blind accusations and plaintive appeals are the first signs of compassionate love. Though their devotion is yet immature, their sorrow reminds me of the suffering of King Nanda, Queen Yaśodā, and the other residents of Vṛndāvana whenever Lord Kṛṣṇa leaves them. Kṛṣṇa, that bluish-black monsoon cloud, always showers them with the nectar of His pastimes. But in His absence their hearts dry up, and they nearly leave their bodies out of intense separation. Their only consolation is to wander in those places where Kṛṣṇa sported.

(*Suddenly looking to the side*) Oh, someone is coming. It must be some deer moving through the thickets. (*She strains to see*.) Is it a cow that has wandered from its herd?

(*Damodara Prasada Sastri, the Ramanuja* kavirāja*, enters, bending low to search for herbs*.)

**Śraddhā:** (*recognizing him*) Ah, the *kavirāja*. (*She moves to the side to observe.*)

**Kavirāja:** (*a bit out of breath, while searching, to himself*) I have collected all but one of the twenty-two ingredients needed for preparing *brikka-sanjivani-arak*. Though I've searched the riverbanks and forests, I cannot find the one missing herb. If only I were in Bengal, where it is so widely available.

(*Frustrated in his search, he stops to pray with joined palms*.)

O Lord Dhanvantari, as Viṣṇu's *avatāra*, You are the supreme healer of all ills. I've devoted an entire lifetime to Your service and never refused anyone who sincerely sought my help. Now I have many rich patients lying in Calcutta, totally dependent on me. Though I have taken an oath never to allow someone to suffer, for the first time I am guilty of breaking this vow. Have I disgraced my profession? Then let my reputation go to hell!

(*He sits down*.) There's something more important than wealth or honor, something that fame and fortune cannot purchase. My Lord, I have had the association of a pure Vaiṣṇava, and by that most rare association my heart has been changed. Not only his association, but his devotees' as well. Though I was born in a family of Śrī Vaiṣṇavas and have strictly followed their principles throughout my life, l've had to learn from these young foreign disciples the real meaning of devotion to *guru*. But now their master is lying in bed, and as his breathing becomes difficult, the whole world suffocates, as Mahārāja Dhruva's austerities choked the breathing of the entire universe.

My Lord, You have always heard my prayers. It is You only who make my medicines potent. I need that one last herb—*iksu-gandha-mool*. And most of all, I need Your mercy to cure Prabhupāda.

**Śraddhā:** (*to herself*) The whole of Vṛndāvana—the entire world—is praying for Prabhupāda's life.

**Kavirāja:** (*Exhausted he feels his head*. *To himself*) I am feverish. (*He takes his pulse*.) The beat is uneven—sometimes strong, then Weak. I have become ill from exhaustion, no regular sleep or meals.

**Śraddhā:** (*to herself knowledgeably*) These are symptoms of fatigue caused by extreme anxiety and grief for Lord Kṛṣṇa's pure devotee. I shall help him. (*Unseen by the* kavirāja*, she moves forward, commanding*.) By my touch, let the herb *iksu-gandha-mool* be amply available. (*She waves her hand to create the herb everywhere*.)

**Kavirāja:** (*alert, to himself*) What is that fragrance? It smells just like *iksu-gandha-mool*. (*He gets up and suddenly discovers it is*. *Happily*) Why it *is*! (*With eyes closed*) Thank you, O Lord, for hearing my prayers. (*He bends to pick the herb*.) Now I'll return to my quarters and slowly distill the ingredients throughout the night. (*Confidently*) Prabhupāda, you are as good as healed! (*He exits*.)

**Śraddhā:** All of Vṛndāvana has become distraught. Dear Lord, is this the work of Your internal potency, *yogamāyā*, fulfilling one of Your plans? Otherwise, how is it possible for devotees to feel such distress, especially in Vṛndāvana, which is under Your personal protection?

Dear Lord, sometimes You bring the inhabitants of Vṛndāvana so much pain that they are at the point of giving up their lives. Fortunately, Uddhava, your constant companion, comes with Your life-giving message. Still, You are sometimes accused of cruelty by those who fully depend on You. Now I am afraid You may again incur blame—this time from millions throughout this world who fear the loss of their only shelter, Your pure devotee Śrīla Prabhupāda. Let me take up their cause and rally others to their aid. By protecting these budding devotional creepers, I shall show my appreciation for their exalted spiritual master. In the meantime, please think of a way to intensify their love, but not by the pain of separation.

(*She looks*.) They went to the Yamunā. Let me not lose any more time. (*She exits*.)

## Act VI

**Śraddhā:** (*entering*) Ah! Beautiful Yamunā! And here's that glorious *kadamba* tree Lord Kṛṣṇa graced with His lotus feet. Its body is decorated with ecstatic symptoms in the form of festive flowers.

(*Suddenly noticing*) A half-strung garland of *kadamba* flowers! (*She picks it up*.) The uneven arrangement of the buds indicates its maker was not fully concentrating. The flowers are very fragrant. (*After a moment's though*!) Prabhupāda's devotees were surely here. One of them began to string a garland for him, but due to anxiety the devotee's hands were unsteady, and ultimately he forgot the garland altogether, overwhelmed with worry. (*She puts it down*.)

Look how the dark waters of the Yamunāare so still, as if waiting with anticipation for Lord Kṛṣṇa. When feeling the absence of Govinda, hot tears fill her eyes, forming the fine mist that rises from her surface in the heat of the day. But when the Lord arrives to perform His pastimes, her eyes shed jubilant tears, pleasantly cool like morning dew.

Oh! I hear someone coming. (*She looks*.) The entire atmosphere has become surcharged. Some very great personality is approaching. Ah! Look! It is Yogamāyā, Lord Hari's own potency for managing the spiritual world. Her partial expansion, Durgā, bewilders the conditioned souls. But as Yogamāyāshe personally arranges the Lord's pastimes with

His devotees by inducing spiritual loving sentiments. Seeing her, one's heart is immediately satisfied, one's senses are cleansed, and one no longer cares even for liberation, much less for worldly pleasures. Her presence can fulfill all spiritual desires. (*Yogamāyā* *enters*.)

**Śraddhā:** (*reverently*) O Mother, I offer my respects to you.

**Yogamāyā:** (*blessing her*) Good fortune to you, auspicious child. You are radiant with devotion for Lord Hari.

**Śraddhā:** (*humbly*) I am merely reflecting sacred Yamunā's love for the Lord.

**Yogamāyā:** (*agreeing*) The lotuses that fill her waters seem to perform a constant *ārati* to the Lord. Whoever has not bathed in those purifying waters is certainly a sinner.

**Śraddhā:** Mother, your presence is certainly my greatest fortune, but may I ask what brings you here?

**Yogamāyā:** I have come on a special mission, one which concerns a devotee most dear to the Lord.

**Śraddhā:** If I may guess, could it be Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda, without whose presence this entire earth would now be useless?

**Yogamāyā:** Yes, precisely! He is the most illustrious ambassador of Vṛndāvana-candra's glories, the best of the *paramahaṁsas*, Vṛndāvana's most worthy son. Though this age is dark, Prabhupāda's continuous travels have illuminated the earth like a second sun-god. But that brilliant luminary is on the verge of departing, and the entire world is crying in fear of the coming darkness.

**Śraddhā:** I know. I have seen his disciples. Like small jewels reflecting sunlight, they each glow with devotion to their *guru*. What is your mission?

**Yogamāyā:** To assist Prabhupāda in deepening his disciples' love.

**Śraddhā:** How will you do that?

**Yogamāyā:** Using his illness as a churning stick, Prabhupāda is churning the milk of their devotion until it thickens into the nectar of pure spontaneous love.

**Śraddhā:** O revered lady, what are the stages through which this milk will be churned?

**Yogamāyā:** First comes faith, for which you are in charge. In fact, your mercy is essential throughout. Through regulated service to one's *guru*, despite omissions and faults, the milk of devotion thickens and the gaps in service are sealed. Then the devotee, fully sensitive to the desires of the master, is ready even to sacrifice his own well-being. If there is some threat to the master, the disciple seeks to turn it back. And when the relationship seems about to be severed, the devotee, mad with fear and anxiety, will do anything and everything to preserve it.

**Śraddhā:** Though such devotion seems full of pain, it is actually relishable.

**Yogamāyā:** There is no pleasure higher than that of the devotee in the mood of separation. Fully absorbed in meditating on his beloved master, he transcends the dualities of material existence.

**Śraddhā:** How can ordinary people understand this?

**Yogamāyā:** They cannot, for they see only the external lamentation, not realizing that the spiritual energy is at work.

**Śraddhā:** Mother, forgive me, but seeing the sorrow of Prabhupāda's devotees throughout the world, I'm afraid they may not be able to maintain their budding faith. They go without eating or sleeping, neglecting their timely devotions, their minds plagued with anxiety, fear, moroseness—even madness. Some are even contemplating suicide.

**Yogamāyā:** (*sympathizing*) My child, what can I do? On Lord Kṛṣṇa's order I have induced in them these divine feelings. How can I disobey His command?

**Śraddhā:** Perhaps you could report to the Lord that your mission has been quite successful and that there is no need to put these devotees in further anxiety.

**Yogamāyā:** (*thoughtfully*) Yes . . . yes, that I could do.

**Śraddhā:** Mother, I have seen the physician who is treating Prabhupāda. Judging by his anxiety, there is no time to delay. If you do not act immediately, it will certainly be too late.

**Yogamāyā:** (*deliberating, then decisively*) All right, I shall do as you ask.

**Śraddhā:** (*thankful*) The whole world will be indebted. I'll now do whatever I can to ease their distress until you return. (*They exit*.)

## Act VII

(*Haṁsadūta Swami enters the sitting room and sits to one side*. *He chants Hare* Kṛṣṇa *softly, accompanying himself on the harmonium*. *Śrīla Prabhupāda enters, lying down, surrounded by Jayapatāka Swami, Bhavānanda Goswami, Tamal Krishna Goswami, Bhakti Charu Swami, and Girirāj*. *It is noon on November 11*. *After sometime Lokanātha Swami enters and offers full daṇḍavats*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** (*softly, near Prabhupādas ear*) Śrīla Prabhupāda? Lokanātha Mahārāja has come.

**Prabhupāda:** (*pleasantly surprised* ) Ah, Lokanātha!

**Lokanātha:** Jaya, Śrīla Prabhupāda.

**Prabhupāda:** (*warmly*) You are well?

**Lokanātha:** By your mercy, Śrīla Prabhupāda.

**Prabhupāda:** Your *saṅkīrtana* party is traveling?

**Lokanātha:** We are just coming from Uttar Pradesh, where we flooded all the villages and towns with your transcendental books.

**Prabhupāda:** (*yearningly*) When I think of how you are traveling, I feel like joining you immediately. A *sannyāsī's* life. Always moving, free from attachments. An ideal life.

**Lokanātha:** During our travels we get to visit many holy places.

**Prabhupāda:** (*with keen interest*) Which have you visited?

**Lokanātha:** We went to Badarīnātha. I displayed a set of your *Śrīmad*-*Bhāgavatams* in honor of Śrīla Vyāsadeva.

**Prabhupāda:** Thank you very much.

**Lokanātha:** And we went to Bhim Kapur, where Bhīma left this world. When Lord Kṛṣṇa desired that the Pāṇḍavas go back to Godhead, Mahārāja Yudhiṣṭhira led his brothers into the Himalayas. After Arjuna, Sahadeva, Nakula, and Draupadīhad all given up their bodies, Yudhiṣṭhira and Bhīma went on until Bhīma also dropped down. That place is known as Bhim Kapur. (*There is a long silence*.)

**Prabhupāda:** (*after reflecting*) Jayapatāka? Bhavānanda? (*Jayapatāka and Bhavānanda lean forward*.) So, you all manage. I've done everything a dying man must do. l've given my will. It's not good all you big managers are bound here and I am just lying. I want you to manage very nicely, as if I am dead. You can say civil suicide: although living, consider me dead. Then—(*longingly*) let me travel to all the holy places of pilgrimage without responsibility. And if I die, what is that? They can bring the dead body to Māyāpur or Vṛndāvana. A little medicine—no! No medicine! Just a little milk, and travel one place to another. Nowadays in India there is sunshine. During the daytime I shall travel and at night make a camp underneath a tree. In this way, let me travel to all the *tīrtha*-*sthānas*. (*Confiding*) I am thinking in this way. What is your opinion?

**Bhavānanda:** It's a very nice program. We can all accompany you.

**Prabhupāda:** No. You men are bound up with your local responsibilities. Lokanātha's party has some experience, so let them take me. If I recover, very good. If not, what is the wrong? (*Hopeful*) If I live, it will be a great thing—another life.

JAYAPATAKA (*disapprovingly*) But the *kavirāja* says you have six years of life left.

**Prabhupāda:** (*rebutting*) But even a healthy cow, when living in one place, becomes ill. Therefore I say, don't keep me locked up. Set me free. Lokanātha can take me village to village. (*Forcibly*) It has to be settled. You manage. lf I live, I shall come again.

**Bhavānanda:** (*hedging*) I think it is a good idea, but you should not take unnecessary risk.

**Prabhupāda:** (*challenging*) What is the risk? You have tried doctors. *kavirājas*, medicine—and all have failed.

**Bhavānanda:** (*feebly arguing*) But the medicine you are taking now is working.

**Prabhupāda:** (*pointedly*) If it is working, why don't I feel strength?

**Bhavānanda:** It is working, but it takes time.

**Prabhupāda:** (*contending*) You think that taking medicine is not a risk. (*He pauses for effect*.) lf I die without the *kavirāja*, what is the harm? I want simply *parikrama*—medicine or no medicine.

**Jayapatāka:** But the other day you fainted, so how will you survive on *parikrama*?

**Prabhupāda:** (*confidently*) If I die on *parikrama*, that is glorious. I welcome death. Why not allow me? Give me this facility: one *parikrama* and leave me to my fate. Now suppose I die, what wrong is there'? You go and bring the body to Māyāpur or Vṛndāvana. In Māyāpur that land for my house is already selected. And in Vṛndāvana you have a gate site.

**Jayapatāka:** Ṭhākura Bhaktivinoda said that a *samādhi* near the gate was not proper.

**Prabhupāda:** No. There is ample room.

**Jayapatāka:** (*persisting*) I see that you are getting stronger. Look how you are talking. The *kavirāja* is sure that by taking a little milk . . .

**Prabhupāda:** (*interrupting impatiently*) I'll take milk. Milk is available everywhere. I shall take a little milk and sleep. If I live, that is all right. If I don't, that is also all right.

**Jayapatāka:** (*Seeing Prabhupāda's determination, he takes a new tack*) Which holy places would you go to?

**Prabhupāda:** India is full of holy places. Gradually we will travel to Māyāpur.

**Haṁsadūta:** (*encouraging*) Śrīla Prabhupāda, tomorrow is Govardhana-pūjā. You could go to Govardhana Hill.

**Prabhupāda:** (*conclusively*) Ah,just see! The residents of Vṛndāvana always celebrate this day by circumambulating Govardhana. That such an auspicious ceremony comes at this time is Kṛṣṇa's sign that I must go.

**Jayapatāka:** But Prabhupāda, you are very famous. Wherever you go there will be crowds of people.

**Prabhupāda:** So they'll be seeing me. I have no objection. I want a little milk from them, that is all. (*Tamal Krishna takes Bhakti Charu aside and whispers to him*. *Bhakti Charu exits*.) Is Tamal here?

**Tamal Krishna:** (*returning to the bedside*) Yes, Prabhupāda?

**Prabhupāda:** There are two things: life or death. If there is death, it is natural.

**Tamal Krishna:** For us, it is very unfortunate.

**Prabhupāda:** You have to live by my words, by my training.

**Jayapatāka:** But for the disciple the spiritual master's separation is like death itself. Narottama dāsa Ṭhākura sings, *pāṣāṇe kuṭibo māthā anale paśibo*: I will smash my head against the rock and enter into fire.

**Tamal Krishna:** In the Purañjana story, you have described that the disciple laments the departure of his *guru* exactly as the queen lamented the death of Malayadhvaja.

(*The kavirāja enters, followed by Bhakti Charu*.)

**Prabhupāda:** You have to admit that all medical treatments have been a failure.

**Jayapatāka:** But the doctors say you defy all medical laws. Sometimes you become very weak, sometimes very strong.

**Girirāj:** (*impartially*) Śrīla Prabhupāda, I agree that *parikrama* is a good idea. I don't have any faith in the doctors or their treatments. Ultimately it depends on Kṛṣṇa. He can exercise His will in any condition.

**Prabhupāda:** (*canvassing*) Yes. So all of you give permission. Then I will go.

**Bhavānanda:** Previously you decided not to go to Māyāpur, because you felt you might die on the road and that was not good. (*Confused*) But now you say go.

**Prabhupāda:** No. When my only concern was my health, to travel would have been artificial. But *parikrama* is natural. Death is inevitable, but artificial is not good.

**Bhavānanda:** I feel that by traveling now there would be unnatural death.

**Prabhupāda:** No. I shall gain strength by traveling.

**Haṁsadūta:** (*urging*) You can begin by circumambulating Vṛndāvana.

**Prabhupāda:** Yes. Let us make an experiment in Vṛndāvana.

**Haṁsadūta:** (*pressing*) So we should meet and make plans for traveling around Vṛndāvana. You will begin tomorrow morning?

**Prabhupāda:** Yes.

**Jayapatāka:** By bus?

**Prabhupāda:** No bus. By bullock cart.

**Many:** (*shocked*) Bullock cart!

**Prabhupāda:** Yes. With bullocks you get the cow dung for cooking.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*pleading*) But Śrīla Prabhupāda, the cart will jerk and be too painful. We think you can get stronger slowly. The *kavirāja* has something to say. (*He urges the kavirāja* *to speak*.)

**Kavirāja:** There are seven patients dying in Calcutta whom I had to neglect to stay here with you. Is it wrong that I may be sacrificing their lives for yours? (*He pauses for effect*.) It is true that everyone has to die sooner or later. But if your life can be extended, how many millions will be spiritually saved? This proposal of yours will prove fatal. (*As if issuing a firm prescription*) As your physician, under no circumstances can I advise you to go.

**Bhavānanda:** The risk is too great!

**Prabhupāda:** Vṛndāvana *parikrama* is not a risk.

**Jayapatāka:** You have advised us never to take unnecessary risk when doubtful.

**Prabhupāda:** (*deprecatingly*) That is regarding material decisions. (*After some moments*) Let us call Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājīand then decide. Either Rāvaṇa will kill or Rāma will kill. But of the two, it is better to be killed by Rāma. This was Mārīca's position. If he hadn't assisted Rāvaṇa, he would have been killed by him immediately, whereas by assisting in stealing Sītāhe met his certain death at the hands of Rāma. My death is sure. Let it be glorious. Personally, I think I shall be cured by *parikrama*.

**Lokanātha:** I have seen nice bullock carts in Pune that are just like chariots.

**Prabhupāda:** (*inspired*) You can purchase one immediately. (*To the others*) See! He already has experience.

**Haṁsadūta:** (*rallying*) Your vision is transcendental! If you say you'll get better, then we have to believe you!

**Prabhupāda:** Rest assured, I shall not die in one day. (*Spirited*) We can go to Govardhana and make our own cooking there—build a fire and cook our own foodstuff.

**Lokanātha:** We can collect grains from the farmers.

**Prabhupāda:** That will be very nice.

**Lokanātha:** In my native place there are old men who pilgrimage sixty miles every Ekādaśī.

**Prabhupāda:** And I have your assistance. (*Reassuring*) Don't hesitate. As far as possible take me in a comfortable condition. Tomorrow is a great festival. We are in Vṛndāvana, so we must take part. So, Lokanātha, what do you think?

**Lokanātha:** (*boldly*) To please you, we should make the experiment.

**Prabhupāda:** Thank you very much. Hare Kṛṣṇa. Now all of you go and arrange.

(*Śrīla Prabhupāda takes rest*. *All offer obeisances and exit, talking excitedly among themselves*. *The* kavirāja *speaks to Tamal Krishna and Bhakti Charu*.)

**Kavirāja:** (*very upset*) If Prabhupāda leaves the gates of the Krishna-Balaram temple, not even Lord Brahmāor Lord Viṣṇu Himself will keep me here another moment!

## Act VIII

(*Śrīla Prabhupāda is lying down in his sitting room on one side of the stage. Then Tamal Krishna Goswami, Bhavānanda Goswami, and Bhakti Charu Swami enter the other side of the stage, the reception room.*)

**Bhavānanda:** (*disturbed*) The road to Vṛndāvana has more potholes than the streets of Calcutta.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*dismayed*) And the bullock-cart is in no better shape.

**Bhavānanda:** (*anxiously*) You've seen it?

**Bhakti Charu:** (*distraught*) Bhavānanda Mahārāja, it's a typical villager's cart for carrying hay or vegetables to the market. Prabhupāda's bones are as fragile as glass. He's lost so much weight, there's nothing left to protect them from being shattered by the jerking and jarring. Is there no one to help us? Tamal Krishna Mahārāja, please think of something! (*Tamal Krishna remains silently contemplative*.)

**Bhavānanda:** (*exasperated*) Seeing Lokanātha in such a hurry, I'm losing my patience. I'd like to break his "Pune chariot" to pieces. Especially when he mentioned how his village elders could walk sixty miles! Is he trying to serve Prabhupāda or punish him?

**Tamal Krishna:** Lokanātha is sometimes naive. He meant no harm.

**Bhavānanda:** (*sarcastically*) Yes, a *brāhmaṇa* always speaks the truth, even if painful.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*thoughtfully*) What I wonder is, why is Haṁsadūta so insistent?

**Bhakti Charu:** For weeks he has been convinced that Prabhupāda is determined to depart. I think he sees the *parikrama* as the fulfillment of that desire. (*Haṁsadūta Swami enters*.)

**Bhakti Charu:** (*greeting Haṁsadūta*) Haribol, Mahārāja.

**Haṁsadūta:** (*with a lightheartedness that makes everyone else all the more solemn*) Why's everyone so sad? For weeks the mood's been depressing, but now Prabhupāda wants a festival. Lokanātha's already rented the cart.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*provoked*) That's nice. And when I see you driving Prabhupāda to a slow, painful death . . .

**Haṁsadūta:** (*resentful*) Knock it off!

**Bhavānanda:** (*exploding*) You knock it off! Why are you pushing for this *parikrama!*

**Haṁsadūta:** (*challenging*) Why are you stopping it?

**Bhavānanda:** (*acrimoniously*) Anyone in his right mind can see that Śrīla Prabhupāda will never be able to survive even one hour.

**Haṁsadūta:** (*defensively*) Prabhupāda's not an ordinary man. He defies all material laws. Didn't you hear him say there was no risk? If you really care for Prabhupāda, you'll follow his order. Or are you envious that you won't be coming?

**Bhakti Charu:** (*to Haṁsadūta, appealing for reason*) Mahārāja, think of what the public will say if Prabhupāda were actually to leave his body while on *parikrama*.

**Tamal Krishna:** They would say that we'd killed him. And by foolishly disregarding his welfare we actually *would* have.

**Haṁsadūta:** (*contemptuously*) We're not concerned with what the public thinks. What do they understand? To them he's merely a dying old man. But he's our eternal spiritual master, and to fulfill his desire, especially what may be his final desire, is our only duty.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*accusing*) But it's not our duty to *make* it his final desire.

**Haṁsadūta:** (*retaliating*) Prabhupāda's not under your control. Stop trying to manage everything.

**Voice Offstage:** (*from a distance*) Bhakti Charu Mahārāja! (*They turn to look in the direction of the voice*.)

**Bhakti Charu:** It's Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī, with Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja. (*Straining to see*) And Bon Mahārāja as well.

**Tamal Krishna:** You take care of them. I want to discuss this *parikrama* with the other devotees.

(*Tamal Krishna, Bhavānanda, and Haṁsadūta all exit*. *Kṛṣṇadāsa* *Bābājī, Bon Mahārāja, anil Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja enter*. *Bhakti Charu offers them obeisances and greets them*. *He then brings them into Prabhupāda's room, and they sit an chairs behind Prabhupāda's bed in such a way that they face the audience*.)

**Bhakti Charu:** (*kneeling close to Prabhupāda*) Śrīla Prabhupāda— Bābājī Mahārāja, Bon Mahārāja, and Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja have come to visit you.

**Prabhupāda:** (*raising his palms in salutation*) *Jaya!* First of all I want to say, forgive me for all my offenses. (*Apologetically*) I didn't mean to hurt anyone, but while preaching one has to say something that may offend others. Disputes and misunderstandings arise. Will you forgive me?

**Nārāyaṇa:** (*devotedly*) You didn't commit any offenses. We never thought that you did anything wrong. Rather, you please bless us—we need your blessings. If anyone is offended by any of your activities, it is his fault, not yours.

**Bon:** If the moon has some dark spots, who cares?

**Kṛṣṇadāsa:** You are our eternal ruler. You can rule over us, chastise us, or guide us.

**Prabhupāda:** (*humbly*) No. I am *mahā-patita*.

**Kṛṣṇadāsa:** (*correcting lovingly*) You are *mahā-patita-pāvana*.

**Prabhupāda:** Perhaps I became a little puffed up with my opulences.

**Nārāyaṇa:** No. You never became proud. When you started preaching, opulence and success followed you as Lakṣmīfollows the Supreme Lord Nārāyaṇa. They are the blessings of Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma.

**Prabhupāda:** That may be, but in this world one unknowingly commits offenses. Now Kṛṣṇa has shattered my pride—"If you don't have your body, what is there to be proud of?"

**Bhakti Charu:** You can never commit offenses, Śrīla Prabhupāda. You are God's dear one.

**Prabhupāda:** I am a little temperamental. Often I used words like "rascal." I never compromised. They used to call it *kurul niye prachar:* preaching with a pickaxe. *Kurul* in one hand, *Bhāgavatam* in the other. Somehow Kṛṣṇa has been pleased.

Our Guru Mahārāja wanted us to preach in America and Europe. And my desire is that we all work together to preach. The whole world is there. Many big temples have been built. There is no dearth of money. There is no lack of people. Simply it requires the right spirit. We are distributing not less than ten million books in a year. Even in Russia they are appreciating. Show them the certificate.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*passing it to them*) This is a certificate presented to our Bhaktivedanta Book Trust at the Moscow Book Fair.

**Prabhupāda:** So let us all cooperate; then Caitanya Mahāprabhu's words will come true: *pṛthivīte āche yata nagarādi grām*.

**Nārāyaṇa:** (*He whispers to the others that Prabhupāda should be allowed to rest*) I'll come again tomorrow. Now you take rest.

**Prabhupāda:** Bābājī Mahārāja can stay a while longer?

**Kṛṣṇadāsa:** As you order me.

(*Bon Mahārāja and Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja leave Prabhupāda's roam by walking to the other side of the stage, where they are met by Tamal Krishna and Bhavānanda*. *who enter and offer obeisances*. *Prabhupāda continues to talk \[silently\] to Bābājī* *Mahārāja, with Bhakti Charu at his side*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** Have you seen Śrīla Prabhupāda?

**Bon:** We have. It is amazing that his consciousness is so perfect.

**Nārāyaṇa:** (*appreciatively*) He is not even slightly less alert. In fact, his all-attractive qualities stand out now more than ever. As the sun sets, it casts brilliant colors across the sky. (*Offering* praṇāmas) We will come again tomorrow.

(*Bon Mahārāja and Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja exit*. *Suddenly Bhakti Charu rushes out of Prabhupādas room*.)

**Bhakti Charu:** (*frantically*) Tamal Krishna Mahārāja, Bhavānanda Mahārāja, something terrible has happened.

**Both:** What?

**Bhakti Charu:** Śrīla Prabhupāda just asked Kṛṣṇadāsa Bābājīto come on *parikrama*, and Bābājī Mahārāja has agreed. They are talking about the details now. What can we do?

**Tamal Krishna:** Somehow you have to explain to BābājīMahārāja the real facts. Remind him how the road is. If he hears that the *kavirāja* tells us Prabhupāda won't survive even an hour of it, he'll change his mind at once.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*apprehensively*) But how will I say these things in front of Prabhupāda?

**Bhavānanda:** Invite Kṛṣṇadāsa to take *prasādam*. When you are feeding him, you will have your chance.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*satisfied*) Good. I'll go at once. (*He reenters Prabhupāda's room*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** (*perturbed*) Bhavānanda, I feel like I'm losing my sanity.

**Bhavānanda:** Prabhupāda seems to want to die, as if he's welcoming death with open arms. I'm exhausted. (*They are both silent in thought*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** (*perplexed*) This is the dilemma: As his disciples it is our duty to serve his desires, but how can we help him fulfill *this* desire? By which act do we prove our love? (*They pause*. *staring at each other*. *Tamal Krishna is losing control of himself*) Bhavānanda, I can't tolerate this any longer!

**Bhavānanda:** What are you going to do?

**Tamal Krishna:** Beg Prabhupāda not to go!

(*They both enter Prabhupāda's* *room and offer obeisances, coming to the bedside*.)

**Prabhupāda:** (*Seeing them crying, he says compassionately*) You request me not to go?

**Tamal Krishna:** (*very agitated* ) Well, Śrīla Prabhupāda, I'm getting so upset about this. Two devotees told me this road is so bad that if you 'go, you're going to be jolted back and forth. And the cart—it’s the type used for caning vegetables. I Just can't understand, Śrīla Prabhupāda, why we have to go tomorrow. If anybody wants you to travel, I do. But why do we have to go in this condition? I can't understand. Why are you throwing out this *kavirāja* when he's having good results? I can't understand.

**Prabhupāda:** (*soothingly*) All right. I don't want to upset you. That is not my position. Bhavānanda, what do you think?

**Bhavānanda:** I am very upset, Śrīla Prabhupāda. I don't want you to go.

**Prabhupāda:** Bhavānanda has suffered so much for Māyāpur. How can I go against his will? If my right and left hand say no, how can I go?

**Bhakti Charu:** (*joyous*) *Jaya*, Śrīla Prabhupāda!

**Prabhupāda:** (*to Bhavānanda*) You're satisfied?

**Bhavānanda:** (*smiling*) Now I am, Śrīla Prabhupāda. I was in too much anxiety.

**Prabhupāda:** Never mind. I shall not put you in anxiety.

**Tamal Krishna:** Actually, Śrīla Prabhupāda, we're so attached to you that you practically drive us to madness sometimes. Tonight I thought we would go mad!

**Prabhupāda:** No, no, I shall not do that. (*To Kṛṣṇadāsa*) BābājīMahārāja, just see how much they all love me.

**Tamal Krishna:** Śrīla Prabhupāda, the way you deal with us simply deepens our attachment at every moment.

**Prabhupāda:** (*smiling*) That is my duty. (*After a pause*) Call the *kavirāja*. (*Bhakti Charu exits and then enters with the* kavirāja. *Afier offering praṇāmas, the* kavirāja *goes to Prabhupāda's side and takes his pulse*.) This disease has no material cure. Only because you are a devotee, I am continuing the treatment.

**Kavirāja:** This morning your pulse was 115 and missing some beats. But now the heart is again in order. Even from a purely material point of view, your body is not ordinary. Otherwise how could it immediately reverse its condition?

**Prabhupāda:** If Kṛṣṇa likes, He may bring a dead man like me back to life.

**Kavirāja:** (*to Bhakti Charu*) How much milk has he drunk?

**Bhakti Charu:** Five hundred cc. And just as you promised, there is no mucus.

**Kavirāja:** (*as a doctor, to inspire confidence*) Tomorrow I will give medicine for building up your muscles.

**Prabhupāda:** (*more amused than hopeful*) Muscles? There are only bones left.

**Kavirāja:** (*optimistically*) The medicine I am giving you contains crushed pearls. And tomorrow I will go to Jaipur to secure emeralds. That medicine is even more powerful than crushed diamonds. You may not care about your body, but I will see that you get all the proper foodstuffs and medicines. (*Confident*) You give me your cooperation for one week and I will cure you.

**Prabhupāda:** I am submissive, but it is you who have threatened to leave.

**Kavirāja:** (*strongly denying*) Who said that! I promise, I shall never leave you.

**Prabhupāda:** Thank you. (*Pausing, then with a choked voice*) I am taking so much service from all of you, and in this condition I cannot return it. (*Prabhupāda is crying*.) Please excuse me.

**Tamal Krishna:** We have an eternal debt we can never repay.

**Prabhupāda:** I have an eternal debt to all of you. It is Kṛṣṇa's desire. Who could be served like this even by his sons?

**Bhavānanda:** (*after a moment*) Prabhupāda, we will let you rest. Though there was no *parikrama*, we are all exhausted. (*They all offer obeisances and exit*.)

## Act IX

(*Tamal Krishna Goswami, Bhavānanda Goswami, Bhakti Charu Swami, Haṁsadūta Swami, and Bhagatjī enter solemnly*. *Soft chanting of Hare Kṛṣṇa comes from behind the scenes during the entire act*. *It is 5*:*15 P.M., November I4, I977*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** Where are the other GBC men?

**Bhavānanda:** I've told Satsvarūpa, Jagadīsh, and Svarūpa Dāmodara to come. But we shouldn't stay away for very long. The attacks come regularly every three hours.

(*Satsvarūpa dāsa Goswami, Jagadīsh, and Svarūpa* *Dāmodara enter*.)

**Satsvarūpa:** (*somewhat annoyed with Tamal Krishna*) Why have you called us away from Prabhupāda's bedside?

**Tamal Krishna:** Because the situation has reached the critical point.

**Bhakti Charu:** Do you think I should stop forcing him to drink? It has become nearly impossible. When I try to encourage him there is no response.

**Bhavānanda:** (*sorrowfully*) It's so painful to see these attacks. Even when healthy, Prabhupāda couldn't tolerate pain. I'm afraid he'll prefer to die.

**Svarūpa Dāmodara:** But the last two times have been different. Didn't you see how Prabhupāda was moving his arm gracefully backward and forward, almost as if dancing? It was quite different from yesterday, when he was moaning and complaining of pain.

**Bhakti Charu:** Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja said that it was simply from moving of the air in the body, not actual pain. For a moment we were allowed to witness some of the bodily symptoms of Śrīla Prabhupāda's ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa.

**Bhavānanda:** Since canceling the *parikrama*, he has become completely withdrawn.

**Haṁsadūta:** (*prophesying*) You've held his body, but you cannot hold his soul.

**Tamal Krishna:** Yes, his consciousness is perfect. Though apparently he was distant, when we whispered in his ear that his Godbrothers had again come, he joined his palms, offering them respects.

**Bhakti Charu:** It was the only time today he was actually responsive.

(*The* kavirāja *enters*, *unnoticed by the others*.)

**Satsvarūpa:** His consciousness is very deep.

**Bhakti Charu:** Then how shall we communicate with him?

**Kavirāja:** (*ominously*) I'm afraid that may not be possible. (*They all turn to see who has spoken*. *They are stunned upon realizing that it is not an ordinary opinion but that of the* kavirāja*, who continues gravely*) Prabhupāda's condition is irreversible.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*beseechingly*) Sastriji, please, can't you do anything?

**Kavirāja:** (*defeated*) Charu Swami, don't torture me. In the past I have cured lepers, brought youth to the aged, even made the dead come back to life. My medicines have never failed. But still Prabhupāda's life is slipping away. It must be as he said, "This disease has no material cure."

**Jagadīsh:** From the start Prabhupāda said he was living only due to our love.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*full of self-doubt*) Then has our love failed?

**Bhagatjī:** (*reassuringly*) Mahārājas, how often have l heard Prabhupāda say that no other father in the world could ever hope to have such loving sons?

**Bhavānanda:** (*losing composure*) O Kṛṣṇa, tell us! Is this what you want! (*After a long silence, anguished*) No! No! (*Piteously*) Oh, please let Prabhupāda live.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*pleadingly*) Please, Sastriji, please find some way. You must!

**Kavirāja:** Temporarily . . .

**Bhavānanda:** (*desperately*) Temporarily what?

**Kavirāja:** . . . If I insert a catheter, he will get relief for some few hours.

**Bhakti Charu:** A tube?

**Kavirāja:** His life may be extended three or four hours, but not more.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*firmly*) No! No tubes. Remember what Prabhupāda said, "Don't let them take me to a hospital." He didn't appreciate that his Godbrother got the latest medical help and had to die ingloriously with tubes stuck in his body.

**Satsvarūpa:** That's true. Prabhupāda would never want this. (*Others confirm with "no" and "definitely not."*)

TAMAl. KRISHNA. Do you realize what our "no" means?

**Bhakti Charu:** We are denying Śrila Prabhupāda this last relief.

**Jagadīsh:** His relief is to join Kṛṣṇa, not to remain in this world.

**Bhagatjī:** Prabhupāda has borne our suffering long enough. It is right that all of you allow him to go. (*There is a long silence*.)

**Bhavānanda:** (*devastated*) So it has come to this. For months I pushed this thought away. I never believed Prabhupāda would leave.

**Tamal Krishna:** None of us did.

**Bhagatjī:** That is the lesson Prabhupāda is teaching: One day we must all go.

**Kavirāja:** (*aggrieved*) In the past I have always been the one to give consolation to others, but now my heart is broken like all of yours.

**Bhakti Charu:** (*remembering*) Śrīla Prabhupāda was lying there and I could do nothing to help. Then Pisīmācame and placed her hand over his heart and chanted the prayers to Lord Nṛsiṁha for his protection. She rubbed some Ganges water on his lips and placed a picture of Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta and the *Śrīmad*-*Bhāgavatam* on the pillows by his head. Seeing Śrīla Prabhupāda's face resting between his Guru Mahārāja and the *Bhāgavatam*, so serene yet so still, I wanted to call out, "Prabhupāda! Prabhupāda!" (*He breaks dawn crying and is consoled by Tamal Krishna*. *Then with a voice choked with tears*) I understand. Prabhupāda no longer wants to be disturbed. He only wants to be with Kṛṣṇa. I understand. (*All are deep in thought*.)

**Satsvarūpa:** Prabhupāda has used these final days to teach us the greatest lesson of all.

**Svarūpa Dāmodara:** Śrīla Prabhupāda always taught us how to live in perfect Kṛṣṇa consciousness, and now just as perfectly he is teaching us how to die.

**Satsvarūpa:** And how to love. By allowing us to serve him in his broken health this year, Prabhupāda has been urging us toward unconditional surrender. It is a lesson that will always continue—so long as we remain faithful.

**Tamal Krishna:** (*enlightened*) Now I realize how Arjuna felt as he released his arrows at Grandfather Bhīṣma. Though the arrows entered Bhīṣma's body, it was Arjuna who felt the pain, while Bhtsma felt only joy. This final service will be the most difficult. Are we ready to allow Prabhupāda to leave? Is our love so selfless, so intense, we can endure the pain of real separation? Prabhupāda has been waiting only for this. Otherwise, he would have left long ago. The time has now come to give our permission. (*Declaring*) If Prabhupāda must depart, let it be glorious!

(*The chanting behind the scenes rises suddenly in speed and volume*. *Everyone looks at one another, and then they all rush out through the exit*. *After everyone exits, the chanting reaches a crescendo, with repeated cries of "Prabhupāda!"*  *and* *"Kṛṣṇa*!*" along with agonizing sobs.*)

## Act X

(*The sounds of lament diminish, then vanish altogether*. *At last, only a harmonium is heard*. *The harmonium plays a steady drone and from this chord comes the sound of a* kīrtana *at the base of Govardhana Hill*. *Joyous* kīrtana *procession enters*.

*Kṛṣṇadāsa* *Bābājī, Bon Mahārāja, Nārāyaṇa Mahārāja, Haṁsadūta Swami, Lokanātha Swami, Jayapatāka Swami, Bhavānanda Goswami, Jagadīsh, Satsvarūpa dāsa Goswami, Svarūpa Dāmodara, Tamal Krishna Goswami, Bhakti Charu Swami, Girirāj, Bhagatjī, the kavirāja, and others enter*. *They bear on a palanquin a* mūrti *image of Śrīla Prabhupāda*. *Tamal Krishna and Bhakti Charu, walking together, begin to talk*.)

**Tamal Krishna:** Well, Mahārāja, this was Prabhupāda's last desire, to go on *parikrama* to Govardhana with us all. He seemed so ill that we wanted to protect him. (*He pauses*.) But we also wanted so much to satisfy his every desire.

**Bhakti Charu:** This *kīrtana* is so wonderful. *Jaya* Prabhupāda, . . . *Jaya* Prabhupāda, . . . *Jaya* Prabhupāda, . . . Jaya Prabhupāda! (*Turning to Tamal Krishna, with hand on his shoulder, he speaks slowly with a gradual realization*.) Mahārāja, don't you see? Prabhupāda's departure is glorious! Don't you see?

(*Lights dim and* kīrtana *fades away*. *Tamal Krishna sees as if in a vision*. *A flute sounds, at first barely audible, then stronger*, *at last breaking into a dance melody evoking Lord Kṛṣṇa*.

*Prabhupada enters, his arms outstretched with eager anticipation, the flute music faintly audible*. *Yogamāyāand Śraddhāenter behind Prabhupāda*.)

**Prabhupāda:** O son of Nanda, where are You? (*He stops, having lost sight of Kṛṣṇa*. *Entranced, he speaks to himself as much as to Yogamāyāand Śraddhā*.) My eyes were drinking the beauty of Govinda's form, and the air was wondrously filled with the scent of musk and *aguru*. Then He smiled at His devotees and put His flute to His lips and began to play so sweetly. I raced toward them with overwhelming anticipation—when suddenly (*Prabhupāda tums to Yogamāyā and Śraddhā*.) He *disappeared*!

**Yogamāyā:** (*delicately*) I am Yogamāyā. I arrange the loving affairs of the Lord and His devotees. And this is Śraddhā, who enriches their devotional love.

**Prabhupāda:** It is by your wonderful mercy that I have been brought to Kṛṣṇa.

**Śraddhā:** By serving you we hope that we have pleased Lord Hari.

**Prabhupāda:** Ahhhh . . . do you smell the cool, perfumed breeze? (*He is visibly attracted by the fragrant breeze*, *and he looks off in the distance to find its source*.) Such a beautiful place captivates my heart and mind.

**Yogamāyā:** Though it may not be possible to reward you as you deserve, we did want to fulfill a very special desire of yours.

**Śraddhā:** See? There . . . !

**Prabhupāda:** (*surprised but ecstatic*) Why, it's *Govardhana*! Yes, this is my desire. I felt such intense separation from Kṛṣṇa, yet held by the love of my disciples I could not leave them. Nor could I live without Govinda. So I wished to see Govardhana and thus satisfy both desires. My disciples disagreed about my going on *parikrama*, though all loved me equally. You have kindly brought me here, so let them all be equally satisfied. (*Again looking in the distance*) There is no sight more beautiful than this.

**Yogamāyā:** Yes, Śrī Govardhana is indeed the monarch of all mountains. Though unwilling to be defeated by mighty Indra, who sent forth phalanxes of fierce clouds, furious lightning, and torrential rains, this powerfully courageous mountain becomes humbly pleased to wear the dark black cloud of Lord Kṛṣṇa as a crown upon his peak. When the golden lightning of Śrīmati Rādhārāṇī flashes amidst that cloud, ŚrīGovardhana's pleasure becomes boundless. Indeed, his tears of bliss form a river which the divine loving couple are pleased to sail down in one of Their sweetest pastimes. ŚrīGovardhana's caves also are the favorite sporting places of the divine couple. Therefore, who can surpass the loving service provided by Śrī Govardhana, who protects Vṛndāvana like a great umbrella whose handle is the beautiful arm of Lord Hari?

**Prabhupāda:** (*running ecstatically*) Let us go at once! (*The sound of a flute comes from offstage*. *Prabhupāda listens intently*.) Do you hear? It's Kṛṣṇa's flute!

**Śraddhā:** It must be, for the blessed mountain is shedding rivers of tears, and the trees and plants are standing erect in ecstasy.

**Prabhupāda:** Such special favor you have shown me!

**Śraddhā:** We are attentive to every desire of one as dear to Lord Kṛṣṇa as you, as well as to the legions of your disciples and followers.

**Yogamāyā:** Not a moment goes by when you are not in their thoughts. Because they are so dear to you, they have become dear to us as well.

**Śraddhā:** They continuously prayed to Kṛṣṇa not to take you away.

**Prabhupāda:** I told them that we must all accept whatever Kṛṣṇa desires.

**Yogamāyā:** True, but in their love for you they simply disregarded your instruction because they could not bear even the thought of being separated from you.

**Śraddhā:** Yogamāyā and I knew you wanted them to attain this exquisite happiness, and so we intervened.

**Prabhupāda:** But will they survive our separation from each other?

**Yogamāyā:** (*with particular emphasis*) If they remain absorbed in Lord Kṛṣṇa's service, they are united with you and will see you in the same dear form they have always worshiped, just as we have always seen your eternal form.

**Śraddhā:** Mother, did you not request Lord Kṛṣṇa to allow the disciples to be with their *guru* for some time longer?

**Yogamāyā:** Even better! I have arranged for the devotees to be eternally with their spiritual master, even while physically separated. See! (*She motions in the direction of a* kīrtana *sounding offstage*.)

**Śraddhā:** AND **Prabhupāda:** (*with joyous surprise*) Ahh!

**Prabhupāda:** They are *also* at Govardhana!

**Yogamāyā:** By Lord Kṛṣṇa's inconceivable potency there is no difference between Govardhana Hill in the spiritual sky and that manifested by Him on earth. Although the Lord concluded His earthly pastimes, devotees who remember them feel His presence even now. Similarly, your followers, absorbed in remembering your loving pastimes with them, are feeling your presence still.

(*They look with benediction upon the assembled devotees*)

**Yogamāyā:** Now tell us, is there anything further we can do for you?

**Prabhupāda:** What more can I possibly ask? If there is anything to ask, let it be this:

That the Deities and temples I have established continue to be maintained, the books I have written distributed without end, and the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement I founded continue to expand until it embraces the entire world.

**Yogamāyā:** O exalted, saintly one, be assured that Lord Kṛṣṇa shall certainly fulfill your prayers. Now without further delay, let us hasten to join Him and give Him pleasure.

(Kīrtana *grows louder and TamaI Krishna and Bhakti Charu turn to each other and smile joyously, dancing, and chanting "*Jaya *Prabhupāda* Jaya *Prabhupāda!" All exit*.)
