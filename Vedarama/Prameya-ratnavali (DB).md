# Prameya-ratnavali (DB)

## Introduction

## Preface

The prolific and astounding literary production that characterised Gaudiya Vaisnavism in the 16th century was followed by a drastic recess after Sri Jiva Gosvami left this world. Only a few authors were then engaged in writing Bengali compositions, and even rarer were those who wrote in Sanskrit. This picture did not change until late 17th century, when Sri Visvanatha Cakravarti became another luminary, whose writings revived the old scholarly tradition started by Lord Caitanya’s followers. Despite their substantial numbers, Gaudiya works were mostly within the scope of poetry, dramaturgy, and theology, besides commentaries on prominent Vaisnava texts. Until the beginning of the 18th century, Gaudiyas had shown little interest in presenting purely philosophical treatises, let alone comment on standard philosophical texts such as the Brahma-sutra. This made them somewhat alienated from other schools of thought that had established themselves by actively engaging in writing Vedanta commentaries and debating various opposing systems. Each of the four original Vaisnava sampradayas— Sri, Brahma, Rudra, and Kumara— had one or more Vedanta commentaries to their credit. So was the case with those who claimed affiliation to one of them, such as the Ramanandis and the Pustimargis. It was possibly with the intention of filling this gap that King Sawai Jai Singh u (1688-1743 AD) commissioned Sri Baladeva Vidyabhusana (c. 1700-1793 AD) to compose a Gaudiya commentary on the Brahma-sutra. In response, Sri Vidyabhusana first presented the Brahma-sutra-karika-bhasya in a very condensed format, and years later, the Govinda-bhasya, a much more extensive and elaborate work. In order to duly facilitate the comprehension of the latter, he subsequently composed the Siddhanta-ratna, a dense treatise covering major philosophical and theological topics. Being requested by his students, he further penned the Prameya-ratnavali, a Vedanta-prakarana in which he succinctly focused on the objects of knowledge or theorems (prameya) dealt with in Vaisnava philosophy.

Prameya is an essential category in any philosophical system. For instance, in the Nyaya-shastra (1.1.1), Gautama delineates the Nyaya philosophy in sixteen categories: pramana (means of knowledge), prameya (objects of knowledge), samsaya (doubt), prayojana (aim), drstanta (example), siddhanta (conclusion), avayava (members of a syllogism), tarka (hypothetical reasoning), nimaya (settlement), vada (discussion), jalpa (wrangling), vitanda (cavilling), hetvabhasa (fallacy), chala (quibbling), jati (sophisticated refutation), and nigraha-sthana (point of defeat). In sutra 1.1.9, he acknowledges twelve kinds of prameyas: atma (soul), sarira (body), indriya (senses), artha (sense objects), buddhi (cognition), manah (mind),pravrtti (activity), dosa (defects),pretyabhava (rebirth),phala (results), duhkha (suffering), and apavarga (liberation). In his commentary, Vatsyayana defines prameya in the following words: yo’rthas tattvatah pramiyate tat prameyam, “Prameya is an object that is accurately known.” Anything that can be known is a prameya, but Indian philosophers occupied themselves only with the prameyas they deemed meaningful to attain liberation.

The following definition appears on the top page of several manuscripts of the Prameya-ratnavali: pramatum yatharthyena jnatum yogyani prameyani tany eva ratnani tesam avail prameya-ratnavali, “Worthwhile theorems (prameya), which are to be truly known, are themselves jewels (ratna). A string (avail) of such jewels is called Prameya- ratnavali.” In this treatise, Vidyabhusana delves in the nine prameyas that have been handed down through the Madhva-parampara, as highlighted in a famous verse often attributed to Sri Vyasa Tirtha:

*sriman-madhva-mate harih paratamah satyam jagat tattvato*  
*bhedo jiva-gana harer anucara nicocca-bhavam gatah\|*  
*muktir naija-sukhanubhutir amala bhaktis ca tat-sadhanam*  
*aksadi-tritayam pramanam akhilamnayaika-vedyo harih\|*

**“In the view of the venerable Madhvacarya, 1) Lord Hari is the Supreme; 2) the world is real; 3) difference is real; 4) the living entities are servants of Lord Hari; 5) they attain a low or high state; 6) liberation means to experience bliss in one’s own constitutional form; 7) selfless worship of the Lord is the means to liberation; 8) direct perception, inference, and verbal testimony are the three kinds of evidence; and 9) Lord Hari is to be known through all sacred scriptures.”**

In spite of this outward approach, some of these prameyas have been explained by Vidyabhusana from a primarily Gaudiya perspective, rather than what would be considered as an orthodox Madhva exposition. The first prameya comprises half of the book and presents evidence of the supremacy of Lord Visnu, Who is non-different from Lord Krsna or any Visnu-tattva. Similarly, Goddess Laksmi is non-different from Sri Radha. The apparent difference between Their avataras is attributed to the gradation of the potency manifested by each of Them. While the Madhvas may have reservations regarding the ontological status of Radha, they strongly object to seeing difference in the Supreme Lord. The second prameya deals with the knowability of God through the scriptures, something that is universally accepted by Vaisnavas of all designations. The third prameya discusses the reality of the world, which is said to be manifested by the Lord’s energy. This theory of sakti-parinama-vada does not entail any transformation in the Supreme, which is in conformity with Madhvacarya’s philosophy. The fourth prameya clarifies the difference between God and the individual souls, which remains even after liberation. He is eternally one, and they are eternally many. In this connection, several monistic arguments are rebutted. The emphasis being on difference, there is no conflict with the Madhva ideology. The fifth prameya describes the jiva as an eternal servant of Lord Visnu. This is a further elaboration on the first prameya, elucidating how even the highest demigods are subordinate to Lord Visnu. Again, this is unarguable among Vaisnavas. The sixth prameya explains that although the jivas share common attributes such as atomic size and consciousness, there are gradations among them: in the material world, on the basis of their karma-, and in the spiritual world, on the basis of their mode of worship. The latter is described in terms of the five major rasas. This is an instance in which the author propounded a primarily Gaudiya tenet. The seventh prameya defines liberation as the attainment of Lord Krsna or any of His forms. The eighth prameya states that bhakti is the means to attain liberation. These two propositions are commonly shared with other schools, although they may differ as to the details. The ninth prameya establishes direct perception, inference, and verbal testimony as the valid means of knowledge (pramana), and Madhvacarya is mentioned as an authority in this regard. In the Tattva-sandarbha and Sarva-samvadini, Jiva Gosvami defends this same view and expounds that any further means of knowledge is encompassed within these three. Vidyabhusana presented these nine prameyas in a very succinct manner, leaving out much scope for dissension, and thus smoothly highlighted a relatively harmonious connection between the views propounded by Madhvacarya and those propounded by Caitanya Mahaprabhu.

A large part of the Prameya-ratnavali consists of ample quotations from the sruti and the smrti, interspersed by the author’s own verses, and the whole book is concise enough to be easily memorised, as he suggests at the end.

## Authorship and Time

Among Baladeva Vidyabhusana’s independent works, Prameya- ratnavali is by far the most copied, distributed, and published one. This is in great part due to its conciseness and didactical nature as an outline of the Madhva-Gaudiya system of thought. The plentiful manuscript copies, the consistency of the text in each copy, the historicity of the earlier copies, the correlation between other of his works, the internal evidence, and the coordination with the commentator leave zero scope to doubt that Vidyabhusana is the legitimate author of the original text or that his work was tampered with by an interpolator. The claims to the contrary will be debunked and exposed below as a deliberate and insidious conspiracy.

Out of the multiple manuscript copies consulted for preparing this critical edition, which are described below, two have an enormous historical significance and are decisive evidence to silence naysayers. Manuscript number 20 (described below) was thoroughly penned by Dayanidhi, who was Vidyabhusana’s main scribe for many years. We know his name from a manuscript of the Govinda-bhasya preserved in Gopiballabhpur, the Syamanandi-pitha in West Bengal, dated Samvat 1815 (1758 AD), in which he identifies himself as a brahmana and the son of the minister of Kurmacala. He not only copied most of Vidyabhusana’s works but also wrote several of his personal letters. This manuscript includes the Suksma-tika, so it can be safely assumed that 1758 AD is the year of its completion, for it would have been unethical to wait for years to gift a copy of the text to the Gosvamis in Gopiballabhpur. The Govinda- bhasya might have been completed either in the same year or pemaps one or two years earlier. In corroboration to this, despite Sawai Jai Singh’s avid interest in collecting Brahma-sutra commentaries, there is no copy of the Govinda-bhasya in the Khasmohor Collection, his personal library, for he passed away in 1743 AD.

The Kanti-mala commentary on the first verse explicitly states the purpose for which this book was written: “A commentary on the Brahma­sutra entitled Govinda-bhasya has been composed by Baladeva, also known as Vidyabhusana, who is exclusively devoted to Lord Govinda. Having been asked by some students about the objects of knowledge (prameya) in that commentary, and being about to briefly speak about these, he now recites an auspicious invocation. ” The commentary also refers to the Vedanta- syamantaka (on 8.8 and 9.1), Govinda-bhasya with Suksma-tika (on 1.16 and 8.10), and Gita-bhusana (on 8.10). On the basis of all this evidence, the Prameya-ratnavali may have been written between 1758 and 1768 AD. The manuscript number 16 indicates that the place of its composition might have been Jaipur, where Vidyabhfisana resided for many years while managing the Vijaya-Syamasundara Temple, which enjoyed the royal patronage for as long as it existed, as recorded in several documents of the period.

## Commentator

At the end of the Kanti-mala, the author clearly identifies himself as “Vedantavagisa.” He also wrote commentaries on the Govinda-bhasya and Siddhanta-ratna. Although his name is not openly mentioned in these two texts, therein it is evident that the commentator was thoroughly familiar with Vidyabhusana’s works and could be none other than a close associate. Indeed, the very fact that these three commentaries— which had been just recently written and would be known only to a few individuals— have cross-references to one another, indicates that they were penned by the same person. By a comparative study of these commentaries, it becomes apparent that they share the same explanations, illustrations, vocabulary, and quotations in multiple instances. This could have been possible only if they were composed by the same person or if one of the commentators were a plagiarist. Since these works were not yet available to others, the latter option is totally ruled out. In fact, Govinda-bhasya, Siddhanta-ratna, and Prameya-ratnavali are a trilogy, the last two being supplementary works on the first. Thus, it is quite expected that Vedantavagisa would have commented not only on one or two, but on all three of them.

In 1925 AD, the Gaudiya Matha published the Prameya-ratnavali with Kanti-mala. Sundarananda was the editor, and Ananta Vasudeva undertook the printing work. For reasons better known to them, they decided that the name of the commentator was Krsnadeva Vedantavagisa, although such a name is not seen in any of the innumerable manuscripts. To make matters worse, henceforth Vedantavagisa has been consistently mistaken for Krsnadeva Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya, a prominent siksa disciple of Visvanatha Cakravarti and a descendant of Jagannatha Cakravarti (Mamu Thakura). Documentary evidence suggests that Bhattacarya had been initiated in the Advaita-parivara.  Bhattacarya’s name is mentioned among five of Cakravarti’s disciples in a document dated Samvat 1769 (1712 AD) in connection with the Gokulananda ji Kunja at Radhakunda. Soon after, he settled in Amber and became the mahanta of the Vinodi Lal Temple. A document dated Samvat 1773 (1716 AD) records a grant he received from King Sawai Jai Singh u to be used in the service of the deity. He also played an important role in the Jaipur debates, and ample documentation corroborates his relationship with the King, who commissioned him to compose several treatises, such as Bhakti-vivrti, Karma-vivrti, Jnana-vivarana, Bhakti-phala-viveka, and Siddhantaikya-prakasika, all of which are part of the Khasmohor Collection, preserved at the Maharaja Sawai Man Singh u Museum in the City Palace in Jaipur. Most of these works seem to have been written between 1719 and 1723 AD. His best known book is the Padanka-dutam, whose earliest known manuscript is dated Sakabda 1645 (1723 AD). Commentaries on various works of Visvanatha Cakravarti, such as Alankara-kaustubha, Krsna-bhavanamrta, and Sankalpa-kalpa-druma also have the name “Krsnadeva Sarvabhauma” at the end. Bhattacarya was a householder, and by 1735 AD, his son Ramanatha Deva Sarma was already sharing some of his duties. Krsnadeva Bhattacarya passed away either in the mid or late 1740s, as a document dated Samvat 1802 (1749 AD) describes that after his demise, his grandson, Vrndavana Bhattacarya, claimed the same grant that had been previously given to his grandfather.

The obvious conclusion from all this is that Krsnadeva Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya can by no means be the author of the commentaries on the Vedanta works of Vidyabhusana, which were likely composed between 1745 and 1770 AD. On the one hand, Vidyabhusana would have been about half the age of Bhattacarya and an absolutely unknown personality when he first arrived in Jaipur. On the other, Bhattacarya was already reputed as a mahanta and scholar as early as 1715 AD, while Vidyabhusana’s name is not seen in any document related to Jaipur before 1741 AD. Moreover, in none of his works has Bhattacarya signed ‘Vedantavagisa,’ ‘Vagisvara,’ or any similar name. We also know from the commentary on the Siddhanta-ratna that its author was a sadhu (ascetic) rather than a householder.

Some believe that Cakravarti had another disciple called Krsnadeva Sarvabhauma Vedantavagisa, who is the actual author of the commentaries on the Alankara-kaustubha, Krsna-bhavanamrta, and Sankalpa-kalpa-druma. Such a claim could be definitely proved if we could actually find manuscripts of these commentaries where the name Vedantavagisa is mentioned, but up to the present day, I have not come across any, despite searching far and wide. This proposition is haunted with too many coincidences to be easily taken for granted. In view of the above dates and facts, both of the above theories are likely to be another distortion of the incidents, so common when hearsay prevails over recorded history. Furthermore, oral tradition says that one Krsnadeva accompanied Vidyabhusana to Jaipur at Cakravarti’s command. If there were two individuals named Krsnadeva, which one is meant in this case?

At present, there is hardly any known information about Vedantavagisa. Apart from the three mentioned commentaries, no other work seems to be credited to him. Even his first name is not revealed in the commentaries, which appears to be intentional. In the Gita-bhilsana (8.24) and Namartha-sudha (108), Vidyabhusana quotes a verse composed by Vedantavagisa (from Suksma-tika, 4.3.3) and refers to him as “the commentator” (nirnetrbhih). It would be unethical and disrespectful to refer to a contemporary and senior scholar like Krsnadeva Bhattacarya in such an informal and vague way. Rather, in the Aisvarya-kadambini (7.15), he is directly referred to as “Sri Sarvabhauma Prabhu.” That casual referral to Vedantavagisa indicates that the commentaries were indeed commissioned by Vidyabhusana himself. Ample manuscript evidence confirms that the commentaries were written in coordination with the author. In corroboration to this, very few manuscripts of the three Vedanta works do not include the commentary. In the latter case, some of the scribes might have deliberately copied only the main text due to time and paper constraints. As we know that Vedantavagisa was a sadhu and a scholar, it is natural to conclude that he was probably an associate of the same generation, who, out of humility, preferred to shun the limelight.

## Gloss

Among the large number of extant manuscripts, only number 16 (described below) has a gloss, which although different from the Kanti- mala, is closely related to it. Unfortunately, the manuscript is incomplete and abruptly ends on paragraph 6.3. No name is mentioned, but nevertheless a few factors corroborate Vidyabhusana’s authorship. Firstly, the gloss was noted down by his personal scribe, Dayanidhi. Rather than something written, it seems more like notes the author would have dictated while teaching the text. Being of a very terse nature, the gloss is mostly limited to word equivalents, definitions, syntactic connections, and a few quotations. Had it been written by Vedantavagisa, it is likely that the gloss would thoroughly match its equivalents in the Kanti-mala. However, this is often not the case, and in several instances sentences were paraphrased and selected vocabulary was replaced by synonyms, while some statements were omitted. This suggests that Vedantavagisa just borrowed from those notes wherever he felt appropriate and was otherwise at liberty to develop his own explanations.

## Polemics

For anyone who accepts Sri Vidyabhusana’s authority, this exposition of nine prameyas, added to the guru-parampara given in the very beginning of the book, leaves no scope to doubt that the Gaudiyas are a branch of the Brahma-Madhva sampradaya. For those who may question Vidyabhusana’s word on the matter, there is also extensive earlier evidence that corroborate the same conclusion. There are some who, when faced with all the available evidence, opt for distorting, dismissing, or desecrating it, to the extent of even damaging Vidyabhusana’s name and works, in which Madhva is often acknowledged as a previous acarya. Although there seems to have been a certain amount of confusion for centuries, while investigating such an outrageous plot, we are led to a few individuals who had something peculiar in common— avowed hatred towards Srlla Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and his mission. As bizarre as it may sound, such individuals did not hesitate to turn nearly anything into ammunition in their attempt to malign Sarasvati Thakura and his words, even at the cost of the previous acaryas. In their desperate efforts to pose as scholars while criticising an exalted Vaisnava, they simply made fools of themselves, as do those who accept their words instead of verifying the facts as they are. This will become apparent in the following analysis of some of their statements.

## Sundarananda Vidyavinoda

After joining the Gaudiya Matha in Dhaka, Sundarananda served as editor of their publications for many years. After Bhaktisiddhanta’s disappearance, he sided with Ananta Vasudeva and thus contributed to the disintegration of the mission. He did not officially leave the Gaudiya Matha like Ananta did, but rather took advantage of his position to create internal havoc. By the association of such dubious individuals, Sundarananda gradually became averse to the teachings of Sarasvati Thakura, and his views on Gaudiya philosophy and history drastically changed, culminating in the serious deviations he propounded in books such as Acintya-bhedabheda-vada (1951) and Gaudiyar Tin Thakur (1953). The former includes a whole chapter demoralising Baladeva Vidyabhusana, in which he put forward blatant lies and distortions that later inspired several other Gaudiya Matha haters to adopt and promote the same views. Some of his statements will be dealt with below.

After a short introduction of Vidyabhusana’s life, Sundarananda straight away accuses him of fabricating the philosophical and disciplic link between the Gaudiyas and the Madhvas, which Sundarananda claims to have never been pointed out by any previous acarya. Primarily, he dismisses Kavi Karnapura’s Gaura-ganoddesa-dipika (GGD) because, on the following grounds, “many” consider the relevant passage to be interpolated: 1) the verses sampradaya-vihina ye mantrah, etc. are not found in the Padma Purana; 2) Madhavendra’s sannyasa title is Puri, while all Madhva sannyasis are named Tirtha; and 3) In the Caitanya- candrodaya (8.4), Karnapura describes that Mahaprabhu belittled the Tattvavadis.

From the outset, it is clear how malicious and dishonest the naysayers are and how intent they are on desecrating evidence. Sundarananda again reveals his real face in Gaudiyar Tin Thakur (pg. 399), where he quotes Rasabihari Samkhyatirtha’s assertion that GGD was purposely written by Vidyabhusana under Karnapura’s name to forward his agenda of promoting the Madhva link. I have consulted multiple manuscript copies of GGD, none of which lacked the said verses, none of which had the slightest indication of interpolation, and many of which belong to the 16th and 17th centuries. Among the various manuscript copies preserved at the Vrindavan Research Institute, the one under accession number 20118, which was received from the Radha-Damodara Temple, is clearly from late 16th century and used to be Jiva Gosvami’s personal copy. On the top of that, GGD has been quoted by several authors that preceded Vidyabhusana, such as Narahari Cakravarti in the Bhakti-ratnakara.

As for their arguments, the response is as follows: 1) If the non­existence of a particular verse in the available editions at present amounts to its illegitimacy, then why the naysayers are not ranting about the countless instances seen in nearly every Gaudiya book? Why are they obsessed only with this single instance? Some Puranas have gone through several recensions and it is puerile to assume that all of them should be available today. In the beginning of the Tattva-sandarbha, Jiva Gosvami informs that although he has not personally seen many of the verses he will quote, he relies on Madhvacarya’s word on their authenticity. Moreover, verses similar to those attributed to the Padma Purana are found in the Garga-samhita. As we will see below, before the Gaura-ganoddesa-dipika, which is dated Saka 1498 (AD 1576), those verses were also quoted by Devakinandana in the Vaisnava-vandana, of which a manuscript copy is dated Samvat 1618 (1561 AD), and also in the Nava-ratnam of Harirama Vyasa, a contemporary of the Vrndavana Gosvamis. Another set of very similar verses were provided by Dr. B.N.K. Sharma: "Of course, the Puranic traditions have it that Madhva is connected with the Brahma- sampradaya: atah kalau bhavisyanti catvarah sampradayinah\\ sri- brahma-rudra-sanaka vaisnavah ksiti-pavanah\\\\ ucyate kramatah siddhis tatracarya-catustayam\\ sampradaye tu sanake tv acaryo hi nimanujah\\ tatra sri-sampradaye tu namna ramanujo matah\\ sampradaye brahma- namni madhvacaryah pratisthitah\\\\ (Padma Purana, Narayana to Sanatkumara) ”

2\)  It is clear from the example of Mahaprabhu and his associates that in those days, sannyasa and philosophical affiliation were not necessarily related. There is a rumour that Madhavendra took sannyasa in the line descending from Visnu Puri, the author of Bhakti-ratnavali, who was also known as a disciple of Jayadharma Tirtha. There is also a possibility that Laksmipati Tirtha named some of his sannyasi disciples in Northern India with different titles to distinguish them from the orthodox Southern tradition. Contrary to the belief that Madhvas hold no title other than Tlrtha, one of the most famous disciples of Narahari Tirtha (13th-14th century) was Gopala Krsna Sarasvati. The title Dasa also became standard among the Haridasas. Madhavendra could have taken sannyasa from a Puri sannyasi from the Visnu Svami sampraddya, or even from an Advaiti, as did Mahaprabhu and Madhvacarya himself. Madhavendra Puri was widely known in those days as a Madhva, a fact corroborated not only by multiple Gaudiya authors, but by Pustimargis too, who certainly have no stake in any dispute on the matter. By the end of his Sri-Vallabha- digvijaya, Yadunatha (16th century AD), the grandson of Vallabhacarya, described the episode of Vallabhacarya’s taking sannyasa from Madhavendra Puri, whom he describes as a sannyasi of the Madhva- sampradaya. In his Do Sau Bavan Vaisnava Ki Varta, Gokulanatha (16th century AD), another grandson of Vallabhacarya, narrated how Vitthalanatha studied the scriptures from a Madhva renunciant named Madhavendra Puri.  One of the earliest known references to Madhavendra as a Madhva is found in an unpublished Odia manuscript entitled Bhakti-jnana-brahma-yoga, attributed to Acyutananda Dasa (early 16th century), one of the members of the panca-sakha of Odisha. Prabhat Mukherjee refers to another two Odia texts that mention the same: Isvara Dasa’s (end of 16th century) Caitanya Bhagavata and Divakara Dasa’s (early 17th century) Jagannatha Caritamrta. In the Vrajotsava-candrika, written in Samvat 1612 (1555 AD), Narayana Bhatta, a grand-disciple of Gadadhara Pandita, repeatedly presents himself as a member of the Madhva-sampradaya. Similarly, in the introduction of the Nava-ratnam, Harirama Vyasa (early 16th century), a grand-disciple of Madhavendra Puri, also presents his disciplic succession coming down from Madhvacarya. His descendants still live at Kishor Van in Vrndavana and unhesitatingly assert their connection with Madhva. So does Narahari Cakravarti (early 18th century) in the Bhakti-ratnakara, fifth wave, in the section dealing with Raghava’s description of Lord Gauranga’s pastimes to Srinivasa. Therein, he states that the mentioned parampara list was heard from Gopalaguru, a contemporary of Lord Caitanya. In verse 5.2082, he clearly describes Advaitacarya as a member of the Madhva- sampradaya. Those same verses containing this list were quoted by Manohara Dasa in his Sampradaya-bodhini. Manohara Dasa described the four Vaisnava sampradayas at the end of the Anuragavallt, dated Samvat 1753 (1696 AD), in which he counts the Gaudiyas among the Madhvas and assures that there is no reason to doubt this connection. Similarly, in his Bhakta-mala, dated 1717 AD, Raghava Dasa also describes the Madhva-Gaudiya parampara. In the Narayana-bhatta-caritdmrta (3.33.47), Janakiprasada Bhatta also corroborates the same disciplic succession.

3\)  The Caitanya-candrodaya (8.4) says: sri-krsna-caitanyah— kiyanta eva vaisnava drstas te’pi narayanopasaka eva. apare tattvavadinas te tatha- vidha eva. niravadyam na bhavati tesam matam. apare tu saiva eva bahavah. pasandas tu maha-prabala bhuyamsa eva. kintu bhattacarya ramananda-matam eva me rucitam, “Sri Krsna Caitanya: ‘I have seen many Vaisnavas who are worshippers of Lord Narayana. I have also seen similar Vaisnavas known as Tattvavadis. Their philosophy is not free from blemish. I have seen many others who are worshippers of Lord Siva. I have also seen many powerful heretics. However, О Bhattacarya, I only like the philosophy of Ramananda Raya.’” The objection raised on the basis of this passage is fallacious, for Mahaprabhu is merely referring to those He happened to meet during His travels. If this statement is taken in an absolute sense, then it implies that He does not like the philosophy of Sridhara Svami, Madhavendra Puri, or Isvara Puri, which contradicts narrated facts. In any case, neither Karnapura, nor Vidyabhusana, nor any Gaudiya acarya who corroborates the Madhva link ever suggested that this implies a full subscription to every idea that is circulated among the Madhvas. Nor do the Madhvas agree among themselves on countless topics. Philosophical diversity within the same religious group is far from being something unheard of. Madhva and Ramanuja marked a major turn from their predecessors. In all Vaisnava sampradayas there is a substantial divergence among their respective followers, which sometimes may lead to the formation of sub-sects. Among Sri Vaisnavas, for example, the division of Tenkalais and Vadakalais is notorious. Nevertheless, both are undisputedly connected to Ramanujacarya in many respects. The very fact that the Ramanandis of Jaipur had declared their affiliation to the Sri- sampradaya and the Pustimargis had declared their affiliation to the Visnu-svami-sampradaya, despite their respective independent views, corroborates that philosophy was not the main criteria to be accepted as legitimate. From the mentioned Padma Purana verse, it seems that receiving a mantra from a sampradaya was indeed the main factor under consideration to ascertain whether one’s affiliation was genuine or not, which would be possible only by identifying one’s religious lineage. While the disputants seemed to be somewhat lenient regarding the dynamics of philosophy throughout the centuries, the same certainly could not be applied to the Vedic mantras. Then follows some supposed lines from the Sarva-samvadini (3.78), which the naysayers proclaim as if they were the ultimate “evidence” on the case: gautama-kanada-jaimini-kapila-patanjali-mate tu bheda eva, sri-ramanuja-madhvacarya-mate cety api sarvatriki prasiddhih, sva-mate tv acintya-bhedabhedav evacintya-saktimayatvad iti, “In the view of Gautama, Kanada, Jaimini, Kapila, and Patanjali, there is difference. It is also well known everywhere that it is so in the view of Ramanujacarya and Madhvacarya. In my view, however, by dint of the Supreme’s inconceivable energy, there is inconceivable difference and non-difference.” First of all, the topic under discussion in this paragraph of the Paramatma-sandarbha and its commentary is restricted to the relation between cause and effect. Secondly, those who cannot see that there is something terribly wrong with the above reading obviously lack a minimum background in the mentioned philosophical systems. According to the Samkhya-karika (9), the effect is of the nature of its cause (karana-bhavat). In his commentary named Tattva-kaumudi, Vacaspati Misra specifies: karyasya karanabheda-sadhanani ca pramanani— na patas tantubhyo bhidyate, iha yad yato bhidyate tat tasya dharmo na bhavati, yatha gaur asvasya, dharmas ca patas tantunam, tasman narthantaram, “The following evidences establish that an effect is non- differentfrom its cause. A cloth is non-different from its threads. An object is not the attribute of something it differs from. For instance, a cow is not the attribute of a horse. A cloth is the attribute of its threads, and therefore it is not a different substance. ” This view is corroborated by Sankaracarya in the Brahma-sutra (1.4.28): sa ca karya-karanananyatvabhyupagamat pratyasanno vedanta-vadasya, “As Samkhya accepts that cause and effect are of the same identity, this system is close to that of Vedanta. ” In the Sloka- vartika (5.4.123), Kumarila Bhatta refers to a Samkhya system that accepts difference and non-difference (bhedabheda). Yet no Samkhya-related text advocates difference between cause and effect. Sharing similar concepts of Satkaryavada, so is the case with Patanjali’s school, which is portrayed in the Mana-meya-rahasya-sloka-vartika (9.6.2) is this way: patanjalah punar bhedabheda-vadavalambinah, “The followers of Patanjali hold the view of difference and non-difference. ” Nor do Mimamsa proponents such as Prabhakara Misra and Kumarila Bhatta defend difference. The latter’s position is thus defined: atyanta-bhinnatasmabhir naiva kasyacid isyate, sarvam hi vastu-rupena bhidyate na parasparam (Ibid. 12.3.7), “Any object’s absolute difference is not accepted by us, for nothing is different in its essential form. ” Ramanujacarya’s position is very clear from his own words in the Sri-bhasya (2.1.15-23), in which he extensively deals with this topic and concludes: tasmat karanad ananyat karyam, “Therefore, the effect is non-differentfrom the cause. ”

As for Madhvacarya, the statements in the Bhagavata-tatparya- nirnaya are unambiguous and well-known: visesasya visistasyapy abhedas tadvad eva tu, sarvam cacintya-saktitvad yujyate paramesvare, tac- chaktyaiva tu jivesu cid-rupa-prakrtav api, bhedabhedau tad-anyatra hy ubhayor api darsanat, karya-karanayos capi nimittam karanam vina (11.7.49/ Brahma-tarka), “Similarly, there is non-difference between qualifier and qualified. By dint of His inconceivable potency, everything that has been stated suits the Supreme Lord. Also by His inconceivable potency, non-difference exists in the jivas and in the spiritual nature as well. Difference and non-difference exist elsewhere (in the material nature), for both are seen in other elements. There is also difference and non-difference between cause and effect, with the exception of the efficient cause (Brahman). ” bhedabhedau parijneyau karya-karanayor api (11.22.45), “It should understood that difference and non-difference also exist between cause and effect.” prakrtes tu vikaranam koty-amso bheda isyate, tathaivaikamsato bhedah so’pi nabheda-varjitah, bhedabhedam atah prahur abhedam va tayor budhah (11.24.17/ Viveka), “Difference is considered to exist among the transformations of material nature with its millions of parts. Similarly, there is also difference from one part to another. Yet such difference is not devoid of non-difference. Therefore, the wise say that there is either difference and non-difference or just non-difference between material nature and its transformations. ” In this way, those who accept the above reading as authentic make fools of themselves and a mockery of Jiva Gosvami’s scholarship and integrity.

The minor differences in the lists seen in various texts prove that they were not copied or interpolated based on a single source, but were rather received through oral tradition, which may not have been thoroughly consistent from Bengal through Vraja. Vidyabhusana himself opted for presenting a list as he heard from the Gaudiya tradition. If he had forged it, he would at least have adopted one of the tables current among the Udupi Madhvas verbatim. Sundarananda’s imputation that the names of Madhavendra Puri and Isvara Puri do not appear in any of the Southern tables of the Madhva-parampara is preposterous, as if he did not know that they include only pontiffs and heads of the major monasteries, a post never occupied by either of them.

## Radhagovinda Natha

Relatively renowned among Bengali readers for a number of publications, such as Gaudiya Vaisnava Darsana, Radhagovinda Natha openly expressed enmity towards the Gaudiya Matha from its very inception. The conflict escalated until a complaint was lodged against him, and under the order of a local magistrate, he was compelled to withdraw the allegations and submit an apology, a notice of which was published in the Gaudiya magazine issue of the 11th February, 1928. In his early writings, he unhesitatingly defended the Madhva link, and it was only after becoming familiar with Sundarananda Vidyavinoda’s cited book that he realised the potential of the case in his muffled campaign against the Gaudiya Matha, as Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati not only defended the Madhva link but even included Baladeva Vidyabhusana in the Bhagavata-parampara, a line of prominent predecessor acaryas connected either by diksa or by siksa.

No learned man disputes that Murari was a brahmana.

The statement that Rasikananda Murari was the fourth predecessor of the author according to the pancaratrika guru-parampara must be rejected. A householder surely does not mention his fourth predecessor guru without having mentioned his own father, nor does a wandering ascetic recall the name of his fourth predecessor guru without mentioning the name of his own guru, for this is against the etiquette. ” If only Aksaya had educated himself before attempting to lecture, then pemaps he could have spared himself from so much embarrassment. First of all, the commentary on the Siddhanta-ratna was written by Vedantavagisa, who made exactly the same statement in his commentary on the last verse of the Prameya- ratnavali. Thus, trying to take help from another book was far from ingenious. Secondly, Vidyabhusana acknowledges Sri Radha-Damodara as his guru, who was a disciple of Nayanananda Gosvami, whose guru was Rasikananda Murari.

Aksaya then quotes the Govinda-bhasya on the apasudradhikarana (1.3.34-36) to show how Vidyabhusana concludes that only brahmanas have the right to teach the Vedas: “It is not at all possible for a non-brahmana to make such a statement that only brahmanas have the right to teach the Vedas. How would he, being a vaisya, accept the position of an acarya and teach after having debarred his own right? No sane man can speak in this way. Therefore, their incoherent talk about brahmana by diksa, seminal brahmana, brahmana by occupation, and so on is rejected, ‘sarvatraskhalitadesah sapta-dvipaika-danda-dhrk, anyatra brahmana-kulad anyatracyuta-gotratah.’ These words of the Bhagavatam establish that one is a brahmana by birth only. In Vidyabhusana’s Vedanta-symanantaka, the word ‘vipra ’ also corroborates that he was a brahmana. ” At this point, it is not difficult to say who lacks sanity and coherence in this discussion. Although Vidyabhusana never used the words “seminal brahmana," this is the only thing that Aksaya can presume. It is obviously impossible for him to see anything beyond his casteist glasses. He is also oblivious to the status of Vaisnavas. In the Padyavali (115), Rupa Gosvami quotes a verse that explains this mentality: arcye visnau sila-dhir gurusu nara-matir vaisnave jati-buddhih... naraki sah, “One who considers a Vaisnava on the basis of caste is a resident of hell.” Although Aksaya fancies himself as a knower of etiquette, he totally ignores the fact that even ordinary renunciants give up family ties and are then not to be questioned about their caste. While Aksaya ignored the aforementioned verses, he now quotes the Srimad-Bhagavatam as if it endorsed his views, although the verse says nothing about birth. The words brahmana-kula simply mean ‘the community of brahmanas,’ and acyuta- gotra is a widely known synonym of‘Vaisnava.’ Yet, in Aksaya’s opinion, it can only mean ‘the seminal descendants of Vasudeva,’ as if there were any during Prthu’s time. Finally, Vedanta-syamantaka was written by Radha-Damodara, whom both Vidyabhusana and Vedantavagisa attested as a brahmana from Kanyakubja.

In this way, it is apparent why Aksaya remains an obscure author who took upon himself the task of correcting Sarasvati Thakura. May this help him attain the recognition he deserves.

## Prameya-ratnavali

### Text 1

*jayati sri-govindo gopinathah sa madana-gopalah*  
*vaksyami yasya krpaya prameyaratnavalim suksmam*

First interpretation

**Перевод:**

**All glories to the lover of the gopis, Lord Govinda, that cowherd boy Who delights the hearts of His devotees. It is by His mercy that I shall now recite this concise treatise named Prameya-ratnavali.**

Second interpretation

**Перевод:**

**All glories to Lord Govinda, Lord Gopinatha, and Lord Madana- gopala, by Whose mercy I shaU now recite this concise treatise named Prameya-ratnavali.**

**Комментарий:**

*gaudodayam upayatas tamah samastam nihanti yo yugapat*  
*jyotis ca yo’tisitah pitas tam upasmahe krtanjalayah*

**With folded hands, we worship Lord Krsna Caitanya Mahaprabhu, Who appeared in Gaudadesa like a very cooling and golden moon, dispelling all darkness at once.**

A commentary on the Brahtna-sutra entitled Govinda-bhasya has been composed by Baladeva, also known as Vidyabhusana, who is exclusively devoted to Lord Govinda. Having been asked by some students about the objects of knowledge (prameya) in that commentary, and being about to briefly speak about these, starting with the words jayati sri- govindah, he now recites an auspicious invocation for the completion of this treatise without any obstacles. What is Lord Govinda like? He is the lover of the gopis (gopinathah). He is called Madana because He delights (madayati) the hearts of His devotees, and His name is Gopala because He tends (pala) the cows (go). Therefore, this is a karmadharaya compound. The meaning of the rest is clear. As a pun, this is a glorification to the three presiding deities of Vrndavana named Govinda, Gopinatha, and Madana-gopala, Who are the favourite arcavataras of all of Lord Caitanya’s devotees. In both interpretations, this is an auspicious invocation done as an obeisance, for this is indicated here by the word jayati (all glories).

### Text 2

*bhakty-abhasenapi tosam dadhane*  
*harmadhyakse visva-nistari-namni*  
*nityanandadvaita-caitanya-rupe tattve*  
*tasmin nityam astam ratir nah*

First interpretation

**Перевод:**

**The Supreme Soul, Krsna, is pure consciousness, eternally blissful and non-dual. He is the promulgator of religious principles, His name delivers the whole universe, and He becomes satisfied with even a reflection of devotion. May we always have attachment for Him.**

Second interpretation

**Перевод:**

**The Supreme Soul in the form of Caitanya, Nityananda, and Advaita becomes satisfied with even a reflection of devotion. He is the promulgator of religious principles, and His name delivers the whole universe. May we always have attachment for Him.**

**Комментарий:**

Here again the author recites an auspicious invocation requesting attachment for the Supreme Lord. The word tattve means “to the Supreme Soul, Krsna.” According to the Visva-kosa (Va, 18), the word tattva means a kind of musical instrument, one’s own nature, and the Supreme Soul. What is He like ? The author says: He becomes pleased (tosam dadhane) with even a reflection (abhasena) of devotion (bhakti), just as it is seen that He became pleased with Ajamila when he was uttering His name, although Ajamila meant his son. He is the promulgator (adhyaksa) of religious principles (dharma). He is endowed with eternal (nitya) bliss (ananda). There is no duality (advaita) in Him, that means there is no difference between His body and its possessor. He is pure consciousness (caitanya). This is a karmadharaya compound. These attributes are His very Self (rupe). Alternatively, this verse can be interpreted as follows: Lord Krsna descended in this Age of Kali along with Sahkarsana and Siva to deliver the people. Lord Krsna’s name now became Caitanya, Sankarsana’s name became Nityananda, and Siva’s name became Advaita. May we (nah) always (nityam) have attachment (ratih) for this (tasmin) threefold form (rupe) of the Supreme Lord (tattve). The rest of the verse is the same as in the previous interpretation. The evidence for this should be understood from the original texts on the matter.

### Text 3

*ananda-tirtha-nama sukha-maya-dhama yatir jiyat*  
*samsararnava-taranim yam iha janah kirtayanti budhah*

**Перевод:**

**All glories to the ascetic named Ananda Tirtha, an abode of bliss, whom the wise in this world glorify as a boat to cross the ocean of repeated birth and death.**

**Комментарий:**

Here the author salutes the previous acarya. Ananda Tirtha is another name of Sri Madhvacarya. The word yatih means “wandering ascetic,” and taranim means “boat.”

## Guru-parampara

### Text 4

*bhavati vicintya vidusaih*  
*niravakara guru-parampara nityam*  
*ekantitvam sidhyati yayod*  
*ayati yena hari-tosah*

**Перевод:**

**The learned should constantly meditate on a spotless lineage of preceptors, through which one attains exclusive devotion, by which Lord Hari becomes pleased.**

**Комментарий:**

The author here says that one should meditate on a guru- parampara from which the objects of knowledge discussed in the Govinda- bhasya are received. Guru-parampara is a lineage (parampara) of spiritual preceptors (guru). According to the Visva-kosa (Ra, 247), the word parampara is used in the sense of “succession,” “lineage,” and in some places, “hurting.” The word niravakara means “spotless.” What is the use of such meditation ? The author says: by meditating on such a parampara, the meditator attains (sidhyati) exclusive devotion (ekantitvam) for Lord Hari, and by such devotion, He becomes pleased (tosa udayati). This is corroborated in the smrti, such as: tesam jnani nitya-yukta eka-bhaktir visisyate, priyo hi jnanino’tyartham aham sa ca mama priyah (Bhagavad ­gita, [7.17](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/7.17)), “Among them, a wise person who has exclusive devotion for Me is the best, for I am exceedingly dear to the wise, and the wise are also exceedingly dear to Me.”

### Text 5

*yad uktam padma-purane—*  
*sampradaya-vihina ye mantras te viphala mat ah*  
*atah kalau bhavisyanti catvarah sampradayinah*  
*sri-brahma- rudra-sanaka vaisnavah ksiti-pavanah*  
*catvaras te kalau bhavya hy utkale purusottamat, iti*

**Перевод:**

**As stated in the Padma Purana: “The mantras received outside a sampradaya are considered fruitless. Therefore, in Kali-yuga there will be four founders of sampradayas: Sri, Brahma, Rudra, and Sanaka Kumara. These Vaisnavas will be the sanctifiers of the earth in the Age of Kali and will be sent from Jagannatha Puri in Odisha.”**

**Комментарий:**

Formerly, there were four promulgators of the path to instruct the objects of knowledge, and from them, others become manifest, just as the streams of the Ganga. Without the path taught by them, Visnu- mantras obtained from treatises on mantra do not give liberation. This is spoken in the words of the Padma Purana here. Sampradaya is the path instructed by a spiritual master who was taught by a learned preceptor. To be such a learned preceptor means to follow the authority of the Vedas. Although being chanted, Visnu-mantras are not fruitful if devoid (vihina) of a sampradaya, and therefore (atah) in the beginning of the Age of Kali (kalau), four (catvarah) founders of sampradayas (sampradayinah) appeared. Who were they? The next line gives the answer. The word purusottamat means “sent by Lord Jagannatha from Jagannatha Puri.”

### Text 6

*ramanujam srih svicakre madhvacaryam caturmukhah*  
*sri-visnu-svaminam rudro nimbadityam catuhsanah*

**Перевод:**

**Goddess Laksmi accepted Ramanujacarya, Brahma accepted Madhvacarya, Rudra accepted Visnu Svami, and the four Kumaras accepted Nimbarkacarya.**

**Комментарий:**

Observing that their respective sampradayas had not expanded much, these four founders established four leading acaryas, one in each of their respective spiritual lineages. This is what the author says here. Goddess Laksmi (srih) accepted Ramanuja as capable to forward Her sampradaya. The meaning of the rest is clear.

### Text 7

*tatra sva-guru-parampara yatha—*  
*sri-krsna-brahma-devarsi-badarayana-samjnakan*  
*sri-madhva-sri-padmanabha-sriman-nrihari-madhavan*  
*aksobhya-jayatirtha-sri-jnanasindhu-dayanidhin*  
*sri-vidyanidhi-rajendra-jayadharman kramad vayam*  
*purusottama-brahmanya-vyasatirthams ca samstumah*  
*tato laksmipatim sriman-madhavendram ca bhaktitah*  
*tac-chisyan srisvaradvaita-nityanandan jagad-gurun*  
*devam isvara-sisyam sri-caitanyam ca bhajamahe*  
*sri-krsna-prema-danena yena nistaritam jagad iti*  
*iti guru-parampara*

**Перевод:**

**Among these, our own (sva) guru-parampara is as follows: the Supreme Lord Sri Krsna, Brahma, Devarsi Narada, Badarayana, Madhva, Padmanabha, Nmari, Madhava, Aksobhya, Jaya Tirtha, Jnanasindhu, Dayanidhi, Vidyanidhi, Rajendra, Jayadharma, Purusottama, Brahmanya, Vyasa Tirtha, Laksmipati, and then Sri Madhavendra Puri. We glorify all of them with devotion. We worship Madhavendra Puri’s disciples named Isvara Puri, Advaita, and Nityananda, the gurus of the whole world, and also Isvara Puri’s disciple, Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, the Supreme Lord Himself. By freely giving love for Krsna, He delivered the world. This is our guru-parampara.**

**Комментарий:**

Leaving aside the paramparas of Laksmi, Rudra, and the four Kumaras, since they are not the main purpose here, the author now mentions his own lineage, the Brahma-parampara. In the first section of the Gopala-tapani Upanisad it is clear that Brahma is a disciple of Lord Krsna. It is well known from history that the sage Madhva was a disciple of Badarayana (Vyasadeva). The following incident is also well known: Once Madhva and Sankara, while fasting, were having a philosophical discussion amidst an assembly of thousands of scholars at Manikarnika- ghata in Varanasi. Resembling a dark-blue cloud, Vyasadeva was then seen by all in the sky. He accepted Madhva’s view and rejected Sankara’s. Isvaracarya, Advaitacarya, and Nityananda were disciples (sisyan) of Madhavendra Puri (tat). Lord Krsna Caitanya was a disciple of this same Isvara who was a disciple of Madhavendra Puri. In this way, it is demonstrated that everyone should know their respective guru­paramparas by connecting them to the current lineal successors of these three masters. The word yena means “by Lord Caitanya.”

## Prameyas

### Text 8

*atha prameyany uddisyante—*  
*sri-madhvah praha visnum paratamam akhilamnaya-vedyam ca visvam*  
*satyam bhedam ca jivan hari-carana-jusas taratamyam ca tesam*  
*moksam visnv-anghri-labham tad-amala-bhajanam tasya hetum pramanam*  
*pratyaksadi-trayam cety upadisati harih krsna-caitanya-candrah*

**Перевод:**

**The objects of knowledge are now enunciated: Sri Madhva declares that 1) Lord Visnu is the Supreme; 2) He is to be known by means of all sacred scriptures; 3) the world is real; 4) difference is real; 5) the living entities are servants of Lord Hari’s feet; 6) there is gradation amongst them; 7) liberation is the attainment of Lord Hari’s feet; 8) selfless worship of Him is the cause of liberation; and 9) direct perception, inference, and verbal testimony are the three kinds of evidence. Krsna Caitanya-candra, Lord Hari Himself, teaches the same thing.**

**Комментарий:**

After having thus mentioned his own guru-parampara, the author now enunciates its objects of knowledge. The sage Madhva, our previous acarya, declares (aha) that Lord Visnu is the Supreme (paratamam) to be known (vedyam) by means of all (akhila) sacred scriptures (amnaya). He refutes the concept that Visnu is non-different from all living entities and that He is defined in the scriptures as being mere consciousness and one alone. He declares that the world is real, and so is difference. The meaning is that he refutes the misconception of others who imagine that due to ignorance (avidya), the material manifestation and its difference from the Supreme are false. Madhvacarya states that the living entities (jivan)— the conditioned ones, the liberated ones, and the eternally liberated ones— are all servants (jusah) of Lord Hari’s feet (carana). He refutes the idea that they are Lord Hari Himself. He says that although the living entities have (tesam) the same spiritual nature, there is dissimilarity (taratamyam) among them on account of the results manifested by spiritual practice. He also refutes the concept that they enjoy the same results, as propounded by certain Tridandi-vaisnavas. He explains that liberation (moksam) of the living entities is the attainment (labham) of Lord Visnu’s feet (ahghri), that means realising Lord Visnu. He refutes the idea that the living entities are a form of Visnu, as supposed by others. He states that worship (bhajanam) of Lord Visnu (tat) without any personal desire (amala) is the cause (hetum) of liberation (tasya). He refutes the concept that the cause of liberation is the knowledge that ‘I am Brahman.’ He explains that in his view, there are three kinds (trayam) of evidence (pramanam): direct perception (pratyaksa), inference (anumanam), and verbal testimony (sabda). He refutes the idea that comparison (upamana) and so on (adi) are additional kinds of evidence besides these three. These are the nine objects of knowledge accepted by the sage Madhva, and Sri Krsna Caitanya, Who is Lord Hari Himself, accepted initiation in his line and teaches (upadisati) them to his disciples. The present tense here (upadisati) applies to both Madhva and Caitanya, since this holds good to both.

It is well known from the Kena Upanisad that Vayu, the wind-god, the life-air of the universe, is exclusively devoted to Lord Visnu. It was he who, in the form of Hanuman, pleased Lord Rama; in the form of Bhima, he pleased Krsna; and in the form of Madhva, he pleased the great sage Vyasadeva by defeating all views contrary to his. Although Sri Krsna Caitanya is God Himself, considering Madhva’s philosophy as the best and desiring the welfare of people, He accepted initiation in his spiritual line, where pure dualism (dvaita), the non-difference between Lord Hari’s Self and body, and other such views are explained.

## First Prameya

### Text 9

*tatra sri-visnoh paratamatvam yatha sri-gopalopanisadi— tasmat*  
*krsna eva paro devas tam dhyayet tam raset tam bhajet tam yajet, iti*  
*svetasvataropanisadi ca— jnatva devam sarva-pasapahanih ksinaih*  
*kiesair janma-mrtyu-prahanih tasyabhidhyanat trtiyam deha-bhede*  
*visvaisvaryam kevalam apta-kamah etaj jneyam nityam evatma-*  
*samstham natah param veditavyam hi kincit, iti sri-gitasu ca— mattah*  
*arataram nanyat kincid asti dhananjaya, iti*

**Перевод:**

**Among these, Lord Visnu’s supremacy is described in the Gopala-tapani Upanisad (**[**1.54**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/1.54)**) in the following words: tasmat krsna eva paro devas tarn dhyayet tam raset tarn bhajet tarn yajet, “Therefore, Krsna alone is the Supreme God. One should remember, utter, serve, and worship Him in the form of His mantra and its referent.” It is also said in the Svetasvatara Upanisad (**[**1.11-12**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/1.11)**): jnatva devam sarva-pasapahanih ksinaih kiesair janma-mrtyu-prahanih, tasyabhidhyanat trtiyam deha- bhede visvaisvaryam kevalam apta-kamah, “Knowing God, all bonds vanish, and when all distress is thus destroyed, one’s repeated birth and death also vanish. By meditating on the Supreme Lord, upon breaking out of the subtle body, one attains His pure abode, which has unlimited opulences, and all one’s desires are fulfilled. ” etaj jneyam nityam evatma- samstham natah param veditavyam hi kincit, “The eternal Supreme Soul Who dwells within the individual soul is to be known, beyond Whom there is nothing else to be known.” In the Bhagavad-gita (**[**7.7**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/7.7)**) is also stated: mattah parataram nanyat kincid asti dhananjaya, “O Arjuna, there is no Supreme other than Me.”**

**Комментарий:**

Having thus enunciated the objects of knowledge, with the present verse, the author starts to substantiate them gradually. The word paratamatvam means “supremacy.” “Therefore” (tasmat) means “on account of the various reasons that have been previously mentioned. ” One should remember (dhyayet), utter (raset), serve (bhajet), and worship (yajet) Krsna (tam) in His twofold form as being both His mantra and its referent. After one who is resolved to attain liberation knows (jnatva) the Supreme God (devam) from the scriptures explained by a bona fide spiritual master, all (sarva) bonds (pasa) in the form of the material body and bodily identification vanish (apahanih). The distresses (klesaih) caused by such bonds are also destroyed (ksinaih) along with them, and thus vanish (prahanih) the birth and death (janma-mrtyu) of a particular person who has been repeatedly born for the completion of the unripe results of one’s past activities. The meaning is that one no more experiences suffering from birth and death, just as a kitten does not suffer by the touch of the teeth of the mother cat. Then, by meditating (abhidhyanat) on God (tasya) more and more, when one’s subtle body (deha) is destroyed (bhede), one who meditates on the Supreme Lord attains His abode, which is the third (trtiyam) abode in relation to the abode of the moon-god and that of Lord Brahma. That means one becomes liberated. What is this third abode like? It has complete opulences (visvaisvaryam) and is not even slightly tainted (kevalam) by material nature. Then, one who meditates on God has all desires fulfilled (apta-kamah). This (etat) Supreme Lord is to be known (jneyam), beyond (atah) Whom (param) there is nothing (na kincit) to be known (veditavyam), for He is the Supreme. Lord Krsna says that “there is no (na kincit) Supreme (parataram) other (anyat) than Me (mattah), that means you should know that I alone am the Supreme of all.” The word parataram means “supreme.” The suffix tarap should be taken as retaining the original meaning of the word para (supreme).

### Text 10

*hetutvad vibhu-caitanyanandatvadi-gunasrayat*  
*nitya-laksmy-adimattvac ca krsnah paratamo matah*

**Перевод:**

**Lord Krsna is considered the Supreme because He is both the instrumental and the material cause, is a reservoir of qualities such as all­pervasiveness, consciousness, and bliss, and is endowed with eternal potencies such as Laksmi.**

**Комментарий:**

Here the author states the reasons for Lord Visnu’s supremacy. He is both the instrumental and the material cause (hetutvat) of the material manifestation. It should be understood that being endowed with the superior energy, He is the instrumental cause (nimitta), and being endowed with pradhana and the living entities, He is the material cause (upadana). The meaning of the rest is clear.

### Text 11

*tatra sarva-hetutvam yathahuh svetasvatarah— ekah sa devo bhagavanvarenyo yoni-svabhavan adhitisthaty ekah\| yac ca svabhavaih pacati visva-yonih pacyams ca sarvan parinamayed yah, iti\| vibhu- caitanyanandatvam yatha kathake— mahantam vibhum atmanam matva dhiro na socati, iti\|*  
*vijnana-sukha-rupatvam atma-sabdena bodhyate*  
*anena mukta-gamyatva-vyutpatter iti tad-vidah*

**Перевод:**

**The Supreme Lord is the cause of everything, as stated in the Svetasvatara Upanisad (**[**5.4-5**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/5.4)**)- ekah sa devo bhagavan varenyo yoni- svabhavan adhitisthaty ekah, yac ca svabhavam pacati visva-yonih pacyams ca sarvan parinamayed yah, “God, the highest of all, is the worshipable Supreme Lord Who presides over the various natures of the causal elements without being touched by them. He is the ultimate cause of the universe, He develops the natures of the causal elements, and He transforms them into subsequent elements.” His qualities such as all­pervasiveness, consciousness, and bliss are described in the Katha Upanisad (**[**1.2.22**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/katha-bs/1.2.22)**): mahantam vibhum atmanam matva dhiro na socati, “After understanding that the Supreme Soul is worshipable and all- pervasive, a wise person does not grieve.” According to those who know the Supreme, a transcendental form of consciousness and bliss is understood by the word atma here, for its etymological meaning is “that which is attained by the liberated souls.”**

**Комментарий:**

God (devah) is the Supreme Lord (bhagavan), the highest of all (ekah). Therefore, He is worshipable (varenyah). Without anyone’s assistance (ekah), being invested with His superior energy, He presides over (adhitisthati) the natures (svabhavan) of the causal elements (yoni) such as pradhana and mahat-tattva. According to the Amara-kosa (3-3.16), the word eka is used in the sense of chief, other, and alone. The Visva-kosa (Na, 21) states that the word yoni is used in the sense of place of origin and the female organ. According to the Haima-kosa, the word yoni means cause, the female organ, and copper. The Amara-kosa (1.6.39) says that the words svabhava and svarupa are synonyms of “nature.” Or else, the word ekah here means “other than all these elements,” that is to say “untouched by them.” It is the Supreme Lord Who develops (pacati) the natures (svabhavan) of the primordial elements such as pradhana, which means that He brings them towards the states in which their effects in the form of the mahat-tattva and so on become manifest. It is the Supreme Lord Who transforms (parinamayet) all (sarvan) those primordial elements, starting with pradhana, which are capable of turning into an effect (pacyan); that means He brings pradhana into the state of mahat-tattva, mahat-tattva into the state of ahankara, so on and so forth. Thus, it is the Supreme Lord Who, invested with His superior energy, is the instrumental cause of the universe, and invested with pradhana and the living entities, is the material cause of the universe (visva-yonih). The word mahantam means “worshipable,” and matva means “after understanding and worshipping Him.” Someone may argue, “In this sentence, we find a mention of God’s all-pervasiveness, but there is nothing about consciousness and bliss.” In response, the author says, “According to those, etc.” The word atma means “that which is attained (atyate) by the liberated souls.” The affix manin is used after the verbal root at (to attain) when the objective case is expressed. The meaning is that the liberated souls certainly meditate on and attain such a Supreme Lord.

### Text 12

*vajasaneyinas cahuh— vijnanam anandam brahma ratir datuh parayanam, iti\| sri-gopalopanisadi ca— tam ekam govindam sac-cid- ananda-vigraham, iti\|*  
*murtatvaih pratipattavyam cit-sukhasyaiva ragavat\|*  
*vijnana-ghana-sabdadi-kirtanac capi tasya tat*  
*deha-dehi-bhida nastityetenaivopadarsitam*

**Перевод:**

**The texts of the Vajasaneya branch of the Vedas speak as follows: vijnanam anandam brahma ratir datuh parayanam (Brihad-aranyaka Upanisad,** [**3.9.28**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bau/3.9.28)**), “The Supreme Lord is knowledge and bliss personified, the one Who gives the results, and the ultimate goal of the sacrificer. ” In the Gopala-tapani Upanisad (**[**1.33**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/1.33)**) it is also said: tarn ekam govindam sac-cid-ananda-vigraham, “I satisfy Lord Govinda, Whose form is eternity, knowledge, and bliss personified.” The form of the conscious and blissful Supreme Lord should be understood to be like the form of a raga. His form is described in the scriptures by words such as vijnana-ghana, and by this it is demonstrated that there is no difference between His body and its possessor.**

**Комментарий:**

This being so, the author now describes the Supreme Lord, Who is expressed by words. The word datuh means “sacrificer,” and rdtih means “one Who gives the results.” The meaning of the text quoted from the Gopala-tapani Upanisad is clear. Someone may argue: “How can something that consists of consciousness and bliss have form?” The author replies with the words “murtatvam, etc.” The meaning is that just as the form of a raga such as the Bhairava is perceived by an expert ear infused with music, the Supreme Lord’s form is similarly perceived within the heart by one infused with devotion. The Supreme Lord’s possession of form (murtatvam) is described in the Gopala-tapani Upanisad ([2.97](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/2.97)) by using words such as vijnana-ghana in connection with Him: manusi vijnana-ghanananda-ghana sac-cid-anandaikarase bhakti-yoge tisthati, “My human-like form is the embodiment of consciousness and bliss, and it is always present in unalloyed devotional service, which also consists exclusively of consciousness, eternity, and bliss.” In the Astadhyayi (33-77), Panini states: murtau ghanah, “The word ghana is used in the sense of form.” According to Sanskrit grammar, the affix ap comes after the verbal root han, which is then substituted by ghan when it is used in the sense of solidity. An example is saindhava-ghana (a lump of salt). This should be understood as being possible due to His inconceivable potency (acintya-sakti). “By this” (etena) means that by establishing the form of such substance as consisting of consciousness and bliss, it is also expressed that there is no (nasti) difference (bhida) between the Supreme Lord’s body (deha) and its possessor (dehi).

### Text 13

*murtasyaiva vibhutvam yatha mundake— vrksa iva stabdho divi tisthaty ekas tenedam purnam purusena sarvam, iti\|*  
*dyu-stho’pi nikhila-vyapity akhyanan murtiman vibhuh*  
*yugapad dhyatr-vrndesu saksatkarac ca tadrsah*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord’s form is all-pervasive, as described in the sruti: vrksa iva stabdho divi tisthaty ekas tenedam purnam purusena sarvam, “The ruler of all is situated in the spiritual sky, immovable like a tree, and this whole universe is pervaded by Him, the Supreme Person.” By the statement that although situated in the spiritual sky, the Supreme Lord pervades everything, it is understood that He possesses form and is all- pervasive. He is also understood as such on account of being simultaneously perceived by multiple meditators.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “How can something that has form be all-pervasive?” The author answers in the present paragraph. The ruler of all (ekah), the Supreme Person, Lord Hari, is situated (tisthati) in the spiritual sky (divi). He is immovable (stabdhah) like (iva) a tree (vrksah), for He is to be revered by everyone different from Him. That means He does not bow down to anyone. This (idam) whole (sarvam) universe is pervaded (vyaptam) by this Supreme Person (purusena) alone. His possessing form is understood by the statement that He, the Supreme Person, stands in the spiritual sky. His all-pervasiveness is understood by the statement that this whole universe is pervaded by Him. The all­pervasiveness of His form is also understood on account of His being simultaneously (yugapat) perceived (saksatkarat) by multiple (vrndesu) meditators (dhyatr) who have attained love for Him and who are very far from each other. It is not that He runs to approach them, for this would contradict simultaneity.

### Text 14

*sri-dasame ca— na cantar na bahir yasya na purvam napi caparam\| purvaparam bahis cantar jagato yo jagac ca yah\| tam matvatmajam avyaktam martya-lingam adhoksajam\| gopikolukhale damna babandha prakrtam yatha, iti\| sri-gitasu ca— maya tatam idam sarvam jagad avyakta-murtina\| mat-sthani sarva-bhutani na caham tesv avasthitah\| na ca mat-sthani bhutani pasya me yogam aisvaram, iti\|*  
*acintya saktir astise yoga-sabdena yocyate*  
*virodha-bhanjika sa syad iti tattva-vidam matam*

**Перевод:**

**In the Srimad-Bhagavatam (**[**10.9.13-14**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/9.13)**) it is also said: na cantar na bahir yasya na purvam napi caparam, purvaparam bahis cantar jagato yo jagac ca yah, tam matvatmajam avyaktam martya-lingam adhoksajam, gopikolukhale damnababandha prakrtam yatha, “The Supreme Lord has neither interior nor exterior, neither front nor back, but He is situated inside and outside the universe, in its front and its back, and He is Himself the universe. That Supreme Lord, Who is imperceptible to the senses and is the enjoyer of His Own intrinsic bliss, is now in the guise of a human. Considering Him as her son, gopi Yasoda tied Him to a mortar with a rope as if He were an ordinary child.” In the Bhagavad-gita it (**[**9.4- 5**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/9.4)**) is also stated: maya tatam idaih sarvam jagad avyakta-murtina, mat- sthani sarva-bhutani na caham tesv avasthitah, na ca mat-sthani bhutani pasya me yogam aisvaram, “This whole universe is pervaded by Me in My form as Paramatma. All created beings are sustained by Me, but I am not sustained by them. Yet those beings are not sustained on Me. Behold My divine power.” The Supreme Lord is endowed with an inconceivable potency that removes all contradiction, and which is expressed here by the word yoga. This is the opinion of those who know the truth.**

**Комментарий:**

The Supreme Lord (yah) is not divided in places such as inside (antah), outside (bahih), front (purvam), or back (aparam), and therefore He is simultaneously situated in all places of the universe (jagatah). Through His Own energy, He takes the form of the universe (jagat). Considering (matva) Him as her son and at fault, the cowherd lady (gopika) Yasoda tied (babandha) Him to a mortar (ulukhale) with a rope (damna). What is He like? He is in a two-handed human form (martya- lingam), and rejects all happiness derived from the senses (adhoksajam); that means He is the enjoyer of His Own intrinsic bliss. From the statement that she tied Him as if He were an ordinary child, it is clear that the all- pervasive Lord is an embodiment of consciousness (vijnana-ghana) and possesses form. The meaning of the verses quoted from the Gita is as follows: “This (idam) whole (sarvam) universe (jagat) is pervaded (tatam) by Me (maya) in My form as Paramatma (avyakta-murtina). All beings (sarva-bhutani) are sustained by Me (mat-sthani), but I (aham) am not (na) sustained (avasthitah) by them (tesu). Yet those beings (bhutani) are not sustained in Me (mat-sthani) like water in a jar, but they are sustained by My mere will.” This is what Lord Krsna intends to say by the words “na ca mat-sthani, etc.” Someone may argue, “How can this be possible?” In the next line, the Lord replies: “behold (pasya) this extraordinary power (yogam) of Mine (me), the Supreme Lord.” According to the following etymological definition, yoga is the Lord’s inconceivable power: yujyate durghatesu karyesv anena, “ Yoga is that by which one becomes fit for tasks that are difficult to accomplish.”

### Text 15

*adina sarvajnyam yatha mundake— yah sarvajnah sarvavit, iti\| ananditvam ca taittiriyake— anandam brahmano vidvan na bibheti kutascana, iti\| prabhutva-sauharda -jnanadatva-mocakatvani ca svetasvatara-srutau— sarvasya prabhum isanam sarvasya saranam suhrt, iti\| prajna ca tasmat prasrta purani, iti\| samsara-bandha-sthiti- moksa-hetuh, iti ca\| madhuryam ca sri-gopalopanisadi— sat- pundarika-nayanam meghabham vaidyutambaram\| dvi-bhujam mauna- mudradhyam vana-malinam isvaram, iti\|*

**Перевод:**

**The Supreme Lord’s omniscience is expressed by the word adi (etc.). As stated in the Mundaka Upanisad (**[**1.1.9**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/mundaka/1.1.9)**;** [**2.2.7**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/mundaka/2.2.7)**): yah sarvajnah sarvavit, “The Supreme Lord is omniscient and all-knowing.” His blissfulness is described in the Taittiriya Upanisad (**[**2.4.1**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/taittiriya/2.4.1)**): anandam brahmano vidvan na bibheti kutascana, “One who knows the Supreme Lord’s blissfulness never fears.” Qualities such as lordship, kindheartedness, and being the giver of knowledge and liberation are stated in the Svetasvatara Upanisad: sarvasya prabhum isanam sarvasya saranam suhrt (**[**3.17**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/3.17)**), “The Supreme Person is the omnipotent Lord and Ruler of all, the shelter and friend of all.” prajna ca tasmat prasrta purani (**[**4.18**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/4.18)**), “It is because of the Supreme Lord that primeval knowledge becomes manifest.” samsara-bandha-sthiti-moksa-hetuh (**[**6.16**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/6.16)**), “The Lord is the cause of the state of bondage in the cycle of birth and death, and also the cause of liberation from it.” His sweetness is described in the Gopala-tapani Upanisad (**[**1.9**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/1.9)**): sat-pundarika-nayanam meghabham vaidyutambaram, dvi-bhujam mauna-mudradhyam vana- malinam isvaram, “One should meditate on Lord Govinda, the Supreme Lord, Who has beautiful lotus eyes and two hands. His complexion is like that of a cloud, and His dress is effulgent like lightning. He wears a garland of forest flowers and is in a posture of meditative silence.”**

**Комментарий:**

The word adi (etc.) here should be understood as the one mentioned in the compound vibhu-caitanyanandatvadi. One who is aware (janati) of everything (sarvam) is omniscient (sarvajnah), and one who knows (vindati) everything (sarvam) is all-knowing (sarvavit). One who knows (vidvan) the Supreme Lord’s (brahmanah) quality of blissfulness (anandam) is never (na kutascana) afraid (bibheti) of time, karma, and so on. The meaning is that one who knows His attributes becomes liberated. The Supreme Person is the omnipotent Lord (prabhum) and Ruler of all, the shelter (saranam) that removes distress, and the friend (suhrt) who, without any self-interest, does good to all. It is by worshipping the Supreme Lord (tasmat) that the living entities’ primeval (purani) dharma-bhuta knowledge (prajna) becomes manifest (prasrta). Sweetness (madhuryam) is the performance of activities that are accomplished by His supreme power in a human form. For instance, taking away Putana’s life air by sucking her breast, breaking a very hard wooden cradle by striking it with His soft feet, holding Govardhana Hill in the form of a seven year-old child, etc. The author then illustrates the Lord’s human form by quoting the Gopala-tapani Upanisad.

### Text 16

*na bhinna dharmino dharma bheda-bhanam visesatah*  
*yasmat kalah sarvadastity-adi dhir vidusam api*

**Перевод:**

**Although the attributes are non-different from their possessor, this cognition of difference is due to visesa, on account of which even the wise hold concepts such as ‘time always exists.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “Are attributes such as all-pervasiveness different from Lord Hari or not? The first proposition is not valid, for this would contradict the following sruti statement, which denies such difference: yathodakam durge vrstam parvatesu vidhavati, evam dharman prthak pasyams tan evanuvidhavati (Katha Upanisad, [2.4.14](https://vedarama.com/en/books/katha-bs/2.4.14)): ‘Just as rain water on the hills runs down into impassable places, similarly one who sees the Lord’s attributes as different from Him falls down.’ The second proposition is also not valid, for this would imply that Lord Hari has no attributes, which is denied by you.” Here the author solves this doubt. Although there is no difference, the effect of difference exists due to visesa, and therefore His being devoid of attributes is out of the question. Visesa is a semblance of difference, and not difference. Someone may argue, “Where has such a concept been seen?” The author replies in the second line of the above verse. By the word adi (etc.), sentences such as ‘existence exists’ should be included. Although there is no difference, in the sentence ‘time always exists,’ time is understood as the substratum of time, and in the sentence ‘existence exists,’ existence is understood as the substratum of existence. Similarly, so is the case in the topic under discussion. More about this can be learnt from the Govinda-bhasya and its Suksma-tika (3.2.28-31).

### Text 17

*evam uktam narada-pancaratre— nirdosa-purna-guna-vigraha atma- tantro niscetanatmaka-sarira-gunais ca hinah\| ananda-matra-kara- pada-mukhodaradih sarvatra ca svagata-bheda-vivarjitatma, iti\|*

**Перевод:**

**It is similarly stated in the Narada-pancaratra: nirdosa-purna- guna-vigraha atma-tantro niscetanatmaka-sarira-gunais ca hinah, ananda-matra-kara-pada-mukhodaradih sarvatra ca svagata-bheda- vivarjitatma, “The Supreme Soul is independent. His self is totally free of internal differences, and His hands, feet, face, belly, and so on exclusively consist of complete bliss.”**

**Комментарий:**

One whose body is devoid of defects such as ignorance, and is full of qualities such as omniscience, is the Supreme Lord Visnu. Is His body made of pure material goodness as proposed by the Mayavadis ? In response, the text says that His body is devoid (hinah) of the qualities of a body (sarira-gunaih) made of insentient matter (niscetanatmaka). This means that His body consists of consciousness and is manifest as having the quality of consciousness on account of visesa. Does His body have only consciousness for its single element as proposed by the Samkhya philosophers? The text says that His body consists of complete bliss (ananda-matra). The meaning is that His body consists of consciousness and bliss. Is His soul different from His body as proposed by some followers of Visvaksena? The text says that although the concept of a body and its possessor, and the concept of qualities and their possessor exist in the Supreme Lord, His self is also totally (sarvatra) free of internal differences (svagata-bheda-vivarjitatma). There are three kinds of differences. ‘A mango tree is not a jackfruit tree’— this is an example of difference amongst things of the same class (sajatiya-bheda). ‘A mango tree is not a stone’— this is an example of difference amongst things of a different class (vijatiya-bheda). ‘Mango flowers are not a mango tree’— this is an example of internal difference (svagata-bheda).

How can places such as Mathura, which are within the domain of material nature, be His abode?” In response, the author says, “prapance, etc.” The reason for understanding His abode (lokam) as consisting of His Own nature (svatmakam) is the use of words such as brahma. By the word adi (etc.), words such as mahima and samvyoma should be included. “If it is so, then why do Mathura and other holy cities seem to be material?” The author says, “govinde, etc.” The words nara-darakata mean “an ordinary boy.”

### Text 28

*atha nitya-lilatvam ca\| tatha hi srutih— yad gatam bhavac ca bhavisyac ca, iti\| eko devo nitya-lilanurakto bhakta-vyapi bhakta-hrdy antaratma, iti ca\| smrtis ca— janma karma ca me divyam evam yo vetti tattvatah\| tyaktva deham punar janma naiti mam eti so’rjuna, iti*

**Перевод:**

**Lord Visnu also has eternal pastimes. As stated in the sruti: yad- gatam bhavac ca bhavisyac ca (Brhad-aranyaka Upanisad,** [**3.8.4**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bau/3.8.4)**), “The qualities and activities that pertain to the Supreme Lord and exist in the past, present, and future.” eko devo nitya-lilanurakto bhakta-vyapi bhakta-hrdy antaratma (Purusa-bodhini Upanisad, 4), “The same Supreme Lord is engaged in eternal pastimes, pervades the devotees, and is also the Supreme Soul within the hearts of the devotees.” The smrti also confirms: janma karma ca me divyam evam yo vetti tattvatah, tyaktva deham punar janma naiti mam eti so’rjuna (Bhagavad-gita,** [**4.9**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/4.9)**), “One who factually knows My transcendental appearance and activities in this way does not, upon leaving the body, take birth again in this material world but attains Me, О Arjuna.”**

**Комментарий:**

The text ‘yad-gatam’ is from the Bmad-aranyaka Upanisad. Qualities and activities pertaining (gatam) to the Supreme Lord (yat) are eternal, for their permanence in all the three times is understood by the words past, present, and future. The text ‘eko devah’ is from the Purusa- bodhini Upanisad, which belongs to the Pippalada branch of the Atharva Veda. The eternity of the pastimes is clearly expressed here. The text ‘janma karma’ is from the Bhagavad-gita. The word divyam means “transcendental,” “eternal.”

### Text 29

*rupanantyaj jananantyad dhamanantyac ca karma tat*  
*nityam syat tad-abhedac cety uditam tattva-vittamaih*

**Перевод:**

**Since the Supreme Lord has eternal forms, eternal associates, and eternal abodes, His activities also must be eternal, for all these are non- different from Him— this has been declared by the best among those who know the truth.**

*iti prathamam prameyam*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the first object of knowledge.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “In the absence of reasoning, the eternity of the Lord’s pastimes, as understood from the scriptures, is unsubstantiated.” The author replies to this objection with the present verse. In this regard, the learned speak as follows: “Because there is action in a pastime, it must be said to be accomplished through a beginning and an end, even at each of its parts. Without a beginning and an end, its own character would not be accomplished. Thus, since a pastime has a beginning and an end, it is certain to have a perishable nature. Therefore, how can it be eternal?” In response, the following is stated: Since the Supreme Lord at all times has eternal (anantyat) forms (rupa), eternal associates (jana), and eternal abodes (dhama), His pastimes (karma) cannot be non-eternal. Although the pastimes in connection with a particular form of the Lord have a beginning and an end, when some particular parts of a pastime may have ended or not in a particular manifestation and place, they may have begun in another manifestation elsewhere. Thus, it is proved that the pastimes exist eternally, for they have no interruption.

Someone may argue, “There may be no interruption in the pastimes. Yet, since it has a different beginning, how is a pastime said to be the same?” In response to this, the following is stated: Although they are manifested at different times, there is oneness in the activities of a particular form. As it is said: dvih pako’nena krto na tu dvau pakau, “When it is said that someone has cooked twice, the act of cooking is only one, and not two.” dvir go-sabdo’yam uccarito na tu dvau go-sabdau, “When the word ‘cow’ is uttered twice, the word ‘cow’ is only one, and not two. ” Just like the oneness of words is ascertained by understanding these examples, the Lord’s pastimes should be similarly understood. This is what the author says next, “for all these are non-different (abhedat) from Him (tat),” that means all these four— His forms, associates, abodes, and pastimes— are non-different from the Lord.

Thus, the section of the Prameya-ratnavali entitled “Lord Visnu’s Supremacy” has been explained.

## Second Prameya

### Text 1

*athakhilamnaya-vedyatvam\| yatha sri-gopalopanisadi— yo ’sau sarvair vedair giyate, iti\| kathake ca— sarve veda yat padam amananti tapamsi sarvani ca yad vadanti, iti\| sri-harivamse ca— vede ramayane caiva purane bharate tatha\| adav ante ca madhye ca harih sarvatra giyate, iti*

**Перевод:**

**Lord Visnu’s knowability through all the Vedas shall now be discussed. As stated in the Gopala-tapani Upanisad (**[**2.27**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/2.27)**): yo’sau sarvair vedair giyate, Lord Krsna is sung about by all the Vedas." It is also said in the Katha Upanisad (**[**1.2.15**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/katha-bs/1.2.15)**): sarve veda yat padam amananti tapamsi sarvani ca yad vadanti, “That spiritual nature which all the Vedas describe and because of Whom they lay down austerities.” The Hari-vamsa (3.132.95) also confirms: vede ramayane caiva purane bharate tatha, adav ante ca madhye ca harih sarvatra giyate, “Lord Hari is sung of everywhere in the Vedas, Ramayana, Maha-bharata, and Puranas, in the beginning, middle, and end.”**

**Комментарий:**

In order to explain that Lord Hari is to be known through all the Vedas, the author says, “athakhilamnaya-vedyatvam.” In the text from the Gopala-tapani Upanisad, the word yah refers to Sri Gopala, Krsna. In the text from the Katha Upanisad, the words ‘yat padam’ mean “that spiritual nature called Brahman.” As stated in the Amara-kosa (33-93), the word pada means “business,” “protection,” “abode,” “characteristic,” “foot,” and “object.” The meaning of the verse from the Hari-vamsa is clear.

### Text 2

*saksat-paramparabhyam veda gayanti madhavam sarve*  
*vedantah kila saksad apare tebhyah paramparaya*

**Перевод:**

**Both directly and indirectly, all the Vedas certainly glorify Lord Madhava— the Upanisads in a direct way, and the karma-kanda in an indirect way.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “In the Vedas, explanations of ritualistic ceremonies (karma) are largely seen. How do you reconcile this with the aforementioned verses that were given as examples?” In response, the author says: The Upanisads (vedantah) glorify (gayanti) Lord Madhava directly (saksat), while other (apare) than these (tebhyah), i.e., the ritualistic section (karma-kanda) of the Vedas, glorify Him indirectly (parampara) by the method of prescribing rituals that cause a purification of the heart, which is an auxiliary means to understand God. Thus, it is well demonstrated that Lord Hari is to be known through all the Vedas.

### Text 3

*kvacit kvacid avacyatvaih yad vedesu vilokyate*  
*kartsnyena vacyam na bhaved iti syat tatra sangatih*  
*anyatha tu tad-arambho vyarthah syad iti me matih*

**Перевод:**

**It is seen that in certain places in the Vedas, Lord Hari is said to be indescribable. This must be reconciled by understanding that the Lord cannot be fully described. Otherwise, the effort of the Vedas would be useless. This is my opinion.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “It is seen (vilokyate) in the scriptures that Lord Hari cannot be described (avacyatvam) by the Vedas. For instance: yato vaco nivartante, aprapya manasa saha (Taittiriya Upanisad, [2.4.1](https://vedarama.com/en/books/taittiriya/2.4.1)), ‘Failing to attain the Supreme, words return accompanied by the mind.’ What is the meaning of such sentences?” The author answers this here. Although Mount Meru is visible, it is also said to be invisible, for it cannot be fully (kartsnyena) seen. Similarly, so is the case here. Otherwise (anyatha), if God cannot be described by the Vedas at all, the effort (arambhah) in studying the Vedas to know Him would be useless (vyarthah).

### Text 4

*sabda-pravrtti-hetunam jaty-adinam abhavatah*  
*brahma nirdharmakam vacyam naivety ahur vipascitah*

**Перевод:**

**Merely based on the absence of semantic causes for the use of words, such as generic (jati), the learned do not say that Brahman is devoid of attributes.**

**Комментарий:**

According to the monists, since generic words (jati-sabda), quality words (guna-sabda), action words (kriya-sabda), and arbitrary words (samjna-sabda) do not exist in Brahman, Brahman cannot be described by the words of the Vedas, which consist of those.

### Text 5

*sarvaih sabdair avacye tu laksana na bhaved atah*  
*laksyam ca na bhaved dharma-hinam brahmeti me matam*

**Перевод:**

**If Brahman cannot be described by any word at all, then a secondary meaning is not possible. Nor can a Brahman devoid of attributes be indirectly indicated. This is my view.**

*iti dvitiyam prameyam*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the second object of knowledge.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “Since the words of the Vedas are applied to Brahman in a secondary sense, the effort to study them is not useless.” In response, the author says: “You accept that Brahman cannot be described (avacye) by any (sarvaih) word (sabdaih). Consequently (atah), a secondary meaning (laksana) is not possible (na bhavet).” Just as in the sentence ‘so’yam devadattah’ (This is that same Devadatta), bhaga-laksana is understood in Devadatta’s body, which is expressed by the word ‘body’ (pinda).

Thus, the section of the Prameya-ratnavali entitled “Lord Visnu is to be Known Through all the Vedas” has been explained.

## Third Prameya

### Text 1

*atha visva-satyatvam\|*  
*sva-saktya srstavan visnur yathartham sarva-vij jagat*  
*ity ukteh satyam evaitad vairagyartham asad-vacah*  
*tatha hi svetasvataropanisadi— ya eko’varno bahudha sakti-yogad varnan anekan nihitartho dadhati, iti\| sri-visnu-purane ca— eka-desa- sthitasyagner jyotsna vistarim yatha \| parasya brahmanah saktis tathedam akhilam jagat, iti\| isavasyopanisadi— sa paryagac chukram akayam avranam asthaviram suddham apapa-viddham\| kavir manisi paribhuh svayambhur yathatathyato’rthan vyadadhac chasvatibhyah samabhyah, iti*

**Перевод:**

**The reality of the universe shall now be discussed. The omniscient Lord Visnu factually created the universe through His Own energy— according to scriptural statements like this, the universe is indeed real. It is said to be unreal only for the sake of detachment. For instance, in the Svetasvatara Upanisad (**[**4.1**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/4.1)**) it is said: ya eko’varno bahudha sakti-yogad varnan anekan nihitartho dadhati, “The unique Supreme Lord is devoid of material designations. Having a particular purpose, He creates manifold varieties of categories by engaging His energy.” It is also stated in the Visnu Purana (**[**1.22.56**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vp/1.22.56)**): ekadesa- sthitasyagner jyotsna vistarini yatha, parasya brahmanah saktis tathedam akhilam jagat, “Just as its light spreads in all directions, although fire is situated in one place, similarly this whole universe is the energy of the Supreme Lord.” In the Isa Upanisad (**[**8**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/iso/8)**) it is declared: sa paryagac chukram akayam avranam asthaviram suddham apapa- viddham, kavir manisi paribhuh svayambhur yathatathyato’rthan vyadadhac chasvatibhyah samabhyah, “The Supreme Soul has pervaded all directions. He is effulgent, devoid of subtle and gross bodies, undecaying, free from passion, and free from karma. That omniscient, wise, and causeless Supreme Lord, the subjugator of illusion, truly creates the material elements through everlasting years.”**

**Комментарий:**

In order to explain the reality of the material manifestation, the author says, “atha visva-satyatvam.” Someone may argue, “It is said: tasmad idam jagad asesam asat-svarupam svapnabham asta-dhisanam puru-duhkha-duhkham (Srimad-Bhagavatam, [10.14.22](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/14.22)), ‘Therefore, this whole universe has an unreal nature like a dream, is devoid of knowledge, and is replete with great distress.’ According to those who propound that the universe is real, how is this statement reconciled?” The author replies, “It is only for the sake of detachment (vairagyartham).” The meaning is that such statements are just for the sake of giving up desire for happiness in this transient universe, and not for establishing its falsity, since we find evidence to its reality. By quoting the Svetasvatara Upanisad, the author then gives evidence that Lord Visnu created (srstavan) through His Own energy (sva-saktya). The Supreme Lord (yah) is Himself devoid of material designations (avarnah), i.e., different from classes such as brahmana and ksatriya, yet by engaging His energy (sakti-yogat), He creates (dadhati) manifold varieties (anekan) of classes such as brahmana and so on.

According to the Visva-kosa (Na, 13), the word varna is used in the sense of “brahmana, ksatriya, etc.,” “colours, such as white,” “praise,” “form,” “fame,” and “letter.” Or else, He is Himself devoid of material form (avarnah). Having a particular purpose (nihitharthah) in mind, He creates varieties (anekan) of things in various colours (varnan), such as white and blue. The sum and substance of the verse from the Visnu Purdna is that this (idam) visible universe (jagat) is an effect of the energy (saktih) of the Supreme (parasya) Lord Hari (brahmanah), Who resides in the spiritual sky, although this universe is so far from there. By quoting the Isa Upanisad, the author gives evidences that the Lord is omniscient (sarva­vit) and the creation is factual (yathartham). The Supreme Soul (sah) under discussion has pervaded (agat) all directions (pari). The words starting with sukram are to be changed into the masculine gender, since the word in the beginning is sah. He is effulgent (sukrah), devoid of a subtle body (akayah), devoid of a gross body (asthavirah), undecaying (avranah), free from passion, aversion, etc. (suddhah), free from karma (apapa-viddhah), omniscient (kavih), wise (manisi), the subjugator of illusion, etc. (paribhuh), and causeless (svayambhuh). The word yathatathyatah means “truly.” According to the Halayudha-kosa (144), the words rta, satya, samidna, samyak, tathya, and yathatatha are all synonyms of “true.” The word arthdn means “the material elements, starting with the mahat-tattva." The word samah means “through years.” According to the Amara-kosa (1.3.20), the words samvatsara, vatsara, abda, hayana (both in the neuter and masculine genders), sarat, and sama are all synonyms of “year.”

### Text 2

*sri-visnu-purane ca— tad etad aksayam nityam jagan muni-varakhilam*  
*avirbhava-tirobhava- janma-nasa-vikalpavat, iti*

**Перевод:**

**It is further said in the Visnu Purana (**[**1.22.60**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vp/1.22.60)**): tad etad aksayam nityam jagan muni-varakhilam, avirbhava-tirobhava-janma-nasa- vikalpavat, “O best of sages, this whole universe is eternal and imperishable, and it has alternate appearances and disappearances, births and deaths.”**

**Комментарий:**

“O best of sages (muni-vara), this (etat) whole (akhilam) universe (jagat) consists of isvara (God), jiva (living entities), and prakrti (material nature).” Its form consisting of prakrti and the living entities is, by nature, imperishable (aksayam) in the sense that it is changeable. The changes in prakrti happen through its elements, starting with the mahat- tattva, and the changes in the living entities happen through the expansion of knowledge. Its form consisting of God is eternal, i.e., unchangeable. The same thing is said in the second line of the verse: The portion of the universe that consists of God appears (avirbhava) and disappears (tirobhava), while the portion that consists of the material nature and the living entities goes through birth (janma) and death (nasa). This has been explained according to the order of the contextual meaning, regardless of the order of the text. In the Visnu Purana, it had been previously stated: dve rupe brahmanas tasya murtam camurtam eva ca, ksaraksara-svarupe te sarva-bhutesv avasthite, aksaram tat param brahma ksaram sarvam idam jagat, “The Supreme has two forms— embodied and unembodied, which are situated in all beings, and whose nature is perishable and imperishable. The imperishable form is Parabrahma, and the perishable form is this whole universe.” After saying this, in the middle of the subsequent verses, the forms of Brahma, Visnu, and Siva are mentioned, after which the verse ‘tad etad aksayam’ is read.

### Text 3

*mahabharate ca— brahma satyam tapah satyam satyam caiva prajapatih*  
*satyad bhutani jatani satyam bhuta-mayam jagat, iti*

**Перевод:**

**It is also stated in the Maha-bharata (14.35.24): brahma satyam tapah satyam satyam caiva prajapatih, satyad bhutani jatani satyam bhuta-mayam jagat, “The Supreme Lord is real, His reflection is real, Prajapati is real, all beings are born from the real, and therefore the universe composed of material elements and living entities is real.”**

**Комментарий:**

The Supreme Lord (brahma), Who is eternity, consciousness, and bliss personified, and Whose desires are always fulfilled, is real (satyam). His austerity in the form of reflection (tapah) is real. Prajapati, who was generated by the Supreme Lord from His lotus-navel, is real. The living creatures (bhutani) are born (jatani) from the real (satyat). Therefore, this universe (jagat) composed of material elements and living entities (bhuta-mayam) is real.

### Text 4

*atma va idam ity-adau vana-lina-vihangavat*  
*sattvam visvasya mantavyam ity uktaru veda-vedibhih*

**Перевод:**

**In statements such as ‘atma va idam,’ the reality of the universe must be understood like the analogy of a bird that merged in the forest. This is declared by those who know the Vedas.**

*iti trtiyam prameyam*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the third object of knowledge.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “In the sruti, there are statements that before creation, there was only the Supreme Soul, and not the material manifestation. For instance: atma va idam eka evagra asit (Aitareya Upanisad, 1.1), ‘Before creation, the Supreme Soul alone was all this.’ This statement of identity of case-relation (samanadhikaranya) in the words atma and idam is like that of a rope and a snake, for the universe is a superimposition on the Supreme Soul. Therefore, the material manifestation is false. ” In response to this, the author writes the above verse. Just as a bird that merged in the forest is still there, the universe remains existing within the Supreme Soul in a subtle form. Otherwise, the effect would not exist in the cause (asat-karyata).

Thus, the section of the Prameya-ratnavali entitled “The Reality of the Universe” has been explained.

## Fourth Prameya

### Text 1

*atha visnuto jivanam bhedah\| tatha hi svetasvatarah pathanti— dva suparna sayuja sakhaya samanam vrksam parisasvajate\| tayor anyah pippalam svadv atty anasnann anyo’bhicakasiti\| samane vrkse puruso nimagno’nisaya socati muhyamanah\| justam yada pasyaty anyam isam asya mahimanam eti vita-sokah, iti*

**Перевод:**

**The difference between Lord Visnu and the living entities shall now be discussed. For instance, the Svetasvatara Upanisad (**[**4.6.7**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/4.6.7)**) declares: dva suparna sayuja sakhaya samanam vrksam parisasvajate, tayor anyah pippalam svadv atty anasnann anyo abhicakasiti, “Two birds are sitting together on the same tree as friends. One of them, the Jiva, with great taste eats the fruits of work, while the other one just shines without eating.” samane vrkse puruso nimagno’nisaya socati muhyamanah, justam yada pasyaty anyam isam asya mahimanam eti vita-sokah, “ Being attached to that same tree-like body and being bewildered by tnaya, the living entity suffers. When one meditates on the Supreme Lord, Who is different from oneself and endowed with auspicious qualities, then, becoming free from sorrow, one goes to His abode.”**

**Комментарий:**

In order to explain the difference between God and the living entities, the author quotes the Svetdsvatara Upanisad. The words dva suparnd sayujd sakhaya have the case ending a according to the following aphorism: supam su-luk-purva-savarnac-che-ya-da-dya-yaj- alah (Astadhyayi, 7.1.39), “In the Vedas, the regular case endings are replaced by the following ones: su, the Ink elision, a homogeneous vowel (purva-savarna), a, at, se, yd, da, dya, yac, and dl.” Two (dva) birds (suparna) in the form of God and the individual soul are sitting (parisasvajate) on the same (samanam) banyan tree (vrksam) in the form of the body, having taken possession of it. It should be understood that the living entity is situated there for enjoyment, and God for controlling. What are they both like? They are companions (sayuja), like friends (sakhaya). Out of the two (tayoh), one (anyah), the living entity, with great taste (svadu) eats (atti) the fruits (pippalam) of his work in the form of happiness and distress. The other one (anyah), God, simply shines (abhicakasiti), although not eating. The jiva (purusa) is attached (nimagnah) to that same (samane) tree-like (vrkse) body, and being bewildered (muhyamanah) by may a (anisaya), it suffers (socati). When (yada) one meditates (pasyati) on God (isam), Who is different (bhinnam) from oneself and Who is endowed (justam) with a multitude of His Own auspicious qualities, one then becomes free from sorrow (vita-sokah) and goes (eti) to His (asya) abode (mahimanam).

### Text 2

*upakramopasamharav abhyaso’purvata phalam*  
*arthavadopapatti ca lingam tatparya-nirnaye*  
*iti tatparya-lingani sad yany ahur manisinah*  
*bhede tani pratiyante tenasau tasya gocarah*

**Перевод:**

**It is said: upakramopasamharav abhyaso’purvata phalam, arthavadopapatti ca lingam tatparya-nirnaye, “The beginning (upakrama) and the end (upasamhara), repetition (abhyasa), singularity (apurvata), result (phala), praise (arthavada), and reasoning (upapatti)— these are the indications to ascertain the purport of a text.” The learned say that these six kinds of indications to the purport of the scriptures are understood in regard to difference. Therefore, difference is the subject matter of the scriptures.**

**Комментарий:**

In order to demonstrate that difference is the purport of the scriptures, here the author quotes the Brhat-samhita. The uniformity of the beginning (upakrama) and the end (upasamhara) comprise one indication (lingam). The aforementioned text from the Svetasvatara Upanisad begins (upakrama) with the words dva suparna (two birds) and ends (upasamhara) with the words anyam isam (God, Who is different). Repetition of the same words (abhyasah) is seen in the statements that there are two (dva) birds; one (anyah) out of the two is eating, and the other one (anyah) is not eating (anasnan). The singularity (apurvata) is that without the scriptures, this difference as counterparts distinguished by opposite and eternal attributes such as atomic size and all­pervasiveness would not be understood from other people. Becoming free from sorrow (vita-sokah) is the result (phalam). To go (eti) to the Lord’s (asya) abode (mahimanam) is the praise (arthavadah). The statement that one eats (atti) and the other does not eat (anasnan) is the reasoning (upapattih). Thus, the purport of the scriptures (tasya) has difference (asau) as their subject matter (gocarah).

### Text 3

*kim ca mundake— yada pasyah pasyate rukma-varnam kartaram isam purusam brahma-yonim\| tada vidvan punya-pape vidhuya niranjanah paramam samyam upaiti, iti\| kathake ca— yathodakam suddhe suddham asiktam tadrg eva bhavati\| evam muner vijanata atma bhavati gautama, iti\| sri-gitasu ca— idam jnanam apasritya mama sadharmyam agatah\| sarge’pi nopajayante pralaye na vyathanti ca, iti\|*  
*esu mokse’pi bhedokteh syad bhedah paramarthikah*

**Перевод:**

**Furthermore, in the Mundaka Upanisad (**[**3.1.3**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/mundaka/3.1.3)**) it is stated: yada pasyah pasyate rukma-varnam kartaram isam purusaih brahma-yonim, tada vidvan punya-pape vidhuya niranjanah paramam samyam upaiti, “When the meditator sees the Supreme Person of golden complexion, the Lord and creator, the source of the brahma-jyoti, then shaking off pious and sinful reactions, this wise seer, whose heart is pure, attains transcendental similarity with the Supreme Lord.” It is also said in the Katha Upanisad (**[**2.1.15**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/katha-bs/2.1.15)**): yathodakam suddhe suddham asiktam tadrg eva bhavati, evam muner vijanata atma bhavati gautama, “Just as pure water poured into pure water becomes alike to it, similarly the soul of a wise person who realises Brahman becomes alike to Brahman, О Gautama.” It is also confirmed in the Bhagavad-gita (**[**14.2**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/14.2)**): idam jnanam apasritya mama sadharmyam agatah, sarge’pi nopajayante pralaye na vyathanti ca: “Having attained this knowledge, the living entities attain a nature similar to Mine and thus they are neither born at the time of creation, nor afflicted at the time of dissolution.” Since in these sentences difference is mentioned even in the state of liberation, difference is absolutely real.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “These indications are not exclusively meant to prove difference, for they are also shown to prove non-difference. For instance: brahma-vid brahmaiva bhavati (Mundaka Upanisad, [3.2.9](https://vedarama.com/en/books/mundaka/3.2.9)), ‘One who knows Brahman becomes Brahman.’ brahmaiva san brahmapy eti (Brhad-aranyaka Upanisad, [4.4.6](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bau/4.4.6)), ‘Being Brahman, one attains Brahman.’ In these sentences, non-difference in the state of liberation is ascertained. Therefore, difference must pertain to the empirical state of existence (vyavaharika). In response to this, the author quotes the above texts. In the text from the Mundaka Upanisad, the word pasyah. means “the living entity who meditates.” In the text from the Katha Upanisad, vijanatah means “of one who realises that.” In the verse from the Bhagavad-glta, the word apasritya means “ having attained. ” Since in these three verses (esu) difference (bheda) even (api) in the state of liberation (mokse) is mentioned (ukteh) by the words samyam, tadrg eva, and sadharmyam, difference is real (paramarthikah). Likewise, the text ‘brahmaiva san’ just means that one becomes similar to Brahman. According to the Visva-kosa (Avyaya, 63), the word eva is used in the sense of similarity and restriction.

### Text 4

*brahmaham eko jivo’smi nanye jiva na cesvarah*  
*mad-avidya-kalpitas te syur itittham ca dusitam*  
*anyatha nitya ity-adi sruty-artho nopapadyate\|*  
*tatha hi kathah pathanti— nityo nityanam cetanas cetananam eko bahunam yo vidadhati kaman\| tarn atma-stham ye’nupasyanti dhiras tesam santih sasvati netaresam, iti*

**Перевод:**

**The following view is disproved in the same way: “ I am Brahman. I am the only living entity. God and other living entities do not exist. They are imagined due to my ignorance. ” Otherwise, the meaning of scriptural statements such as ‘nityo nityanam’ would be unsubstantiated. For instance, the Katha Upanisad (**[**2.2.13**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/katha-bs/2.2.13)**) declares: nityo nityanam cetanas cetananam eko bahunam yo vidadhati kaman, “There is one eternal being among many eternal beings, one sentient being among many sentient beings, Who fulfils their desires. The wise who meditate on Him situated within the heart attain everlasting cessation of material existence, and not others.”**

**Комментарий:**

Based on the apparent meaning of scriptural statements such as ‘sa eva maya-parimohitatma sariram asthaya karoti sarvam,’ some followers of Sankaracarya fancy as follows: “There is actually only one jiva, which is Brahman itself bewildered by avidya. I am that. Other jivas besides me are just imagined due to my ignorance. They and the person called the Supreme God are reflections of consciousness and are all like things seen in a dream, such as chariots and horses. Therefore, God and other jivas do not exist for me, who am steady as mere consciousness and have realised the self, just as dream-chariots and horses do not exist for a person who is awake. Thus, there is only one real jiva.” The author refutes this view in the present paragraph. The word ittham (in this way) means “by propounding difference even in the state of liberation.” The word anyatha (otherwise) means “if difference is not accepted as real.” The author then quotes the Katha Upanisad as an example. The compound atma-stham means “situated within the heart.”

### Text 5

*ekasmad isvaran nityac cetanat tadrsa mithah*  
*bhidyante bahavo jivas tena bhedah sanatanah*

**Перевод:**

**Such numerous living entities are mutually distinct from the unique, eternal, and conscious Supreme Lord. Therefore, difference is eternal.**

**Комментарий:**

Here the author gives the import of the sruti text quoted above. The Supreme Lord is one (ekah), eternal (nityah), and conscious (cetanah). He fulfils (vidadhati) the desires (kaman) of the numerous (bahunam) conscious (cetananam) and eternal (nityanam) living entities in conformity to their means (sadhana) adopted by them. The wise (dhirah) who meditate (anupasyanti) on Him attain everlasting (sasvati) cessation (santih) of the pains of repeated birth and death. The learned certainly cannot say that the eternal and conscious living entities are imagined due to avidya. Therefore, this sentence of the sruti is an axe on the throat of the theory that there is only one j iva. The word tadrsah means “such eternal and conscious living entities.” The word tena means “by propounding difference between the eternal and conscious Supreme Lord and the eternal and conscious jivas.”

### Text 6

*pranaikadhina-vrttitvad vag-adeh pranata yatha*  
*atha brahmadhina-vrtter jagato brahmatocyatej*  
*tatha hi chandogye pathyate— na vai vaco na caksumsi na srotrani na manaihsity acaksate) prana ity acaksate prano hy evaitani sarvani bhavati, iti*

**Перевод:**

**Just as the sense organs, starting with speech, are called prana (life air), since their functions and sustenance are exclusively dependent on prana, similarly this universe is said to be Brahman, for the universal activities and sustenance are dependent on Brahman. For instance, in the Chandogya Upanisad (**[**5.1.15**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/chan-upa/part-5.chapter-1.5115)**) it is mentioned: na vai vaco na caksumsi na srotrani na manaihsity acaksate, prana ity acaksate prano hy evaitani sarvani bhavati, “The wise do not call the senses as speech, eyes, ears, or mind. They call them prana. Prana actually becomes all the senses. ”**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “In this way, what would be the meaning of sentences such as ‘sarvam khalv idam brahma’ and ‘tat tvam asi?’” In response to this, the author speaks the above words. The sense organs, such as speech (vak), are not designated by words such as vak, but rather by the word prana, for their functions and sustenance are dependent on prana, and thus they are also a form of prana. Similarly, this material manifestation, consisting of sentient and insentient beings, is designated by the word brahman, since the activities and sustenance of the universe are dependent on Brahman, and thus it is also a form of Brahman. This is the meaning.

### Text 7

*brahma-vyapyatvatah kaiscij jagad brahmeti manyate*  
*yad uktam sri-visnu-purane— yo’yam tavagato deva samipam devata- ganah\| sa tvam eva jagat-srasta yatah sarva-gato bhavan, iti*

**Перевод:**

**Some consider the universe as Brahman because it is pervaded by Brahman. As stated in the Visnu Purana (**[**1.9.69**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/vp/1.9.69)**): yo’yam tavagato deva samipam devata-ganah, sa tvam eva jagat-srasta yatah sarva-gato bhavan, “O Lord, this host of demigods that has come before You is You Yourself, for You are omnipresent, the creator of the universe.”**

**Комментарий:**

The scriptural statements about non-duality should be reconciled by another conventional principle as well— whatever is pervaded is a form of the pervader. This is what the author says here. The verse from the Visnu Purana was spoken by the demigods to Lord Visnu. Its meaning is clear. In a similar way, in the text ‘sa eva maya- parimohitatma,’ the non-difference between the jiva and the Supreme Soul should be understood according to the explanation that the jiva is pervaded by the Supreme Soul, and its activities and sustenance are dependent on Him.

### Text 8

*pratibimba-pariccheda-paksau yau svikrtau paraih*  
*vibhutvavisayatvabhyam tau vidvadbhir nirakrtau*

**Перевод:**

**There are others who have accepted the views that the jiva is either a fragment or a reflection of Brahman. Both views are refuted by the wise on the principle that Brahman is all-pervading and beyond perception.**

**Комментарий:**

The monists argue that upon being reflected on a limiting adjunct (upadhi), or upon being divided by a limiting adjunct, Brahman itself appears in the form of the jiva, and that once the upadhi disappears, there is Brahman alone. In order to refute this theory, the author speaks the above verse. Since Brahman is all-pervading and formless, it cannot have a reflection. As the monists do not accept that Brahman is fragmented or an object of perception, the jiva cannot be its fragment. If Brahman is factually fragmented, then it would undergo transformation like pieces of stone cut by a chisel, it would have a non-eternal nature, and so on.

### Text 9

*advaitam brahmano bhinnam abhinnam va tvayocyate*  
*adye dvaitapattir antye siddha-sadhanata sruteh*

**Перевод:**

**According to you, is the non-duality of the jiva and Brahman said to be different or non-different from Brahman? If the first proposition is valid, there would be duality. If the second proposition is valid, then the scriptures would be a futile means to something already accomplished.**

**Комментарий:**

Since this theory also does not bear scrutiny, non-dualism is not acceptable. This is what the author says here. Is the non-duality (advaitam) of the jiva and Brahman different (bhinnam) or non-different (abhinnam) from Brahman? The first proposition is not valid, for it would imply duality. The second proposition is also not valid, for by teaching non-duality, the scriptures (sruteh) would be a futile means to something already accomplished (siddha-sadhanata). If non-duality is Brahman itself, then this is an established fact (siddha). What is the use of teaching it?

### Text 10

*alikam nirgunam brahma pramanavisayatvatah*  
*sraddheyaih vidusaih naivety ucire tattva-vadinah*

**Перевод:**

**Those who know the truth say that the learned should not believe the false concept of a Brahman devoid of qualities, for this is beyond the scope of any evidence.**

*iti caturtham prameyam*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the fourth object of knowledge.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “According to the sruti, only Brahman devoid of qualities is real: saksi cetah kevalo nirgunas ca, ‘The Supreme is the witnessing pure consciousness without any material quality.’” The author responds to this here. Direct perception is certainly no evidence to prove a Brahman devoid of qualities, since Brahman has no colour, sound, touch, taste, or smell. Nor is inference evidence, for there is no sign invariably concomitant to Brahman. Nor is verbal testimony evidence, since no semantic causes apply to Brahman. Nor is a secondary meaning (laksana) evidence, for Brahman cannot be expressed by any word. Nor can Brahman be proved by bhaga-laksana, for this is not possible in the case of something that cannot be described by any word, as it has been previously explained.  
Thus, the section of the Prameya-ratnavali entitled “Difference is Real” has been explained.

## Fifth Prameya

### Text 1

*atha jivanam bhagavad-dasatvam\| tatha hi svetasvatarah pathanti— tam isvaranam paramam mahesvaram tam daivatanam paramam ca daivatam\| patim patinam paramam parastad vidama devam bhuvanesam idyam, iti*

**Перевод:**

**The living entities’ servitude to the Supreme Lord shall now be discussed. For instance, the Svetasvatara Upanisad (**[**6.7**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/6.7)**) declares: tam isvaranam paramam mahesvaram tam daivatanam paramam ca daivatam, patim patinam paramam parastad vidama devam bhuvanesam idyam, “May we know the Great Lord, the supreme of all lords, the Supreme Deity of the demigods, the Supreme Ruler above all Rulers, the Supreme God, the worshipable Master of the world.”**

**Комментарий:**

In order to explain the living entities’ servitude to Lord Hari, the author speaks the above words. Someone may argue, “If servitude to Lord Hari is something already established in the svarupa of the living entity, then what is the purpose of instruction on the matter?” You should understand that the purpose is to manifest that servitude. The sruti says so: ghrtam iva payasi nigudham bhute bhute vasati vijnanam, satatam manthayitavyam manasa manthana-dandena (Brahma-bindu Upanisad, 20), “Just as ghee is concealed in milk, knowledge is concealed in every living being and should be constantly churned by the mind as a churning rod.” In the text from the Svetasvatara Upanisad, the word isvaranam refers to lords such as Brahma; daivatanam refers to demigods such as Indra; and patindm refers to rulers such as Daksa.

### Text 2

*smrtis ca— brahma sambhus tathaivarkas candramas ca satakratuh\| evam adyas tathaivanye yukta vaisnava-tejasa, ity-adya\| sabrahmakah sarudras ca sendra deva maharsibhih\| arcayanti sura-srestham devam narayanam harim, ity-adya ca\| padme ca jiva-laksane— dasa-bhuto harer eva nanyasyaiva kadacana, iti*

**Перевод:**

**Smrti texts such as the following also confirm this: brahma sambhus tathaivarkas candramas ca satakratuh, evam adyas tathaivanye yukta vaisnava-tejasa, jagat-karyavasane tu viyujyante ca tejasa, vitejasas ca te sarve pancatvam upayanti ca (Visnu-dharmottara Purana, 1.171.5- 6), “Brahma, Siva, Surya, Candra, Indra, and other demigods are all endowed with the power of Lord Visnu. However, at the end of their duties in the universe, they become devoid of that power. Being powerless, all of them perish.” sabrahmakah sarudras ca sendra deva maharsibhih, arcayanti sura-srestham devam narayanam harim, bhavisyatam vartatam ca bhutanam caiva bharata, sarvesam agranir visnuh sevyah pujyas ca nityasah (Maha-bharata, 12.328.27-28), “The demigods accompanied by Brahma, Rudra, Indra, and the great sages worship Lord Narayana, also known as Hari, the best among divine beings. О descendant of Bharata, Lord Visnu is to be always served and worshipped before everyone else who has ever existed, who exists, and who will exist.” Regarding the individual soul’s characteristics, it is also said in the Padma Purana (Uttara-khanda, 226.37): dasa-bhuto harer eva nanyasyaiva kadacana, “The individual soul is a servant of Lord Hari only, and never of anyone else.”**

*iti pancamam prameyam*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the fifth object of knowledge.**

**Комментарий:**

The verses starting with ‘brahma sambhuh’ state that the power of Brahma, Rudra, and so on are given by the Supreme Soul. In the verse from the Padma Purana, the words папуasya mean “not of anyone else, such as Rudra.”  
Thus, the section of the Prameya-ratnavali entitled “The Jivas’ Servitude to Lord Hari” has been explained.

## Sixth Prameya

### Text 1

*atha jivanam taratamyam*  
*anu-caitanya-rupatva-jnanitvady avisesatah*  
*samye saty api jivanam taratamyam ca sadhanat*

**Перевод:**

**The gradations among living entities shall now be discussed. Although there is similarity among the jivas in the sense that they are universally endowed with an atomic and conscious form, are knowers, and so on, there is also gradation among them according to the means they adopt.**

**Комментарий:**

In order to explain the gradations among living entities, the author speaks the above words. By the word adi (etc.), attributes such as being the doer, being the enjoyer, and being free from sin should be included. The means (sadhanat) are those in the form of material activities (karma) and devotional service (bhakti). The gradation of the results should be understood as belonging to this world according to the gradation of material activities, and as belonging to the next world according to the gradation of devotional service.

### Text 2

*tatranutvam uktaih svetasvataraih— balagra-sata-bhagasya satadha kalpitasya ca\| bhago jivah sa vijneyah sa canantyaya kalpate, iti\| caitanya- rupatvam jnanitvadikam ca satprasnyam— esa hi drasta sprasta srota ghrata rasayita manta boddha karta vijnanatma purusah, iti*

**Перевод:**

**The living entities’ atomic size is described in the Svetasvatara Upanisad (**[**5.9**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/5.9)**): balagra-sata-bhagasya satadha kalpitasya ca, bhago jivah sa vijneyah sa canantyaya kalpate, “The individual soul is meant for liberation and should be understood as having the size conceived as the hundredth part of the tip of a hair again divided by one hundred.” Their attributes such as being conscious and being knowers are mentioned in the Prasna Upanisad (4.9): esa hi drasta sprasta srota ghrata rasayita manta boddha karta vijnanatma purusah, “The individual soul is consciousness itself, the one who sees, the one who touches, the one who hears, the one who smells, and the one who tastes. It is the thinker, the knower, and the doer.”**

**Комментарий:**

In the text from the Svetasvatara Upanisad, the words sajivah refer to the individual soul that, being surrendered unto the Supreme Lord, is fit (kalpate) for liberation (anantyaya). The word anta means death, and anantya means “deathlessness.” By the word adi (etc.) in the compound jnanitvadikam, being a doer and an enjoyer should be included. In the text from the Prasna Upanisad, the words vijnanatma purusah mean “the individual soul, which is consciousness itself.” By being described as the one who sees, hears, etc., it is clear that there are objects of enjoyment such as colour, sound, etc. Ifprakrti were the doer, the sruti texts that instruct one to worship and meditate on the Supreme Lord would be useless. Deliberation would also not exist. “I am different from prakrti”— such deliberation is not possible for prakrti, which is insentient. Nor is it possible for something to be different from itself.

### Text 3

*adina gunena deha-vyapitvam ca\| sri-gitasu— yatha prakasayaty ekah krtsnam lokam imam ravih\| ksetram ksetri tatha krtsnam prakasayati bharata, iti\| aha caivam sutra-karah— gunad valokavat, iti\| guna- nityatvam uktam vajasaneyibhih— avinasi va are ayam atmanucchitti- dharma, iti*

**Перевод:**

**By the word adi (etc.) it is also understood that the individual soul pervades the body by its quality. As stated in the Bhagavad-gita (**[**13.34**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bg/13.34)**): yatha prakasayaty ekah krtsnam lokam imam ravih, ksetram ksetri tatha krtsnam prakasayati bharata, “Just as a single sun illuminates this whole world, a single soul illuminates the whole body, О descendent of Bharata.” It is similarly said by Vyasadeva, the compiler of the Brahma-sutra (2.3.24): gunad valokavat, “Or because the quality of consciousness pervades the whole body, just like a lamp.” The eternity of the qualities of the individual soul is mentioned in texts of the Vajasaneya branch of the Vedas: avinasi va are ayam atma anucchitti- dharma (Brhad-aranyaka Upanisad,** [**4.5.14**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bau/4.5.14)**), “O Maitreyi, the individual soul is imperishable and has indestructible attributes.”**

**Комментарий:**

The meaning of the verse from the Bhagavad-gita is clear. The meaning of the aphorism from the Brahma-sutra is as follows: just as by the quality (gunat) named light, a lamp (alokah) and the like pervades the whole house, the individual soul pervades the whole body by the quality named consciousness. The meaning of the text from Bmad-aranyaka Upanisad is this: О (are) Maitreyi, this (ayam) individual soul (atma) is imperishable (avinasi) by nature. One who has indestructible (anucchitti) attributes (dharma), such as consciousness, is called anucchitti-dharma. That means the individual soul is imperishable by its qualities as well. This compound should be interpreted as “one whose quality (dharma) is indestructibility (anucchitti),” for this meaning is understood by the very statement that the soul is imperishable (avinasi).

### Text 4

*evam samye’pi vaisamyam aihikam karmabhih sphutam*  
*prahuh paratrikam tat tu bhakti-bhedaih sukovidah*  
*tatha hi kauthumah pathanti— yatha kratur asmiml loke puruso bhavati, tathetah pretya bhavati, iti\| smrtis ca— yadrsi bhavana yasya siddhir bhavati tadrsi, iti*

*santy-adya rati-paryanta ye bhavah panca kirtitah*  
*tair devam smaratam pumsam taratamyam mitho matam*

**Перевод:**

**Thus, those who are very learned say that despite the similarity among the jivas, there is a clear disparity in this world due to their material activities, and in the Supreme Lord’s abode due to the different kinds of devotional service. For instance, the texts of the Kauthuma branch of the Vedas declare: yatha kratur asmiml loke puruso bhavati, tathetah pretya bhavati (Chandogya Upanisad,** [**3.14.1**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/chan-upa/part-3.chapter-14.3141)**), “According to one’s spiritual practice in this world, so is the result after departing from here.” The smrti also corroborates: yadrsi bhavana yasya siddhir bhavati tadrsi (Skanda Purana, 5.3.227.20), “As one meditates, so is the result.” Neutrality, servitude, friendship, parental affection, and conjugal love— these are said to be the five emotions by which a mutual gradation is known among those who worship God.**

*iti sastham prameyam*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the sixth object of knowledge.**

**Комментарий:**

Thus (evam), after mentioning the similarity among the jivas based on their being atomic and so on, the author now explains the disparity due to the means adopted by them. The word aihikam means “belonging to this world,” and paratrikam means “belonging to the Supreme Lord’s abode. ” In the text from the Chandogya Upanisad it is said that according (yatha) to the spiritual practice (sadhanam) one performs in this (asmin) world (loke), so (tatha) is the result after departing (pretya) from this world (itah) to the next world. The meaning is that one attains a result corresponding to the kind of spiritual practice adopted. The meaning of the verse ‘yadrsi bhavana’ has just been spoken. The author then concludes with the words “santy-adyah, etc.” Neutrality (santi), servitude (dasya), friendship (sakhya), parental affection (vatsalya), and conjugal love (rati)— these (ye) are the five (panca) emotions (bhavah) by which (taih) there is a clear gradation (taratamyam) among those who worship (smaratam) God (devam).

As for those followers of Visvaksena who, on the basis of the sruti text ‘niranjanah paramam samyamupaiti,’ accept absolute equality among the jivas during the state of liberation, it is difficult for them to deny disparity even at that state, for they also accept that Goddess Laksmi is distinguished (sesi) for all jivas, and Visvaksena is their ruler.

Thus, the section of the Prameya-ratnavali entitled “The Gradations among Living Entities” has been explained.

## Seventh Prameya

### Text 1

*atha krsna-prapter moksatvam\| yatha— jnatva devaih sarva- pasapahanih, ity-adi\| eko vasi sarva-gah krsna idyah, ity-adi ca\|*

For instance: jnatva devaih sarva-pasapahanih ksinaih kiesair janma-mrtyu-prahanih, tasyabhidhyanat trtiyam deha-bhede visvaisvaryam kevalam apta-kamah (Svetasvatara Upanisad,** [**1.11.12**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/1.11.12)**), "Knowing God, all bonds vanish, and when all distress is thus destroyed, one’s repeated birth and death also vanish. Having worshipped Him, those who desire liberation dwell happily in His eternal abode.**

**Комментарий:**

In order to explain that liberation is the attainment of Lord Krsna, the author quotes the Svetasvatara Upanisad. The meaning of the text has been previously explained. Just as those who worship Lord Krsna attain liberation, so do those who worship Lord Rama and other forms of Visnu, but the gradation of happiness among them is unavoidable.

*iti prameyaratnavalyam krsna-prapter moksatva-prakaranamvyakhyatam*

**Перевод:**

**Thus, the section of the Prameya-ratnavali entitled “Liberation is the Attainment of Krsna” has been explained.**

## Eighth Prameya

### Text 1

*athaikanta-bhakter moksa-hetutvam\| yatha sri-gopalatapanyam— bhaktir asya bhajanam tad ihamutropadhi-nairasyenamusmin manah- kalpanam etad eva naiskarmyam, iti\| narada-pancaratre ca— sarvopadhi-vinirmuktam tatparatvena nirmalam\| hrsikena hrsikesa- sevanam bhaktir ucyate, iti*

**Перевод:**

**Devotional service as a cause of liberation now shall be discussed. As stated in the Gopala-tapani Upanisad (**[**1.15**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/gopala-tapani/1.15)**): bhaktir asya bhajanam tad ihamutropadhi-nairasyenamusmin manah-kalpanam etad eva naiskarmyam, “Bhakti is the worship of the Supreme Lord, rejecting all material designations in this world and the next. It is attaching the mind to Him. This alone is freedom from karma. ” It is also said in the Narada- pancaratra: sarvopadhi-vinirmuktam tatparatvena nirmalam, hrsikena hrsikesa-sevanam bhaktir ucyate, “Utilising the senses in Lord Hrsikesa’s pure service, free from all material designations and with full devotion unto Him, is called bhakti”**

**Комментарий:**

In order to explain that selfless devotional service causes liberation, the author speaks the above words. In the text from the Gopala- tapani Upanisad, it is said that Lord Krsna’s (asya) worship (bhaktih) is performed in a favourable way by hearing, chanting, and so forth. The mind (manah) should also be attached (kalpanam) to Lord Krsna (amusmin). The etymological explanation of the word kalpanam is “that by which the mind becomes absorbed.” This refers to the stage caused by chanting, hearing, and so on. In order to demonstrate the superiority of pure devotional service, it is stated that in this world (iha) and in the next (amutra) bhakti is performed with the rejection (nairasyena) of all material designations (upadhi). The meaning is that such devotional service is performed with the sole desire for Krsna and is devoid of desires for any result other than Krsna. This itself is freedom from all karma (naiskarmyam), which consequently causes liberation.

The meaning of the verse from the Narada-pancaratra is as follows: “Freedom from all material designations” (sarvopadhi- vinirmuktam) means “freedom from any desire other than Lord Krsna.” “Pure (nirmalam) devotional service” is that which is not contaminated by karma, yoga, jnana, and so on. “With full devotion” (tatparatvena) means “especially favourable.” Service (sevanam) to Lord Krsna (Hrsikesa) performed with the sense-organs, such as ears, eyes, and so forth, means full dedication with body, mind, and words. The superexcellence of devotional service is clear here.

### Text 2

*navadha caisa bhavati \| yad uktaih sri-bhagavate— sravanam kirtanam visnoh smaranam pada-sevanam\| arcanam vandanam dasyam sakhyam atma-nivedanam\| iti pumsarpita visnau bhaktis cen nava-laksana\| kriyate bhagavaty addha tan manye’dhitam uttamam, iti\|*

*sat-seva guru-seva ca deva-bhavena ced bhavet*  
*tadaisa bhagavad-bhaktir labhyate nanyatha kvacit*

**Перевод:**

**Devotional service is ninefold. As stated in the Srimad- Bhagavatam (**[**7.5.23-24**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-7/5.23)**): sravanam kirtanam visnoh smaranam pada- sevanam, arcanam vandanam dasyam sakhyam atma-nivedanam, iti pumsarpita visnau bhaktis cen nava-laksana, kriyate bhagavaty addha tan manye’dhitam uttamam, “Hearing and chanting about Lord Visnu, remembering Him, serving His feet, worshipping Him, praising Him, servitude to Him, friendship with Him, and offering oneself to Him— if these nine kinds of devotional service are directly offered unto the Supreme Lord Visnu while being performed by a devotee, I consider such devotional service the best thing I have studied.” If one serves the saintly persons and the spiritual master considering them like God, then such devotional service unto the Supreme Lord is obtained, and never otherwise.**

**Комментарий:**

Here the author speaks about the various kinds of spiritual practice. These nine kinds (nava-laksana) of devotional service (bhaktih) are offered (arpita) while being performed (kriyate) by a devotee (pumsa), and not that they are offered after being performed. “Among these, if (cet) service is performed directly (addha) unto Lord Visnu, and not indirectly with a desire for other results, I (aham) consider (manye) this the best devotional service, which is the best (uttamam) thing I have studied (adhitam).” The author then tells the reason by which devotional service is attained: “sat-seva, etc.”

### Text 3

*deva-bhavena sat-seva yatha taittiriyake— atithi-devo bhava, iti\| taya tad-bhaktir yatha sri-bhagavate— naisam matis tavad urukramanghruh sprsaty anarthapagamo yad-arthah\| mahiyasam pada-rajo-’bhisekam niskincananam na vrnita yavat, iti*

**Перевод:**

**One should serve saintly persons as if they were God, as stated in the Taittiriya Upanisad (**[**1.11.2**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/taittiriya/1.11.2)**): atithi-devo bhava, “You should serve devotees like God.” By such service, one attains devotional service to the Supreme Lord, as declared in the Srimad-Bhagavatam (**[**7.5.32**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-7/5.32)**): naisam matis tavad urukramanghrim sprsaty anarthapagamo yad-arthah, mahiyasam pada-rajo- ’bhisekaih niskincananam na vrnita yavat, “For as long as one does not beg to smear upon oneself the dust of the feet of the great devotees whose only wealth is Krsna, the mind of those of superficial vision does not touch the feet of Lord Visnu. The result for one whose mind touches His feet is the annihilation of repeated birth and death.”**

**Комментарий:**

The word atithi refers to a homeless devotee of Lord Hari, and atithi-devah means “he for whom such a devotee is worshipable like Lord Hari (devah).” “You should become so”— this is the instruction here. The verse from the Srimad-Bhagavatam was spoken by Prahlada. The mind (matih) of those (esam) of materialistic vision does not touch the feet (anghrim) of Lord Visnu (urukrama). The result (arthah) for one whose mind touches His feet is the annihilation (apagamah) of repeated birth and death (anartha). For how long do they remain as such ? For as long (yavat) as one does not beg for smearing the dust of the feet of the great devotees (mahiyasam) whose only wealth is Krsna (niskincananam). That means for as long as one does not fully serve them.

### Text 4

*deva-bhavena guru-seva yatha taittiriyake— acarya-devo bhava, iti\| svetasvataropanisadi ca— yasya deve para bhaktir yatha deve tatha gurau\| tasyaite kathita hy arthah prakasante mahatmanah, iti\| taya tad- bhaktir yatha sri-bhagavate— tasmad gurum prapadyeta jijnasuh sreya uttamam \| sabde pare ca nisnatam brahmany upasamasrayam\| tatra bhagavatan dharman siksed gurv-atma-daivatah\| amayayanuvrttya yais tusyed atmatma-do harih, iti*

**Перевод:**

**One should serve the spiritual master like God, as stated in the Taittiriya Upanisad (**[**1.11.2**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/taittiriya/1.11.2)**): acarya-devo bhava, “You should serve the spiritual master like God.” It is also confirmed in the Svetasvatara Upanisad (**[**6.23**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/6.23)**): yasya deve para bhaktir yatha deve tatha gurau, tasyaite kathita hy arthah prakasante mahatmanah, “The meaning of what is spoken in the scriptures becomes revealed to a great soul who has utmost devotion unto the Supreme Lord as well as the spiritual master.” By such service, one attains devotional service to the Supreme Lord, as declared in the Srimad-Bhagavatam (**[**11.3.21-22**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-11/3.21)**): tasmad gurum prapadyeta jijnasuh sreya uttamam, sabde pare ca nisnatam brahmany upasamasrayam, tatra bhagavatan dharman siksed gurv-atma-daivatah, amayayanuvrttya yais tusyed atmatma-do harih, “Therefore, one who is inquisitive to know the highest good should surrender unto a spiritual master who is conversant with the Vedic scriptures and the Supreme Lord, and whose mind and senses are under control. One should accept the spiritual master as one’s very soul and deity, and by serving him without deceit, one should learn from him the various forms of devotional service unto the Supreme Lord, by which Lord Hari, Who gives Himself to His devotees, becomes pleased.”**

**Комментарий:**

The word acarya refers to a spiritual master who gives a mantra, and acarya-devah means “he for whom such a spiritual master is worshipable like Lord Hari (devah).” “You should become so”— this is the instruction here. The meaning of the text from the Svetasvatara Upanisad is as follows: the meaning (arthah) of what is spoken (kathitah) in this Upanisad becomes revealed (prakasante) to an inquisitive person (yasya) who has utmost (para) devotion (bhaktih) unto the Supreme Lord (deve) as well as the spiritual master (gurau), and not to anyone different from this. According to the verses from the Srimad-Bhagavatam, one who is inquisitive (jijnasuh) to know the highest (uttamam) good (sreyah) should surrender (prapadyeta) unto a spiritual master (gurum). What should the spiritual master be like? He should be conversant (nisnatam) with the Vedic scriptures (sabde) and the Supreme Lord Krsna (pare brahmani). Being situated in the proximity (tatra) of such a guru and by serving (anuvrttya) him without deceit (amayaya), one should learn (sikset) from him the various forms of devotional service (dharman) unto the Supreme Lord (bhagavatan). The meaning of the rest is clear.

### Text 5

*avapta-panca-samskaro labdha-dvi-vidha-bhaktikah*  
*saksatkrtya harim tasya dhamni nityam pramodate*

**Перевод:**

**A person who has undergone the five purificatory ceremonies and has attained devotion— which is twofold— realises Lord Hari and is happy forever in His abode.**

**Комментарий:**

In order to explain other divisions of devotional service, the author speaks the above words. The compound labdha-dvi-vidha- bhaktikah means “he by whom devotion (bhakti), which is twofold (dvi- vidha)— that accompanied by rules and regulations (vidhi), and that accompanied by spontaneous love (ruci)— has been attained (labdha). ” Someone may argue, “It is contradictory that one attains both kinds of devotion.” This is true. One attains the same sort of devotion as the preceptor with whom he has good association. Thus, there is no contradiction.

### Text 6

*tatra panca samskara yatha smrtau— tapah pundram tatha nama mantro yagas ca pancamah\| ami hi panca samskarah paramaikanti-hetavah, iti*  
*tapo’tra tapta-cakradi-mudra-dharanam ucyate*  
*tenaiva hari-namadi mudra capy upalaksyate*  
*sa yatha smrtau— hari-namaksarair gatram ankayec candanadina\| sa loka-pavano bhutva tasya lokam avapnuyat, iti\|*

*pundram syad urdhva-pundram tac chastre bahu-vidham smrtam*  
*hari-mandira-tat-padakrty-ady atisubhavaham*  
*namatra gaditam sadbhir hari-bhrtyatva-bodhakam*  
*mantro’stadasa-varnadih svesta-deva-vapur matah*  
*salagramadi-puja tu yaga-sabdena kathyate*  
*pramanany esu drsyani puranadisu sadhubhih*

**Перевод:**

**The five purificatory ceremonies are mentioned in the stnrti in the following words: tapah pundram tatha nama mantro yagas ca pancamah, ami hi panca samskarah paramaikanti-hetavah (Bharadvaja- samhita, parisista, 2.2), “Tapa,pundra, nama, mantra, and yaga— these five purificatory ceremonies are causes of exclusive devotion for the Supreme Lord.” In this verse, branding the body with marks in the form of a cakra and so on is called tapa. By this, it is also implied marking the body with Lord Hari’s names and so forth. Such marks are described in the smrti in this way: hari-namaksarair gatram ankayec candanadina, sa loka-pavano bhutva tasya lokam avapnuyat, “One should mark the body with the syllables of Lord Hari’s names by using sandalwood and so on. Thus, becoming the purifier of the world, one attains Lord Hari’s abode.” Pundra means the upright lines on the forehead, which are described in the scriptures as being manifold, such as Hari-mandira and Hari-pada, all of which bring great auspiciousness. According to the saintly devotees, nama is said to be the name that designates someone as a servant of Lord Hari. Mantra, such as the eighteen syllable mantra, is considered to be a form of one’s worshipable deity. The worship of salagrama and so on is expressed by the word yaga. Devotees should look for evidences of these five items in the various Puranas and other texts.**

**Комментарий:**

Tapa, pundra, nama, mantra, and yaga— these (ami) are the five (panca) purificatory ceremonies (samskarah). The author then explains them. The words tenaiva mean “by the use of heated branding in the form of a cakra and so on.” Some fallen souls think that wearing a heated brand of a cakra and so on is too difficult for those whose minds have been polluted by the Age of Kali. Desiring to deliver them, Sri Krsna Caitanya, the Supreme Lord, instructed the use of marks (mudra) consisting of the Lord’s names and made with sandalwood and so forth, which has also been accepted by previous acaryas. The meaning is that the use of such marks is implied (upalaksyate) in the verse on the five samskaras by the statement about the use of heated branding in the form of a cakra, etc. The word pundram refers to the various kinds of tilaka, such as the Hari-mandira. According to the Halayudha-kosa (541), the words tilaka, tamalapatra, citraka, visesaka, andpundra are all used in the sense of “a mark in the forehead.” The meaning of the rest of the paragraph is clear.

*navadha bhaktir vidhi-ruci-purva dvedha bhaved yaya krsnah*  
*bhutva svayam prasanno dadati tat-tad-ipsitam dhama*

**This ninefold devotional service is of two kinds— that accompanied by rules and regulations, and that accompanied by spontaneous love. Becoming pleased by devotion, Lord Krsna Himself bestows the abode desired by a particular devotee.**

Kantimala: Here the author clarifies the two kinds of devotional service that have been previously mentioned. Devotional service accompanied by rules and regulations (vidhi) is called vaidhi, and devotional service accompanied by spontaneous love (ruci) is called raganuga. Details about this are found in the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu. The meaning of the rest of the above verse is clear.

### Text 8

*vidhinabhyarcyate devas catur-bahv-adi-rupa-dhrt*  
*rucy-atmakena tenasau nr-lingah paripujyate*

**Перевод:**

**The Supreme Lord in His forms as four-handed and so on is worshipped by vaidhi-bhakti, and in His human-like form, He is worshipped by raganuga-bhakti.**

**Комментарий:**

Here the author explains the different objects of worship in the different modes of devotional service. The four-handed forms (catur- bahuh) are those of Vasudeva, the Lord of the spiritual sky, and Aniruddha, the Lord of Svetadvipa. By the word adi (etc.), forms with eight and ten arms are also understood. As stated in the smrti: catur-bhujah syamalangah sri-bhu-lilabhir anvitah, vimalair bhusanair nityair bhusito nitya-vigrahaih, pancayudhaih sevyamanah sahkha-cakra-dharo harih (Visvaksena-samhita), “The four-handed Lord has a dark blue body and is accompanied by Sri Devi and Bhu Devi. This topic is discussed in detail in the Vedanta-syamantaka.

### Text 9

*tulasy-asvattha-dhatry-adi-pujanam dhama-nisthata*

### Text 10

*loka-sangraham anvicchan nitya-naimittikam budhah*  
*pratisthitas caret karma bhakti-pradhanyam atyajan*  
*dasa-namaparadhams tu yatnatah parivarjayet*

**Перевод:**

**Desiring the welfare of people, a wise person situated in an asrama should perform regular and occasional duties, without giving up the predominance of devotional service. One should diligently avoid the ten offences against the Holy Name.**

**Комментарий:**

Sanistha, parinisthita, and nirapeksa— these are the three kinds of persons entitled to perform devotional service. Among them, the sanistha is situated in a particular asrama and should perform his prescribed duties without any violence and without personal desire until they produce results. The nirapeksa is already attached to Lord Hari and should therefore just worship Lord Hari mentally. Such a person does not belong to any asrama and naturally gives up material activities. The parinisthita is one who, in case he is highly situated (pratisthitah) in a particular asrama, should perform his prescribed duties for the benefit of people (loka-sahgraham) in a secondary time, but should perform devotional service as the real aim. This topic is discussed in detail in the Govinda-bhasya with Suksma-tika (3.4.1), and in the Gita-bhusanam as well. It is also similarly discussed at length in the Bhakti-sandarbha.

The offences in the service of Lord Hari, such as going inside His temple while riding on a vehicle or wearing shoes, are mentioned in the Vardha Purana and other texts. They should be absolved by performing devotional service, which is easy to accomplish. Thus, they should be definitely avoided. The ten offences against the Holy Name, which are pointed out in the Padma Purana (4.25.15-18), should be absolved by constantly repeating the Holy Name. These ten offences should be diligently avoided, since such a constant repetition of the Holy Name is not easy to accomplish. This is what the author says at the end of this paragraph. These offences are as follows: 1) to find fault with saintly persons; 2) to consider the names of Siva, Brahma, and so on as independent from the names of Lord Visnu; 3) to disrespect the spiritual master; 4) to deride scriptures that follow the sruti; 5) to consider the glories of the names of Lord Hari as mere exaggerated praise; 6) to imagine a different meaning of the Holy Names; 7) to engage in sinful activities on the strength of the Holy Names; 8) to consider the Holy Names equal to other auspicious ritualistic activities; 9) to instruct a faithless and indifferent person about the Holy Names; 10) to lack attachment for the Holy Names, despite hearing about their glories. These offences taught by Sanat Kumara to Narada should be understood.

*jnana-vairagya-purva sa phalam sadyah prakasayet*

**Перевод:**

**Devotional service is said to be exclusive (ekanta) when its result is the attainment of Lord Krsna. If such devotion is accompanied by scriptural knowledge and detachment, it quickly manifests the result.**

*ity astamam prameyam*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the eighth object of knowledge.**

**Комментарий:**

Here, the author concludes the present topic. Devotional service that has any fruit other than Krsna as its result is not exclusive (ekanta). If such devotional service (sa) is accompanied (purva) by scriptural knowledge (jnana) and detachment (vairagya), it quickly (sadyah) manifests (prakasayet) the result (phalam) in the form of the attainment of Krsna. Otherwise, the result is delayed. This is confirmed by the smrti, such as: tac-chraddadhana munayo jnana-vairagya-yuktaya, pasyanty atmani catmanam bhaktya sruta-gmitaya (Srimad-Bhagavatam, [1.2.12](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-1/2.12)), “Being endowed with scriptural knowledge and detachment, the sages who have faith in the Supreme Soul see Him within themselves by means of devotional service learnt from the scriptures.” The word jnanam refers to scriptural knowledge.

Thus, the section of the Prameya-ratnavali entitled “Pure Devotional Service yields Liberation” has been explained.

## Ninth Prameya

### Text 1

*atha pratyaksanumana-sabdanam eva pramanatvam*  
*yatha sri- bhagavate— srutih pratyaksam aitihyam anumanam catustayam iti*

**Перевод:**

**The authoritativeness of only direct perception, inference, and verbal testimony shall now be discussed. For instance, it is stated in the Srimad-Bhagavatam (**[**11.19.17**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-11/19.17)**): Srutih pratyaksam aitihyam anumanam catustayam, “The Vedas, direct perception, tradition, and inference— these are the four kinds of evidence.”**

**Комментарий:**

In order to explain that there are only three kinds of evidence, the author speaks the above words. The three kinds of evidence are considered an object of knowledge here. By the word eva (only), it is meant that other kinds of evidence, such as comparison, are not additional to these three, since they are included within them. This topic should be examined in the first ray of the Vedanta-syamantaka, entitled “Ascertaining the Evidences.” Intending to demonstrate the superiority of the Vedas, the Srimad-Bhagavatam mentions them first of all.

### Text 2

*pratyakse’ntarbhaved yasmad aitihyam tena desikah*  
*pramanam tri-vidham prakhyat tatra mukhya srutir bhavet*

**Перевод:**

**As tradition is included in direct perception, Sri Madhvacarya has declared that there are only three kinds of evidence, among which the Vedas is the foremost.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “Tradition is mentioned as an additional evidence, so how can you say that there are only three kinds of evidence?” In response to this, the author speaks the above words. Aitihya is something that is well known from tradition coming down from unidentified narrators. For instance, when it is said, “There is a Yaksa living in this Banyan tree.” Such a tradition is included in direct perception, for it is based on the experience of the first person who saw the Yaksa there. Therefore, there are only three kinds of evidence. The word desikah (spiritual preceptor) refers to the sage Madhva. The Manu- samhita (12.105) also declares: pratyaksam canumanam ca sastram ca vividhagamam, trayam suviditam karyam dharma-suddhim abhipsata, “One who desires to attain a clear understanding of dharma should be well conversant with these three— direct perception, inference, and the various sacred scriptures.” Among the three kinds of evidence, the foremost is the sruti, which consists of a collection of supramundane sentences, for it is the authority on spiritual knowledge.

### Text 3

*pratyaksam anumanam ca yat-sacivyena suddhimat*  
*maya-mundavalokadau pratyaksam vyabhicari yat*  
*anuma catidhume’drau vrsti-nirvapitagnike*  
*atah pramanam tat tac ca svatantram naiva sammatam*

**Перевод:**

**It is by the assistance of verbal testimony that direct perception and inference produce valid knowledge, for sometimes direct perception fails, such as when one sees an illusory head and thinks, “This is the head of such-and-such.” Inference is also seen to fail, such as when it is assumed that on account of smoke, there is fire on a hill where a lot of smoke has been rising, but where the fire has been put out by the rain at that moment. Therefore, direct perception and inference are not considered independent kinds of evidence.**

**Комментарий:**

In order to demonstrate that verbal testimony is the foremost kind of evidence, the author speaks the above words. It is by the assistance (sacivyena) of verbal testimony (yat) that the other kinds of evidence produce valid knowledge (suddhimat). For instance, a person who has seen an illusory head moving may, by mistake, not believe when seeing a real one, but his perception is corrected when a voice from the sky states, “This is the head of such-and-such.” Also, inference is corrected when someone says, “O travellers afflicted by cold, do not think that there may be fire on this hill. I have seen that the fire has just now been extinguished by the rain. Yet there is fire on that hill emitting smoke.” The author then states that independently and directly, perception and inference are liable to inaccuracy (vyabhicari). For instance, direct perception is erroneous when a magician displays an illusory head and says, “This is the head of such-and-such,” for in fact it is not so. Inference is erroneous when it is assumed that on account of smoke, there is fire on a hill where a lot of smoke has been rising for a long time, but where the fire has been put out by the rain at that moment. In this example, there is certainly a sign in the form of enough smoke to sting the eyes, and so on. The meaning of the last line of the paragraph is clear.

### Text 4

*anukulo matas tarkah suskas tu parivarjitah*

**Перевод:**

**Favourable hypothetical argument is accepted, but dry, hypothetical argument is rejected.**

**Комментарий:**

Someone may argue, “Then inference should be rejected.” In response to this, the author speaks the above words. Favourable (anukulah) is that which corroborates the meaning of the sacred scriptures, and unfavourable is that which is contrary to the meaning of the Vedas. Since doubt is removed by the recognition of invariable concomitance (vyapti-graha), hypothetical argument (tarka) is a limb of inference. Therefore, if hypothetical argument is not accepted, then inference must also be understood as not being accepted, since inference includes hypothetical argument.

### Text 5

*tatha hi vajasaneyinah— atma va are drastavyah srotavyo mantavyo nididhyasitavyah, iti\| kathas ca— naisa tarkena matir apaneya proktanyena sujnanaya prestha, iti*

**Перевод:**

**Similarly, the texts of the Vajasaneya branch of the Vedas speak as follows: atma va are drastavyah srotavyo mantavyo nididhyasitavyah (Brhad-aranyaka Upanisad,** [**4.5.6**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bau/4.5.6)**), “O Maitreyi, the Supreme Soul, Lord Hari, should be realised, heard of, thought upon, and meditated on.” The Katha Upanisad (**[**1.2.9**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/katha-bs/1.2.9)**) also declares: naisa tarkena matir apaneya proktanyena sujnanaya prestha, “Dear Naciketa, this intellect must not be ruined by logic. When it is taught by another person, it leads to the knowledge of the Supreme.”**

**Комментарий:**

Regarding the acceptance of favourable logic, here the author quotes the Brhad-aranyaka Upanisad. “O (are) Maitreyi, Lord Hari (atma), should be realised (drastavyah).” Yajnavalkya then explains the means: Lord Hari should be heard of (srotavyah), that is to say He should be comprehended by hearing from the mouth of a spiritual master conversant with the Vedas. He should be thought upon (mantavyah), which means He should be ascertained by logic in conformity with the Vedas. He should be meditated on (nididhyasitavyah). Meditation here is the injunction (vidheya), for it is something unknown. Since hearing is something known from the injunctions prescribing the study of the Vedas, and since thinking is meant to strengthen what was heard, both hearing of and thinking upon are mere repetitions (anuvada). Regarding the rejection of unfavourable logic, the author then quotes the Katha Upanisad. “Dear (prestha) Naciketa, this (esa) intellect (matih) entitled to knowledge of the Supreme must not be ruined (apaneya) by logic (tarkena).” “Then how shall I have knowledge ?” “When the intellect is taught (prokta) by another person (anyena), a spiritual master conversant with the Vedas, it becomes capable of attaining correct knowledge (sujnanaya) of the Supreme.”

### Text 6

*smrtis ca— purvaparavirodhena ko’trartho ’bhimato bhavet\| ity adyam uhanam tarkah suska-tarkam tu varjayet, iti*

**Перевод:**

**The smrti also declares: purvaparavirodhena ko’trartho’bhimato bhavet, ity adyam uhanam tarkah suska-tarkam tu varjayet,“Without contradicting the previous and subsequent statements, what must be the intended meaning in this scriptural passage?— Such deliberation is real logic. Mere dry logic should be given up.”**

**Комментарий:**

uktam vyavastham pramanayati— purvapareti

Here the author gives evidence confirming the stated principle.

### Text 7

*naveda-vidusam yasmad brahma-dhir upajayate*  
*yac caupanisadam brahma tasman mukhya srutir mata*  
*tatha hi srutih— navedavin manute tarn bmantam, iti*  
*aupanisadam purusam prcchami, iti ca*

**Перевод:**

**Knowledge about the Supreme does not arise in those who do not know the Vedas, for the Supreme is taught in the Upanisads. Therefore, the Vedas are considered the foremost kind of evidence. As stated in the sruti: navedavin manute tam bmantam (Taittiriya Brahmana, 3.12.7), “One who does not know the Vedas does not know the Supreme.” aupanisadam purusam prcchami (Brhad-aranyaka Upanisad,** [**3.9.26**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/bau/3.9.26)**), “I am asking you about the Supreme Person taught in the Upanisads.”**

*iti navamam prameyam*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the ninth object of knowledge.**

**Комментарий:**

Here the author concludes the topic by directly and indirectly demonstrating the superiority of the Vedas: indirectly by stating that knowledge of the Supreme (brahma-dhih) does not (na) arise (upajayate) in those who are devoid of knowledge of the Vedas (aveda-vidusam), such as the logicians, and directly by saying that the Supreme (brahma) is taught in the Upanisads (aupanisadam). The meaning of the quoted texts has been thus explained.

Thus, the section of the Prameya-ratnavali entitled “The Three Kinds of Evidence” has been explained.

## Epilogue

### Text 1

*evam uktam praca—*  
*sriman-madhva-mate harih paratamah satyam jagat tattvato*  
*bhedo jiva-gana harer anucara nicocca-bhavam gatah*  
*muktir naija-sukhanubhutir amala bhaktis ca tat-sadhanam*  
*aksadi-tritayam pramanam akhilamnayaika-vedyo harih*

**Перевод:**

**These objects of knowledge have been stated by our previous acarya in the following words: “In the view of the venerable Madhvacarya, 1) Lord Hari is the Supreme; 2) the world is real; 3) difference is real; 4) the hosts of living entities are servants of Lord Hari; 5) they attain a low or high state; 6) liberation means to experience bliss in one’s own constitutional form; 7) selfless worship of the Lord is the means to liberation; 8) direct perception, inference, and verbal testimony are the three kinds of evidence; and 9) Lord Hari is to be known through all sacred scriptures.”**

**Комментарий:**

“The very same objects of knowledge accepted by our previous acarya (praca) have also been taken up by me in this treatise”— this is what the author says here. The word anucarah means “servants.” The low (nica) or high (ucca) state (bhavam) is the gradation of the result according to different spiritual practices. Liberation (muktih) is defined in the Srimad-Bhagavatam ([2.10.6](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-2/10.6)): muktir hitvanyatha-rupam svarftpena vyavasthitih, “Liberation means being situated in one’s original constitutional form after giving up any form other than that.” The jiva s own constitutional (naija) form is minute consciousness that experiences bliss and is a servant of Lord Hari. Servitude is inherently connected with the attainment of His feet, and therefore it does not contradict the statement that liberation is the attainment of Lord Hari’s feet. The meaning of the rest is clear.

### Text 3

*nityam nivasatu hrdaye caitanyatma murarir nah*  
*niravadyo nirvrtiman gajapatir anukampaya yasya*

First interpretation

**Перевод:**

**May Lord Murari, Whose transcendental form is pure consciousness, eternally reside within our hearts. By His mercy, Gajendra, the king of elephants, gave up his animal body and attained liberation.**

Second interpretation

**Перевод:**

**May Lord Murari in the form of Caitanya Mahaprabhu eternally reside within our hearts. By His mercy, Gajapati Prataparudra was freed from all worldly passions and became absorbed in the happiness of love for Krsna.**

Third interpretation

**Перевод:**

**May Sri Rasikananda Murari, whose heart was fully fixed on Lord Caitanya, always reside within our hearts. By his mercy, Gopala dasa, the king of elephants, gave up his animal nature and became ecstatic upon serving the devotees of the Lord.**

*iti prameyaratnavali purtim agat*

**Перевод:**

**Thus ends the Prameya-ratnavali.**

**Комментарий:**

Here at the end, the author recites an auspicious invocation revealing the worshipable personalities within His heart. This verse can be explained in connection with three personalities: Lord Krsna, Lord Caitanya, and Rasika Murari, who was the fourth preceptor before the author. In the first interpretation, the compound caitanyatma means that Lord Murari (another name of Krsna) has a transcendental form consisting of pure consciousness. The king of the elephants (gaja-patih) is Gajendra, who was afflicted by a crocodile. In the second interpretation, the compound caitanyatma means that Lord Murari has appeared in a form (atma) named Caitanya as the son of Jagannatha Misra and Saci devi. Gajapatih is King Prataparudra. In the third interpretation, the name murarih refers to Rasika Murari, whose heart (atma) was fully fixed on Lord Caitanya, and the compound gaja-patih (king of elephants) refers to the elephant named Gopala dasa.
