## Foreword

Spiritual seekers take the availability of sacred texts for granted. Digital scanning and other efforts to preserve the past have rendered rare manuscripts easily accessible. Add tools such as Google, Amazon.com, and online electronic archives, and the exegetic treasures of faith traditions lie virtually at our fingertips. Gaps of course exist. Inventor Louis Daguerre created the first chemical plates in 1829. Prior to that time, preservation of sacred teachings was the domain of oral and written transmission, both subject to faulty reporting and prejudiced interpretation. As technology progressed, so did the accessibility of source materials. Today, anyone can conduct an online search for chapters of the *Zohar* or words to the Sanskrit Gayatri *mantra* and obtain documents previously restricted to the most advanced practitioners.

Still, physical possession of texts does not equate to understanding them. Here are the writings, but what do they mean? How do these precepts translate into practice? Do realizations posited hundreds or perhaps thousands of years ago apply to a post-modern world? What relevance do they have in the current environment of globalization, secularism, genetic engineering, and conventions on universal human rights?

One consequence of having access to sacred texts is the increased importance of mature commentary. Without the insights of seasoned practitioners, wise words dissolve into anachronisms, artifacts of a remote time, pages gathering dust. We can, then, be thankful that disciples of His Holiness Bhakti Tirtha Swami had the foresight to record their *guru’s* many lectures on Vaisnava (Hindu devotional) precepts, given roughly between 1990 and August 2005 when he succumbed to cancer. The result is a series of books titled *Reflections on Sacred Teachings*, in which this is the sixth volume.

Bhakti Tirtha Swami was a *sannyasi* (renunciant) in the Vaisnava tradition. Many Vaisnava teachers have provided commentaries on the extensive library of Sanskrit devotional texts. Bhakti Tirtha Maharaja’s own *guru*, His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, produced more than 100 volumes of commentary that now form the doctrinal foundation of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness, a worldwide confederation of temples, farms, schools, and community service centers. Inspired by that example, Maharaja left an impressive literary legacy of his own.

The unique appeal of Bhakti Tirtha Swami’s commentaries is their relevance to twenty-first century Western seekers. Born John Favors in 1950, Bhakti Tirtha Swami was an African American who served on front lines during the civil rights marches of the sixties. He was a participant in human rights initiatives of the 1970s, ’80s, and ’90s and in his later years became a sought-after counselor who offered practical advice to decision-makers in business and government. This was at a time when corporate scandals and the catastrophe of 9/11 had stripped the cloak of immunity from capitalist culture. Assumptions about America’s physical and financial invincibility had proven wrong. In an instant, the tallest buildings and most profitable businesses could be reduced to ashes. Hardened executives and seasoned politicians contemplated their mortality and struggled to revise fundamental tenets of their trade. For capitalism to survive a different paradigm was called for, one that would give new meaning to terms such as profit, fulfillment, success, and growth.

Into this unique moment in history stepped Bhakti Tirtha Swami with straight talk, brilliant insight, a light heart, and a smile that lit up the room. Here was no theorist engaged in liturgical debate, but a savvy child of the postwar years with good advice for working people seeking spiritual fulfillment. Here was no religionist arguing the subtleties of textual interpretation, but a Princeton University educated pragmatist who saw both the virtues of democracy and the necessity of enlightened leadership. Ecstatic dancing became the trademark of his thirty-year career as a Krishna swami. Practical instruction for people with families and careers distinguished his role as teacher. And profound, heartfelt concern for everyone’s spiritual progress became his most memorable trait.

Which brings us to the work at hand. This sixth volume of commentary shines a spotlight on the central tenet of Vaisnava character—service to others—as described in Indic texts such as the *Padma Purana* and *Narada-pancaratra* and in medieval works such as *Caitanya-caritamrta* and writings of the Gosvamis of Vrndavana. These texts differentiate Salvationists, whose primary interest is their own safety, from Vaisnavas who place higher value on the well-being of others. So dear to God are these compassionate souls that any disrespect shown them provokes dire consequences for the offender. If breaking basic principles of Vaisnava behavior (such as gambling, consuming flesh, taking drugs, or having illicit sex) impedes one’s spiritual progress, *aparadha* or offense to a Vaisnava can lead to the complete withdrawal of God’s mercy (*radha*) and a halt to spiritual progress altogether. In the opening chapter, the author explores the causes behind such offenses, including arrogance, pride, and insecurity, and reminds aspiring devotees to offer respect not only to Vaisnavas but to all people irrespective of their faith.

The author’s commentaries are relevant to life in the secular world as well as within devotional circles. “If we are holding grudges,” he says, “seeing others as non-Vaisnavas, that mentality will create problems…. We must consciously release \[those who have offended us\] from blame.” He speaks to aspiring Vaisnavas, but his words could just as well be an explanation of principles governing Truth and Reconciliation Commissions such as those operating in South Africa. Established after the dissolution of apartheid in the early 1990s, the Commissions offer a chance for amnesty to perpetrators of race crimes, provided they make a full disclosure before their victims and undertake reparations, and provided, too, that their victims accept their repentance and agree to the reparations. It is not a foolproof system, but it has been effective in rekindling dignity among victims and in edging court systems from punitive to restorative justice.

By linking justice to character, Bhakti Tirtha Swami captures a critical dimension of current international peacekeeping theory. The most progressive analysts agree that resolving conflict will not be achieved on political or military grounds but on spiritual grounds where deeper bonds may be forged between warring parties. Maharaja ends his first chapter with a reminder that we change others by first changing something within ourselves.

Chapter two addresses, among other topics, the unheralded role of women gurus in Vaisnava history. Drawing heavily on arguments that recall humanist Ashley Montague’s acclaimed work, *The Natural Superiority of Women* (1952), he argues that the female of the species is biologically, sexually, emotionally, and even intellectually superior to the male—a position supported by recent research and social scientific data. His goal is not to foster revolution in male-dominant Vaisnava tradition or promote a society dominated by women. Rather, he points to emotional and interpersonal qualities typically ascribed to and devalued in women as key to healthy social and spiritual development. If women seem to have played a lesser role historically in advancing Vaisnavism, he says, it is not because they have less to offer but because their contributions usually take place behind the scenes. Women spiritual leaders make their mark through what conflict negotiators call Track II diplomacy: non-official, anonymous. One can reasonably conjecture that Bhakti Tirtha Swami’s purpose in tackling this controversial issue within a series of lectures on character was to promote a world of equality where the beautiful characteristics of women rule: a world of cooperation, sensitivity, and human kindness.

The balance of this slender book occupies itself with a wide range of issues relating to spiritual progress, including an analysis of behavior patterns detrimental to that progress. Here the author emphasizes intent. Courts of laws also recognize the importance of intent, and parallels can be drawn between divine law and civil law. Two persons performing the same act, one with intent to do good and another with intent to do harm, will be judged accordingly. In a court of civil law, their motives will count in determination of innocence, guilt, mitigation of sentence, clemency, and even reward if merited. In the court of spiritual law, a similar principle applies. Pilgrimage will prove more or less purifying, depending on the pilgrim’s intent. Chanting will achieve a greater or lesser effect on consciousness, depending on the chanter’s intent. Service offered purely, even if awkwardly performed, will be more pleasing to God than proficient service offered without such purity of intent.

Other issues are addressed in this sixth volume. What constitutes an offense to the Lord’s devotees? How can such offenses be rectified? If spirit is by nature pure, why would anyone choose to leave the peaceful spiritual world and enter this contentious environment in the first place? The author has found provocative parallels between misuse of free will by conditioned souls and misuse of power by arrogant leaders. Neither is capable of sustaining a lasting peace or building a stable society. By the exercise of humility, partnerships can be salvaged, wars disarmed, and material instability rectified. One particularly stimulating passage in Chapter Four urges readers to not mistake humility for low self-esteem. While seeking no credit for themselves, effective spiritualists honor God within their own hearts and the hearts of others—a gesture that energizes rather than dampens enthusiasm and initiative.

Perhaps the most memorable teaching in *Reflections on Sacred Teaching VI* is simply this. Before pointing fingers at others, take a good look at yourself and remember, “There but for the grace of God go I.” In his calm yet unambiguous style, Bhakti Tirtha Swami sends his message out to devotees young and old. The spiritual world, he reminds us, is a place governed by respect, compassion, forgiveness, and love. It was a message he embodied in his too brief career and one that, if taken to heart, can transform a world at war to a world at peace.

—Joshua M. Greene (Yogesvara dasa) teaches in the Religion Department of Hofstra University. His books and documentaries on war crimes and justice have appeared in several languages. His most recent book “Here Comes The Sun” (www.herecomesthesunbook.com) traces the spiritual journey of the late Beatle George Harrison. He is a trained mediator and serves on the Counseling Initiative of ISKCON’s Strategic Planning team.

## Introduction

Bhakti Tirtha Maharaja has asked me to write this introduction, because he is merciful. He was merciful enough to allow me to edit this book. A question may be asked—why? Why should someone as insignificant, offensive and generally useless as me edit the teachings of a great soul and, what’s more, write an introduction to a wonderful book about overcoming offenses, the greatest obstacles to devotion. The answer is again—Mercy.

In February of this year (2005), I traveled with my wife, Sharad-bihari, my family, and Atma devi dasi, a disciple of Bhakti Tirtha Maharaja, to his Vyasa-puja Festival in Gita-nagari (a farm community in Pennsylvania). I had met with Bhakti Tirtha Maharaja (after many years of separation) in 2004 at the Festival of Inspiration in New Vrndavana. It had been almost 30 years from our earlier association as fledgling devotees in Dallas in the early 1970’s. I was the fledgling; he was the devotee! I spent an hour or so in deep conversation with that great soul and felt myself filled with new hope and enthusiasm for Krishna consciousness. Therefore, I contemplated the chance to see him again in Gita-nagari with great anticipation. I wasn’t sure what to expect, but I knew it could only be wonderful.

How can I describe that festival and his association? We arrived in a snowstorm and were soon snowbound but sheltered by his love. He showered mercy on us all—there was intense chanting, tears, amazing talks, ecstatic *kirtanas*—at one point Maharaja became so overcome with spiritual emotion he actually began to dance on his one remaining leg! He spent hours giving us his insights, his humor, his benedictions. He invited us to intimately associate with him, and I feel that my life was transformed. Not only my life, but my whole family’s. Our *sraddha* deepened immeasurably. Much that appeared to be lost was restored to us all. My teenaged daughter actually began to chant sixteen rounds a day and has continued to the present. She had never experienced anyone like Bhakti Tirtha Maharaja before. Even for those of us who have experienced Srila Prabhupada’s association, the encounter with his superexcellent disciple, Bhakti Tirtha Maharaja, was amazing and profound.

Finally, on the day of our departure from Gita-nagari, I begged Maharaja to give me some service. Since I earn a living as an editor and writer, I thought perhaps I could serve him somehow in that capacity. He agreed, and he gave me the perfect subject matter: *aparadha*! This book not only details the various kinds of offenses, but it also shows the attentive reader how to remove the obstacles to devotion and experience the deep and unlimited happiness of Krishna consciousness. What could have been more appropriate? The experience of editing this book was non-different than association with Maharaja—encouragement and enlightenment all the way. I corresponded with him throughout the process, and, despite the debilitated state of his body, he instructed me in the most empowering, kind and merciful way:

When you’re editing this book, don’t just see it as an editing experience, it’s actually a little more than that. See it more in the mood that we’re writing this book together. Therefore, this should give you much more freedom in what you add or refine. Hopefully, I’ll still be in the body when the book is finished, and I or my last editors will look at the final product and any small changes that need to be made will be done at that time. You see, I feel that Srila Prabhupada himself has connected us at this time, so our connection has a certain spiritual blessing.

When I had finished my final edit and sent him the book, I accompanied it with some questions about how to rectify unknowing offenses or offenses committed to those no longer with us. He replied:

With regards to offenses: As you know we all make offenses, especially in Kali-yuga. We are forgiven as we internally recognize our mistakes. More importantly, as we increasingly desire to serve the Vaisnavas better, Krishna will allow this desire to be the servant of the servant to cancel out the negative effects of our offenses. Simply become more intense in trying to be a servant of the servant.

I offered the title *Missing Mercy* for this book. The reason for this is that Radha-sunya (*aparadha*), according to the Vaisnava lexicon, means “devoid of Radharani.” When we speak of being “devoid” of Radha, that expression indicates Radharani’s withdrawal or absence from our lives. This is the existential experience of one who has committed offenses. In reality, Radharani is ever-present, but She may withdraw from us so that Her presence is “conspicuous by its absence.” We begin to experience the void created by Her removal and we suffer. We then begin to intensely hanker for Her presence in our lives, which is the beginning of our rectification from offenses. Radharani represents Krishna’s compassionate side or merciful side—so when we are devoid of Radharani, we are missing (feeling the absence of) Mercy. We are separated from and in separation from Mercy Herself. It is only one way to define Radha-sunya, but I think it is appropriate. Missing Mercy indicates being separated from mercy *and* feeling separation from mercy—Radharani.

However, the practical experience of this book is just the opposite of missing mercy! The experience is Mercy Gained, Mercy Showered! This is the nature of a pure Vaisnava—he feels intense compassion for others, and he lives to make arrangements for others to achieve or regain the merciful association of Srimati Radharani, Bhakti Devi. This is the nature of Bhakti Tirtha Swami Maharaja. He has lived his entire life to make others happy as the servant of the servant of Radha-Krishna. I feel that my life has been transformed by his mercy, and I shall try to serve him and remain grateful to him until the day I die. Hare Krishna!

—Rupa-vilasa dasa

## 1. Offenses Enumerated

## Aparadha: Devoid of Radha

If we examine the culture of *bhakti*, we are always reminded that there are practices to be accepted and practices to be rejected. There is the positive path, the *pravrtti-marga*, and the path that enjoins certain restrictions, the *nivrtti-marga*. There are certain things that guarantee a Vaisnava’s growth, that guarantee healthy watering of the devotional creeper, such as the avoidance of *anarthas* (unwanted values and activities which bring about stagnation) and *aparadhas* (offenses). Furthermore, there are many types of *aparadhas*. In the execution of service, there are thirty-two offenses to be avoided known as *seva-aparadha*. There are also ten offenses to avoid while chanting (*nama-aparadha*), which we generally recite in the morning. Also to be avoided are *vaisnava-aparadha* (offenses to the Vaisnavas), *dhama-aparadha* (offenses to the holy places), and *guru-aparadha* (offenses to the spiritual master).

In the Gaudiya Vaisnava dictionary (Bengali version), the term “*aparadha*” is explained or defined as “*radha-sunya,”*devoid of Radha. Srimati Radharani is the *hladini-sakti*, the internal potency of Krishna. She is the mother of devotion, the topmost devotee, and therefore the overseer of the entire province of *bhakti*. Thus, *aparadha* means becoming devoid of Radharani by creating a situation by which the blessings, the mercy, the protection, and the benediction of the divine caretaker of *bhakti* is absent. We know that we cannot advance without the mercy of Lord Nityananda and the mercy of Srimati Radharani. There are many camps of devotees following the path of *bhakti*. All are under the jurisdiction of Lord Nityananda, the original spiritual master, and the care and jurisdiction of Srimati Radharani. It is through the agencies of these great personalities that we attain Krishna. The mercy of Lord Nityananda comes to us through *chadiya vaisnava-seva nistara payeche keba*—the dust of the feet of the *sadhus*. By approaching Lord Nityananda in this way, He becomes pleased.

In Kali-yuga our development of devotion is often connected to *krpa-siddhi* (perfection through mercy) and is not based as much on our expertise in the execution of *sadhana-bhakti*, even though we may try our best. Even in trying, we often miss the mark. We do not want to miss the mark in achieving the *prasadam* or mercy of Lord Nityananda or Srimati Radharani. It is *sunya* (void) or *radha-sunya* that we are inviting when we commit *aparadha*, whether it is *nama-aparadha*, *dhama-aparadha*, *vaisnava-aparadha*, or *seva-aparadha*. It means to be bereft of the mercy of Radharani.

We want to share a few quotes and stories throughout this book in relation to the blockades and inhibitors that prevent us from achieving the results of the *bhakti-yoga* process. Why? Because devotional service works! If we engage in devotional service, our spiritual growth is guaranteed, *except* where there are offenses. Offenses stunt our progress even to the point of stagnating our spiritual growth for lifetimes. Sometimes offenses can take away lifetimes of pious deposits and credits. From another perspective, however, where there is the presence of the mercy of Radharani and Lord Nityananda, we can accelerate or upgrade our progress by leaps and bounds! In the university or school when a student progresses from one level to the next level, occasionally their achievement is so tremendous that they are able to pass through certain studies very quickly because of their absorption in the subject matter. Blessings or benefits have come based on their success in pleasing the teacher, authority, instructor, mentor, etc.

## The Ten Offenses Against the Holy Name

So many Vaisnavas have a daily habit of reciting the *dasa-nama-aparadhas*, the ten offenses. We need this daily reminder, and not only should we meditate on what the offenses are, but we should develop strategies for avoiding them. In the *Padma Purana*, Sanat-kumara instructed Narada Muni in the following way:

*satam ninda namnah paramam*  
*aparadham vitanute*  
*yatah khyatim yatam katham u*  
*sahate tad-vigarham*

*sivasya sri-visnor ya iha*  
*guna-namadi-sakalam*  
*dhiya bhinnam pasyet sa khalu*  
*hari-namahita-karah*

*guror avajna sruti-sastra-nindanam*  
*tathartha-vado hari-namni kalpanam*  
*namno balad yasya hi papa-buddhir*  
*na vidyate tasya yamair hi suddhih*

*dharma-vrata-tyaga-hutadi-sarva-*  
*subha-kriya-samyam api pramadah*  
*asraddadhane vimukhe ’py asrnvati*  
*yas copadesah siva-namaparadhah*

*srutvapi nama-mahatmyam*  
*yah priti-rahito narah*  
*aham-mamadi-paramo*  
*namni so ’py aparadha-krt*

**1. Blasphemy of the devotees of the Lord, who are engaged in the propagation of the Hare Krishna** ***mantra*** **is the worst offense at the lotus feet of the holy name. The** ***nama-prabhu*****, who is identical with Krishna, will never tolerate such blasphemous activities, even from one who passes as a great devotee.**

**2. In this material world the holy name of Visnu is all-auspicious. Visnu’s name, form, qualities, and pastimes are all transcendental, absolute knowledge. Therefore, if one tries to separate the Absolute Personality of Godhead from His holy name or His transcendental form, qualities, and pastimes, thinking them to be material, that is offensive. Similarly, to think the names of the demigods such as Lord Siva to be as good as the name of Lord Visnu is also blasphemous.**

**3. To disobey the order of the spiritual master, or to consider the spiritual master to be material, and therefore to envy his exalted position.**

**4. Blasphemy of Vedic literature or literature in pursuance of the Vedic version.**

**5. To give some interpretation on the holy name of the Lord.**

**6. To consider the glories of the holy name to be imaginary.**

**7. To think that since the Hare Krishna** ***mantra*** **can counteract all sinful reactions one may therefore go on with all his sinful activities and then at the same time chant the Hare Krishna** ***mantra*** **to neutralize them is the greatest offense at the lotus feet of** ***hari-nama*****.**

**8. It is a great offense to consider the chanting of the Hare Krishna** ***mantra*** **to be equal to the performance of religious ceremonies, following austere vows, practicing renunciation, and fire sacrifices which are all materialistic auspicious activities.**

**9. It is an offense to preach the glories of the holy name to those who will not hear and to those who have no faith in the chanting of the holy name.**

**10. If one has heard the glories of the transcendental holy name of the Lord but nevertheless continues in a materialistic concept of life, thinking, “I am this body, and everything belonging to this body is mine (*****aham mameti*****),” and does not show respect and love for the chanting of the Hare Krishna** ***maha-mantra*****, that is an offense.**

**It is also an offense to be inattentive while chanting.**

***Padma Purana*****,** ***Brahma Khanda*****, 25.15—18**

To recite the offenses to chanting the holy name and to discuss them in lectures when taking initiation is so much an integral part of our Vaisnava tradition. When we come into formal connection with the *parampara* (disciplic succession) at the initiation ceremony, our promise to avoid offenses, especially the ten offenses, facilitates our connection to and recognition by the *parampara*, Krishna, the *guru*, and the *sadhus*. What can break us or ruin our efforts is not following the process of *bhakti* properly and maintaining or engaging in the ten offenses. Srila Vyasadeva describes one offense as the *param aparadha*—the greatest offense: *satam ninda namnah paramam aparadham vitanute*—to blaspheme the saintly persons who are engaged in preaching the Hare Krishna *mantra*. Offenses against Vaisnavas are further discussed by Srila Jiva Goswami in the *Bhakti-sandarbha*, and he explains in the section concerning *sadhu-ninda* how devastating *vaisnava-aparadha* is, especially if such a horrible practice is not removed from our devotional lives.

## Impediments that Devastate the Creeper

We want to present some illustrations from the *sastra* (scripture) that will help us gain more insight into the importance of making certain commitments when formally connecting with the *parampara*. Why does one person advance constantly in devotional service, while another excels wonderfully at a certain stage, and then, somehow, the creeper seems to become choked up? We see again and again in a variety of environments that certain incidents transpire and devotees seem to become altered. Year after year we see cases in which devotees who were very enthusiastic, eager to chant, read, associate, and pioneer in many ways somehow appear to become different people. They become devoid of the mercy of Sri Radha, or they begin to take in less of the mercy of Radha. This is the manifestation of *aparadha*. *Bhakti* is like a flower or a plant. When the sun is present and the water is absorbed nicely, the plant or flower will grow. However, one day we see the flower changing color, wilting, waning until it dies or is on the verge of death. This is due to *aparadha*.

Recognizing *vaisnava-aparadha* and understanding it is very important. When one is advanced, one can see the importance of devotees and, therefore, never disrespect another devotee. Disrespecting another devotee is the greatest offense, *vaisnava-aparadha*. It is a very serious offense. Therefore, we emphasize Srila Prabhupada’s instruction to address devotees as “prabhu.” This should not simply be spoken by the lips; it should be realized. Other devotees are our *prabhus* (our masters); it is not that we should try to become *their* masters:

*trnad api sunicena*  
*taror iva sahisnuna*  
*amanina manadena*  
*kirtaniya sada harih*

**One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree, and who does not expect personal honor, but is always prepared to give all respects to others, can very easily always chant the holy name of the Lord.** ***Siksastaka*****, 3**

We should always be ready to offer all respects to others. This is such a serious matter for the development of the Vaisnava community. *Vaisnava-aparadha*, which often stems from arrogance and pride, is our greatest enemy. Srila Prabhupada has explained that Maya is always present and can do so much damage. Maya’s biggest weapon is to influence us to criticize *sadhus*. Such criticism is a clear indication of our lack of coherence, respectful association, and pridelessness in dealing with devotees in our own communities. This is Maya’s special weapon because *aparadha* is really the only thing that acts to impede our movement. External forces cannot really stop our movement. We have to deal with so many challenges, but when we are not able to deal with outside attacks or when we are not able to deal with the external influence of Maya, it is simply due to an internal lack of spiritual potency.

Maya is never stronger than Maya’s boss. Maya is working for Krishna to test our sincerity. Her allurements are external aspects of Krishna’s opulence. When Maya manages to get inside of *us*, then devotees of Krishna may also become propagators, maintainers, or repositories for Maya. This is a great problem, because if we allow Maya to work through us, how will Radha’s mercy, shelter, protection, and guidance come to us? Her mercy can only manifest to the same degree that we are free from offenses and *anarthas*.

It is not always so important who is right and who is wrong, but what is more important is *how* one is right. You can be right but still be offensive to others, causing you to naturally suffer the reactions of those offenses. You may be wrong *and* offensive and suffer double or triple. You can also be right in your idea and still be wrong in your expression of it. You can be right that one of the most important services is distributing books, but then you can also be condescending, arrogant, and disrespectful to the Vaisnavas. Even though an activity performed as service may be authorized, still the consciousness or the interaction that one is engaged in may be offensive. These considerations are so important because our actions are in one sense secondary to consciousness. Even if we are trying to support the right action but are in the wrong consciousness, such consciousness indicates the presence of impurity. In the final analysis, Krishna does not need anything or anyone. He wants our love and devotion. He wants our hearts.

This is one reason why we see devotees in big positions make mistakes or even fall. Where there are *aparadhas*, the spiritual immune system becomes weak, and we become susceptible. Normally we would not have so much interest in analyzing how people in higher positions fall; nevertheless, if this phenomenon is studied, we can clearly understand how the fall takes place and how such devotees become overwhelmed. They are now practicing what they were preaching *against* a year or two years ago, even while fully understanding that such activity takes place under the spell of Maya. Now the same people are living and practicing according to a lower standard rejected by them previously.

It is not a mystery. Falldown is always connected to *aparadha*. If we are growing and developing, it means we are doing something right—something is flourishing. Where there is stagnation or deterioration, it means negative change is taking place, even though previously there had been growth. Does this reversal mean that Krishna never really existed? Does it mean that there was never really any advancement? Was the whole thing a bluff? Or is it that the science is real and potent? When we are practicing in the right way, we will grow and benefit, and when we are not following, no matter who we are, we will suffer a disturbance or deviation from our devotion. When someone has a dwindling of their devotional creeper, or someone falls under the control of Maya, it is additional proof that Krishna consciousness is real. We have the medicine: *nama-ausadhi*—the medicine of the holy name. Whether we take it or not is another thing, but we *have* the medicine. If we have medicine, and by taking it we see that our health is restored, we have concrete proof of its efficacy. If we see that we get progressively weaker when we do not take it, then we also know there is power in the medicine. If someone is taking medicine according to the right prescription, then there is no question of success. If it is not taken properly, then there must be a consequence. If we are no longer taking the medicine of the holy name in the prescribed way, the deviation or fault shows us clearly that the medicine works. The deviation or fault shows that when the medicine is not taken, there is a distinct lack of result (conspicuous by its absence) in contrast to when it was being taken according to prescription. Therefore, when we are bewildered by Maya, succumbing to fall down, that falldown is a sign that the *bhakti* process works; it is a sign that one should be careful not to minimize the potency of the medicine and the prescribed method for taking it.

## Never Insult Anyone

Having insulted Dhruva Maharaja, Suruci became maddened upon the death of her son, entered a forest fire, and thus ended her life. This was specifically mentioned by the Lord to Dhruva Maharaja, because Dhruva was determined to take revenge against her. From this we can take a lesson: never insult a Vaisnava. Not only should we not insult a Vaisnava, we should not insult anyone. When Suruci insulted Dhruva Maharaja, he was just a child, and she did not know or recognize his position as a Vaisnava. She insulted him before his full manifestation as a great Vaisnava, although we know that this was his destiny. By her insulting him, even as a child, the offense had such a serious effect on her. Therefore, we should be careful in our dealings with everyone. We don’t know who they might be or who they might become.

The seriousness of offenses is proportionate to the purity and advancement of the devotee offended. Normally what happens is that devotees take each other for granted, and offenses are made on both sides (or all sides), and consequently all the offenders suffer because such offenses bring contamination to the beautiful culture of *bhakti*. Devotees tend to take the importance of proper association lightly. Sometimes they have issues with each other and do not handle those issues in the proper way, creating in-groups, out-groups, grudges, and offenses. Everyone involved suffers due to this unfortunate process. The level of suffering is not always the same for all parties involved. According to the level of advancement, offenses may become more serious. If we are facilitated by someone who is very pure, we are blessed proportionately by such association. Conversely, where there is an offensive mood, the offense will be greater due to the purity and the devotion of the offended Vaisnava. Sometimes the offense is one-sided. Someone may be very advanced, or not so advanced but not offensive to others. If we offend *that* devotee, the offense takes place only for the offending party. As a matter of fact, offenseless Vaisnavas feel sorry for the offenders because they realize that such unfortunate persons will suffer due to the offense. The devotee may forgive the offender, but Krishna may not excuse it.

For example, when Haridasa Thakura was beaten in twenty-two marketplaces, he forgave all who had offended him, but Krishna punished those who deserved to suffer. We have a duty to be polite and kind to everyone. Even while driving your car or pumping gasoline, if someone is offensive to you, then they will suffer for that. If you find yourself getting into an argument with someone, it is best to get away from the situation, because if you cannot help them, you do not want to cause them to undergo additional suffering. We must be very careful with materialists, even with atheists, to try not to offend them, because we do not simply desire to be “right.” We want to help them *and* be right; otherwise, we can be “right,” but help no one. We may have simply caused that person to become angry or bitter, or managed to inspire them to blaspheme us, blaspheme the society, or blaspheme Krishna. Although we may be philosophically well situated when expressing certain ideas, that unfortunate person, whom we may call a *karmi* or think destined to be punished by Yamaraja, is having his misfortune *created* by us.

This is why we recite the offense not to preach to the faithless. This injunction does not mean that we should not preach to people who are not devotees because, in actuality, so much of our preaching is to the people who are not connected to Krishna consciousness. However, when someone who we approach for the purpose of sharing Krishna consciousness becomes more and more disturbed, then we should not preach to them, because preaching to such a person will make their situation worse. They will become offensive, speak obnoxiously, and perhaps even become violent. If we find that they resort to those behaviors, then we should tactfully try to remove ourselves from the situation. To continue trying to present Krishna to them is just going to make them angry, disturbed, offensive, and often result in the voicing of harsh words. A Vaisnava must think of the welfare of others. Raghunatha dasa Gosvami has stated in his *Vilapa-kusumanjali*, verse 6, *krpambudhir yah para-duhkha-duhkhi*: “The Vaisnava is an ocean of mercy to the fallen, conditioned souls; indeed, he is so compassionate that, although for himself he knows no unhappiness, he feels unhappiness on account of their suffering.”

Even if we have the right point of view, we must be careful to try to create an environment where others will not be inspired to become offensive. A high level devotee is always trying to find ways not only to tell the truth, but to encourage the greatest number of people possible. If someone is openly antagonistic towards religion and God, it doesn’t matter what we do, that person is going to be disturbed. We may have differences with others, but we are not interested in creating their destruction or misfortune. Suruci had her own ideas about justice and was very determined that Prince Dhruva would not get the opportunity to sit on the throne. Thus, she was offensive, and Srila Prabhupada explains that she was forced to undergo suffering.

## Taking Compassion a Step Further

When we are moving about in society, we must try to see everyone as a devotee even though some may be more covered over than others. Recently I had a disappointing experience while traveling to a fair with two of my students. As we were proceeding, having almost reached our destination, a homeless man who was not looking where he was going (perhaps he was intoxicated or mentally disturbed) ran into our car. It was Krishna’s mercy that we did not strike him head on. Actually he struck us—ran into our car. We were moving, and he bumped into us. We stopped, called the police and got an ambulance to come for him. He seemed to have fractured his foot. The thing that was somewhat disturbing to me (and the devotees who were there on the scene can reflect on this) is that the two devotees who were with me did not even get out of the car. I purposely got out.

It is not that we devotees are so important that we do not have time to care for others, thinking that when someone is homeless he is no more important than a rock! That homeless person is also a devotee who happens to be more covered over. The two devotees who were in the car never moved. We could not leave this person without arranging assistance for him. The police were called in and then later an ambulance came to take care of him. Later I was thinking: if this had been a so-called respectable person, every one of us in the car would have been more attentive to this individual. If this were a person in devotee garb, we would have been much more sensitive in some ways. What our indifference indicates is that we don’t understand the real nature of compassion and caring. Compassion and concern does not mean only for those in our *asrama*, in our Vaisnava culture, living in our temple, or in our management line. Compassion does not only apply to those who do the same service we do or think the same way we do. It means that we are Krishna conscious in our attitude toward all living beings. If we are seriously trying to see and find Krishna everywhere, trying to glorify Krishna everywhere and also trying to see how to bring more of Krishna out in every situation, then we must practically apply our beliefs at all times and in all situations.

We are all affected by the sort of thinking which is characterized by material designations:

*sarvopadhi-vinirmuktam*  
*tat-paratvena nirmalam*  
*hrsikena hrisikesa-*  
*sevanam bhaktir ucyate*

***Bhakti*****, or devotional service, means engaging all our senses in the service of the Lord, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the master of all the senses. When the spirit soul renders service unto the Supreme, there are two side effects. One is freed from all material designations, and, simply by being employed in the service of the Lord, one’s senses are purified.** ***Narada-pancaratra***

*Sarvopadhi-vinirmuktam* means to be free from all kinds of material designations, and ultimately means to be free from all desires except the desire to render service to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. We should become *nirmalam*, spotless and pure in our activities.

The covered soul who bumped into us probably has all sorts of mental problems, or could have even been drunk, so it may be our tendency to think: “Well, it’s his fault! He should be careful! Let’s see if he scratched our car!” This is the way materialistic people think. This is the way that all of us are conditioned to think: that someone is important and someone else is unimportant. If one has more wealth, prestige, and position, then that person is considered more important or valuable, and when one does not have much wealth, prestige, and position, they have less value or importance. Actually, everyone has importance and value. I had to examine my own mentality, because maybe if that homeless man had been a very respectable person by material estimation, I would have jumped out even faster or become even more alarmed. However, the mood was more along these lines: “This is a disturbing personality, interfering with the important work we have to do.” What we have to *do* is increasingly be able to understand others’ needs and see how to help, serve, and assist them. Otherwise, the same kind of materially designated mentality will lead to offenses amongst Vaisnavas. We should not simply think of our idea and be insensitive to caring for and facilitating others. Otherwise, we will make offenses, perhaps with all good intentions, but still it will be offensive.

Sometimes it is said, “The road to hell is paved with good intentions.” A devotee who is distributing books might be harsh, rude, and condescending towards others due to the idea that his or her service is far more important than what anyone else is doing. Such a devotee will find it hard to appreciate someone else’s services and activities. This mentality is inimical to the mood of the spiritual world, a place where the residents compete to serve others, care for others, and facilitate others. Our mundane consciousness will position us in *radha-sunya*. It will distance us from the mercy of the divine facilitator who is overseeing *bhakti*. It is not always the big things that draw attention to our state of consciousness as much as the little things we do and say. The real quality of our consciousness is not so much based on how we are serving when we are in front of the Deities exhibiting reverential devotion. The real test of our spiritual consciousness is not so much based on how we serve when we are in the direct presence of our spiritual masters—no. Our consciousness will be tested by how we relate to each other, especially to our peers and those who we may feel ourselves superior to. It is easy to appear very devotional or reverential in the *Bhagavatam* class, hearing the class with attention; in front of the Deities having *darsana*; or directly in service to our *guru* where the physical presence alerts us. That “reverence” doesn’t really show what is deeply embedded in our consciousness and what we are really all about.

The good student does not really exhibit the depths of his or her character when the teacher is present in the classroom. When the teacher leaves, those students who were only superficially well-behaved are now laughing, joking, cheating, and doing whatever whimsical thing occurs to them. However, there may be one student in the group who will be even more conscientious because the teacher is *not* there. Such a student thinks, “What does my teacher want me to do?” When the teacher is not present to be directly asked questions or to monitor the students, the good student is even more meticulous and careful, even more than when the teacher is physically present. This student does not just act based on a fad or because they want to look good to the teacher, but they act according to what they have imbibed from the teacher. When the teacher is absent, the good student is trying to reflect even more, “What was he saying? What was I told to do? How was I supposed to do it? What can I remember?”

## How to Respect the Faithless

Where there is the ever-increasing mercy of Srimati Radharani, there is constant conscientiousness in all matters of service. Sometimes I look at myself and think, “Why do I not have the same level of affection and compassion for this homeless man who is in a horrible situation, this person who is half asleep, half drunk, looking wretched, smelling horrible, and dressed in filthy clothes.” Yes, he was wretched, but why could I not have the same level of concern for him as I would have had if he been a so-called respectable person or a Vaisnava? The point to remember is that the homeless person or any wretched person is more covered over and may have to go through many lifetimes, perhaps even through the animal species. Such a person is especially in need of some form of mercy.

It is not that if someone is wretched or just obnoxious we are obligated to bring him to the temple or embrace him. Our mentality—and our concern—should be recognizing that here is an eternal *jiva* who belongs to Krishna, our Lord. We do not bring a drunkard or a dacoit to our centers because we know that such people will most likely be devious. The main reason that we do not bring them into the temple is not because they are wretched, but because if they come into the temple, they will injure themselves by making offenses. Therefore, we do not want them in the temple. When they come, they will walk in with their shoes, talk very loudly, or do wretched, crazy things. If they come, we are likely to commit the ninth offense: preaching to the faithless. If, by showing them the temple or preaching to them, they become offensive, we are injuring them. By allowing them to come, they will make offenses in this great *tirtha*. We do not keep degraded people (or any people) away because we do not have concern for them; rather, we care most deeply for their soul.

Antagonistic people may be Christians, Buddhists, impersonalists, derelicts, or the homeless. We practice the etiquette of careful interaction with them because we are concerned that whoever connects with us will be benefited. If we cannot give them any benefit, we do not want them to get more entangled in karmic reactions and have to suffer more. Therefore, we may pass out some *prasadam* to them, say something friendly to them in the yard, or when we see them in town we may say Hare Krishna to them. But we do not bring them into our environment because that would not help them. In this way, the Vaisnava is always thinking about the welfare of others.

If someone tries to harm one of our devotees or rob one of us, we must deal seriously with that person. If we do not, then their offense is greater, and there is a chance that they may repeat the offense in other environments. Vaisnavas accept the value of capital punishment, but out of mercy. If a murder is immediately dealt with, then in the next life, the offender will not have to suffer as much. In Kali-yuga, however, capital punishment cannot be honored because presently the laws are not just. The executive and judicial branches may be deviant or morally unfit themselves, and there are so many other mitigating circumstances. In a proper society one is dealt with according to the crime, and it is clear how crimes are addressed. Now there are so many other variables which have to be addressed, because there is a good chance that an innocent person may be killed because the judge may make the wrong decision due to ignorance or bias. An innocent man may be framed or some other gross injustice may be committed. Sometimes the wrong person is accused.

## Help Our Peers Rather Than Criticize

We find in the purport of the *Nectar of Instruction*, Text Six, “…Even if a devotee sometimes seems to be engaged in abominable activities he should be considered a *sadhu*, a saintly person, because his actual identity is that of one engaged in the loving service of the Lord. In other words, he is not to be considered an ordinary human being.” We sometimes get angry at a person for being dirty, even though that person may be in the shower getting clean. The point is that yes, there is some dirt, but such person is becoming clean by taking a shower. Should we then throw them out or consider them to be useless, treating them like an outcaste or getting angry? When a devotee behaves like a non-devotee, becomes angry at an authority or the authority gets angry at the devotee in an excessive way, it can easily result in *aparadha*. Srila Prabhupada wrote in a letter to Vrinda devi on October 14, 1971, “I have noted your several complaints against the devotees but it would be better to set the example rather than to criticize the defects of the devotees. We should always remember that we recruit members from people in general. It is not expected that every one of our members should be immediately to the standard qualification. The best thing for you is to set the example by your personal behavior and try to reform the others, not by criticizing but by friendly behavior.” It’s easy to criticize. The real challenge for us is to help improve the situation by helping in some way, especially by setting a good example ourselves.

Lord Nityananda made it very clear that in this age we don’t kill the Jagais and Madhais (icons of immorality), but rather we attempt to make them devotees—we help improve their qualities and character. This will show others the potency of Krishna consciousness. Haridasa Thakura could have belittled the prostitute who tried to seduce him by saying, “How can you possibly approach me? I am a *sadhu*.” He could have cursed her and cursed all the generations connected with her, or cursed her to get leprosy. Although she came with ill intent, he raised her consciousness. There are dozens and dozens of examples in our literature which clearly show that we are not supposed to attack only the influence of Maya, but we must attempt to upgrade and improve an unfortunate situation. It is one thing to attack and then to get into a tit for tat mentality or combative pattern. Our position is to not waste energy in trying to put down, defeat, or combat others because it wastes energy. Even if we are successful in defeating materialistic ideas, we have wasted valuable time in combat.

Sometimes people know they are going to lose an argument, but they want to slow things down. They want to hurt us, and since they don’t care if they lose, they want to inflict as much damage as they can. Some people will unleash an onslaught of revenge, hatred, and enmity just to create suffering. This happens in the legal system also. There are times when people harass others through excessive litigation even though they know they will eventually lose, because they want the opposing parties to suffer and struggle. This is a dangerous aspect of Maya’s tactics: to try to get Vaisnavas distracted from their actual mission. The result is that when the devotees try to go about the real business of distributing the mercy of Lord Caitanya, they have less enthusiasm, less energy, less resources, and less eagerness.

This is Maya’s espionage: Maya enters into the environment wherever there is devotion. She seeks out those who are the carriers of devotion and influences them to become enemies or to attack each other. Anyone can criticize. We may criticize any leader, any authority, or criticize any devotee, but how much do we step forward to help, how much do we try to set the example of Krishna consciousness. If one does not immediately leave upon hearing the Lord or the Lord’s devotees blasphemed, he falls down from devotional service. That means that *vaisnava-aparadha* is so heavy, that even if we are not committing offenses, but remain in an environment that permits them, our creepers will gradually weaken. We may not be criticizing devotees, but if others are engaging in such criticism, by constantly hearing such criticism our devotion will be weakened. If someone repeatedly tells you about their problems with another Vaisnava, you have to let them know that you don’t want to hear it: “Prabhu, is there anything good about that person?”“Prabhu, how can we help this person?” We should put a stop to that critical type of conversation. It does not mean that we are being arrogant; rather, we are obligated to protect our devotional creepers. “Prabhu, what can we do to help?” Get them to reflect, or let them know that please, don’t drop all of this on me because I do not want to have my devotional experience shortened or limited.

## Seek the Association of Devotees

Giving up the regulative principles and living according to one’s whims are compared to a mad elephant, which by force uproots the *bhakti-lata* and breaks it to pieces. In this way the *bhakti-lata* shrivels up. Such an offense is especially created when one disobeys the instructions of the spiritual master. This is called *guru-avajna*. The devotee must therefore be very careful not to commit offenses against the spiritual master by disobeying his instructions. As soon as one is deviated from the instructions of the spiritual master, the uprooting of the *bhakti-lata* begins, and gradually all the leaves dry up.

*Caitanya-caritamrta*, *Madhya-lila*, 19.156 purport

When a mad elephant enters a beautiful garden, it destroys everything, leaving a barren field. Similarly, the power of *vaisnava-aparadha* is so great that even an advanced devotee becomes almost devoid of his spiritual assets if he commits it. Since Krishna consciousness is eternal, it cannot be destroyed altogether, but advancement may be checked for the time being.

*Srimad-Bhagavatam*, 5.1.5 purport

That means that the positive is always stronger. When there is *radha-sunya* or *aparadha*, the positive development is put on hold, and we may have to go through this lifetime and sometimes several lifetimes before, by our previous devotion, we are able to move forward in spiritual life. *Aparadhas* come from associating with non-devotees—this is one category of *vaisnava-aparadha*. This does not mean we should cloister ourselves or be averse to the general public. Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura states in *Jaiva-dharma* that “associating with nondevotees” means to have loose and emotionally invested worldly dealings—to fail to be in the world, but not of it. It is a great issue, because naturally we imbibe the qualities of those with whom we associate. Srila Prabhupada discusses this point as follows:

When one associates with non-devotees, the maddened elephant is set loose. Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu has said *asat-sanga-tyaga,*—*ei vaisnava-acara* \[*Cc. Madhya* 22.87\]. The first business of a Vaisnava is to give up the company of non-devotees. A so-called mature devotee, however, commits a great offense by giving up the company of pure devotees. The human being is a social animal, and if one gives up the society of pure devotees, he must associate with non-devotees (*asat-sanga*).

*Caitanya-caritamrta*, *Madhya-lila*, 19.157 purport

If we are not happy, satisfied, feeling protected, feeling loved, and getting love amongst the Vaisnavas, it is inevitable that we are later going to find a greater connection with the non-devotees, because all of us are inherently pleasure seeking, *ananda-mayo ‘bhyasat* (*Vedanta-sutra*, 1.1.12), and there are things that we need to help us feel that we are valued, that we are esteemed. If we are not getting that healthy *sadhu-sanga* or if one is making *aparadhas* and not being able to experience what is available from the *sadhu-sanga*, then in time one will find the *sanga* of materialists.

By contacting non-devotees and engaging in non-devotional activities, a so-called mature devotee will fall victim to the mad elephant offense. Whatever growth has taken place is quickly uprooted by such an offense.

*Caitanya-caritamrta*, *Madhya-lila*, 19.157 purport

Many times we wonder what has happened to a certain devotee who used to be so dedicated. Now their faith has become weak and they are dancing with different kinds of Maya because the *aparadhas* have taken away the protection of Radharani. The devotional creeper is no longer receiving any nourishment.

One should be very careful to defend the creeper by fencing it in—that is, by following the regulative principles and associating with pure devotees… By associating with non-devotees, one breaks the regulative principles and is thereby lost.

*Caitanya-caritamrta*, *Madhya-lila*, 19.157 purport

## Vaisnava-aparadha Manifests in Many Forms

In the category of *vaisnava-aparadha*, there are six types of *aparadhas* listed in the *Skanda Purana*: “(1) One who kills a Vaisnava. (2) One who blasphemes a Vaisnava. (3) One who upon seeing a Vaisnava does not offer obeisances. (4) One who gets angry at a Vaisnava. (5) One who upon seeing a Vaisnava does not feel delighted. (6) One who thinks ill of a Vaisnava in the mind.” All of these constitute offenses, although there may be a gradation of seriousness. We understand the seriousness of murder and blasphemy, but even to hold a grudge or feel anger towards a Vaisnava is considered an offense. It means that if there is someone who did something wrong or created a problem, we must consciously release that person from blame. If we are holding grudges, seeing others as non-Vaisnavas, that mentality will create problems in our devotional lives. Even if someone is covered by the mode of ignorance and has wronged us by their activities, if he or she is a Vaisnava, we are at risk if we offend them.

We do not have the *yata mata, tata patha* attitude (there are as many paths as there are opinions), accepting all ideas as legitimate and not accepting accountability for our activities. No, we have to be careful, cautious about what we bring forth from each other as we are associating. There are standards. Even if someone is a new *bhakta* or *bhaktin*, we have to be careful. We must try to guide them and serve them by offering whatever advice and support is helpful. We are not to have a destructive mindset, “Oh, there he goes again—he can’t do anything right.” No. The proper mentality is, “Yes, this is someone who has come under the shelter of Mahaprabhu and wants to create a new life.”

Sometimes we have great intentions, but the *aparadha* is there. Sometimes devotees offend Jesus or offend Mohammed, but these personalities are also servants of God teaching bona fide systems of religion. Even though we do not celebrate their systems in all respects, we honor them personally. We remember that Lord Siva Himself, the greatest Vaisnava, appeared as Sankaracarya to teach Mayavada (impersonalism). We know that Lord Buddha was Visnu, and He is glorified by Jayadeva Goswami, but still we do not place Him on the altar since He taught a voidist (atheistic) philosophy. We honor Jesus Christ; Srila Prabhupada told the seminarians in Australia that we accept Jesus as our spiritual master. Nevertheless, we do not embrace all the tenets of Christianity, certainly not as it is now being presented. As we give honor to our tradition, we must have great appreciation for what others are doing, based on their respective situations and practices. We should not allow ourselves to get into an angry mood. Even when someone is doing something wrong, we do not want to succumb to Maya’s tactics or come down to a lower level of consciousness.

What can easily happen is that Maya will play an interesting trick: She inspires those who are trying to leave her sphere of influence to start fighting, so that gradually they fall back into her grips. It means that both parties will begin to lose some of their spiritual strength, their spiritual credits. It is so typical of fighting. Someone may “win” the fight, but both individuals have dirty clothes, black eyes, torn dresses, torn shirts, or torn pants. Someone may say, “Well, I really beat him up!”“Yes, but look at you; you’ve got a black eye, your clothes are all torn, and you wasted so much energy and time. You should have been doing something else.” This is what makes Maya very happy. She thinks, “These entities would normally be combating my influence and bringing others out of my grip, but now they are distracted by fighting and varieties of anxiety.”

To be upset or angry with a Vaisnava, or even to be unhappy to see a Vaisnava are also victories for Maya. Why should being unhappy to see a Vaisnava be an offense? The reason is that whether a devotee is a *kanistha*, *madhyama*, or *uttama* Vaisnava, we should be happy to see a person who is chanting the holy name. Of the billions of people on this planet, here is someone who is honoring Krishna as God and trying to make some offering to the Vaisnava society in general. To see such a person and not to feel happy, but to instead feel angry or disturbed, is another expression of an offensive attitude.

By examining these injunctions of the *Skanda Purana*, we can realize that being a devotee is something very powerful, more powerful perhaps than we can understand. Thus, we minimize others because we minimize the real meaning of being in Lord Caitanya’s army. We minimize or take for granted our own involvement. Although there are billions of people on the planet, only a small number of people really have a clear understanding of the actual identity of Krishna: the Supreme Personality of Godhead. And among these devotees, someone may have a personality that we do not appreciate. These considerations of our personalities or others’ personalities are all a part of a temporary situation we falsely identify with. We may not be comfortable with certain other devotees, but we should not let that discomfort cloud our view, any more than we should have allowed our minds to be clouded in relation to that homeless man we discussed earlier. In this phase of his existence, that derelict is in a miserable condition, and we are not obligated to eat with him, embrace him, or invite him to our temple to associate with us. At the same time, we should have the same level of concern for him as we have for others and understand that he is a devotee, more lost than most.

## Questions and Answers

**Question:** Can you discuss the distinction between feeling disturbed and holding a grudge?

**Answer:** Try to understand the meaning of the word “grudge” which goes beyond a feeling of disturbance. It is not our view to accept *any* concocted standard and consider it OK. No. We have a spiritual science with specific standards, and we are supposed to constantly examine and analyze: what is the *siddhanta* (proper conclusion), what does the *sastra* say, what is the proper activity of *sadhus*, what is the *guru’s* instruction? We have to be very scrutinizing and not neglect the structures and standards. *Parampara* means knowledge transmitted from the spiritual realm, coming down through the disciplic succession. We have definitive standards. However, there is a difference between trying to rectify an improper behavior for the benefit of the other person, or seeing that improper behavior and just becoming angry and vindictive toward a person. In the second case, *we* are creating the disturbance. Instead, we should find a way to make a positive change. We can find ways to talk to people more productively, help them serve in better ways, or see what we need to change in ourselves so that we can better facilitate our peers. We can often change others by changing something within ourselves. That will have a powerful effect on those we associate with.

We can approach people and think, “What do I need to do to help this individual get free from their bad habits? What do I need to change in myself to instill trust in the heart of a person, to inspire them to work with me more? What do I need to do to help this person be more of a team player?” We tend to think, “What does this person need to do for me so that I can trust him?” If both parties are thinking in a humble way, it is easy to have harmony. If both parties refuse to think humbly, at least *someone* should think that way. The humble person will make all of the difference. It does not matter which one of us thinks that way. “What do I need to change in myself to help improve the situation?” Whoever is thinking in that way will have a positive effect, and they are going to advance more than the person who is mired in negative considerations. Such negative persons remain at narrow limits and are not thinking outside of themselves. We need to think, “What do I need to change in my mentality so that things will be better between this person and me?” It is unhealthy to think, “What do I need to do so that this person will accept what I want? What do I need to do to get this person out of my life?” We want to forgive others; we want to help others; we want to understand others because we want to be forgiven and helped and understood ourselves. If we are not ready to reach out and help others, why should someone help us?

You would be surprised how many bums in the streets were once wealthy politicians or businessmen in previous lives. Because of so many sinful activities, they have become like vagabonds, street derelicts. Srila Prabhupada told us that an astrologer saw the destination of Nehru, who had been a prime minister in India, and an extremely materialistic individual who was attracted to western culture and insulting towards spiritualists. The astrologer saw that this leader came back in his next life as a *dog* living in Sweden. The astrologer claimed that the Swedish man had two dogs, and one of them was Nehru. Nehru was attracted to western standards and values, and apparently he took birth in one of the most materially developed countries in the world which, as a nation, is characterized by atheistic values. He came back in a plush, economically strong, and financially sound country with values opposed to spiritual life, as a *dog*.

This is an extreme example, but it indicates to us that we do want to develop deeper compassion, because we see that people are undergoing heavy karmic reactions due to offenses or other kinds of deviation. The result is that they are placed in a very unfortunate state of existence. We do not deny the fact that such an existence is wretched, but at the same time we may be creating a wretched situation for ourselves when we hold a grudge against another devotee with whom we do not agree. We do not have to agree with them and we do not have to accept what they are thinking. However, if we are holding a grudge and are not trying to understand how that devotee is feeling and thinking, then there is a tendency to think: “That person bothers me. He or she simply gives me trouble in my mental or physical space.” It is almost as if we want to expose that person and show just how ridiculous he or she is. We want revenge because we feel that he or she has caused us some inconvenience and disturbance. It is a matter of changing the polarity and not being simply absorbed in thinking about our own security, shelter, or confidence.

We need to examine what else is happening here. When the drunkard ran into our car that day, if I had been a dullard, I might have thought, “I’m the spiritual master and am busy with service, but now this wretched soul has bumped into our car. Rather we just leave him on the side of the road since we’re so busy with our important preaching work.” When we get too caught up in our obligations, even if we have a chance to do what is Krishna conscious, we may be too distracted. In one of my *Beggar* books, I wrote a prayer describing how we can sometimes be so busy serving the spiritual master that we have no time to serve the spiritual master. We are so busy with our *idea* of serving Krishna that we have no time to really serve Krishna. We are busy dovetailing, trying to do what we want to do for Krishna, and then we propose to Him, “Krishna, just accept this offering.” There are so many subtle things we have to be aware of. It’s a matter of our consciousness. Are we angry at that devotee or are we sad that the devotee is hurting themselves? Are we angry at that person, or are we disturbed because that person is cheating themselves? If we are angry at them, then there is something lacking in our depth of understanding. If we are oblivious and just do not care, then there is also something wrong with us because we are reinforcing deviant activities. We should be concerned because that person is hurting themselves, and they can hurt others when other people start following their bad example. There is a difference between that kind of disturbance and the disturbance created by anger and holding a grudge. The presence of a grudge or anger means that I am going to create an obstacle. I am going to create a way to make life more miserable for others.

**Question:** What should we do if we have angry thoughts about another devotee?

**Answer:** Be honest. If we have angry thoughts about others, we can apologize and go out of our way to do good things for those people. If others seem to have more trouble with us, then we should go out of our way to make some change in our behavior or to make a change in them by doing some good things in the relationship. Even if you cannot change them, at least you can stop yourself from holding on to grudges or anger. The mind is definitely wicked, and it often behaves independently. We cannot always control all the things the mind goes through, but we do have the choice of how we respond. Sometimes we may lose our composure. Now that we have lost it, what will we do to try to make amends? We see an activity that seems improper to us and we attempt to speak up, but then we realize that we should not have said what we said. It can happen—no problem. The only question that remains is: What do we do after that?

Do we continue? Do we consider it to be insignificant? Do we consider it someone else’s fault? Do we start planning to make life more difficult for that person? Do we consider how we can understand things differently, so it will not cause a person to misunderstand? Can we appreciate what that individual is undergoing that may cause them to act in a way that is seemingly not proper? In many cases there is some deeper issue that people are struggling with when they act in an improper way in their devotional environment. Sometimes when we think along these lines, we get more insight into how to help that person.

A final example: the family. Parents will see that some of their children’s behavior can disturb them. It does not mean that they hate their children, but they do have a duty to offer guidance and to be sensitive to their children’s situation. A parent may chastise or instruct, but still they are to see children as *jivas* who are under their care and who need help. They *must* figure out a way to help them; otherwise, it is considered to be a lack of proper parenting on their part. A child is a parent’s responsibility! The application of this principle comes in our interactions with our colleagues, *sankirtana* leaders, or devotees with whom we clean, cook, or serve. There are bound to be some issues. Personality conflicts can come up in different ways. When, however, there is anger or a grudge, there is a tendency to overreact to a situation. And because it is an overreaction, we have to make amends. Some fault is definitely present; we are not denying that. We are looking at subtle things that can cause tremendous fall and stagnation. It is like a cancer, and it is one of Maya’s very special tricks: getting devotees distracted by causing them to fight with each other or to have conflicts where all parties, even the atmosphere itself, gradually gets bogged down. The offenses create an atmosphere where the spiritual immune system of everyone gets weaker. People start taking things for granted, and then they begin to even honor the words and mindsets that are connected with offenses because they do not think that such activities constitute a serious issue.

Every devotee is actually special, and the more that we appreciate them, seeing ourselves as the servant of the servant, we will try to do our service nicely and in a way that encourages someone else. We must remember that each devotee is our master. Srila Prabhupada instructed us to call each other *prabhu*. By thinking and acting like that, Krishna will constantly send help for us in our own spiritual life. You will see that those devotees who are the most wonderful *sadhus* have the most caring mood. It is an integral part of their character because they really are more Krishna conscious. They are in Krishna consciousness constantly, and they have uniquely creative ways to help others. Such elevated devotees know how to inspire each person to come up to a higher standard by lovingly approaching and helping them feel that they are special and can make a difference.

## 2. Offenses Against Devotees

## Authentic and Inauthentic Methods of Service

In most endeavors there are authentic methods, and inauthentic methods that are bogus, counterfeit, or hypocritical. We know that there is genuine currency: the rand, pound, dollar, mark, etc., but there is also counterfeit currency. Although there are genuine lawyers, doctors, and members of other professions, there are also those professing expertise who appear to be representing a particular profession, but who are in fact neither competent nor genuine. Sometimes they are outright frauds. There are certain practices that encourage healthy growth, healthy professions, and healthy service. There are responsible, accountable people, who are the bona fide practitioners of professions, but there are also bogus practitioners whose behavior is quite the converse, who are irresponsible, and who offer no accountability for their actions.

In the spiritual line, we have a parallel dilemma. There are bona fide religious ideas and practitioners, but also inauthentic “religious” ideas and practitioners, which pose a big threat. Certain people are misusing religion and are creating all kinds of havoc, fragmentation, and division, going against the principles of real *dharma*: *dharmah projjhita-kaitavo ’tra paramo nirmatsaranam satam*. The *Srimad-Bhagavatam* completely rejects all religious activities that are materially motivated. Srila Prabhupada calls such so-called religion (*kaitava-dharma*)—cheating religion.

Therefore, Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura discusses the phenomena of *adharma*, or irreligious practices and philosophies that are opposed to the principles of religion and to God. He identifies five general categories of *adharma*; *vidharma*, *para-dharma*, *upadharma*, *dharmabhasa*, and *chala-dharma*. In all of these cases, speculation, conjecture, and cheating take the place of *dharma*, they create doubt, cynicism, and fear, and they reduce the edification that *dharma* is meant to provide. *Aparadha* is a great catalyst for *adharma* to proliferate. When we are not engaged in offenses, our spiritual life improves wonderfully and continuously, always accelerating. When offenses are present (especially *vaisnava-aparadha*), the development of *bhakti* becomes corrupted and, consequently, our spiritual development becomes perverted. It can then easily latch onto some form of *adharma* or even mutate into a new form. This will be graphically illustrated later in this section.

## Offending the Holy Name and the Holy Dhamas

There are also serious offenses committed when we chant the holy name in the wrong consciousness. We must chant in the proper consciousness, and in the proper mood to get the desired effect. Otherwise, the *nama-ausadhi*, the medicine of the holy name, will not have its beautiful effect.

*enechi ausadhi maya nasibaro lagi’*  
*hari-nama maha-mantra lao tumi magi’*

**Srila Bhaktivinoda has stated the order of Lord Gauranga: “I have brought the medicine for destroying the illusion of** ***maya*****. Now pray for this** ***hari-nama maha-mantra*** **and take it.”** ***Arunodaya-kirtana*****, (part two, verse four)**

By not taking the medicine according to prescription, it can either be ineffective or destructive. Many problems are created due to the improper mixture of medicines or taking too high a dose.

There is another kind of *aparadha*: *tirtha-aparadha* or *dhama-aparadha*. These are offenses that one may commit when visiting sacred places. *Tirthas*, pilgrimage places, are surcharged with the special pastimes of the Lord and of his exalted agents. They are further intensified with the consciousness of people who come there in a prayerful state, in a mood of surrender. Such places are not ordinary environments. In the case of Vrndavana and Mayapura, these two *dhamas* are replicas of the spiritual world and are as worshipable as Krishna Himself. Srila Prabhupada writes in the *Nectar of Devotion*, citing the *Padma Purana*: “Actually, if someone goes to Vrndavana, he will immediately feel separation from Krishna, Who performed such nice activities when He was present there.” In Srila Rupa Gosvami’s book, *Sri Mathura-mahatmya*, he cites verse after verse which describes the transcendental effects of visiting the Mathura-mandala. In the *Padma Purana* it is also stated, “To travel to different places of pilgrimage means to attain emancipation from material bondage. This emancipation, however, is not the highest perfectional stage. After attaining this liberated stage, one has to become engaged in devotional service to the Lord. After attainment of the *brahma-bhuta* \[liberation\] stage, one can further advance to engagement in devotional service. So this attainment of transcendental loving devotional service to the Lord is the goal of life, and it can be achieved very easily for one who lives in Mathura-mandala even for a few seconds.”

It is further said, “Who is that person who will not agree to worship the land of Mathura? Mathura can deliver all the desires and ambitions of the fruitive workers and of the salvationists, who desire to become one with the Supreme Brahman. Certainly Mathura will deliver the desires of the devotees, who simply aspire to be engaged in the devotional service of the Lord.” It is further stated, “How wonderful it is that simply by residing in Mathura even for one day, one can achieve a transcendental loving attitude toward the Supreme Personality of Godhead! This land of Mathura must be more glorious than Vaikuntha-dhama, the kingdom of God.” There are hundreds of verses and many books compiled by the Gosvamis about the glories of the *dhamas*.

Such *dhamas* or *tirthas* in their original spiritual glory are not perceived by the external senses, but are revealed as is described in *Brahma-samhita* by devotion:

*premanjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilocanena*  
*santah sadaiva hrdayesu vilokayanti*  
*yam syamasundaram acintya-guna-svarupam*  
*govindam adi-purusam tam aham bhajami*  
*Brahma-samhita, 5.38*

By the salve of love one’s spiritual eyes are awakened, and the *dhama* can be revealed in all its spiritual glories. One who executes *bhakti* in these places can make very rapid advancement. However, by committing offenses, one can rapidly create disaster. The *dhamas* are places where one can advance more quickly because of the nature of the environment—intense remembrance of the Lord. In the *Padma Purana* it is stated,

The Lord may offer liberation or material happiness to a devotee, but after some devotional service has been executed, particularly in Mathura during the month of Karttika (October/November), the devotees want only to attain pure devotional service to the Lord.

*Nectar of Devotion*, Chapter 12

Srila Prabhupada explains,

The purport is that the Lord does not award devotional service to ordinary persons who are not serious about it. But even such unserious persons who execute devotional service according to the regulative principles during the month of Karttika, and within the jurisdiction of Mathura in India, are very easily awarded the Lord’s personal service.

*Nectar of Devotion*, Chapter 12

The *dhamas* are also places where we can hurt ourselves by behaving improperly. The benefits are amplified, and the negative effects are also amplified if one commits offenses to or in those places. There are holy places in all the different religious systems. For the Muslims there is Mecca, for the Christians there is Jerusalem, and there are many holy places, rivers, and shrines where devoted souls go on pilgrimage. We have Vrndavana, Mayapura, Mathura, Hardwar, Jagannatha Puri, and so many other powerful holy places. We also have our centers which are designed for concentrated worship, and they should be *tirthas*. The mosque, the church, or the temple are places which should motivate the worshipper to serve God better, to maintain vows, and to make his or her life an expression of the theology.

Furthermore, the advanced devotee is also a *tirtha*:

*bhavad-vidha bhagavatas*  
*tirtha-bhutah svayam vibho*  
*tirthi-kurvanti tirthani*  
*svantah-sthena gadabhrta*

**My Lord, devotees like your good self are verily holy places personified. Because you carry the Personality of Godhead within your heart, you turn all places into places of pilgrimage.** ***Srimad-Bhagavatam*****, 1.13.10**

Advaitacarya told Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, *tumi yahan, sei vrndavana*: “Wherever You are, that is Vrndavana” (*Caitanya-caritamrta*, *Madhya-lila*, 3.33).

There are so many *aparadhas*: *nama-aparadha* (offenses to the holy name), *dhama-aparadha* (offenses to the holy places), *seva-aparadha* (offenses that are made while rendering service), *vaisnava-aparadha* (offenses to devotees) and *guru-aparadha* (offenses to the *guru*). With all of these recipes for disaster, one can become overwhelmed. It seems complex; there are so many ways we can make mistakes in Krishna consciousness. However, it is not necessary for an aspiring Vaisnava to spend so much time constantly worrying about offenses. The key to success is to be compassionate, humble, appreciative, and grateful for Krishna’s mercy, and then the Lord in the heart will keep us from making offenses. When we lose humility and compassion, not only will we be in trouble, but it is guaranteed that we will commit offenses.

## Women Gurus in Vaisnava History

I would like to share two or three short stories, giving some emphasis on the dynamics of offenses, what can happen as a result, and also how to avoid offenses. Sometimes people inquire from us as Hare Krishnas, “I see that you people have *gurus*, but they all seem to be men. Where are the women spiritual masters?” We talk about the fact that we are not these bodies and that everyone is an eternal soul; we mention the nonsectarian approach to religious matters, without regard to race, ethnicity, gender, etc.; we may further explain that we all serve the same God, and that there is ultimately one God, and yet, there seems to be so much emphasis on bodily consciousness. We see in our communities gender consciousness: the men in one place and the women in the other along with other divisions based on such. I am giving this lecture at present, a man. Where are the women giving lectures? Yes, it is a fact that there seems to be a dominance of men, because as you know we have our big egos. In almost every sphere of activity you find that men usually seem to take the lead. However, there is the power of the mother or the woman in the background. After all, where do the men come from but out of the wombs of women.

We find that in most places in the world that women live much longer than the men. In much of the west, in America, and several places in Europe, women live on average at least six to seven years longer than men. In so many ways they actually outshine men. Look at the very hardcore facts of science and biology: Their perception of sound is keener than the man’s; the sense of sight is sharper; their ability to use both sides of the brain is more prominent. Some of the articles in various journals list up to ten different areas of physical/sensual superiority possessed by women. At least from a scientific point of view, women do live longer and practically every sense is stronger. Women can endure more pain, are sexually more powerful—so many different aspects. Men may like to think differently about these topics, but the evidence is against them. What does this indicate? In Vedic culture women are the carriers of the culture. If a woman is surrounded and dominated by materialistic culture, the culture of sense gratification, who is going to be more prominent? Women! That means that when women are absorbed in the modes of material nature, they will be more dominant, more dominant in the context of illusion. If, however, they are able to break their attraction for the normal scheme of things, then they also can use that same power to become more spiritual. In Kali-yuga the majority of the people are captured by attraction to sense gratification, men as well as women. It is stated in the *Skanda Purana*, *kalau sudra sambhavah*: “In the age of Kali everyone is born a *sudra*.”

*prayenalpayusah sabhya*  
*kalav asmin yuge janah*  
*mandah sumanda matayo*  
*manda-bhagya hy upadrutah*

**O learned one, in this iron age of Kali men (or the public) have short lives. They are quarrelsome, lazy, misguided, unlucky and, above all, always disturbed.** ***Srimad-Bhagavatam*****, 1.1.10**

From the material perspective, the more power one has, the more one tends to use it for sense gratification. Thus, in Vedic culture women are asked to tune themselves down a bit and are sent the following message: “Sisters, mothers, use your power in a merciful way. Be a little discrete; cover your head and dress in a way not to expose yourself.” The implied message is: “Be merciful to the men because of your superior power!” It can be understood in this way. This is a big topic necessitating further discussion, but in this discussion we want to look closer at the issue of women *gurus*.

There are women *gurus* in our tradition although we don’t see them so often in today’s society. There are so many things we don’t see in today’s society, which is full of chaos and confusion. There are the stories of Jahnava Devi and Krishna Priya Thakurani, two women *gurus* in our Gaudiya Vaisnava line, but we should note that there are several other very powerful women like Ganga-mata Devi. Jahnava Devi was the wife of none other than Lord Nityananda. There was a time in Vaisnava history when she was so prominent and powerful that Vaisnavas from all over India were eager to have her *darsana*, to get blessings from her, and to hear her explain philosophical matters. She had such power in bringing various groups of Vaisnavas together at a very critical time in Vaisnava history. Disagreements had begun to arise among the followers of Lord Caitanya about how to present Krishna consciousness. She was depended upon to mediate such disputes.

Sri Narahari Cakravarti narrates two stories. Once she went to Vrndavana to help organize the Vaisnavas, to give a discourse, and to give her blessings. There was a group of so-called spiritualists who were followers of Chandi or Durga, and they noticed that wherever she would go many *sadhus* (mostly men) were following her. The *sadhus* expressed such reverence for her. The followers of Durga thought, “What is this nonsense? Who are these Vaisnavas and who is this woman receiving their worship? Why are they not serving Chandi?” They were furious because they thought that everyone should think as they did: that the worship of Durga was the real *seva* (service) and *puja* (worship) should be performed for her. These angry followers went to the deity of Durga in the temple, and they prayed and made a vow to murder those followers of Jahnava Devi who appeared to be committing blasphemy by neglecting Durga and worshiping Jahnava Devi.

Party spirit and sectarianism often predominate. People who imagine themselves religious are caught up in some type of inhumanity to humankind, or some kind of abuse and exploitation. They hide behind religious sentiments while they carry out their own agenda, which is generally materially motivated. Their sectarian views are fueled by ulterior motives. Often these so-called religionists are not religious at all, but are wicked and cruel people who will use any pretext to achieve their ends. They may utilize commerce, politics, educational institutes, or anything else that they feel can help them to propagate their own particular sovereign, surreptitious plan. Using religion to achieve some materially motivated goal is not uncommon. In fact, this *kaitava-dharma* (cheating religion) has become the norm. Materially motivated people use whatever they can to gain leverage for their ideas. Since they are aware of the power of religious ideas and how religion motivates people, they take advantage.

To be honest, the most prominent, cruel, and sinful people are those empowered for destruction and who often take shelter in religious institutions. Your most powerful soldiers or your most powerful warriors are those who engage in espionage. They may not only be good at fighting, but they are very tricky and know how to appear to be something that they are not. One of the most devastating tactics is to create false appearances, giving the impression of authenticity. There are those in every religion who come to destabilize, to fragment, and to get people who have tried to turn to a theistic lifestyle to literally hate God. Initially such agents may try the overt method, preaching against genuine religion, without even attempting to mask their intentions. Those who are not affected by this tactic are more easily deluded when such people join their institutions, become a part of the fellowship or congregation, and then find ways to destabilize the environment so that those who were trying to surrender will flee. Some of the most expert people in any kind of struggle are those who are trained to go inside of the enemy camp, create havoc, and not be noticed.

These followers of Durga were not as demonic as they initially appeared to be; however, they did not understand real *dharma*, certainly not *sanatana-dharma*, eternal religion. They were self-righteous. They had actually made plans to kill the devoted worshipers of Jahnava Devi, the Vaisnavas, and they were going to kill her as well. They imagined that Durga would be pleased and bless them. This is how dysfunctional their thinking was. The night before the day they were planning to carry out these heinous offenses, each one of them had a dream in which Durga was shouting at them, threatening them, and exclaiming just how foolish they were. She promised to deal with them very seriously if they in any way troubled such great servants of her own master. She revealed the identity of Jahnava Devi as the eternal consort of the Lord to the foolish fanatics. After having this dramatic encounter with Durga Devi, the shaken followers awoke. They were so overwhelmed with fear and remorse that they immediately begged forgiveness and took shelter of Jahnava Devi.

Narahari tells another story about Jahnava Devi. There was another group of worshipers who were robbers by profession. Jahnava Devi was so powerful and prominent that wherever she would go there would be a large crowd of followers. When the dacoits observed this entourage, they were contemplating a rich harvest of all the wealth, the jewelry, gold, silver, diamonds and bangles that would be available from this group. They thought, “Yes, we will attack these people and make off with a good haul tonight. There are so many of them. We will rob them all!” And so they made plans to attack the followers of Jahnava Devi. A strange thing happened while Jahnava and her group rested. The robbers had ascertained that the opportune moment to accost them was at night. The plan was to rush in, surround the area, and steal as much as possible, as quickly as possible. The head robber gave the signal, and the gang of dacoits charged in. This attempt went on for hours, running and rushing, and yet, somehow, mystically, they were never able to come to the resting place of Mother Jahnava and her followers. They continued to run forward, but the distance between the two parties increased. They were never able to reach them, even though it wasn’t far away. Finally they realized the circumstances were unusual, extraordinary. “There is something very special about these people. Why are we are not able to take advantage of them?” The Lord in their hearts gave them the realization that they had made a serious mistake. They realized that Jahnava Devi wasn’t an ordinary person, that even among saints she was extraordinary, and she surely wasn’t someone who they should offend. They begged forgiveness and tried to join her entourage. One way to become free from an offense is to approach the offended person and sincerely take up their service.

Let me tell you one other short story about Krishna Priya Thakurani. Lord Caitanya gave Ragunatha dasa Gosvami a Govardhana-sila, a very special form of Krishna to worship. Ragunatha dasa Gosvami later gave that same *sila* that he had worshipped for many years to the great devotee Mukunda datta. Later Mukunda datta himself had become quite ill. There were a couple of times when Krishna Priya Thakurani took care of him as if he were her son, and before he left his body, he gave her the very same *sila*. This Deity was a very powerful expansion and manifestation of Krishna’s mercy and was, of course, extremely blessed because He had been passed down from Lord Caitanya to Raghunatha to Mukunda datta to Krishna Priya. This gives us some indication of just how special, rare, and powerful she was, having been blessed by other great personalities.

Once there was a great scholar and devotee called Rupa Kaviraja, who had also been blessed by Mukunda datta and other great, pure, and advanced devotees. He was a powerful *pandita* and *sastri*. The Vaisnavas were enthralled when he would explain the philosophy of Krishna consciousness. One day he was seated in a massive assembly, sitting on the seat of Vyasa, and speaking *Srimad-Bhagavatam*. The assembly was listening to his discourse with rapt attention. As he was beginning to explain some profound and intricate points regarding the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, Krishna Priya Thakurani came into the assembly. As she entered, the devotees offered respects to her because they recognized her as an exalted Vaisnava. Rupa Kaviraja, however, didn’t even seem to notice or appreciate her appearance. To the contrary, he was disturbed and, ignoring her, threw himself more vigorously into his presentation. He tried to behave as though he was oblivious to her presence. The Thakurani was sitting rather close by, and she was listening to him intently and gazing at him. While hearing Rupa Kaviraja speak, simultaneously her mouth was moving, chanting, “Hare Krishna Hare Krishna Krishna Krishna Hare Hare/ Hare Rama Hare Rama Rama Rama Hare Hare.” This chanting was soft but constant.

Rupa Kaviraja became very irritated and, at one point, he glanced over to her and said, “How can you do two things at the same time? I am speaking from the *Bhagavatam*, and it should be honored. We are speaking about the deep knowledge of the *sastra* and you are moving your mouth and chanting!” She very humbly replied, “I apologize, but this is my habit.” The holy names spontaneously and automatically flowed from her. She was so absorbed in Krishna consciousness that, without even being aware of it, the holy name was vibrating throughout her whole being, causing her mouth to move. The *acaryas* explain that Rupa Kaviraja received her reply with scorn and glanced at her disapprovingly thinking, “You are merely a sentimental woman.” He then proceeded to give the rest of his discourse.

The *acaryas* explain that as soon as he thought in such an offensive manner and indulged in scornful behavior toward the great Krishna Priya Thakurani, there was *radha-sunya* or *aparadha*, the absence of Srimati Radharani’s blessings. This incident brings to mind the killing of Romaharsana, the disciple of Vyasa, who failed to offer proper respect to Lord Balarama when He entered the assembly of sages at Naimisaranya. Srila Prabhupada comments on this incident in *Krsna* book, chapter 78:

When a person is seated on the *vyasasana*, he does not generally have to stand to receive a particular person entering the assembly, but in this case the situation was different because Lord Baladeva is not an ordinary human being. Therefore, although Romaharsana Suta was voted to the *vyasasana* by all the *brahmanas*, he should have followed the behavior of other learned sages and *brahmanas* present and should have known that Lord Balarama is the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Respects are always due Him, even though such respects can be avoided in the case of any ordinary man.

Srila Prabhupada makes the point that Romaharsana’s behavior revealed his disqualification, despite his vast learning and exalted position, classifying his disease as a “deficiency of realization” and a “puffed up” attitude.

Rupa Kaviraja was so elevated that he was already experiencing the initial stages of *prema*. He was situated in *bhava*, a very high absorption of *bhava*. He was at the doorway or entering the initial domain of *prema*, love of God. He had extensive knowledge of the scriptures, but due to his offense to an exalted devotee who constantly chanted out of ecstasy, he later began to propagate the peculiar idea that only renunciates could be *gurus*. Even though Lord Nityananda, Advaitacarya, Srinivasa, and so many other exalted personalities were *gurus* who were not in the renounced order, Rupa Kaviraja insisted that only renunciates could be *gurus*. He also claimed that *sravanam* and *kirtanam*, hearing and chanting about the glories of the Lord, are not necessary. The only necessity was to engage in *smaranam* (remembrance). He considered other practices unnecessary. Due to his offense he unwittingly began to preach a bogus philosophy. His whole consciousness changed, and even though he had achieved such a high level of realization and deep understanding, due to the *aparadha* his spiritual immune system became shattered, and he became a *sahajiya* (one who imports mundane or fallacious ideas into the spiritual process).

It took a while before his falldown was really understood, because he appeared to know so much and he had such a large following. For quite some time people continued to listen to him and follow his instructions. However, it soon became obvious to his followers that he had deviated philosophically. This was shocking because when a devotee is bona fide or authentic, or has been authentic, one expects to receive some spiritual insight. Initially a devotee who has been offensive may seem all right, but as time elapses and that devotee becomes increasingly strange, his or her improper behavior becomes clear to everyone. The situation gradually grew worse. Sometimes when we are wrong and don’t want to admit it, we may go overboard in trying to justify our situation. Rupa Kaviraja did just that, eventually becoming very ill from leprosy and dying as a result. It wasn’t even an ordinary death, or an ordinary transition. Once he left his body he was not able to get another body right away. He became a ghost. And do you know what his service was? His service was to haunt those persons who offended Vaisnavas! This was his *prayascitta* (atonement) so that he might eventually be forgiven and was also his way of expressing remorse for his mistake. His duty was to harass those who were offending Vaisnavas so as to be freed of some of his offenses. He had come from the position of practically being an unalloyed lover of Krishna, absorbed on the platform of *bhava*, to a position of deviation, complicity with Maya, duality, and suffering. All this took place, even after coming so close to the perfectional stage!

It is so sad. For example, a person may work very hard at a university to get a master’s or doctor’s degree. Perhaps they only have a little more work to finish, one paper that will finalize the degrees, but then something drastic happens that ruins everything. Perhaps some cheating was involved which was discovered and made public. The instructor finds out and then tells the head of the department. Upheaval results and the authorities want the cheater out of the university. He leaves in disgrace, after coming so close. He tries to get into another school, but now the disgrace is on record, and the chance for a bright career is lost. He now works in a parking lot, parking cars. A former associate says, “You know I went to school with him. He was second in the class and could have been a very powerful such and such. Now he is parking cars.”

These two stories we told above are examples of women *gurus*. If anyone asks you about the *gurus* who are women in our movement, you can point out these two examples. And when they ask, “Well, where are the present ones?” You can reply, “Well, I am going to be a *guru*!” Some of you become *gurus* and deal with these guys! It is important that you men realize that women live longer. Most men, if they are lucky, have a wife to take care of them during the last days of their lives. It can be generally observed that there are many women who are widows at the pilgrimage places. Therefore, some of you who have treated your wives and partners badly should remember that you are going to be dependent on that woman during the last years of your life! She is going to outlive you, and she is going to be with you during the last years or last months. She will remember what you used to do, so be careful, men, because what goes around comes around.

One can be elevated almost to the perfectional stage, and then suffer such a fall. This is how dangerous the committing of *aparadha* is. We must be extremely careful. The other day we were giving some scriptural analysis and evaluation of what can happen. *Aparadha* (*radha-sunya*) is one of the greatest dangers for the aspiring devotee. The beauty is that if we can serve and relate in a healthy way, then spiritual endeavor becomes most glorious because one’s spiritual life will continue to grow and develop. Anything that is very powerful, used in the right way, is of tremendous benefit. If we use the powerful practices of *bhakti-yoga* in an improper way, the ramifications and difficulties will simply increase. Krishna consciousness is like that. It is extremely powerful and scientific, and therefore it must be practiced with care. Otherwise, an intelligent, spiritually-minded, and learned person like Rupa Kaviraja who achieved such heights later deviated and eventually became a ghost. Don’t become a ghost! Be careful!

## Questions and Answers

**Question:** Sometimes we make an offense, but we haven’t done it intentionally. We may then go on for a long period of time wondering if we have done something wrong, but only after some time do we begin to suffer. What are the signs that will arise in our consciousness which will indicate that we have made an offense?

**Answer:** We begin to feel an internal shift although we can try to deny it. When we have money that we put in the bank, that money builds up. At other times we withdraw it until one day we realize that we are overdrawn and have no money left. Sometimes our devotional lives unfold in this way. A person may be coasting from previous devotional credits and continue unhampered for the moment, but at some point he or she ends up bankrupt. When that happens, it means we have reached a crisis. We may ask, “Why does Krishna allow us to get to that point where we are in crisis or where we lose our service, lose our position, lose our chance to be able to associate?” Krishna is constantly accommodating our desires. These desires then produce some kind of concomitant response, but not always as we expect. As we are more desperate for association, Krishna will gradually arrange for us to have more association. As we are desperate to do the right thing, Krishna will show us how to accomplish it. However, we must ask ourselves if we do in fact act upon it when the Lord does show us which route to take. If we don’t act, then the consequences build up.

There is never a time that Krishna leaves a devotee unprotected or unguarded. We are the ones who remove ourselves from His protection. A good mother or father never leaves the child in a dangerous state, but sometimes children have their own idea and are not ready to accept the mercy; therefore, they put themselves outside of the offered protection. Consequences will be there. When we experience calamities in our spiritual lives, such calamities have to do with our own deficiencies: lack of attentiveness to the scriptures, not honoring *sadhus,* or not trying to achieve blessings from the *guru*. If we are attentive to scripture, offer honor to *sadhus*, and get the blessings of the spiritual master, consistent progress will take place.

## 3. Sin and Aparadha

## Understanding What to Accept and Avoid

We want to examine more closely what really helps to accelerate our devotion, as well as the main factors that stagnate our devotional service. There are specific practices that water the devotional creeper and allow it to flourish, and there are certain activities that cause procrastination and stagnation. We must realize the importance of *sraddha* or faith. When we are attempting to attain a goal we must have conviction that the goal is accessible or attainable. The more we are convinced, the more zeal and enthusiasm we will have in trying to make our connection with Krishna. By seeing those who have attained the goal, the conviction will come that we can also experience the same result. The more we are conscious of the negative consequences which arise from improper practice, the more we run toward the positive activities of *bhakti*. Our advancement is not only based on awareness and remembrance of the goal, although that is certainly the role of healthy association (we remind each other). However, while we are trying to remain aware of where we are going, we must also keep in mind what we should avoid and what it would mean if we do not attain the goal. This dual approach (accepting what is favorable and rejecting what is unfavorable) is the healthy way to achieve Krishna consciousness.

*anukulyasya sankalpah*  
*pratikulyasya varjanam*  
*raksisyatiti visvaso*  
*goptrtve varanam tatha*  
*atma-niksepa-karpanye*  
*sad-vidha saranagatih*

**The six divisions of surrender are the acceptance of those things favorable to devotional service, the rejection of unfavorable things, the conviction that Krishna will give protection, the acceptance of the Lord as one’s guardian or master, full self-surrender and humility.** ***Hari-bhakti-vilasa*****,** **11.147, cited in** ***Caitanya-caritamrta*****,** ***Madhya-lila*****,** **22.100**

If we have faith, determination, and enthusiasm, accompanied by the realization of how wonderful it will be when we attain Krishna consciousness and simultaneously what suffering will be there if the goal is not attained, then we can make steady progress.

We understand that when a student passes the proper courses and exams at a law school for example, he or she can then practice law. The student will have a degree, and a whole scheme of professional opportunities based on that accomplishment. If students do not pass their tests, it then means that they put in so many years and so much money, but still came out unsuccessful. It means that they lost money and the chance for certification; consequently, they cannot function in that field without the degree. Being aware of the different consequences, students become enthused about making a sacrifice and simultaneously become fearful of a failure resulting from a lack of effort. Although there is no loss or diminution in the execution of devotional service, devotees should still have an awareness of both sides of the process: the goal, its practice, and keys to success; and the pitfalls and their consequences. Both the positive and negative features operate as a method of bringing us closer to the *siddhanta* (proper conclusion) and the goal of developing love of God.

There is quite a gamut of offenses by which people are retarded in their spiritual development. We have also explained that our focus is not to study every single offense and understand all of the details and the dynamics, but rather we have to be humble, honest, and determined, really desiring to serve the Lord. Krishna will constantly help to show us the way. Where we allow Maya to cover us, it is to that degree that our determination is revealed as not so genuine. There is never a time that Maya is stronger than Krishna. If our devotion and determination is sufficiently strong, then although there may be rough periods, there will always be recuperation and progress. In New Vrndavana in 1972 Srila Prabhupada was asked the question, “Why is Maya so strong if our purpose is to be with God?” Srila Prabhupada immediately replied, “Your purpose is not strong.”

Our devotional service is not so much vertical in development. It is vertical in cases when an entity picks up from a previous life, but once having regained and reawakened his or her previous spiritual development, progress may come more slowly. There are times when we have to work things out on a certain level or plateau and then progress a little more. At other times we go through periods in life when we are really unfolding fast. Sometimes there is *prarabdha-karma* or *karma* from the past that we are now facing. Some reactions have not quite caught up with us, but then they gradually do. These are minimized by devotional service, but still some obstacles may be before us. Srila Prabhupada gives the example of the fan to explain residual *karma*. By practice of devotional service, the engine of fruitive activity is shut down, and the plug of the fan is pulled. Still, there is some momentum of the turning blades, and some suffering may come our way. This is further discussed in Chapter One of the *Nectar of Devotion*:

*aprarabdha-phalam papam*  
*kutam bijam phalonmukham*  
*kramenaiva praliyeta*  
*visnu-bhakti-ratatmanam*

**There are different stages of dormant reactions to sinful activities to be observed in a sinful life. Sinful reactions may be just waiting to take effect (*****phalonmukha*****), reactions may be still further dormant (*****kuta*****), or the reactions may be in a seedlike state (*****bija*****). In any case, all types of sinful reactions are vanquished one after another if a person engages in the devotional service of Lord Visnu.** ***Padma Purana***

Thus, there are times we experience some negative *karma* or fallout from the past, and then, by Krishna’s grace, we move through that period.

## What Devastates Our Spiritual Lives the Most?

What is more serious—a sin or an *aparadha*? Think about it. They’re actually different. An *aparadha* is more serious because it is not as easy to rectify as a sinful activity. The sin means engagement in improper or unhealthy activities, and may only involve us personally. It may also be against someone else or involve someone else, but essentially a sin is an assault on morality and on ordinary *dharma* or piety. It involves a breach in ethics, truthfulness, righteousness, cleanliness, etc. But an *aparadha* is an assault on *sanatana-dharma*, entering the domain of spirituality or *bhakti* and sabotaging the attempt for transcendence. *Aparadhas* have grave consequences. A sin can be forgiven by paying the price of undergoing rectification or atonement. In material dealings, if we break the laws of society or an institution, we have to pay a fine or suffer a penalty. Rectifying sinful behavior can be an impersonal affair. We can break a law and then pay a fine, go to jail, or suffer another consequence that has nothing to do with the sin or the particular person who was transgressed against. If we rob someone and then go to jail after having been caught, we are dealt with by institutions and laws. Usually there is no necessity of any interaction with the person sinned against. We serve our time, pay our fine, and then it’s over. We don’t have to be forgiven for the crime or be forgiven by an individual. It’s not necessary. Sometimes it may be so for more serious sins or crimes. With an *aparadha*, it is more serious because it is *radha-sunya*. When we become divested of the mercy of Srimati Radharani, it has a direct effect on our ability to execute devotional service and to progress in Krishna consciousness.

Let us see what some of our *acaryas* have to say in relationship to sin (*papa*) and offenses (*aparadha*). Srila Gour Govinda Maharaja explains:

One should understand that there is a difference between sinful activity and offense. One may be a greatly sinful person who has committed many sinful activities in this life and previous lives, but all of these sinful reactions are like *dhuli-lepa*, layers of dust. They can be washed off very easily. But *aparadhas* are like *vajra-lepa*, very strong layers like a thunderbolt. They cannot be washed off. This is the difference. The reaction to sinful activities can be easily washed off. If you merely chant Hare Krishna and attain the stage of *namabhasa*, then karmic reactions are washed away. The example is Ajamila. He was a very sinful person. Chitragupta, who records all of the sinful activities of the conditioned souls, had made a very big book for him. When the Yamadutas came to take him, Ajamila uttered the name of Narayana. He was only calling his son, yet he attained the stage of *namabhasa* and his sins were completely washed off, finished…But *aparadha* is a layer like a thunderbolt.

Sin is like a layer of dust which is very easily washed off, but *aparadha* is like a thunderbolt. When Durvasa Muni offended Ambarisa Maharaja and approached Krishna for shelter, Krishna explained that He could not help him. Durvasa Muni had to be forgiven by Ambarisa Maharaja. If the Supreme Lord says He cannot wash it off, then who can? Only the Vaisnava at whose feet one has committed the *aparadha* can excuse the offender. Mahaprabhu instructs us in the *Caitanya-bhagavata* that anyone who blasphemes Krishna’s dear devotees is a great offender. If he offers food to Krishna, Krishna never accepts it. His tongue will never utter Krishna’s holy name and sometimes life after life he is put into hell and will suffer there. He will lose all of his education, the results of his *tapasya*, *sadhana*, and anything else he has acquired. In *Rupa-siksa* (*Caitanya-caritamrta*, *Madhya-lila*, Chapter 19), it is described that even if a very spiritually powerful person whose *bhakti-lata* (devotional creeper) has climbed up to the feet of Krishna commits offenses, everything can be finished.

The story of Rupa Kaviraja who offended Krishna-priya Thakurani is a very good example of the above principle. He was an elevated devotee who had come all the way to the platform of *bhava*, and was standing at the threshold of *prema*. Due to an offense he not only lost his intelligence, but he became a *sahajiya*, started speaking *apasiddhanta* (wrong conclusions), left his body, and became a ghost.

## Witnessing the Effects of Aparadha

We are sharing these kinds of stories so that all of you will execute your devotional service more carefully. We must also understand why devotees who have been doing well get weak and then fall. Otherwise we might doubt the process, thinking that something is wrong with *bhakti-yoga* or that it is too difficult. If someone highly placed can fall, we may think “What is the hope for us?” Lots of hope! Maya is an equal opportunity employer! As long as someone is eager to be employed by Maya, Maya will offer all kinds of opportunities that are tailored for that person. But if an individual maintains a sense of humility and compassion, constantly focusing on *sastra*, honoring *sadhus*, and serving the *guru*, then there is no question of losing the shelter of Radha and Krishna. As we go on serving under protection, there is constant acceleration and elevation. One will maintain *ruci* (taste) and there will be longevity in service. Otherwise, the unfortunate downfall and consequential suffering that happened to Rupa Kaviraja, Durvasa Muni, and hundreds of others can happen to us.

The very powerful *yogi* and mystic Saubhari Muni had undergone tremendous austerity, but he made an offense to Garuda, the Lord’s personal carrier. Although he had been performing austerities underwater in a river, he saw two fish copulating and his mind became totally bewildered as a result of offending Garuda. Previously he had regularly observed the fish without agitation or distraction. After he made this offense to a great Vaisnava, the *cit sakti* (spiritual potency) was withdrawn and he fell down. Saubhari Muni, after being agitated by the mating fish, now wanted to change his lifestyle. He wanted to pursue sex life. As a powerful *yogi*, he was quite capable of fulfilling his desires because he had *sakti*, mystic power. The sage went to the king and requested him to give him one of his daughters in marriage. The king was amazed. He knew Saubhari Muni as a *sadhu* and was wondering had happened, but he was also appalled because the *muni*, who had been such an austere *tapasvi*, had been doing these meditations and *vratas* in the water. Physically he looked horrible—his body was a wreck from all of the austerities. The king was looking at him, thinking, “My daughter…how could he marry my daughter? This *muni* is a material and spiritual failure. Previously he may have looked wretched, but at least he was spiritually situated and renounced. Now he has nothing spiritually and nothing materially either, not even an attractive body.”

The sage, however, because he had previously undergone austerities, had *sakti*, even to the point that he could read the mind of the king. The king thought he would be able to trick the sage and announced, “No problem. You come tomorrow and I will have all of my daughters, the princesses, present, and if any one of them chooses you, then you have my full permission and blessings to marry her.” Of course the king was thinking, “None of my daughters would marry this wretched looking man, so we will be free of him.” Saubhari Muni understood the king’s mind, and he left the palace. Since he had mystic power, he was able to completely transform his body. When he came back the next day, he looked so handsome and vibrant that every one of the princesses was completely enthralled by his beauty and chivalrous behavior, and they all wanted to marry him. There were fifty of them, and he married them all.

He tricked the king, but in actuality he tricked himself, because it is not easy to maintain one wife, what to speak of fifty. The former sage underwent tremendous endeavors trying to take care of and satisfy his wives and children. When we become overwhelmed and despondent, we may think, “What is happening to me? What is going on? How did I get myself into this?” The sage was thinking, “How have I complicated my life so much?” What had really happened was that he lost his sense of direction and focus, he had lost his spiritual strength due to the *aparadha*.

The obvious question is how do we become freed from the effects of an *aparadha*? Mercy is required for us to even become aware of our situation because so often we cannot even see the extent of our own condition or suffering until a crisis arises. At that time we are able to recognize that something is terribly wrong, but we can’t quite understand the problem. There are times where we feel choked in our spiritual lives. We have no desire to read, we have more trouble chanting, we have difficulty hearing and associating, which leads to the inevitable conclusion that some sort of *aparadha* has brought on such problems in our spiritual lives. When we are trying to clear ourselves of these *aparadhas*, it is as though (and we can visualize) someone has hands around our throats. We can barely breathe. Our life force is cut off. Similarly, the *aparadhas* are choking the growth of the creeper. We have to be careful, whether it is in our chanting, in our service, or in our association with *sadhus* and *gurus*. In all of these circumstances and situations we have to be careful so that we can receive the full benefits of devotional activities, because they are all so powerful.

In the *Caitanya-bhagavata* it states, “Raising His arms, the fair-complexioned Lord Gauranga declared to the world, ‘Renouncing criticism of others, everyone please chant Lord Krishna’s name. I will personally deliver anyone who avoids criticizing others and who once chants Krishna. I vow this is the truth.’” Again in the *Caitanya-bhagavata* Lord Caitanya instructs, “My holy name, which is a *kalpa-vrksa* tree, destroys anyone who blasphemes my servants.” Each morning before we chant our rounds we recite the greatest offense (*sadhu-ninda*): to be careful not to blaspheme devotees who have dedicated their lives to the propagation of the holy name of the Lord. That means all devotees. It does not say that we should only avoid blaspheming the *mahabhagavata*, the *nitya-siddha*, the *guru*, or the *acarya*. It says one should not blaspheme the devotees.

When devotees respect and value each other sufficiently, they will realize that each devotee is carrying the potency to deliver a benediction or a curse. That potency is based on the level of purity of the devotee who is being blasphemed. The more advanced the devotee, the more serious the offense, or in the case of *dhama-aparadha* the more exalted the environment, the more serious the offense. Sometimes devotees go on a sacred pilgrimage and come back with a chronic disease. Sometimes a devotee goes to Vrndavana or some of the other sacred places, and when they return, they fall from the path of devotional service. Others who go to the holy *dhamas* in reverential consciousness and in a mood of *vandanam* or prayer are transformed when they return. They have more faith, more dedication, determination, etc. Both parties go to the same place, but they entered in different kinds of consciousness and had different experiences. Whatever service we perform in the holy pilgrimage places is amplified; therefore, if we act properly, we will receive great blessings, but if we acted neglectfully or offensively, the negative consequences will be that much worse.

## Forgiveness

As we conclude this chapter, we want to examine *ksama* (forgiveness) and *anugraha* which we will define later. The account of Prajapati Daksa offending Lord Siva, given in the Fourth Canto, Second Chapter of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, offers a special illustration in this connection. Daksa, the son of Lord Brahma, organized a great sacrificial ceremony, but in the process he committed an offense. The offense was to the topmost Vaisnava, Lord Siva. Lord Siva did not take Daksa’s offense seriously, and afterwards Daksa even cried and begged for forgiveness from Lord Siva. Siva naturally forgave him, because the devotee does not take the offense, but the Lord does not forgive an offense to His devotee so easily. Daksa was able to complete his sacrifice. However, in spite of achieving the forgiveness of Lord Siva, Daksa had to suffer a reaction for his offense.

*te ca brahmana adesan*  
*marisam upayemire*  
*yasyam mahad-avajnanad*  
*ajany ajana-yonijah*

**Following the order of Lord Brahma, all the Pracetas accepted the girl as their wife. From the womb of this girl, the son of Lord Brahma named Daksa took birth. Daksa had to take birth from the womb of Marisa \[the wife of the Pracetas\] due to his disobeying and disrespecting Lord Mahadeva (Siva). Consequently, he had to give up his body twice.** ***Srimad-Bhagavatam*****, 4.30.48**

As a result of his offense to Lord Siva, Daksa had to take birth again, this time in a *ksatriya* family (the Pracetas). In the next birth Daksa also committed an offense at the lotus feet of Sri Narada Muni. Jiva Gosvami comments that this offense to Narada was an additional cause of Daksa’s previous offense to Lord Siva. When we make one of the offenses in chanting the holy name and do not rectify or change our consciousness, then one offense leads to another. Sometimes we tell small lies. We have to keep telling other lies to cover the last lie until the lying becomes so pervasive that we begin to believe our own lies. Srila Prabhupada writes in the purport of *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, 4.4.13, “He \[Daksa\] offended the lotus feet of Lord Siva because he thought that his body, being the father of the body of Sati, was superior to Lord Siva’s. Generally, less intelligent men misidentify in that way, and they act in the bodily concept of life. Thus they are subject to commit more and more offenses at the lotus feet of great souls.”

We may question, “If Lord Siva forgave Daksa, then why is it that Daksa still had to suffer multiple reactions?” We explained that Lord Siva didn’t take Daksa’s behavior as an offense and he forgave Daksa when he offered apologies, but at the same time Daksa had to take the body of a *ksatriya* and he later behaved offensively to Narada Muni. This second offense was due to some karmic connection to the first offense. We may ask, “Where is complete forgiveness if Daksa still had to undergo some type of difficulty?” Let us read this verse: “In order to be free from offenses unto great souls, one should please that same personality that one originally offended through constantly engaging in the process of *bhakti* beginning with the chanting of the holy names.”*Nama-kaumudi* confirms this as follows: “Offenses to great devotees may be relieved either by experiencing the calamities they bring or by attaining the favor, *anugraha*, of the offended devotee.” This is an important point.

A sin can be more easily forgiven. It can be eradicated even without interaction with the person to whom the sinful behavior was directed. However, after the committing of an offense, even when one asks for forgiveness, there can still be a reaction. This is quite different from our general conception, because we tend to examine such topics superficially. We normally consider that if we approach the offended party, then naturally we shall be forgiven. We may reason that if the offended party doesn’t accept it as an offense and we have asked for forgiveness, then why are there still reactions? Why do we have to undergo some calamities? Why do we have to get the favor (*anugraha*) of that person? Why isn’t the favor automatically given if the person doesn’t accept it as an offense? It may seem a little confusing.

An illustration of how the principle of *anugraha* works is this: Someone steals your car. Later you find out who it was, and the thief knows that you know. The thief comes to your house and very humbly apologizes, “I did it. I made a mistake. I wasn’t in a proper state of mind, but I did do it and I beg forgiveness.” The owner of the car forgives the thief. That is *ksama*, forgiveness. *Anugraha*, gaining the favor of the offended party, is different. In the act of forgiveness one has given up anger or agitation concerning the theft, but that does not constitute *anugraha*, getting the favor of the wronged party. What constitutes *anugraha* is this: The next step after begging for forgiveness is that the offender works overtime, raises money, buys the same kind of car that the owner originally had, and brings it to the rightful owner. This is the difference between the two. The extra step of rectifying the situation beyond the apology must be there for forgiveness to be complete and for favor to be bestowed upon the offender.

In the one case forgiveness was granted, but the offender had not really changed the situation, even though the offended party did not hold any grudge and never did. Nevertheless, the situation had still not been completely rectified. The offended person no longer had a car even after having forgiven the person. The situation had not been placed in order because the car was still missing. This is one of the reasons why sometimes a person may carry a scar from offenses even when the other person who was offended does not accept it as an offense and even if one is wise enough to make an apology.

There is a positive side to this principle. There is so much power in good association, and such power acts on many levels to help the benefited person. How much power there is when Vaisnavas are honoring each other as *sadhus*! When there is a problem, Vaisnavas address it openly, in a positive mood, and with honesty. Everything is placed on the table. We do not want to be in denial about a real problem or afraid to address an issue or speak up. Speaking up or addressing difficult issues should not be discouraged, but such discussions should take place in a mood of humility. In that way if one is proven wrong, then there are no consequences if later an offense is revealed to have taken place. A devotee shall then have the full assistance of the Vaisnavas in rectifying the situation. There is a difference between criticizing and trying to find ways to improve or to help a situation.

One of the ways to make sure that exchanges are not just based on fault finding and criticism is that when there is a discussion about an issue, problem, or person, then there should be, on the part of one or both of the parties, a practical solution: “Prabhu, how do you think we can help?” Now what happens if a devotee has that mindset of trying to help, but another devotee is not ready to accept the help, or is not ready to take advantage of good association? Such reluctant people are likely to fall into *aparadha* and stagnate their devotional progress. As devotees, we are foremost the servants of other devotees. We value them, and want to only assist in the growth of their devotion. A sincere devotee does not want to see the growth of another devotee’s devotional creeper hampered. Therefore, the devotee must try to function in such a way so as not to put him or herself in a situation where they commit *aparadhas* and cause others to make *aparadhas*. It is an important part of the process to act as the servant of the servant and to try to protect the devotional creepers, our own as well as those of others.

## The Power of Devotion and Extreme Forbearance

There was a great *acarya* in the Vaisnava lineage, Ramanujacarya, who was an eternal associate of Krishna and extremely potent and powerful. King Koluttunga the First, whose capital was in Kancipuram and whose dominion included Sri Rangam, the temple where Ramanuja was especially influential, became very disturbed and envious of Ramanuja. King Koluttunga was a follower of Lord Siva and, unfortunately, most followers of Lord Siva do not care to heed the connection that Siva has with his own worshipful Lord, the Supreme Godhead (Visnu). Again, this is the problem with so much of religion: a narrow-minded focus that lacks a holistic vision. Due to this narrow-minded focus, King Koluttunga was disturbed by Ramanuja’s ability to spread Vaisnava *dharma*. He wanted to convert Ramanuja and his followers into worshipers of Lord Siva so that other people would also take to the worship of Lord Siva. He had two plans. His first idea was to bring Ramanuja to an assembly where Ramanuja would argue with the King’s Saivite *panditas*. If Ramanuja defeated the *panditas*, then the second plan would be to kill Ramanuja. His idea was to stop the propagation and spreading of Vaisnava *dharma* at all costs. Ramanuja had two disciples: Mahapurna (who was actually the disciple of Yamunacarya and in the position of Ramanuja’s *guru*, but served Ramanuja sincerely as though he were a disciple) and Kuresa who was the initiated disciple of Ramanuja. They both knew how this king had been duplicitous in the past. When King Koluttunga invited Ramanuja to come to his palace for a spiritual discussion, these two loyal followers revealed their suspicions of the King. Kuresa offered to take Ramanuja’s cloth and to present himself as Ramanuja, and Mahapurna would go as the spiritual master of Ramunuja.

The situation unfolded as they expected. The *panditas* gave their arguments in favor of Lord Siva’s supremacy, and Kuresa and Mahapurna gave arguments showing Lord Visnu’s undeniable supremacy. The two Vaisnavas were repeatedly successful in the debate. The King flew into a rage, imprisoned and tortured them, and finally ordered that their eyes be plucked out. Even after being severely beaten, tortured, abused, as well as blinded, when they were presented to the King, Kuresa stated, “May Lord Narayana bless you for allowing me to serve my *guru* in this way.” Even the hard-hearted King and his men were awestruck. The King’s servants gave some money to a beggar to lead the two *sadhus* out of the city. Mahapurana was quite elderly and was mortally wounded by the torture, but he died gracefully in Kuresa’s arms, accepting the suffering as his service to Ramanuja. He was proud that he was able to protect his wonderful disciple, Ramanuja, in whom he saw the embodiment of Yamunacarya, his spiritual master. He spoke his final words to Kuresa, “You must go alone to Sri Rangam, for I can tell that the time has now come for me to leave this world. There is nothing to lament over, for my mind is fixed on Yamunacarya, and I am longing to be reunited with that great soul. When you meet again with my beloved disciple, Ramanuja, offer millions of obeisances at his feet on my behalf.” Not long after this tragic incident, King Koluttunga became incurably ill and died a short time later. His son, Vikrama, became the King and later on became an initiated disciple of Ramanuja.

When Ramanuja heard what had happened to Kuresa and Mahapurna, he was overwhelmed with compassion, contemplating the great trauma these great Vaisnavas had undergone. He was very concerned about their welfare. Mahapurna had gone back to the spiritual world, but it was very painful to learn that he was tortured and had suffered so horribly. Ramanuja requested Kuresa to go to Kancipuram and worship a very special Deity named Varadaraja, knowing that this Deity was extremely powerful and that Kuresa was pure and dedicated. Ramanuja knew that there was bound to be some special reciprocation between the two.

By the dedicated and steady worship of this Deity, the Deity became inclined to speak to Kuresa and offered to give him whatever he wanted as a way of reciprocating Kuresa’s devotion and service. Normally we understand that a pure Vaisnava is not interested in boons; they only want to serve Krishna and never forget Krishna, but Kuresa was ready to accept the boon. He asked that the person named Caturgrama, who had agitated King Koluttunga against him and created the situation by which he was blinded and tortured, be delivered from material existence and receive the Lord’s mercy. The Lord agreed but pressed him further to receive another boon. Kuresa replied, “I pray that King Koluttunga shall be the object of Your greatest mercy.” He did not hold a grudge against all those who had tortured him, nor did he try to destroy them. Not only did he forgive them, but he asked that they be given the highest possible benediction. What could the Lord do? The Lord responds to His pure devotee—*ye yatha mam prapadyante*. They were both liberated.

When Ramanuja heard how the Deity had reciprocated and how Kuresa, instead of asking something for himself, had asked for mercy for those who had been great offenders, he was overwhelmed. Ramanuja requested Kuresa to continue with his *puja* or service. He sent a message to Kuresa, “Next time you have such a special encounter with the Deity, for my sake and on my behalf, you ask Varadaraja to give back your sight.” Ramanuja reminded Kuresa that Kuresa’s body belonged to him and that to make him happy, Kuresa should make this request of the Lord. Kuresa was very pure, loving, and dedicated, and the next time the Deity spoke to him, he apologetically prayed, “O most merciful Lord, some time ago I lost two valuable possessions belonging to my master. By your grace I would like to recover them this day.” The Deity, Varadaraja, immediately gave him back his sight.

This story is very powerful. It teaches that the power of devotion, love, and extreme forbearance is greater than any deviation, even to the point of humbly accepting an assault or *aparadha*. By such tolerant and saintly behavior, there will always be blessings and growth for the devotee who remains Krishna conscious in spite of the situation. Krishna consciousness means being conscious of Krishna in all circumstances. It doesn’t mean we should be Krishna conscious only when it is easy or only with those who like us or treat us well. No. It means that we remain connected with Krishna and manifest a loving mood, regardless of whatever else is going on around us, good or bad, using all situations to become more Krishna conscious. This attitude is neither nañve nor an exercise of denial. The interior reality exhibited by Kuresa and Mahapurna was powerful love and devotion from the very beginning of the incident.

The worst thing that can happen to advanced devotees who are fixed in Krishna consciousness is that they will go back to the spiritual world. Thus, they are enabled by calamitous situations to go back to Krishna more quickly. It is a part of the process of devotion that from time to time everyone must be tested in various ways. When we take to devotional service we are declaring war against Maya. We are on a battlefield, engaged in our own battle of Kuruksetra. We shouldn’t read the *Bhagavad-gita* and think that it is merely some fascinating ancient history and philosophy. We should also understand that Arjuna is representing every person who is trying to take shelter of Krishna. Arjuna had to undergo bewilderment and serious choices, but Krishna was present to protect him.

We will also have to undergo challenges and deal with all kinds of choices, but Krishna is always there for us too. Krishna did not tell Arjuna, “We’ve been together since time immemorial, so you just sit back and watch me deal with all of these soldiers. You can go meditate and sit in your *bhajana-kutira* while I destroy them all with my Sudarsana *cakra*.” No. Krishna explained the actual philosophy of life from all angles and then told Arjuna, “Now you must carry out your duty.” Although Krishna was present, Arjuna had to fight. Krishna is present for us also, but He wants His devotees to receive the credit by maintaining their devotion despite all difficulties that may arise on the battlefield. Krishna explains that such devotees are most dear to Him: *sraddadhana mat-parama bhaktas te ’tiva me priyah*. Such a devotee is not affected by happiness or distress. Krishna does not say that a devotee is very dear to Him who remains focused only when He provides all opulence, fame, security, comforts, etc. Rather He makes it clear that the devotee who is unaffected by all kinds of reversals and difficulties is most dear. Krishna will arrange many experiences for us. In the midst of duality and strife we should not become *of* the world even though we may have to participate in worldly things or be situated in a materialistic environment. We should not give in to duality even if others do.

*tat te ’nukampam su-samiksamano*  
*bhunjana evatma-krtam vipakam*  
*hrd-vag-vapurbhir vidadhan namas te*  
*jiveta yo mukti-pade sa daya-bhak*

**My dear Lord, one who earnestly waits for You to bestow Your causeless mercy upon him, all the while patiently suffering the reactions of his past misdeeds and offering You respectful obeisances, with his heart, words and body, is surely eligible for liberation, for it has become his rightful claim.** ***Srimad-Bhagavatam*****, 10.14.8**

Those who give in are like the students in school who cheat or don’t study. Later such people fail, but they have cheated themselves because they missed out on the opportunity to learn something which would have been of use. They cheat themselves. We must be careful not to play into a culture of cheating, deviation, and *aparadha* just because it seems easier or is prominent in certain circles. There are always consequences for such behavior.

We have discussed the difference between sin (*papa*) and offenses (*aparadha*). Sins can be forgiven even without personal interaction with the one sinned against. Sins can be forgiven by chanting the holy name and trying to engage in some aspect of devotion. *Aparadha*, however, is not easily forgiven by Krishna. *Krishna will forgive the aparadha when the offended devotee begs Krishna to forgive the offender. The aparadha is also forgiven where there is anugraha, favor bestowed.* There has to be restitution, the setting of things back in their rightful situation. We have given the example of the person who steals a car to illustrate this point. Apologizing is a first step. However, a simple apology without the accompanying service and restitution to the offended party is incomplete.

## Gadadhara Pandita Rectifies His Offense

There is one more illustrative story in this connection. The great devotee Mukunda datta was telling Gadadhara Pandita about a wonderful Vaisnava, and Mukunda datta suggested that they go and have his *darsana*. When they arrived, they saw what appeared to be a complete sense gratifier, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, who was dressed opulently, surrounded by wealth, in gorgeous attire, and living in a royal palace, with servants all around him. His hair was oiled and perfumed, he wore expensive ornaments, and he appeared to be a materialistic person. He was lying on a beautiful bedstead and eating very opulent foodstuffs. When Gadadhara Pandita saw him he thought, “Where is the *sadhu*? Where is the special devotee?” Mukunda datta perceived Gadadhara Pandita’s mentality and began to chant a verse from *Srimad-Bhagavatam* about the amazing pastime in which Krishna gave liberation to Putana (the demonic witch who tried to poison Krishna) by awarding her the position of His mother in the spiritual world.

*aho baki yam stana-kala-kutam*  
*jighamsayapayayad apy asadhvi*  
*lebhe gatim dhatry-ucitam tato ’nyam*  
*kam va dayalum saranam vrajema*

**Oh, how wonderful it is! Putana, the sister of Bakasura, wanted to kill Krishna by smearing deadly poison on her breasts and having Krishna take it. Nonetheless, Lord Krishna accepted her as His mother, and thus she attained the destination befitting Krishna’s mother. Of whom should I take shelter but Krishna, who is most merciful?** ***Srimad-Bhagavatam*****, 3.2.23**

Just by hearing these verses, Pundarika Vidyanidhi went into a deep trance. He lamented over his misfortune, contemplating how Krishna gives liberation even to the demons, yet he had not received the Lord’s mercy. Many servants and friends could not control him. He began to thrash about in ecstasy, even destroying many of his furnishings. His body became absorbed in a trance of Krishna *prema*. He stayed in that state for many hours. Gadadhara Pandita could then understand that he had been thinking offensively and had not recognized the devotional character of Pundarika Vidyanidhi. By hearing a special pastime about Krishna, Pundarika became immediately absorbed, showing his complete disinterest in his physical environment and situation. When he heard something connected to his worshipful Lord, his consciousness became ecstatically absorbed in the experience of *prema* (*sambhoga*, *vipralambha*, etc.) How do you think Gadadhara Pandita responded to this revelation? Gadadhara Pandita not only apologized, but he asked Mahaprabhu and others if he could take shelter of Pundarika Vidyanidhi by initiation. In this way he not only received forgiveness for the offense, but he made restitution also.

This is a nice example of the point we have been discussing. We may wonder, “Well, why did he do that?” Gadadhara Pandita was so devotional and sincere that Krishna allowed him to show us a perfect example of elevated Vaisnava behavior. Mukunda datta and Gadadhara Pandita are eternal servants of Krishna, who are already pure. An offense was made, as part of the *lila* of Lord Caitanya, and Gadadhara Pandita showed us how to receive *anugraha*, the favor of a great soul. People who have offended Vaisnavas will have to suffer so much. For some of them, the only way they can be freed from all reactions is to become devotees. In other words, the reaction can be so overwhelming that they must now take to the devotional process, become absorbed in the process of purification, and in this way make not only an adjustment, but make restitution, as well as receive forgiveness.

This story was meant to give us more focus on how to rectify offenses. We should not be afraid when we can’t act genuinely in devotional service, or we can’t act with ease or comfort, but we must at least remind ourselves to be very careful. If we do commit *aparadhas*, our devotional careers, our devotional lives will become very difficult. That which could have been easy becomes complex. It is so important to try always to think well of others and serve them as best we can, because we never know what level of *aparadha* may surface when we are inattentive in our devotional service. Furthermore, we must try to do things in such a way that we don’t cause others to make *aparadhas* toward us, which will result in their suffering.

## Questions and Answers

**Question:** Is it simply the act of surrendering that is important? If we are in the material world, which is like a hospital, and we have problems, we then have to struggle to discover the disease and look for treatment. Isn’t there a danger of becoming complacent and not taking responsibility if we just surrender ourselves, thinking that Krishna will do everything?

**Answer:** If we surrender, then we will be saved. Surrender is neither complacency nor irresponsibility. Surrender means: “Dear Lord, Thy will be done.” If we are making that kind of prayer as we endeavor, we will be constantly helped. We must be attentive to the external aspects of our situation. However, when we are engaged in the externals but our mind thinks, “What’s in it for me? How can I get what I want?” that mood simply deepens our implication in Maya. Consciousness has everything to do with our recovery from the spiritual point of view.

**Question:** Our position in the solar system is so insignificant. It sometimes seems almost absurd to believe that the Supreme Lord should have any particular interest in what we do or what happens to us. Our lifetimes, when compared to the duration of the universe (trillions of years), are equivalent to a nanosecond of existence. Isn’t it absurd to think that our lives have significance?

**Answer:** This is why we find in history that some of the most intelligent people have been philosophers. Some of the most intelligent of the philosophers have been existential philosophers who have concluded that life is absurd. They see that people are living in so many different ways and yet it all ends in death. When we have profound material knowledge, it produces sorrow. Such knowledge helps us realize the full scope of relativism—most of the things that people focus on are pointless. That is the paradigm and the summit of material knowledge, material relationships, and interactions. However, there is another paradigm: the spiritual perspective or the anti-material perspective. That is a different realm of knowledge. The anti-material perspective helps us to realize that our existential situation in these material bodies is a reformatory process, whereby, from a deviated situation, souls are being offered a chance for rehabilitation and achieving spiritual significance as eternal servants of God.

Our existence as human beings in this world is akin to being in a reformatory institution. Therefore, Socrates and Plato discuss the idea of matter, anti-matter, and reincarnation while Jesus, Buddha, Mohammed, and others all talk about the fact that something greater exists than this world. They emphasize the spiritual world, and their mission to transform us so that we can experience the great treasures that lie within us as we reclaim the natural state of our existence. Presently we are in an unreformed situation. This flickering, temporary material world has the atmosphere of material duality and is far away from our natural home.

**Question:** Is the initial choice to be here deliberate?

**Answer:** It is deliberate in the sense that we have free will and with free will we can make foolish choices. Once we make those choices, there are consequences which we cannot control. Yes, our present situation is the result of our own misuse of power. We chose to forget God, run away from God, and run away from our own divinity. We are divine, but we have made choices to temporarily forget our divinity, and now we continue to act, based on the choices we have previously made. If we are from wealthy or royal families and are connected with untold opportunity and wealth, but somehow have chosen to leave that atmosphere, we will find ourselves struggling, practically homeless. We lose the desire to think about and remember who we are and what we are connected with. We struggle in a forgetful state. When something happens to remind us that we don’t have to live like homeless people, then we may go back to our families and again receive all that we are given. We all have so much that is available to us. Our souls are full of knowledge, bliss, and eternity. We are in a state of amnesia. Self-realization means reviving our original, dormant spiritual consciousness. Our original *sac-cid-ananda* consciousness is dormant, and the process of self-realization is to revive it. All the teachings, rituals, prayers, and practices of different religious systems given by saintly persons are designed to wake us up and bring us back to our normal divine selves.

**Question:** If the object is to revive our divinity, why is it that our society has not developed a psycho-spiritual technology for addressing this problem? It seems that none of our thinkers in the West even deal seriously with this fundamental problem of the meaning of our existence. None of these leaders have really evolved a technology for attacking this fundamental problem.

**Answer:** This is why power, position, and influence can be so dangerous: it can cause a person to become insensitive to the obvious. The more power we have, the more we must try to empower and facilitate others rather than sit on the power, focusing only on our egocentric interests. This is the danger for a successful person; often success can be the greatest enemy. Along with success comes a big ego and a lack of sensitivity to feedback. We become our own greatest enemy. We see this pattern time and time again. Many of the problems in the world stem from a crisis in leadership, from those who have power but are unable to function as servant-leaders who facilitate others. They become autocrats, tyrants. Therefore, their motto becomes, “It’s my way or the highway.” They tend to think of themselves as God’s blessing to the world, and they block out input from others who may try to help them. They posture, create propaganda, and do many things that later come back upon them.

To try to transform the world and its institutions, which are engaged in a struggle for power resulting in the misuse of power; to try to create a culture of responsibility where people are literally trying to facilitate others; and to reach out and care is the real point of our preaching. Real leaders are not those who want to lead because they feel they have to dominate, coerce, and impose. Real leaders are those who are not even interested in leading, but at the same time they are not willing to stand back and watch things happen while complaining from the sidelines. They get involved because they want to guarantee that the processes of facilitation and caring go on. They are trying to see to the welfare of others. That kind of leader is very rare because generally a leader imposes in some way.

You have summarized quite profoundly some of the points we have been discussing. These are some of the causes of *aparadha*: misuse of power and lack of real understanding. One should be in a position of power in order to see how to take care of the greatest number of people in the greatest way. Otherwise, we will repeatedly have coup d’etat, because one person is cheating and others are being cheated. Then a new cheater comes from the cheated and cheats some more. We have a shifting of bodies rather than of consciousness.

In history we consistently see that there is a change of bodies but not a change of policy or consciousness. The tendency is for the group who is in charge to create an elitist arrangement for their people and to often minimize caring for others on a more holistic level. No matter what race or ethnic group you examine, that tendency is prominent. Whoever holds the power will often try to see how to use it for selfish interests. Occasionally there is the rare leader who is concerned about everyone, seeing him or herself as a caretaker who honors diversity within unity. Such a leader has genuine concern about individual needs and tries to address those needs.

## 4. Humility is The Key

We see that the spiritual world is characterized by an atmosphere of selflessness and of the animated love of dynamic association. There is an intense mood of appreciation and caring that creates the dynamic synergy of devotees working together on different services which are perfectly Krishna-centered. If we are blessed by Srimati Radharani, it is guaranteed that we will gradually be able to re-enter the land of pure love. If we do not receive Her blessings, it is guaranteed that we will remain disqualified. We have discussed *dhama-aparadha*, *seva-aparadha*, *nama-aparadha*, *vaisnava-aparadha*, *guru-aparadha*, and we have shared different stories to illustrate each of these categories. We want to look at three more illustrative stories in this regard.

## Bhrgu Muni Searches for the Supreme Lord

Bhrgu Muni was deputed by an assembly of sages to make an appraisal of the three predominating deities in the universe, Brahma, Siva, and Visnu, to discover which of them is Supreme. When we are in doubt, we must scrutinize and investigate. Krishna consciousness is a deep science, an intense science. We should be introspective. That means to take close notice of what constitutes proper devotional activity and to analyze the *siddhanta* (proper conclusion). What are the goals to be achieved? What is the proper way to understand and even to worship the *sastra*? What are the qualities of a *sadhu* and how shall we imbibe those qualities? How shall we celebrate and honor saintly qualities when we see them in others? How can we recognize a bona fide *guru* and understand our duty as proper disciples?

Bhrgu Muni’s task was to test the great personalities. He first came in contact with his father, Lord Brahma. In spiritual culture, respect is always offered to elders because elders pass down wisdom and serve as mentors or role models. Bhrgu approached his father, but he did not approach him in a mood of respect. Lord Brahma was distinctly disturbed and angered by the disrespect, apparent lack of gratitude, and lack of culture. Next Bhrgu approached Lord Siva, and, while interacting with him, made a verbal offense. In the first instance the offense had to do with mental culture—the lack of recognition of his elder and absence of a reverential mood. In Brahma’s case, Bhrgu made a mental offense. In Lord Siva’s case, he spoke harshly, remarking that Lord Siva dressed in tatters, engaged in *puja* in graveyards, accepted all kinds of unqualified followers, and associated with hobgoblins. He verbally lashed out at Lord Siva, describing him as filthy, dirty, and situated in the mode of ignorance. Lord Siva was furious. He is Asutosa, easily pleased and easily angered, and was prepared to attack Bhrgu. Only by the intervention of Parvati was Bhrgu Muni saved from the trident of Lord Siva.

Bhrgu Muni took careful notice of Lord Siva’s mood. Sometimes a judgment can be made by watching a person’s reaction after his or her ego has been challenged. When there is fruitive attachment, agitation will erupt. The love between two spouses is not shown as much when everything is going well, but more so when they face upheaval. Then we will see how much they support each other and how much they try to understand and help each other. Within a team, we can also see how strong the individuals work together when they face challenges or setbacks. People can work together easily when life does not present any problems. Ideal circumstances do not reveal the extent of our commitments or how much determination, understanding, and appreciation we have. In most cases, when things get rough, we see the other side (the negative side) of most people. On the other hand, sometimes we see the best of people in calamities, in traumas, or in crises. The very best in a person may come out when the individual has a need to extend him or herself to help or assist others. However, more often than not, when there is a crisis, people tend to resort to a survival instinct. Bhrgu Muni delivered deliberate mental assaults (*aparadhas*), and in Lord Siva’s case a verbal assault. He could then examine the reactions of the two powerful controllers.

In secular life, what to speak of in spiritual life, we cannot fully control what happens around us, but we always have the power to control our response, and we will be judged by how we respond. Bhrgu Muni was trying to find the greatest personality, the one most firmly situated in spiritual identity and most fixed in spiritual absorption. Therefore, he performed negative tests by making different calculated offenses, and the offenses increased in gravity. Lord Siva became very disturbed and angry, almost ready to destroy him. Bhrgu observed the reactions. He reflected on how Lord Brahma was angered by his mental offense and Lord Siva by his verbal offense. Consequently he began to consider how to test Lord Visnu, and devised an even more obnoxious test. When Bhrgu approached Lord Visnu, He was lying down. Bhrgu then greeted Him by kicking Him in the chest. Surprisingly, Lord Visnu’s response was apologetic. He expressed concern, wondering if Bhrgu had injured his foot on His hard chest. Lord Visnu displayed a great instruction through this pastime.

Real devotees are always concerned about others, even if others are in the wrong. Great devotees want to try to help others think and act beneficially. It takes energy to descend to a lower standard of behavior. Sometimes we think we have to fight or behave aggressively, and some adversaries particularly desire that. An agent of Maya desires for the Vaisnavas to become distracted so that they do not act and consequently lose their focus and resources. In the material world there are always things which we have to address. We are not impersonalists nor are we in denial. Our duty is to protect the important gifts that Krishna offers to the world. We must simultaneously proceed on two tracks, maintaining but also remaining proactive. Otherwise there is grave danger. There is a real danger in focusing too much on maintenance because Maya does not maintain. Eventually there will be a de-escalation of positive effort. If we stand in one place while some force is constantly pulling us, and we simply continue our attempt to maintain our position, we will be forced to eventually move. Therefore, we should always try to accelerate and run outside of Maya’s grips rather than just maintain. If we are sidetracked by a combative mood, we might still “win,” but there will be so much suffering and distraction. A devotee works on a positive track.

In ancient cultures, when a person deviated, it was addressed. The crime was punished in accordance with the degree of seriousness of the crime and punished with competence. There were clear systems and clear rules that would act as deterrents. Nowadays, people commit so much crime because the common perception is that crime pays. They have the common conception that they are very likely to get away with their criminal behavior. In some countries where organized crime permeates the system, even if culprits are caught, they do not worry very much. They go to jail and get special facilities, even to the extent of a “penthouse” in jail. Such rascals run their businesses with outside help. In some cases, because they “suffered” for some time in jail, when they come out they get a promotion or a big bonus. Such so-called punishment does not act to deter them, and therefore, by their example, more people are inspired to perform criminal acts. Thus the culture of deviation, exploitation, and all kinds of manipulation increases.

Lord Visnu used the very powerful weapon of humility. If we are harsh, insensitive, or ridiculous, a person who does not descend to our level can defeat us. Sometimes we can be defeated by acts of kindness. If the recipient of such mercy or kindness is very gross, then there is a need for a strict and comprehensive chastisement. At other times, there is an opening for the exhibition of the higher principle of *bhakti*. The Sufis, generally considered to be Muslim mystics, have an interesting way of approaching Maya. They give the example of a thief. If we are upstairs and the thief breaks into the ground floor, what can we do? We can run downstairs with the broom, but the thief may have a hatchet! If we come down with a revolver, there may be three of them with shotguns! What should we do? We can turn on the light! The rational thief will run once he sees the light. Of course, nowadays we have irrational thieves, and they will tell you, “You had better turn that light off, or we will kill you and come back later for the rest of your family!” They may not care about the light, being so animalistic; however, rational thieves, once they realize they have been discovered, will at once drop everything and get out. The essential point is that if we turn on the light or put forth more devotion, we will chase the nescience out.

*krsna—surya-sama; maya haya andhakara*  
*yahan krsna tahan nahi mayara adhikara*

**Krishna is compared to the sunshine, and Maya is compared to darkness. Wherever there is sunshine there cannot be darkness. As soon as one takes to Krishna consciousness, the darkness of illusion (the influence of the external energy) will immediately vanish.** ***Caitanya-caritamrta*****,** ***Madhya-lila*****, 22.31**

Srila Prabhupada made that verse the motto of his *Back to Godhead* magazine. He put it more concisely still: “Godhead is light. Nescience is darkness. Where there is Godhead there is no nescience.” The more the devotion permeates, the less we will have deviation and nescience. While we have to address nescience as it manifests, if we increase the devotion, we create an atmosphere where there is less opportunity for deviations to surface. A rat is eager to go to a filthy place because it can find food and shelter, but if the environment is very clean, the rat will move on because it will not feel welcome. It is so important to always increase the *bhakti* because it will then attract more *bhakti* and less nescience. In Kali-yuga, the atmosphere is becoming so vicious and negative because there are so many places where varieties of deviation can fester and grow like cancer. Lord Visnu exhibited *bhakti*, the selfless, caring mood of concern, and *bhakti* acted like a weapon. The weapon of *bhakti* radiates, but also blocks and checks deviation.

## Sacimata’s Offense

When Krishna and His eternal servants descend in His many *lilas*, they come in a very revolutionary mood. Their lives and examples go against the grain of material culture, because they bring the culture of the spiritual world. They come to create the atmosphere of the spiritual world, to share the culture of our original home with us. Mahaprabhu and the great Vaisnava *acaryas* give us so many important instructions and examples: how to be the servant of the servant, how to execute devotional service avoiding *seva-aparadhas*, *nama-aparadhas*, *vaisnava-aparadhas*, *guru-aparadhas*, etc. We will share one story in this regard about Lord Caitanya and His mother Sacidevi.

Once Lord Caitanya was at Srivasa Thakura’s house, relishing very intimate pastimes with His unalloyed servants and associates. He began to exhibit the mood of supreme benefactor in His position of God, instead of remaining situated in His role as a devotee of God. Lord Caitanya is Krishna in the *bhava* of Srimati Radharani, showing everyone how to love Him. Yet He entered into His reverential feature as the summon bonum, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, in his *maha-prakasa* pastimes. He took the *salagrama-silas* (as explained in the *Caitanya-bhagavata*), put them on His lap, and explained His identity as the source of all things. He explained His different appearances such as Lord Rama, Vamanadeva, Matsya-avatara, and Kurma-avatara. Lord Nityananda, accommodating His mood, placed an umbrella over the Lord, honoring His particular *bhava* and revelation.

The devotees, because they are eternally connected with Him, also immediately entered into His mood and, relating to Him as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, began to ask Him to give benedictions and blessings for others. None of them were asking for blessings for themselves, but rather utilized the Lord’s ecstatic mood as a chance to extend the Lord’s wonderful mercy to others. Mahaprabhu was reciprocating in the mood of Krishna. For any request He received, the Lord promptly gave that person love of God, removed obstacles, and gave them His full blessings. Then Srivasa Thakura requested, “Please give Mother Saci pure love, Krishna-*prema*.” The Lord became disturbed. He replied, “Mother Saci will never get Krishna-*prema*. She cannot receive Krishna-*prema* because she has been an offender.”

How could He make such a statement about His own mother? This was an eternal associate who had appeared with Him in this world, and the devotees were shocked to hear Him make such an emphatic statement, while at the same time giving the benediction of Krishna-*prema* to so many others. To hear this statement was devastating to the devotees’ ears, and because they had so much affection and appreciation for Mother Saci, they were totally bewildered. Lord Caitanya gave some clarification, “Mother Saci has been an offender at the lotus feet of Advaitacarya.”

The background of this statement is that Visvambara, Lord Caitanya’s brother, was associating with Advaitacarya, and Advaitacarya was considered responsible for inspiring Him to take *sannyasa*. Mother Saci never commented on this incident. She held it in, repressing her feelings. Later when Nimai started associating with Advaitacarya, he was becoming similarly inspired. Mother Saci was thinking, “You have already taken one of my sons, and now you are ready to take the other!” She criticized the mode of address offered to Advaitacarya. She explained that people were offering respect by calling him Advaitacarya. She specifically remarked, “To the world he may be known as ‘Advaita’, non-dual, but to me he is ‘Dvaita’, full of duplicity. He has already driven out my moonlike effulgent first son and now he will not leave this younger one in peace. I am a destitute mother, yet no one has any pity for me. This Advaita has deceived me.” The meaning of her addressing Him as Dvaita was that she accused Advaitacarya of being full of duplicity. This was her “offense.” Lord Caitanya made it very clear that He was in a position to instruct her, but the devotees were thinking, “This is your mother. How can you speak like this?” Lord Caitanya explained that although He was in a position to instruct her, still He was unable to remove the obstacle created by the offense.

This was brought to Advaitacarya’s attention. When He heard about Mahaprabhu’s remarks, He was overwhelmed, “How is this? Mother Saci is the Lord’s mother. From her womb, Mahaprabhu has appeared. She has always been so graceful, merciful, and magnanimous to all of us. How can any of you think that she has offended me, or that I should receive her apology? She is five times more of a devotee than anyone else…” While speaking like this in deep absorption, meditating on her wonderful qualities, He entered into an ecstatic trance. Such great devotees sometimes enter into *samadhi* or trance. Madhavendhra Puri saw a black cloud once, which reminded him of Krishna as Ghana-syama, and he went into trance. Sukadeva Gosvami wanted to avoid mentioning the name of Radharani directly in the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, fearful that it would serve as a catalyst to take his consciousness outside of the body. After all, he only had seven days to speak the *Bhagavatam* to Maharaja Pariksit.

Compelled by his ecstatic love, Advaitacarya exhibited the pastime of entering into such deep absorption, *prema*, due to an inability to sustain external consciousness. The body was apparently in an unconscious condition. Mother Saci saw this unconscious state of Advaitacarya as an opportunity to take the dust from his feet. She submitted herself in the mood of His servant and begged forgiveness in this way. As she was touching Advaitacarya’s lotus feet, she became overwhelmed with affection and appreciation, situated in the mood of begging for forgiveness. However, this act excited the tremendous *prema* active in her. Her body could not contain it and she also entered into trance. Advaitacarya was down and she was also down. They were both fully absorbed in *prema*. The devotees witnessing this became so overwhelmed as they watched these two great eternal servants of the Lord, fully enraptured with affection for each other. This was Mahaprabhu’s arrangement for benedicting Saci with *prema*. Now how do we understand this pastime?

First we should understand that Lord Caitanya was in the *aisvaryabhava* in the *maha-prakasa*. Otherwise, this incident was outside the boundaries of etiquette involved in their usual relationship. Saci is His mother, and not an ordinary mother. The devotees were also temporarily relating to Him in this *aisvaryabhava*, and in this way Mother Saci was temporarily not seeing Him as her son but as the summon bonum Supreme Personality of Godhead. He was simultaneously Lord Caitanya and Krishna. In this mood He was showing the world that we must be careful in how we associate and not engage in the commission of offenses. Even His own mother is being called upon to teach this lesson. We must constantly let *sastra* be our guide so that we remain very clear and do whatever we do in a way that accelerates *bhakti*.

## Perfecting the Yajna

The *Mahabharata* describes how Yudhisthira Maharaja was once performing a very elaborate *rajasuya* sacrifice. In ancient times, this sacrifice was awesome. Amazing paraphernalia was gathered and expense was no object. Many exalted personalities would attend, and the sacrifice would continue for months. Yudhisthira organized this spectacular *rajasuya* sacrifice, and all the great personalities were there, even Krishna Himself. At the close of the *yajna*, something very special was expected to happen. A bell situated in the center of the arena was supposed to ring of its own accord to show that all things were properly performed, all parties were suitably honored, and all *mantras* were properly chanted. This arrangement was made so that everyone could see how Maharaja Yudhisthira was the dominant emperor of the world. In the previous age, while performing *yajnas*, the priest had to light the fire by *mantra*. If the priest could not perform the task, it would be clear that he was unqualified and did not have the required potency. He would be asked to desist. No one could fake it. Similarly, the starting of the fire by *mantra* before the *yajna* and other certifications after the *yajna* were arranged to show that everything was in harmony and had been done to perfection.

Everything went seemingly well, and the ceremony had come to an end, but the bell did not ring. Yudhisthira was in complete anxiety, because although such a *yajna* was capable of bringing about tremendous blessings to those involved, if it was not performed properly, the opposite effect might take place. It could create inauspiciousness or a great curse. Yudhisthira was shocked and appalled, and turning to Krishna, he called out, “Hey Govinda? What happened?” Krishna looked at him in a very grave way and said that all the parties meant to be honored and served in the sacrifice had not been honored and served. The service of a humble, pure, simple, austere Vaisnava *brahmana* had been neglected.

Arjuna, upon hearing this, immediately tried to find such a *brahmana* Vaisnava. He ran to a nearby village and in a little hut found a pure Vaisnava *brahmana* who he then invited to the grand *rajasuya* ceremony. The Vaisnava *brahmana* was not interested in being around so much pomp and opulence. He thanked Arjuna for the offer, but declined the invitation. Arjuna then explained how Krishna would be there, and the *brahmana* thought, “Oh yes, here is a chance to take *darsana* of the Lord.” He accepted the offer, which made everyone happy. At the *yajna*, they worshipped the *brahmana*, Draupadi made a wonderful feast, and after the *brahmana* had eaten, the bell still did not ring. Now Yudhisthira was really in anxiety. “Hey Govinda, what shall I do? What happened?” Krishna explained that someone in the assembly, in their family, had made an offense to the *brahmana*. The Pandavas were wondering who had committed the *aparadha*. They looked at each other, and then noticed that Draupadi was hanging her head. It became clear that Draupadi had made the offense, but they were wondering, “How? She cooked such an elaborate meal fit for a king and served him.”

The explanation was that the *brahmana* had an unusual habit that went hand in hand with his austerities: whenever he ate, he merged all the preparations together. Even though Draupadi had prepared a gorgeous plate of food, he still followed his usual practice. Although she made an opulent and aromatic feast, the *brahmana* just rolled up his sleeves, mixed the *prasadam* all together, and started eating. Draupadi, after so much elaborate preparation and effort, became angry when she saw his habits, thinking, “This uncouth village boy is merging everything together, ruining the meal. What a foolish, uncultured person.” She had committed a mental offense, which caused an obstacle to the perfection of the *yajna*. Krishna confirmed that it was Draupadi who made the offense. The Pandavas wondered what they should do since the *brahmana* had gone home.

Arjuna went back to the *brahmana* and begged, “Please you must come again.” The simple devotee was thinking, “No way!” He was happy with his simple life, with his simple *bhajana*, in his *bhajana-kutira*. He was glad to have seen Krishna, but, after all, He had already seen Krishna. However, he felt sorry because Arjuna was pleading with him, and out of respect, he returned to the *yajna*. This time everything was even more elaborate—the *puja* for the *brahmana* and the honor he was afforded. Draupadi made an even more fantastic feast and placed it before the *brahmana*. When he sat down, he once again mixed everything together and started eating. When he finished, the bell started ringing, and the devotees were in ecstasy, because the ceremony had been perfectly completed.

The purport is rather easy to understand: We may have so many things in place, but we must always be careful about the quality of our relationships. In Kali-yuga, we are not held responsible for offensive thoughts, otherwise we would all be finished! As a matter of fact, it is quite the opposite. In Kali-yuga, if we think healthy thoughts, Krishna will give us credit even if we cannot finish a service. In this age, due to the excessive stagnation and deviation, good intentions and effort are the major components of success. This is another reason why we must be careful not to think small. If we think and act small, then there will be small results. We should try to realize that when Krishna is pleased, miracles can happen (and must happen). If Krishna is really pleased, things will happen far beyond the scope of our normal resources, whether intellectual, financial, etc. When things are happening based on our resources alone, we can call this good management, but it is not necessarily spiritual. When there is a high level of spirituality, things happen even beyond our normal capacity and resources. It means that Krishna is coming through, and the *krsna-sakti* is present and active. We all experience this from time to time in our lives, in our families, in our projects. We make some efforts, but the results go far beyond our efforts. At other times we make a tremendous effort, but since Krishna and *guru* are not pleased, we get diminishing returns. In this story, many wonderful arrangements were made, but the most important ingredient was missing for complete success, for the bell of success to ring.

*Aparadhas*, or the absence of the blessings of Radharani, is Maya’s most fearsome aspect. Good association must be taken. It is very important to avoid Maya’s misdirection. Nothing hurts our movement more than *aparadha* because we can only be hampered slightly by outside sources. It is from the inside that we suffer. The movement is quite transcendental. The Krishna consciousness movement is inaugurated by the Lord and His eternal associates; therefore, it will be successful. Who will get the credit for making things happen and how will it unfold? That is the real story. A lot of people will miss out on the chance to be a part of the victory or success of Krishna consciousness. Sometimes ten to twenty-five years may go by in the life of a devotee, but then, all of a sudden, he or she becomes a different person. Why? It is no mystery— it is because of *aparadhas*.

We may see other devotees who are weak, but year after year they remain in Krishna consciousness and never leave. Such devotees may have some weaknesses but they are not making *aparadhas*. They have humility and appreciate Vaisnavas. This will give them protection. If we look at those in high-level positions, renunciates even, we see that sometimes they fall. In every single case it is associated with *aparadha*, because this process works. We are engaging in devotion, and constantly depositing and receiving credits. We take *darsana*, take *caranamrta*, water *tulasi*, and chant our rounds. We should not think fruitively about these activities, but by this process we are actually building up our spiritual bank account. As we are committing *aparadhas*, even though we are depositing, we are also withdrawing even more quickly. We start to withdraw more than we deposit, and, after a while, even though we may have had wealth, strength, and opulence, it all withers away and we become different persons. Where previously we had *ruci* (taste) and so many other auspicious things associated with devotion, we now dry up because of *aparadhas*. It is so important to revisit and investigate our practice when we find ourselves at a low ebb in our devotional service. We should immediately start taking an inventory to see if *aparadhas* are causing us to lose focus and appreciation for what our great *acaryas* have given us.

## Questions and Answers

**Question:** How can we heal the wounds we create. Sometimes it seems overwhelming.

**Answer:** It can be a tremendous burden to think about each offense—an overwhelming task. Srila Prabhupada gave us essential principles, along with certain rituals. We would feel both overwhelmed and discouraged if we had to practice all of the injunctions of *sastra*. We cannot imagine the type of rituals that a *brahmana* performs before activities such as eating, chanting *gayatri*, sleeping, etc. We should not allow the details to overwhelm us or allow paranoia to create negative situations. Turn on the light of *bhakti*. As we increase our compassion, our devotion, and our fear of Maya, we will make progress. Let me explain. When we focus more on those things that are a natural part of devotional life and execute them with quality, Krishna will give us the intelligence to avoid offenses. Krishna will give us the intelligence.

*tesam satata-yuktanam*  
*bhajatam priti-purvakam*  
*dadami buddhi-yogam tam*  
*yena mam upayanti te*

*tesam evanukampartham*  
*aham ajnana-jam tamah*  
*nasayamy atma-bhava-stho*  
*jnana-dipena bhasvata*

**To those who are constantly devoted to serving me with love, I give the understanding by which they can come to Me. To show them special mercy, I, dwelling in their hearts, destroy with the shining lamp of knowledge the darkness born of ignorance.** ***Bhagavad-gita*****, 10.10-11**

The Lord is in the heart, and He sees if we really want to come to Him and avoid offenses. A sense of humility will arise in the heart. When we are consciously trying to avoid offenses, He will give us clarification. When we become insensitive, arrogant, unreceptive to feedback, we will make *aparadhas* and won’t understand the mess we have created until we face a real calamity. Then we will be forced. This sometimes results in what we call falldown. Falldown is Krishna making it distinctly clear that we are off track, both to us as well as to others. This occurs after we are given many chances for rectification. If we do not make a shift and become properly situated, then Krishna arranges for a gross exposure. If a postal worker is delivering the mail, but later begins to steal the mail by taking checks or dropping it off in the wrong place, he is no longer a legitimate deliverer. At such times Krishna has to let people know that this is not a proper postal worker anymore. He exposes such people.

When someone falls, it is a sign that Krishna consciousness works rather than a sign that it is too difficult. When we don’t follow the process, some negative reaction will manifest. If we dishonor the science, the spiritual immune system becomes weak and Maya enters. And usually when we fall, we fall down to enticements that previously could not disturb us. We fall into traps that we know are Maya. Previously the intelligence kept the mind and senses under control, but now, due to offenses, the intelligence becomes weak, and the mind becomes a slave of the senses. The fall takes place and we wonder how Maya captured us. We knew so much and even preached against all the allurements in which we now participate. However, we have lost the protection of the *parampara*—*radha-sunya*. We have lost the mercy of Srimati Radharani!

**Question:** Is pride a big problem?

**Answer:** Yes, pride is a huge problem, because pride is associated with *ahankara* or the false ego. Pride causes us to become insensitive and causes us to only see or hear what we want to see or hear. It causes us to see everything as an extension of ourselves and our own desires. It causes us to lose empathy and sympathy, and it is part of the original desire that brings us to the material world—enviousness of Krishna. We want to be Krishna. It is associated with a lack of gratitude and egocentricity. For this reason, humility is such a universal antidote. So many good things happen when we have genuine humility. We are not talking about being foolish or nañve, or having low self-esteem. That is not humility. *Humility is not thinking less of oneself, but it is thinking of oneself less.* Humility is not low self-esteem. Low self-esteem is not Krishna-centered. Feeling incapable and insecure, and fearing that other people think negatively of us simply relate to our own image. Humility is the opposite of such a negative emotion. It is recognizing that we are in the presence of something wonderful, and we have tremendous appreciation. We feel that we have been causelessly and mercifully placed in a fortunate position; therefore, we are humbled as we try to become worthy of what is being offered to us.

Low self-esteem causes us to focus on what we do not have. Humility helps us appreciate what is possible by Krishna’s grace. Through honesty, we recognize the exalted nature of Krishna consciousness and feel humbled by the mercy we are afforded. Humility does not include insensitivity, self-pity, a defeatist mentality, or cynicism. Through humility, we are able to realize what is available for us in the present and endeavor to become worthy of the blessings that we have received. We are to be humble, but not foolish, nañve, or impersonal. I will give you the instruction that Prabhupada gave to me about humility. He said, “You have to be selfless, humble, and brave.” Humility gives us an awareness of the possibilities and an eagerness to achieve them. Selflessness prevents our own gratifications and pleasures from distracting us from the path of devotional service. Bravery develops when we understand that Krishna’s mercy is available. We must be determined to get that mercy and bravely live a life of righteousness and integrity even if, all around us, no one follows such standards. It takes courage to stay focused in spite of the many allurements and challenges. The combination of humility, selflessness, and bravery is a little unusual materially, but spiritually, humility and bravery go together, and selflessness is like the sister of humility.

**Question:** If a devotee has fallen, he or she may have a feeling of hopelessness. It may seem as if all is lost. We may even feel too ashamed to come around or come back to the practice of Krishna consciousness. How can we regain what we have lost?

**Answer:** Take the example of a parent. Parents are happy when they see their child doing well, but the parents also love the child when the child deviates or gives the parents pain. When the child returns and takes guidance from the parents, the parents are more than happy to assist their deviant child. The child has free will. Sometimes he or she wants to experience something new and consequently runs away from the protection, guidance, love, and affection of the parents. Krishna has given us free will, and He is always present in the background as the Supersoul or the witness when we come to the material world in these material bodies and engage in abominable activities. Love is not a matter of force, but the reposing of our attention and affection by choice. Krishna does not force us. He gives us the full capacity to act according to our free will because we are part and parcel of Him. We are finite and fallible, but we are also like Krishna. As He has freedom, we also have freedom. He gives us total freedom, but when we have a desire to come back to Him, either partially or fully, He arranges a variety of methods for us to return.

His desire to accommodate us is one of the reasons that we find so many different bona fide religions. Even when we try to reclaim our eternal identity, we have the desire to know the Lord to different degrees. Bona fide religious systems deliver various levels of awareness of the Supreme. One system will give us awareness of the Lord’s *sakti*, or His energies, and another system will give increased awareness of the Lord’s *siddhis* (mystic powers). We may be interested in achieving some degree of those same powers. Krishna is making arrangements to invite those who have fallen back home. He allows them to return at the pace and to the degree that they desire. There is never a time when we are fully lost, even if we have to take another birth. There is always an opportunity to reform and once again receive the Lord’s blessings.

When we use the term “fallen,” we are not indicating that someone’s situation is hopeless. It is a matter of designating the situation of a person who has deviated to a certain extent and who needs help. Such nomenclature is simply a means to distinguish a person in need. Whether we accept that we are in need now or later, we are in need. At some point in our devotional life, we will enter a low period in our consciousness. It is rare for a person to progress without any setbacks year after year and then return to Godhead. No, we go through different processes. Our spiritual growth is not perfectly vertical. At times it is vertical, and, at other times, it is circuitous or horizontal. As we advance, we come to a point where we may appear stationary while we work on a particular issue or stagnation, but then we advance. The cycle often continues in this way.

During the times in which we face challenges, we need support because the task can become awesome and overwhelming. When we are struggling, we sometimes feel alone and start questioning our value as devotees, because we do not have the same zeal as in the past. We have progressed to a certain level, but now we face experiences or lessons that we need to understand and move through before we can go to the next level. There are times when a person’s fall is really Krishna’s arrangement for growth. The teacher gives us tests so that we can graduate. On the other hand, a test can also lead to a failure and it can expose our lack of preparation. The teacher does not want us to fail, but he or she does want us to take the time to study all of the material. The teacher hopes that we have imbibed and appreciated the lessons, but he or she gives a test to make sure. Maya functions in a similar way. She does not want us to fail. Maya is presenting a material attraction to see what we want—the so-called attraction or Krishna. Maya is obligated to continue presenting us various offerings, because she is a pure devotee, working for Krishna. No one can return to the spiritual world unless Krishna is the first and only priority. Maya is testing us to see if we are ready. The *Brahma-samhita* explains that the same Maya who seemed to be our enemy later becomes like a ladder or stepping stone who helps us reach the spiritual world. She wants to make sure that we are no longer allured by her offerings.

When we see other people struggle, we should try to be sympathetic. That does not mean we should join them or deny their submersion in Maya. We should understand that, in many cases, devotees could be at a point in their Krishna consciousness where they are working through a particular problem. If they have caring but strict association in the background, and if they have assistance and support, that sort of concern can make a difference in helping them move beyond an obstacle more quickly. In other instances, when the person feels excluded and shunned, they may begin to feel hopeless. If we realistically analyze our movement, most of the devotees who joined have left. It does not matter what center, project, or country we name—they will leave and they have left. Thousands of devotees have joined, but take a look at who is still around. More leave than stay. Many of those who leave come back. Many will come back when situations in the world become more distinctly chaotic, more than we can presently imagine. Many devotees, who have seemingly blooped, are constantly thinking about how they can again come into the association of the Vaisnavas. As a part of the group of devotees who are still around, we should encourage devotees undergoing difficulties to perhaps reconnect later on. We never know when we will have difficult challenges, and when we will need to be given support, but that time will definitely come. When we see devotees going through struggles, we should learn from those struggles and we should offer assistance. We should be in a prayerful state, asking Krishna to please direct us to be less distracted by anything that will interfere with our return to Godhead in an expedient way.

If we maintain humility and a healthy fear of Maya, we will not experience overwhelming obstacles. We will be able to address our problems and move on. If we lose our humility, forsake quality association with Vaisnavas, and then allow *aparadhas* to invade our consciousness, we will be presented with difficult tests and not have the strength to pass them. It is as if Srimati Radharani is saying, “You are not ready to come any closer.” The *aparadhas* are offering direct proof that we are not ready for that higher level of divine and sweet association in animated, loving, selfless, and pure Krishna consciousness.

## The Significance of Radha Kund

## By Sri Nandanandana Dasa

Radha Kund is the bathing place of Srimati Radharani and Her most elevated assistants, the *gopis* or cowherd girls. Radha Kund is located on the northern end of Govardhan Hill. Sri Rupa Gosvami explains in verses 9 and 10 of his *Sri Upadesamrita* that out of all the places in Vraja, Radha Kund is superior to them all because it is flooded with the ambrosial nectar of Krishna *prema*, ecstatic love of God. Therefore, those who live here are the most fortunate in the universe. The *Padma Purana* also explains that just as Radharani is most dear to Krishna, Her bathing place of Radha Kund is also dear to Him.

Across from Radha Kund is Shyama Kund, Krishna’s bathing place. Krishna dug Shyama Kund when Radharani insisted that He purify Himself by bathing in all the holy rivers after He had killed the Aristasura demon who was in the form of a bull. So, rather than going to all the sacred rivers, He simply dug His heel into the earth and called the presiding deities of the various holy rivers of India to merge their waters into the pool. After bathing, He explained to Radharani and the *gopis* that the bull was actually a demon, so they should also take a purifying bath to rid themselves of the sin of siding with a demon. Radharani became upset and decided She could also make a kund or lake. Thus, She and the *gopis* broke their bangles and dug a hole, but it remained dry to Krishna’s amusement. However, by using water from the nearby Manasi Ganga lake and the waters from the presiding deities of the holy rivers who appeared, Her kund also filled up. Thus, these kunds are very special to pilgrims who visit. It is said that anyone who bathes here will get the perfection of Krishna *prema*, which is far beyond mere liberation from material existence. It is said that Radha Kund is the liquid form of Radharani’s ecstatic love for Krishna. Even the local ladies when they go to take their holy bath in Radha Kund say, “Today we are bathing in Radharani.” Also, since Radha Kund is Lord Krishna’s favorite bathing place, anyone who bathes in Radha Kund gets the mercy of Lord Krishna and will attain Krishna prema, ecstatic love for Lord Krishna. Similarly, Radharani also said that anyone who bathes in Shyama Kund will get Her special favor. So, many devotees also bathe in Shyama Kund. In this way, the mercy and the means to understand the power of Radha Kund is difficult to attain unless one receives the blessings of the devotees.
