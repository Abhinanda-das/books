## Introduction

The Ramayana of Valmeeki is surely one of the greatest stories ever told. It is a stupendous epic that stretches from horizon to horizon, encompassing almost all that the soul can aspire to, and more than the mind could ever imagine. It is the oldest poem known to man: a repository of moral and spiritual truths that has entranced good men for millennia. And above all it is a grand story, one that will be told — upon the word of Brahmaji — as long as mountains stand and rivers flow upon the earth. I speak, of course, as a believer, for the Ramayana is above all a holy scripture, and accepted by millions, and especially by the Vaishnavas (the devotees of Rama and Krishna), as the literal record of the deeds of the Godhead. The Western mind, however, is always prone to consign works of this kind to the dust of the library shelf, along with the myths of Greece and Rome, and to cast out all thought that the events described in the Ramayana might have actually occurred. Their conditioned intellects shy from any inclination to consider the narratives of the Ramayana as anything more than fairy tale, contrived by a superstitious and deceitful priesthood to enslave the hearts of ingenuous peasants. Such beliefs are naturally held by men and women reared in the modern world. Modern rationalism demands the rejection of that which is beyond our sensory experience; with the exception of the process of evolution of the species, which is, regrettably, well beyond our experience and our ability to prove beyond doubt, or the great 'bang' that purportedly began our universe, and which has, it appears, left no substantial evidence of its ever having taken place, and so on. That is to say, the modern thinker can accept certain unproven, unverifiable things as true, and not others — the crux of the issue being that we are no longer allowed to believe in a Superhuman Entity capable of superhuman deeds, only in theoretical alternatives to His existence, no matter how improbable they may be.

At any rate, the dispassionate thinker should be willing to accept that incredible things may exist beyond his ken, and should not reject them out of hand. The Vaishnavas have ample experience of a revelationary kind to convince them that their scriptures speak the truth, but non-vaishnavas will be unable to appreciate their conviction, or to experience their revelations, unless they are inclined to become Vaishnavas themselves. The Supreme Lord justly declines to reveal Himself to those who have no desire to become acquainted with Him.

That there are many items of contention in the pages of the Ramayana, I do not deny. The proposition most in need of explanation, however, is that of Lord Rama's Godhead. Rama is generally thought of as an imaginary hero, an idealized man and prince, or perhaps some historical figure around whom a legend has evolved. But the text repeatedly refers to Him as the Self-Existent Lord, the Supreme Personality of Godhead etc. Even among the Hindus, there are many that believe Rama is little more than an allegorical figure, or someone on the brink of self realisation who needs to be reminded of His divinity. Many Hindus regard Lord Rama as an advocate of happy family life and devotion to one's spouse, and of the performance of human duty at all costs. However, neither the views of the agnostics nor those of the Hindu householders can be supported in the light of the text itself, which, though it plainly fails to do so, is supposed to substantiate their various opinions.

The problem with the Hindu community regarding Rama's Godhead is that many disreputable persons have commented upon, and translated the Ramayana. They have invariably presented Rama in the light of the monist philosophy of Sankara, which has so caught hold of the Hindu imagination, and which declares the Supreme Being to be ultimately non-differentiated and without attributes — either material or spiritual. From this viewpoint Rama must be an inferior entity to the attributeless Brahman, or spirit. But the philosophy of the Vaishnavas is more subtle than this and deems the Ultimate Entity to possess innumerable personal, spiritual attributes — including an impersonal feature. He is capable of descending, by His own volition, into the realm of matter — which is held to be a real entity eternally subject to His will — without surrendering a jot of His supremacy, or losing any of His transcendent qualities. It follows, therefore, that Lord Rama is always transcendental to this world of matter, which is fashioned from His inferior energy, that He is always replete with knowledge and bliss — both being attributes of spirit — and that He enacts the deeds of a human being out of an independent desire for the weal of the world, and to facilitate His own enjoyment with relation to His internal pleasure potency — in the shape of Mother Sita — and His dislocated portions: the individual souls. Furthermore, the philosophical writings of the Vaishnavas inform us that the Rama of Valmeeki's Ramayana is not a momentary manifestation of the Supreme. As we have stated, the Vaishnavas reckon God to be possessed of eternal form, beauty, radiance, wisdom, artistry etc. He has numberless expansions residing in the realm of spirit, all surrounded by His adoring followers (who also possess real attributes like their Master) and with landscape, architecture, flora and fauna, and so forth: likewise manifested by His spiritual power.

These plenary extensions of Godhead occasionally descend to the material sphere — while simultaneously remaining in Their original station — in order to perform blissful pastimes for the benefit of worldly creatures. Thus a Rama, who is non-different from the Rama of this Ramayana, is to be found in the Spiritual Universe. There too is found an Ayodhya, the model of His capital on this planet. There are found His eternal, liberated servitors, such as Kausalya, Hanuman and Sugreeva. There His immediate expansions, Lakshmana, Bharata and Satrughna, are to be seen as well. The question may arise from this explanation as to the distinction between the activities of Rama in the Spiritual Universe, and those recorded in the Ramayana. The difference is essentially twofold. Firstly, that in the Ayodhya (Rama's divine capital) eternally manifest in the spiritual sky, there is no 'leela', or pastime, of the Lord's birth, childhood, and so on. These things are seen in the Ayodhya below alone, and are shown for the pleasure of those devotees who feel the Lord to be their son. In the Ayodhya above the Lord is ever youthful. Secondly, there is only the hint of evil in the spiritual realm, not the reality. Thus Rama exhibits the killing of atheists in this world, where alone such entities exist, and not in the other. There is, as I say, a hint of evil in the Ayodhya in the transcendental universe, but this has its existence merely to enhance the spiritual sentiments of the Lord's devotees present there with Him.

Thus I have described the ontology of Sri Rama in brief. Now for a few words on the author of the Ramayana, Valmeeki Muni, the dating of the Ramayana and so forth. According to the Ramayana itself, Valmeeki composed the work during the presence of Rama upon the earth. Lord Rama appeared, Valmeeki informs us, at the beginning of the Treta Yuga, the second world age of the present cycle of ages. And the Treta Yuga began some two-million years past. This is an assertion that presents no difficulty to the Vaishnavas, though it be contrary to generally accepted theories of human history and development. The Vaishnavas count the history of mankind as beginning, more or less, with the creation of this universe, and hold the theories of the evolutionists to be a flimsy contrivance meant to negate the existence of the Supreme Lord. By all accounts, the Ramayana is very ancient, and was composed long before books were written down. Valmeeki composed the Ramayana in what was, apparently, a novel poetic metre. He termed his invention, 'sloka', and employed it in every one of the Ramayana's 24,000 verses. Thus he is known as the 'Adi Kavi', or the original poet of Sanskrit literature. He was a brahmana by birth, but was abandoned by his parents and was found and reared by highway robbers. When he grew he made a regular profession of thievery and is said to have sometimes killed his victims if it proved necessary. It so happened that he once attempted to rob Narada Muni, the great sage amongst the celestials. Narada enquired whether Valmeeki had ever considered the future results of his actions. He told him to go home and ask his wife and children if they were ready to share the punishment due him in the afterlife. They demurred, and put their provider in something of a quandary. He returned, dismayed, to Narada, and asked him if he could provide the means of his deliverance from the sure reactions to his past iniquities. Narada ordered him to chant the Holy Name of Lord Rama, saying that would purify him of all his past sins, but Valmeeki was somehow unable to do so. Narada therefore enjoined him to chant 'mara — mara', which is but 'Rama' inverted. The repeated chanting of this mantra presently produced an ecstatic trance in Valmeeki, and he was thus absorbed for many years. Ants took the opportunity to build their nest over him, completely covering him, until Narada returned and got him out. Having thus emerged from the anthill, called 'valmeeka' in Sanskrit, he acquired the epithet, 'Valmeeki'. The rest of his story, and especially how he came to compose the Ramayana, is told in the Ramayana itself.

In conclusion, I must express humble gratitude to my Eternal Master, His Divine Grace Om Vishnupada A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, who on several occasions requested devotees, including an old schoolmate, to translate Srimad Valmeeki Ramayana into English. I learnt all I know of Lord Rama and His ontology from him, and would hardly have dreamed of learning Sanskrit, let alone translated this glorious epic, if not for him. I therefore humbly submit this work at his lotus feet, and pray, fervently, that it will afford him some little pleasure.

*Karnamrita Dasa Adhikari.*

## Preface

This translation of the Ramayana of Valmeeki is rendered verse-by-verse, and is based upon a standard text, namely the Chowkamba Sanskrit Series Ramayana, published in Varanasi, and currently in print, and was checked against the Gita Press English edition of Valmeeki's Ramayana, as suggested by Srila Prabhupada. We have attempted, with varying degrees of success, to both retain the mood of the original text and to render each verse accurately. Such an endeavour is naturally fraught with difficulties. Sanskrit and English are extremely dissimilar, and the Ramayana is composed entirely in verse. We could hardly have done the original justice, and have at times had to make compromises in the translation of one idiomatic, and highly sophisticated language, into the barbarous parlance that is the English tongue.

As regards the rendering of Sanskrit words, it was decided to employ a phonetic system of spelling, rather than the more accurate, but scholarly diacritical spelling system. The reasons for this are: 1) that the phonetic spelling of Sanskrit names is more natural and accessible to the diverse readership for whom this translation is intended; 2) that the adoption of a phonetic system of spelling greatly eases editorial endeavour, which is not inconsiderable in a work of this nature, undertaken as it is by a single person. The readers will note, however, that 'long a' (a) is marked, thus improving the chances of proper pronunciation.

## Canto 1. The Ramayana Summarised

Once, the austere Narada Muni, the most learned scholar of the Vedas, was questioned by the ascetic Valmeeki. 2) "Who", Valmeeki enquired, "presently possesses merit and courage in this world? Who knows the religious principles and how they are to be enacted? Who is truthful and firm in his resolution? 3) Whose activities are irreproachable? Who is the friend of every living being, and who is possessed of real knowledge and power? Who has a singularly beautiful appearance? 4) Who is self-possessed and has conquered anger? Who has a lustrous appearance and is envious of no one? Whose rage in battle is feared even by the demigods? 5) Out of great curiosity I desire to hear of such a man. O great sage, as you are so powerful, he surely must be known to you."

6\) Sri Narada, the knower of the three worlds, having heard the entreaty of Valmeeki said: "Please listen!" Having thus addressed him, filled with ecstasy, he spoke the following: 7) "The many qualities enumerated by you are certainly difficult to find, O muni. I have, however, reflected upon your enquiry, so kindly hear about a man endowed with such characteristics.

8\) "In the dynasty of Ikshvaku the most pre-eminent is known by the people as Rama. His mind is controlled, He is greatly powerful, determined, dazzling and alluring. 9) He is intelligent, and possessed of political and philosophical wisdom. He is eloquent, wealthy, and is the annihilator of His foes. His limbs are thick, His arms powerful. His neck is marked with three lines, and His jaw is firm. 10) His chest is large; He is a great archer, His collar bone is inconspicuous and He is the subduer of His enemies. His arms extend to His knees and His head is beautiful, as are His brow and His gait. 11) He is of medium height, His limbs of good proportion; He has a gorgeous complexion and great majesty. His chest is muscular, His eyes large, and there are auspicious fortune marks upon His body. 12) Knowing the principles of religiousness, He is true to His word and delights in the welfare of His subjects. He is famous, pure and dutiful. He has full knowledge and is of profoundly concentrated mind. 13) He is opulent, the slayer of His adversaries, and maintains the living beings like Prajapati. He is the protector of all creatures and is the defender of the faith. 14) He is the protector of His kin and of the ritualistic traditions of His dynasty.

"He knows the purport of the Vedas and the supplementary Vedic literatures and is a past master in the Dhanurveda.15) He knows the true meaning of the scriptures, is possessed of perfect memory and endowed with presence of mind. Dear to all beings, He is honourable, noble-minded and discerning. 16) He is always sought by the sages just as the ocean is sought by streams. He is refined, equally disposed to all and is always delightful to behold. 17) The depth of His voice and character resembles the ocean; His firmness — the Himalayas. Being endowed with all the virtues, He increases the bliss of His Mother, Kausalya. 18) Resembling Vishnu in splendour, He is as soothing to look upon as the moon. He is as forbearing as the earth, but angered, He is like the fire at the time of the cosmic dissolution. Liberal as Kuvera, in truth and piety none surpass Him.

"As Rama had all the prerequisite qualities, and the interests of the people at heart, and as He was the eldest and dearmost son of King Dasaratha, the sovereign desired to invest Him with the office of Heir Apparent. At that time, Kaikeyi, a wife of Dasaratha, beheld the preparations for Rama's inauguration. 22) Formerly, the lady had been awarded a boon \[which was to be redeemed when she wished\], and on that account she asked for the banishment of Rama and the consecration of her son, Bharata, in His stead. 23) Due to his practice of speaking truthfully, and bound as he was by his promise, Dasaratha duly banished his beloved son, Rama.

24\) "In obedience to His father's command, given for the appeasement of Kaikeyi, our Hero went to the forest. 25) His dear brother, noble Lakshmana, the bliss of Mother Sumitra, out of love for Rama, dutifully followed Him into exile. 26-28) Thus They exhibited perfect fraternal affection. Lord Rama was also accompanied by His darling wife, Sita, who was always companionable and as dear to Him as His own life. Born in the family of Janaka, she possessed a form as wonderful as the Lord's divine illusory potency, and endowed with all the marks of fortune. Among women she was the most excellent wife, and she followed Rama just as Rohinifollows the moon. Even the citizens and His father Dasaratha followed Him as far as they were able. 29) At Sringaverapura, on the bank of the Ganges, Rama, the personification of duty, met His dear servant, Guha, king of the Nishadas, and dismissed His charioteer \[who had thus far accompanied Him\]. 30) Together with Guha, — Rama, Lakshmana and Sita journeyed from forest to forest, fording many deep streams. 31-34) Having got directions from the sage Bharadvaja they reached Chitrakoota. There they built a delightful cottage, and the three of Them lived very happily together, sporting like the celestial Gandharvas. Upon Rama's getting to Chitrakoota, King Dasaratha, wailing in great anguish for his son, went to his heavenly abode. On the king's departure from this world, Vasishta, together with other prominent brahmanas, urged the greatly powerful Bharata to accept the crown. Bharata did not desire the realm however, and the hero went to the forest to obtain the mercy of Rama. 35) Having met the great-souled Rama, noble Bharata bowed before Him and begged Rama as follows: 36-40) 'Will You and You alone be King, O Knower of religious principles?' Beautiful Rama of great fame and magnanimity did not wish to disobey His father's command. He therefore gave His slippers as a token to Bharata, together with the charge of the kingdom, and after conversing with Him for some time persuaded Him to return home. Thus Bharata did not have His desire fulfilled, and having touched His brother's lotus feet He went to Nandigrama, from whence He ruled, ever longing for the return of Rama.

"After the departure of Bharata, the truthful Rama, anticipating the return of the townsfolk to that place, entered the Dandaka forest with controlled senses. 41-44) Having penetrated the great forest, the lotus-eyed Rama killed the Rakshasa known as Viradha. He then encountered the sages Sarabhanga and Suteekshna, and Agastya and his brother. Agastya presented the delighted Rama with a bow called Sarasana, a sword and two inexhaustible quivers. While He lived in that forest, Rama was approached by the forest dwellers, together with certain sages, who begged that demons who were harassing them should be killed. Having heard their entreaty, Rama promised that they would have His protection. 45) Rama assured the inhabitants of Dandakaranya and the rishis, who were as brilliant as fire, that the Rakshasas would be slain in combat.

46\) "While He was dwelling there, Rama disfigured the demoness known as Soorpanakha, who could assume any form at will. 47-50) Because of Soorpanakha's bitter complaint, all the Rakshasas, headed by the generals Khara, Trishira and Dushana, were killed by Rama. Altogether, fourteen thousands of them were slain there in the forest. Hearing of the slaughter of his kinsmen, Ravana, filled with rage, sought the aid of his follower, Mareecha, who repeatedly tried to restrain him. 51) 'It is better not to tempt one so powerful, O Ravana', he said. But Ravana, due to the influence of time, disregarded his counsel. 52) He went with Mareecha to the hermitage where Rama dwelt, and by a sorcerer's trick he lured the two princes far away. 53-54) Ravana abducted Lord Rama's wife and killed the vulture Jatayu \[who attempted to rescue Sita\]. Rama was grief stricken to see the dead bird and to hear of the kidnapping of Sita, and with great remorse He burned Jatayu's corpse.

55\) "Following Sita's path through the forest, Rama encountered the monstrous Rakshasa known as Kabandha, who was dreadful to look upon. 56-58) Having slain him, the mighty-armed Ramachandra cremated his body and Kabandha ascended to the heavenly planets. He told Rama: 'You should seek the righteous ascetic, Sabiree. She is well versed in the principles of religion, O Raghava. Rama therefore approached her and was duly worshipped by her.

"He then met the monkey Hanuman on the bank of Lake Pampa. 59-62) Thereafter, Rama was introduced by Hanuman to Sugreeva. The mighty Rama related to him all that had occurred from the very beginning, especially with regard to Mother Sita. Having thus heard from Him, Sugreeva, with great happiness, made a pledge of friendship before the sacred fire. The unhappy king of the monkeys then lovingly informed Rama of his enmity with Vali, and Rama promised that Vali would be killed. 63) Sugreeva, however, fearing for the safety of the heroic Raghava, was greatly apprehensive and described Vali's great strength unto Him. 64) In order to convince Rama, Sugreeva showed Him the colossal corpse of the demon Dundubhi, which resembled a mountain, and had been killed by Vali. 65) Seeing the enormous skeleton, the mighty Rama merely smiled, and with His big toe flung it away a full eighty miles. 66) In order to further allay Sugreeva's fears, He pierced seven palm-trees, a mountain and the Rasatala planet with one tremendous arrow. 67) Then, with a happy mind, the great monkey went fearlessly with Rama to Kishkindha and Vali's hiding place. 68-69) Upon their arrival, Sugreeva, the best of the monkeys, whose body was of a golden hue, let out a fearful roar which brought out the great monkey king. Having reassured his wife, Tara, Vali engaged in combat with Sugreeva. But Raghava slew him with a single arrow. 70) Having thus dispatched Vali as requested by Sugreeva, Lord Raghava personally inaugurated him as king. 71) The bull-like monkey then summoned all his subjects, and being eager to see Mother Sita, sent them in all directions to seek her out. 72) Upon the advice of the vulture Sampati, the powerful Hanuman leapt a full eight hundred miles across the salt ocean to Lanka. 73) There, in Ravana's capital, he found Sita in a grove of asoka trees, rapt in meditation. 74) Hanuman gave her a token sent by Rama, and having given her consolation and a full report of all that had occurred, he demolished an ornamental gate. 75) He next killed five of Ravana's lieutenants and seven of his ministers' sons, and after crushing the hero Aksha, he allowed himself to be seized. 76) Although he knew himself to be as good as released because of a particular weapon given as a boon by Brahma, the hero voluntarily endured the torments of the man-eating demons. 77) Shortly after, he burned the city of Lanka, sparing Sita, the Princess of Mithila, and returning to Lord Rama, gave Him the delightful news. 78) Approaching Rama, the great souled Hanuman circumambulated Him in a clockwise fashion and assured Him that he had truly seen Sita.

79\) "Rama then journeyed to the shore of the great ocean in the company of Sugreeva, and agitated its waters with arrows as brilliant as the sun. 80) Out of fear, the ocean, lord of rivers, revealed himself, and on his insistence Nala constructed a bridge. 81-82) Upon reaching the city of Lanka, Rama slew Ravana in battle and got back Sita, but He became somewhat ashamed \[of her residence in another man's house\] and publicly criticized her. Not being able to tolerate such treatment the chaste Sita entered fire. 83-84) By the words of Agnihowever, it could be understood that she was free from impurity. Lord Rama's extraordinary feat satisfied all the living beings within the universe, both moving and non-moving, and the blissful Raghava was duly adored by the assembled host of demigods. 85) Thereafter, the Lord installed Vibheeshanaupon the throne of Lanka, and considering that He had accomplished something worthwhile, He felt great ecstasy. 86) Heaving received a benediction from the Devas, all the monkeys were revived, and Rama flew to Ayodhya in a flower aeroplane with His relatives and friends. 87-88) Our Hero first visited the hermitage of Bharadvaja, and He sent Hanuman from thence to Bharata. After relating to the sage all that had occurred, He remounted His aircraft and flew to Nandigrama with Sugreeva. 89) At Nandigrama He and His brothers untangled their matted hair, and the sinless Rama thus got back Mother Sita and regained His kingdom. 90-91) During His reign all His subjects will be blissful and prosperous. They will all be very pious, free from lamentation and disease, famine and fear. Never will men see the death of their sons, nor will chaste ladies, being ever devoted to their spouses, see their demise. 92) Never will any man have need to fear fire, wind or fever, nor will any person ever drown in water. 93) No one will know hunger, nor fear of thieves, and both village and city will be replete with riches and grain. 94-95) Everyone will be as happy as they were in Satya-yuga. Hundreds of Asvamedha sacrifices will be performed, with abundant paraphernalia made of gold. Millions upon millions of cows will be donated to the learned according to the injunction of the scriptures, and untold wealth will be given to the glorious brahmanas. 96) Lord Raghava will establish a royal dynasty hundreds of times more glorious than ever before, and He will engage everyone in the world in the respective duties of his occupational class. 97) Having governed His kingdom for eleven-thousand years, Rama will ascend to the spiritual sky.

98\) "He who reads this sacred account of the deeds of Rama, which destroys evil and is equal in merit to the Vedas, is freed from all reactions to his past sinful deeds. 99) Any man who studies this life-giving Ramayana is transferred to the Kingdom of God at death, along with his sons, grandsons and followers. 100) Any brahmana who recites it becomes a powerful speaker, any kshatriya — an emperor. A vaisya who recites it gets the fruit of many pious deeds, and even a sudrabecomes glorious!"

*Thus ends the first canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 2. Brahma's Visit

After hearing Narada's description, the eloquent Valmeeki and his disciples worshipped the great sage. Then, having taken his leave, the powerful rishi rose into the sky. 3) Shortly after Narada left for his celestial abode, Valmeeki went to the bank of the river Tamasa, not far from the sacred Ganges. 4-5) Upon reaching the water's margin, and seeing the bank to be free from mud, he spoke thus to his disciple: "O Bharadvaja, behold, this spotless place of pilgrimage, with its charming and transparent water, is like the mind of a self realised soul! 6) Set down the pot and give me my bark clothing child; this place is ideal. I shall descend the bank just here." 7) Being so addressed, Bharadvaja dutifully obeyed his preceptor and handed his garment over to him. 8) Taking the bark from the hand of his disciple, Valmeeki, whose senses were restrained, began to stroll along the bank surveying the dense forest.

9\) The powerful sage noticed a pair of sweetly calling cranes not too far away, moving here and there, absorbed in the act of mating. 10) Then, while they were engrossed in the sexual-act, a cruel aboriginal killed the male bird as Valmeeki looked on. 11-12) Seeing her copper-crested husband killed, struck down by an arrow while his lust was inflamed, his bloodied wings still moving, the female let out a piteous cry. 13-15) Knowing the act of the Nishada to be sinful, and hearing the bird's pathetic calls, the sage was moved to pity, and he spoke the following to the hunter: "O Nishada, for countless years, Do not have a peaceful heart, for You have killed one of these creatures While it was absorbed in mating!"

16\) Having spoken in this manner, Valmeeki began to consider thus within his heart: "What is this that I have uttered out of pity for this bird?" 17) Dwelling upon the matter for some time, the vastly learned muni said the following to his disciple: 18) "This utterance of mine, brought about by pity \[soka\], would seem to have poetic symmetry, is fit to be sung to the accompaniment of the vina, and should therefore be known as a 'stanza' \[sloka\], and not otherwise!" 19) Delighted by his preceptor's extraordinary enunciation, Bharadvaja committed it to memory, thus pleasing the sage. 20) Valmeeki then took his bath at that holy place according to the scriptural ordinance, and still meditating upon the incident, he returned to his hermitage. 21) Bharadvaja, his humble and learned disciple, followed behind it is said, carrying the waterpot. 22) Upon reaching his ashram, the learned sage took his seat and began to discourse on other subject matters, while his mind stayed rapt in contemplation.

23\) At that time, the four-faced creator of the universe, Lord Brahma, of great splendour, came to visit the ashram. 24) Beholding him, Valmeeki quickly rose from his seat, greatly astonished, and stood silently, his palms joined. 25) He then worshipped the lord, according to the scriptural rule, by offering him a proper seat, washing his feet and hands, bowing to him and enquiring after his welfare. 26) Taking an elevated seat, the powerful lord indicated to Valmeeki that he should also be seated. 27-30) The sage sat himself down as requested, but even though the grandsire of all creatures was directly present before him, his mind was still sunk in reverie. He considered thus: "What a cruel deed was perpetrated by that wicked sinner. He killed a sweetly singing creature without cause, his intelligence carried away by enmity." Grieving for the bird's mate, he repeated the stanza he had spoken to the hunter, and being absorbed in such thoughts, he gave himself up to grief.

Lord Brahma then laughed and addressed the powerful sage as follows: 31) "Do not forswear the stanza you composed, O brahmana, nor brood upon it any more. By my will alone did this utterance issue from your lips. 32) You should describe in their entirety the earthly deeds of the greatly intelligent, Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Rama. 33-35) Tell the resolute Lord's history just as you have heard it from Narada, both in terms of those things which are evident and those which are confidential. Relate everything regarding the determined Lord Rama, together with Lakshmana, and the Rakshasas, for anything, even if it be unknown, will become known to you. Nothing whatsoever in your poem will be found to be untrue. 36-38) Compose this sacred glorification of Lord Rama in stanzas which are pleasing to the mind. As long as mountains stand and rivers flow upon the earth, the message of the Ramayana will be propagated amongst the people of the world. As long as your description of the travels of Rama is current, you will reside in my creation, both in the heavenly planets and here on earth."

Having thus spoken, Brahma vanished from that place, and the fortunate sage and his disciples were struck with wonder. 39) Valmeeki's happy disciples repeated his stanza again and again with great amazement. 40) "The muni's curse, uttered because of grief, took the form of a stanza divisible into four parts, each having equal numbers of syllables!" they declared. 41) The elated Valmeeki then came to the following decision: "I shall compose the entire Ramayana in the form of a poem made of such stanzas!" 42-43) The illustrious sage wrote the whole of the glorious Ramayana in hundreds and hundreds of beautiful verses, with all the literary and grammatical ornaments, and the sweet sentiments of devotion. Behold the rein the unequalled deeds of Raghava and the slaying of the ten-headed Ravana!

*Thus ends the second canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 3. Brief Outline of the Contents of the Ramayana

Having heard from Sri Narada the full essence of the Ramayana, which is in accordance with the goals of religiosity, the wise Valmeeki desired that everything be revealed to him. 2) He therefore touched water, according to the scriptural injunction, and joining his palms he sat on blades of kusa grass, their ends pointing to the east, and he began to meditate upon the matter. 3-4) By dint of his spiritual power Valmeeki was able to observe everything with regard to Rama, Lakshmana and Sita. He beheld King Dasaratha, his wives and his kingdom, and he observed how they all laughed, spoke, walked, and indeed, everything he wished to see. 5) He also saw how the truthful Rama moved about the forest together with Sita and Lakshmana.

6\) Situated in yogic trance, and unconscious of his surroundings, he witnessed the past as clearly as one sees a fruit held in the palm of one's hand. 7) Beholding the entire life of the beautiful Rama, the greatly intelligent muni commenced his narration of the Lord's deeds. 8-9) The sage described the exploits of the dynasty of Raghu, which contain elements conducive to sense pleasure, economic improvement and especially to religiosity, as he had heard them from Narada. His composition is like an ocean full of jewels, and it is as enchanting as the Vedas. 10) He fully delineated the nativity of Rama, His supreme power, His concern for all beings, His dearness to the people, His peacefulness, cheerfulness and truthfulness. 11) He described many different activities of the Lord and various narratives that the Lord heard in the company of Visvamitra.

He related Lord Rama's marriage to Janaki and the breaking of Siva's bow. 12-13) He described Rama's dispute with Parasurama and the qualities of Dasaratha as well. He narrated the anointing of Rama and the malignancy of Kaikeyi; the interruption of the installation of Rama and His exile to the forest; the grief and lamentation of His father the king and the latter's return to the spiritual world. 14-15) Valmeeki told of the dejection of the citizens of Ayodhya, and how the Lord abandoned them.

He narrated the Lord's conversation with the King of the Nishadas and the dismissal of His charioteer, the crossing of the Ganges, the meeting with Bharadvaja, and Their taking leave of the sage in order to go to Chitrakoota. 16) He described the building of a cottage and how Sita, Rama and Lakshmana established Their residence there; the arrival of Bharata, Rama's mercy, and how He offered libations of water to his departed father. 17) He recounted the installation of Rama's slippers and Bharata's residence at Nandigrama, the penetration of the Dandaka forest and the slaying of Viradha. 18-22) He described the following: the meeting with Sarabhanga and Suteekshna, Sita's sojourn with Anasooya and Sita receiving an offering of sandalwood paste upon her limbs; the meeting with Agastya and the gift of a bow; Soorpanakha's proposal and her disfigurement also; the killing of Khara and Trishira, Ravana's expedition, the slaying of Mareecha and the abduction of Sita; the meeting with Kabandha, the meetings at Lake Pampa and with Sabiree, and her offering of fruits and roots; Rama's incoherence and His meeting with Hanuman.

23\) Valmeeki described how Rama arrived at Mount Rishyamukha and met Sugreeva, how Rama inspired confidence in Himself, His bond of friendship with the monkey, and Sugreeva's fight with Vali. 24-26) He gave an account of the destruction of Vali, the installation of Sugreeva, Tara's lamentation, Lord Rama's covenant with Sugreeva and His residence at Kishkindha during the monsoon, Raghava's anger, the mustering of Sugreeva's forces and how they were dispatched in all directions, Sugreeva's description of the earth, the giving of Rama's ring to Hanuman, the finding of a Riksha's cave, the monkeys' determination to fast to death and their meeting with Sampati. 27-29) He described how Hanuman scaled a mountain and leapt across the sea; how, at the insistence of the sea-god, he encountered Mainaka and was threatened by two demonesses; his encounter with the shadow-thief, Simhika, and her destruction; Hanuman's nocturnal entry into the city of Lanka and his solitary meditations, his arrival at the banqueting hall and his discovery of Ravana's aeroplane. 30-33) He described Hanuman's inspection of the aircraft, how he observed Ravana, his arrival at the asoka grove and his discovery of Sita; how he made himself known to Sita by means of a token, his conversation with Sita; how she was threatened by the Rakshasees; the dream of Trijata; how Sita gave Hanuman a jewel; how he destroyed the grove; how the demonesses fled and the annihilation of Ravana's servants; the capture of Hanuman, son of the Wind, and his roars as he set Lanka on fire; his leap back across the ocean and his theft of honey.

34-35) Valmeeki described how Lord Raghava was solaced, and the handing over of Sita's jewel; the meeting with the presiding deity of the ocean and Nala's construction of a bridge; the contact with Vibheeshana and the disclosure of the means of killing Ravana and others. 36-38) He gave an account of the destruction of Kumbakarna, Meghanada and Ravana; the recovery of Mother Sita from the enemy's capital; the inauguration of Vibheeshana, and the inspection of the flower-aeroplane; the flight to Ayodhya and the meeting with Baradvaja; the dispatching of Hanuman to Bharata and the Lord's reunion with Him; Rama's inaugural bath and the disbanding of the simian army; the rejoicing of the citizens, and lastly, even those deeds of Lord Rama which had not yet been enacted on earth were described by the great sage.

*Thus ends the third canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 4. The Ramayana Sung by Kusa and Lava

The greatly powerful sage, Valmeeki muni, described the deeds of Lord Rama, Who had regained His kingdom, in beautiful, meaningful verses. 2) He rendered the Ramayana in twenty-four thousand eloquent stanzas, divided into about five-hundred cantos, and six kandas, as well as an epilogue. 3) Having completed the work, which included descriptions of deeds not yet performed by Rama, the intelligent muni began to consider who might be capable of its recitation. 4) While he was reflecting upon the matter, Kusa and Lava, wearing the dress of renunciates, approached him and caught hold of his feet. 5) The ascetic observed the renowned brothers, sons of King Rama, who were well versed in spiritual matters, were possessed of excellent voices, and who resided in the sage's hermitage. 6-7) Noting their intelligence and their complete familiarity with the Vedas, in order to increase their knowledge, Valmeeki taught them the entire Ramayana, the great history of Mother Sita, which he entitled: 'The slaying of Ravana'.

**8-9)** The two princes sang this poem, which was sweet to read and to recite, which could be sung with three different musical measures and the seven notes, to the accompaniment of the vina. It fully contained the spiritual mellows of conjugal love, compassion, laughter, heroism, dread, chivalry and so on. 10) They knew the art of the Gandharvas and how the lungs, cerebrum and nasal passage should be employed to produce the proper sounds; they sang as sweetly, and were as beautiful to behold as the celestial singers. 11) Their bodies were endowed with all auspicious characteristics and their voices were beautifully modulated; they appeared like two rising suns, and having issued from the transcendental body of Rama they appeared like two Ramas.

**12-13)** The two irreproachable princes performed the entire Ramayana, which they committed to memory and which is of the highest authoritativeness, in the assembly of sages, brahmanas and ascetics.

**14-17)** Once, the two blessed princes sang the poem at a gathering of powerful sages. Hearing the wonderful narratives, their eyes brimming with tears, they spoke the following to Kusa and Lava with great pleasure and amazement: "Oh, what a sweet song, and the verses are especially exquisite! 18-20) Although these narratives are of things which occurred long ago, it is as if they took place before our eyes! Because the boys have completely entered into the spirit of the composition they have sung it attractively and with perfect devotion." Being thus applauded by the rishis, who were celebrated for their asceticism, the princes were enthused to sing with even greater love and sweetness.

Pleased by their performance, a certain sage rose from his seat and gave them a water-pot. 21) One famous muni gave them a gift of tree-bark clothing, another the skin of a black deer, and yet another a sacred thread. 22) One sage gave them a kamandalu, another great muni a sacred girdle of grass; one gave a kusa-grass mat, another, an undergarment. 23) One blissful ascetic donated them an axe, another, rust coloured cloth. 24) They were given a hair-band, a rope for binding a bundle of wood, a sacrificial vessel, and sacrificial fuel. 25) With great ecstasy, one muni presented them a seat of audumbri wood, another blessed them with good fortune, while one great soul benedicted them with duration of life. 26) In this way every one of the truthful sages gave some token to Kusa and Lava. "What an amazing narration Valmeeki has composed!" they declared. 27) "This hymn, composed as it is with due regard to chronology, will serve as an example to other poets. You have sung this composition with great skill! 28-29) It is conducive to long life and is productive of prosperity. It is as enchanting as any of the scriptures."

It so happened that Lord Rama saw the princes, who were applauded wherever they roamed, wandering upon the highays of the capital, and brought them to His palace. 30-32) After giving them a fitting reception, Rama seated Himself upon a divine golden throne, and the two brothers sat along with the Lord's associates. Observing the beauty and humility of the two brothers, Lord Rama spoke to Lakshmana, Bharata and Satrughna as follows: "Please listen to the recitation of these two divinely brilliant persons."

**33-34)** The Lord then urged Kusa and Lava to sing the celebrated Ramayana, which is replete with wonderful verses. They sang as sweetly as the sound of the vina, with devotion, and melodious voices. Their song thrilled all one's limbs, one's mind and one's heart, and it gladdened the ears of those who heard it in that assembly. 35) "These two powerful ascetics, Kusa and Lava, are marked with the characteristics of the princely order", said Rama. "Kindly be attentive to their wonderfully ecstatic narration, which describes My superhuman deeds." 36) Thus, as requested by Lord Rama, they sang the Ramayana in the style known as Marga; and in that assembly, the Lord quietly allowed His mind to become absorbed in their song.

*Thus ends the fourth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 5. Description of Ayodhya — the Narration Commences

Since long past, the entire world has been in the care of valorous monarchs, beginning with the progenitor of men. 2) In their line appeared King Sagara, on whose account the ocean bed was excavated, and who, when he marched, was followed by sixty-thousand sons. 3) It is said that this Ramayana has arisen in the dynasty of kings which began with Ikshvaku. 4) We shall relate the entire history, which has elements of religiosity, sensual pleasure and material advancement, from the very beginning; may it be heard only by those who are without envy!

5\) Situated on the bank of the Sarayu is an extensive kingdom called Kosala. Its inhabitants are happy and wealthy, and have sufficient food grains. 6) In that realm is a celebrated city known as Ayodhya, which was constructed under the personal supervision of Manu, the lord of men. 7) That great city is ninety-six miles in length and twenty-four in breadth, and is divided by a broad traffic way. 8-9) The king's highway is daily sprinkled with water and strewn with flowers, thus beautifying the capital of King Dasaratha, which he has caused to be as populous as the city of the king of the Devas. 10) The city has many doors and portals, festooned with garlands of flowers; there are well arranged markets; it is protected by all kinds of deadly war machines, and numerous craftsmen are to be found there. 11) It throngs with charioteers and grooms, with actors and singers, it shines with the brilliance of innumerable Goddesses of Fortune, and there are tall watchtowers decorated with flags and bristling with hundreds of cannons.

12\) Troupes of female dancers are to be found throughout the metropolis, and there are gardens and mango groves enclosed within high walls. 13) Around the city is a deep moat, which is difficult to cross, and the entire capital is fortified in such a way that it is unassailable.

Ayodhya is crowded with horses and elephants, cows, camels and mules. 14) There are various kings come to pay their yearly tribute to the emperor and splendid looking merchants from many lands. 15-16) There are gorgeous palaces like great mountains decorated with gems; there are lofty attics, and altogether the city is wonderfully arrayed, having the appearance of a chess board.

There are groups of the most beautiful womenfolk; every dwelling is covered with precious stones, and there are even exquisite aerial mansions. 17) There is row upon row of closely constructed residences, all equal in splendour; there is an abundance of rice, and drinking water as sweet as sugar-cane juice. 18) The air vibrates with the sounds of kettle drums, mridangas, vinas and cymbals, and it is altogether the most powerful and extraordinary city in the world. 19) It has superbly constructed dwellings which look like the aeroplanes of the divine Siddhas, which are achieved by penance, and it is the resort of the best and most refined men.

20-21) Ayodhya throngs with powerful warriors, adept in the use of a variety of weapons, who are highly skilled and chivalrous — who do not break the codes of war by killing solitary men, men without issue, or men whose position is fixed by the sound of their voice or the clatter of arms — and who are practised in the killing of dangerous bellowing beasts in the forest, such as lions, tigers and boars.

22\) In such a fashion did King Dasaratha cause Ayodhya to be peopled by thousands of great fighters, as well as by the most qualified brahmanas, who maintained the sacred fire and were conversant with the six branches of Vedic wisdom; by men of great magnanimity and honesty, and by sages of the very highest order.

*Thus ends the fifth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 6. Defence of the Kingdom of Dasaratha

In the city of Ayodhya dwelt King Dasaratha. He lacked nothing. He was the protector of the world in exactly the way Manu had been, and the residents of both town and village held him very dear. He was powerful, farsighted, strong, and the conqueror of both his adversaries and his senses. He was a formidable warrior, born in the dynasty of Ikshvaku. He was devoted to righteous conduct and to the performance of sacrifice. Indeed, his fame as a saintly king, the equal of a great sage, was celebrated throughout the universe. He had amassed considerable wealth, and on that account he vied with Indra and Kuvera. 5) Ayodhya was as well protected by the truthful Dasaratha as the celestial city Amaravatee is guarded by Indra. 6) The people of that best of cities were blissful, fully devoted to the Godhead and learned in the scriptures. They were honest, wealthy and free from avarice. 7) Not a single householder dwelt there who had not acquired a goodly fortune in the form of cows, horse, treasure and grain. 8) Every lady and gentleman in the city was of righteous character. They were neighbourly and joyful, and their spotless character made them as good as great sages.

9\) There was no one who did not wear earrings, crown and flower garlands, or whose senses were not satisfied; neither was there anyone who was unwashed or whose limbs were not anointed with sandalwood paste and perfumes. 10) Nobody in Ayodhya would consume impure foodstuffs or would be uncharitable. All were adorned with golden ornaments such as bracelets, all were self-controlled. 11) There was no one who had not served the sacred fire, neither were there any ignoble persons or thieves, nor was there any unwanted progeny there.

12\) Pure brahmanas dwelt there, who were always deeply absorbed in the performance of their duties. They gave charitably, studied the Vedas, and restrained themselves from accepting more than was necessary for their maintenance. 13) Not one of them was in the least bit atheistic, deceitful or devoid of scriptural knowledge. None of them were envious, impotent or ignorant.

14\) Not a single inhabitant of Ayodhya was unfamiliar with the supplementary Vedic sciences, was unable to perform penances, was not a donor of thousands of cows. No one was miserable, distracted in mind, or afflicted with misery. 15) Every man and woman in the city was fortunate, wealthy, beautiful to behold, and devoted to the king. 16) The members of the four occupational orders, headed by the brahmanas, were accustomed to worship guests and the demigods. They were all mindful of favours; they were grateful, heroic and forceful. 17) They were all longlived, and having resorted to piety and truth, were never parted from their sons, grandsons and wives. 18) The brahmanas governed the kshatriyas, the vaisyas were obedient to the kshatriyas and the sudras happily rendered service to the other three castes. 19) King Dasaratha protected the city just as its wise founder, Manu, lord of mankind, had done in days of yore. 20-21) Being filled with warriors who were passionate and proficient in the arts of war, the city resembled a cave full of lions. They were mounted on the best steeds, bred in Bahleeka, Vanayu and Kambhoja, and born of rivers. 22) The city was crowded with intoxicated elephants born in the Vindhyas and Himalayas, which were as huge as mountains and possessed of prodigious strength. 23-24) There were elephants descended from Airavata, and belonging to the breeds known as Mahapadma, Anjana, Vamana, Bhadra, Mandra and Mriga, all decked out in golden ornaments, as well as cross breeds known as Bhadramandra-mriga, Bhadra-mandra, Bhadra-mriga and Mrigamandra. 25) Ayodhya was thus full of mountainous elephants always in rut. Ayodhya means 'Unassailable', and the city was rightly named, being impregnable for sixteen miles. And while he resided there, King Dasaratha protected the whole world. 26) The greatly powerful Dasaratha ruled that principality just as the moon rules the starry firmament. 27) He governed the happy city called 'Unassailable', which had massive gates furnished with mighty bolts, where there were mansions of dazzling splendour, and which thronged with thousands of citizens, just like Indra, the king of heaven.

*Thus ends the sixth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 7. Description of the Emperor's Ministers.

The ministers of the very exalted King Dasaratha were endowed with all good qualities; they were experienced in giving counsel, were expert in divining the thoughts and intentions of others, and were devoted to their beloved sovereign. 2) They were eight in number and were heroic, laudable, pure, loyal, and faithful to the interests of the king. 3) Their names were Dhrishti, Jayanta, Vijaya, Surastra, Rastravardhana, Akopa and Dharmapala — Sumantra being the eighth.

4-5) His beloved family priests were the great sages Vasishta and Vamadeva, and there were other counsellors, such as Suyajna, Jabali, Kasyapa, Gautama, the ancient Markandeya and the twice-born Katyayana. 6) These brahma-rishis acted as advisors in concert with his ministers and family priests. The king's ministers were all humble as a result of their knowledge of the Self; they were modest, sharp-witted and had fully controlled senses. 7) They were affluent, great souled, and conversant with weaponry; they were firm, powerful, renowned, sympathetic and obedient. 8) They were energetic, forgiving, accomplished, and their speech was accompanied with smiles. They would never speak an untruth either in anger, nor for the purpose of fulfilling some personal desire. 9) There were no secrets kept between them, neither were they unaware of anything whatsoever regarding their kin or any other persons. They learnt the intentions of the citizens by means of spies. 10) These expert ministers had all been examined in the matter of loyalty and would take the rod to their own sons if necessary. 11) They were eager to swell the king's treasury and to strengthen his armed forces. They were not known to injure even an enemy if he were not actually a transgressor. 12) These heroic and zealous gentlemen were well versed in the diplomatic, administrative and military arts, and were prepared to give full protection to virtuous citizens.

13\) They filled the state coffers without harassing the brahmanas and kshatriyas, and they administered stringent punishment upon wrongdoers, having first ascertained the seriousness of a man's crime and his ability to endure punishment. 14) As a result of the ministers' purity and single-mindedness not a single citizen was to be found either in the city, or anywhere in the realm, that was in the least bit dishonest. 15) No one was at all wicked in the entire kingdom; not even a single adulterer was to be encountered there. Everyone was peaceful and content.

16\) All of the king's ministers were arrayed in fine clothing, were resolutely pure, and were devoted to the interests of the king, seeing everything with the eye of prudence. 17) They had the full blessings of the king's preceptor. They were able to come to conclusions about any matter by means of good intelligence and were even well known in foreign lands.

18\) They were qualified all round, not lacking a single virtue; they were conversant with waging war and maintaining peace, and were crowned with glory. 19) They were well able to conceal secret designs as well as to comprehend subtle issues. They were especially well-versed in the scriptures dealing with political science and always spoke endearingly. 20) The sinless King Dasaratha ruled the earth with the help of such faultless and qualified ministers. 21) Observing the citizens by means of spies, they protected the realm according to religious principles. They gave full protection to the people and shunned irreligiousness.

22\) The magnanimous King Dasaratha, who was glorified throughout the three worlds, who was true to his promise and, like a tiger among men, ruled the entire world. 23) Aided by his followers, the peerless King Dasaratha, who was never bested by an enemy, plucked thorn-like persons from the side of the state, brought all the neighbouring kings under his dominion, and governed the earth like Indra governs the heavenly planet. 24) Together with his counsellors, who were given the task of advising him and were sagacious, capable and loyal, he shone with the dazzling splendour of the rising sun.

*Thus ends the seventh canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 8. The King Resolves to Perform a Horse Sacrifice

Despite performing austerities in order to get a son, the great-souled Dasaratha was without an heir. 2) While contemplating his lack of issue, the following occurred to him: "Why should I not perform a horse sacrifice for the pleasure of the Lord and for the purpose of getting a son?" 3-4) He made a firm resolution to perform just such a sacrifice in consultation with all of his self-disciplined ministers. "Sumantra," he said, "best of counsellors, quickly bring our guru and our family priests." 5) Sumantra departed at once, walking briskly, and returned with those preceptors, who were well-versed in the Veda. 6) There was Suyajna, Jabali and Kasyapa, King Dasaratha's family priest Vasishta and others, all highly elevated, twice-born men. 7) Having duly worshipped them, the great-souled sovereign addressed them with words full of piety and sincerity. 8) "Although I have wept in order to get a son, I have achieved no happiness at all. I have therefore decided that I ought to perform a horse sacrifice. 9) It is my desire that it should be performed in accordance with the injunctions of the scriptures. How shall I have my desire fulfilled? Please consider what is to be done." 10) All the assembled brahmanas headed by Vasishta, immediately applauded the king's proposal saying: "Well done! Well done!" 11) With great happiness, they addressed King Dasaratha saying: "Your Majesty should collect the ingredients required for the successful performance of the sacrifice, and the horse should be released! 12-13) The sacrificial arena should be established on the north bank of the Sarayu. There is no doubt that as a result of Your Majesty's pious design that you will get the sons you desire."

The king was delighted by the brahmanas' words. 14) He replied as follows, his eyes brimming with tears of joy: "Kindly prepare whatever is necessary under the guidance of my preceptors. 15) Let the horse be released together with a suitable military force and the presiding priest! Prepare the sacrificial arena on the north bank of the Sarayu! 16-17) Perform the propitiatory rites elaborately, in due sequence and according to the scriptural rule. This sacrifice can be successfully performed by any of the kings of this earth provided one avoids offences and guards against evils in the performance of this superior rite. Wicked brahmarakshasas who are familiar with Vedic ritual are ever eager to find some defect in a sacrificial performance and spoil it. 18-19) If one performs such a ritual without following the prescribed regulations, he is immediately vanquished. I therefore beg you that the sacrifice I have proposed is carefully performed, according to the prescribed rules, and brought to a successful conclusion; for you are all extremely competent."

20\) Having heard the proclamation of the king, the assembled brahmanas blessed him, saying: "Let it be so!" 21) Taking their leave, those learned brahmanas left as they had come and, having dismissed them, King Dasaratha addressed his ministers as follows: "Let the sacrifice be concluded according to the directions of the priests!" 22-23) Having thus dispatched his counsellors, that best of kings entered his private apartments and met his beloved wives. "Begin the preparatory rites," he said, "I am going to sacrifice for a son!" 24) Hearing this delightful news, their beautiful faces blossomed like lotuses at the close of winter.

*Thus ends the eighth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 9. The King Acquainted with Rishyasringa

Hearing of the kings's intention to perform the Asvamedha sacrifice, Sumantra, the king's charioteer, spoke to his master in private: "My liege, kindly listen to an ancient prophecy I heard in a Puranic recitation. 2-3) Formerly, I heard a prediction regarding this ceremony which your priests are now arranging. Once, in the assembly of sages, the greatly powerful Sanatkumara related certain events with regard to the birth of your son. 'There is a son of Kasyapa Muni', he said, 'by the name of Vibhandaka. 4) He will have a son called Rishyasringa who will dwell in the forest, ever wandering from one place to another, and living the life of an ascetic.

5\) 'This king among brahmanas will know no one apart from his father, whom he will constantly serve, and he will strictly adhere to the principles of brahmacharya.6-9) He will be celebrated by twice-born men throughout the world, O king, and he will pass his days in worship of the sacred fire and serving his noble father. At this time, a mighty king by the name of Romapada will rule the country of Anga. Because of some offence on his part, a terrible drought will arise that will cause great anguish to all creatures. Due to want of rain the king will be afflicted with great unhappiness. 10) Bringing together brahmanas advanced in scriptural knowledge, he will announce the following: "Good sirs, you have heard of my misdeeds and are learned in the ways of the world. 11) Please recommend some means by which I may atone for my offence."

12-13) 'Those brahmanas, being learned in the Vedas, will give the king the following reply: "O king, by any means possible bring the son of Vibhandaka, the learned Rishyasringa, to your capital. Give him the hand of your foster daughter, Santa, according to the proper rule and with a controlled mind." 14) Hearing their counsel, King Romapada will reflect upon the means by which to bring the mighty sage to his kingdom. 15) In consultation with his self-possessed ministers, he will order his righteous family priest and counsellors to the forest to fetch Rishyasringa.

16\) 'Perturbed by his request, their heads bowed, they will submit the following: "We cannot go, O king. We fear we may arouse the sage's anger", and they will attempt to pacify the king. 17) They will consider the means of luring the ascetic to the city. "We shall bring the brahmana here", they will say, "in such a manner that we will remain blameless".

18\) 'Thus King Romapada will have Rishyasringa lured to his capital by beautiful courtesans, and when his daughter is bestown upon the sage, Indra will cause it to rain.' 19) My lord, it is this selfsame Rishyasringa, your son-in-law, who will procure your sons for you. Thus I have recounted Sanatkumara's prophecy." 20) Elated by this revelation, King Dasaratha replied to Sumantra, saying, "Kindly tell me by what means Rishyasringa was brought to Romapada's capital."

*Thus ends the ninth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 10. The Mode of Fetching Rishyasringa to the Capital

Being urged to continue by the king, Sumantra spoke as follows: "Your Majesty, please hear from me, along with your ministers, how Rishyasringa was brought to the capital of King Romapada. 2) The king's family priest, together with his advisors, addressed Romapada thus: 'We have carefully devised the following infallible plan', they said. 3) 'The sage Rishyasringa is a simple forest dweller; he is austere, studious, and consequently completely ignorant where women and sense pleasures are concerned. 4) We shall attract him with the lure of sensual pleasures and arouse in him those desires that stimulate the minds of men. Let the necessary steps be taken and we shall quickly bring him here. 5) Send beautiful, well decorated prostitutes to him, and by various feminine wiles they will lure him here.'

6\) "Hearing the proposal of his priest, Romapada gave his consent, 'So be it!' he said. His priest then commanded the king's ministers in the matter, and they immediately complied. 7-8) The most beautiful courtesans in the city responded to the king's command and entered the great forest. They stayed quite close to the hermitage of the sage Vibhandaka and attempted to somehow or other meet with his sober-minded son. Rishyasringa, for his part, was fully satisfied serving his father, and never strayed from the ashram. 9) Since the day of his birth the ascetic had never seen a woman, or any man apart from his father. He had seen neither one born in a village nor one born in a town. 10) One day he happened to come, quite by chance, to the place where the women were residing and caught sight of the beautiful creatures.

"Gorgeously apparelled, those wanton women were singing a sweet song. They approached the sage's son and began to enquire from him as follows: 12) 'We should like to know who you are, O brahmana, and why you are wandering abroad on your own in this uninhabited and remote forest. Please tell us.' 13) Rishyasringa had never in his life seen such beauties as these in the forest — women with desirable forms — and within his heart there arose the desire to inform them of the name of his father. 14) 'My father', he said, 'is Vibhandaka and I am his begotten son. My name is Rishyasringa, and my name and deeds are celebrated throughout this land. 15) Our hermitage is close by, O beautiful ones! Do come there with me. I shall worship you all according to the scriptural rule!'

16\) "Hearing his entreaty, those comely women went with him on the plea of wanting to see his home. 17) Arriving at his cottage, he worshipped them all. 'Here is arghya',he said, 'and here is padya.Here are our vegetables and our fruits!'

18\) "They accepted his worship but felt rather uneasy, and decided to leave shortly, fearing the return of the sage's father. 19) 'O brahmana, here are our most excellent fruits,' they said, 'be so good as to eat them quickly, for they will do you good.' 20) With great delight they began to embrace him, and fed him sweetmeats and a variety of other delicacies. 21) He ate them with relish, taking them to indeed be some kind of fruit, never having tasted anything but fruit before in his life in the forest. 22) Then, on the pretext that they had to attend to some religious observance, the women departed, fearful that Rishyasringa's father might return.

23\) "After their departure, Rishyasringa felt uneasy in his heart and, being unhappy withal, he began to wander about disconsolately. 24-26) The next day, the powerful son of Vibhandaka, who could not rid his mind of thoughts of those gorgeously dressed women, went to the spot where he had encountered them the previous day. As soon as they saw him, they eagerly approached him and spoke sweet words to him. 'Please come with us to our cottage, O best of men', they said. 27) 'You will get a more wonderful variety of vegetables and fruits to eat than is to be found here!' 28) Hearing their captivating words, he decided to go with them, and they therefore led him away. 29) As the brahmana was being escorted to the city, Indra suddenly sent a shower of rain which brought delight to the world. 30) The sage arrived with the rains and was approached by King Romapada who, with all humility, bowed his head to the ground before him. 31) He offered the sage arghya, as befits a great personality, and begged the favour that his father might not be angered by what had occurred. 32) Having entered the women's apartments, and duly bestowing his foster daughter Santa upon the brahmana with a peaceful mind, the king became overjoyed. 33) Thereafter, Rishyasringa, who was worshippable, and of great power, dwelt there with all desirable things in the company of his wife, Santa."

*Thus ends the tenth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 11. Dasaratha Brings Rishyasringa to Ayodhya

Sumantra continued, saying, "O king of kings, please hear the kind words uttered by the intelligent Sanatkumara: 2) 'In the dynasty of Ikshvaku there will arise a pious, opulent, and truthful king by the name of Dasaratha. 3) He will establish a friendly relationship with the king of the Angas, who will have an extremely fortunate foster daughter called Santa. 4) That monarch, whose father will be known as King Anga, will be celebrated by the name Romapada. And in due course of time Maharaja Dasaratha will approach him with respect to his lack of issue. 5) "O Righteous one," he will say, "kindly permit your son-in-law to perform a sacrifice for the sake of my family, for I am without an heir."

6\) " 'Having heard this request, he will consider the matter, and then will hand over Rishyasringa, who will by that time have a son of his own. 7) King Dasaratha will convey the brahmana to his kingdom, his mental agony at an end, and he will perform the sacrifice with a blissful heart. 8-9) With joined palms, being desirous of glory, the monarch will invite Rishyasringa to preside over the rite, especially over the pressing of juice from the soma plant, and he will have his desire for a place in heaven fulfilled because of that superior brahmana. 10) He will obtain four sons of unsurpassable valour, who will perpetuate his dynasty and will be celebrated among all living beings.' 11) Thus, my liege, did the powerful personality, Sanatkumara, prophecy in the last Satya-yuga. 12) You should therefore bring Rishyasringa to Ayodhya, with due honour, going personally to fetch him with a contingent of infantry, chariots, elephants and conveyances, O tiger among men."

13-14) King Dasaratha was thrilled by Sumantra's disclosure, and after consulting Vasishta he proceeded towards the brahmana's residence in the company of his wives and ministers, his retinue gradually passing through forests and fording streams. 15-20) At length they reached the country where Rishyasringa dwelt. They beheld the sage by the side of King Romapada, his body resplendent like fire. And with great happiness King Romapada worshipped Maharaja Dasaratha — with due regard to his rank, and specifically on account of their amity. The king informed Rishyasringa of his friendship and familial connection with the emperor,and the sage then returned his salutation.

Maharaja Dasaratha was thus afforded a suitable welcome, and, having abided as the king's guest for seven or eight days, addressed the sovereign thus: "My dear king, please permit your daughter, Santa, and her good husband to go to my capital, for I need his assistance in an undertaking of great moment." King Romapada immediately assented and promised that Rishyasringa would accompany him. 21) He told the brahmana to proceed to Ayodhya with his wife, and Rishyasringa responded saying "Let it be so!"

22-23) Taking permission, the sage departed for Ayodhya, and the two monarchs offered each other respects with joined palms. With happiness and affection the heroic kings heartily embraced, and with Maharaja Romapada's consent, King Dasaratha left for his capital. 24-48) He despatched swift heralds with the following orders for his subjects: "Let the entire city be gorgeously bedecked. Let incense be burnt, have water sprinkled on the roads and let the buildings be festooned with flags!" Delighted to hear of the king's imminent arrival, the townsfolk did everything according to his instructions. Placing Rishyasringa at the head of his company, King Dasaratha then entered the decorated city to the tumultuous blasts of conches and the thundering of kettledrums. Every one of his subjects was thrilled to see the brahmana entering with such honour, and he was conducted into the city by the king in the same way as Vamanadevawas conducted into the celestial kingdom by the thousandeyed Indra.

29\) Ushering him into his private apartments, King Dasaratha worshipped the sage according to the scriptural ordinance and, having brought him there, he thought it the accomplishment of his life. 30) The king's wives, on seeing the large-eyed Santa and her husband, also experienced great bliss. 31) For her part, Santa, being treated reverentially by the queens, and especially by the emperor, dwelt there happily for some time with her spouse.

*Thus ends the eleventh canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 12. Collecting Ingredients for the Sacrifice

After a considerable time had elapsed, a gorgeous spring set in, and King Dasaratha decided that the preparations for the sacrifice should begin. 2) He offered obeisance to Rishyasringa with his head to the ground, and begged him to perform the sacrifice, for the sake of an heir and the perpetuation of his dynasty. 3) The sage gave reply as follows: "Collect the ingredients required for the sacrifice, O king, and free the horse. 4) Let the sacrificial arena be established on the northern bank of the Sarayu."

The king then gave the following command: "Quickly summon learned brahmanas, well-versed in the Veda. Bring Suyajna, Vamadeva, Jabali and Kasyapa. 5-7) Bring our family priest, Vasishta, and other elevated brahmanas."

Going swiftly, the fast-striding Sumantra brought all those brahmanas who were expert scholars of the Vedas into the presence of the pious king, who saluted them reverentially. 8) He next addressed them with sweet, sanctified, and meaningful words. "My austerities, performed for the purpose of getting a son," he said, "have not brought me any satisfaction. 9-10) I therefore concluded that I should perform a horse sacrifice, with a view to be getting an heir, and I am certain that my desires will be fulfilled by the power of Rishyasringa." At that, the assembled brahmanas applauded his speech saying: "Well done! Well done!"

11-12) All the assembled brahmanas, headed by Vasishta, as well as Rishyasringa and his assistants, made reply to the king's infallible statement, saying, "Gather all the prerequisites for the ritual, O king. Set free the horse! Prepare the sacrificial arena on the northern bank of the Sarayu! 13) Having decided upon such a virtuous means of getting offspring, Your Majesty will, without any doubt, be blessed with four sons of immeasurable might." 14) King Dasaratha was elated by the approving words of the brahmanas, and he gave the following instructions to his ministers: 15-18) "Undertake the necessary arrangements as you are commanded by my preceptors. Release the horse, together with a strong military attachment and the presiding priest. Prepare the site for the yajna on the north bank of the Sarayu. Ensure that there is a peaceful atmosphere and that everything is done systematically and according to the scriptural injunctions. There is no reason why this ritual should not be successfully concluded by any monarch, provided he guards against offences and avoids evils in the performance of this most excellent of sacrifices. Malicious ghosts, who were learned brahmanas in their past birth, seek defects in the performance of sacrifices, and one who commits errors of omission in such a sacrifice is immediately destroyed. 19) If my instructions are strictly implemented and the rules and procedures properly adhered to, then you are no doubt quite competent to conclude this undertaking."

20\) The king's ministers happily consented to their sovereign's edict saying, "So be it!" and did as they were commanded to do. 21) The brahmanas then eulogised the righteous king and, taking leave of him, they left as they had come. 22) Upon the departure of his priests, and having dismissed his ministers, the sagacious King Dasaratha entered his private chambers.

*Thus ends the twelfth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 13. The Entry into the Sacrificial Hall

Once again spring came, a full year having elapsed, and the mighty king thought to commence the horse sacrifice for the sake of getting progeny. 2) He humbly addressed Vasishta, having first greeted and worshipped him a befitting fashion: 3) "O embodiment of spiritual knowledge, let my sacrifice be performed according to the scriptural ordinance. O best of sages, kindly ensure that no obstruction occurs in any part of it! 4) Your Holiness is both my greatest well-wisher and spiritual guide, and the burden of this sacrifice I have undertaken is to be borne by you alone."

5\) "Let it be so," replied that superexcellent brahmana. "I shall do everything you desire." 6) Vasishta then conferred with elderly and supremely righteous brahmanas, who were well versed in the execution of sacrifices and in sacrificial architecture. 7-8) He recruited labourers and craftsmen, carpenters and excavators, astrologers and artists, dancers and actors, as well as pure souls learned in the scriptures and possessed of prodigious scholarship. "Perform your respective duties zealously," he told them, "as decreed by His Majesty the Emperor. 9) Let thousands of bricks be brought without delay, and palaces be erected that are furnished with every luxury befitting kings. 10) Let hundreds of goodly dwellings, equipped with a wide variety of eatables and drinkables, be constructed for brahmanas. 11) Well-appointed and spacious accommodation should also be provided for the citizens, and foreign kings who have come from afar should each have a residence of their own. 12) There should be stables for both horses and elephants. There should be dormitories for our troops and guest houses for visitors from abroad. 13-14) Every residence should have an ample supply of foodstuffs and anything our guests may desire should be provided for.

The king's subjects should be fed magnificently; nothing should be done light-heartedly, but according to the scriptural rule, so that all the members of the four varnaswill receive proper worship. 15) Those artisans who are who are engaged in their respective duties should not be treated contemptuously out of anger, lust or because of their inferior station. 16) Let them all be worshipped, as long as they are performing their prescribed tasks, with gifts of money and foodstuffs. 17) As long as you act with an affectionate mentality, then everything will be well accomplished, and nothing will be left undone!"

18\) Approaching Vasishta, all those connected with the enterprise assured him thus: "We shall do everything to the best of our ability, and nothing shall be left undone. 19) Everything will be done just as you have said and nothing shall be wanting."

Vasishta then called Sumantra, and spoke to him as follows: 20) "You should invite whatever kings are to be found on the face of the earth, as well as thousands of brahmanas, vaisyas and sudras. 21-22) You should treat such refined men with respect, no matter what their place of origin may be.

"You should invite the truthful hero, Janaka, King of Mithila, and bring him personally to Ayodhya with all due honour. I especially mention him because of his pre-ordained relationship with the Emperor. 23) You should personally bring the king of Kashi, who is affectionately disposed towards the Emperor. He is sweetly spoken, virtuous, and has the semblance of a demigod. 24) Bring the 44 Srimad Ramayana elderly king of the Keykayas and his son. He is profoundly religious and is father-in-law to the Emperor. 25) Bring the lord of the Angas, King Romapada, the great bowman, together with his son. He is of course the intimate friend of the Emperor. 26-27) Bring the splendid king of Kosala and the heroic king of Maghada, Pratiprajna by name. He is well versed in the scriptures and is a bull among men. Invite the eastern kings, the rulers of Sindhusauveera and Saurastra, in accordance with the Emperor's decree. 28) Bring all the kings from the south, and any friendly monarch that rules anywhere in the world. 29) Bring them all quickly, together with their relatives, by means of greatly distinguished ambassadors."

30\) Having heard Vasishta's command, Sumantra immediately began to direct various persons in the business of fetching those illustrious sovereigns. 31) Indeed, Sumantra personally departed to bring those kings that he had been ordered by Vasishta to bring. 32) All the artisans who were engaged in the matter of the sacrifice reported everything that had been accomplished to the great sage Vasishta. 33-34) Being gratified by the progress that was being made, that superlative brahmana gave them additional instructions. "No charity", he declared, "is to be given disrespectfully or irreverently, for doubtless such disrespect causes the destruction of the donor."

With the passing of some days and nights, a number of kings arrived at Ayodhya. 35) They brought presentations of precious gems and so forth for the pleasure of Maharaja Dasaratha. It is said the delighted Vasishta then spoke as follows to the emperor: 36) "A number of kings have arrived, O tiger among men, in obedience to your decree, and I have received them according to their individual merit. 37) Your men have undertaken everything in connection with the sacrifice with great attentiveness. Please go now to the sacrificial hall, which stands close by, for the commencement of the ceremony. 38) Your Majesty will observe that the hall is furnished with all manner of attractive things gathered together for this enterprise, and appears as though it were a creation of the mind."

39\) At the instigation of both Vasishta and Rishyasringa, on a day when the moon passed through an auspicious constellation, the emperor entered the sacrificial arena. 40-41) The brahmanas, of whom Vasishta was the chief, entered the hall with Rishyasringa at their head. The sacrifice was then begun according to the scriptural injunctions, and the fortunate emperor began the initiatory rites along with his wives.

*Thus ends the thirteenth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 14. The Asvamedha Yajna

At the close of a full year the horse returned, and the emperor's sacrifice was begun on the northern bank of the Sarayu. 2) The great-souled Rishyasringa was appointed to supervise the ceremony, and the various priests discharged their allotted duties. 3) They were intelligent and learned in the Veda, and they avoided irregularities, everything being done in accordance with the scriptures. 4) They first performed the rite known as pravargya, in which milk is poured into a red-hot vessel. That was followed by the upasada, the distribution of the milk, everything being done strictly in accordance with the scriptures. 5) With great happiness, the powerful sages performed the preliminary worship, in conformity with the ordinances, beginning with the morning pressing of soma-juice. 6) The portion of the sacrifice intended for Indra was offered by the king, and the noon pressing of the soma-juice was successfully completed.

7\) With constant reference to the scriptures, exalted brahmanas performed the third pressing of soma-juice on behalf of the great-souled king. 8) Thereafter, Rishyasringa and his assistants invited the demigods, headed by Indra, by means of the recitation, with the appropriate intonations and accents, of specific Vedic mantras. 9) The hot a-priests offered the various demigods their respective portions of the sacrifices with sweetly sung hymns, pronounced with affection and respect. 10) No offering was left undone, neither was there any interruption or deficiency, everything being done under the expert eye of self-controlled brahmanas. 11) In those days one never saw a brahmana exhausted, agitated, bereft of knowledge or with less than a hundred followers. 12-13) Every day of the sacrifice, the brahmanas were sumptuously fed, so too the serving classes, the ascetics, brahmacharis and sannyasis, the elderly and the diseased, and women and their children. Continual feasting went on day and night without satiation being perceived. 14) "Give me more to eat, give me more, and give me a variety of clothing too!" Such cries were uttered then and were repeatedly obeyed. 15) Day after day, as enjoined in the scriptures, mounds of foodstuffs resembling great mountains were seen there. 16) Numerous men and women from a variety of countries were gorgeously feasted at that sacrifice by the great-souled king. 17) The brahmanas extolled the excellence of the food, and King Dasaratha heard them saying: "We are well satisfied, O king, may good fortune attend you!" 18) The brahmanas were waited upon by richly dressed men, who had servants of their own, adorned with highly polished, bejewelled earrings.

19\) Between the various rituals, eloquent brahmanas held learned debates on the nature of the Absolute Truth, each trying to outdo the other with increasingly advanced realisations. 20) Day by day those well-bred priests discharged their duties exactly according to the prescribed rules and regulations. 21) Not a single twice born man attended King Dasaratha's sacrifice who was not conversant with the six limbs of Vedic knowledge, who was not austere, who was not greatly learned, or who was not an accomplished preacher. 22) Soon, the appropriate time to erect the sacrificial pillars came. Six pillars made of bilva-wood were set up, and six of khadira-wood, each by the side of a pillar of bilva, as well as six of parna-wood. 23) A pillar of sleshmataka-wood and a pillar of devadaru-wood are also recommended for the asvamedha sacrifice, and they were duly set up two cubits apart. 24) They were erected by skilled artisans, as enjoined in the scriptures and, for the purpose of beautifying the sacrificial arena, they were all plated with gold. 25) Each column was twenty-one cubits in height, and each was hung with drapery. 26) Every one of those massive pillars was exquisitely fashioned by craftsmen. They were octagonal and highly polished. 27) Decorated with cloth, and worshipped with flowers and sandalwood paste, they shone as brilliantly as the heavenly constellation known as the Seven Rishis.

28\) The bricks for the sacrifice were heaped up to contain the sacred fire, and had been made to a precise measure by brahmanas skilled in the art of sacrificial masonry. 29) The king's altar was cunningly fashioned in the shape of Garuda, the bird carrier of Vishnu. Thrice the normal size, it contained eighteen pits and had wings of gold. 30) Beasts, serpents and birds were tethered there according to the ordinance, each one intended for a different demigod. 31) The sacrificial horse was bound there by the sages, together with aquatic creatures, in accordance with the scriptural rule. 32) Three-hundred creatures were bound at that moment to the pillars, including King Dasaratha's most excellent steed. 33) Then, Queen Kausalya circumambulated the horse all round and touched it with three swords in great bliss. 34) With pious intent the Queen spent the night by the fire in a happy frame of mind.

35\) \[The following day\] the priests known as the hota, adhvaryu and udgata caused the Emperor's three wives to come into contact with the horse. 36) Then the presiding priest drew out the fleshy part of the root known as asvagandha, and cooked it according to the scriptural regulation. 37) At the appropriate moment the king smelt the steaming odour from the root, and as he did so all the residue of his past sins was driven away.

38\) The sixteen presiding brahmanas then simultaneously cast the requisite ingredients of the horse sacrifice into the sacred fire. 39) In the Asvamedha sacrifice, all the ingredients intended for consignment to the fire are kept on branches of plaksha wood, except for one portion, which is kept in a reed basket. 40) According to the Kalpa Sootra and the scriptures meant to regularize the Vedic rituals, known as the Brahmanas, the Asvamedha Yajna proper lasts for three days. On the first day the ritual known as Chatushtoma, the pressing out of soma juice, takes place. 41) On the second day the ritual known as Ukthya takes place, and on the final day — the Atiratra. During those three days diverse sacrificial performances were successfully concluded. 42) The great yajna consisted of the rituals known as the Jyotishtoma and Ayushtoma, and there were two performances each of Atiratra, Abhijit, Visvajit, and Aptoryama.

43-44) The illustrious emperor donated his eastern empire to the hota priest, his southern empire to the brahmana, and his northern empire to the udgata.Such is the prescribed fee paid to the performers of the Asvamedha sacrifice, which was first performed by Lord Brahma. 45) At the conclusion of the yajna, the bull-like king gave the entire world in charity to the presiding priests. 46) Having bestown this gift upon them with great satisfaction, that fortunate descendant of Ikshvaku was addressed by the assembled sages. 47) "O king," they said, "you are well able to protect the entire world, but we have nothing to do with it, neither do we have the power to protect it. 48-49) We are constantly engaged in scriptural study, Your Majesty; be so kind as to remunerate us some other way. Give whatever you think fit, O king. Give us jewels, gold or cows — the earth is of no use to us!"

50\) In response to the request of the brahmanas, all of whom were learned in the Vedas, the sovereign gave them a million cows. 5153) He donated them a hundred million gold coins and four-hundred million silver coins, and the priests in turn gave everything to Rishyasringa and Vasishta. Each brahmana then received a fitting portion of the fee, and they each expressed immense delight; even assistant priests received large amounts of gold. 54-58) Having disposed of ten-million pieces of gold, the king then gave a priceless bangle to a poor brahmana who begged some charity. Then, Maharaja Dasaratha, who was intensely devoted to the twice-born, offered obeisance to the assembled brahmanas with great bliss. As he lay prostrate before them, they were happy to give him their benedictions. the sacrifice, which destroys sins, makes one eligible to enter the heavenly kingdom and is difficult for even powerful sovereigns to perform, was thus successfully concluded, and the king, with a happy heart, spoke thus to Rishyasringa: 59) "Please do that which will benefit our dynasty, O great sage."

"It shall be as you wish," rejoined Rishyasringa, "you will obtain four sons who will perpetuate your family."60) Hearing this sweet reply, the great-souled emperor felt a thrill of ecstasy and, bowing before the sage, he repeated his request.

*Thus ends the fourteenth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 15. The Putra-kameshti Sacrifice and the Demigods' Prayers for the Killing of Ravana

Rishyasringa entered into a meditative trance as he considered what ought to be done next. Upon returning to normal consciousness, he addressed the king as follows: 2) "As you desire sons, O king, I shall perform the Ishti sacrifice, which is meant for obtaining offspring. It is the crest-jewel sacrifice of the Atharva Veda. It is performed with the utterance of the appropriate hymns and is certain to be successfully concluded." 3) He forthwith began the sacrifice meant for getting sons, by casting oblations into the sacred fire while reciting mantras according to the prescribed rule. 4) Thereafter, the various demigods, Gandharvas, Siddhas, and celestial sages assembled there, having come to take their portions of the offering.

5\) After assembling there according to their station, the various demigods approached the creator of the universe, Brahma, and submitted the following: 6) "By your grace, O mighty lord, a demon by the name of Ravana is harassing us, and we are unable to subdue him. 7) Inasmuch as you were pleased by the austerities he performed, O lord, you gave him a certain benediction, and out of respect for it we have always borne his actions patiently. 8) He causes the inhabitants of the three worlds to shudder in fear, and has raised himself up high in hatred of the pious. The evilminded wretch even desires to assault the throne of Indra, king of the demigods. 9) Infatuated with the power conferred on him by your boon, the monster abuses sages, Yakshas, Gandharvas, brahmanas and even other demons. 10) The sun is unable to scorch him, the wind is unable to buffet him and the ocean, although normally garlanded with waves, becomes still at the sight of him.

11\) "We are mightily afraid of this Rakshasa, the sight of whom fills us with horror. O lord, kindly devise the means of his destruction." 12) Petitioned thus by the demigods, Brahma pondered a while. "The method of killing the heartless fiend has occurred to me", he said. 13) "He asked me to make him invincible by Gandharvas, demigods and demons, and I assented, saying, 'So be it!' 14) Out of contempt for humans he did not ask for protection from them; he must thus meet death at the hands of a human being. There is no other means."

15\) Hearing Brahma's welcome pronouncement, the demigods and sages were filled with joy. 16-17) While this transpired, Lord Vishnu, the Lord of the universe, approached, mounted on His bird carrier, Garuda, like the sun upon a cloud. He was greatly effulgent. He carried a conchshell, discus and club, and was arrayed in yellow garments. He wore bracelets of refined gold upon His upper arms, and was being panegyrised by various great demigods. 18) Upon meeting with Brahma, Healighted with a peaceful demeanour. All the demigods bowed low before their Lord, and glorified Him with various hymns. They then appealed to Him as follows: 19-27) "Dear Lord Vishnu, we request you, for the sake of the world, to advent Yourself, in four plenary expansions, as the sons of the pious and magnanimous King Dasaratha, lord of Ayodhya, in his three wives, who are like Hree, Sri and Keerti.Appearing like an ordinary man, please slay the troublesome Ravana in battle with the assistance of the demigods. The fool is giving pain to the celestials and the sages with his excessive and cruel strength. He even cast down and killed certain sages, Gandharvas and Apsaras who were wont to sport in the Nandana Forest. We have assembled here, one and all, in order to take shelter of Your Lordship so he may be destroyed. You are the Supreme Shelter and Lord of us all, O Destroyer of Your foes; so You may kill this enemy of the demigods, please advent Yourself in the world of men."

Being thus praised by the thirty principal Devas, headed by Brahma, who were all assembled there, Lord Vishnu, the cynosure of the three worlds, spoke to them, saying, 28-32) "Be not afraid! All good fortune to you. For your sake I shall slay the pitiless Ravana in battle, together with his sons and grandsons, his ministers and counsellors, and all of his kinsmen. I will dwell in the world of men for eleventhousand years and will personally protect the earth."

Having thus benedicted the demigods, the Supreme Soul, Lord Sri Vishnu, reflected upon the place of His advent, and thereafter He expanded Himself as four plenary persons, and chose King Dasaratha as His father. Celestial sages, Gandharvas, the Rudras and celestial courtesans then began to praise Madhusoodana, Lord Vishnu, with transcendentally lovely hymns. 33) "Kindly eradicate the haughty Ravana, the enemy of Indra", they prayed. "Swollen with pride on account of his terrible strength, he causes the world to wail and cry, and the ascetics live in fear of him. 34) Having slain that cause of lamentation — Ravana of dreadful prowess — as well as his army and his kin, please return, O Lord of Your devotees, to Your transcendental abode, where there is neither sorrow, imperfection, nor sin."

*Thus ends the fifteenth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 16. The Presentation of Sweet Rice

Being thus commissioned by the principal demigods, Lord Narayana, Vishnu, although omniscient, addressed His devotees with the following sweet words: 2) "How should I accomplish the task of killing the king of the Rakshasas, the molester of sages?" 3) The demigods replied thus to the imperishable Lord: "Assuming human form, slay Ravana in combat. 4) He performed severe penance for many years, O victorious One, by which he satisfied Brahma, the forefather of the universe. 5) Being pleased with him, the creator gave the Rakshasa the benediction that he would have nothing to fear from any living being, save from mankind. 6) At the time of receiving the boon, Ravana regarded mankind with contempt. Naturally, he grew proud because of Brahma's benediction. 7) He is bringing ruin to the three worlds and is even polluting woman kind. His death is therefore pre-ordained, O Chastiser of foes!"

8\) Hearing the earnest submission of the demigods, the Supreme Soul, Vishnu, was pleased to accept King Dasaratha as His father. 9) At that time the illustrious monarch was without an heir, and was performing the Putra Kameshti sacrifice with a view to obtaining a son. 10) Lord Vishnu, becoming thus resolved, bade farewell to Brahma, and disappeared as He was being adored by demigods and great sages. 11) Just then a personality of great power and strength appeared from the sacrificial fire. 12) His complexion was blackish; he was clad in red and had a reddish face. His voice was as deep and resonant as a kettle-drum, and fine hair, like a lion's, grew on his head and body. 13) Various fortune marks were visible on his person, and he was decorated with divine ornaments. He was as tall as a mountain peak and moved like a proud tiger. 14-15) He was as resplendent as the sun and shone like a tongue of blazing fire. In his hands he held — as if it were his dear wife — a large golden bowl with a silver lid, which contained payasa, rice pudding, produced as it were, by magic.

16\) Beholding Maharaja Dasaratha, he spoke to him as follows: "Know that I have come from Lord Vishnu, O king!" 17) The king replied with joined palms, saying, "Welcome, Your Holiness, what shall I do for you?"

18\) "You have obtained this," rejoined the servant of Prajapati, "as a result of worshipping the demigods and Sri Vishnu. 19) Take this payasa, prepared by the Devas; it will not only procure sons for you, but will also bring fortune and good health to whoever partakes of it. 20) Deliver this to your good wives with the words: 'Kindly eat'. Your wives will then conceive the sons for whom you sacrifice, O king."

21\) The Emperor immediately assented and, with his head bowed, he joyfully accepted the golden vessel. 22) He reverently saluted that extraordinary being and circumambulated him with great transcendental ecstasy. 23) Maharaja Dasaratha experienced happiness on receiving that divine gift akin to that felt by a beggar who discovers treasure. 24) Having thus performed his duty, that resplendent personality vanished from sight.

25\) Beaming with delight, the king shone in his harem like the autumn moon in the starry firmament. 26) Within his private apartments he spoke to Queen Kausalya. "Take this payasa," he said, "for it will cause you to conceive a son." 27) He gave half the payasa to Kausalya, and one quarter to Queen Sumitra. 28-29) He gave half of what remained to Queen Kaikeyi and, after some consideration, gave the remainder of that nectarean sweet-rice to Queen Sumitra. Thus did he distribute the payasa to each of his wives.

30\) Those best of women, having thus partaken of the payasa, simultaneously perceived a thrill of ecstasy arisen within their hearts and minds. 31) Thereafter, when they had each consumed the Emperor's sweet rice, a radiance that vied with fire and with the sun immediately entered in their wombs. 32) Seeing that his wives were with child, the king, realising that he had achieved his desire, experienced a joy equal to that felt by Indra, the king of heaven, when he is worshipped by the Siddhas and the sages.

*Thus ends the sixteenth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 17. The Monkeys Born

Upon Lord Vishnu assuming the role of son to the righteous King Dasaratha, the mighty Brahma addressed the host of demigods as follows: 2) "In order to assist the well-wisher of us all, Lord Vishnu, in His transcendental pastimes, beget powerful portions of yourselves endowed with the ability to change their forms at will. 3-6) So that your offspring may be heroic, swift as the wind, highly intelligent, insuperable, inventive, endowed with divinely robust bodies and with every virtue; so that they may be like Lord Vishnu in valour, familiar with creating illusions, with logic and with all kinds of weapons: beget sons in the form of monkeys, equal to yourselves, in the wombs of Apsaras and Gandharva women, in Yaksha and Naga maidens, in the aboriginals, Kinnaras and the apes. 7) Formerly, I created the bear-like Jambavan. He was generated from my mouth while I was yawning."

8\) Being so addressed by Lord Brahma and heeding his command withal, the demigods begot sons in the form of monkeys. 9) Moreover, broad-minded sages, Siddhas, Vidyadharas, and Charanas begot heroic simian sons. 10) Even the celestial Indra begot Vali, king of the monkeys as his son, and Sugreeva was begotten by the sun-god, the great generator of heat. 11) Brihaspati begot the great monkey known as Tara, chief of all the monkeys in wisdom. 12) The fortunate Gandhamadana was born the son of Kuvera, treasurer of the demigods, and Visvakarma, the celestial engineer, begot mighty Nala. 13) The fire-god begot Neela, who resembled the fiery element, and surpassed all in power, fame and glory. 14) The divine twins, the Asvini Kumaras, who are possessed of great physical beauty, begot Mainda and Dvivida as their own sons. 15) Varuna, the sea-god, begot Sushena, and mighty Parjanya, god of rain, begot the monkey known as Sarabha. 16) Vayu, the wind-god had the glorious Hanuman as his son. He is endowed with a body as strong as a thunderbolt and he is as swift as Lord Vishnu's bird carrier, Garuda. 17) Hanuman is the best of all the monkeys, and he is intelligent and strong as well. Monkeys such as these were generated in many thousands and were all enthusiastic to kill the ten-headed Ravana.

18\) Those mighty heroes, whose strength was unequalled, were able to alter their forms at will, and their mighty bodies resembled elephants and mountains. 19-20) The Rikshas, monkeys and long-tailed monkeys were soon born, each being the image of the demigod who had sired him; though the long-tailed monkeys were somewhat superior in prowess to the others. 21-22) Monkeys were born from Rikshas and Kinnaras. Famous demigods, rishis and Gandharvas, Garuda and the Yakshas, the great serpents known as Nagas, the Kimpurushas, Siddhas and Vidyadharas begot many thousands of such sons with great joy. 23-24) The Charanas begot powerful sons with massive bodies, forest dwellers all, capable of changing form at will, in the wombs of the principal Apsaras, Vidyadharas, Naginees and Gandharvees.

25\) They were like lions and tigers in pride and strength and were accustomed to doing battle with great rocks and mountain tops as their weapons. 26) They could fight with their teeth and nails and were conversant with the use of all the regular missiles. They could shake huge mountains and split mighty tress. 27) They agitated the sea, lord of waters, with their impetuosity, split the earth with their feet and leapt over great oceans. 28) They leapt into the firmament and caught hold of clouds, and they seized intoxicated elephants as they wandered in the forest. 29-33) With a roar they would cause shrieking birds to fall from the sky.

The demigods begot ten-million great-souled monkeys, who were able to change their forms at will. These were the chiefs of the foremost monkey tribes, and they begot other heroic monkeys who became great captains in their own right. Some descended upon Mount Rikshavatan, while others dwelt on other mountains or resorted to the forests. Many of the chief tribes allied themselves with the son of the sun, Sugreeva, and the son of Indra, Vali, while others placed themselves at the disposal of Nala, Neela and Hanuman. 34) They were all as powerful as Garuda and proficient in the arts of war; wandering here and there they despatched lions, tigers and huge snakes. 35) Great-armed Vali of extraordinary prowess protected, by the strength of his arms, the various species of monkeys.

36\) Those heroes were scattered all over the world, with its mountains, forests and oceans, and they each had individual beauty and unique personal characteristics. 37) Endowed with terrible forms and features, resembling a mass of clouds or the peaks of mountains, the monkey chiefs appeared in order to assist Lord Rama in His transcendental pastimes.

*Thus ends the seventeenth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 18. The Appearance of Lord Rama

The great asvamedha sacrifice was concluded. the demigods received their respective portions of the oblations, and they returned as they had come. 2) Having fulfilled the obligations of the initiatory rites, the king entered his capital together with his wives, his servants and his military force. 3) The visiting kings had received fitting hospitality from the Emperor, and having offered their obeisances to Vasishta Muni, they returned to their kingdoms in a happy frame of mind. 4) The armies of those illustrious monarchs went each to their own habitation, garbed in resplendent uniforms and shining brilliantly. 5) Upon their departure, King Dasaratha re-entered Ayodhya with the brahmanas at the head of his company. 6) The worshippable Rishyasringa, together with his wife, Santa, returned home, the Emperor escorting them part of the way. 7) Having seen off his various guests, the king dwelt very happily in his capital anticipating the appearance of his sons.

8-10) Six seasons passed after the conclusion of the sacrifice when, in the twelfth month, on the ninth day of Chaitra, when the Punarvasu constellation was in the ascendant, when five planets were exalted, and Jupiter and the moon were in conjunction in the zodiacal sign of Cancer, Mother Kausalya gave birth to Rama,Lord of the universe, Who is worshipped by all of creation and was endowed with all the marks of an incarnation of the Supreme Godhead. 11) The child was a plenary expansion of Lord Vishnu, the bliss of the dynasty of Ikshvaku. His eyes were tinged with red, His arms were powerful and His voice \[in adulthood\], sounded like the note of a kettledrum. 12) Kausalya shone with immeasurable splendour because of her son, just as Aditi had done with Indra, who holds a thunderbolt in his hand. 13) The mighty Bharata was born from Kaikeyi. He was directly one of Lord Vishnu's quadruple extensionsand was possessed of all transcendental qualities. 14) Thereafter, the two brothers Lakshmana and Satrughna were born from Mother Sumitra. The two heroes were expert in the use of weapons and were also plenary portions of the Lord. 15) The blissful and devoted Bharata appeared when the asterism known as Pushya was in the ascendant and the sun was in Pisces. The twin sons of Mother Sumitra appeared when the stars known as Sarpa rose in the sky, and the sun was in the sign of Cancer.

16\) The king's four sons were each born separately, each of them being endowed with spiritual characteristics, and Their beautiful forms defeated the splendour of the stars known as the Bhadrapada. 17) At that time Gandharvas sang melodiously, and Apsaras began to dance; the demigods sounded kettledrums and flowers rained from the sky. 18) The citizens of Ayodhya held a great festival in celebration, and the highways were crowded with people, with the special attraction of dancers and actors. 19) Made uproarious by singers, musicians and others, the capital's extensive thoroughfares being strewn with every variety of gem, shone brilliantly. 20) The king gave in charity to the grooms, charioteers, actors and singers \[the members of the mixed castes\] and donated thousands of cows to the brahmanas.

21-22) On the eleventh day he had the delighted Vasishta perform the name-giving ceremony. the eldest, the Supreme Soul, was named Rama. The son of Kaikeyi was named Bharata, and the sons of Mother Sumitra were named Lakshmana and Satrughna. 23) He caused the brahmanas and his other subjects to partake of a feast, and he donated mountains of pure gems to the priestly class. 24) He had all the purificatory ceremonies performed for the princes, especially those pertaining to the birth of a child. The eldest of them, Rama, had the splendour of a blazing comet and was the cynosure of His father. 25) It was the generally held conclusion that He was the Self Existent Lord of all beings. The four Heroes grew to be fully conversant with the Vedas and were devoted to the interests of the people. 26) They had full knowledge and were possessed of every virtue. Of the four, however, Rama was especially powerful and was factually unsurpassable. 27) He was as attractive to the people as the unobscured moon and was renowned for His mastery of elephants, horses and the battle chariot. 28-34) He was devoted to the military arts and was ever obedient to His father.

From His infancy, Lakshmana, the delight of the Goddess of Fortune, was extremely affectionate towards His glorious elder brother, Lord Rama. That Supreme Personality of Godhead did not take rest without His younger brother, neither did He eat without His company. Whenever Lord Raghava, mounted on His horse, went to hunt, Lakshmana rode at His back, guarding Him with His bow. Satrughna, the younger brother of Lakshmana, was the Life and Soul of Bharata, and Bharata was ever dear to Satrughna.

King Dasaratha was as delighted with his four beloved sons as the grandsire of the universe is with the demigods. They were perfectly wise and were richly endowed with transcendental characteristics. 35-36) They were modest, praiseworthy, all-knowing, far-sighted, and They possessed a brilliant splendour which filled Their father with the happiness of a Brahma. Those tigers among men were devoted to the study of the Vedic scriptures. 37-38) They were obedient to the command of Their father and were skilled warriors.

In due course of time, the Emperor began to reflect upon the marriage of His sons, and he considered the matter together with his relatives and in consultation with his greathearted ministers. 39) At that time the powerful sage Visvamitra arrived in Ayodhya. Desiring to have an audience with King Dasaratha, he addressed the gatekeeper as follows: 40-45) "Go quickly and inform His Majesty that Visvamitra, the son of Gadhi, has come to see him."

Stunned by the sage's instruction, they ran to the royal palace and informed the king of the rishi's arrival. The happy king immediately went to greet him in the company of his family priest, just as Indra goes to meet Brahma. The sage shone brilliantly on account of the merit he had accumulated by the performance of austerity under strict vows, and with a blissful expression the king brought him arghya. The rishi accepted the offering in accordance with scriptural injunction and enquired after the emperor's welfare, as well as that of the capital, the king's treasury, his subjects, relatives and friends. 46) "Are all your subjects obedient," he asked, "and your enemies defeated? 47) Are all your duties with regard to the demigods and to men being properly discharged?" The great sage then met Vasishta and enquired after his well-being. 48-50) He also conversed with the various rishis assembled there, each according to his merit. Then, with great happiness they all entered the royal palace where, having been worshipped by the king, they took the respective seats. The emperor praised the sage in great transcendental ecstasy, saying: "Your Holiness's arrival is just like acquiring nectar, or like rain falling upon parched land. 51) It is like a childless man begetting a son in his wife; it is like finding something precious one had lost; it is the greatest good fortune imaginable. 52) I am delighted by your auspicious arrival, and I feel I should gratify your innermost desire, whatever it may be. 53) You are deserving of honour, and it is by the will of Providence the Your Honour has come here. I think that today the purpose of my birth and my existence has been fulfilled, and that my life has become perfect. 54-55) The sight of the king of brahmanas is the good morning at the close of my night! I offer you my repeated obeisances, who previously were celebrated as a king-sage, but by the power of austerity attained the status of brahmarshi. It is a wonder that you have come here, O sage, and it is the cause of my complete purification. 56) O master, merely by seeing you I have obtained the benefit of visiting all the holy places.Please tell me the purpose of your coming here. 57) I desire, if you will so favour me, to aid you in your mission, I shall not hesitate to do whatever I am able! 58) Your Honour is as good as the Supreme Lord in my estimation, and I will attempt to satisfy you completely. I have attained the greatest good fortune and the highest religious merit because of your coming, O brahmana!"

59\) Having heard the king's humble speech, which made the heart glad and was a delight to the ear, the rishi par excellence, Visvamitra Muni, whose virtues and glory were widely celebrated, became supremely happy.

*Thus ends the eighteenth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 19. Visvamitra Asks for Rama and Lakshmana

Hearing the wonderfully extensive speech of that lion-like monarch, mighty Visvamitra gave reply, the hair on his body standing erect because of spiritual ecstasy. 2) "Your speech is worthy of you and none other, O tiger among kings," he rejoined, "who are born in a great dynasty and are instructed by Vasishta! 3) You should give me an assurance that you are resolved to fulfil the desire in my heart. 4) I am under a vow to perform certain rites with a view to attaining a particular perfection, O king, but two Rakshasas, capable of altering their shape at will, are putting obstacles in the in the way of its completion. 5-7) Towards the conclusion of the sacrifice, when my vow is more or less fulfilled, the two Rakshasas, Mareecha and Subahu, who are very powerful and well trained, shower heaps of flesh and blood upon the alter. Because of the disruption of the sacrifice and my vow, which I have been at pains to complete, I have lost all enthusiasm for it and have thus left the place. My intelligence will not allow me to vent my wrath, O king. 8-10) It is a characteristic of the particular vow I am following that no curse may be released.

"You should give me your eldest son, the truthful and heroic Rama, Whose forehead is decorated with two black locks, O tiger among men. Guarded by me, and by dint of His own transcendent might, He will certainly vanquish those Rakshasas. I will bestow many attributes upon Him \[in the form of weapons\], have no fear! 11) He will attain glory for His deeds throughout the three worlds, and these two demons have no power whatsoever to stand against Rama if they attack Him. 12) No one but Raghava has sufficient might to destroy them. The strength of these sinful wretches has made them arrogant, and they have brought themselves within the jurisdiction of the noose of time. 13) They are no match for the mighty Rama, O Emperor, and you should not allow your love for your son to sway you. 14) I give you my word: you should know that they are already killed. I perceive the great-souled Rama to be truly possessed of valour. 15-21) Vasishta and other great ascetics will readily confirm this. If you desire everlasting merit and fame in the world, then you must hand Rama over to me.

"If your counsellors, headed by Vasishta, give their approval, then you should certainly release Him unto me. You are naturally devoted to Him, but He is detached and independent. At least you should allow the lotus-eyed Rama to spend the ten nights of the sacrifice with me. The recommended period for the performance of my sacrifice should not be allowed to elapse, O king. My blessings upon you! Do not let grief overwhelm your mind!"

Having thus spoken, the greatly powerful and intelligent Visvamitra became silent. The king however was seized with anguish, his body trembled and he fell in a swoon as he heard Visvamitra's auspicious request. Coming to his senses, he raised himself up, but he lamented still, being overwhelmed by fear. 22) Although he was a great and powerful monarch, King Dasaratha's heart was torn with grief by the words of the sage, and, his mind reeling, he tumbled from his seat.

*Thus ends the nineteenth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 20. Dasaratha's Refusal

Hearing Visvamitra's request, the Emperor fell unconscious for more than an hour it seemed. Upon coming to his senses he spoke as follows: 2) "My lotus-eyed Rama", he said, "is less than sixteen years of age, and I do not see how He could possible engage in combat with Rakshasas. 3) I am the commander of a vast army: I shall personally lead it and fight them. 4) My soldiers are heroic, valiant and fully conversant with the use of weapons. They are quite fit to battle with demons. You should not take Rama.

5\) "I shall guard your sacrifice with my bow in my hand. I shall stand in the vanguard of the battle, and as long as there is life in my body I shall fight with the night-stalkers. 6) You will be well guarded. Your sacrifice will be concluded without impediment. I shall personally come; you should not take Rama!

7\) "He is but a child, and His knowledge is incomplete. He does not know how to estimate the strength of the enemy, neither does He have the strength of weaponry or experience of battle. 8-12) He is certainly unable to defend Himself against the illusions Rakshasas employ when fighting.

"I cannot bear to live apart from Rama for a single moment, O sage — you should not take Him. If you desire to take Raghava, take me also, together with my army. I am sixty-thousand years old, O Kausika, and my Rama was begotten with great difficulty; please do not take Him! Of my four sons, my pious Rama is most dear to me; I beg you not to take Him.

"How powerful are these Rakshasas, O sage. Whose sons are they? 13-15) How big are they and whose protection are they under? How can Rama or my followers and troops prevail against their treacherous tactics, O brahmana? Please tell me everything my lord. Tell me how I may stand in battle against these evil beings, who are undoubtedly swollen with pride on account of their power."

Hearing the king's speech Visvamitra replied as follows: 16) "There is a Rakshasa called Ravana, born in the line of Paulastya. He was given a benediction by Brahma, and he sorely oppresses the three worlds because of it. 17-19) He is immeasurably strong and fearless, and has many demonic associates. It is said, O king, that Ravana, the Lord of Rakshasas, is the son of Visrava Muni and brother to Kuvera. He does not personally hinder the sacrifice, but instigates the two powerful demons, Mareecha and Subahu, to do so."

20\) Being thus addressed by the sage, the king replied, saying: "I do not have the power to stand against that evil wretch. 21) Please be merciful on my son, O knower of religion, and on me also. I am bereft of good fortune, but Your Holiness is a divinity, and a spiritual preceptor, without a doubt. 22) If neither demigods nor demons, Gandharvas nor Yakshas, birds nor serpents can prevail against Ravana, then how is a man to do so? 23-26) Indeed, Ravana robs the powerful of their power in battle. I am not able to fight either with him or with his forces, neither with my army nor with my sons, O best of sages. I cannot give you my son, who looks like a demigod but is unfamiliar with war. When, at the height of the sacrifice, the two terrible and highly trained sons of Sunda and Upasunda, namely Mareecha and Subahu, cause a disturbance in it, I shall not send my dear son! 27) I shall personally go to fight the two of them together with my friends, otherwise, my relatives and I will humbly beg you to excuse us."

28\) Because of the babbling of that lord of men, the king of brahmanas, Visvamitra Muni, entered into a great rage. The great rishi began to blaze with anger like the sacred fire when oblations of clarified butter are cast into it.

*Thus ends the twentieth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 21. Vasishta's Instructions

Having listened to the confused words of the king, which were brought about by deep transcendental love for his son, Visvamitra spoke in a terrible rage, as follows: 2) "Having first given your word, you now wish to forswear it. This does not befit a descendant of Raghu, and will bring about the destruction of your family. 3) If you are able to endure having uttered such a lie, O Emperor, then continue to live a life of ease surrounded by your friends!"

4\) As a result of Visvamitra's mighty rage, the earth began to quake, and the demigods became afraid. 5) Observing the entire world to be trembling, the austere and sober minded Vasishta addressed King Dasaratha thus: 6) "Born in the dynasty of Ikshvaku, Your Majesty is the unequalled personification of religiousness. You are determined and steady in your vows; you ought not to abandon truthfulness. 7) You are a descendant of Raghu, and are celebrated throughout the three worlds for your piety. You should not forsake the principles of religion and be diverted by unrighteousness. 8) Having declared 'It shall be done', you should uphold your pledge. You must release Rama, lest the merit you have accrued by the performance of sacrifices and various acts of philanthropy be vanquished.

9\) "Whether He be conversant with the use of armies or not, Rama will not be overpowered by the Rakshasas while He is protected, just like the celestial nectar by fire, by Visvamitra.

10\) "Visvamitra is the embodiment of religiosity, and the best of those possessed of might. He is the most learned among men and is the last word in asceticism. 11-12) He knows the use of a variety of missiles. There is no creature in the three worlds, either moving or non-moving, who knows him apart from myself; not among the demigods, sages, demons or Rakshasas, nor even among eminent Gandharvas; Yakshas, Kinnaras or Nagas.

13\) "His weapons were originally born as the exceedingly pious sons of Krishasva and were given to him when he ruled his kingdom. 14) These weapons were begotten by Krishasva in the wombs of the daughters of Prajapati Daksha. They have distinctive forms and are extremely powerful; they possess dazzling splendour and bring victory to whoever wields them.

15\) "Jaya and Suprabha, the slender-waisted daughters of Daksha, gave birth to one hundred weapons of unbelievable brilliance. 16) Jaya obtained her fifty sons as the result of a benediction. They are of unequalled splendour and, being devoid of corporeal form, are intended for the destruction of the armies of the demons. 17) Suprabha gave birth to fifty more such sons, who are known as the Samharas. They are extremely strong, difficult to counteract, and are altogether invulnerable.

18\) "The righteous Visvamitra knows how to employ these weapons and even possesses the power to generate new ones. 19) There is nothing that is unknown to this pre-eminent sage, O scion of Raghu, that exists either in the present or the past. 20) If you do not send Rama with this powerful and famed rishi, then you are certain to be criticised. 21) Visvamitra is quite powerful enough to restrain the Rakshasas himself; he has approached you and begged for Rama only so that he might favour Him."

22\) Hearing Vasishta's disclosure, King Dasaratha (whose glories are widely known) became happy within his mind and agreed to let Raghava accompany Visvamitra.

*Thus ends the twenty-first canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 22. Rama and Lakshmana Instructed in the Use of the Weapons Known as Bala and Atibala

While Vasishta was speaking, King Dasaratha went personally to call Rama and Lakshmana with a happy countenance. 2-3) Having been blessed by His mother and father and consecrated with auspicious Vedic mantras by Vasishta, Rama had His head smelled by the King, who then handed Him over to Visvamitra in a blissful mood. 4) Seeing the lotuseyed Rama leaving with Visvamitra, the wind blew soft to the touch and free from dust. 5) The demigods rained flowers upon the prince, and the sky resounded with the sound of conchshells and kettle drums. 6) Visvamitra proceeded first, then the illustrious Rama, His brow graced with two locks of black hair, and Lakshmana followed Him, bow in hand.

7-8) The two brothers illumined the ten directions, and sporting quivers of arrows on one shoulder and bows on the other They resembled a pair of triple-headed serpents. They followed the sage just as the Asvini Kumaras follow Brahma, causing him to become more and more brilliant than before. 9-12) Armed with swords, Their wrists protected by alligator skin, the blameless Rama and Lakshmana were beautifully decorated and were highly effulgent. Their transcendental bodies were delightful to behold, and following behind Visvamitra, They increased his splendour with Their lustre, just as the sons of fire do when they follow the inconceivable Lord Siva.

When they had journeyed twelve miles along the southern bank of the Sarayu, Visvamitra spoke the following sweet words: "O Rama," he said, "purify Yourself with water. Let us waste no time! 13) Please accept the mantras known as Bala and Atibala; they will counteract exhaustion and fever and will preserve Your youthful beauty. 14) Rakshasas will not dare to assault You while You sleep or are inattentive; no one in the world will equal You in might and valour. 15) None will be Your match in the three worlds, O Rama, while You chant the mantras, Bala and Atibala. 16) O sinless one, no one in the world will excel You in fortune, in dexterity, in knowledge or intelligence, nor will You be equalled in power, or the ability to contradict specious arguments.

17\) "Once You have knowledge of these two mantras none will surpass you, for Bala and Atibala are the mothers of wisdom. 18) You will know neither hunger nor thirst as long as you recite these mantras, O Raghava. 19) They are daughters of Brahma and possess great splendour; upon learning them You will become famous the world over. 20-23) You are worthy to have such knowledge bestowed upon you, O Prince, and You doubtless possess all desirable qualities. Once You have procured them they will certainly become exceedingly powerful." Rama then touched water and, with a blissful smiling countenance, was initiated into the use of the mantras by the great sage. Infused with this knowledge, the immeasurably powerful Personality of Godhead, Sri Rama, exhibited thousands of brilliant effulgent rays, like unto the autumn sun. Thereafter, Visvamitra instructed the princes in the worship of the spiritual preceptor, and the three of them passed a happy night on the bank of the Sarayu. 24) The princes lay alongside each other on grassy beds unworthy of such personalities, and spent a happy night, caressed by the words of Visvamitra.

*Thus ends the twenty-second canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 23. Their Stay at the Kama Ashram

When night turned to day, Visvamitra addressed Rama and Lakshmana as They lay upon Their leafy bed: 2) "O Rama, beautiful son of Kausalya, the sun is rising, You should get up and worship the Lord." 3) Hearing the noble words of the sage, the princes bathed in the waters of the Sarayu, offered oblations of water to the sun-god, and recited the Gayatri mantra. 4) Having performed the various rites of morning worship, Rama and Lakshmana stood respectfully before the heroic Visvamitra, who was rich in asceticism.

5\) Later, as They proceeded along the bank of the Sarayu \[together with the sage\], They noticed the river's confluence with the holy Ganges. 6) They also came upon the ashram of certain meritorious ascetics, who had been performing severe penance for thousands of years. 7) Seeing that sacred hermitage, the princes were greatly delighted, and They addressed Visvamitra as follows: 8) "Kindly tell us master, who dwells in this ashram? We are curious to hear of such a great soul."

9\) Hearing the boys' enquiry, the sage laughed and said, "Hearken, Rama, as I tell You whose residence this was of yore. 10-12) Kandarpa was the incarnation of Kama \[Cupid\], so the wise say. It so happened that Lord Siva had been practising austerities and meditation in this place, and as he departed, together with his newly wedded wife and the Maruts \[the wind-gods\], he espied Kama, who was intent on doing some mischief. Lord Siva uttered a sound of disgust, and opening his third eye, he consumed the evil-minded wretch's body with blazing fire.

13\) "Because of Lord Siva's anger, Kama's limbs were reduced to ashes, and hence he is now bereft of a substantial body. 14) He is now known as Ananga \['Limbless'\], O Raghava, and the land where he lost his limbs is called Anga. 15) This hermitage is Lord Siva's; the virtuous sages who dwell here are his disciples of old, O Hero, and they are pure and without sin. 16) Let us spend the night in this beautiful place, betwixt the two holy rivers, O handsome prince; tomorrow we shall cross the Ganges. 17) Let us bathe and then go in. We shall sojourn here happily once we have chanted the Gayatri mantra and have offered clarified butter in the sacred fire."

18-20) While they were conversing there, the sages, who could see them from afar, became supremely happy. Upon their arrival, Visvamitra, Rama and Lakshmana were offered arghya and padya, and the sages performed all the rites pertaining to the reception of guests. Receiving respectful worship in return, they related various transcendental narratives to their guests. 21-22) At sunset they all muttered the appropriate prayers, and Visvamitra, Rama and Lakshmana spent a delightful night in the company of the righteous sages of Kama Ashram. The noble-minded Visvamitra, for his part, delighted the royal princes with all sorts of beautiful stories.

*Thus ends the twenty-third canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 24. Their Arrival at Tataka's Forest

In the pure dawn of the next day, the powerful princes performed Their morning duties, and placing Visvamitra before Them, They went to the bank of the river. 2) Having obtained a good, strong boat for them, the diligent munis addressed Visvamitra as follows: 3) "Please get up into this boat Your Holiness, who are followed by two royal princes. May you have a safe journey. Do not delay now." 4) Visvamitra replied, "So be it!" and having returned their salutations he crossed the river Ganges together with Rama and Lakshmana. 5-6) Upon reaching the middle of the stream, Rama and His younger brother heard a great noise amidst the violent flurry of waters. Wishing to ascertain the cause of that sound, Rama questioned the bull-like muni thus: 7-11) "What is that thunderous tumult that can be heard within the rushing waters, O sage?" In reply to Rama's enquiry, he described its cause as follows: "On Mount Kailasa, O Rama, there is a lake born from the mind of Brahma, which is hence known as Lake Manasa. From that lake flows the holy Sarayu. It streams forth from Brahma's pond and encircles Ayodhya. That unparalleled roar is caused by the shock of the Sarayu entering the waters of the Ganges. Offer Your respectful obeisances, O Rama!" The princes obeyed his command and bowed before the sacred rivers.

12-14) Having alighted at the southern shore, Rama and Lakshmana began to walk briskly. Noting the fearful appearance of the forest, its paths overgrown, the peerless Rama questioned Visvamitra, saying, "Oh, this wood seems utterly impenetrable. It appears to be the abode of crickets and crows, and it resounds with the shrieks and howls of fierce beasts and birds of prey! 15-21) What is this formidable forest that is adorned with invincible lions, boars and tigers with shrubs and creepers and with horse-ear, arjuna, bilva, ebony, and jujube trees?"

Visvamitra replied thus: "Hearken, my Dear, and I shall tell You. Formerly there were two kingdoms existing here, Malada and Karoosha by name, which were established by demigods. Long ago, when the demon Vritra was slain, Indra, the thousand-eyed, was overwhelmed with hunger. The demigods, rishis and other ascetics cleansed him of his sinby bathing him with pitchers of pure water, and his impurities were shed upon this tract of land, gladdening both Indra and the Devas. 22-26) Thereafter, being mightily pleased with this country, Indra bestowed an excellent benediction upon it. these two countries', he declared, 'will become famous in the world and will be celebrated as Malada and Karoosha, because they have borne the impurities from my limbs.' Seeing this land thus honoured by the intelligent Indra, the demigods eulogised him, saying, 'Well done! Bravo!'

"For many a year these countries flourished, O Conqueror, and were rich in grains and wealth. After some time, however, a demoness who was capable of altering her form at will, and who possessed the strength of a thousand elephants, was born hereabouts. All good fortune unto You! She was named Tataka, and she was wed to the crafty Sunda. 27) Her son is the Rakshasa known as Mareecha, the equal in strength of Indra. He is possessed of thick, round arms, a monstrous head, a huge mouth and a gigantic body. 28-29) This ghastly looking Rakshasa constantly troubles the people of these two countries, and the evil Tataka creates havoc along twelve miles of the highway. 30) We ought, therefore, to go to her forest, and by the strength of Your mighty arms the sinful creature should be slain. 31) By my order make this land secure once more. No one is able to visit this place, O Rama, because of its destruction by that horrible demoness. 32) Thus I have told You of everything regarding this dreadful forest, and the atrocities of Tataka, which go on unceasingly."

*Thus ends the twenty-fourth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 25. Rama Urged to Kill Tataka

Having listened to the unfathomable utterance of the great sage, Rama, a tiger among men, replied in a sweet voice, saying: 2) "It is said that women have little strength, O muni, how is it that this demoness has the strength of a thousand elephants?" 3-4) The mighty sage, hearing Rama's polite enquiry spoke, to the delight of both the princes, as follows: "Please hear the cause of her might. This frail woman became powerful because of a benediction bestowed on her. 5) Once there was a great Yaksha possessed of might, known as Suketu. He was childless, despite his virtuous character, and he therefore performed severe austerities \[with a view to getting offspring\]. 6) Lord Brahma, being delighted with his penance, awarded him a jewel of a daughter called Tataka. 7) The grandfather of the worlds also endowed her with the strength of a thousand elephants, but he avoided giving the Yaksha a son. 8) When she arrived at puberty and her youthful body began to develop, he gave her as a wife to Sunda, the son of Jambha.

9\) "In due course she gave birth to a boy they called Mareecha, who grew to be invincible, and was transformed into a Rakshasa as the result of a curse. 10) Sunda was killed, O Rama, by the great sage Agastya, and Tataka and her son consequently wished to destroy him. 11-13) She rushed towards him in a furious rage, screaming, and she meant to devour him, but the sage marked her and her son flying towards him. He cursed Mareecha with the words: 'May you become a Rakshasa!' In his anger he cursed Tataka as well, saying, 'May you lose your beauty and be transformed into a hideous man-eater!'

14\) "Her form altered, and stupefied with rage, she terrorizes the place where Agastya once dwelt. 15) For the sake of the cows and the brahmanas, O Raghava, be so good as to kill this supremely cruel and tyrannical demoness! 16) No one in the three worlds but You has the power to slay her, O bliss of the Raghu dynasty, now that she is thus transformed.

17-18) "You should not be soft-hearted and think it improper to kill a woman; princes must perform both cruel and kind deeds for the sake of the four classes of men. In order to protect his subjects, the guardian of society must always be prepared to do that which is considered sinful or blameworthy. 19) This is the burden of kings, their perpetual duty; You must slay her Rama, for there is not a shred of goodness in her.

20\) "It is said that, formerly, Indra killed Manthara, the daughter of Virochana, when she sought to destroy the world. 21) Lord Vishnu slew the devoted wife of Bhrigu, the mother of Sukra, when she desired the annihilation of Indra. 22) There are numerous other instances of great men killing irreligious women, O Prince; therefore, give up any pity You may feel and dispatch this demoness according to my order!"

*Thus ends the twenty-fifth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 26. The Slaying of Tataka

Having heard the sage's potent speech, the determined Raghava replied with folded hands. 2-3) "In Ayodhya, in the midst of My gurus," He said, "I was instructed thus by My great-souled father, King Dasaratha: 'Out of respect for Your father, do whatever Visvamitra commands You, without hesitation!' Naturally, the words of My father are not to be disregarded. 4) Having heard the words of My sire, and being instructed by an advocate of God's supremacy and infallibility, I will, without a doubt, make the killing of Tataka My supermost duty! 5) For the well-being of the cows and the brahmanas, and for the sake of this tract of land, I am eager to do your bidding."

6\) Having thus spoken, and grasping the middle of His bow, He twanged the bow-string, and all directions echoed with the sharp sound. 7) The demonical residents of the forest were terrified by the noise, and Tataka became maddened with anger. 8) Hearing that sound, and stupefied with wrath, the furious demoness ran towards the source of the vibration. 9) Seeing her loathsome appearance, her deformed and distorted face, her dishevelled hair and her enormous bulk, Raghava addressed Lakshmana saying: 10) "Behold, Lakshmana, the fierce and pitiless form of this Yakshinee; the hearts of the timid would cleave in two were they to see her. 11) She is very dangerous and has the power to create illusions. Watch Me deprive her of her nose and ears and turn her out of here forthwith! 12) I am not especially eager to kill her, protected as she is by a woman's body. I think that I had better render her powerless instead."

13-15) While Rama was thus speaking, Tataka rushed upon Him with raised arms, screaming furiously. 14) For his part, Visvamitra derided her with a 'Humph!' and cried, "All good fortune to You. victory to Rama and Lakshmana!" Raising up a frightful dust storm, Tataka bewildered the princes with a great, dusty cloud for a while. 16) Then, by sorcerous means, she rained huge showers of stones upon Them, making Raghava furious. 17) He counteracted the great downpour of stones with a stream of arrows and, even as she ran towards Them, He cut off both of her hands. 18) As she screamed with pain close by, her arms severed, Lord Lakshmana angrily deprived her of her ears and the tip of her nose.

19\) She began to assume a variety of forms, and finally became invisible, bewildering the princes with her illusions. 20-22) Moving here and there, she discharged a terrible shower of rocks upon Them. Seeing Rama and Lakshmana obscured by the torrent of stones, Visvamitra spoke as follows: "Enough of pity, O Rama. This evil creature is a spoiler of sacrifices of old. You must slay her now; her power will increase after sunset! 23-30) Rakshasas become invincible during twilight, O Rama!"

Although she was veiled by a shower of stones, Rama was able to fix her position by sound, and He covered her with arrows. He covered her with a net of missiles, but by dint of her mystic power she ran bellowing towards the princes. She rushed upon Them with the speed and might of a thunderbolt, but Rama pierced her in the breast with an arrow and she fell, slain.

Seeing the fierce looking creature killed, Indra and other demigods applauded Rama with the words, "Well done! Well done!" Thousand-eyed Indra and his companions addressed Visvamitra with great happiness. "O sage," they said, "all good fortune to you! Indra and the host of Maruts are delighted by Raghava's feat. Please show affection to Him. Instruct Him in the use of the mighty weapons of Prajapati Krishasva; He is competent to receive them and is your devoted follower. 31) A tremendous feat is yet to be performed by Rama for the sake of the demigods, O brahmana!"

Having thus spoken, they departed with pleasure into the skies, honouring Visvamitra as they went, and at that the sun set, and twilight prevailed. The sage was delighted to see Tataka slain, and smelling Rama's head he said the following: "Let us stay here tonight, O beautiful Rama! 34) In the morning we shall go to my ashram." Hearing this, the son of Dasaratha became blissfully happy. 35) They rested in Tataka's forest until the morrow. The forest, now free from the cursed Yakshinee, shone with the splendour of Chaitraratha's garden. 36) Having despatched the demoness, Rama, Who was praised for His deed by the assembled demigods and perfected beings, stayed there in the company of Visvamitra and was awoken at sunrise.

*Thus ends the twenty-sixth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 27. Rama Instructed in the Use of Certain Weapons

Having rested that night, the greatly celebrated Visvamitra laughed and spoke these words to Rama with a sweet voice. 2) "I feel entirely satisfied, O illustrious prince, all good fortune unto You! With supreme affection I shall bestow all manner of weapons upon You. 3) With their assistance, You will subdue and conquer all Your adversaries, whether they be demigods, Gandharvas or Nagas. 4-6) I shall give You the divine discus weapon known as Danda-chakra, O Raghava, the discus called Dharma-chakra and the potent Kala-chakra as well. I shall give You the Vishnu-chakra, the terrifying thunderbolt weapon known as the Indra-chakra, the weapon presided over by Lord Siva called the Shoolavata, the missile known as Brahma's Head, and the weapon called Aisheeka too. 7-9) I will give You the unequalled Brahmastra and two beautiful, shining maces named Modaki and Shikari, O best of men! I shall give you the fetters known as Dharma-pasha and Kala-pasha, the fetter-weapon of Varuna and two thunderbolt weapons — one dry, the other wet.

10-12) "I will give You the weapons belonging to Lord Siva and Lord Narayana; the beloved weapon of Agni — the fiery Shikhara; the superexcellent weapon belonging to Vayu \[the wind-god\]; the Horsehead weapon and the Kraunchastra \[the Heron-beak\], a pair of darts, the weapons called Kankala \[the skeleton\], the horrible Musala, the Kapala \[the Skull\] and Kinkinee \[the Drum\]. 13-15) I shall give You whatever weapons are meant for killing demons as well as the mighty weapon belonging to the Vidyadharas: the jewellike sword called 'The Delighter'.

"I will give You the weapon of the Gandharvas known as 'The Stupefier', the weapon of the moon-god, called Prasvapana, which pacifies the enemy; Varshana \[the Rain weapon\], Soshana \[the Parching weapon\], Santapana \[the Heater weapon\], and Vilapana \[the Weeping weapon\]. 16) You will obtain the invincible Madana \[the Maddening\], beloved weapon of Cupid, and the Gandharva missile known as Manava. 17-18) I shall give You the famous weapon employed by the witches called Mohana \[the Bewilderer\], the missile known as Tamasa \[the Darkness weapon\], the unconquerable Samvarta \[the Dissolution weapon\], and the terrible Mausula \[the Club\], O Prince.

19\) "You will obtain the powerful Satya-weapon, the supreme Mayamaya, and the sungod's missile called Tejahprabha \[the Brilliant\], which robs the enemy of his power. 20) You will get the moongod's weapon, Sishira \[the Frigid\], the devastating weapons belonging to Tvashta and Bhaga, and Manu's weapon, called Siteshu \[the Cold-arrow\]. 21) All of these weapons are extremely powerful and can assume any form at will, O Rama. Please take these most noble of missiles immediately, O Prince!" 22) Having purified himself, and standing with his face to the east, Visvamitra gave Him, in great ecstasy, the mantras with which to invoke the weapons. 23) The sage then handed over all those weapons, which are difficult for even the demigods to acquire, unto Lord Rama, who committed them to memory. 24) As the muni muttered the appropriate mantras, the worthy missiles stood respectfully before Raghava. 25-27) Their palms joined, and with great bliss, they addressed Rama as follows: "Magnanimous Raghava, all good fortune unto You, we are Your surrendered servants; whatsoever You may desire, that we shall do!" Having thus been addressed by those immensely strong personalities, Lord Rama was most gratified, and caressing them with His hand He replied thus: "Kindly remain within My mind!" 28) Then, in a blissful mood, He humbly saluted the mighty sage, Visvamitra, and prepared to depart.

*Thus ends the twenty-seventh canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 28. Rama Instructed in the Withdrawal of the Weapons

Having received those weapons, Lord Rama, who was leaving Tataka's forest, addressed Visvamitra with a blissful smile upon His countenance. 2) "O lord," He said, "now that I possess these weapons, I am unassailable even by the demigods. I should like to know how to recall them also." 3) Being so addressed, the great ascetic taught Lord Rama the mantras for withdrawing the missiles.

4-10) He said, "O Raghava, as you are a fit recipient, please receive these brilliant weapon-sons of Krishasva as well, namely: Satyavan, Satyakeerti, Dhrishta, Rabhasa, Pratiharatara, Paranmukha, Avanmukha, Lakshya, Alakshya too, Dridhanabha, Sunabha, Dasaksha \[Ten-eyed\], Shatavaktra \[Hundredface\], Dasasheersha \[Ten-heads\], Shatodara \[Hundred-bellied\], Padmanabha and Mahanabha, Dundunabha and Svanabha, Jyoti, Shakuna, Nairasya and Vimala both; Yaugandhara and Vinidra \[Sleepless\], Daitya \[Titan\], Pramathana too, Suchibahu, Mahabahu, Nishkali, Virucha, Sarchimali, Dhritimali, Vrittiman, Ruchira as well, Pitrya, Saumanasa, Vidhoota and Makara, Paraveera, Rati also, Dhana, and Dhanya, O Raghava; Kamaroopa, Kamaruchi, Moha, Avarana, Jrimbhaka, Sarpanatha \[Lord of snakes\], Panthana and Varuna. All good fortune unto You!"

11\) "Yes", assented Lord Rama, Who inwardly felt very blissful upon seeing the transcendentally brilliant bodies of those weapons. 12) Some had the appearance of glowing coals, and others resembled smoke; some looked like the sun or the moon, and they bowed before Him with joined palms. 13) Standing with folded hands, they spoke to Him in sweet tones thus: "Here we are, O Tiger among men, say what we should do for You!"

14\) "Please be gone!" said Rama, "Whenever I require your aid you should appear from My mind."

15\) "As You wish!" they replied, and bidding Him farewell, they circumambulated Him and left as they had come. 16) Having thus become ac quainted with the weapons, Lord Rama spoke the following sweet words to the great sage as they proceeded: 17) "What is that wood close by a mountain that has the appearance of a mass of clouds? I am very curious to know! 18) It is a beautiful place, full of deer and adorned with a variety of sweetly singing birds. 19) I understand, by the cheerful appearance of this country, that we have just emerged from the forest that made one's hair stand on end. 20-22) Please tell Me, My lord, whose hermitage is this? Have we reached your ashram, where those evil creatures, slayers of brahmanas, disrupt your sacrifice, O great muni? Is this the place where I am meant to protect your sacrifice and to slay the Rakshasas who disturb it, O sage? Please tell Me, master, for I am eager to hear!"

*Thus ends the twenty-eighth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 29. Their Arrival at Siddha Ashram

In reply to the unequalled Lord's speech, the great sage, Visvamitra Muni, spoke as follows: 2-3) "Here, Rama, O Mighty-armed, dwelt Lord Sri Vishnu, Who is adored by the demigods, for countless years, and for one hundred ages more, and betook Himself to the performance of penance in order to contemplate Himself. This ashram formally belonged to Lord Vamana, the self-same Personality of Godhead. 4-5) This hermitage is known as the Siddha Ashram, for it was here that the Lord completed His austerities.

"At that time, Bali Maharaja, the son of Virochana, having conquered the hosts of demigods, including Indra \[their king\], and the Maruts as well, ruled over their kingdom and was celebrated throughout the three worlds. 6) The immensely powerful Bali, the King of the demons, performed a great sacrifice. The demigods, headed by Agni, came and personally spoke to Lord Vishnu with regards to Bali, here, in this hermitage.

7\) " 'Dear Lord Vishnu, Bali, the son of Virochana is performing a great sacrifice', they said, 'Please allow the object of Your servants to be fulfilled while he has yet to conclude his undertaking! 8) Persons come from here and there to petition him, and he always gives them whatever they desire. 9) By means of Your transcendent illusory potency \[Yoga Maya\] assume the form of a dwarf and go to him for the sake of Your devotees.'

10\) "At that moment, O Rama, the sage Kasyapa, who was equal in splendour to Agni, arrived there with his wife, Aditi, seeming to blaze, as it were, with his power. 11) Together with that goddess, the sage, who had just performed penance for one thousand years of the demigods, prayed to the Godhead as follows: 12) 'Being greatly purified by the penance I have performed, I perceive You to be the embodiment of austerity, the light of austerity, the transcendent form of austerity, the Self of austerity, and the Supreme Personality of Godhead! 13) O Lord, I perceive the entire universe to be within Your body. You are without any origin, beginningless and indescribable. I fully surrender unto You!'

14\) "Lord Sri Hari being pleased with Kasyapa, who had rid himself of all impurities, addressed him thus: 'All good fortune to you! O Kasyapa, please ask a boon of Me! In My estimation you are worthy of a benediction.' 15-16) Hearing this, Kasyapa spoke as follows: 'It is the desire of Aditi, myself, and the demigods also, that You be pleased to give the benediction of becoming my son, O unconquerable Lord. 17) Please become the younger brother of Indra \[our son\], O Slayer of demons, and thus You will aid the demigods, who are afflicted with misery. 18) Let this ashram be known as the Siddha Ashram \['Perfect Ashram'\] by Your grace, O Lord of the demigods, Who are perfect indeed! Please arise and go hence!'

19\) "Thereafter, the inconceivably powerful Lord Vishnu was born from Aditi and, having assumed the form of a dwarf, He approached Bali. 20-21) He strode across the universe for the sake of its inhabitants \[on the pretext of begging three paces of land\]. Having thus procured the three worlds from the powerful Bali, He restored them to Indra and gave him dominion over them once more. 22) This ashram was trodden by Him in days of yore, and is capable of destroying weariness and distress. I received this hermitage as a reward for my devotion to Lord Vamana. 23) the evil Rakshasas come to this place and spoil my sacrifices, and it is here that You must slay them, O Tiger among men. 24) Today we will reach the most excellent Siddha Ashram, O Rama. This hermitage, my dear, is as much Yours as it is mine!"

25\) Visvamitra then led Rama and Lakshmana into the ashram, and as he did so, he shone as brilliantly as the unobscured moon. 26) Beholding the sage, the munis, all residents of the Siddha Ashram, stood up as a body and began to worship him. 27) Not only did they pay homage to Visvamitra, but they performed all the rites pertaining to guests in honour of Rama and Lakshmana.

28\) The valiant princes rested for an hour or so, and then They addressed the great muni with joined palms, as follows: 29) "Please begin the initiatory rites of your sacrifice immediately, O bull-like sage. Today this perfect ashram will indeed become perfect, and your word will prove to be true!" 30) Being so addressed, the great rishi then performed the consecration of the sacrifice with controlled senses. 31-32) The two princes rested peacefully that night, and in the morning They bathed and chanted the Gayatri mantra in adoration of the rising sun, according to the scriptural rule. Thereafter, They worshipped Visvamitra, who was seated, having first offered oblations in the sacred fire.

*Thus ends the twenty-ninth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 30. Guarding Visvamitra's Sacrifice

Then the two royal princes, Who were cognizant of the principles of time and place, and Who knew what They should say at that time and in that place, put the following enquiry to Visvamitra. 2) "O Master," They said, "please tell Us the exact moment we shall be needed to ward off the two demons, for not an instant should be lost!" 3) The assembled sages praised the princes with great delight, for They were so enthusiastic to fight. They said: 4) "Today and for the next six days and nights You must watch over us. Now that Visvamitra has performed the initiatory rites he will observe silence." 5) Hearing the munis' pronouncement, Rama and Lakshmana guarded the ascetic's grove, without sleeping, for six days. 6) Armed with excellent bows, the watchful princes waited expectantly, guarding the best of sages, Visvamitra.

7\) Time passed by, and on the sixth day, when the somajuice was meant to be pressed, Rama spoke thus to Lakshmana: "Be on guard!" He said. 8) As Rama expressed His eagerness to fight, the sacred fire blazed up in the presence of the officiating priests. 9) The altar was adorned with kusa grass, the vessel intended for the soma-juice, a wooden ladle meant for pouring clarified butter into the fire, the wood meant to be consigned to the fire, and heaps of flowers. The sacred fire blazed up in the presence of Visvamitra and his assistants.

10\) As the sacrifice was proceeding, accompanied by the appropriate hymns and according to the prescribed rule, a terrifying sound was heard from the sky. 11) Just as the firmament is seen to be obscured by clouds in the rainy season, so the sky was covered by the Rakshasas, their forms altered by their mystic power. 12) Together with their followers, the fierce looking Mareecha and Subahu flew over the sacrificial arena and discharged a continuous stream of blood. 13) Beholding the altar sprayed with gore, Rama immediately ran to the spot and saw them flying in the sky. 14) Seeing the Rakshasas rushing upon Them, the lotuseyed Rama glanced at Lakshmana and spoke as follows: 15-17) "Lakshmana, look at these vile man-eating demons. I shall drive these aggressors away by means of the Coldarrow. I am not enthusiastic to actually kill such as these!"

So saying, Rama fitted Manu's exceedingly mighty and dazzling weapon to His bow, and with great speed He shot it at the furious Mareecha's breast. 18) The demon was thrown a full eight-hundred miles into the waters of the ocean by that most excellent of weapons. 19-20) "See how Mareecha has been worsted by the powerful Cold-arrow," said Rama to Lakshmana. "Although he has been tossed a great distance, he is not dead but rather is unconscious instead. 21)1 shall slay these other evil Rakshasas, for they are irredeemably sinful; they love to disturb sacrifices and are wont to feed upon human blood!" 22) Speaking thus, Rama fixed the weapon presided over by Agni to His bow with great alacrity, as if He were showing off His prowess to Lakshmana. 23) He shot the arrow into Subahu's breast, and being thus impaled, he fell dead to the earth.

The illustrious Rama slew the remainder of the demons with the missile belonging to the wind-god, bringing supreme joy to the sages. 24) Having thus killed all the Rakshasas, who were disturbers of sacrifices, Lord Raghava was worshipped by the rishis, just as Indra was celebrated in victory of yore.

25\) As soon as the sacrifice was complete, Visvamitra, seeing all directions free of disturbance, spoke as follows to Rama: 26) "I have fulfilled my vow, Powerful One, and You have done the bidding of Your guru. This place is now truly made the Perfect Ashram, O Hero!" Praising Lord Rama in that way, he chanted evening Gayatri with the two princes.

*Thus ends the thirtieth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 31. Setting out for Mithila

Rama and Lakshmana, having thus accomplished what They had set out to do, passed the night in the Siddha Ashram and enjoyed transcendental bliss within their minds. 2) At dawn, upon completing Their morning duties, the princes approached Visvamitra and the other sages. 3) They addressed Visvamitra, who shone as brilliantly as fire, very happily and with sweet voices. 4) "We two are your surrendered servants, O Tiger among sages", They said. "Please command Us, say what We should do next!"

5\) In reply to Their enquiry, the assembled sages, with Visvamitra at their head, spoke as follows: 6) "The king of Mithila, the pre-eminent Janaka, is about to perform a most righteous sacrifice; let us go there! 7) You should accompany us, O Rama, and You will be able to see a wonderful jewel of a bow at that place. 8) It was formerly bestown by certain demigods upon the dynasty of Mithila. It is of unequalled strength, is terrible to behold and is of dazzling splendour. 9) Neither demigod, Gandharva, demon nor Rakshasa has the power to string it, nor even any human being. 10) Numerous kings and princes have tried to ascertain its strength, but none have been able to bend it. 11) You will observe that wonderful bow of King Janaka's as well as his marvellous sacrifice, O Tiger among men! 12) That bow, which has an attractively fashioned hand-grip, was given to a former king of Mithila, as the fruit of a sacrifice, by the demigods. 13) It was installed in the king's palace, O Raghava, where it is presently worshipped with a variety of sandalwoods, incense, and aguru oils."

14\) Having thus spoken, Visvamitra set out for Mithila together with the assembled rishis and Rama and Lakshmana. He addressed the demigods who resided in the grove of Siddha Ashram saying: 15) "Farewell! Having completed my vow, I shall repair to the Himalaya mountain on the north bank of the Ganges." 16) Speaking thus, that Tiger among sages, enriched by his penances, started out in a northerly direction. 17) As he proceeded on his journey, Visvamitra was accompanied by one-hundred carts belonging to his followers, who were all advocates of the supremacy of the Godhead. 18) Even the beasts and birds of Siddha Ashram followed the austere sage. 19) After some while, Visvamitra and the company of sages sent those winged creatures, as well as the beasts, back to their habitation. 20-23) When they had journeyed a considerable distance, the sun set, and they made camp for the night on the bank of the River Sona. After taking a twilight bath, they offered oblations into the sacred fire, and the sages, who were immeasurably powerful, then sat Visvamitra at their head. Rama and Lakshmana, for Their part, offered Their obeisances to the muni, and sat before him. Lord Rama, the possessor of tremendous potencies, then questioned the tiger-like sage with great curiosity. "Whose is this beautiful country, Your Grace, with its flourishing flora and fauna? 24) I am curious to know, and you are able to speak factually. All good fortune unto you!" The mighty penitent, Visvamitra, then described that land in the midst of the congregation of sages, as requested by Lord Rama.

*Thus ends the thirty-first canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 32. Description of the Dynasty of Kusa

There was once a great ascetic, called Kusa, born of Brahma. He never deviated from his vow; he was conversant with the religious principles, and he worshipped the servitors of Lord Vishnu. 2) He begot four mighty sons, like unto himself, in the womb of a worthy lady from Vidarbha. 3-4) They were Kusamba, Kusanabha, Asoortarajasa and Vasu, and they were possessed of splendour and great enthusiasm. Being eager for them to perform the duties of the warrior caste, Kusa addressed his sons, who were both pious and truthful, as follows: 'My dear sons, prosecute your duty of protecting the people and you will acquire great religious merit!'

5\) "Hearing the words of their father, those noble princes established four cities. 6) The immensely powerful Kusamba founded the city called Kausambee, while the righteous Kusanabha founded Mahodaya. 7) The brilliant Asoortarajasa established Dharmaranya, and King Vasu the excellent town known as Girivraja. 8) The great-souled Vasu made the city called Vasumati his capital, and these five hill towns spread their effulgence in all directions. 9) The beautiful river Magadhee flowed in the midst of the five mountains like a shining garland. 10) This is the selfsame River Magadhee, O Rama, which flows to the east and is garlanded with goodly fields replete with grain. 11) The saintly Kusanabha begot one-hundred lovely daughters in the womb of the Apsara Gritachee, O Bliss of the Raghu dynasty. 12) Their beauty was enhanced as they attained maidenhood, and they would enter the royal park, where they appeared like a hundred shining lakes during the rainy season. 13) They would sing, dance, and play upon musical instruments. Supremely happy, they would be seen fully decorated with valuable ornaments. 14) The limbs of their bodies were possessed of a charming beauty, and upon entering the gardens they shone like stars between masses of clouds.

15\) "They had all the feminine virtues, and youthful grace, and upon seeing them, Vayu, the all-pervasive wind-god, spoke to them as follows: 16) 'I desire you all to be my wives. If you thus give up the nature of ordinary human beings you will attain a long duration of life. 17) Youth is fleeting, especially amongst human beings; if you marry me you will attain unchanging youthfulness and will become immortal too!'

18\) "Hearing Vayu's proposition, which was engendered by unbridled lust, the one-hundred daughters of Kusanabha laughed scornfully and replied to him thus: 19) 'O Best of demigods, you dwell within all living creatures, and we are conscious of your great power. To what end do you treat us with contempt? 20) We are the daughters of Kusanabha, O lord, O best of demigods, and we can have you fall from your exalted station. Let our chastity be preserved! 21) Do not be so presumptuous as to think that we will disregard the order of our truthful father by accepting a husband without his approval. 22) Our father is our master, O god, second to none in our estimation. That man to whom he gives us will be our husband and no other!'

23\) "Hearing their pronouncement, Vayu became utterly furious and, entering their bodies, the powerful demigod shattered their limbs. 24) Having thus been crippled, the princesses entered the king's palace. They were terribly shocked, and ashamed by what had befallen them, and they shed tears from their eyes. 25) Seeing his beloved, beautiful daughters in this pathetic condition, their limbs twisted, the baffled monarch spoke as follows: 26) 'What has happened? Who, disregarding all religious principles, could possibly have done such a thing? Who has made you into hunchbacks? Why do you merely gesture and tell me nothing?' So saying, the king heaved a sigh and composed his thoughts."

*Thus ends the thirty-second canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 33. The Marriage of Kusanabha's Daughters

Hearing the words of the intelligent Kusanabha, his daughters touched his feet with their heads and replied as follows: 2) 'The all-pervasive Vayu wished to violate our chastity, O king; he took to the path of unrighteousness and disregarded all religious principles. 3) We told him: "All good fortune unto you, we are obedient to our father and cannot act independently. You must ask him if you want us given as your wives". 4) Being of wicked intent he injured us like this, despite our pleading with him!'

5\) "The supremely pious king hearkened to their words and replied to his one-hundred daughters thus: 6) 'O my daughters, you have exhibited such tolerance as only the most forbearing could do. Not only did you agree unanimously with respect to your chastity, but you also considered the good name of my family! 7) Forbearance is the ornament of women and of men as well. It is hard to bear difficulties as you have done, and such tolerance as is to be found in the character of demigods is found in all of my daughters in an equal measure! 8) O my daughters, tolerance is charity, it is truth and it is sacrifice to God! Tolerance is fame, it is true piety and the forbearing earth is established in that vein.' 9) The mighty king then dismissed his daughters, O Rama. 10) He discussed the matter of their marriage with his counsellors, for it was necessary to give them to a worthy man at the proper moment and appropriate place.

11\) "At that time there was a splendid fellow by the name of Chooli: he had never discharged semina, his character was excellent and he had lately under taken severe austerities. 12) While he was engaged in his penances a certain Gandharvee rendered service to him. Her name was Somada and she was the daughter of Oormila. 13) She offered him her obeisances and was always most attentive to his needs, and in due course her guru \[Chooli\] became pleased with her. 14) At the appropriate time he spoke to her, saying: 'My blessings upon you! Tell me how I can reward you!' 15) Knowing the eloquent sage to be pleased with her, the Gandharvee, who was an accomplished speaker, replied with great joy and in a sweet voice: 16) 'By the grace of the Goddess of Fortune you want for nothing, and being established in the transcendence, O practitioner of penances, you are united with the Supreme Godhead. O sage, I desire a son who, by dint of his asceticism, is one with the Supreme Being. 17) Of course, I am without a husband — I am wife to no one — but you are able to endow me with a son by means of your spiritual potency.'

18\) "Delighted with her submissive request, Chooli awarded her a son whom he generated from his mind. He was called Brahmadatta and was ever absorbed in activities relating to Lord Vishnu. 19) King Brahmadatta \[as he became\] resided in the city known as Kampilya in great opulence, just as Indra does in the heavenly planets.

20\) "It was unto him, O Rama, that the exceedingly pious King Kusanabha resolved to give his one hundred daughters. 21) Summoning the immensely powerful Brahmadatta to his capital, the mighty sovereign gave away the one hundred princesses in great ecstasy. 2223) As Brahmadatta took the hand of each of them according to seniority, by his touch their deformities vanished, and they were endowed with the beauty and splendour of Lakshmi, the Goddess of Fortune. 24) When King Kusanabha saw them to be free from the injuries inflicted by Vayu, he experienced a thrill of happiness again and again. 25) When the marriage rites were completed, King Kusanabha sent Brahmadatta home with his wives and his teachers. 26) Seeing her son with suitable wives, the Gandharvee, Somada, welcomed her daughters-in-law according to their rank, and embracing the mover and again she praised King Kusanabha' s generosity."

*Thus ends the thirty-third canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 34. Visvamitra's Origin

When Brahmadatta had departed for home after his nuptials, King Kusanabha undertook a sacrifice for the purpose of getting a son. 2) At that time the exalted Kusa, son of Brahma, spoke to Kusanabha with great joy, saying: 3) 'My dear son, you will get a righteous male heir of befitting nobility. He will be called Gadhi, and he will acquire perpetual fame for you in the world!' 4) Having delivered this pronouncement, O Rama, Kusa left for the eternal abode of the Supreme Godhead in the spiritual sky.

5\) "In due course of time, a son named Gadhi was born unto the wise Kusanabha. 6) That supremely pious Gadhi is my father, and I am born in the dynasty of Kusa, O Raghunandana. 7) I also have an elder sister, O Raghava, called Satyavatee, who was given in marriage to Richika. 8-9) In obedience to her husband she ascended in her mortal body to the heavenly planets. After some time she was transformed into a most distinguished river, Kausikee by name. Her course clings to the Himalayas, and her delightful and transcendental waters confer spiritual merit upon those who bathe in her, thus benefiting the entire world.

10\) "Since that time I have dwelt happily at the foot of the Himalayas, full of affection for my sister. 11) That righteous and truthful Satyavatee still flourishes in the form of the river Kausikee, the best and most fortunate of streams!

12\) "Because of my vow I left that place and took to the Siddha Ashram, where I have become a perfected being by Your power! 13) Thus I have described my lineage, O Rama, and have replied to Your enquiry about this country.

14\) "Half the night has passed while I related these things, and now You should sleep. All good fortune unto You! May there be no obstacles upon our travels. 15) The trees now stand motionless, the beasts and birds are rapt in slumber, and darkness has spread in all directions. 16) Tranquillity has dismissed the twilight, the sky is densely spangled with stars and other heavenly bodies, and it appears to be covered with shining eyes. 17) The moon is rising, dispelling the darkness of the world with his frigid rays, delighting the minds of all living beings with his splendour. 18) Now the creatures of the night are wandering abroad: fierce, flesheating Rakshasas and Yakshas."

19\) Having thus spoken, the powerful sage became silent, and the assembled rishis honoured him saying: "Well done! Well done! 20) The dynasty of Kusa is great and supremely righteous, and his descendants are qualitatively as good as the Supreme Lord! 21) Your Holiness is especially glorious, while your sister Kausikee, the best of rivers, has added considerably to the splendour of your family!" 22) As the joyful sages eulogised Visvamitra, the fortunate sage entered sleep, just as the sun sets on the horizon. 23) Rama and Lakshmana, being somewhat amazed, worshipped the tiger-like sage, and then They too dozed off to sleep.

*Thus ends the thirty-fourth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 35. Description of the Origin of the Ganges and Uma

They rested that night on the bank of the Sona together with the sages. In the beautiful dawn that ensued, Visvamitra spoke as follows: 2) "The night has ended in a lovely sunrise, O Rama, and the morning twilight now prevails. My blessings upon You! Arise, arise! Be pleased to continue on Your journey!" 3) Hearing his request, Rama performed His morning duties, and being eager to depart He spoke thus: 4) "The Sona is shallow here and is adorned with sandy banks. By which of these two paths shall we ford the river, O brahmana?" 5) Being so addressed by Rama, Visvamitra said, "The path I pointed out is that by which great sages cross, O great-souled One." 6) They forded the stream, and having journeyed a considerable distance, when half the day was spent, they beheld the river Ganges, which is worshipped by great saints. 7) Seeing her transcendent waters, attended upon by swans and cranes, the royal princes and the sages became joyful.

8\) Thereafter, they made camp upon the bank of the river, and taking bath there, they offered libations of her water to the forefathers, according to the scriptural ordinance. 9) Next, They poured oblations into the sacred fire and enjoyed the remnants of the sacrifice, which tasted just like nectar. They then sat upon the pleasant shore of the Ganges in a blissful frame of mind. 10) They sat around Visvamitra according to their rank, and Lord Rama, in a joyous mood, questioned him as follows: 11) "Your Divine Grace, I wish to hear about the River Ganges, which flows through the three worlds. How, having traversed three planetary systems, did she reach the ocean?" 12) Enthused by Rama's enquiry, the great Visvamitra Muni began to describe the origin and growth of the Ganges. 13) "There is a monarch among mountains, a repository of minerals and gems, known as Himavan, King of the Himalayas. He has two daughters whose beauty is unequalled on earth. 14) Their mother was the slender-waisted daughter of Mount Meru known as Mena, beloved wife to Himavan. 15) This Ganges is the eldest of the two girls, O Rama, and the younger is Uma. 16) Desiring to perform the duties pertaining to celestials, the demigods asked the king of the mountains for the eldest daughter, who is now a river that flows along three courses. 17) Desiring the weal of the three worlds, and out of a sense of religious duty, Himavan gave them his daughter Ganga \[Ganges\], who flows as she does of her own volition. 18) Having fulfilled their purpose, the demigods took her for the sake of the three planetary systems, \[whose well-being they desired\] and feeling satisfied within, they returned to their abode.

19\) "The other daughter of the mountain, whose wealth was her austerity, practised severe penances, O Raghava. 20) Because of the cruel mortification she inflicted upon herself, Himavan gave Uma, who is worshipped all the world over, to Lord Siva. 21-22) Thus, the two illustrious daughters of the king of the mountains, namely: the Ganges, best of rivers, and the Goddess Uma, have been described to You, O Raghava.

"Ganga first of all rose into the sky, which is the highest resort of moving creatures. 23) There the daughter of Himavan took the form of the Milky Way, ascended to the abode of the demigods, and finally assumed the form of a river capable of delivering one from sin."

*Thus ends the thirty-fifth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 36. Description of the Glories of Uma

Upon the sage concluding his narration, which Rama and Lakshmana heard with joy, the two Heroes addressed that bull among ascetics thus: 2) "This narrative, full of righteousness, is the most excellent thing you have told Us! Please give us a more extensive account of Ganga, the eldest daughter of Himavan. You are well acquainted with her and are competent to describe her dealings with men and demigods elaborately. 3) What was the reason for her taking three different courses and thereby cleansing the world of its sin? How did she come to be celebrated as the best of rivers? 4-6) With which deeds is she connected in the three worlds?"

Being so addressed by Lord Rama, the great ascetic, Visvamitra, related the entire history, in the midst of the assembled sages, as follows: "Once, O Rama, the immensely austere Lord Siva, the performer of severe penances, having just wed the goddess Uma, looked upon her and approached her for copulation. The intelligent Lord Siva, Mahadeva, sported with her thus for one-hundred celestial years. 7) No offspring resulted from their union, O Chastiser of enemies, and the demigods, headed by Brahma, reasoned among themselves thus: 8) 'If a child is produced it will undoubtedly be very powerful; who among us will be able to withstand its might?' The demigods therefore approached Lord Siva, and falling prostrate before him submitted the following: 9) 'O Lord of demigods, O Mahadeva, the welfare of the universe is held dear by you: please bestow your mercy upon the demigods who now lay prone before you! 10) O best of demigods, O you who practice penance with the Goddess and are thus united with the Supreme Brahman, Lord Vishnu — the planets will not be able to sustain your embodied potency! 11) For the sake of the three worlds, please retain your powerful semina within your glorious body. Please protect the universe, O Lord, do not bring about its destruction!'

12\) "Hearing the prayers of the demigods, Lord Siva, the supreme lord of the mundane universe, assented, and spoke thus: 13) 'I will indeed retain my semina by my power, and so shall Uma also. May the planets of the Devas, and the earth too, have no fear! 14) Tell me though, O demigods, who will bear my unequalled semina? for it has already been shaken from its proper place, and it must be discharged.'

15\) "Being thus questioned, the demigods replied to Lord Siva, whose flag is emblazoned with a bull, as follows: 'That semina which was aroused today will be borne by the earth!' 16) Lord Siva then let loose his powerful seed, which covered the entire world together with its mountains and forests. 17) The demigods spoke once more: 'O Fire-god,' they said, 'enter Rudra's \[Siva's\] mighty semen along with Vayu!' 18) Agni duly covered Lord Siva's semina, and a white mountain was generated. It had the appearance of a celestial forest of reeds, and it shone as brilliantly as fire or the sun. 19-20) From that mountain the greatly powerful Kartikeya, the equal of Agni, was born. The demigods and rishis were exceedingly pleased, and they thus began to worship Lord Siva and Uma.

"Then the daughter of the king of mountains spoke to the demigods as follows. 21) In an angry mood, her eyes reddened with rage, she cursed them all: 'I was engaged in sexual union with Lord Siva for the purpose of getting a son, but you have frustrated my desire! 22) I therefore curse you that you will be unable to beget offspring in your wives. From this day on they will be without issue!' 23) Having thus addressed the demigods, she also cursed the earth: 'Your surface will no longer be uniform,' she said, 'and you will be the wife of many husbands! 24) Neither shall you have the pleasure of bearing a son, being thus defiled by my anger, O you who wish me childless!'

25\) "Seeing the demigods thus afflicted, Lord Siva started towards the residence of Vayu. 26) Arriving there, he sat down on the north face of Mount Himavatprabhava, and together with his consort, Goddess Uma, began to perform penances. 27) Thus I have given a detailed account of the younger daughter of the king of mountains. Now listen, along with Lakshmana, as I relate the origin of the Ganges."

*Thus ends the thirty-sixth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 37. The Birth of Kartikeya

While Lord Siva performed penances, the demigods, together with Indra and under the leadership of Agni, approached Lord Brahma, desiring a commander for their armies. 2) Making obeisance before the powerful grandfather of the universe, the demigods addressed him thus: 3) 'Lord Siva previously undertook to provide us with a leader for our army, but now he engages in penance along with Goddess Uma. 4) Please immediately do that which will benefit the world. You know what ought to be done under the circumstances, and you are therefore our only resort.'

5\) "Being thus petitioned by the demigods, Lord Brahma consoled them with the following sweet words: 6) 'That which Uma spoke regarding your wives' sterility will no doubt prove to be true, for her curse cannot be counteracted. 7) Agni can beget a son in the heavenly Ganges who will lead your armies to victory. 8) The eldest daughter of the mountain will consider him to be her own son, and Uma will undoubtedly think him hers as well.'

9\) "Hearing the satisfying words of Brahma, the demigods bowed down before him and worshipped him. 10) Having reached the superexcellent Mount Kailasa, which is adorned with various minerals, the demigods urged Agni to beget a son, O Rama. 11) 'For the benefit of the Devas, O god, gather up Lord Siva's mighty semina, and discharge it in Ganga, the daughter of the king of the mountains.' 12) Promising to fulfil the Devas' request, the fire-god approached Ganga and said, 'O Goddess, please bear this seed for the pleasure of the demigods!' 13) Being so addressed, Ganga assumed a transcendentally beautiful form, and beholding her majesty, Lord Siva's semen melted all around.

14\) "Thereafter, Agni sprinkled her on all sides, filling the orifices in her body, O Raghava. 15-18) Ganga then addressed the leader of the demigods, saying, 'O Lord, I am unable to bear this semen combined with your fiery seed; I feel as if I am burning, and my mind is greatly afflicted!' Agni, who eats the offerings cast into the sacred fire on behalf of the Devas, said: 'Let this seed fall here on the mountain's flank, O Goddess!' Hearing Agni's utterance she discharged that fiercely resplendent semina from her bodily orifices, and it came forth as brilliant molten gold! 19) The semina that fell on the earth became gold of unequalled splendour, and because of its fierce heat, copper and black iron were also generated. 20) From the dross of that gold, tin and lead were produced, and a variety of minerals grew within the earth.

21\) "Upon that semina being discharged, the entire mountain, as well as the reed like shards of Lord Siva's semen, were transformed into lustrous gold! 22) From that day on, gold, which is as splendid as fire, has been known as jata-roopa, O Raghava. 23) Indra and the Maruts entrusted the Krittikaswith the duty of suckling the infant born from that semina. 24) Being resolved that the child would be the son of them all, they began to nurse him immediately after he was born. 25) The demigods then named the boy Kartikeya \[after his foster mothers\]. 'His fame will spread throughout the three worlds; of this there is no doubt!' they declared.

26\) "Hearing their prophetic words, the Krittikas began to bathe the boy, who had just trickled from Ganga's womb, and he shone with a majesty as dazzling as fire. 27) They also gave the mighty-armed Kartikeya the name 'Skanda'because he had oozed from his mother's womb. 28) Thereafter, milk appeared in the breasts of his foster mothers, and because there were six of them he developed six heads and began to drink from the breasts of each of them simultaneously. 29) Having drunk their milk for only one day, he developed a strong, youthful body, and very soon, by reason of his valiant nature, he was able to master the demonic hordes. 30) The refulgent Kartikeya was consecrated as the supreme commander of the celestial army by the assembled demigods, headed by Agni.

31\) "Thus, O Rama, I have declared unto You a detailed description of the Ganges and the origin of Skanda, the hearing of which is productive of affluence and piety. 32) A devotee of Kartikeya is assured of a long life and a place for himself, his sons and his grandsons in Kartikeya's abode."

*Thus ends the thirty-seventh canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 38. The Episode of King Sagara

Having related the above histories in sweet accents, Visvamitra then described the following incidents to Rama. 2) "Once, there was a righteous king of Ayodhya, O Hero, called Sagara. Being without any issue, he was desirous of obtaining offspring. 3) King Sagara's principal wife was the daughter of the king of Vidarbha, Keshinee by name. She was virtuous and ever true to her word. 4) His second wife was the daughter of Arishtanemi and sister to Garuda; her name was Sumati. 5) Together with his wives he went to the Himalayas, and on Mount Bhriguprasravana the three of them performed austerities.

6\) "After they had executed one hundred years of penance, the truthful sage, Bhrigu Muni, became pleased with them, and he therefore conferred a benediction upon King Sagara. 7) 'O sinless one,' he said, 'you will get many sons, and you will achieve unequalled fame in the world! 8) You will beget a son in one of your wives who will perpetuate your dynasty, and in the other you will beget no less than sixty-thousand sons, O King!' 9) Having thus benedicted that tiger among men, the great sage was addressed by the two princesses, the wives of Sagara, who spoke to him in great happiness, their palms joined.

10\) " 'Which of us will bear one son,' they asked, 'and which of us will have many? We long to hear, O brahmana, please tell us truthfully!' 11) The supremely righteous Bhrigu replied thus: 'Let your free will settle the matter! 12) One of you can have a son who will perpetuate your royal family; and the other may have many sons, who will be mighty, renowned and enthusiastic. Which of you would like which? 13) Hearing the sage's reply, Keshinee elected to have a son who would perpetuate the royal dynasty, O Rama. 14) Then, the sister of Garuda, Sumati, chose to have the sixty-thousand enthusiastic and renowned sons. 15) They then circumambulated the sage, and bowing their heads down before him, they returned to the city; the king and his two wives.

16\) "In due course of time the eldest son of King Sagara was born and was named Asamanja. 17) Sumati for her part gave birth to a gourd-like foetus, and when it was split open, sixty-thousand individual sons issued out of it. 18) Their nursemaids nurtured them in ghee-filled pots, and after an unusually long time they attained maturity. 19) A further lengthy period of time having elapsed, King Sagara was the proud father of sixty-thousand handsome youths.

20\) "The eldest son, however, used to seize children and would toss them into the water of the Sarayu. 21) He would watch them sink in the river and would laugh heartily as they drowned. He always conducted himself in an evil fashion; he would mistreat saintly persons and was inimical toward the king's subjects. For that reason his father banished him from Ayodhya.

22\) "The son of Asamanja was the heroic Amsuman. 23) Amsuman was loved by all the people, and he would always speak endearingly to whomever he met. After some time, King Sagara made the momentous decision to perform a horse sacrifice. 24) Having made that resolution, he prepared for the rite together with priests who were learned in the Veda and the performance of sacrifice."

*Thus ends the thirty-eighth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 39. The Sacrificial Horse Sought by the Sons of King Sagara

As Visvamitra concluded his narration, Raghava spoke to the sage, who shone like fire, with great happiness. 2) "All glory to you!" He said, "I long to hear this story in more detail. Please tell Me; how did My ancestor perform the sacrifice, O brahmana?" 3) Encouraged by Rama's enthusiastic enquiry, the sage began to speak in a jolly mood. 4) "Please listen, and I shall relate everything, O Rama! Lord Siva's father-in-law is known as Himavan. 5) The Vindhya mountains and Himavanoverlook one another, and the sacrifice was performed in the region between them. 6-9) It is recommended that yajnas be performed in that vale, O tiger among men. The sacrificial horse was overseen by Amsuman, who, being a great warrior, was armed with a mighty bow and was at King Sagara's command.

While the sacrifice was in progress, however, Indra assumed the form of a Rakshasa and stole the sacrificial horse. As the steed was being carried off, the great-souled priests who were conducting the rite spoke thus: 'The sacrificial horse is being forcibly led away! 10) The thief must be killed, O king, and the horse retrieved. If there is a hiatus in the sacrifice there will be evil consequences for us all! 11-13) You must therefore ensure that the rite is without any such disruption, O monarch.' Hearing the priests' warning in that assembly, Maharaja Sagara commanded his sixty-thousand sons as follows: 'Since this magnificent rite is in the hands of distinguished brahmanas, who have been purified by the appropriate mantras, I fail to see how a Rakshasa could penetrate the sacrificial arena. Nevertheless, you must go and search out the whereabouts of the missing horse, and may good fortune attend you. 14) You should search throughout the entire earth, which is decorated with oceans. Each one of you should undertake to scour sixty-four square miles of land. 15) In order to seek out the horse, be prepared to dig within the ground, searching until, by my command, the thief is found! 16) As I have been duly initiated into the rite I shall stay here with my grandson and the priests until the horse is returned. May you be successful in your mission!'

17\) "Being thus obligated by the command of their father, the mighty princes set out across the world with happy minds, O Rama. 18) Travelling far and wide, they were unable to catch a glimpse of the horse, and they began to split open the earth with their arms, which were like thunderbolts to the touch, each within an area of sixty-four square miles. 19) The earth began to weep as, with great ferocity, they tore into her with pikes as keen as lightning and with mighty ploughshares. 20) There arose the bellows and shrieks of demons, serpents and goblins that were being killed, O Bliss of the Raghus! 21) They excavated three-million, eight-hundred and forty-thousand square miles of land, O Rama, and practically penetrated through to the unequalled subterranean planet known as Rasatala. 22) They dug up the entire surface of this planet, Jamboodweepa, — which abounds in mountains — in all directions.

23\) "In great distress, Gandharvas, demons and serpents, along with certain demigods, approached Lord Brahma. 24) Having offered him due homage, they addressed the grandfather of the worlds, with miserable faces and trembling with fear, as follows: 25) 'O lord, the entire earth is being dug up by the sons of King Sagara. Many great souls and aquatic creatures are being killed! 26) They think that anyone they see is the disturber of their sacrifice and has carried off their horse; for that reason they are slaying every creature in the world!'"

*Thus ends the thirty-ninth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 40. They Encounter Kapiladeva

Hearing the submission of the Devas, who were shivering with fear and were be wilder ed by the strength of the sons of Sagara, Lord Brahma replied thus: 2) 'The personality of the earth is consort to the Supreme Godhead, Lord Vishnu, the husband of the Goddess of Fortune. 3) Having assumed the form of Kapiladeva, He continually supports the earth. The sons of King Sagara will be burnt by the fire of His wrath! 4) The excavation of the earth is always to be seen \[in some universe or other\], and the destruction of the sons of Sagara has been foretold by those who can descry the future!' 5) Hearing the grandsire's prophecy, the demigods, who were capable of subduing their foes, went as they had come, with supreme joy.

6\) As the sons of Sagara were splitting open the earth, a terrible sound arose like the din of a thunderstorm. 7) Having excavated the entire earth and having circumambulated her as well, the princes returned to King Sagara and made the following report: 8) 'We have encircled the earth, and demigods, titans, demons, witches, serpents, and Nagas have been slain in the process. 9) We have seen neither the steed nor its thief. Please be so gracious as to determine what we should do next!'

10\) "Hearing the words uttered by his sons, the best of kings, Maharaja Sagara, replied angrily, saying: 11) 'Continue excavating the earth, and having penetrated her, search out the purloiner of the horse. When you have accomplished the task, then you may return to me!' 12) In response to their father's command, the sixty-thousand princes quickly dug their way to the subterranean Rasatala. 13) While they were thus digging, they encountered one of the mountainous elephants, Viroopaksha by name, who supports the earth planet. 14) That great elephant bore the earth, with its mountain ranges and forests, upon his head, O Rama. 15) On occasions, to get relief from exhaustion, he shakes his great head and earthquakes occur. 16) Having circumambulated and shown respect to him, they penetrated through to Rasatala.

17\) "Then, having tunnelled through to Rasatala in the east, they dug through the earth in the south. There, as in the east, they beheld a gigantic elephant supporting that quadrant of the world. 18) This elephant is known as Mahapadma; he resembles an immense mountain, and the princes were mightily astonished to see him. 19) The sixty-thousand sons of Sagara circumambulated him and then penetrated the western quadrant. 20) There also they beheld a mountainous elephant: in this instance the one named Saumanasa. 21) They circumambulated him, enquired after his well-being, and began to dig towards the northern quadrant, which is under the superintendence of the moon. 22) In the north they saw a colossal snow-white elephant bearing the earth upon his lovely body. 23) Having stroked his limbs, they circumambulated him, and then the sixty-thousand sons of Sagara began tunnelling once more.

24\) "They headed towards the celebrated north-easterly region, digging through the earth with great ferocity. 25) There, the powerful princes, being full of terrible impetuosity, descried the eternal Lord Sri Vishnu in the form of Kapiladeva. 26) Not far from the Lord the missing horse was grazing, and the princes experienced unequalled delight upon seeing it. 27-28) They assumed Kapiladeva was the spoiler of their father's sacrifice and taking up various weapons, such as shovels, tree trunks and boulders, they rushed upon Him with anger-filled eyes. 'Stop, stop!' they cried, 'you have stolen our sacrificial horse! 29-30) You evil-minded wretch, know that the sons of Sagara have arrived!' Hearing their challenge, Lord Kapila became filled with rage, and He uttered a sound of disgust. At that, the sixty-thousand sons of Sagara were reduced to heaps of ashes by the Supreme Soul, the un qualled Lord Kapiladeva."

*Thus ends the fortieth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 41. The Conclusion of King Sagara's Sacrifice

Thinking his sons had been absent an unduly long time, Maharaja Sagara spoke thus to his brilliantly effulgent grandson, Amsuman: 2) 'You are heroic, knowledgeable, and in power the equal of your illustrious ancestors. Go and search out the whereabouts of your uncles and ascertain who has purloined the horse! 3) The subterrestrial beings are both fearless and formidable. In order to combat them please take a bow and sword. 4) Offer respects to whoever deserves respect and kill whoever obstructs you. In order that my sacrifice may be completed, return only when you have accomplished your purpose!'

5\) "In obedience to King Sagara's command, Amsuman took up a bow and sword and left with swift steps. 6) Emboldened by the king's words, he found the tunnel to the subterranean regions dug by his noble uncles. 7) Having traversed its length he beheld a great elephant being worshipped by Devas, titans, goblins, witches and serpents. 8) After circumambulating him, he asked after his well-being and enquired about his uncles and the horse-thief. 9) Hearing Amsuman's queries, the elephant replied thus: 'O son of Asamanja, you will be successful in your mission and will shortly return with the missing steed!' 10) Being thus assured, Amsuman successively approached the o the r elephants, with due respect, and he put the same query to all of them. 11-12) They were all eloquent and wise, and having treated him with honour, they informed him that he would return with the horse.

"Emboldened by their prophetic words he proceeded with swift paces till he came upon the remains of his uncles, the sons of Sagara, who had been reduced to heaps of ashes by fire. 13) Being already grief-stricken on account of their death, and giving way to sorrow, he cried very piteously. 14) Afflicted with misery, that tiger-like personality spotted the sacrificial horse a short distance off, moving hither and thither. 15) He desired to perform the ceremony of offering water to his deceased ancestors but could see no reservoir of water thereabouts. 16) Extending his powerful vision he then descried the king of birds, Garuda, the maternal uncle of the sons of Sagara, whose gorgeous wings bore him as swiftly as the wind. 17) The mighty Garuda addressed the prince with the following words: 'Be of good cheer, O tiger among men. Your uncles were killed for the weal of the world! 18) These powerful warriors were burnt by the unequalled Lord, Kapiladeva. You will not be able to offer them terrestrial water, O learned one. 19) You should perform the funeral rites with the sacred water of the Ganges, the eldest daughter of the Himalayas. 20) Once their ashes are wetted by her waters, which are dear to everyone, the sixty-thousand sons of Sagara will ascend to the celestial planets. 21) Return with the horse, O fortunate one, and having thus retrieved the beast you can ensure the completion of your grandfather's sacrifice.'

22\) "In response to Garuda's command, the heroic Amsuman took the steed and swiftly returned to his grandfather. 23) Thereafter, having approached King Sagara, who was prepared to continue the sacrifice, he related everything that occurred, as well as the advice of Garuda. 24) Maharaja Sagara heard Amsuman's apparently dreadful report, and then, to the best of his ability, he had the sacrifice concluded. 25) When the ritual was over, the king returned to the city, but he did not come to any particular conclusion in the matter of fetching the River Ganges. 26) King Sagara never came to any concrete resolution with respect to his son's funeral rites, and having ruled for thirty-thousand years, he ascended to the spiritual sky."

*Thus ends the forty-first canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 42. King Bhagiratha Endeavours to Bring Down the Ganges

Time decreed the departure of Maharaja Sagara, O Rama, and the people desired the coronation of Amsuman. 2) He duly ascended to the throne and proved himself to be a mighty sovereign. His son in turn was very great; he was celebrated by the name Dileepa. 3) Having entrusted the rulership to Dileepa, Maharaja Amsuman repaired to the beautiful summit of Mount Himavan and performed severe penances. 4) He practised austerities for a period of three-million, two-hundred thousand years, and was then transferred to the Kingdom of God.

5-6) "When mighty Dileepa heard of the destruction of his antecedents, his intellect was impaired by his grief, and he was thus unable to determine how the Ganges should be brought down to Rasatala, by what means his uncles' funeral rites might be performed and how they might be delivered from their predicament. Considering these questions, he became absorbed in contemplation. 7) Dileepa was constantly rapt in meditation and was consequently renowned for his piety. He sired a son by the name of Bhagiratha, who was a great, spiritually orientated being. 8) Maharaja Dileepa performed many sacrifices for the pleasure of Lord Vishnu, and the sovereign governed his realm for thirtythousand years. 9) He could not, however, come to any particular conclusion in the matter of his great-uncles' deliverance, and in due course of time he fell ill and died. 10) He ascended, by his own power, to the abode of Indra, and the bull-like Bhagiratha was coronated king.

11\) "Bhagiratha was a king-sage, O Raghava, and he desired progeny, for he was at that time without issue. 12) He was intent on bringing down the Ganges, and he therefore entrusted the kingdom into the care of his ministers. He then performed austerities for an exceedingly long time in the pilgrimage place known as Gokarna. 13) He stood with raised arms in the midst of five blazing fires and ate but once a month, and suffering this severe mortification for onethousand years, he brought his senses under control. 14) At length, the master of kings, Lord Brahma, became pleased with the powerful Bhagiratha. 15) He approached him, in the company of the demigods, as the sovereign performed his penances, and spoke to him as follows: 16) 'O lord of men, King Bhagiratha, I am satisfied by your austerities. Kindly ask a boon of me, O determined one!'

17\) "Thus spoke Lord Brahma unto Maharaja Bhagiratha, the emperor of the entire world. The king for his part stood respectfully with joined palms. 18) 'O Lord,' he replied, 'if you are pleased with me, let my reward be that my forefathers, the sons of Sagara, may receive the funerary water from me. 19) When the ashes of my ancestors are wetted by the waters of the Ganges they will immediately attain to the heavenly kingdom. 20) I also beg you my lord, so that the dynasty of Ikshvaku will not perish, that you benedict me with a son!'

21\) "Hearing his request, Brahma, the grandsire of all living beings, replied with sweet words and sweet expressions: 22) 'O Bhagiratha, mighty warrior, this is a lofty ambition. May it come to pass! My blessings upon you, O joy of the Ikshvaku dynasty! 23) You should persuade Lord Siva to bear the violent descent of the Ganga, eldest daughter of Mount Himavan, for he alone is able to do so! 24) The earth is not able to sustain the precipitous fall of the Ganges, O King, and I see none but Lord Siva who is capable of doing so.' 25) Having thus spoken to the king, and to the Ganges as well, Lord Brahma, the creator of the world, left for his abode, Brahmaloka, along with the assembled demigods and the Maruts."

*Thus ends the forty-second canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 43. The Descent of the Ganges

After the chief of the demigods, Lord Brahma, had departed, King Bhagiratha stood upon his great toe for an entire year in order to propitiate Lord Siva. 2) At the end of that year the worshippable Lord Siva, husband of Uma and Lord of beasts, appeared before the king and spoke as follows: 3) 'I am delighted with you, O best of men, and I shall fulfil your desire. I will bear the daughter of the Himalayas upon my head!'

4-8) "At that moment, the Ganges, who is worshipped the world over, assumed a greatly destructive form, and fell from the heavens upon the head of Lord Siva with unbearable violence. 'I shall flow on to Patala and shall carry Lord Siva in my stream!' thought Ganga, who is difficult to restrain. But Lord Siva, the three-eyed, perceiving her arrogance, became angry and resolved to cause her disappearance.

"Thus the pure Ganges fell upon the divine head of Lord Siva, whose impenetrable, matted locks resemble a mountain. Being thus entrapped in his hair, she was unable to proceed to the earth despite making every effort to do so. 9) She could find no outlet from Lord Siva's labyrinthine locks, even at their extremities, and she wandered about within them for many aeons. 10) Seeing her thus ensnared, Maharaja Bhagiratha again performed severe penance, greatly pleasing Lord Siva, O Bliss of the Raghus. 11) Lord Siva then propelled the Ganges towards Lake Bindusara, and as he did so, she issued forth in seven streams. 12) Three branches of her auspicious and delightful waters flowed east as the rivers Hladinee, Pavinee and Nalinee. 13) To the east flowed three great streams called Suchakshu, Seeta and Sindhu. 14) The seventh branch flowed behind King Bhagiratha, who was mounted upon a divine chariot. 15) Having descended from the firmament, the mighty Ganges fell upon Lord Siva's head. From there she came to earth and followed behind the king.

16-18) "As she glided along, her waters made a terrible din, and the earth was beautified with fishes, turtles and porpoises that fell along with her. At that time, demigods, sages, Gandharvas, Yakshas and Siddhas observed the descent of the Ganges from the sky and her progress too. They appeared in aeroplanes fashioned like cities, along with horses and excellent elephants. 19-22) And distinguished celestials went there in ships, being desirous to witness the wonderful descent of the Ganges to the earth. the demigods, who were possessed of unequalled splendour, assembled in the heavens, fully decorated with brilliant jewellery, and the cloudless sky appeared to blaze with thousands of suns. Porpoises and snakes thrashed about in the mighty downpour, and the sky seemed to be covered with lightning, as thousands of flecks of foam burst from the flood. 23-24) The firmament appeared to be scattered with white, autumnal clouds, as multitudes of swans flew hither and thither.

"At times Ganga careered along, at others she took a crooked course; at times she followed cautiously, and then became very placid. Sometimes her waters were buffeted against each other, over and over again, and her progress was thus somewhat impeded. 25-30) She repeatedly rose into the air and fell again to the earth.

"Having first plummeted upon the head of Lord Siva, and thence to the ground, her spotless waters vanquished the sins of whoever bathed in her. Various sages and Gandharvas who resided at the place where she washed the limbs of Lord Siva, touched her waters, considering them to be pure. Those who had fallen to the earth from the celestial planets due to being cursed, bathed in the Ganga and were cleansed of their impurities. Their sins having thus been removed, O Lord, they again ascended to their respective abodes. The denizens of the earth were delighted by her sparkling waters, and by bathing in her had all their impurities washed away.

"King Bhagiratha, a sage among monarchs, was mounted upon a divine chariot. 31-32) The great sovereign proceeded first, with the Ganges at his back, and the demigods, sages, titans, demons, goblins, Gandharvas, Yakshas, Kinnaras, serpents and Apsaras followed behind his car. 33-35) They followed the Ganga with great joy along with various aquatic creatures. Wherever King Bhagiratha went, the Ganges, the best of rivers and the vanquisher of sins, followed.

"It so happened that the Ganga flooded the sacrificial arena of the sage known as Jahnu, the performer of extraordinary feats. Perceiving her arrogance, O Raghava, he became furious. 36-37) He drank every drop of her water, a supremely wonderful deed, and the astonished Devas, sages, and Gandharvas worshipped the great ascetic, and brought Ganga before him and presented her to him as his daughter. 38) Becoming pleased with her at that, the powerful sage discharged her waters from his ears, and because of her being the daughter of Jahnu, they named her Jahnavee.

39-40) "As she proceeded on her course with King Bhagiratha's chariot at the fore, she reached the ocean \[the bed of which was excavated by the sons of Sagara\], and at that point she penetrated through to Rasatala, thereby making the king's undertaking successful. 41) Beholding his granduncles reduced to ashes, he lost consciousness a while. Maharaja Bhagiratha then caused the transcendent waters of the Ganges to wash over the heaps of his forefathers' ashes, and being thus purified of the reactions to their sins, the sons of Sagara attained the heavenly planets."

*Thus ends the forty-third canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 44. The Sons of Sagara Attain the Heavenly Planets

Maharaja Bhagiratha reached the ocean with the Ganges in his train; thereafter he entered the underworld, where the sons of Sagara had been reduced to ashes. 2) Having washed the ashes of his ancestors with the waters of the Ganga, Maharaja Bhagiratha was addressed by Lord Brahma as follows: 3) 'O King, you have redeemed the sons of the great-souled Sagara and they have attained the status of demigods in the celestial planets. 4) Just as the waters of the ocean endure upon the earth, so have the sixty-thousand sons of Sagara been established in their celestial abodes. 5-6) Henceforth, because of what you have done, the Ganga will be celebrated as your eldest daughter. Thus she will be called the divine Bhagirathi, who flows along three coursesand because she now flows along three paths, she shall be known as Tripathaga as well. 7) Now you should fulfil your vow by offering her water to your forefathers, O king.

8\) " 'Previous to this, the desire of your forefathers to bring down the glorious Ganga has proved fruitless. 9-10) Even Amsuman of unsurpassable valour wished to convey her here, but despite his being a king-sage, with all the virtues of the warrior caste, and endowed with the splendour of a maharshi he was disappointed, being unable to do so. 11) Neither was your mighty father, the fortunate Dileepa, able to bring her here, O sinless one! 12) You have conveyed the Ganga here just as you promised, and have thus won yourself unsurpassed fame and glory on earth. 13) Because you have caused the descent of the Ganges you have earned yourself a place in the kingdom of God, O chastiser of the enemy. 14) Bathe yourself in these waters, which are ever pure, and let purity be the reward for your pious deed! 15) Perform the funeral rites for your forefathers, O best of men. May all good fortune attend you. Farewell! Now I shall return to my abode.' 16) Having thus spoken, Lord Brahma, the master of the Devas, left for his heavenly residence.

17-18) "King Bhagiratha, for his part, performed the funerary rites in a befitting manner, and according to the scriptural ordinances, with the transcendental waters of the Ganges. And having thus purified the remains of the illustrious sons of Sagara, he entered his capital city. He then took up the rulership of his kingdom and greatly enhanced its prosperity. 19) His subjects were happy to have regained their king, and under his guidance their sorrows were vanquished, their wealth increased and their afflictions ceased.

20\) "I have now declared the history of the Ganges in detail, O Rama. Please accept my blessings; may all be well with You! The time for evening gayatri passes by. 21-22) Whoever recites this beautiful account of the descent of the Ganges, whether he be a brahmana, a kshatriya or of the lower castes, will get grain, fame, sons, long life, and will satisfy both his forefathers and the demigods \[without any extraneous effort\]. 23) He who gives ear to this wonderful narrative will have all his desires fulfilled, his sins will all be eradicated and his good name and duration of life will undoubtedly prosper and increase!"

*Thus ends the forty-fourth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 45. Description of the Churning of the Ocean of Milk

Rama and Lakshmana listened to Visvamitra's narrative with very great astonishment. As soon as he finished They addressed him thus: 2) "This account you have given Us of the descent of the Ganges and the filling of the ocean \[whose bed was hollowed out by the sons of Sagara\], is truly amazing, O sage! 3) While We were reflecting upon your narrative, O chastiser of foes, the entire night seems to have passed in an instant. 4) As Lakshmana and I dwelt upon this wonderful story, the night has come to an end!"

5\) In the spotless dawn that ensued, Raghava addressed the austere Visvamitra, who had completed his morning duties, as follows: 6) "Let Me cross the Ganges, who is the best and most righteous of rivers. That blessed night wherein We listened to her glorious history has now passed. 7) Here is a boat which is nicely strewn with soft matting. It was obtained from magnanimous sages who knew we would come to cross the river." 8) Hearing the submission of the Supreme Godhead, Lord Ramachandra, Visvamitra had the princes and the party of sages ferried across the Ganges.

9\) They crossed to the farther shore of the river, and having paid homage to a group of rishis who awaited them, they sat on the bank and looked towards the town known as Vishala. 10) Thereafter, they proceeded with haste to that beautiful city, which vies with the heavenly planets. 11-12) Upon their arrival, Rama folded His hands and questioned Visvamitra with respect to that most excellent of cities, saying, "Please tell Me, O sage, for I am very curious to know, which royal family presently resides in Vishala?" 13) Hearing Rama's enquiry, the bull-like muni began to relate the history of the city. 14) "Kindly be attentive," he said, "while I relate what I heard with regard to Vishala, O Rama, and that which actually transpired in this country. 15-16) Once, in Satya Yuga,the powerful sons of Diti and the fortunate and righteous sons of Aditi considered thus: 'How shall we become deathless and ever youthful?' 17) While they were considering the matter, the following occurred to them: 'Let us churn the ocean of milkand then we shall get nectar!' 18) They resolved to employ Vasuki, the lord of the serpents, as the rope for churning, and Mount Mandara as the churning stick, and they proceeded to churn the milk ocean with unequalled vigour.

19\) "When they had been churning for one-thousand years, Vasuki bit the rocks of Mandara Mountain and began to vomit poison. 20) Thereafter, a fierce, fiery venom called Halahala was generated that began to consume the entire universe, along with its demigods, demons and men. 21) The demigods then approached Sankara, Lord Siva, and begged him for protection saying, 'Please save us, please save us!' 22-24) Lord Sri Vishnu, Who holds a conchshell and disc, then appeared. He smiled and said the following to Rudra, the trident-bearer: 'This poison which was previously churned up by the demigods seems to have fallen to your share. It is customary to worship some great person at the commencement of a ritual; since you are the foremost among the Devas, remain here and accept the poison as your portion of the rite.'

25-26) "Having thus spoken, Lord Vishnu, the leader of the demigods, vanished from that place. Lord Siva, perceiving the fear of the Devas and hearing the Supreme Godhead's instruction, swallowed the dreadful Halahala poison as if it were nectar. Having thus delivered the demigods, Lord Siva departed for his abode.

27\) "The demigods and demons then began to churn the ocean of milk once more, but the churning rod, Mount Mandara, sank down towards the subterranean Patala planet. 28) Together with the Gandharvas, the Devas made the following submission to Lord Sri Vishnu, the slayer of the demon Madhu: 'O Lord, You are the ultimate resort of all living beings, especially the demigods. 29) Please protect us, O Mighty-armed One; You alone are able to raise Mount Mandara!' In response to their prayers Lord Vishnu, the Supersoul of the universe, took the peak of Mount Mandara in His hand. 31-32) He then \[simultaneously\] placed Himself in the midst of the demigods and took part in the churning with them.

"Another thousand years passed as the demigods and demons churned, when Dhanvantari, the personification of the medical sciences, arose with great brilliance. He held a staff in one hand and a water-pot in the other, and He was accompanied by the Apsaras. 33) Because those uncommonly beautiful girls were generated by the churning of the milky watersthey are known as Apsaras. 34) Six-hundred million of those splendid creatures came into existence as well as innumerable maidservants. 35) The demigods and demons were unwilling to take the Apsaras as their wives and consequently they were declared to be the common property of many men.

36\) "Then Varunee, the blessed daughter of Varuna, the sea-god, was generated, and she began to seek a husband. 37) The sons of Diti, the demons, did not accept her as such, but the sons of Aditi, the demigods, immediately took her as their wife because of her irreproachable behaviour. 38) Because the demons rejected Varunee, who is also known as Sura, they are called Asuras, and because the demigods accepted her with great delight, they are known as Suras. 39) The best of horses, Uchchhaihsrava, and the jewel of jewels, Kaustubha, next rose out of the ocean, O Rama, and then nectar, the best of beverages! 40) A terrible and destructive struggle ensued between the two parties as the demons and the demigods fought over the immortality giving nectar. 41) The demons and Rakshasas combined their forces and there was a horrendous battle that bewildered the residents of the three worlds. 42) Then, when almost total destruction threatened, the mighty Lord Sri Vishnu assumed the form of a woman, Mohinee by name, and by exercising His illusory potency He was able to snatch the nectar away from the demons. 43) Those Asuras who were foolish enough to stand against the imperishable Supreme Personality of Godhead, Sri Vishnu, were ground to pieces by the mighty Lord. 44) The powerful sons of Diti were totally annihilated in this dreadful encounter between the demons and the demigods. 45) Having killed the sons of Diti, Indra acquired the heavenly kingdom, and he ruled the three worlds, with their sages and Gandharvas, with great contentment."

*Thus ends the forty-fifth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 46. The Origin of the Maruts

Diti was grief-stricken to hear of the slaying of her sons, \[the demons\] and she spoke as follows to her husband Kasyapa, the son of Mareechi: 2) 'O my husband, I am bereft of my children, for they have been killed by my powerful step-sons! I desire a son, obtained by lengthy penance, who will slay Indra, for he is responsible for their demise. 3) I am willing to perform austerities, and you are capable of begetting a son in my womb who can kill him. Please permit me to do so!'

4\) "Hearing her entreaty, the resplendent Kasyapa replied to his poor, miserable wife as follows: 5) 'Let it be so; do not grieve. Purify yourself by performing penance, and you will bear a son who is able to kill Indra! 6) If you practice austerities for a full one-thousand years you will give birth to a son who is capable of destroying him.' 7) Consoling Diti in that way, Kasyapa stroked her with his hand and, after embracing her, and having told her to be of good cheer, he left to resume his austerities.

8\) "Diti went to the hermitage known as Kusaplava, and in a cheerful mood she began to perform severe mortifications. 9) While she was observing her vow, the thousandeyed Indra waited upon her with great attentiveness. 10) He brought her fire, kusa-grass, firewood, water, fruit, vegetables and whatever else she wished for. 11) He would massage her limbs to dispel her fatigue, and he was at her beck and call at all times.

12\) "By the time all of the one-thousand years but ten had elapsed, O Rama, Diti felt herself much obliged to Indra, and she addressed him thus: 13) 'Ten years of my onethousand years of penance yet remain. Bless you! Soon you will behold your brother! 14) I will instruct the son I sought with a desire to have you overthrown, to be at peace with you. You will rule the three worlds without anxiety. 15) Your father gave me the boon that such a son would be born to me if I performed penances for one-thousand years, O best of the demigods.'

16\) "Having thus reassured Indra, Diti was overcome by sleepiness. It was noon time, and she lay down with her feet where her head should have been. 17) Seeing her lying in an impure position, with her hair at the foot of her bed and her feet at its head, Indra laughed and was very pleased. 18) He entered through her vulva, and he cut her embryo into seven parts. Each part possessed individual consciousness.

19\) "As he cut each foetus with his thunderbolt, which possessed a thousand limbs, into a further seven parts, the resultant children began to cry with shrill voices, and Diti was consequently awoken. 20) Within her womb Indra was heard to say, 'Ma ruda! Ma ruda! \[Don't cry! Don't cry!\]', but his brothers continued crying forcefully. 21) Diti for her part said: 'Don't kill them, please don't kill them!' And out of respect for his stepmother's plea, Indra preserved the children from harm. 22) With folded hands he addressed her thus: 'You are lying in an impure state with your feet where your head ought to be. 23-25) Having gained entry within your womb, I intended to slay the child that was meant to kill me, and I have cut him into seven parts, and each part into yet another seven. Please pardon me for what I have done, O goddess.' "

*Thus ends the forty-sixth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 47. An Exhaustive Account of Vishala

Because her son had been cut into forty-nine parts, Diti was most unhappy. Nevertheless, she spoke in a conciliatory manner to the thousand-eyed Indra. 2) 'O killer of the Bala demon, because of my transgression you were able to divide my child into forty-nine parts,' she said, 'but I cannot blame you for doing so. 3) I wish my children to be dear to you, therefore let them take the place of the fortynine wind gods slain in the fight over the nectar. 4) Let seven of them control the different quarters from which the winds blow in the heavenly planets, and let them have suitable celestial bodies for doing so. And because you told them "ma ruda, don't cry", let them be called the Maruts from now on.

5\) " 'Let one party reside in Brahmaloka and another in your abode, and let a third party be known as the celebrated Divyavayu, the heavenly breeze. 6) Let the other four groups of seven blow from the different quarters according to your instruction, O best of demigods, all good fortune unto you! 7-8) On account of what you said, let them be known as the Maruts.'

"Hearing Diti's submission, Indra stood before her with joined palms and replied thus: 'Whatever you have said will be, do not fear! 9-12) They will move about as you desire, endowed with celestial forms.' Having thus concluded their business in the penance grove of Kusaplava, stepmother and son returned to their respective heavenly abodes, so I have heard.

"This is the very place where Indra served Diti of yore as she performed penances, O Rama! Ikshvaku's son, the exceedingly pious Alambusha, who was generally known as Vishala, constructed the city of Vishala upon the site of Diti's hermitage. 13) Vishala's son was the mighty Hemachandra, and Hemachandra's son was called Suchandra. 14) Suchandra begot the famous Dhoomrasva, O Rama, and Dhoomrasva begot Srinjaya. 15) Srinjaya's son was the opulent Sahadeva, who was possessed of great dignity, and the righteous Kushasva was begotten by him. 16) Kushasva's son was the splendidly majestic Somadatta, and his son was the celebrated Kakutstha. 17) Kakutstha's son is the indefatigable and renowned Sumati. He presently resides in this city. 18) By the grace of Ikshvaku the kings of Vishala are long lived, magnanimous, heroic and righteous.

19\) "We shall rest here happily for one night. In the morning You will be able to see King Janaka, O best of men." 20) the reafter, the glorious King Sumati approached Visvamitra, having heard of his arrival. 21) The king worshipped the muni gorgeously, together with his teachers and his relatives. He stood before Visvamitra with joined palms, enquired after his well-being and then spoke as follows: 22) "O sage, I am fortunate and honoured that I have been able to receive you in my kingdom. Indeed, there is no one more fortunate than I!"

*Thus ends the forty-seventh canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 48. Ahalya Cursed

Having enquired after Visvamitra's well-being, and having made the various exchanges to be expected on the occasion of such a meeting, King Sumati addressed the muni as follows: 2) "These two princes appear to be demigods. They are heroic and have the gait of an elephant and a lion. Indeed, They are just like a tiger and a bull. 3) Their large eyes are like the petals of a lotus. They resemble the Asvini Kumaras,and both have the figure of beauteous youth. 4) They appear like two immortals who have come by chance from the celestial planets. How is it They have travelled here by foot, and whose sons are They, O muni? 5) They are gracing this country like a sun and a moon, and They are identical in manner, gesture, and deportment. 6) For what purpose have these best of youths, these heroes armed with excellent weapons, travelled upon so long a road? I wish to hear the facts, O sage!"

7\) Hearing his query, Visvamitra told the king all that had occurred with respect to Their residence at the Siddha Ashram and the slaying of the Rakshasas. The king was truly amazed to hear the sage's reply. 8) Having got the most exalted guests in the shape of the two sons of Dasaratha, and as They were worthy of honour, he worshipped Them according to the scriptural rule.

9\) Having been worshipped very opulently by Maharaja Sumati, Rama and Lakshmana rested for one night in his capital and journeyed to Mithila the next day. 10) Beholding the delightful city of King Janaka, the sages exclaimed: 'O, how beautiful, how beautiful', and praised Mithila with great admiration. 11) Rama descried an ashram close by Mithila that was evidently very ancient. It was set in a lovely glade and appeared to be deserted. Rama then questioned Visvamitra as follows: 12) "This ashram looks as though it lies vacant. Please tell Me, Your Holiness, who used to live here?" 13) Hearing Raghava's enquiry, the eloquent and brilliantly effulgent Visvamitra gave the following account: 14-15) "Ah! Listen, O Raghava, I will tell you all! I shall inform You whose hermitage this once was and about the great-souled Gautama's curse, uttered out of anger. Formerly, this ashram of celestial appearance, the haunt of demigods, belonged to Gautama Muni. 16) He performed austerities here, in the company of his wife, Ahalya, for many years. 17) One day, knowing the sage to be absent, Indra the thousand-eyed approached Ahalya in the guise of her husband, and spoke to her thus: 18) 'Those who are really eager for something do not wait until the proper season, O beautiful lady! I desire to have intercourse with you, O slenderwaisted one!'

19\) "Even though he resembled her husband, Ahalya perceived him to be the thousand-eyed Indra. Nevertheless, out of curiosity, the evil-minded girl felt inclined to enjoy with the king of the Devas. 20) When she had satisfied her inner longing, she spoke as follows: 'O best of demigods, I have fulfilled my desire, now leave here quickly! 21) Please protect us both in all respects from my husband, Gautama!' 22-23) 'O beautiful hips,' said he to Ahalya smilingly, 'I am extremely gratified, and I shall go as I came.' Having thus copulated with her, he made to leave the sage's cottage, and he went quickly, being anxious lest he met Gautama. At that moment, however, he saw the great sage coming in. 24) The sage was difficult for demigods and demons to withstand, for he had accumulated great power by his austerities. He was drenched in the water of the Ganges, and he shone like fire. 25) He held firewood and kusa grass in his arms, and beholding him, Indra bore a dejected look upon his face. 26) Seeing how the king of the Devas had assumed his appearance and understanding the vile deed that had been perpetrated, the righteous sage cursed him angrily, saying: 27) 'Because you have disguised yourself in this manner, being of evil intent, and because you have done what should not be done, you will be deprived of your testicles.' 28) The instant that Gautama pronounced this curse, Indra's testicles fell to the ground.

29-30) "Having cursed the king of the demigods, the sage cursed his wife, saying: 'You will dwell here for many thousands of years, subsisting on air alone, unable to eat or drink and thus performing penance \[for what you have done\]. Lying on ashes, you will remain invisible to all living beings. 31) You will dwell in this terrible grove until Ramachandra, the son of Dasaratha, comes here. At that time you will be purified of your sin! 32) By offering Him hospitality, O wicked woman, you will become free of lust and illusion. You will then regain corporeal form and will be happy in my company once more.' 33) Thus spoke the sage Gautama unto his wicked wife. He then quit this hermitage and went to perform austerities on the delightful summit of Mount Himavan, which is frequented by Siddhas and Charanas."

*Thus ends the forty-eighth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 49. The Deliverance of Ahalya

Glancing nervously, and deprived of his testicles, Indra addressed the demigods, headed by Agni, as well as the Siddhas, Gandharvas and Charanas, as follows: 2) 'I have foiled the great-souled Gautama in his attempt to become more powerful by means of his austerities. While I was acting on behalf of the Devas he became angry with me. 3) He cursed me in a rage; I lost my testicles, and he abandoned his wife as well. By my inciting him to pronounce this curse I have robbed him of vast amounts of merit. 4) As I have suffered thus on your behalf, O best of demigods, sages and Charanas, you should restore my testicles to me.'

5\) "Hearing Indra's submission, the demigods, headed by Agni, approached the manes,in the company of the Maruts. 6) 'Here is a ram', they said, 'which has testicles while Indra does not. Quickly geld the ram and give his testicles to Indra! 7) The castrated ram will afford you great pleasure. Whenever men, desiring happiness, offer you gelded rams from now on, you should bestow great and imperishable merit upon them.' 8) In response to Agni's request, the assembly of forefathers plucked out the ram's testicles and implanted them in Indra's body. 9) From that time, O Rama, the forefathers have enjoyed offerings of gelded rams and have bestowed the same fruits upon their worshippers as they would have done had whole rams been offered. 10) Indra has had the testicles of a ram ever since, O Raghava, because of the great souled Gautama's austerity and power. 11) Go to the hermitage of that pious muni, O powerful One, and deliver the fortunate Ahalya, who possesses a brilliant form."

12\) In obedience to Visvamitra's command, Rama and Lakshmana entered the ashram with the sage going before Them. 13-14) They then beheld the blessed lady, who was resplendent as a result of her austerities. Human beings were barely able to look upon her, and even demigods and demons found her difficult to behold. She seemed to have been formed after some exertion on the part of the creator, as if she were the product of an illusion. Her limbs appeared to be filled with smoke, and they shone like tongues of fire. 15) She had the appearance of a full moon wrapt in mists and clouds, or a light as bright as the sun blazing in the midst of celestial waters. 16) Because of Gautama's pronouncement, all the inhabitants of the universe found it difficult to look upon her.

As soon as Ahalya caught sight of Lord Ramachandra, however, her husband's curse expired, and she came within the range of their vision. 17) Rama and Lakshmana then grasped her feet with great joy, and Ahalya, for her part, recollecting the words of Gautama, gave Them both a fitting welcome. 18) She offered Them padya and arghya and performed whatever rites are enjoined for guests. 19) At that time the Devas began to shower flowers upon them and to sound kettledrums, while the Gandharvas and the Apsaras observed a great festival. 20) The demigods applauded Ahalya, whose body was purified by the strength of the penances performed in obedience to Gautama Muni, with the words: 'Well done, well done!' 21) The mighty Gautama \[who just then had returned\], worshipped Ramachandra according to the scriptural rule and recommenced the performance of austerities along with his wife. 22) Then, having received the highest honour from the great sage, Lord Rama proceeded the short distance to Mithila.

*Thus ends the forty-ninth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 50. The Arrival at King Janaka's Sacrificial Hall

Proceeding in north-easterly direction, Rama and Lakshmana, with Visvamitra before Them, reached King Janaka's sacrificial arena. 2) Rama and Lakshmana then spoke the following to the great sage: "Oh, how wonderful! King Janaka has made such lavish arrangements for the performance of his sacrifice. 3) There are many thousands of brahmanas here, who are residents of different lands and are Vedic scholars, O muni! 4) Encampments for sages are to be seen, crowded with hundreds of carts and carriages. Let a place be found where we may stay, O brahmana.'

5\) In response to Lord Rama's request, Visvamitra arranged for a solitary accommodation with a good water supply. 6-10) Hearing that the sage had arrived, King Janaka went quickly to greet him together with his presiding priest — whom he placed at the head of his entourage — and his assistant brahmanas. He took arghya to wash the rishi's hands and exhibiting great humility, he worshipped him according to the scriptural injunctions. Visvamitra then enquired after the king's well-being and the unobstructed progress of his sacrifice. Thereafter, he embraced the sages and the king's family priests, according to their station and with great delight. King Janaka then stood before Visvamitra with joined palms and submitted the following: 11) "Your Divine Grace, bull-like sages, please be seated!" Hearing Janaka's entreaty, the great sage, Visvamitra, sat down. 12) The king, his brahmanas and his chief priest then took their respective seats as well.

13\) Gazing upon Visvamitra, the king spoke to him thus: "Today the gods have made my sacrifice successful. 14-15) Seeing you, Your Grace, I have achieved my desire. I am very fortunate, O bull-like sage, and I am obliged that you have come, together with these munis, to my sacrifice. According to my advisors, I am initiated into the rite for a further twelve days. 16-18) Thereafter, you will be able to see the demigods assemble to receive their portions of the sacrifice, O scion of Kusa."

Having thus addressed the powerful ascetic, his countenance full of transcendental happiness, Janaka spoke eagerly once more, his hands folded before him: "These two princes have the appearance of celestial beings. They move like elephants, They are apparently very heroic, and They are respectively like a tiger and a bull. Their eyes are like lotus petals, They are armed with swords, quivers and bows, and Their juvenile beauty reminds one of the Asvini Kumaras. 19) How, and to what end have They travelled so far on foot, O sage? They are like two demigods who have come by chance from the celestial planets. 20) These valiant youths are holding first class weapons. Whose sons are They? They are decorating my kingdom like a sun and a moon. 21) They are alike in looks, gesture, manner and so on, and Their brows are beautified with locks of black hair. Who are these two heroes? I am eager to know the truth about Them!"

22\) Hearing the great-souled Janaka's query, Visvamitra made everything known about the two unequalled sons of King Dasaratha. 23) He described Their sojourn at the Siddha Ashram, the destruction of Mareecha and Subahu, Their safe arrival at Vishala and Their inspection of the city. 24) He recounted the meeting with Ahalya and with Gautama too, and how They had come to inspect the great bow at last. 25) Having made everything known to King Janaka of immense prowess, the sage Visvamitra became silent.

*Thus ends the fiftieth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 51. Shatananda's Description of Visvamitra's Lineage

When he heard the words of the wise Visvamitra, ecstatic horripilation occurred upon the person of mighty Shatananda \[King Janaka's priest\]. 2) He was Gautama's eldest son, and was resplendent on account of the mortification he had undergone. He was supremely amazed by the sight of Lord Rama. 3) Gazing upon the two princes, Who sat comfortably close by, he addressed the foremost saint, Visvamitra, as follows: 4) "O great sage, tell me, did my illustrious mother look upon Rama at the completion of her long penance? 5) Did my famed mother offer Rama, Who is worshippable by all embodied souls, whatever wild fruits and berries were at her disposal? 6) Did you tell Rama the history of the outrage perpetrated against my mother by Indra? 7) O Kausika, glory unto you; was my father, who is my preceptor, reunited with my mother after she met Rama? 8) Was Rama worshipped by my father and did you come thence once He had received fitting hospitality? 9) When Rama was received with honour by my father, did He offer respects to him with a peaceful mind before coming here, O sage?"

10\) Hearkening to his enquiry, Visvamitra Muni, who was a learned orator, replied thus to that accomplished speaker: 11) "I have overlooked nothing that should have been done, O best of munis. Sage Gautama was reunited with his wife like Bhargava with Renuka."12) Hearing his reply, Shatananda addressed Lord Rama as follows: 13) "Welcome, O Best of men: it is by the will of Providence that You have come here with the unconquerable sage, Visvamitra, before You! 14) He has performed inconceivable feats, and has attained the unequalled power of a brahmarshi by dint of the penance he has performed. I know the mighty Visvamitra to be the highest resort of those who take shelter of him. 15) There is none more fortunate than You, O Rama, in all the world, because You are under the protection of one who has performed terrible austerities. 16) Listen, and I shall describe the high-minded Visvamitra's power and also the reality of him. Hearken as I tell you!

17\) "This sage was for a long time a pious-minded king. He was the subduer of his enemies, he knew the religious principles and was devoted to the interests of his subjects. 18) Brahma had a son called Kusa, a great king he was, and his son was the righteous Kusanabha, who was possessed of great strength. 19) Kusanabha's son was the celebrated Gadhi, and Gadhi's son is the powerful Visvamitra Muni.

20\) "Visvamitra ruled over his kingdom and protected his citizens for many thousands of years. 21) Once, having mustered an army, and accompanied by a military force of one akshauhini,Visvamitra toured the world. 22-23) He gradually passed through villages, capital cities, rivers, mountain ranges and hermitages, until at length he came to the ashram of Vasishta. It was surrounded by various kinds of flowers, creepers and trees. It was crowded with deer and other beasts, and was frequented by Siddhas and Charanas. 24) It was made splendid by demigods, demons, Gandharvas and Kinnaras and by peaceful deer, and it was the meeting place of twice-born men. 25-26) There were brahma-sages scattered here and there, as well as deva-sages who would come and visit them. It thronged with great personalities who were performing austerities and, having achieved the various mystic perfections, were refulgent like fire. The great-souled sages were the equals of Brahma, and many of them lived on water alone, and fasted from all solid foodstuffs, while some lived on air alone, and others on the leaves that fell from trees. 27-28) Others fasted from grains and ate only fruits and roots. They had all conquered passion and had subdued their senses. The entire hermitage was resplendent with rishis, Valikhilyasand vanaprasthas,who had all devoted themselves to the chanting of the holy names of God and the offering of oblations in the sacred fire. Such was the ashram of Vasishta Muni, which the best of the victorious, King Visvamitra, beheld on that occasion."

*Thus ends the fifty-first canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 52. Vasishta Receives Visvamitra as His Guest

Beholding Vasishta, the foremost of those who chant the Names of God, with supreme delight, the mighty Visvamitra bowed down with humility before him. 2) 'You are welcome!' Said the great-souled Vasishta, and indicated that he should be seated, it is said. 3) When he had sat down, the sage brought him a repast of fruits and vegetables according to the rules of etiquette. 4-5) Having thus been honoured by Vasishta, the king enquired whether the sage's austerities and performance of fire sacrifice were proceeding satisfactorily, and also after the health of his disciples. He even asked whether the trees and plants were faring well. Vasishta assured him that everything was as it should be.

6\) "When the king was comfortably seated, Vasishta, who was wont to mutter the holy names of God, questioned Visvamitra as follows: 7) 'I hope that all is well with you, O king, and that you are eager to follow the religious principles. Are you protecting your subjects according to the codes of regal conduct? 8) I hope your servants are well cared for and that they are obedient to your commands. Have you managed to defeat all your enemies, O valiant one? 9) Is your treasury full and are your friends reliable; how are your sons and your grandsons, O sinless one?

10\) "The brilliantly effulgent Visvamitra, who was endowed with great nobility, informed Vasishta that all was well with him. 11) They conversed with each other for a good while, and they felt extremely happy in each other's company. 12) Thereafter, when they had ceased conversing, Vasishta, being in a jolly mood, said the following to Visvamitra: 13) 'Please allow me to entertain your military force, as well as your unequalled self, in a befitting fashion. 14) Allow me the honour of offering you hospitality, O king, for you are the most honourable and worshippable guest!'

15-17) "Being thus addressed by Vasishta, Visvamitra replied as follows: 'You have already received me in your ashram with sweet words, with padya, with achamanaand by the audience of an unalloyed devotee of Vishnu such as yourself, O intelligent one. I have been well received in every respect. All glories unto you! I shall now depart. Please regard me with a friendly eye.' 18) Hearing the king's reply, the sagacious Vasishta invited him again, and when the king demured he asked him yet again and again. 19) Visvamitra, the son of Gadhi, eventually replied, 'Very well! As it pleases you, then let it be so, O sage.'

20\) "Being thus addressed by the mighty sovereign, Vasishta called for his desire-fulfilling cow. Her hide was spotted, and she was spotlessly clean. 21) 'Shabala, come here!' he cried, 'Come quickly and hear what I have to say! I have undertaken to feed this king's army very sumptuously. Please attend to their needs for me. 22) Shower upon his soldiers, each one according to his preference, whatever kind of food they desire, O wish-fulfilling cow, of any of the six kinds of tastes.23) Be quick, Shabala, and provide them with heaps of savoury foods, sweetmeats, drinks, foods that can be licked and foods that can be sucked!' "

*Thus ends the fifty-second canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 53. Visvamitra asks for the Wish-fulfilling Cow

Being thus commanded by Vasishta, Shabala began to yield whatever edibles the king's troops desired. 2) She produced sugar cane juice, sugar and rock candy, different types of honey, fried rice, different varieties of liquor, various non-alcoholic drinks and costly dishes of many kinds. 3) She provided mountainous heaps of hot, high quality rice, different varieties of broth, and rivers of yoghurt. 4) She produced brimming jugs of delicious fruit juices and thousands of dishes piled high with sweets. 5) Every man in Visvamitra's army was completely satiated, and thus his military force consisted of happy, satisfied men.

6-7) "Visvamitra, who was a king-sage, was delighted by the repast, as were his favourite wives, his family priests, his ministers, his counsellors and his servants \[who all accompanied him on his tour\]. Supremely gratified, he addressed Vasishta thus: 8-9) 'O brahmana, I have been exceedingly well honoured by you, who are yourself worthy of honour! Hearken while I make this request, O eloquent one. Give me Shabala in exchange for one-hundred thousand cows! She is a precious jewel, and such a gem should be surrendered to the king. 10-12) On that account give Shabala to me, O sage, for that would be the rightful thing to do.'

"Being thus addressed by the righteous Visvamitra, Vasishta, a bull among sages, replied as follows: 'I shall not part with Shabala for one-hundred thousand cows, nor even for one-hundred million, O king, nor yet for stacks of silver. She ought not to be parted from me, O chastiser of enemies! 13) Shabala is by rights eternally mine, just as fame is the property of the self-realised. From her I obtain the ingredients for fire-sacrificeand for the worship of the forefathers. Indeed, she is my only means of subsistence. 14) I depend on her for the ingredients of the bali offering,for ghee for the homa sacrifice,and for the different ingredients required in the rites that call for the chanting of 'svaha' or 'vashat'. 15) I am dependent upon her in every respect, 0 king, of that there can be no doubt. In truth she is my pride and joy. 16-20) For many reasons I am quite unable to give Shabala to you.' "Being thus addressed by Vasishta, the eloquent Visvamitra spoke with great agitation, as follows: 'I will give you fourteen-thousand elephants decorated with golden girths, chains and goads; eight-hundred golden chariots each pulled by four white steeds adorned with little bells. I shall give you eleven-hundred finely bred, spirited horses, O sage. I will give you ten-million full-grown cows of a variety of colours if you will part with Shabala. 21) Whatever jewels or gold you may desire I shall give you: let Shabala be mine!'

22\) "Thus spoke the intelligent Visvamitra, the blessed Vasishta replying as follows: 'I shall not give you Shabala under any circumstances, O king! 23) Verily, she is my jewels and my wealth. She is everything to me, and she is the very means of my subsistence. 24) She is the sacrifice performed on the day of the new moon and that performed on the full moon day; she is the fees paid at the conclusion of sacrifices. She is, as it were, a variety of religious performances. 25) Without doubt, she is the foundation of all my religious activities. What is the use of beating about the bush: I will not give you this desire-fulfilling cow!' "

*Thus ends the fifty-third canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 54. The Wish-fulfilling Cow Carried Off

When Vasishta Muni would not part with his desire cow, Visvamitra began to drag her off forcibly. 2) As Shabala was led off by the king, she felt distressed, and being tormented with grief she wept and thought the following: 3) 'Have I been abandoned by the exalted Vasishta that I must be carried off by the king's wretched servants, greatly aggrieved as I am? 4) What wrong have I done that great sage of pure heart and mind that he disavows me, though he has witnessed my innocence and my devotion to him?' 5-6) Thinking thus, she sighed deeply again and again. Thereafter, having shaken off hundreds of the king's men, she ran towards the feet of the mighty Vasishta with the speed of the wind.

7\) "Lowing and weeping, she stood before him, and she addressed him with a voice like the rumbling of thunder. 8) 'O son of Brahma, am I abandoned by you to be led away by the king's slaves in your very presence?' 9-10) Vasishta replied to Shabala, whose heart was inflamed with sorrow, as if she were his sister, saying, 'I am not abandoning you, O Shabala, and neither have you done me any wrong. This king, being drunk with power, is leading you away by force. 11) My strength is no match for his, especially today when he is my guest. Not only is he a kshatriya, but other kings pay homage to him. 12) He has an unequalled army comprising one akshauhini of elephants, horses, chariots and ensign elephants, and for that reason his might surpasses mine!'

13\) "Being thus addressed by Vasishta, whose splendour was unmatched, Shabala spoke with humility, as follows: 14) 'Is it not said that a brahmana is more powerful than a kshatriya? The power of a brahmana is spiritual and is thus superior to that of a warrior! 15) There is no army equal to or mightier than you; Visvamitra cannot stand in the face of your might. 16) O powerful one, please command me, and infused with spiritual strength, I shall destroy the evil wretch's army and his plans!' 17) Being thus addressed by Shabala, Vasishta spoke as follows, saying: 'Create an army capable of annihilating Visvamitra's military force!'

18-21) "Hearing his command, the wish-fulfilling cow produced hundreds of fierce Pahlavas.Created by her bellowing, O king, they destroyed Visvamitra's entire army before his very eyes. He became supremely angry, and with eyes wide with fury he in turn destroyed them with a variety of weapons. Beholding Visvamitra slaying Pahlavas by their hundreds, Shabala created horrible Shakasand Yavanas,who covered the earth with their numbers. 22) They were powerful and extremely heroic, and resembled the golden filaments of a lotus. Armed with sharp tridents, they wore golden coloured garments. 23) Visvamitra, however, confounded those Yavanas, Kambojasand other barbarians with powerful missiles, and their forces were consumed as if by a blazing fire."

*Thus ends the fifty-fourth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 55. Visvamitra Obtains the Knowledge of Certain Weapons

Shatananda continued: "Perceiving the barbaric warriors to be in disarray, having been bewildered by Visvamitra's weapons, Vasishta commanded his desire cow to create more soldiers by means of her yogic power. 2) From her bellowing, Kambojas as brilliant as the sun were generated, and from her udder, other barbarians with weapons in their hands. 3) From the region of her genital came Yavanas and from her dung Shakas. From the pores of her skin came mlechchhas, Haritas and Kiratas. 4) In a single moment, O Raghava, they slew all of Visvamitra's elephants, foot soldiers, horses and charioteers.5-6) Seeing the destruction of Visvamitra's army, the one-hundred sons of the king rushed towards Vasishta very angrily, with various weapons in their hands, but merely by uttering a sound of disgust the great sage burnt them all to death! 7) They were reduced to ashes in a second, together with their horses, chariots and foot soldiers, by the great-souled Vasishta.

8\) "Seeing his entire military force annihilated, the illustrious Visvamitra was filled with shame. 9) He resembled an ocean bereft of its fury or a serpent with broken fangs. He suddenly lost his bodily lustre, like the sun obscured by an eclipse. 10) With his sons and army killed, he looked as miserable as a bird with severed wings. Deprived of his strength and enthusiasm, he became completely indifferent to his rank, kingdom and so forth. 11) He handed over his realm to his one surviving son and commanded him: 'Rule according to the religious principles enjoined for kings', and he departed for the forest. 12) He went to a slope of the Himalayas frequented by Kinnaras and serpents, and he began to perform penance in order to get the grace of Lord Siva.

13\) "After some time had elapsed, Lord Siva, whose pennant is emblazoned with a bull, revealed himself to the great sage. 14) 'Tell me, O king,' he said, 'what is the purpose of your austerities? What do you desire from me? I am a bestower of benedictions: tell me what you want!' 15) Being thus addressed by that great demigod, Visvamitra fell prostrate before him and spoke to him as follows: 16) 'If you are satisfied with me, O Mahadeva, impart unto me the Dhanurveda along with its various limbs, supplementary philosophical portions and secret formulae. 17) Please reveal to me whatever weapons are known to the demigods, demons, great sages, Gandharvas, Yakshas and Rakshasas. 18) May whatever I desire come to pass by your grace, O lord of demigods!' Lord Siva replied, 'Let it be so!' and having thus spoken left that place.

19-20) "Having received those weapons from Lord Siva, the mighty Visvamitra, though already very arrogant, became utterly swollen with pride, and his impudence increased like the ocean tide when the moon is full. He thought Vasishta, the best of sages, was as good as dead. 21) He therefore proceeded to Vasishta's hermitage and began to discharge the weapons. The grove in which the ashram was set was soon ablaze due to the fierce heat of the missiles. 22-23) Seeing Visvamitra muttering the mantras which controlled the various weapons, hundreds of alarmed sages and disciples of Vasishta, as well as terrified birds and beasts in their thousands, began to flee from the hermitage.

24\) "Within minutes Vasishta's ashram became desolate; not a sound was to be heard, and it resembled a desert. 25) For his part, Vasishta called out to the residents of the hermitage again and again. 'Do not be afraid', he said, 'today I shall destroy Visvamitra just as the sun vanquishes mist!' 26) Having thus reassured the members of his community, Vasishta spoke angrily to Visvamitra, saying: 27) 'This ashram flourished for a long time, and now you have destroyed it. Because you are so foolish you will shortly cease to be!' 28) Having thus rebuked the king, that best of God-conscious men raised his staff in a wrathful mood. He resembled the smokeless conflagration at the time of the universal dissolution, and he was like a second Yamaraja, the lord of the dead!"

*Thus ends the fifty-fifth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 56. Brahminical Power is Unsurpassable

Being thus addressed by Vasishta, King Visvamitra took aim with the missile presided over by Agni and cried: 'Stand where you are, stand, \[I say\]!' 2-3) Raising his brahminical staff, which resembled the rod of death-personified, Vasishta replied angrily, saying, 'O sham warrior, here I stand, show me your strength, such as it is! Today I will vanquish your pride as well as your impudent missile, O son of Gadhi. 4) What is your regal might in comparison to that of a brahmana? Behold the spiritual power of a brahmana, O disgrace of the warrior caste!'

5\) "So saying, Vasishta extinguished the terrible fiery weapon with his staff, just as one quenches blazing fire with water. 6) Thereafter, Visvamitra angrily discharged the missiles presided over by Varuna, Lord Siva and Indra, as well as that known as Aiseeka. 7) He released the weapon presided over by Manu, called the Bewilderer, the weapon of the Gandharvas — which induces sleep, the missile which induces yawning, the intoxicating weapon, the scorching weapon and the missile that induces lamentation. 8) He released the cruel Parching Weapon, the invincible Thunderbolt Weapon, the Brahmana-noose, the Death-fetter and the noose of Varuna.

9-10) "He discharged the trident weapon beloved of Lord Siva, two more thunderbolt weapons — one dry and one wet, the Rod Weapon, the witches' missile, the Heron, the Dharma-discus, the Time-discus, Lord Vishnu's discus, the weapon presided over by the wind-god, the Churning Weapon and the Horse-head Weapon. 11) He fired a pair of Lance Weapons, the Skeleton Weapon, the Club, the missile presided over by the Vidyadharas,the Mahastram \[the Great Weapon\], and the pitiless Time Weapon. 12-13) He discharged the horrible Trident, the Skull Weapon, and the weapon known as Kankana, O Raghava. He discharged all these weapons at Vasishta, who ever chants the Holy Names of God, and the wonder of it all was that the sage neutralised every one of them with his brahminical staff.

14-15) "Upon those weapons being extinguished, Visvamitra prepared to loose the Brahmastra.Seeing that dreadful missile ready in the king's hand, the Devas headed by Agni, the deva-rishis, the Gandharvas and the serpents became confused. The inhabitants of the three worlds were terrified, as Visvamitra discharged the Brahmastra. Vasishta, however, by dint of his brahminical power, was easily able to neutralise that horrible weapon with his staff, O Rama!

17\) "As the great soul, Vasishta, extinguished the Brahmastra, he assumed a fierce and utterly pitiless aspect which stupefied the three worlds. 18) There shot from every pore of his body, as if they were the sun's rays, innumerable flames swathed in smoke. The rod Vasishta raised aloft in his hand blazed like the smokeless fire at the time of the universal conflagration, and it resembled the staff of the god of death. 20) The host of sages then began to propitiate Vasishta, who ever chants the Names of God, as follows: 'O brahmana, your might is unfailing, please restrain your power by means of that power! 21) You have curbed the immense strength of Visvamitra, O brahmana. Your might is unfailing, O best of men! Let the inhabitants of the universe be rid of their anxiety!'

22\) "Hearing the submission of the sages, Vasishta composed himself, while Visvamitra, his pride shattered, and hissing like a serpent, spoke thus: 'Curse the might of kshatriyas; the might of brahmanas alone is might. Vasishta has vanquished all my weapons with a single brahmana's staff! 24) I now acknowledge that brahminical power is unsurpassable. With my senses and my mind subdued, I shall undertake severe penances so that I may attain to brahmanhood!' "

*Thus ends the fifty-sixth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 57. King Trishanku Begs for a Sacrifice to be Performed

Thereafter, his heart burning as he recalled his discomfiture by the great soul he had made his foe, and hissing again and again, Visvamitra headed in a southerly direction with his chief wife, O Raghava. There he engaged in terrible austerities. 3) He subsisted upon wild fruits and roots, and he performed the severest penances with restrained senses. At that time he sired four truthful and righteous sons. 4-5) Their names were Havishpanda, Madhushpanda, Dridhanetra and Maharatha.

"When one thousand years had elapsed, Brahma, the grandfather of the universe, addressed Visvamitra with the following sweet words: 'O scion of Kusa, the planets of the king-sages have been won by your austerity. 6-7) Because of the penance you have undergone, we declare you to be a rajarshi, a king-sage!' Having thus spoken, the mighty lord departed with the demigods for Brahmaloka. Hearing that pronouncement with some shame, Visvamitra bore a miserable expression on his face. 8-12) Filled with great sorrow he spoke dejectedly, saying, 'I underwent the most terrible austerities, but the hosts of sages and demigods only recognise me as a rajarshi. I do not feel that a sufficient reward for my penances!' Pondering thus within his heart, the great-souled Visvamitra performed even greater austerities, O Rama.

"At that time there was a king in the dynasty of Ikshvaku celebrated as Trishanku. He was truthful and had conquered his senses. It occurred to him that he should perform a sacrifice so as to ascend to the heavenly planets in his mortal body. He therefore summoned Vasishta and disclosed his intention to him. 13) Vasishta merely said: 'I am unable to do such a thing!' and being thus spurned by the sage he departed \[from his kingdom and headed\] southward. 14) In order to fulfil his purpose, King Trishanku went to the place where the sons of Vasishta had been performing lengthy penances.

15\) "There he beheld the one-thousand powerful and intensely effulgent sons of Vasishta undergoing austerities. 16) He approached the worshippable sons of his guru, and having previously been rejected, he bowed his head with shame. 17) He stood before them with joined palms and spoke as follows: 'I take shelter of you all, who those needy of aid approach for refuge. 18) All glories unto you! I have been spurned by the great-souled Vasishta but I desire to perform a great sacrifice and you are able to authorise it. 19) I offer my obeisance unto all the sons of my guru and beg, with my head bowed before you, who are brahmanas engaged in the performance of austerities, that you bestow your mercy upon me! 20) If, sirs, you perform the sacrifice for the fulfillment of my purpose, then I will attain the abode of the demigods in this very body. 21) Having been rejected by Vasishta I see no other resort but the austere sons of my guru. 22) The family priest is the highest resort of all the Ikshvakus. After him you are my worshippable lords!' "

*Thus ends the fifty-seventh canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 58. Trishanku Cursed

Hearing Trishanku's request, the one-thousand sons of Vasishta became extremely angry and replied to the king as follows: 2) 'You have been spurned by your spiritual master, O evil-minded one. Why do you approach another authority, disregarding him? 3) The family priest is the highest resort of all the Ikshvakus; it is not proper to disdain the instructions of a truthful sage. 4) His Divine Grace said: 'I am not able to perform such a sacrifice', and we are therefore unable to execute such a rite under any circumstance. 5) You are simply childish, O king, and should return to your capital. Vasishta is capable of performing the sacrifices of the entire universe. 6-9) How, then, could we be disrespectful towards him!'

"Hearing their stern admonition the king addressed them yet again: 'I have been spurned both by my guru and my guru's sons but I shall seek shelter elsewhere. May all good fortune betide you!' On hearing the king's abominable proposal, the sons of Sage Vasishta cursed him thus: 'May you become a chandala!' and they each entered his own cottage.

10\) "With the passing of the night, Trishanku's body was transformed into that of a chandala. He found himself wearing coarse black cloth, his body had turned black and his hair had all fallen out! 11-17) Wearing a garland of crematorium flowers, he was anointed with the ashes of cremated corpses, and his ornaments had turned to iron. Seeing him with the body of chandala, his ministers and other subjects that had accompanied him immediately abandoned him and ran away in fright. He then proceeded all alone, with great fortitude, despite there being a burning within his heart day and night, to the powerful ascetic, Visvamitra.

"Seeing the king greatly disappointed and with the body of a dog-eater, O Rama, Visvamitra became compassionate upon him. Out of pity, the mighty sage addressed the repulsive looking sovereign thus: 'God bless you, mighty prince! What is the purpose of your coming here, O king of Ayodhya, cursed with the body of a chandala?' Hearing that enquiry, the king, now transformed into a dog-eater, stood with folded hands and replied with sweet words: 'I have been rejected by both my guru and my guru's sons. 18-20) I have not attained my desire, indeed, the contrary is the case. I intended to ascend bodily to the celestial planets, and onehundred sacrifices were to have been performed to that end by me. I have not attained my wish! I have never told an untruth in my life and shall never do so in the future, even if I am put in great difficulty. I swear by the office of kings that this is so! I have worshipped Lord Vishnu with the performance of a variety of sacrifice, and I have governed my subjects righteously. 21) I have satisfied both my preceptors and other great souls with my virtuous conduct, yet none of them wanted to undertake this sacrifice on my behalf. 22) Indeed, my gurus were displeased with me, O mighty sage. I think that Providence alone is supreme and that human endeavour is of no avail. 23) Everything is subject to the will of Providence, and Providence is the supreme resort! All glory unto you! You are able to aid me. Be merciful on me, who am desirous of your grace, having been dealt a cruel blow by destiny. 24) There is nowhere else I can go. I have no other shelter but you. You alone can act to avert my ill fortune!' "

*Thus ends the fifty-eighth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 59. The Sons of Vasishta Cursed

Visvamitra, being compassionate upon the king, who had factually become a chandala, replied with the following sweet words: 2) 'My dear fellow, O descendant of Ikshvaku, welcome! I know you are extremely pious. I shall give you shelter, do not fear! 3) I shall summon maha-rishisimbued with spiritual merit, who will assist in the execution of the sacrifice, and you will conclude the rite without difficulty. 4) You will ascend to the celestial planets in the body inflicted upon you by the curse of your guru. 5) I consider you to have already attained your heavenly abode, O king, for you have taken shelter of Visvamitra, who is able to give you refuge.

6\) "Having thus spoken, the sage instructed his righteous sons to prepare for the sacrifice. 7-9) Summoning all his disciples, he commanded them as follows: 'Bring every sage in the world and the sons of Vasishta too, and inform them they are commanded by me to attend along with their most learned students, friends and officiating priests. If anyone who hears of my summons utters anything disrespectful, then whatever they have said should be repeated to me in its entirety'. Hearing Visvamitra's decree, his followers set out in all directions, according to his command.

10\) "Thereafter, from every country of the world came brahmanas and their disciples, and they approached the sage, who was resplendent like fire. 11-14) Visvamitra's disciples informed him of everything uttered by those approached, saying, 'All the twice-born brahmanas who heard your summons are presently arriving, and have come from every nation of the earth, with the exception of Mahodaya and the one-hundred sons of Vasishta. Please listen while we repeat their angry words, O bull-like sage. The sons of Vasishta said: "How do you expect the hosts of demigods and sages to partake of the offerings of a kshatriya who has been reduced to the status of a dog-eater; and how, for that matter, can brahmanas be expected to eat his remnants? 15-18) How on earth will they attain the heavenly kingdom under the patronage of Visvamitra?" Thus spoke the sons of Vasishta, along with Mahodaya, O sage, their eyes reddened with anger.'

"Visvamitra heard all they reported, and becoming wrathful, his eyes red with fury, spoke as follows: 'Let scoundrels who repudiate me, though I perform terrible austerities, be reduced to ashes forthwith! Today they will be led by the noose of time to the abode of Yamawithout any doubt. 19-20) They will be born for seven hundred successive lives as callous mritapastand will subsist on the flesh of dogs, performing abominable activities and wandering over the face of the earth in grotesque bodies. And as for Mahodaya, he spoke ill of me although I am beyond reproach. 21-22) He will become a Nishadaand will be censured wherever he roams. Acquiring a merciless disposition, he will resort to spiteful acts and will live in miserable circumstances for many years.'

"Having thus pronounced his curse in the assembly of sages, the great sage, Visvamitra Muni, fell silent.

*Thus ends the fifty-ninth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 60. Trishanku's Ascension

When Visvamitra understood that the sons of Vasishta and Mahodaya had perished by the strength of his curse, he spoke in the midst of the powerful sages as follows: 2) 'This descendant of Ikshvaku, who is celebrated by the name Trishanku, is pious and truthful, and he has taken refuge of me. 3-5) He wishes to ascend to the abode of the Devas in his present body. So that he may have his desire fulfilled, I shall make preparations for an appropriate sacrifice along with your good selves.'

"Hearing the words of Visvamitra in their entirety, the great sages briefly conferred amongst themselves in conformity with the tenets of religion. 'This Visvamitra,' they said, 'is of a very volatile disposition. 6) Without a doubt we must conform with his proposal, for otherwise the great sage, who resembles fire, will become enraged and will pronounce a curse on us. 7) Therefore, let us commence a rite that will enable Trishanku to ascend to the celestial planets in his mortal body by dint of Visvamitra's personal power. 8) Let us begin the sacrifice, and may everyone be attentive to their duties!' Having thus spoken, the great sages jointly commenced the yajna.

9\) "In that rite Visvamitra was the presiding priest, and his assistants did everything in due order and with the appropriate Vedic hymns. 10-11) Every aspect of the sacrifice was attended to according to the scriptural rule. Thereafter, for a considerable period of time, Visvamitra called the demigods to partake of their portions of the sacrifice. The demigods, however, did not appear to take their shares. 12) Visvamitra became filled with rage, and raising aloft the sacrificial ladle spoke angrily to Trishanku, saying: 13) 'Behold the might of my asceticism, O best of men. I hereby send you to the celestial planets by my own power! 14-18) Go to the heavenly sphere, which is difficult of attainment in one's earthly body. If any merit has accrued to me by dint of the penance I have performed, then go forthwith to the heavenly planets in your present body!' As soon as the sage made that pronouncement, the king ascended heavenward in the vision of the assembled sages. When Indra, together with the host of Devas, noted Trishanku's ascension, they called out to him thus: 'Be gone, Trishanku! You cannot make your residence in heaven, afflicted as you are by your guru's curse. You idiot, fall headlong to earth again!'

"Being thus commanded by Indra, Trishanku began to fall earthward. 19-21) As he fell he cried out to the austere Visvamitra, saying, 'Save me, help!' Hearing his cries the sage was transported with rage, and he bellowed: 'Stop! Stay where you are!' In the midst of the assembly of sages, the powerful Visvamitra then began to create in great anger, as if he were a second Brahma, a duplicate Seven Rishis constellation in the southern sector of the firmament, and a new system of lunar mansions.22) Seated in the midst of the assembly of sages, he created a group of celestial planets in the southern sky in an intensely angry mood. 23) I shall create a second Indra,' he said, 'or let my creation be without an Indra!' and he generated various demigods and furnished the new worlds with residents. 24) The inhabitants of the universe: the sages, demigods and demons, were extremely bewildered by all this, and they therefore courteously petitioned the great-souled Visvamitra. 25) 'This king has been inflicted with the curse of his spiritual master', they said. 'He ought not to enter the celestial planets in his present body, O greatly-fortunate one!' 26) Hearing their submission, the bull-like sage replied in a great voice as follows: 27-28) 'I assured King Trishanku that his ascension, in this very body, would be successful, and I am not able to rescind my promise. Let Trishanku reside perpetually in the heavens and let the constellations I have generated remain permanently as well. 29) Let all of these worlds I have generated stay in their orbits and be maintained for as long as the universe is manifest. You are all able to authorise this.'

30\) "Being thus addressed, the host of Devas replied to that bull among sages, saying, 'Let it be so, all glory unto you! Let all these creations remain as they are. 31-32) These manifold constellations will stay in the firmament beyond the orbit of the sun, and Trishanku will float, head downwards, in their midst as a blazing luminary. These planets will follow the same orbit as the great king \[as he traverses the sky\]. 33) He will be regarded as one whose desire was fulfilled and who ascended to the heavenly world!' Visvamitra was thus panegyrised by the demigods, and in the midst of the assembly of sages he agreed to their proposal. 34) Then, at the conclusion of the sacrifice, the Devas and the sages, whose austerity was their wealth, all left for their respective abodes, O Rama."

*Thus ends the sixtieth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 61. The History of Sunashepa

Observing the preparations of the sages to depart, Visvamitra spoke to them as follows: 2) A great obstacle to my progress has now arisen in the south; I shall hasten in another direction, and there I will perform further austerities. 3) I shall happily perform penance in the great western region, in the delightful groves on the banks of Lake Pushkara.' 4) Having thus announced his intention regarding Lake Pushkara, the great sage went there and engaged himself in cruel asceticism, for many years subsisting on roots and wilds fruits alone.

5\) "In those days there was a great king of the realm of Ayodhya known as Ambareesha, and it is well known that he undertook to perform a horse sacrifice. 6) While he was performing the rite, Indra purloined the sacrificial animal. Seeing the creature had vanished, the presiding priest spoke thus to the king: 7) 'The horse has been stolen as a result of your negligence, O king! The monarch who fails to protect the sacrificial animal is vanquished by the evil reaction to his carelessness. 8) Go quickly before the rite is concluded and retrieve the creature, or else find a human being willing to give his life in atonement for the loss!'

9-11) "Hearing the instruction of his guru, the intelligent King Ambareesha went to search for the missing animal, taking one-thousand cows with him. He journeyed through many lands with their several races and cultures. He passed through villages and forests and sacred hermitages, until, O Raghava, he descried the sage Richeeka atop Mount Bhrigutunga, seated with his sons and his wife. 12) He offered obeisance to the rishi, and sitting himself down, Maharaja Ambareesha, who shone with the unequalled splendour of a king-sage, enquired after his well-being. 13-14) Having quizzed him in all respects, the king spoke thus to Richeeka: 'If you let me have one of your sons in exchange for one-hundred thousand cows, O greatly fortunate one, so that he may be substituted for the missing sacrificial animal, then I will have done my duty. I have journeyed the world over but have not retrieved the beast. 15) Please give me one of your sons for the price of these cows.'

"Richeeka replied as following: 16-18) 'I am not prepared to barter my eldest son under any circumstances, O best of men!' Hearing the words of Richeeka, the mother of those great-souled boys spoke thus to the king: 'My lord and husband has declared his unwillingness to exchange our eldest son; our youngest son, Sunaka, is my favourite, I will not part with him, O king! 19) As a rule the eldest son is dearest to the father and the youngest son to the mother. Therefore, I will not part with him, O king!' 20-21) Thus spoke the sage and his spouse. The middle son, Sunashepa, spoke for himself, O Rama, saying, 'Father says he cannot sell my elder brother, and mother says she cannot sell my younger brother. Being the middle son, it seems you must take me, O prince!'

22-23) "King Ambareesha then concluded his transaction with the sage by bestowing upon him millions of pieces of gold and silver, heaps of jewels and the one-hundred thousand cows. He then took possession of Sunashepa with great satisfaction and left for home, O Raghava. 24) King Ambareesha had Sunashepa mount his chariot, and he sped towards his capital."

*Thus ends the sixty-first canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 62. The Completion of King Ambareesha's Sacrifice

Having procured Sunashepa, the illustrious King Ambareesha paused to rest by the side of Lake Pushkara at noontime. 2-4) While the king rested, Sunashepa went to see Visvamitra, his maternal uncle \[who was residing there\], and who was performing austerities along with others. With a pitiable expression upon his face, thirsty and fatigued, he fell down near the sage and spoke as follows: 'I have neither father nor mother, where then am I to find kinsmen? 5) Good sir, you are able to save me, as is proper, for you are the deliverer of everyone and the promoter of everyone's welfare! 6) Let the king conclude his sacrifice and let me live a long life, perform penance and ultimately attain the heavenly kingdom. 7) I do not have any refuge; be so gracious as to become my protector, O righteous one. You alone ought to deliver me from the reaction to my past sins, as a father redeems his son!'

8\) "Hearing this petition, the greatly austere Visvamitra consoled the boy in many ways and addressed his sons thus: 9) 'Fathers beget sons to ensure auspiciousness in the future, and the time has come to do something that will profit us in the life hereafter. 10) This boy, a sage's child, desires my protection. Please sacrifice your lives for his sake, my sons. 11) You are all performers of pious deeds and are the last word in religiosity. Take the place of the sacrificial animal and thereby satisfy the god of fire, Agni. 12) Sunashepa will thus have a protector, and the king's sacrifice will go on unimpeded. The demigods will be satisfied, and my promise to him will be fulfilled.'

13\) "Hearing the sage's proposal, his sons, headed by Madhuchanda, replied with haughty insolence. 14) 'How is it, father, that you are prepared to abandon your own sons,' he said, 'and give protection to someone else's son? We think this is as wicked as putting the flesh of dogs in someone's food.'

15\) "Hearing his son's retort, the bull-like sage's eyes became reddened with anger, and he began to speak thus: 16) 'Your fearless criticism of my just command is both cruel and shocking! 17) You will all be born, like the sons of Vasishta, as dog-eaters, and you will wander the earth for a full one-thousand years!'

18\) "Having thus pronounced a curse upon his sons, that best of sages addressed the unhappy Sunashepa, convincing him he would be protected. 19) 'Having been bound with clean ropes, somehow reach Lord Vishnu's post. Wearing a red garland and besmeared with scarlet powder, invoke Agni with the prayers I shall teach you! 20) Here are two hymns for you to recite, O sage's son, so that you will escape death at King Ambareesha's sacrifice.'

21\) "Sunashepa memorized the two prayers with intense concentration and returning quickly to the king addressed him as follows: 22) 'O lion-like king, O intelligent one, let us make haste to the sacrificial arena, and you can begin the initiatory rites.' 23) Hearing Sunashepa's submission, the king hastened to the sacrificial enclosure with great enthusiasm. 24) With the consent of the superintending priest, Maharaja Ambareesha had the boy marked with a pure mineral, dressed in red attire and bound to the sacrificial pillar. 25) Though secured to the post, Sunashepa recited the two hymns in glorification of Agni, Indra, and Indra's younger brother, Lord Vamana, as he had been taught by Visvamitra. 26) Thereafter, being satisfied by those secret prayers, Indra the thousand-eyed benedicted Sunashepa with a long life. 27) The king, O best of men, obtained the manifold fruits of a completed sacrifice by the grace of that god. 28) The righteous Visvamitra, for his part, performed very stringent austerities on the bank of Lake Pushkara for a further one-thousand years."

*Thus ends the sixty-second canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 63. Visvamitra Undergoes Terrible Penances

After one-thousand years of penance, when Visvamitra had bathed at the conclusion of his austerities, the hosts of demigods approached him, intending to bestow a benediction upon him. 2) The mighty Brahma addressed him with sweet words saying: 'You are now a rishi! My blessings upon you. You have attained this status by your own pious endeavour!' 3) Having conferred this title upon him, Brahma returned to his heavenly abode, and Visvamitra embarked upon a further course of stringent penances.

4\) "Thereafter, with the passing of many years, Menaka, the supreme Apsara, went to Lake Pushkara to bathe. 5-7) Beholding her unequalled beauty, which was like lightning against a cloud, Visvamitra was smitten by Cupid's arrow, and he spoke to her as follows: 'Welcome, O Apsara, kindly stay here in my hermitage! I am mad with desire for you, please be kind upon me!' Being thus requested, the slender waisted Apsara took up her residence with the sage. 8-12) In this way a great obstacle was put in the way of Visvamitra's asceticism, and for ten years she dwelt happily in his ashram.

"With the passing of time, Visvamitra became somewhat ashamed, and great unhappiness arose in his heart. It occurred to the indignant sage that this was the work of the demigods and that he had been completely diverted from his penances! 'A great obstacle to my austerities has arisen,' he mused, 'and ten years have passed like a day and a night, victimised as I was by bewilderment born of desire!' The great sage thus came to regret his folly and sighed unhappily. 13-16) Seeing the terrified Menaka standing before him with joined palms, quivering with fear, he dismissed her with sweet words and departed for the Himalayas. The illustrious sage firmly resolved to observe celibacy and to conquer lust, and having reached the banks of the Kausiki, he commenced the most difficult austerities. He performed terrible mortifications for thousands of years upon the northern mountain, O Rama, and at length the demigods became alarmed. Together with the celestial sages they approached Brahma and petitioned him as follows: 17-21) 'Please invest this worthy seer with the title of maharshi.'

Hearing the request of the Devas, the grandfather of the universe addressed Visvamitra with these agreeable words: 'Well done, dear child, I am pleased by your cruel penances; I hereby award you the coveted rank of maharshi!' Hearing Brahma's declaration, Visvamitra, who stood with folded hands and bowed head, replied as follows: 'If, because of my acts of piety, you were to award me the unequalled title of brahmarshi, I would indeed have conquered my senses.' Brahma then said: 'You have not yet subdued your senses! 2224) Persevere in your attempt, O tiger among sages!' and so saying, he departed for his heavenly abode. When the demigods had thus dispersed, the great sage resumed his penances. He stood in the open air with raised arms, and he subsisted on air alone. In the summer he sat in the midst of five blazing fires, and in the winter he stood in the open air or in freezing water. In this way he underwent dreadful austerities for one-thousand years. 25) While he was performing this penance, Indra and the host of demigods became greatly anxious. 26) Indra, together with the Maruts, made a proposal to the Apsara called Rambha, which was in their interests but was contrary to those of Visvamitra."

*Thus ends the sixty-third canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 64. Rambha Cursed

There is a vital service you must render the demigods, O Rambha', they said, 'you must effect the complete bewilderment by lust of Visvamitra at this time.' 2) Thus was Rambha, the Apsara, petitioned by the thousandeyed Indra, O Rama. She replied bashfully, her hands folded, as follows: 3) 'O lord of the Devas, this Visvamitra is a fearful personality and he is certain to pronounce a curse on me. 4-5) For that reason I am very frightened; please be merciful to me!' Being thus addressed with fear and dread, with her body quivering and her palms joined, Indra spoke to her as follows, O Rama: 'Do not be afraid, O Rambha. My blessings upon you; just do my bidding. 6) In the spring, when the greenery of the trees is at its most captivating, in the form of a sweet-voiced cuckoo and in the company Cupid, I shall be by your side. 7) You will adorn your resplendent beauty with all the feminine graces and shall thereby divert the ascetic from his penances.'

8\) "Being thus reassured by Indra, Rambha decorated herself as gorgeously as she was able and, smiling charmingly, she began to allure the mind of Visvamitra. 9) Hearing the sweet notes of the cuckoo's song, the sage was transported with joy and cast his gaze over the Apsara. 10) While he listened to the inimitable sound of the cuckoo and as he surveyed Rambha's lovely form, he became somewhat suspicious. 11) He realised that this was the work of Indra and the bull-like sage cursed the Apsara with great fury, saying: 12) 'O Rambha, unlucky woman, because you are deliberately arousing illicit desire in me when I am striving to conquer lust and anger, I curse you to stand here petrified for ten-thousand years! 13) You will remain in this dreadful condition until some powerful and renounced brahmana rescues you.'

14\) "So saying, Visvamitra was filled with remorse that he was unable to contain his wrath. 15) Rambha, however, was immediately transformed to stone by his curse, and hearing the sages outburst, Cupid and Indra stole away. 16) Visvamitra exhausted vast stores of merit by reason of his loss of temper, and because his senses were still uncontrolled, he could get no peace of mind. 17) Because he had rashly wasted all his potency he considered thus: 'I shall not fall prey to wrath again, neither shall I speak to anyone. 18) I will not draw breath for hundreds of years, I shall cause my body to wither up and shall thereby bring my senses under control. 19) Until I am awarded the status of a brahmana, I shall neither breathe nor eat and shall remain situated in penance perpetually if need be. 20) As long as I am in trance the constituents of my body shall remain intact!' Then, true to his word, for one-thousand years he performed such penances as were never seen in the world before, O Rama!'

*Thus ends the sixty-fourth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 65. Visvamitra Attains the Status of a Brahmana

Quitting the Himalayan region, O Rama, Visvamitra headed to the east and commenced the most severe penances. 2) Observing silence, the sage practised austerities of the most stringent order. 3) For a full onethousand years he observed his vow, standing still like a block of wood, and even though he was frequently disturbed he never once became enraged. 4-5) He was firm in his resolve and was not to be dissuaded from his vow, O Rama.

"At the close of a thousand years, the determined sage prepared to break his fast. Indra took the opportunity to appear in the guise of a brahmana and to beg the entire repast that was prepared for Visvamitra. 6-7) Ever resolute, the sage willingly parted with his meal, and even after presenting every grain of it to the brahmana, he spoke not a word to him, being determined to observe his vow of silence. Continuing to hold his peace in this way, he again suspended his breathing.

8-10) "The bull-like sage did not breathe for yet another thousand years, and, as an eventual consequence, a smoke began to issue from his cranium which bewildered the entire universe and caused its inhabitants great affliction. At that time the demigods, sages, Gandharvas, serpents and Rakshasas were greatly dismayed to find their splendour all but blotted out by Visvamitra's power, and full of consternation they petitioned Lord Brahma as follows: 11) Although Visvamitra has been repeatedly tempted to anger and lust he simply thrives in his asceticism. 12-13) Not even the slightest degree of wickedness is to be seen in him. If you do not give him what he craves, then the entire universe, with its moving and non-moving inhabitants, will be put to ruin by his powerful austerities. There is darkness in all directions, and all the luminaries have been blotted out. 14) The ocean waves are violently tossing, mountain ranges are cleaved in two, the earth is quaking and fierce gales are blowing. 15) O Lord Brahma, we are unable to determine the cause of this disturbance. The inhabitants of the three worlds are so bewildered and perplexed by it that they are losing their faith in God. 16-17) Because of this sage's great power, the sun's rays are not visible, and if you do not bestow your mercy upon him he will not desist in his determination to destroy the universe. It will be completely consumed by fire as it was of yore. 18) You should give whatever he desires even if it be the kingdom of the Devas!'

"The demigods then approached Visvamitra with Brahma at the fore, and he spoke sweet words to him as follows: 'Congratulations, O brahma-rishi; we are thoroughly pleased by your austerities! 20-23) You have achieved brahmanhood by your cruel penance, O scion of Kusa. Along with the Maruts I also bestow long life upon you. My blessings upon you, be pleased to have attained your objective and go peacefully from here.' Hearing Brahma's pronouncement and offering his obeisance with great happiness to all the assembled demigods, Visvamitra said: 'If I have indeed become a brahmana and have got a long duration of life withal, then give me the pranava-omkaraand vasatkara.May the Vedas and the military and regal arts seek me out, and most important of all, give me knowledge of the Supreme Being, Sri Vishnu! 24) Let Vasishta acknowledge me to be a brahmana, and if my supreme desire is thus fulfilled, then please depart from here, O best of demigods!'

25\) "Thereafter, by the intercession of the Devas, Vasishta, the best of those who chant the Names of God, being reconciled in friendship with Visvamitra, declared, 'You are verily a brahmana.' 26) The assembled demigods then departed for their habitations saying, 'Undoubtedly you are a brahma-rishi; all success is yours!' 27) Having attained the highest status, namely that of a brahmana, the righteous Visvamitra worshipped Vasishta, who is supreme among the chanters of the Holy Names of God. 28) Having achieved his desire, he journeyed throughout the world, ever situated in asceticism. Thus was brahmanhood attained by this great soul, O Rama. 29) Here, O Rama, is the best of sages; here is the embodiment of penance; here is the personification of the transcendental religion — service to the Supreme; here is the last word in valour!"

30-32) So saying, the powerful Shatananda fell silent, and King Janaka, who listened throughout with Rama and Lakshmana, addressed Visvamitra with joined palms as follows: "I am happy that you favour me by attending my sacrifice together with Rama, O bull among sages. I am purified simply by seeing you, O brahmana. 33-34) I have acquired all manner of good qualities just by the sight of you. Indeed, together with Rama, the assembled sages and my entourage, we have heard all your great qualities described \[and have benefited thereby\]. 35) Your austerities are unprecedented, your strength is unequalled and your virtues are never found to such a degree in others, O sage. 36) I do not tire of hearing of your astounding exploits, but the time for the performance of religious ceremonies is passing. The sun is setting, O sage. 37) Please meet with me again tomorrow morning. Welcome, O best among those who meditate; kindly permit me to depart."

38\) Being thus addressed, Visvamitra extolled the virtues of the great King Janaka, and then, with a joyful heart, he dismissed the affectionate monarch. 39-40) Thereafter, the king of Mithila quickly circumambulated the sage along with his preceptors and his kinsmen. Visvamitra then proceeded to his lodgings, in the company of Rama and Lakshmana, and was worshipped by the great souls who accompanied him.

*Thus ends the sixty-fifth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 66. The History of Siva's Bow

In the spotless dawn that followed, King Janaka concluded his morning duties and called for Raghava and the great-souled Visvamitra. 2) Having worshipped the sage according to the scriptural ordinances, the king addressed Visvamitra, Lord Rama and Lord Lakshmana as follows: 3) "Your Holiness, again I bid you welcome. What would you have me do, O sinless one? Please order me as you will, I ought to be commanded by you!" 4) Being thus addressed by the righteous Janaka, the eloquent Visvamitra gave reply as follows: 5) "These warrior sons of Emperor Dasaratha, Who are glorified the world over, are desirous of seeing that best of bows which is in your possession. 6) Please show it to Them, bless you; They will be highly gratified by the sight of it."

7\) Being so requested, Janaka replied to the great sage, saying: "Hearken, and I shall tell you the reason for this bow's presence in Mithila. 8) It was given to the hand of Devarata, the eldest son of King Nimi, O powerful sage. 9) Long ago, at the time of the destruction of Daksha's sacrifice,when the heroic Lord Siva had playfully stretched his bowstring, he spoke wrathfully to the demigods as follows: 10) 'O you who seek portions of the sacrifice, because no share was allotted to me, I shall sever your splendid, elegant limbs!' 11) The Devas, being disconcerted \[by this threat\], begged Lord Siva for mercy, and at length he became pleased with them. 12-14) Because of the satisfaction they afforded him, the Lord of the Devas gave them his jewel-like bow, and they in turn bestowed it upon my ancestor.

"Then, some while past, when I was ploughing a field \[in readiness for a sacrifice\], a girl-child, who is now known as Sita,was turned up by the ploughshare. I took that girl, who had arisen from the earth, as my daughter, and she began to grow apace. 15) Intending her to be the prize for one of unsurpassed valour, I kept my daughter (who was not born of a woman) in her maiden state, and she meanwhile grew to maturity. 16-18) Various kings came, seeking her hand in marriage, O sage, and I informed those princes who wished to wed my daughter that they must perform some heroic act, otherwise I would not part with her, whereat, all the assembled monarchs who had come to Mithila, asked me what that valorous act might be. I then had Lord Siva's bow brought before the inquisitive suitors. 19-22) They were not able to take it up nor to test its weight in their hands, and perceiving their prowess to be insufficient, I rejected them all. Please hear what those kings did in retaliation, O sage! In a great fury they besieged Mithila, believing their valour to be in question. Knowing I had rejected them, with great ire, they beleaguered my capital city, until, at the end of one year of siege, everything in Mithila was put to ruin. 23) O sage, I became greatly distressed by the situation, and I therefore began to perform austerities in order to get the mercy of the demigods. 24) With great satisfaction they gave me a military force consisting of elephants, horses, chariots and foot-soldiers, and the enemy soon had their army destroyed and were put to flight in all directions. 25) Deprived of their vigour, their heroism confounded, the wretches fled together with their ministers.

"I shall gladly show Rama and Lakshmana that very same, that highly effulgent bow, O determined one! If Rama is able to string this bow, O sage, then I shall bestow my daughter Sita, who was not born of a woman, upon the prince!"

*Thus ends the sixty-sixth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 67. The Breaking of the Great Bow

Hearing the words of King Janaka, Visvamitra, the great sage, spoke to the monarch saying, "Show the bow to Rama!". Thereafter, King Janaka commanded his attendants thus: "Fetch the divine bow, garlanded and smeared with sandalwood paste." 3) Being so instructed by Janaka, his servants, who were of unequalled power, reentered the city, and placing the bow before them, came out once more. 4) Somehow or other, fifteen-thousand huge men combined together to draw the bow along in a casket mounted on eight wheels. 5) Having fetched that beautiful iron casket containing the bow, the king's ministers spoke unto Janaka, who resembled a god, as follows: 6) "Please show this superlative bow, which is adored by all sovereigns, to whomever you will, O Monarch, O Lord of Mithila, O King among kings!"

7\) Hearing their declaration, the king held his palms together and addressed the great-souled Visvamitra, and Rama and Lakshmana, thus: 8-9) "This most excellent bow, O brahmana, was worshipped by the Janakas, and by other kings, who were greatly heroic and powerful yet were unable to bend it. Neither were the hosts of demigods, nor any demon or Rakshasa, nor pre-eminent Gandharvas and Yakshas, Kinnaras and great serpents able to do so. 10) What possibility, then, is there for mere humans to bend this bow, to string it, to fit an arrow to it, to brandish it and to weigh it in their hands? 11) That very same superlative bow is now brought here. Show it, O bull among sages, to both the princes, Rama and Lakshmana!"

12\) When Visvamitra, together with Rama, heard that which was spoken by Janaka, Visvamitra addressed Raghava, saying, "Dear child, O Rama, behold the bow." 13-14) In response to the muni's enunciation, Rama opened the casket wherein the bow lay. Having looked upon it, the Lord said, "Thus do I clasp this divine bow in My hand: I will attempt to weigh it and, moreover, to bend it." 15) Together the king and the sage said, "So be it!", and being encouraged by Visvamitra's words, Rama playfully grasped the middle of the bow.

16\) In the sight of many thousands of men, the Bliss of the Raghus, the very Soul of religion, strung the bow as if in sport. 17) And, having strung the bowstring, He bent the bow. That Supreme Person of great fame, by pulling on the bowstring, then broke the bow in the middle. 18) The sound of its breaking was stupendous, like a peal of thunder, and the earth shook as if an immense mountain had cracked. 19) Every man present there, excepting the great sage, the king, and the two Raghus, fell down unconscious because of that sound. 20) Upon the people being revived, the eloquent king, who was rid of all apprehension, joined his palms and spoke thus to the great sage: 21) "Mighty one, I have witnessed the extraordinary, inconceivable prowess of Rama, Son of Dasaratha, which was unthought of by me. 22) Once she has obtained this son of Dasaratha as her husband, my daughter, Sita, will bring glory to the dynasty of the Janakas. 23) My solemn vow that her hand would be won by the performance of a valorous deed \[namely, the wielding of the bow\] is now fulfilled, O Kausika. My daughter Sita, who is dearer to me than life, will be given to Rama. 24) With your permission, sir, let my ministers hasten by chariots to Ayodhya without delay. All good fortune to you, O descendant of Kusa! 25) Let them fetch King Dasaratha here by means of courteous speech, and relate to him everything concerning the bestowal of Sita in marriage and the winning of her hand by Rama's prowess. 26) Moreover, let them describe to the king the protection of the two princes by Visvamitra. Indeed, let them bring the emperor, full of delight, with all speed."

27\) Visvamitra, the descendant of Kusa said, "So be it!". And the righteous Janaka, having commanded his ministers, despatched them to Ayodhya with orders to describe everything that had occurred and to fetch the emperor as well.

*Thus ends the sixty-seventh canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 68. King Dasaratha Decides to Go to Mithila

As commanded by King Janaka, his messengers proceeded to Ayodhya, having paused for three days en route to rest their exhausted steeds. 2) In accordance with the king's instruction, they entered the royal palace, where they had audience of the ancient Dasaratha, who resembled a demigod. 3-5) When their feeling of awe had subsided, they stood before him with folded hands and spoke to him sweetly and deferentially, saying, "Janaka, King of Mithila, has repeatedly enquired, in the presence of the sacred fire, after your well-being, and that of your family priest and your preceptors, his voice full of sweetness and affection, O mighty king! 6) Having thus enquired after your undisturbed well-being, and having sought permission of Visvamitra, he commanded us to convey the following invitation to you:

7\) "As is widely known, I formerly vowed that my daughter was to be the bride of one who performed a particular act of chivalry. Many kings who tried for her hand were found to be insufficiently heroic and were consequently put out of countenance. 8) Your son Rama, who chanced to come here with Visvamitra as His protector, was not baffled in His attempt. 9) He broke Siva's jewel-like bow in the middle, O Mighty-arms, in full view of the assembled people. 10) By reason of this valorous deed I am obliged to give the hand of my daughter Sita unto the great-souled Rama. I wish to fulfil my promise — kindly give your consent. 11) Please come at once with your esteemed family-priest and your advisors, O king. All glories to you; you will be able to see Rama and Lakshmana here in Mithila. 12) You should permit me to carry out my promise, O Emperor, and you will thus observe the happiness of your two sons.' 13) These are the sweet words spoken by the king of Mithila, with the authorisation of Visvamitra and the approval of Shatananda."

14\) Hearing the words of Maharaja Janaka's envoys, the emperor was filled with ecstasy, and he addressed Vasishta, Vamadeva, and his counsellors as follows: 15) "Rama, Who increases the bliss of Queen Kausalya, together with His brother, Lakshmana, is presently dwelling in Mithila under the protection of Visvamitra. 16) Having witnessed the valour of Raghava, King Janaka is desirous of bestowing his daughter upon Him. 17) If the great-souled Janaka's proposal is agreeable to you, then I shall proceed to his capital without delay." 18) Maharaja Dasaratha's ministers immediately consented, as did the sages present there, and with great happiness the king told his advisors: "We shall depart tomorrow!" 19) King Janaka's virtuous envoys, for their part, rested that night in Ayodhya and were entertained to their great satisfaction.

*Thus ends the sixty-eighth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 69. The Meeting of Dasaratha and Janaka

Thereafter, at the close of night, in the company of his preceptors and his kinsmen, King Dasaratha spoke in ecstasy to Sumantra, saying, "Today, all of our treasurers should precede us under guard, taking abundant gold, silver and jewels. 3) Let a military force consisting of elephants, horses, chariots and foot-soldiers be simultaneously sent forth by my order, as well as the most luxurious and exquisite carriages. 4-5) Let the twice-born Vasishta, Vamadeva, Jabali, Kasyapa, the long-lived Markandeya and Katyayana go ahead, and \[meanwhile\] have my chariot prepared. There should be no time lost, for King Janaka's messengers bid me make haste."

6\) As commanded by Dasaratha, his army, with its four divisions,followed close behind the emperor, who proceeded in the company of the sages. 7) Having journeyed for four days upon the highway, he entered the kingdom of Videha, and when King Janaka heard the good news, he had preparations made for his reception. 8) The delighted Janaka went forth to meet the ancient King Dasaratha and, upon meeting him, he became supremely happy. 9) The exalted Janaka spoke joyfully to the greatest of monarchs, saying, "Welcome, O best of men. It is by the grace of God that you have come here today. 10-14) You will witness the happiness of your unsurpassable sons, attained by valour. By the Grace of God, the mighty sage Vasishta has come in the company of exalted brahmanas, like Indra in the company of the demigods. By the will of Providence all impediments to my daughter's marriage have been removed, and my family is made glorious by its alliance with the mighty and heroic descendants of Raghu! Tomorrow morning, O king, you will be able to inaugurate the wedding, with the assistance of the most distinguished sages, at the conclusion of our sacrifice."

Having heard that which was spoken by Janaka, King Dasaratha, who was the best of the eloquent, replied, in the midst of the sages, as follows: "I have heard that one who receives charity is subject to the command of the donor. 15-19) Whatever you command, that we shall do, O righteous one!" Hearing the words of that truthful, illustrious and pious sovereign, the King of Videha \[Janaka\] was utterly astonished. Then, all the sages present there met with each other in great happiness and spent a joyous night together.

At that time, the mighty Ramachandra and His brother Lakshmana approached Their father in the wake of Visvamitra and touched his feet, whereupon the monarch was pacified by the princes. Being suitably honoured by Janaka, he abided at that place with supreme delight. Maharaja Janaka, for his part, knew what rituals should be undertaken with respect to his sacrifice and his daughter's nuptials, and having performed them he spent the night there also.

*Thus ends the sixty-ninth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 70. Description of the Dynasty of Ikshvaku by Vasishta

Thereafter, in the dawn of next day, having concluded his morning duties in the company of maharshis, the eloquent Janaka addressed his chief priest, Shatananda, as follows: 2-3) "My powerful brother, Kusadvaja by name, a righteous and heroic soul, took up residence in Sankasya, a delightful city which imbibes the waters of the river Ikshumati. Fortified by defensive walls all around, this meritorious town resembles the celestial aircraft known as the Pushpaka. 4) I should like to see him, for he is the guardian of my sacrifice, and he will thus be able to enjoy the delight of this ceremony with me."

5\) Having thus spoken, in the presence of Shatananda, King Janaka instructed a number of experienced messengers who had just then arrived. 6) In accordance with his command, they set off on swift mounts to fetch the king's mighty kinsman, as if they were sent by Indra to fetch Lord Vishnu. 7) When they reached Sankasya they met Kusadvaja; they informed him of all that had occurred and disclosed Janaka's intentions as well. 8) Hearing what had come to pass, he immediately complied with the king's order, and he set off in the company of those excellent messengers. 9) Thereafter, he met the great-souled Janaka, who held religion most dear, and he offered obeisance to the supremely pious Shatananda, and to Janaka as well. 10-20) He then took his seat upon a divine throne worthy of a king. Seated together, the two brothers appeared extremely effulgent, and they despatched their prime-minister, Sudamana, with the following words: "Go at once, O best of counsellors, to the mighty Dasaratha and bring him here together with his sons and his ministers."

Proceeding to the imperial tent, Sudamana beheld that bestower of the prosperity of the Raghus, and bowing his head before him he spoke thus: "O lord of Ayodhya, O hero, the King of Mithila desires to see you along with your preceptors and your family priest." Being so petitioned, King Dasaratha, together with the company of sages and his kinsmen, proceeded to Janaka's residence, and arriving there with his counsellors, his preceptors and his family, the eloquent monarch spoke as follows: "The divine master of the dynasty of Ikshvaku, the blessed sage, Vasishta, the spokesman in all matters pertaining to sacred duty, is well known to you. With the permission of Visvamitra and the assembled maharshis, the righteous Vasishta will describe our royal lineage."

As King Dasaratha fell silent, the expert speaker, the spiritually opulent sage, Vasishta, spoke to Janaka and his family priest as follows: "Brahma, who was generated by Vishnu, is uninterruptedly absorbed in meditation upon that Imperishable Lord. From him was born the sage Mareechi, whose son was Kasyapa Muni. Kasyapa begot Vivasvan \[the sun-god\], and Vivasvan begot Vaivasvata Manu. 21) Manu was formally a patriarch and he begot Ikshvaku; please know that Ikshvaku was the first king of Ayodhya. 22) Maharaja Ikshvaku's son was the celebrated Kukshi, and Kukshi's son was the fortunate Vikukshi. 23) The mighty Vikukshi begot Bana, who was possessed of great majesty. Bana's son was Anaranya, like unto his father in splendour. 24-25) Anaranya begot Prithu, and Prithu begot Trishanku. Trishanku had an illustrious son known as Dhundhumara, and Dhundhumara begot the mighty warrior, Yuvanasva. Yuvanasva's son was the Emperor Mandhata. 26) Mandhata begot the opulent Susandhi, and Susandhi's sons were Dhruvasandhi and Prasenajit.

27-28) "Dhruvasandhi's son was the illustrious Bharata, and Bharata begot Asita, who was opposed by his enemies: the Haihayas, the Talajangas and the heroic Sasabindhus. 29-33) He counterattacked the rebels, but he was deposed and exiled form his kingdom. He retired to the Himalayas with his two wives and a depleted military force, but he met with his inevitable death there. His two wives, it is said, were pregnant at the time, and one of the wives administered poison to the other in order to kill the child she bore in her womb. At that time, Chyavana Muni, the son of Bhrigu, was residing on that pleasant mountain. Desiring a worthy son, the lucky Kalindi, whose eyes were like lotus petals \[and who was the one to whom the poison had been given\], approached the refulgent sage and offered her respects to him. 34-35) In the matter of her giving birth to a son, the muni replied thus: 'Before long a strong, handsome, heroic and effulgent son will be born form your womb together with the poison administered to you, do not fear, O lotus eyes!'

36\) "The princess, who was devoted to her husband, offered her obeisances to Chyavana, and although she was widowed, she shortly gave birth to a male child. 37) She had been given poison by her co-wife, in order to destroy her foetus, but the child was born along with the poison and was consequently named 'Sagara'.38) King Sagara begot Asamanja, and Asamanja begot Amsuman. Dileepa was Amsuman's son, and Dileepa begot Bhagiratha. 39-41) Maharaja Bhagiratha begot Kakutstha, and Kakutstha begot Raghu. Raghu's son was the powerful Pravriddha, who was cursed to become a man-eater and who subsequently received the epithet 'Kalmashapada'.His son was Sankana, and Sankana begot Sudarsana. Sudarsana begot Agnivarna, and Agnivarna begot Seegraha. Seegraha's son was Maru, Maru's son was Prasruka, and he in turn begot Ambareesha. 42) King Ambareesha's son was the emperor, Nahusha. Nahusha begot Yayati, and Yayati begot Nabhaga. 43) Nabhaga begot Aja, and Aja begot Maharaja Dasaratha. From Dasaratha were born the two brothers Rama and Lakshmana \[as well as Bharata and Satrughna\].

44-45) "O best of men, for the sake of Rama and Lakshmana, Who are descended in the oldest dynasty — the House of Ikshvaku, which is a true line of righteous, heroic and truthful kings, you should give away your two daughters, who are equal to their prospective spouses in all respects."

*Thus ends the seventieth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 71. King Janaka's Genealogy

Being thus addressed, King Janaka replied with joined palms saying: "All glories to you! Hearken as we describe our lineage. 2) With respect to the giving away of one's daughter one must give a full account of one's family, O greatly learned one. 3) There was once a king who was famed throughout the three worlds for his pious deeds. His name was Nimi: he was a truthful soul, fully surrendered unto the Supreme Godhead. 4) His son was called Mithi, and Mithi's son was Janaka the First. Janaka the First begot Udavasu. 5) The righteous Udavasu begot Nandivardhana, and the heroic Nandivardhana begot Suketu. 6) Suketu begot the pious Devarata, and the powerful Devarata, who was a king-sage, begot Brihadratha. 7) Brihadratha's son was the glorious Mahaveera, and Mahaveera begot the steadfast and truly valiant Sudhriti. 8) The righteous Dhrishtaketu was sired by Sudhriti, and the king-sage, Dhrishtaketu, begot the renowned Haryasva. 9) Haryasva's son was Maru; Maru's son was Prateendhaka, and Prateendhaka's son was the righteous King Keertiratha. 10) Keertiratha's son was called Devameedha; Devameedha begot Vibudha, and Vibudha begot Maheedraka. 11) Maheedraka's son was the mighty Keertirata, and Keertirata sired Maharoma. 12) Maharoma sired the pious Svarnaroma, and the king-sage, Svarnaroma, begot Hrasvaroma. 13) The great-souled Hrasvaroma had two sons: the eldest was myself, the younger, my heroic brother Kusadhvaja.

14\) "Being the eldest son, my father consecrated me as king. Placing Kusadhvaja in my care, he entered the forest, and I bore the burden of government. 15) My elderly father soon ascended to the heavenly planets, and I took the yoke of kingship upon myself, and looked with affection after my brother, Kusadhvaja, who has the appearance of a demigod. 16) After some time, King Sudhanva came from the city of Sankasya, and the powerful monarch besieged Mithila. 17) He commanded me, saying, 'Have Lord Siva's excellent bow sent to me and give me your lotus-eyed daughter, Sita!' 18) Because I did not comply, a battle ensued between us, and fighting face to face, I slew the rascal in the fray. 19) Having dispatched King Sudhanva, Iinaugurated my heroic brother, Kusadhvaja, as king of Sankasya. 20) My brother is the younger, and I am the elder of the two of us, O sage. It is with pleasure that I give my two daughters to Rama and Lakshmana. 21-22) I give Sita unto Sri Rama and, all good fortune to you, Oormila unto Lakshmana. My Sita is won by Rama's act of valour; she is like the issue of a god, and Oormila is practically her equal. So that there can be no doubt I shall declare my intention thrice. With supreme happiness I give my daughters unto Rama and Lakshmana! 23) O king, kindly have Rama and Lakshmana give cows in charity \[to the brahmanas\] and have Them perform the rites pertaining to the forefathers. Then you may begin the preparations for the nuptials. 24) Today the moon is in the fifteenth lunar mansion, Magha, and in three days, my lord, when it transits the auspicious Uttara Phalguni asterism, you may perform the marriage rites. Charity should be given, so as to ensure the happiness of Rama and Lakshmana."

*Thus ends the seventy-first canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 72. Description of the Donation of Cows and the Recitation of Auspicious Mantras

Having thus spoken, the heroic King Janaka was addressed by the great sage, Visvamitra Muni, together with Vasishta, as follows: 2) "The lineage of you two sovereigns is altogether inconceivable, and unequalled, O bull among men. No one is the equal of the Ikshvakus or the Videhas. 3) Rama and Lakshmana are evidently well matched with Sita and Oormila as regards their piety and beauty. 4-6) O best of men, I have the following suggestion to make, please hear what I say! Your younger brother, the righteous King Kusadvaja, has two daughters whose beauty is unequalled anywhere in the world. I request that they are given in marriage to Prince Bharata and to Prince Satrughna. 7) The sons of Emperor Dasaratha are possessed of youthful beauty, They are like the divinities who preside over the planets, and are equal to the demigods in point of invincibility. 8) By the union of these couples, the houses of Ikshvaku and Videha shall surely be allied; do not allow yourself to become confused, O pious sovereign!"

9\) Hearing Visvamitra's proposal, which was endorsed by Vasishta, King Janaka spoke as follows: 10) "I consider this the good fortune of my family: that these two exalted persons have commanded us to be allied with such a worthy dynasty. 11) Let it be as you say, all glories to you! Let Kusadvaja's daughters be given to the princes Satrughna and Bharata. 12) Let the four princesses be given into the hands of the four mighty princes on the same day. 13) The learned advise that weddings take place the second day the constellations called Phalguni, which are presided over by the progenitor, Prajapati, are occupied by the moon." 14) Having thus spoken, the monarch again rose to his feet, and with folded hands, he addressed the two sages as follows: 15) "You have done me the highest spiritual good, and I am thus the disciple of your Divine Graces. Here are excellent seats; please be seated, O mighty sages! 16) Just as this Mithila is the property of Dasaratha so is Ayodhya mine. There is no doubt about it: your authority extends here also. Do whatever you think is necessary with regards to the preparations for the nuptials."

17\) As the king of Videha, Janaka Maharaja, was speaking, Emperor Dasaratha became delighted, and he replied to the monarch thus: 18) "You two lords of Mithila are possessed of innumerable qualities and sages and members of the kingly class have been honoured by you. 19) May good fortune attend you! We shall now go to our quarters and shall perform the Sraddha ceremony."

20\) Taking his leave from Janaka, the glorious King Dasaratha immediately performed the Sraddha ceremony according to the scriptural injunctions, and in the morning, when he had risen from bed, he gave away cows in charity. 22) As instructed, he donated one hundred thousand cows to the brahmanas present there, for the sake of his sons. 23-24) He then distributed a further four-hundred thousand cows for the sake of his dearly beloved sons. Each cow was adorned with gold ornaments, and was presented along with a bell-metal milk-pail. 25) When he and his sons had gifted cows upon the brahmanas, King Dasaratha shone as brilliantly as the placid Lord Brahma surrounded by the presiding deities of the three planetary systems.

*Thus ends the seventy-second canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 73. Description of the marriage of Sita and Rama &amp;Co

That same day, as the Emperor performed the grand ceremony of the donation of cows, the heroic Yudhajit, who was the son of the king of Kekaya, and who happened to be Lord Bharata's uncle, arrived in Mithila. Seeing King Dasaratha and enquiring after his well-being, he spoke to him as follows: 3) "The Lord of Kekaya asked with affection if Your Majesty fares well, and he informs Your Highness that all is well with those whose interests you have at heart. 4) The king is desirous of seeing my nephew, Bharata, and for that reason I was sent to Ayodhya, O descendant of Raghu. 5) In Ayodhya I heard that you had gone to Mithila with your sons, to attend the marriage of Rama. 6-8) I therefore came here with all haste, longing to see my nephew." Having met this welcome guest, King Dasaratha honoured him with a fitting reception. The next day, having sojourned with his sons and other great souls, the king rose at dawn, and performed his morning offices. Thereafter, he placed the company of sages at the head of his entourage, and entered the sacrificial hall. At the propitious moment, which is properly termed 'vijaya', Rama and His brothers arrived, gorgeously arrayed with ornaments, with Vasishta and other saintly persons going before Them.

When Vasishta arrived, he addressed Janaka Maharaja as follows. 11) "O king," he said, "together with his sons, Who are duly dressed in festive attire, Maharaja Dasaratha eagerly awaits him who will give his daughters in charity. 12) Both the donor and the recipient shall surely have their desires fulfilled; kindly do your duty and perform this auspicious nuptial rite!" 13) Being so commanded by the exalted Vasishta, noble Janaka of great prowess and righteousness. responded as follows: 14) "Who, standing as a doorkeeper, bars my door, and whose command does the emperor eagerly await? Who need be circumspect in his own house? This kingdom is as much yours as it is mine. 15) O Vasishta, my daughters have performed all the necessary auspicious rites and have now arrived at the foot of the altar, shining as brilliantly as fire. 16) I am sitting by the altar and simply wait for you! There are no obstacles in the path of this ceremony; why, then, is it being delayed?" 17) Hearing King Janaka's utterance, Emperor Dasaratha ushered his sons and the host of sages in to the wedding pavilion.

18-20) Thereafter, Janaka Maharaja addressed Vasishta saying: "O pious sage, please perform the marriage ceremony of Sri Rama, the Bliss of the world!" Saying, "So be it!" to Janaka, and placing Shatananda before him, the powerful sage, Vasishta, prepared the altar in the middle of the hall in conformity with the scriptural injunctions. 21-23) He adorned it with sandalwood paste and with sweet smelling flowers. He set up golden pitchers, excellent pots, fresh vegetable sprouts, dishes, varieties of incense, vessels, ladles meant for pouring ghee on to the sacred fire, ladles meant for transferring ghee preparatory to making an oblation, vessels brimming with arghya, vessels full of parched grain for consignment into the fire, vessels containing barley for the same purpose, ornamental vessels, and blades of darbha grass cut to a uniform length, which he laid across the altar after chanting preparatory hymns. 24) Then, having recited the appropriate mantras, the mighty sage kindled the sacred fire upon the altar and poured oblations of ghee into the flames. 25) Sita was then escorted to the altar, fully decorated with jewellery and ornaments, and she was made to sit facing Lord Raghava. 26) King Janaka addressed Sri Rama, the bliss of Mother Kausalya as follows: "My daughter, Sita, will be your lawful companion. 27) Please accept her, bless You, and take her hand in Yours. She will be devoted to her husband, she is greatly fortunate and she will follow You as if she were Your shadow." 28) So saying, the king sprinkled water purified by mantra, and the demigods and sages cried, "Well done! well done!" 2931) The Devas sounded kettledrums and showered an incessant rain of flowers from the sky.

Thereafter, having given away his daughter Sita, following the sprinkling of sanctified water, Maharaja Janaka spoke in great ecstasy, saying: "Come here, my dear Lakshmana! Please accept my dazzlingly beautiful daughter, Oormila. Take her hand in Yours; let no time be wasted!" Having thus addressed Sri Lakshmana, the sovereign called Lord Bharata. 32) "Take Madhavee's hand in Yours, O descendant of Raghu", he said, and then called Lord Satrughna as well. 33-36) "O mighty-armed Prince," he said, "take Srutakeerti's hand in Yours. You are all gentle and devoted to morality. May you now be married. Let no time be lost!" In obedience to King Janaka's command, and as approved by Vasishta, the four princes took the hands of the four princesses. The four transcendental princes then circumambulated the fire, the altar, Janaka Maharaja, and the sages, together with Their wives. And They then solemnized Their marriage according to the scriptural ordinances. 37) A splendid and torrential shower of flowers fell from the sky, and there was the sound of celestial kettledrums, singing, and the playing of musical instruments. 38) The hosts of Apsaras began to dance jubilantly, and the Gandharvas sang melodiously: such was the phenomenon seen at the wedding of the four delighters of the Raghu dynasty. 39-40) While the resonant sound of musical instruments was thus noised abroad, the mighty princes circumambulated the sacred fire thrice with Their wives, and then led them away. The king was then seen to follow, in the company of the sages and his relatives.

*Thus ends the seventy-third canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 74. The Meeting with Parasurama

When the night had passed, Visvamitra, the great sage, departed for the Himalayas with the consent of the kings. 2) Once Visvamitra had gone, Emperor Dasaratha took leave of the king of Mithila and headed toward Ayodhya. 39) At that time King Janaka gave dowry in respect of his two daughters. He gave hundreds of thousands of cows, fine carpets, linen and silk, and innumerable pieces of clothing. He gave elephants, horses, chariots and foot soldiers, a hundred beautiful and richly ornamented young girls as maidservants, and good manservants too. He gave gold, silver, pearls and coral with respect to his daughters marriage, and having got the permission of the Emperor, he entered Mithila and retired to his personal residence. Maharaja Dasaratha, the lord of Ayodhya, then proceeded to his capital with his great-souled sons, having placed the company of sages and his military force at the fore.

As he journeyed along the highway with the sages and Lord Rama, he heard the inauspicious shrieks of birds overhead and noticed deer passing to his right. 10-13) Noting these omens, he put the following enquiry to Vasishta Muni: "Cruel birds scream overhead whilst deer pass to the right. What does this portend? My heart is throbbing and my mind is troubled!" The great sage replied sweetly to King Dasaratha's query, saying, "Please hear what this signifies: the horrid cries of birds foretell something fearful, while deer bring peace and calm. Put this fear from your mind!" As they spoke, a violent wind suddenly began to blow. 14) The earth quaked, and great trees were felled. The sun was obscured by darkness and it became impossible to discern which direction was which. 15-17) The king's army was enveloped by dust and was thrown into confusion. Only Vasishta, the sages, the king and Rama retained their presence of mind, while everyone else lost consciousness. In that terrible darkness, and as the army was apparently covered with ashes, the Emperor espied the annihilator of kings: Parasurama, son of Jamadagni, his matted locks coiled upon his head and his expression ferocious. 18) He looked as unassailable as Mount Kailasa, and he was as unbearable as the ultimate conflagration of the universe. Being like fire, ordinary men were unable to gaze upon his dreadful effulgence. 19) From his body hung an axe and a dazzling bow of the very best kind. He held an arrow in his hand, like Lord Siva, the slayer of the Tripura Demon.20-21) Beholding Parasurama, who was as radiant as fire, the sages, headed by Vasishta, who were all successful in chanting the Holy Names of God and in the performance of fire sacrifice, gathered together and began to whisper among themselves. "Let us hope", they said, "that, out of anger at the murder of his father, he will not annihilate the monarchial order! 22) He formerly destroyed the warrior caste \[in retribution for his father's death\] and his fury and his passion subsided. Surely he does not wish to destroy it yet again!" So saying, the sages approached Parasurama with arghya, chanting the sweet words: "Rama, Rama" as they went. 24) The refulgent Parasurama accepted their worship, then that son of Jamadagni addressed Rama, the son of Dasaratha.

*Thus ends the seventy-fourth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 75. The Stringing of Lord Vishnu's Bow

Parasurama addressed Rama thus: "O Rama, son of Dasaratha, O Hero, Your prowess is said to be extraordinary. I have heard in detail how You bent and shattered Lord Siva's bow. 2) Hearing of that marvellous and inconceivable feat, I have come here with a delightful bow. 3) Demonstrate Your strength: take Jamadagni's dreadful looking bow, fit an arrow to it, and draw it to its full length. 4) Once I have witnessed Your might, and the bow is drawn, then I shall offer You single combat, and shall thereby add glory to Your valour!"

Upon hearing Parasurama's challenge, Maharaja Dasaratha, his face full of anxiety and his palms joined, spoke as follows: 6) "Since you vented your wrath upon the warrior caste, you have become tranquil and are a brahmana engaged in severe austerities. You ought to give these boys, my sons, an assurance that They have nothing to fear. 7) You are born in the dynasty of Brighu, and you are characteristically devoted to the study of the Vedas. You have sworn before Indra the thousand-eyed to lay down your weapons. 8) Having become intent on righteousness, you gave the earth to Kasyapa Muni and retired to the forest, making your residence upon Mount Mahendra. 9) You have come here intent on my total destruction, O great sage. I will be unable to live for a single moment if you slay my Rama!"

10\) Parasurama was completely indifferent to Dasaratha's plea, and he therefore spoke to Rama alone. He said, "These two excellent, divine bows, which are difficult to bend, were worshipped the world over, and were exquisitely fashioned by Visvakarma. 12) The bow You broke, O Rama, was given by Lord Vishnu to Lord Siva, who was desirous of battle, and it was employed in the slaying of the Tripura demon. 13) The other invincible bow was given to Lord Vishnu by the principal Devas. This is that very bow, O Rama, and it is capable of destroying the cities of one's foes. 14-24) This bow is equal in power to that of Lord Siva! At that time the demigods questioned Lord Brahma, being eager to ascertain the relative strength of Lord Siva and Lord Vishnu. Understanding the demigods' intention, the Grandfather caused hostility to arise between Lord Siva and Lord Vishnu, and as a result a fierce battle ensued which made one's hair stand on end, each of the Lords being intent on defeating the other. After a while, Lord Siva had his indefatigable bow unstrung, and the three-eyed god was paralysed by Lord Vishnu's roar. The demigods, the hosts of sages and the Charanas then assembled on the battlefield, and begged those best of Lords to be at peace with one another. Seeing Siva's bow had been unstrung by Lord Vishnu, the Devas and sages concluded that Vishnu was the more powerful.

"Lord Siva personally gave this bow to Devarata, the sage-king of the Videhas, and Lord Vishnu, for His part, bestowed this bow, which can lay waste the cities of one's foes, upon Richeeka, a scion of Bhrigu. Richeeka, in turn, gave the bow to his son, my father, the great-souled Jamadagni. Thinking my father to be an ordinary man, a certain Arjuna put him to death. Because of the cruel and unjustified murder of my father, out of anger I annihilated the monarchial order twenty-one times. 24-25) Acquiring the world \[from them\] in that way, I gave it as a fee unto the righteous Kasyapa in payment for the sacrifice he performed for me. 26) "I was residing upon Mount Mahendra and had acquired great mystic power by dint of my penances, when I heard that You had broken Siva's bow. As soon as the news reached my ears, I hastened to meet You. 27) Considering Your princely duty to be of paramount importance, You should take the mighty bow which was handed down from my grandsire. 28) If You have the strength to fit an arrow to it and to bend it, then I shall offer You single combat, O Rama!"

*Thus ends the seventy-fifth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 76. Parasurama's Pride Curbed

Hearing Parasurama's challenge, Lord Rama replied out of respect for His father, who was altogether lost for words, saying, "I have heard of the deeds you have done in retribution for the slaying of your father, O brahmana, and I am obliged to you for that. 3) However, as you have treated Me contemptuously, implying that I am cowardly and impotent, you will bear witness today to My insuperable power!"

4\) So saying, the indignant Raghava took that superlative weapon and an arrow from the hand of Parasurama. 5-6) Rama then drew the bow, and fitting an arrow to it, angrily addressed Parasurama, saying, "You are a brahmana and I am obliged to worship you, therefore I am unable to discharge this arrow at you. 7) It is My opinion that I should withdraw your freedom of movement, and the unequalled worlds you have won by means of penance. 8) Indeed, this devastating shaft of Lord Vishnu, which can crush the strength and pride of one upon whom it falls, should not be loosed in vain."

9\) Observing Lord Rama bearing that best of bows, the demigods, together with the host of celestial sages, assembled there with Brahma at their head. 10) Gandharvas, Apsaras, Siddhas, Charanas, Kinnaras, Yakshas, Rakshasas and serpents flocked there to witness that extraordinary deed. 11) The whole world was stunned upon seeing Rama wielding that excellent bow and the son of Jamadagni deprived of his power. 12-13) Awestruck by his loss of potency and the beauty of the lotus-eyed Rama, Parasurama addressed the Lord in a faltering voice, saying, "I formerly gave the earth to Kasyapa, and at that time he told me, 'You must not dwell in my kingdom.' 14) In accordance with the instruction of my preceptor I do not reside upon the earth at night, O Rama. 15) Please do not deprive me of my freedom of movement, O Hero, I shall speed as swift as the mind to Mount Mahendra. 16) As for those unequalled worlds won by my asceticism, O Rama, destroy them with that excellent arrow without hesitation! 17) By the mere fact that You have handled this bow I know You to be the Imperishable Lord Sri Vishnu, the slayer of the demon Madhu, the Overlord of the demigods. 18) The Devas assembled here are gazing upon You, whose deeds are inimitable, and who are unconquerable in battle. 19) You are perfectly capable of shunning me, O Rama, O Lord of the universe, for I am painfully defeated by You. 20) You may release that superb arrow, O Truthful One, and as soon as it is discharged I shall repair to Mount Mahendra."

21\) As Parasurama was speaking to Rama, the son of Dasaratha, the Lord discharged that incomparable shaft. 22) Perceiving the worlds won by his penance to be vanquished by Lord Raghava, the son of Jamadagni went swiftly to Mount Mahendra. 23) At that moment all directions were rid of darkness, and the demigods and the hosts of sages began to offer prayers to Lord Rama, who stood with upraised bow. 24) Having first worshipped Him, Parasurama circumambulated the Lord and headed directly for his own abode.

*Thus ends the seventy-sixth canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

## Canto 77. Rama Arrives at Ayodhya

Upon the departure of Parasurma, the illustrious Raghava became peaceful once more, and He gave Sri Vishnu's bow into the hand of the unequalled Varuna.2) After saluting Vasishta and the principle sages, Lord Rama noticed that His father was in a state of confusion, and he addressed him as follows: 3) "The son of Jamadagni has departed, My lord. Please supervise the dispatching of the four divisions of your army to Ayodhya."

4\) Hearing Rama's submission, King Dasaratha embraced Him and smelt His head. 5) Hearing the words, 'Parasurama has departed', the monarch became supremely happy, and he considered that he and his son had been reborn. 6) He therefore commanded his army to march, and they proceeded swiftly towards Ayodhya. The city was decorated with gorgeous pennants and banners, and the tumultuous sound of musical instruments filled the air. 7) The king's highway was sprinkled with water and profusely strewn with flowers, and upon the entrance of the emperor his subjects greeted him with joyous faces and with auspicious articles in their hands. 8) The king entered a city that thronged with crowds of jubilant citizens. While he was yet some way off from Ayodhya the city's brahmanas went out to meet him. 9) The fortunate and glorious King Dasaratha entered his palace, which was as delightful to behold as the Himalaya mountains, with his sons in his train. 10-22) Once inside his residence, the Emperor was honoured with all manner of desirable things, and he felt great ecstasy within. The lovely-waisted queens, Kausalya, Sumitra and Kaikeyi and other ladies of the king's household received their daughters-in-law with great attentiveness. Then, the greatly fortunate Sita and the renowned Oormila, as well as Kusadhvaja's two daughters, were engaged by the royal womenfolk in the worship of various demigods: they got the felicitations of the priests, and were splendidly attired in new silk. They offered their respectful salutations to whoever was deserving of reverence and they enjoyed great bliss in the intimate association of their respective husbands. Having got wives, weaponary, wealth, and the company of friends, those bull-like princes passed Their time as obedient sons of Their father.

After some time had elapsed, Maharaja Dasaratha spoke to the son of Kaikeyi. "The heroic Yudhajit, son of the king of Kekaya is presently abiding here and is desirous of taking You to visit his father," he said. In response to His father's disclosure, Lord Bharata prepared to depart, together with Lord Satrughna, and taking leave of His father, the indefatigable Lord Rama and His mothers, the Hero left in the company of His brother. Having got the association of Bharata and Satrughna, Yudhajit was filled with ecstasy, and on entering his city his father was delighted, it is said.

Upon Bharata leaving Ayodhya, Lord Sri Rama and the mighty Laksmana treated their father, who resembled a demigod, with the utmost respect. Putting His father's command before all else, Rama performed all the duties relating to his subjects; He became the beloved well-wisher of them all and He served His mothers with great attentiveness. 23) From time to time He would see to the business of His preceptors, and in this way He satisfied His father, the brahmanas and the citizens. 24) Because of His exemplary conduct as He dealt with life's affairs, the truthful Rama became famous the world over. 25) Like a Brahma, He was the most virtuous of beings, and He spent many seasons in the company of His darling Sita.

26\) For her part, Sita thought of nothing but her Rama and within her heart surrendered unto Him. She too was especially dear to Rama, who considered that His marriage to Sita was His father's doing. 27) By reason of Rama's many virtues, and His transcendental beauty, Sita's love for Him increased twofold, and although she attempted to conceal it within herself, that love was directly communicated from her heart to His. 28) That princess of Mithila, daughter of King Janaka, was easily able to divine her husband's thoughts, and she was as lovely as any celestial, vying with the Goddess of Fortune in beauty. 29) The blissful Lord Rama had His beauty embellished by His union with the affectionate daughter of King Janaka, just as Vishnu does by His union with Sri, the Goddess of Fortune.

*Thus ends the seventy-seventh canto of the Bala Kanda of Srimad Ramayana*

*End of the Bala Kanda*
