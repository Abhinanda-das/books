Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura’s commentary on Mahāprabhu’s Śikṣāṣṭakam, the Sammodana Bhāṣyam (‘that commentary which gives bliss to the devotees’ ) was written in Sanskrit in 1886. The Ṭhākura gives a detailed explanation of each verse, starting with the mūlā-śloka (verse one), revealing the progressive stages of the sādhaka leading to prema.

# Sammodana Bhāṣyam \[BS\]

## 1. What is the highest sādhana?

*ceto-darpaṇa-mārjanaṁ bhava-mahā-dāvāgni-nirvāpaṇaṁ*  
*śreyaḥ-kairava-candrikā-vitaraṇaṁ vidyā-vadhū-jīvanam*  
*ānandāmbudhi-vardhanaṁ prati-padaṁ pūrṇāmṛtāsvādanaṁ*  
*sarvātma-snapanaṁ paraṁ vijayate śrī-kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtanam*

**Перевод:**

**Glory to the Śrī Kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtana, which cleanses the heart of all the dust accumulated for years and extinguishes the fire of conditional life, of repeated birth and death. This saṅkīrtana movement is the prime benediction for humanity at large because it spreads the rays of the benediction moon. It is the life of all transcendental knowledge. It increases the ocean of transcendental bliss, and it enables us to fully taste the nectar for which we are always anxious.**

**Комментарий:**

*pañca-tattvānvitaṁ nityaṁ praṇipatya mahāprabhum \|*  
*nāmnā sanmodanaṁ-śikṣāṣṭaka-bhāṣyaṁ praṇīyate \|\|*

**Offering continual respects to Mahāprabhu possessing Pañca-tattva, I write a commentary of Śikṣāṣṭaka named Sammodana-bhāṣyam.**

*bhagavān brahma kārtsnyena trir anvīkṣya manīṣayā \|*  
*tad adhyavasyat kūṭa-stho ratir ātman yato bhavet \|\|*

**The unchanging Lord reviewed three times the Vedas completely with his intelligence and determined that process which produces prema in the self. (SB** [**2.2.34**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-2/2.34)**)**

According to this conclusive statement, bhakti is the only process for bestowing the spiritual goal, and not other processes like karma and jñāna. Pure bhakti cannot be attained without faith which is filled with the understanding of the meaning of scripture or which is filled with greed for the sweetness of the Lord’s pastimes. Even when that faith has developed, without association of devotees, topics of the Lord characterized by hearing and chanting will not manifest.

*satāṁ prasaṅgān mama vīrya-saṁvido*  
*bhavanti hṛt-karṇa-rasāyanāḥ kathāḥ*  
*taj-joṣaṇād āśv apavarga-vartmani*  
*śraddhā ratir bhaktir anukramiṣyati*

**From association with the best devotees, topics of my glorious pastimes become directly realized, bringing the devotee to niṣṭhā. Then the topics become an elixir for the heart and ears at the stage of ruci. By taste for these topics, āsakti, bhāva and then prema for the Lord who is the destroyer of material life, quickly develop in sequence. (SB** [**3.25.25**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-3/25.25)**)**

By the power of devotee association chanting (kīrtana) concerning the Lord’s name, form, qualities and pastimes will arise. Glories of that kīrtana are explained first in Mahāprabhu’s teachings. Since Kṛṣṇa-kīrtana is the svarūpa of all auspiciousness, by the word param in the last line of the verse, only Kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtana in bhajanakriyā which takes place after faith and association of devotees, is meant, rather than saṅkīrtana of the Lord which takes place with pratibimba-bhaktyābhāsa (false bhakti). In the eight verses, under the guise of his personal words, he expresses the necessity for the jīva to deliberate upon sambandha, abhidheya and prayojana. Deliberation on these subjects will take place briefly in this commentary.

Kṛṣṇa-caitanya-candra, whose feet are served by pure devotees, says, “Kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtana is victorious.” How is Kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtana victorious in the universe arising from māyā-śakti? Please listen. Ekam evādvitīyam of the śruti indicates the oneness of the supreme tattva. Neha nānāsti kiñcana indicates that supreme tattva is devoid of qualities. Sarvaṁ khalv idaṁ brahma from śruti indicates that the supreme tattva is always endowed with qualities. Though the Lord is simultaneously endowed with qualities and without qualities, perception of qualities is stronger, since one cannot even perceive the Lord devoid of qualities. Our tattvācārya, Jīva Gosvāmī says:

*ekam eva tat parama-tattvaṁ svābhāvikācintya-śaktyā*  
*sarvadaiva svarūpa-tad-rūpa-vaibhava-jīva-pradhāna-rūpeṇa*  
*caturdhāvatiṣṭhate \| sūryāntara-maṇḍalastha-teja iva*  
*maṇḍala-tad-bahirgata-raśmi-tat-praticchavi-rūpeṇa*

**There is one supreme tattva who is present at all times by his natural, inconceivable śakti, in four forms—his svarūpa, his spiritual vaibhavas (expansions), the jīva and pradhāna. It is like the sun which has internal heat and produces rays externally as its secondary form. (Bhagavat-sandarbha 16)**

The following should be said about this. Bhagavān is the supreme tattva. He is endowed with śakti. Śakti-śaktimator abedhaḥ: the śakti and the possessor of the śakti are non-different. This is according to Brahma-sūtra. But there is the Vedic statement parāsya śaktir vividhaiva śruyate: it is said the Lord has various śaktis (Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad [6.8](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sveta-bs/6.8)). Thus, by his acintya-śakti, the impossible becomes possible. Eternal difference is unavoidable and that is not negated by the logic of kevalādvaita-vāda.

That parā śakti is divided into three: antaraṅga, taṭasha and bahiraṅga. By the antaraṅga or svarūpa-śakti, in its full form, the supreme tattva manifests eternally in the form of Bhagavān, the shelter of all auspicious qualities. In order to perform his pastimes, he remains by his favorable svarūpa-śakti as powerful forms in the spiritual realm of Vaikuṇṭha, etc. By taṭastha-śakti he exists as jīvas, spiritual particles like atoms in the rays of the sun. By his bahiraṅga or māyā-śakti he is seen as material energy, in the material realm, like a mixture of color in a reflection. In this way the Lord, the jīva, matter and Vaikuṇṭha are different and nondifferent from the Lord.

The jīva has a small part of the nature of the Lord since he takes shelter of the Lord. He is external to (different from) the Lord, since he has no knowledge of the Lord. Like a ray, the jīva can be overpowered by the shadow, māyā. Since jīva is a śakti of the Lord, he can assist in the Lord’s pastimes. Because of his borderline (taṭastha) nature, he can be overcome by māyā. Jīvas under control of māyā suffer in saṁsāra. By the disappearance of māyā through relationship with the svarūpa-śakti, jīva’s saṁsāra is destroyed and the jīva becomes situated in his svarūpa. For the jīvas bewildered by māyā, after repeatedly experience suffering in saṁsāra, when faith in the conclusions of scripture arise by association with devotees and greed for the sweetness of the Lord arises, then the jīvas become qualified for bhakti, which is the essential function of the hlādinīśakti of the svarūpa-śakti.

By appearance of faith, from the power of devotee association in the form of surrender to the feet of a guru, hearing about tattva takes place. When kīrtana arises after hearing, the power within jīva’s svarūpa in the form of the method of conquering māyā can be seen. This is the method of victory by the Lord’s kīrtana (vijayate) in this world. By this type of kīrtana, it is shown in the verse that seven results are achieved for the jīva. I will discuss these individually.

Through these descriptions, the nature of the svarūpa of the jīva is described.

Thus Jīva Gosvāmī says:

*jīvākhya-samaṣṭi-śakti-viśiṣṭasya parama-tattvasya khalv aṁśa*  
*eko jīvaḥ \| sa ca tejo-maṇḍalasya bahiś cara-raśmi-paramāṇur iva*  
*parama-cid-eka-rasasya tasya bahiś cara-cit-paramāṇuḥ*

**A single jīva is an aṁśa of the Supreme Lord who is endowed with the śakti consisting of jīvas in total (jīva-śakti). The jīva is a small particle of consciousness external to the supreme conscious entity, like the particle of a light ray external to the sun globe. (Prīti-sandarbha)**

Commentator on Vedānta-sūtra says:

*vibhu-caitanyam īśvaro’nucaitanyam jīvaḥ nityaṁ jñānād-*  
*iguṇakatvaṁ asmad arthatvaṁ cobhayatra jñānasyāpi*  
*jñātṛtvaṁ prakāśasy raveḥ prakāśakatva-vad aviruddhaṁ*  
*\| tatreśvaraḥ svatantraḥ svarūpa śaktimān, prakṛtyādy*  
*anupreveśaniyamanābhyāṁ jagad vidadhat \| bhakti-vyaṅgya*  
*ekarasaḥ prayacchati cit-sukhaṁ svarūpam \| jīvās tv*  
*anekāvasthā bahavaḥ \| pareśa-vaimukhyāt teṣāṁ bandhas*  
*tat sāmmukhyātu tat-svarūpa-tad-guṇāvaraṇa-rūpa-*  
*dvividha-bandha-nivṛttis-tat-svarūpādi-sākṣāt-kṛtir iti \|*

**The Lord is all-pervading consciousness. The jīva is a particle of consciousness. The jīva and the Lord have the quality of knowledge eternally. They both have a sense of I. Being a knower of knowledge is not contradictory. It is like the sun which reveals things. The Lord is independent, having his svarūpa-śakti. By entering and regulating, he controls the universe. Being full of rasa, or bhakti, he bestows a svarūpa of spiritual happiness. The Lord’s pleasure or bhakti manifests. His svarūpa is spiritual bliss. The jīvas are many, in various conditions. Being averse to the Lord, they are bound up. Being favorable to the Lord, they destroy the two types of bondage which cover the svarūpa and jīva’s qualities. They then directly perceive the Lord’s svarūpa. (Govinda-bhāṣya 1.1.1)**

In that state, the jīva is small, with a svarūpa of consciousness. He has pure ahaṅkāra, pure heart and pure body. Because of aversion to the Lord and because of possessing attraction to matter, the pure heart in the pure ahaṅkāra becomes contaminated with ignorance. Though the jīva has a pure heart in his pure svarūpa, when contaminated with ignorance, māyā’s covering, it is not possible for the mirror of the heart to be effective. Because of this it cannot show the true svarūpa.

But when bhakti, the essential function of hlādinī, acts, Kṛṣṇasaṅkīrtana manifests after hearing, and destroys all contamination of ignorance. Then the jīva, manifesting a pure heart, becomes endowed with a pure ahaṅkāra, and sees in their true form on the mirror of his heart the five tattvas: the Lord, the jīva, prakṛti, time and karma. When the mirror of the heart is cleansed, he sees his svadharma since he sees his svarūpa in its true nature. His svadharma is to be servant of the Lord. With that tendency, the functions of saṁsāra transform into functions of serving Kṛṣṇa.

Bhava means the birth of the jīva in the material world. This is a forest fire, and it is not possible to extinguish except by Kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtana. Does Kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtana stop when one attains knowledge of svadharma? No. When eternal dharma is achieved, kīrtana becomes an aṅga of the dharma of one’s svarūpa. That is the meaning of śreyaḥ kairava-candrikā-vitaraṇam. The enjoyment of māyā by the jīva enamoured with māyā is preyas, irrepressible saṁsāra. After becoming disgusted with māyā, the jīvas serve the Lord. This is śreyas. Kīrtana spreads out the śreyas, like moonlight which makes the lotus blossom.

Bhaktyā sañjātayā bhaktyā: Bhakti produces bhakti. According to this principle, pure bhakti arises by bhakti in ābhāsa forms of hearing and chanting in the faithful person. The example of the moon suggests that kīrtana produces nectar, as nectar flows from the moon.

“When will persons who perform pure bhakti attain their svarūpas?” Gauracandra answers, “vidyā-vadhū-jīvanam.” The śakti of the Lord is actually only one. It has two functions: knowledge and ignorance. Knowledge is known as yogamāyā of the svarūpa-śakti. Ignorance is the cause of covering the qualities of the jīva’s svarūpa and produces material consciousness. When pure bhakti arises through performing sādhana of hearing and chanting, the jīva’s ignorance is destroyed and that bhakti, producing disgust for everything except the spiritual, destroys the jīva’s two conditional bodies—gross and subtle. Bhakti manifests the pure spiritual body of the jīva’s svarūpa, even the body of a gopī, the abode of madhura-rasa, by distinct qualification. Thus, Kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtana acts as the very life of knowledge. That the svarūpa-śakti is the wife of Kṛṣṇa can be seen in the descriptions of his various pastimes.

When the gross and subtle bodies disappear, the jīva’s small nature becomes pure.

“The jīva’s happiness must also be very small, since the jīva’s svarūpa is very small.” The son of Śacī then teaches, ānandāmbudhivardhanam. In this state, kṛṣna-saṅkīrtana increases the natural bliss of the jīva unlimitedly, by the essential function of hlādinī. The jīva who has attained his pure svarūpa attains unlimited bliss. In that state, being filled with spiritual affection, the jīva at every moment attains a full taste of sweetness by anurāga. Since there is no extinguishing of thirst, the enjoyment of rasa for the Lord who has an ever-fresh form eternally is ever fresh.

“Since attempts at enjoyment are contrary to pure prema, how can one attain pure bliss in that state.” The crown jewel of sannyāsīs adds the phrase sarvātma-snapanam. Because Kṛṣṇa’s bliss is pure in that state, the jīva, devoid of desire for enjoyment for himself (being bathed, snapanam), attains all bliss related to the pastimes of the couple by his svarūpa as a servant of Rādhikā, who is filled with mahābhāva, and is by her nature hlādinī-śakti. Snapanam or bathing means the jīva is completely purified of the faults of merging in Brahman and the faults of enjoying one’s own desires.

Thus Kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtana, endowed with seven qualities, filled with the various pastimes of prema of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, whose svarūpas are sac-cid-ānanda, remains with all excellence (vijayate).

## 2. Why is nāma-sādhana easy?

*nāmnām akāri bahudhā nija-sarva-śaktis*  
*tatrārpitā niyamitaḥ smaraṇe na kālaḥ*  
*etādṛśī tava kṛpā bhagavan mamāpi*  
*durdaivam īdṛśam ihājani nānurāgaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**In your holy name there is all good fortune for the living entity, and therefore You have many names, such as “Kṛṣṇa” and “Govinda,” by which you expand Yourself. You have invested all your potencies in those names, and there are no hard and fast rules for remembering them. My dear Lord, although you bestow such mercy upon the fallen, conditioned souls by liberally teaching your holy names, I am so unfortunate that I commit offenses while chanting the holy name, and therefore I do not achieve attachment for chanting.**

**Комментарий:**

There are four types of kīrtana: concerning the Lord’s name, form, qualities and pastimes. Since the name is the seed of all bliss and since the name and Kṛṣṇa designated by the name are nondifferent, nāma-kīrtana is the best of all. Showing this first, with a desire to produce faith in the jīvas, Caitanyadeva says: O Lord! Seeing that I am without shelter, being most merciful, you have manifested many names, some primary and some secondary names. Primary names are Hari, Kṛṣṇa, Govinda, Acyuta, Rāma, Ananta and Viṣṇu. Secondary names are Brahman, Paramātmā, Niyantā, Pātā, Sraṣṭā, and Mahendra. You have endowed your primary names with all powers of your svarūpa-śakti (nija-sarva-śaktiḥ).

*na hi bhagavann aghaṭitam idaṁ*  
*tvad-darśanān nṛṇām akhila-pāpa-kṣayaḥ*  
*yan-nāma sakṛc chravaṇāt*  
*pukkaśo ‘pi vimucyate saṁsārāt*

**O Lord! Nothing is impossible for you. Just from seeing you, all my sins have been destroyed. By hearing one of your names once, even the outcaste is delivered from the material world. (SB** [**6.16.44**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-6/16.44)**)**

*vedākṣarāṇi yāvanti paṭhitāni dvijātibhiḥ*  
*tāvanti hari-nāmāni kīrtitāni na saṁśayaḥ*

**There is no doubt that whatever is achieved by brāhmaṇas reciting the syllables of the Vedas is achieved by singing the names the Lord.**

*ṛg-vedo hi yajur-vedaḥ sāma-vedo ’py atharvaṇaḥ*  
*adhītās tena yenoktaṁ harir ity akṣara-dvayam*

**He who recites the two syllables “Hari” has studied the Ṛg, Yajur, Sāma and Atharva Vedas. (Viṣṇu-dharma)**

*mā ṛco mā yajus tāta mā sāma paṭha kiñcana*  
*govindeti harer nāma geyaṁ gāyasva nityaśaḥ*

**Do not chant the Ṛg, Yajur or Sāma Veda. The Lord’s name Govinda should be chanted. Chant it always. (Skanda Purāṇa)**

*avamanya ca ye yānti bhagavat-kīrtanaṁ narāḥ*  
*te yānti narakaṁ ghoraṁ tena pāpena karmaṇā*

**Persons who, disrespecting the chanting of the Lord’s name, leave that place where the name is chanted, go to a terrible hell because of that sinful act. (Padma Purāṇa)**

*etan nirvidyamānānām icchatām akuto-bhayam*  
*yogināṁ nṛpa nirṇītaṁ harer nāmānukīrtanam*

**O King! Continuous chanting of the name of the Lord has been prescribed for devotees, for those with desires for liberation and material elevation, and for those satisfied with the self. (SB** [**2.1.11**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-2/1.11)**)**

*oṁ āsya jānanto nāma cit viviktana*  
*mahas te viṣno sumatiṁ bhajāmahe*

**O Viṣṇu! Your name is spiritual and self-revealed. Knowing that name a little, not even completely, chanting impurely, we have attained fine intelligence about you, because that name oṁ is perfect in itself. (Ṛg Veda 1.156.3)**

*om ity etat brahmaṇo nediṣṭaṁ nāma yasmād uccāryamāṇa*  
*eva saṁsāra-bhayāt tārayati tasmād ucyate tāra*

**Oṁ is another name of Brahman. Since by chanting oṁ one is freed from the fear of saṁsāra, it is called tāra (liberator).**

*sakṛd uccāritaṁ yena harir ity akṣara-dvayam*  
*baddhaḥ parikaras tena mokṣāya gamanaṁ prati*

**Whoever chants the two syllables “Hari” only once is ready for attaining liberation. (Skanda Purāṇa)**

*tad aśma-sāraṁ hṛdayaṁ batedaṁ*  
*yad gṛhyamāṇair hari-nāma-dheyaiḥ*  
*na vikriyetātha yadā vikāro*  
*netre jalaṁ gātra-ruheṣu harṣaḥ*

**That heart which does not transform on hearing the names of the Lord, even though he shows tears in the eyes and hair standing on end, is made of iron. (SB** [**2.3.24**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-2/3.24)**)**

*madhura-madhuram etan maṅgalaṁ maṅgalānāṁ*  
*sakala-nigama-vallī-sat-phalaṁ cit-svarūpam*

**The name of Kṛṣṇa is sweeter than the sweetest, the most auspicious of all things auspicious, the highest fruit in the tree of all the Vedas, and is composed entirely of pure consciousness. (Padma Purāṇa)**

*sakṛd api parigītaṁ śraddhayā helayā vā*  
*bhṛguvara nara-mātraṁ tārayet kṛṣṇa-nāma*

**The name of Kṛṣṇa chanted with faith or without faith delivers all men. (Skanda Purāṇa)**

*gītvā ca mama nāmāni vicaren mama sannidhau*  
*iti bravīmi te satyaṁ krīto ’ham tasya cārjuna*

**By singing my names, a person will attain a position close to me. I make this promise. That person, O Arjuna, purchases me. (Ādi Purāṇa)**

*nāma cintāmaṇiḥ kṛṣṇaś caitanya-rasa-vigrahaḥ*  
*pūrṇaḥ śuddho nitya-mukto ’bhinnatvān nāma-nāminoh*

**The name of the Lord fulfills all desires like cintāmaṇi. It is the very form of Kṛṣṇa. It is full of consciousness and rasa. It is complete, pure, and eternally liberated. This is because of the non-difference of the name and Kṛṣṇa. (Padma Purāṇa)**

*ataḥ śrī-kṛṣṇa-nāmādi na bhaved grāhyaṁ indriyaiḥ*  
*sevonmukhe hi jihvādau svayam eva sphuraty adaḥ*

**Kṛṣṇa and his names cannot be grasped by the material senses, but when a person develops the tendency to accept the Lord’s name and form, Kṛṣṇa then spontaneously appears on the tongue and in the other senses. (Padma Purāṇa)**

By these statements of śruti, smṛti and tantra, it is understood that the name is endowed with all powers. In the sādhanas of karma and jñāna, rules for time, place and candidate are strong. But in remembering your name, these rules are not applicable. You have shown me great mercy concerning the name. However, by my misfortune, I have not developed attraction for your names. Misfortune here means nāmāparādha.

This should be said. The jīva averse to the Lord is bound to various material affairs in this world made of māyā. The jīva never tries to become favorable to the Lord. Thinking that the jīva will not gain eternal auspiciousness by rules of karma or jñāna, the Supreme Lord, out of immeasurable mercy, reveals his names, the very method of attaining him, in order to manifest in the jīva’s heart bhakti, which is the most essential function of hlādinī of his svarūpaśakti. Though one hears the name’s glories and utters those names, because of nāmāparādha, attraction to the names does not quickly develop in the jīva. Thus, persons with faith should always avoid the nāmāparādhas after hearing the name from the mouth of guru and, as much as he can, chant the names.

The offenses to the name are:

*satāṁ nindā nāmnaḥ paramam aparādhaṁ vitanute*  
*yataḥ khyātiṁ yātaṁ katham u sahate tad-vigarhām*

**One who criticizes devotees commits the greatest offense because how can the name tolerate criticism of those who have spread the glories of the name?**

*śivasya śrī-viṣṇor ya iha guṇa-nāmādi-sakalaṁ*  
*dhiyābhinnaṁ paśyet sa khalu hari-nāmāhita-karaḥ*

**He who sees the names and qualities of Śiva and Viṣṇu to be the same injures the Lord’s name.**

*guror avajñā śruti-śāstra-nindanam*  
*tathārtha-vādo hari-nāmni kalpanam*  
*nāmno balād yasya hi pāpa-buddhir*  
*na vidyate tasya yamair hi śuddhiḥ*

**Disrespecting the guru and criticizing the scriptures are offenses. It is an offense to think that the glories of the name are exaggerated or to give imaginary meanings to the name. One who commits sin on the strength of the name cannot be purified by following rules.**

*dharma-vrata-tyāga-hutādi-sarva-*  
*śubha-kriyā-sāmyam api pramādaḥ*  
*aśraddadhāne vimukhe ’py aśṛṇvati*  
*yaś copadeśaḥ śiva-nāmāparādhaḥ*

**Thinking that the chanting of the name is no greater than following dharma, practicing vratas and renunciation, or performing sacrifices or other pious acts is madness. Giving the instructions of the name to those without faith and with no interest in the Lord or the name, who actually do not hear the name, is an offense to the Lord’s name.**

*śrutvāpi nāma-māhātmye yaḥ prīti-rahito ’dhamaḥ*  
*ahaṁ-mamādi-paramo nāmni so ’py aparādha-kṛt*

**The low person who after hearing the glories of the name is still without devotion and is greatly entrenched in concepts of me and mine commits offense.**

Persons who are absorbed in the name do not have to attempt avoiding sin and gathering puṇya within karma since persons with faith are devoid of the qualification for karma. But if they commit nāmāparādhas, they must endeavor a lot to destroy those offenses. Tireless engagement in chanting Kṛṣṇa’s names frees their hearts of the offense naturally, by destroying the occasions for the offense.

*nāmāparādha-yuktānāṁ nāmāny eva haranty agham*  
*aviśrānta-prayuktāni tāny evārtha-karāṇi ca*

**For those who have committed offenses to the name, chanting the name incessantly will be effective in destroying that sin. (Padma Purāṇa)**

When attraction to the name arises from absence of nāmāparādhas, they can achieve all success. That is what is taught.

## 3. What is the method of nāma-sādhana?

*tṛṇādapi sunīcena taror api sahiṣṇunā*  
*amāninā mānadena kīrtanīyaḥ sadā hariḥ*

**Перевод:**

**One can chant the holy name of the Lord in a humble state of mind, thinking himself lower than the straw in the street. One should be more tolerant than the tree, devoid of all sense of false prestige and ready to offer all respects to others. In such a state of mind one can chant the holy name of the Lord constantly.**

**Комментарий:**

The characteristics are humility arising from detachment from material objects, and produced from chanting the name without offense, compassion ornamented with lack of envy, absence of false pride, and offering respects to all according to qualification.

Because of the appearance of Kṛṣṇa’s name, which is the embodiment of spiritual rasa, the devotee become detached from everything except spiritual rasa. The jīva is by nature a particle of consciousness and the servant of Kṛṣṇa. I no longer have any goals related to material objects. I am unfortunate to be situated under matter at present, because of the fault of being averse to Kṛṣṇa. As long as I do not become free of saṁsāra by Kṛṣṇa’s mercy, I will accept objects suitable for maintaining life, with yukta-vairāgya and with knowledge of a relationship with Kṛṣṇa. I must experience the prārabdha results in the form of suffering arising from lack, sickness, lamentation, old age, etc., and the happiness arising from good health, strength, knowledge, etc., while determining that since I have a spiritual svarūpa, I have no relationship with material objects. I will reside in the house or the forest, with complete humility, while saying, “O Kṛṣṇa! O Gauracandra! O Lord of my life! When will I attain pure servitude?”

Thinking oneself like grass, which is unconscious material substance, is not contrary to logic. Since my svarūpa is distorted, I do not have a positive identity. I am actually lower than grass.

The words “more tolerant than a tree” express the fact that the tree helps by giving shade and fruit, even for the person who cuts it down. The devotee of Kṛṣṇa helps friends and enemies showing mercy from a more elevated tendency. Mercy ornamented with lack of envy is the second characteristic of those chanting the name. The person dedicated to chanting without offense says, “O Lord! How will these jīvas associated with me attain attraction to your name? They are all blinded by māyā. They are harassed by the dualities of happiness and suffering related to sons, wife, wealth, victory and defeat. They are not detached from material objects full of anarthas. Or, bound by ropes of hope, they seek insignificant results of karma, which end in suffering and results of impersonal jñāna. How will they develop true vision?”

Saying this, he then sings loudly:

*harer nāma harer nāma harer nāmaiva kevalam*  
*kalau nāsty eva nāsty eva nāsty eva gatir anyathā*

**“In this age of quarrel and hypocrisy the only means of deliverance is chanting the holy name of the Lord. There is no other way. There is no other way. There is no other way.” (Cc. Ādi** [**17.21**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/cc-adi/17.21)**)**

The word amāninā, meaning without false pride, is the third characteristic. Pride arises from powers of yoga, powers of enjoyment, wealth, beauty, birth, varṇa, strength, fame, qualification, etc., related to the jīva’s gross and subtle bodies. This is all false pride since it is contrary to the svarūpa of the jīva. Therefore, being devoid of pride means being devoid of this false pride. One should chant the name, ornamented with the quality of tolerance, caused by identifying with grass and trees.

Devoid of pride in being a brāhmaṇa, etc., while remaining a householder, and devoid of pride in having the signs of a renunciate, while living in the forest, the devotee with his heart dedicated to Kṛṣṇa should chant Kṛṣṇa’s name.

Giving respect to all suitably (mānadā) is the fourth characteristic. Knowing all jīvas are servants of Kṛṣṇa he does not hate anyone or respond to hatred with hatred. He satisfies everyone with sweet words or actions auspicious for the universe. He respects everyone—brāhmaṇas, etc. who have attained qualification in the world, and Brahmā, Śiva and others in higher planets. He respects them with humility. He prays to them for bhakti. And he serves those who are pure devotees with full affection. Chanting Kṛṣṇa’s name while being ornamented with these four characteristics produces the highest spiritual goal. This is instructed by Mahāprabhu.

## 4. What is the desire of the sādhaka?

*na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīṁ kavitāṁ vā jagad-īśa kāmaye*  
*mama janmani janmanīśvare bhavatād bhaktir ahaitukī tvayi*

**Перевод:**

**“O Almighty Lord, I have no desire to accumulate wealth, nor to enjoy beautiful knowledge. Nor do I want any number of followers. What I want only is the causeless mercy of your devotional service in my life, birth after birth.”**

**Комментарий:**

First, those with faith hear the name from the mouth of guru. Then without offense they chant the name. Then they act with the four characteristics mentioned in the previous verse. But bhakti in the form of bhāva will not manifest without manifesting the pure svarūpa of bhakti as the essential function of hlādinī, devoid of relationship directly or indirectly with material bliss. Considering this, in two verses, the pure svarūpa of bhakti is made clear by listing the qualities that should be absent in sādhana bhakti of chanting the name.

If cultivation of Kṛṣṇa favorably, devoid of other desires which oppose bhakti, and not covered by karma and jñāna, does not occur, bhakti will be only bhaktyābhāsa. With a desire to avoid bhaktyābhāsa, Mahāprabhu teaches pure bhakti, by listing absence of certain qualities.

O Lord of the universe! I do not desire wealth, followers, or material knowledge. Dhanam refers to all types of wealth which give material happiness in this and the next world, the wealth of dharma fixed in Varṇāśrama, which give bliss to the senses in the subtle and gross body. This includes a wealth of kāma (sexual pleasures) as well. Sundarīm kavitām refers to ordinary knowledge, not the knowledge of kīrtana of nature of the Lord’s pastimes, according to tan-matir yayā: actual knowledge is knowing the Lord. (SB [4.29.49](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-4/29.49)) I do not request any of this, but birth after birth I request causeless bhakti to Kṛṣṇa, the lord of my life. Causeless bhakti means bhakti devoid of seeking other results, bhakti which takes shelter of the spiritual nature, and which is pure, the form of Kṛṣṇa’s bliss. It is also called kevalā, akiñcanā or amīśrā bhakti. Destruction of the material pains of birth and death is dependent on Kṛṣṇa’s desire and beyond the endeavors of the jīva. One should not pray for this destruction. Thus, the verse says, “birth after birth I will remain in saṁsāra.” When by Kṛṣṇa’s desire birth and death are destroyed, suffering will automatically end. What is the use of praying for this, since it is contrary to the method?

## 5. What is the svarūpa of the sādhaka?

*ayi nanda-tanuja kiṅkaraṁ patitaṁ māṁ viṣame bhavāmbudhau*  
*kṛpayā tava pāda-paṅkaja-sthita-dhūlī-sadṛśaṁ vicintaya*

**Перевод:**

**“My dear Lord, I am your eternal servant, but somehow or other I have fallen into the ocean of this material world. Kindly think of me as a speck of dust at your lotus feet.”**

**Комментарий:**

“Why should the sādhaka not be concerned about the sufferings of saṁsāra?” This verse answers. O son of Nanda! From analyzing things, I am your eternal servant. Now I have fallen in the ocean of disturbing material existence, by the fault of my own activities. Afflicted by lust, anger and many other enemies, I am afflicted by waves of false aspirations and false thoughts. I am moved about by strong wind of bad association. In this state, sometimes the grass clumps of karma, jñāna, yoga and tapas are seen to appear. Taking support of them, desiring to cross the ocean of material existence, I attain no result. Since they are useless, I continue to experience many types of suffering. I do not see any other boat to cross the ocean of material existence except your mercy. Understanding this, I have attained the boat of your name by the mercy of guru. Since you are merciful to the surrendered, think mercifully that I, surrendering while being cleansed of all faults, am like a particle of dust situated at your lotus feet. Being a particle stuck to your lotus feet I will not be separated from you. By this, it should be understood that the devotees must give up desires for attaining enjoyment and liberation--the results of karma and jñāna.

## 6. What are the external signs of perfection?

*nayanaṁ galad-aśru-dhārayā*  
*vadanaṁ gadgada-ruddhayā girā*  
*pulakair nicitaṁ vapuḥ kadā*  
*tava nāma-grahaṇe bhaviṣyati*

**Перевод:**

**My dear Lord, when will my eyes be beautified by filling with tears that constantly glide down as I chant your holy name? When will my voice falter and all the hairs on my body stand erect in transcendental happiness as I chant your holy name?**

**Комментарий:**

In the five verses discussed, first faith, then association of devotees, then bhajana consisting of hearing, chanting, remembering, serving the lotus feet, arcana, vandana, dāsya, sakhya and ātmā-nivedana, then after understanding one’s svarūpa, destruction of anarthas which are forms of ignorance, then niṣṭhā, then ruci, then āsakti, and then bhāva were described as the steps where the supreme dharma of the jīva’s svarūpa manifests, with the help of the essential function of hlādinī.

At the stage of bhāva the pure svarūpa of bhakti becomes firmly established. It is the sprout of prema in the form of rati. Among the various actions at the stage of sādhana such as hearing, chanting, etc., chanting becomes very strong at the stage of bhāva. This is the conclusion from the following:

*kṣāntir avyartha-kālatvaṁ viraktir māna-śūnyatā*  
*āśā-bandhaḥ samutkaṇṭhā nāma-gāne sadā ruciḥ*  
*āsaktis tad-guṇākhyāne prītis tad-vasati-sthale*  
*ity ādayo ’nubhāvāḥ syur jāta-bhāvāṅkure jane*

**The anubhāvas or characteristics of a person who has developed the bud of bhāva are as follows: tolerance, not wasting time, detachment from enjoyment, pridelessness, confidence in the Lord’s mercy, longing for the Lord, taste for chanting the name of the Lord, attachment to discussing about the Lord’s qualities, and attachment to living in the abode of the Lord. (BRS** [**1.3.25-26**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/rupa-brs1/1.3.25)**)**

The following shows that bhāva is the sprout of prema, or a very small portion of prema:

*śuddha-sattva-viśeṣātmā prema-sūryāṁśu-sāmya-bhāk*  
*rucibhiś citta-māsṛṇya-kṛd asau bhāva ucyate*

**That part of bhakti is called bhāva, whose essence is saṁvit and hlādinī śakti, which is one ray of the sun of prema which will soon rise in the heart, and which softens the heart with desires to meet, serve, and exchange love with the Lord. (BRS** [**1.3.1**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/rupa-brs1/1.3.1)**)**

Bhāva is the first stage of prema. The sattvika-bhāvas such as tears and hairs standing on end manifest in small quantity:

*premṇas tu prathamāvasthā bhāva ity abhidhīyate*  
*sāttvikāḥ svalpa-mātrāḥ syur atrāśru-pulakādayaḥ*

**The preliminary state of prema is called bhāva. There are a few sāttvika-bhāvas such as tears in the eyes and hairs standing on end. (BRS** [**1.3.2**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/rupa-brs1/1.3.2)**)**

*dhyāyaṁ dhyāyaṁ bhagavataḥ pādāmbuja-yugaṁ tadā*  
*īṣad-vikriyamāṇātmā sārdra-dṛṣtir abhūd asau*

**Continually meditating on the lotus feet of the Lord, Ambarīṣa developed slight transformations of heart and tears in his eyes. (Padma Purāṇa)**

The anubhāvas and sāttvika transformations of bhakti in the stage of prema bhāva described in the tantras and Purāṇas manifest to some extent during the stage of bhāva.

Anubhāvas are described as follows:

*nṛtyaṁ viluṭhitaṁ gītaṁ krośanaṁ tanu-moṭanam*  
*huṅkāro jṛmbhaṇaṁ śvāsa-bhūmā lokānapekṣitā*  
*lālā-sravo ’ṭṭahāsaś ca ghūrṇā-hikkādayo ’pi ca*

**External transformations known as anubhāvas are actions, such as dancing, rolling on the ground, singing, shouting, stretching the body, bellowing, yawning, breathing heavily, disregarding others, drooling, laughing loudly, whirling around, and hiccups. (BRS** [**2.2.2**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/rupa-brs1/2.2.2)**)**

Sāttvika-bhāvas are described as follows:

*te stambha-sveda-romāñcāḥ svara-bhedo ’tha vepathuḥ*  
*vaivarṇyam aśru pralaya ity aṣṭau sāttvikāḥ smṛtāḥ*

**The eight sāttvika bhāvas are paralysis, perspiration, hairs standing on end, choking of the voice, trembling, changing color, tears, and fainting. (BRS** [**2.3.16**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/rupa-brs1/2.3.16)**)**

Among them, dancing, singing, tears, hairs standing on end, breaking of the voice are particularly seen at the stage of bhāva. Thus, the crest jewel of teachers, Gauracandra speaks, describing this stage.

O Kṛṣṇa, son of Nanda! On chanting your name, when will streams of tears flow from my eyes? When will I transform my face, with choked words because of breaking of my voice? When will my body have its hairs standing on end? O Lord! May I develop these symptoms quickly on chanting your names.

## 7. What are the internal characteristics of perfection?

*yugāyitaṁ nimeṣeṇa cakṣuṣā prāvṛṣāyitam*  
*śūnyāyitaṁ jagat sarvaṁ govinda-viraheṇa me*

**Перевод:**

**“O Govinda! Feeling your separation, I am considering a moment to be like yuga or more. Tears are flowing from my eyes like torrents of rain, and I am feeling all vacant in the world in your absence.”**

**Комментарий:**

That bhakti in the form of rati at the stage of prema, which is filled with the sthāyi-bhāva, becomes bhakti-rasa with the assistance of vibhāvas, anubhāvas, sāttvika-bhāvas and vyabhicāri-bhāvas. In the state of prema, all the anubhāvas and sāttvika-bhāvas are seen in full form.

*samyaṅ-masṛṇita-svānto mamatvātiśayāṅkitaḥ*  
*bhāvaḥ sa eva sāndrātmā budhaiḥ premā nigadyate*

**When bhāva becomes extremely condensed, it is called prema by the learned. It softens the heart completely and produces extreme possessiveness of the Lord in the experiencer. (BRS** [**1.4.1**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/rupa-brs1/1.4.1)**)**

One should understand that prema is bhakti with deep bhāva arising from extreme possessiveness. By the different chief relationships of the āśraya and viśaya of bhakti-rasa, the five chief rasas: śānta, dāsya, sakhya, vātsalya and madhura arise. By secondary relationships seven secondary rasas arise:

*hāsyo ’dbhutas tathā vīraḥ karuṇo raudra ity api*  
*bhayānakaḥ sa bībhatsa iti gauṇaś ca saptadhā*

**There are seven secondary rasas: hāsya (humor), adbhuta (astonishment), vīra (enthusiasm), karuṇa (lamentation), raudra (anger), bhayānaka (fear) and bībhatsa (disgust).(BRS** [**2.5.116**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/rupa-brs1/2.5.116)**)**

Among the chief rasas,madhura is the best. The chief rasas successively become filled with prema, praṇaya, māna, sneha, rāga, anurāga and mahābhāva. When prīti has additional joy, the rati is perceived as śānta-rasa. In that state other things become insignificant. Prīti increased with the appearance of great possessiveness becomes prema, seen in dāsya-rasa. When prema manifests, that prīti does not admit any cause for being disrupted. Prema with familiarity becomes praṇaya seen in sakhya. When praṇaya manifests, reverence is absent. Praṇaya, having various bhāvas while manifesting apparent crookedness with great attachment to the beloved, is called māna. When māna appears, even the Lord experiences fear filled with prema when his lover becomes angry.

Prema in which the heart melts is called sneha. When this appears, profuse tears and other sāttvika-bhāvas manifest. One is not satiated by seeing the Lord. Though they are fully capable, some devotees experience unwanted fear for the Lord’s safety. These two characteristics are seen in vātsalya-rasa. When sneha becomes full of desire it is called rāga. When rāga manifests, one cannot tolerate even a moment’s separation. In meeting Kṛṣṇa, the greatest sorrow becomes happiness. In separation, it is the opposite.

When rāga experiences the object of love at every moment to be newer and newer, it becomes anurāga, with the feeling of everfreshness. When anurāga manifests, each of the lovers falls under the control of the other. This is prema-vaicitrya. One develops the desire to be born as a living entity related to the lover. In separation, internal manifestation of the Lord occurs. Anurāga, increasing in intensity, becomes madness with astonishment. This is called mahābhāva. When this occurs, in meeting, a kalpa seems like moment. In separation, a moment seems like a kalpa. Sāttvikabhāvas manifest in the most intense state for both parties. These are shown in Prīti-sandarbha. In Mahāprabhu’s teaching in this verse, a summary of all the bhāvas manifested in prema and particularly in madhura-rasa are seen.

The verse is easy to understand. The words govinda-virahena means vipralambha or separation.

*sa pūrva-rāgo mānaś ca pravāsādi-mayas tathā*  
*vipralambho bahu-vidho vidvadbhir iha kathyate*

**The wise describe many types of vipralambha, such as pūrvarāga, māna and pravāsa. (BRS** [**3.5.25**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/rupa-brs1/3.5.25)**)**

By the words of Mahāprabhu, the jīvas in the material world should always taste vipralamba in the form of pūrva-rāga, etc.

## 8. What is niṣṭhā in perfection?

*āśliṣya vā pāda-ratāṁ pinaṣṭu mām*  
*adarśanān marma-hatāṁ karotu vā*  
*yathā tathā vā vidadhātu lampaṭo*  
*mat-prāṇa-nāthas tu sa eva nāparaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**Lord Kṛṣṇa, who is the lover of many devotees (women), may embrace this fully surrendered maidservant or may trample me with his feet, or he may render me brokenhearted by not being present before me for a long duration of time, but still he is nothing less than the Absolute Lord of my heart.”**

**Комментарий:**

What is the behaviour of jīvas who have attained prema? That is explained in this verse.

He may embrace and give happiness to me, who am attracted to his feet. He may crush me under his feet. Or, he may cause me pain (marma-hatām) by not being present before me. The best jewel of lovers (lampaṭaḥ) may handle me in any way. He never becomes another’s (aparaḥ) since he is completely the lord of my life.

*martyo yadā tyakta-samasta-karmā*  
*niveditātmā vicikīrṣito me*  
*tadāmṛtatvaṁ pratipadyamāno*  
*mayātma-bhūyāya ca kalpate vai*

**When a person who gives up all karmas offers himself entirely to guru and eagerly desires to render service to me alone, he achieves freedom from death and, more important, is qualified to be an associate of mine in prema. (SB** [**11.29.34**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-11/29.34)**)**

According to this quality, Kṛṣṇa is the only life of the devotees at the stage of prema. The supreme quality of mutual attraction between the devotee and Kṛṣṇa shines at the stage of perfection, prema.

*yathā bhrāmyaty ayo brahman svayam ākarṣa-sannidhau*  
*tathā me bhidyate cetaś cakra-pāṇer yadṛcchayā*

**O brāhmaṇa! As iron moves automatically toward the magnet, my consciousness moves on its own towards Viṣṇu. (SB** [**7.5.14**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-7/5.14)**)**

The following should be considered. In the relationship between the conscious particle, the jīva, and the all-pervading consciousness, Kṛṣṇa, there is a natural quality, which mutually causes their relationship. That quality is almost lost in the jīva’s state of aversion to the Lord.

When the jīva develops his original nature then the previously perfect dharma between the jīva and Kṛṣṇa manifests again. This is like the attraction of purified iron next to a magnet. Manifesting that previously perfected quality, and nothing else, is the goal of dharma. There is no other result in cultivating the dharma for the jīva.

The Lord says:

*na pāraye ’haṁ niravadya-saṁyujāṁ*  
*sva-sādhu-kṛtyaṁ vibudhāyuṣāpi vaḥ*  
*yā mābhajan durjara-geha-śṛṅkhalāḥ*  
*saṁvṛścya tad vaḥ pratiyātu sādhunā*

**I am not able to repay my debt for your spotless service, even within a lifetime of Brahmā. Your connection with me is beyond reproach. You have worshiped me, cutting off all domestic ties, which are difficult to break. Therefore, please let your own glorious deeds be your compensation. (SB** [**10.32.33**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/32.33)**)**

This shows that the result of one’s attraction to Kṛṣṇa is Kṛṣṇa’s attraction to the devotee.

Kṛṣṇa shows that the pain on not seeing the Lord does not cause sorrow, but rather the highest bliss.

*evaṁ mad-arthojjhita-loka-veda*  
*svānām hi vo mayy anuvṛttaye ’balāḥ*  
*mayāparokṣaṁ bhajatā tirohitaṁ māsūyituṁ*  
*mārhatha tat priyaṁ priyāḥ*

**My dear girls, understanding that simply for my sake you had rejected the authority of worldly opinion, of the Vedas and of your relatives, I acted as I did only to increase your attachment to me. Even when I removed myself from your sight by suddenly disappearing, I never stopped loving you. Therefore, my beloved gopīs, please do not harbor any bad feelings toward me, your beloved. (SB** [**10.32.21**](https://vedarama.com/en/books/sb-10/32.21)**)**

Thus, the verse says, “he may embrace me.”

I have explained the glories of this aṣṭaka in brief. Kṛṣṇa thought, “What is the greatness of the love of Rādhā, the svarūpa-śakti? What sweetness does she taste in me? What is the happiness she experiences in that taste?” Desiring that taste, the svarūpa of the supreme tattva, Kṛṣṇa, in Goloka, a special place in Vaikuṇṭha which acts as the place for his eternal pastimes, manifests his pastimes suitable to her bhāva eternally in the form of Kṛṣṇacaitanya, the Lord’s form of great mercy, in Navadvīpa-dhāma. In śaka 1407, Kṛṣṇa manifested as the form of Caitanya, endowed with Rādhā’s complexion and sentiments, in the town of Navadvipa, like another Vṛndāvana within Gauḍadeśa, in the womb of Śacī, wife of Jagannātha Miśra, on the Phālguna full-moon.

During infancy, he showed the fickle nature of a child. During paugaṇḍa age, he studied. During kaiśora age, after marrying, he showed pastimes of married life. Then he began preaching. In this way he flooded Bengal with the highest bliss. By accepting dīkṣā from Īśvara Purī, best of sannyāsīs of the Madhva sampradāya, he taught the jīvas’ necessity of taking shelter of the feet of a genuine guru. At the age of twenty-four, he accepted sannyāsa from Keśava Bhāratī, a Śaṅkara sannyāsī, and gave up his house. Then on the pretext of going to tīrthas, he taught pure bhakti to the inhabitants of western Bengal, Orissa, and South India and established his own philosophy based on acintya-bhedābheda, the essence of all conclusions of the four Vaiṣṇava sampradāyas, after defeating philosophies of various philosophers. In this way he spent six years.

He then spent eighteen years living in Purī and spread his philosophy in the four directions by inspiring his devotee preachers. Served by many perfected devotees, he sometimes preached bhakti and sometimes tasted prema in the form of attaining his three goals (in relation to Rādhā). Through Śrī Rūpa, Sanātana, Raghunātha, Gopālabhaṭṭa, Jīva, Prabodhāṇanda, Svarūpa Dāmodara, Rāmānanda, Kavikarṇapura and others, by inspiring their hearts, he produced many scriptures to support his philosophy. He also wrote this aṣṭaka full of all conclusions and thus taught all jīvas who had qualification. He also tasted its conclusions with his intimate associates Rāmānanda and Svarūpa Dāmodara. All this is described in Caitanya-caritāmṛta.

By his conduct as a householder, Caitanya-candra taught those who were householders who were following sva-dharma. He taught all sannyāsīs by his conduct as a sannyāsī. And he taught all jīvas by his instructions. This is the infinite glory of Śikṣāṣṭaka.

*gaurāṅga-vaktrābja-vinisṛtaṁ śrī-śikṣāṣṭakaṁ ye prapaṭhanti bhaktyā*  
*te gaura-pādāsava-mugdha-cittāḥ majjanti premāmbu-nidhau murāreḥ*

**Those who recite with devotion the śikṣāṣṭaka which arose from the mouth of Gaurāṅga will sink in the ocean of prema for Kṛṣṇa, their hearts joyful with the nectar of Gaurāṅga’s feet.**

*śrī-gaur-candrasya catuḥśatābda ekādhike bhaktivinodakena*  
*sammodanākhyaṁ prabhu-vākya-bhāṣyaṁ kedāranāthena mayā praṇitam*

**This commentary on Caitanya’s words, called Sammodana-bhāṣya was written by Bhaktivinoda, Kedāranatha, in 401 Gaurābda.**

All fortunate people should wear on their necks this aṣṭaka, which, for pure devotees tasting madhura-rasa, is the essence of all the Vedas and which is the mahāvākya, since it arose from the Lord’s mouth. They should recite it and worship it at all times.

## About Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura

Śrīla Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura (1838-1914) is a prominent ācārya in Gauḍīya-vaiṣṇava sampradāya. He was a pioneering spiritual leader, a householder, a magistrate working in colonial India under the British rule, a prolific preacher, writer, and poet. He wrote volumes of books reintroducing the pure teachings of Lord Caitanya at a time when those teachings had practically become lost. He composed hundreds of devotional songs glorifying Kṛṣṇa to uplift the consciousness of the suffering people of this world. He corresponded with philosophers, theologians, leaders, scholars, and professors of his time and sent books, including The Life and Precepts of Lord Chaitanya, to university libraries in foreign countries, planting the seeds for a worldwide movement of Krishna consciousness. Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura discovered and excavated the birthplace of Lord Caitanya. Along with his devoted wife, Bhagavati Devī, he raised ten children, including the illustrious Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, who would become a great spiritual leader in his own time and the spiritual master of ISKCON's founder-ācārya, His Divine Grace A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupāda.
