## Introduction

Just as the supremacy of Lord Krsna is an object of timeless controversy, so is the authority of the scripture that depicts Him: the Srimad-Bhagavatam. No other Vedic personality and no other Purana has been so much attacked by opposing groups - a fact which itself evinces the superiority of both, for the scriptures often describe the particular envy the demons feel towards Lord Krsna. Such envy would be baseless if He were a common being and the Srimad-Bhagavatam a common book. Therefore sastra, history, logic, and Lord Krsna’s exalted devotees stand by Him to dismantle all bogus texts, false evidences, fallacies, and atheistic arguments. With this in mind, Srila Baladeva Vidyabhusana, known as the Gaudlya Vedanta Acarya, compiled the Siddhanta-darpana, an original treatise that presents the Srimad-Bhagavatam as the sound incarnation of the Lord and defeats several objections against its authenticity.

By its very characteristics, Srimad-Bhagavatam far surpasses all other Puranas. There are five basic topics that must be included in a Purana according to the following definition:

*sargas ca pratisargas ca vamso manvantarani ca*  
*vamsanucaritam ceti puranam panca-laksanam*

**“Creation, secondary creation, the dynasties, the ages of the several Manus, and the activities of the various dynasties - these are the five characteristics of a Purana.”**

Another definition states that a Purana, or more specifically, a Maha-purana, must have additional five topics:

*sargo 'syatha visargas ca vrtti-raksantarani ca*  
*vamso vamsanucaritam samstha hetur apasrayah*

**“O brahmana, a Purana is said to contain ten characteristic topics: the primary creation, the subsequent creation, the universal maintenance, the protection of all beings, the ages of the various Manus, the dynasties of kings and their activities, annihilation, motivation, and the supreme shelter. Some state that the Maha-puranas have these ten topics, while lesser Puranas have only five.” (Srimad-Bhagavatam, 12.7.9-10)**

All the subject matters can be distinctly found in one or more books of the Srimad-Bhagavatam. As it contains these ten main characteristics, it is therefore correctly defined as being a Maha-purana:

*sri-suka uvaca*  
*atra sargo visargas ca sthanam posanam utayah*  
*manvantaresanukatha nirodho muktir asrayah*

**“Sri Sukadeva GosvamI said: In the Srimad-Bhagavatam there are ten divisions of statements regarding the following: the creation of the universe, subcreation, planetary systems, protection by the Lord, the creative impetus, the change of Manus, the science of God, annihilation, liberation, and the supreme shelter.” (Srimad-Bhagavatam, 2.10.2)**

The Puranas are also classified as belonging to the mode of goodness (sattvika), passion (rajasika) or ignorance (tamasika). The Kurma Purana (2.43.49) and the Matsya Purana (53.68-69) state:

*sattvikesu puranesu mahatmyam adhikam hareh*  
*rajasesu ca mahatmyam adhikam brahmano viduh*  
*tadvad agnes ca mahatmyam tamasesu sivasya ca*

**“In the Puranas in the mode of goodness, the focus is the glories of Lord Hari; in those in the mode of passion, the focus is the glories of Lord Brahma; similarly, in those in the mode of ignorance, the focus is the glories of Lord Siva and Agni.”**

The Garuda Purana (3.1.52) confirms:

*visnoh puranam bhagavatam puranam sattvottamam garudam cahur aryah*

**“The Aryans declare that Visnu Purana, Bhagavata Purana and Garuda Purana are the best of the Puranas in the mode of goodness.”**

Wherever this topic is dealt with, the Srimad-Bhagavatam is described as a sattvika Purana.This is quite obvious, as Lord Visnu, the subject of the Srimad-Bhagavatam, is also described as the controller of the mode of goodness. The superiority of sattva-guna (goodness) over rajo-guna (passion) and tamo-guna (ignorance) is self-evident and it is superfluous to mention how it is praised by so many scriptures. Once this is understood, it is untenable to think that the Srimad-Bhagavatam counted among the Maha-puranas could be any other text than the one in goodness and praising Lord Visnu. No Purana named ‘Bhagavatam’ could possibly belong to the mode of goodness if it emphasizes the worship of any other deity than Visnu, for this would contradict His direct instruction:

*ye 'py anya-devata-bhakta yajante sraddhayanvitah*  
*te 'pi mam eva kaunteya yajanty avidhi-purvakam*  
*(Bhagavad-gita 9.23)*

**“Those who are devotees of other gods and who worship them with faith actually worship only Me, Î son of KuntI, but they do so in a wrong way.”**

Yet there is a very old controversy in which the Saktas claim that the Devi-Bhagavata Purana\[iii\]is the bona fide Bhagavatam among the eighteen Maha-puranas. This issue has led to innumerable arguments between the disputants. Perhaps the earliest known reference to this controversy is that of Srldhara SvamI (14th century), who in the very beginning of his commentary on the Srimad-Bhagavatam\[iv\]states that one should not doubt it as the authentic Bhagavatam, hinting that this controversy was quite widespread even in his time. Narasimha Vajapeyi (15thcentury) in his Nityacara-pradipa quotes the list of eighteen Maha-puranas and declares that one should not think that the name bhagavata comes from bhagavati,\[v\]another name of Devi. In addition, Vajapeyi mentions the view of Laksmldharathat Puranas such as the Kalika Purana, which are not in the list, are later productions. He further remarks that if one doubts the Srimad-Bhagavatam, which is so famous, then one may well doubt the other Puranas too. In the 15thcentury, Purusottama Tlrtha, a sannyasl in the line of Madhvacarya, wrote the Bhagavata-visaya-svarupa-sanka-nirasa-trayodasa, in which he presents thirteen arguments to prove the Vaisnava Bhagavatam as the legitimate Maha-purana. In the 16thcentury, Jiva GosvamI composed the Tattva-sandarbha, a pioneer work that presents the Srimad-Bhagavatam not only as a bona fide Maha-purana, but also the topmost scripture. Therein he refutes several objections against its authenticity. In the 17th century some peculiar papers were composed: defending the Vaisnava’s view, Ramasrama, son of the famous Bhattoji Diksita, wrote the Durjana-mukha-capetika (A Slap on the Face of the Wicked); to refute it, Kaslnatha Bhatta compiled the Durjana- mukha-maha-capetika (A Big Slap on the Face of the Wicked); and another response written by an anonymous author is called Durjana-mukha-padma-paduka (A Lotus like Shoe on the Face of the Wicked). The discussion goes on to the present day, and unfortunately for the Saktas, on the basis of the large amount of evidence available, the great majority of scholars agree that the Vaisnava Srimad-Bhagavatam is the legitimate Maha-purana. A few of these evidences are as follows.

The famous Bhagavata-mahatmya in the Padma Purana (Uttara-khanda, 195.29) describes:

*grantho'stadasa-sahasro dvadasa-skandha-samyutah*  
*pariksic-chuka-samvadah srimad-bhagavatabhidha*

**“The scripture named Srimad-Bhagavatam has 18,000 verses in twelve books and is a conversation between Sukadeva and King Parlksit.”**

As the Devi-Bhagavata has no conversation between Sukadeva and Parlksit, it is obvious that the Purana referred to here is the Vaisnava Bhagavatam. The same is also true in the case of the Bhagavata-mahatmya in the Skanda Purana, which is part of the Vaisnava-khanda and deals with Lord Krsna. The Agni Purana has a whole chapter (1.93) describing each book of the Bhagavatam which starts with the conversation between Sukadeva and Parlksit, and the main topics are accurately mentioned. The Padma Purana (Uttara-khanda, 193.3) corroborates:

*puranesu tu sarvesu srimad-bhagavatam param*  
*yatra pratipadam krsno giyate bahudharsibhih*

**“The Srimad-Bhagavatam, in whose every word Lord Krsna is praised in many ways by the sages, is the topmost among all Puranas.”**

As for the authorship of the Srimad-Bhagavatam, it is clear from the narration in its first book that it was composed by Vyasadeva under the instigation of Narada Muni. Its authorship was long ago falsely attributed to Vopadeva by unscrupulous persons in an attempt to taint its authority. Such imposition could impress only those who are indifferent to the tradition handed down through the disciplic succession and who lack a minimum knowledge of history. In his own books, Vopadeva gives sufficient information to let us understand that he lived in the 13lhcentury. Vopadeva states that his father was as a physician named Kesava,author of a book on medicine called Siddha-mantra on which Vopadeva wrote a commentary called Siddha-mantra-prakasa. In this book, Kesava mentions that he was honored by King Simharaja, also known as Singhana, who ruled froml210 to 1247. Moreover, some of Vopadeva’s works were composed at the request of and to satisfy Hemadri,a reputed scholar and author of Catur-varga-cintamani, who served as the prime minister of two kings in South India: King Mahadeva,who ruled from 1259 to 1271, and King Ramacandra Yadava of Devagiri,who ruled from 1271 to 1309. Hemadri seems to have been a close friend of Vopadeva, and the existing commentary on Mukta-phalam entitled Kaivalya-dipika is signed by him. So being, he is quite competent to state how many books Vopadeva wrote: “Vopadeva has many excellent compositions, such as ten treatises on grammar, nine on medicine, one wonderful book on astronomy, three on literature, and three expressing the essence of the Srimad-Bhagavatam.”\[xi\]Had Vopadeva in fact authored the Srimad-Bhagavatam, Hemadri would have certainly stated so.

Further evidence of the existence of the Srimad- Bhagavatam prior to Vopadeva appears from Al-Biruni (973-1048), a Muslim scholar who spent years doing research in India and wrote an elaborate book on Indologyin which he clearly mentions Vasudeva’s Bhagavata Purana among a list of eighteen Maha-puranas.

Similarly, King Vallala Sena, who ruled from 1160 to 1178, wrote the Dana-sagara in 1169,wherein he states that he will not quote the Bhagavata Purana because it does not contain any chapter dealing particularly with charity, the main topic of his book.By saying so, he obviously was not referring to the Devi- Bhagavata Purana, which has a whole chapter about charity in the ninth book (chapter 30).

We also know from Vopadeva’s writings and from history that he was a very learned brahmana and a great devotee of Lord Krsna, therefore it is illogical to assume that he would do such a dishonest act as faking a Purana. His veneration for the Snmad-Bhagavatam is expressed in the following words:

*vedah puranam kavyam ca prabhur mitram priyeva ca*  
*bodhayantiti hi prahus trivad bhagavatam punah*

**“The learned say that a Veda instructs like a master, a Purana instructs like a friend, and poetry instructs like a lover, but the Srxmad-Bhagavatam instructs like all three of them.”**

Even greater evidence showing the anachronism pointed above is Madhvacarya’s (1238-1317) commentary on the Srimad-Bhagavatam, named Bhagavata-tatparya-nirnaya. Even if somehow someone could prove that Vopadeva lived a century before Madhva, it would still not substantiate the claimed authorship. The Srimad-Bhagavatam was the only Purana commented by Madhva, who commented only on standard scriptures such as the Vedanta-sutras, Upanisads and Bhagavad-gita. In those days, even in a hundred years a newly composed text would not possibly be so widely accepted that Madhva would prefer to comment on it instead of the Visnu and Padma Puranas, which were largely quoted by Sankara and Ramanuja. In other words, the very fact that Madhva wrote a commentary proves that at his time Srimad-Bhagavatam was a well-known scripture accepted by most scholars.

As for the speculations regarding Ramanujacarya’s (10171137) silence about the Srimad-Bhagavatam, they are baseless. There are innumerable scriptures which were not quoted by him, and this does not even slightly affect their credibility. Ramanuja’s exegetics aimed at defeating Sankara’s arguments and relied on the same passages of the sruti and smrti quoted by him. Since Sankara did not refer to the Bhagavatam to support his philosophy, Ramanuja also did not. It was the custom in orthodox philosophical circles to refer to the sruti as primary evidence and to the smrti secondarily. In the case of the smrti, the texts most quoted were those more widely accepted in the society. The fact that the Bhagavatam might not have been a popular scripture at Sankara’s time (8thcentury) does not undermine its authority. In his Tattva-sandarbha, Jiva Gosvami mentions that many portions of the Vedas are lost. If eventually some of those original texts could be recovered, their credibility would be naturally contested for some time by scholars who never heard of them. Although it was previously known and commented on, it was between Madhvacarya (1238-1317) and Lord Caitanya Mahaprabhu (1486-1534) that the Srimad- Bhagavatam became largely popular all over India, being commented on by scholars from all corners. It then became a standard scripture quoted as authority on innumerable topics. It is remarkable that some scholars like B.N.K.Sharmabelieve that Ramanuja, Abhinava Gupta (10thcentury) and Gaudapada (7thcentury) actually quoted the Bhagavatam in their works, but their authenticity has been questioned by other scholars.

Apart from a good number of commentaries, Srila Baladeva Vidyabhusana wrote only a few philosophical books: Siddhanta-ratna and Prameya-ratnavali (both introductions to Govinda- bhasya), Siddhanta-darpana, Vedanta-syamantaka (also attributed to Radha-Damodara Gosvami) and Tattva-dipika. Siddhanta-darpana is a prakarana-grantha, a treatise briefly dealing with a specific philosophical topic. Comprising a total of 56 verses, the work aims at proving that the Srimad-Bhagavatam is a bona fide Maha-purana, and that being non-different from the Supreme Lord, it is eternal and faultless. The only commentary available was written by Nanda Misra, who, according to the Gaudvya Vaisnava Abhidhana, was Baladeva Vidyabhusana’s disciple. There is hardly any available information about him, but from his commentary on verse 5.4 we may assume that he was either originally from Varanasi or lived there for several years. In any case, it is evident that he was formally trained in the different branches of orthodox philosophical schools and was directly appointed for writing this commentary.

The book is divided in seven prabhas or rays, as follows:

- The First Ray refutes various Nastika arguments, as well as arguments put forward by the Mimamsakas and Vaisesikas, and proves that the Supreme Lord is the creator of the universe and that the sruti and smrti are his sound incarnation.
- The Second Ray refutes the idea that the sastras are human compositions and that if the sruti and the smrti are non-different, then the sudras should be prohibited to read the smrti.
- The Third Ray proves that the Srimad-Bhagavatam is one of the eighteen Maha-puranas by its characteristics and number of verses.
- The Fourth Ray proves that the Srimad-Bhagavatam is different from the Devi Purana and the Kalika Purana.
- The Fifth Ray refutes the doubts raised about the authenticity of the Srimad-Bhagavatam.
- The Sixth Ray refutes the idea that Vopadeva is the author of the Srimad-Bhagavatam.
- The Seventh Ray refutes the idea that the Srimad-Bhagavatam has only 332 chapters.

**The Author**

After Jiva Gosvami, Baladeva Vidyabhusana was the greatest philosopher who appeared in the Gaudlya line. His outstanding literary legacy remains unsurpassed to the present day. There are no available historical records about his early life, however, those in his disciplic succession claim that he was born in a village near Remuna, in Orissa. Most scholars believe that he was born either at the end of the 17thcentury or at the beginning of the 18thcentury, and we know from a few manuscripts that his father was named Gangadhara Manikya. From his own statement at the end of his Siddhanta-ratna we know that he was a staunch follower of Madhvacarya’s tattvavada. It is said that he strongly preached this philosophy all over India until he met Radha-Damodara in Jagannatha Puri and became deeply impressed by his exposition of the Sat-sandarbhas. Accepting him as his guru, he learned the philosophy of Lord Caitanya and received the name “Ekanti-Govinda dasa.”Radha-Damodara eventually sent him to Vrndavana to learn Srimad-Bhagavatam from Visvanatha CakravartI, who was then the leader of the Gaudlya community there and an exceptional scholar. In those days there was a dispute between the Ramanandis and the Gaudiyas regarding the method of worship of Govindadeva, the original deity of Rupa Gosvami. Govindadeva had been transferred from Vrndavana to the place which is now known as Jaipur to protect Him from the attacks of Aurangzeb. The Ramanandis allegedly complained to King Sawai Jai Singh, who ruled from 1699 to 1743, that the Gaudiyas did not seem to be connected to a bona fide sampradaya, that they were worshipping Govinda before worshipping Narayana, and that the worship of RadharanI was unprecedented and irregular. Upon hearing about the case, Visvanatha CakravartI decided to send Ekanti Govinda dasa to Jaipur to respond to the allegatioris. There he invited all the opponents to a debate in which he refuted their objections and established that the sampradaya of Lord Caitanya was coming from the sampradaya of Madhvacarya. Yet one objection remained: the Gaudiyas did not have a commentary on the Brahma-sutras. That being the case, Ekanti-Govinda dasa promised to present a full commentary very soon. Spending the day in intense prayer, at night GovindajI appeared in his dream and directed him to write the commentary, which was completed in a matter of days and became known as “Govinda-bhasya. Having established the authority of the Gaudlya sampradaya and having received the title “Vidyabhiisana” from the King, he went back to Vrndavana where he was appointed as the head of the Radha-Syamasundara temple, where he spent the rest of his life. At the end of the Aisvarya-kadambim we find the mention of the year saka 1701 (A.D. 1779). If the authorship of the book is correctly attributed to him, then Baladeva Vidyabhiisana departed from this world by the end of the 18thcentury.

## First Ray - Refutation of the atheistic systems

### Text 1

*pita parasaro yasya*  
*sukadevasya yah pita*  
*tam vyasam badari-vasam*  
*krsna-dvaipayanam bhaje*

**Перевод:**

**I worship the son of Parasara muni, Srila Krsna Dvaipayana Vyasa, who lives in Badarikasrama and is the father of Sukadeva Gosvami.**

**Комментарий:**

Purport by Nanda Misra:

*syamo’pi yah sruti-saroruha-bodha-raktah*  
*santo’pi yo’syati tamas-tatim antara-stham*  
*pratyak-padam disati yah paramam svagobhir*  
*vyasam tarn adbhuta-ravim saranam prapady*

**“I take shelter of Srila Vyasadeva, who is just like a wonderful sun showing the spiritual world to the conditioned souls by the rays of his transcendental knowledge. Although he is dark-complexioned, he becomes reddish by the contact with the lotus-like Vedic knowledge, and though very peaceful, he destroys the massive ignorance situated in the heart.”**

Those who have a poor fund of knowledge are nonbelievers (nastikas). They don’t accept the Vedas and the Supreme Lord described in them. Others, so-called believers, take to the logic-of-the-half-hen (arddha-kukkutl-nyaya).\[17\]In order to refute all these blasphemous individuals, the author composed this treatise called Siddhanta-darpana, and following the tradition of his predecessors,he begins with the above invocation expressing his devotion to Srila Vyasadeva. Here ‘to worship’ (bhaje) means to propitiate by falling at the feet, etc. By stating that he is the son of Parasara muni, it is indicated that he is an incarnation of the Lord,as it is said in the Visnu Purana (3.4.5):

*krsna-dvaipayanam vyasam viddhi narayanam prabhum*  
*ko’nyo hi bhuvi maitreya mahabharata-krd bhavet*

**“My dear Maitreya, you should know that Srila Krsna Dvaipayana Vyasa is Lord Narayana Himself. Who else on the earth could be the author of the Mahabharata?”**

By the statement that he is the father of Sukadeva Gosvami it is also indicated that Vyasadeva is instructing pure devotional services and that devotion to him brings to the worshiper the desired perfection.

### Text 2

*nityarh nivasatu hrdaye*

*niravadyo nirvrtiman*

**May Lord Murari, Whose transcendental form is pure consciousness, eternally reside within our hearts. By His mercy Gajendra, the king of the elephants, gave up his animal body and became one of the associates of the Lord.**

**Комментарий:**

Here the author recites an invocation in his own words. This verse can be explained in three different ways:

- (1) The word ‘caitanyatma’ means that Lord Murari (another name of Krsna) has a transcendental form consisting of pure consciousness. Gajendra is the king of the elephants, who was afflicted by a crocodile.He became purified (niravadyah), which means free from his animal condition, was liberated (nivrtiman), and assumed a transcendental body as an associate of the Lord in Vaikuntha.
- (2) Lord Murari is the annihilator of this contemptuous mundane life, and He has now appeared in a form (atma) known as Sri Caitanya. By His mercy, Maharaja Prataparudra, the King of Orissa (gajapatih), became free from all worldly passions (niravadyah) and attained pure love for Krsna (nivrtiman).
- (3) By the mercy of Rasika Murari,who was an unalloyed devotee of Sri Caitanya (caitanyatma)- in the fourth generation of acaryas before the author of this book, the king of elephants (gajapatih) received the name Gopala dasa, gave up his aggressive nature (niravadyah), and became jubilant upon engaging in the service of the Lord’s devotees.\[23\]

The first interpretation above is the one intended, while the other two are implied.

**As the One described by words, the Lord possesses multiple forms, such as Matsya avatara, Êurma avatara, etc. Similarly, the words describing Him also appear in many forms, such as the Rg Veda, etc.**

This verse explains how both the vacya and the vacaka possess innumerable forms. Just as the Supreme Lord (vacya) possesses unlimited forms such as Matsya, Kurma, etc., in the same way, the pranava (vakaca) also appears in many forms, such as the Rg, Sama, Yajur and Atharva Vedas, as well as the historical works, the Puranas, etc. The Gopala-tapani Upanisad (1.20) confirms:

*eko vasi sarvagah krsna idya eko 'pi san bahudha óî 'vabhati*

**"Lord Krsna is the worshipable, all-pervading Supreme Controller, and although He is one, He manifests Himself in many forms.”**

*ekaneka-svarupaya sthula-suksmatmane namah*

**“I offer my humble obeisances to Lord Visnu, whose transcendental form is both one and many, tangible and subtle, manifested and unmanifested, and Who is the cause of liberation.”**

In the Nrsimha-tapani Upanisad (1.3) it is stated:

*sarve vedah pranavadikah*

**“All the Vedas start with the pranava (the syllable Om).”**

Then a few lines down (2.1), it is explained that in the pranava, the letters ‘a,’ ‘u,’ ‘m,’ and the ardha-matra\[26\]are indeed the Rg, Yajur, Sama and Atharva Vedas:

etasya ha vai pranavasya ya purva matra prthivy akarah sa rgbhir rgvedo brahma vasavo gayatri garhapatyah sa samnah prathamah pado bhavati. dvitiyantariksam sa ukarah sa yajubhir yajurvedo visnu rudras tristub-daksinagnih sa samnah dvitiyah pado bhavati. trtiya dyauh sa makarah sa samabhih samavedo rudraditya jagaty ahavaniyah sa samnas trtiyah pado bhavati. yavasane'sya caturthy ardha-matra sa somaloka onkarah so'tharvanair mantrair atharva-vedah samvartako'gnir maruto virad eka rsir bhasvati sa samnas caturthah pado bhavati.

The second letter is ‘u.’ It is the sky, the Yajur Veda with its mantras, Lord Visnu, the Rudras, the tristubh meter and the daksina fire. It is the second quarter of the Sama Veda.

*evam va are’sya mahato bhutasya nisvasitam etad yad rg-vedo yajur-vedah samavedo’tharvangirasa itihasah puranam vidya upanisadah slokah sutmny anuvyakhyanani*

**“From the breath of the Supreme Lord emanated the** ***Rg*****, Yajur, Sama and Atharva Vedas, as well as the historical works, the Puranas, the different divisions of knowledge, and the Upanisads along with their verses, aphorisms and commentaries.”**

In the Vayu Purana, Suta Gosvami corroborates the fact that the historical works and Puranas are also Vedas:\[29\]

*itihasa-puranasya vaktaram samyag eva hi*

In the beginning, the Yajur Veda was the only Veda, which Srila Vyasadeva divided into four parts. In these appeared the sacrificial activities performed by four priests, by means of which Srila Vyasadeva arranged for the performance of sacrifice.**

### Text 6

*adyanta-rahitatvena*  
*dvayam nityam prakirtyate*  
*avirbhava-tirobhavau*  
*syatam asya yuge yuge*

**Перевод:**

**Because both the Supreme Lord and the words describing Him are eternally devoid of beginning and end, it is said that both of Them simply appear and disappear in every age.**

**Комментарий:**

Here it is stated that both the vacya and the vacaka are eternal. Beginning (adi) means birth, and end (anta) means annihilation. Being devoid of beginning and end, the Supreme Absolute Truth in the form of vacya and vacaka is considered eternal. In the Kaivalya Upanisad (6), it is stated:

*tam adi-madhyanta-vihinam ekam vibhum*

**“You should meditate on the unique Supreme Lord, Who is devoid of beginning, middle and end.”**

The Svetasvatara Upanisad (1.9) affirms:

*jhajhau dvav ajav isanisau*

**“Both the Supreme Lord and the living entities are never born, but the Lord is omniscient, while the living entities are covered by ignorance.”**

The Katha Upanisad (2.6.1) confirms:

*tad eva sukram tad brahma tad evamrtam ucyate*

**“The Supreme Lord is pure, the Supreme Brahman, He is indeed immortality.”**

The Visnu Purana (1.2.1/1.2.11) says:

*avikaraya suddhaya nityaya paramatmane*  
*sadaika-rupa-rupaya visnave sarva-jisnave*  
*apaksaya-vinasabhyam parinamarddhi-janmabhih*  
*varjitah sakyate vaktum yah sadastiti kevalam*

**“I offer my humble obeisances unto Lord Visnu, Who is eternal, pure, immutable and the all-pervading Supersoul manifesting always the very same form.”**

**“The Supreme Lord is free from birth, growth, transformation, decay and annihilation. It is possible to state that He simply exists eternally in His own transcendental nature.”**

All the above quotes corroborate that the vacya, the Supreme Lord, is eternal.

Similarly, there are many passages from the scriptures that confirm that the vacaka, the words describing God, are also eternal. In the *Rg* Veda (8.75.6), it is stated:

*tasmai nuñṁḍ abhidyave vaca virupa nityaya*

**“O Virupa, glorify the Lord with the eternal words of the Vedas.”**

In the Mahabharata (12,224.55), the eternity of the Vedas, the vacaka, is thus declared:

*anadi-nidhana nitya vag utsrsta svayambhuva*  
*adau veda-mayi divya yatah sarvah pravrttayah*

**"In the beginning of creation, the Supreme Lord emitted the eternal, beginningless, endless and transcendental words in the form of the Vedas, from which all other scriptures have come."**

Here someone may object: “We hear from the Atharvasikha Upanisad (3) that factually Lord Visnu is born:

*brahma-visnu-rudrendras te samprasuyante*

**“Lord Brahma, Lord Visnu, Lord Siva and Indra are all originated.”**

“Moreover, in the Mahabharata and other scriptures there are descriptions of the annihilation of His body. Similarly, being constituted by an agglomerate of words, the Vedas must inevitably have a creation and destruction, therefore how could they possibly be eternal?” In reply to this, the present verse says that the Lord and the words describing Him simply appear and disappear (avirbhava-tirobhavau) in this world. This Supreme Absolute Truth in the form of vacya and vacaka has no birth or annihilation, but just becomes manifested and unmanifested. The passage of the Upanisad quoted above must be understood in the sense that because Lord Visnu associates with the demigods who rule the universe, He is also counted along with them, just like a king who eventually may be in the association of thieves, but nevertheless is not one of them.This is the correct interpretation, for the scriptures thoroughly deny the idea that the Lord takes birth. It must also be understood that the annihilation of his body is something apparent just like the illusory display of a magician, for what is eternal can never possibly be annihilated. Later it will be explained that the Vedas are also never created nor annihilated.

### Text 7

*jagatah sapratikatvat*  
*karyatvam sarva-sammatam*

**Перевод:**

**All the Buddhists, logicians, etc. agree that the universe is an effect on account of its being constituted by parts.**

**Комментарий:**

Above it was shown to those who are believers (astikas) that according to the evidence of the sruti and smrti it is proved that the Supreme Lord and the Vedas are eternal. Now, the author takes to inference in order to- prove the same to those who are non-believers (nastikas) and decry the Vedas. The universe must have a creator, therefore the existence of God is inferred. In order to make this point, the author states in this verse that the universe (jagatah) is an effect (karyatvam), for it is constituted by parts (sapratikatvat). According to the Amara-kosa (2.6.70), the words ‘angam,’ ‘pratlkah’ and ‘vayavah’ are synonyms for ‘part’ or ‘limb.’ The reasoning here is this: The universe is an effect, for it is constituted by parts, just like a pitcher. If something is not constituted by parts, it cannot be considered an effect,as in the case of an atomic particle,or the sky. This conclusion is accepted (sarva-sammatam) by all Buddhists, logicians, etc. Being an effect, the universe could never be otherwise, which disproves the theory that the universe is eternal,as thought by those who became dull due to ritualistic activities.Their inference is this: “The universe is not an effect, for it is not possible that it was produced by someone’s mind. Even a great intellectual would not be able to produce a mountain, an ocean, the sky or the directions even mentally. Therefore, it is an established fact that the universe is beginningless and eternal. The reasoning that each day of Brahma is always preceded by another dayalso corroborates that the universe is beginningless.”

This view will be refuted in the following verses, in which it will be established that the creation of such universe is an easy task for God, Whose wishes are always accomplished.

### Text 8

*sanghatah paramanunam*  
*nastikair yah prakalpyate*  
*sa tu sthirasya samhantu*  
*asvikaran na sidhyati*

**Перевод:**

**Nastikas such as the Buddhists and the Jains hold the theory that the universe is created by an agglomeration of atoms, but this view is erroneous, for they do not accept the existence of an eternal being that mixes the atoms.**

**Комментарий:**

Here the author refutes other theories regarding the universe as being an effect. According to the Buddhist theory of momentariness known as ksanikavada, there are four kinds of atomic particles (paramanunam) of earthly, watery, fiery and airy natures, which mix themselves simultaneously, and their respective agglomeration (sanghatah) becomes known as earth, water, fire and air. According to the Jain theory of postulation known as syadvada, the atomic particles which cause the agglomeration of earth, etc. are not of four kinds, but are of a single nature, and by transforming themselves they assume the particular attributes to become each distinct element. The agglomeration of the elements as conceived by these two schools is not possible, for they do not accept (asvlkarat) the existence of an eternal (sthirasya), conscious being (samhantuh) who could accomplish such a combination. This subject matter is elaborately discussed in the second pada of the second chapter of the Brahma-sutras.

### Text 9

*pradhanasya na kartrtvam*  
*jadatvad eva sampratam*

**Перевод:**

**Pradhana cannot be the creator of the universe at any stage, for it is devoid of consciousness.**

**Комментарий:**

According to Sāṅkhya philosophy, the pradhana becomes conscious when it interacts with the reflection of the purusa, the all-pervading soul. This theory is refuted in this verse. The creation (kartrtvam) of the universe cannot be attributed to pradhana (pradhanasya), for it is unconscious (jadatvat) like wood, etc.It is seen in the world that a pitcher is made by a potter, who is endowed with attributes such as knowledge, willpower and effort. Pradhana, being devoid of such attributes, is just like wood and therefore cannot be the creator of the universe. If it is accepted that pradhana becomes conscious by the reflection of purusa, the soul, and only then becomes the creator, then the creatorship should indeed be attributed to purusa. A hot iron rod burns not because of the iron, but because of the fire.

### Text 10

*idrsasya na karta sya*  
*jivah sakter adarsanat*

**Перевод:**

**The living entities cannot be the creator of such a wonderful universe, for such power is not seen in them.**

**Комментарий:**

Some hold the view that the living entity, by means of adrsta,\[37\]is the creator of the universe. To set aside such a view, the author presents this verse. This universe is indeed such a wonderful (idrsasya) creation, whose structure is inconceivable, consisting in fourteen different planetary systems having many marvelous oceans and mountains, with uncountable souls acting and enjoying, and uncountable enjoyable objects and places. Living entities (jivah) such as ourselves cannot be the creator (karta) of such universe, for we don’t have such power. Otherwise, the living entities would be like God. Nor can adrsta be the power of creation, for the living entities have not acquired such adrsta. Similarly, the totality of adrsta of all living entities combined cannot be the means for creation, for there is no scriptural evidence to prove that such a combination ever happened. Thus, the argument that the living entity is the creator of the universe is insignificant.

### Text 11

*ato jnanadibhir dharmair*  
*visistas tribhir isvarah*  
*etasya jagatah karta*  
*sa nityah sad-akaranah*

**Перевод:**

**Therefore the eternal Supreme Lord, distinguished by three transcendental attributes - knowledge, will and activity - is the creator of this universe and is the ultimate cause of everything that exists, while He Himself has no cause.**

**Комментарий:**

Then, who is the creator of this universe? The author replies here in this verse. The atomic elements cannot be the cause of their own agglomeration, pradhana cannot be the cause of their transformations, and the living entities visibly have no power to create the universe. Therefore (atah), God (Isvarah) alone is the creator (karta) of this (etasya) universe (jagatah) - this must be unavoidably inferred by all of you. The reasoning is this: The universe possesses a creator, for it is constituted by parts, just like a pitcher. If it were not constituted by parts, as in the case of the atomic particles and the sky, it could be said that the universe was not created.This not being the case, God is its creator, for He alone, the omniscient Lord Whose wishes are always fulfilled, has the power to create the universe with all its atomic combinations in the forms of mountains, oceans, etc.

Someone may object: “The above inference proves that the universe has a creator, but it does not prove that attributes such as knowledge, will and activity accomplish the creation.” The author here replies that by the same inference it is also proved that the creator possesses attributes, for God is distinguished (visistah) by three (tribhih) transcendental attributes (dharmaih): knowledge, will and activity (jnanadibhih). By the above inference it is proved that the universe’s creator is God, and thus it is also proved that the creation is accomplished through the agency of three attributes accepted by Him, i.e., knowledge, will and activity, just as we see in ordinary life.

Another objection is raised: “God may be the creator of the universe, but He is not eternal. Being the creator of the universe, God must also be non-eternal like other creators such as Lord Brahma.” The author replies: God is eternal (sa nityah). Here the adjective ‘eternal’ is used to explain the cause.The reasoning here is that God is the creator because He is eternal and He remains existing without any other cause for His existence (sad-akaranah). The statement that God is eternal means that His existence has no end, just like the sky, for there is no cause behind it.On the contrary, something that is not devoid of a cause cannot be eternal. For example, consider a pitcher before and after being produced. Before being produced, a pitcher had no existence as far as there was no cause to create it, and once it is created, its existence has a cause - the potter, the clay, etc. Therefore a pitcher is not eternal, but God is, and thus He is the root cause of everything, as it will be explained more elaborately in text nineteen of this chapter.

There is a theory that God cannot be eternal, for whatever exists is momentary, such as a cloud. The above argument also defeats this inference. It is not sound reasoning to think that simply because something exists, it must be momentary, for eventually there could be an exception, and factually no one can claim to have analyzed every single object to prove it. Moreover, there is no definite evidence that on account of its existence, any object must be momentary, for we do not perceive such momentariness in the case of existing objects such as mountains, etc. These very arguments also defeat the idea that the Vedas are not eternal.

### Text 12

*nirdosesvara-vakyatvad*

**Перевод:**

**The Vedas are considered authoritative, for they were enunciated by the infallible Supreme Lord.**

**Комментарий:**

Having substantiated that the Supreme Lord is eternal and endowed with transcendental qualities such as knowledge, will and activity, the author now proceeds to explain the authoritativeness of the Vedas on account of their being enunciated by Him. The speech of common people is tainted by four defects: (1) bhrama, the tendency to misapprehend the real for the unreal or the unreal for the real, such as a rope for a snake; (2) pramada, the tendency to commit mistakes due to negligence or inattention; (3) karanapatava, imperfect senses which lead to erroneous perceptions; and (4) vipralipsa, the tendency to cheat others. The Supreme Lord, however, is devoid of these defects (nirdosesvara) and is the supreme authority, and thus it is concomitantly proved that the Vedas enunciated by Him (vakyatvat) are also faultless and authoritative (pramanyam), for they are not imperfect human compositions. The words spoken by a person endowed with these four defects are not authoritative, such as the Sāṅkhya philosophy of the atheist Kapila.The Rg Veda (10.90.09) declares:

*tasmad yajnat sarva-huta rcah samani jajnire*  
*chandamsi jajnire tasmad yajus tasmad ajayata*

**“The hymns of the Rg Veda, Sama Veda, Yajur Veda and all the Vedic meters emanated from the Supreme Lord in the original sacrifice.”**

The meaning is that although one may doubt that the Vedas are eternal because in this passage it is stated that they emanated from God, still it is out of the question to doubt their authoritativeness, for they were composed by the infallible and omniscient Lord and are therefore means for real knowledge. On the other hand, the words of common living entities, such as the atheist Kapila, are not authoritative, for they are subject to the abovementioned four defects.

Someone may object: “Lord Rsabhadevaand Lord Buddha are also incarnations of God, therefore their words must also be authoritative.” It is not so, because in their doctrines they are not accepted as supposed incarnations of God. It is inferred that God is eternal and endowed with transcendental qualities such as knowledge, will and activity, but in the Jain doctrine of postulation (syadvada) and the Buddhist doctrine of momentariness (ksanika-vijnanavada) there is no acceptance of an immovable supreme spirit, and thus, according to their own theories, they cannot be considered incarnations of God. Therefore, their words are not authoritative. Moreover, Lord Rsabhadeva did not actually teach the Jain philosophy of King Arhat, the promulgator of Jainism, nor was he ever seen by him. This unfortunate King Arhat, having heard from the common people about the bewildering activities of Lord Rsabhadeva, started himself to imitate them and thus led other unfortunate people to deviate from the real path of religious principles. In this connection, in the Siddhanta-ratna it is explained that God is not partial or unjust, for He awards to each individual the results of one’s own activities.As for Lord Buddha, he preached an atheistic philosophy under the pretext of non-violence in order to deviate the demons from the performance of sacrifices, but his instructions then did not even slightly represent his own view. This is the opinion of those who really know this subject matter. Consequently, as Lord Buddha covered his real identity and denied the Vedas with the purpose of deluding the demons, his instructions are not considered authoritative.

### Text 13

The above arguments, however, did not substantiate that God possesses a form and that the Vedas are eternal. Here the author proves both points by presenting the view of some of Lord Krsna’s devotees. It is seen in the world that a doer is someone endowed with knowledge, will and activity, and thus by inferring that God is the creator of the universe, the same inference also proves that He is the possessor of transcendental knowledge, will and activity through which He accomplishes the creation. Similarly, it is seen in daily life that a doer possesses a body through which he works, therefore it must be concluded that a creator must have a body, for we have no experience of anything being created without the means of a body, senses, etc.

Someone may object: “Without effort, a doer like a potter, merely by the will to work is not able to produce a pitcher, etc., for any action needs the help of repositories such as the body, etc. This is not the case for God, Who is capable of accomplishing anything by His mere will, and therefore does not require the help of a body, etc.” It is not so, for just as it is proved that in the performance of an action the doer must possess the attributes through which the action is accomplished, such as knowledge, will and work, similarly, it is directly and indirectly proved that he must also possess a body, senses, etc.It is seen that wherever there is a doer, such as a potter, etc., there is a body, senses, etc. This is not true in the case of liberated souls who do not possess a body, and thus are not doers of any material activity. Otherwise, if His body were material, then His other attributes such as knowledge would also be.

Yet another objection is raised: “God might have a body, but a single limited body cannot simultaneously act at all places as would be required for creating the universe.” This is also not good reasoning, for the eternal body of the Supreme Lord can be manifested simultaneously everywhere by the agency of His inconceivable energies. It is just like in the case of a particular species of life, for example, a cow. Although all cows belong to a single species of life, each and every cow shares the same nature. Similarly, God’s body is present everywhere in all His activities without any division.

This might appear to be a good argument for a moment, but the fact is that the Supreme Lord has the power of becoming invisible at will, just as the demigods and other beings have. This also replies to the argument that it is not a rule that there must be an embodied doer behind the creation of the universe, for we don’t see anyone causing the appearance of a sprout, etc.

There is yet another argument: “God enters a body created by the reactions of the living entities’ past activities just like a ghost, and after the creation of the universe, He gives it up. Since He has no other specific attributes besides knowledge, will and activity, He does not possess a constitutional original body, senses, etc.” Those who propose such ideas are fools, for there is no evidence to support such concoctions, while there is much evidence that God’s body is eternal. Therefore, by the principle that God is the possessor of transcendental attributes such as knowledge, will and activity, it is also proved that He has a transcendental body.

### Text 18

Nor can it be said that here there is a superimposition, for one may say sentences like ‘Mr. So-and-so is not a lion,’ but no one says sentences like ‘existence does not exist.’Nor can it be said that such sentences are naturally employed, although there is no other existence within existence, etc., for the concept expressed by them is exactly what is being defined here as visesa. The plural of the word ‘satam’ is ‘satani,’ but since it is used here in a dvandva compound, it keeps the singular ending. A plural in Sanskrit consists in at least three elements, and therefore to avoid the idea that any higher number could be meant here, the following maxim is applied: The Yajur Veda (24.20) prescribes that during the spring one should offer partridges in sacrifice (vasantaya kapinjalan alabhate). Here the plural is used (kapinjalan/partridges), but no specific amount is mentioned, so if one asks how many partridges should be offered, the answer is that only three are enough to fulfill the plural number required, and that any higher amount would be superfluous. This logic is called ‘kapinjalalambhana-nyaya.’ In Madhava’s Jaiminlya-nyaya-mala-vistara (11.10) it is stated: kapinjalesu tritvadya paksikas tritvam eva va \| adyo bahutva-samyan na tritvenaiva krtatvatah - “As far as the number of partridges is concerned, it is arbitrary to offer only three or more. As only three suffice to fulfill the injunction, there is no need to offer more than that.” Moreover, ‘dva-trirhsat-tri-satam’ cannot mean 332 for the simple reason that in a dvadva compound each word is counted individually and therefore ‘tri’ and ‘satam’ cannot make one compound within it. If they were to be interpreted as ‘tri-satani’ (300), then the compound would not be a dvandva, and to be grammatically correct, it would need to end as ‘tri-satl’ to agree with its subject, ‘vilasac-chakhah.’
