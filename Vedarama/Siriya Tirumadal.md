# Siriya Tirumadal

## 1. Karar varai

*kārār varaik kongai kaṇṇār kadal udukkaisiṟār sudarc cuttic cengaluḻip pērāṟṟu*

**Пословный перевод:**

kār Ar varai kongai – Having the divine mountains (of tirumālirunjōlai and tiruvēngadam), which are laden with clouds, as her bosoms; kaṇṇār kadal udukkai – Having the expansive ocean as her sari; sīr Ar sudar sutti – having sun, with its beautiful rays, as tilakam (pattern on the forehead), a decorative ornament; sem kaḻaluḻi pēr āṟu – the huge river (kāviri) which is reddish and muddled

**Перевод:**

**The description is about sri bhūmi pirātti.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

parakāla nāyagi (tirumangai āḻvār in feminine mood) would not turn back even when her mothers and friends, saying “one should not have this much of daring”, dissuade her from engaging with *madal.* However bhūmippirātti does not dissuade her. parakāla nāyagi wishes to celebrate bhūmippirātti for supporting her. Since she desires to speak about *kāma puruśārtam*  (the end goal of pure love),  the end benefit which she opted for, she has to explain about the different types of *puruśārtam* (the ultimate benefits). parakāla nāyagi has to speak about cētanas for whom these *puruśārtams* are meant. In order to talk about them, she has to talk about bhūmi (earth) on which they dwell and thus starts describing śrī bhūmippirātti who is the esteemed dēvatā (deity) for earth.

**kārār varaik kongai** –  This talks about the mountains of the lady, bhūmippirātti, as the bosoms. The phrase “*kār ārnda varai*” refers to mountains which are covered with dense clouds, thus implying tall mountains. Since the bosoms of noble women are well developed and lofty, āḻvār is describing the cloud-laden mountains as bosoms. Since they are laden with clouds, the peaks of the mountains are dark, just like the ends of bosoms. Since bosoms are two, which are the two mountains which are referred to? tirumangi āḻvār himself has mercifully said in his [periya tirumadal](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2019/08/periya-thirumadal-7/) “*tennan uyar poruppum vadamalayum ennum ivayē mulayā*” (the tall mountain of tirumālinjōlai in the south and the mountain (tiruvēngadam) in the north are like bosoms) . Thus, parakāla nāyagi is referring to these two mountains \[tirumālirunjōlai and tiruvēngadam\] as bhūmippirātti’s bosoms. For a noble lady such as bhūmippirātti, instead of comparing with very distinguished mountains such as mēru (a celestial mountain), why are these mountains considered as similes?  Since bosoms are a place of dwelling for the husband as brought out in pāsurams such as in [tiruppāvai 19](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2015/01/thiruppavai-19-kuththu-vilakkeriya/) “*nappinnai kongai mēl vaittu kidanda malar mārbā*” (one with soft flower-like chest who lay on nappinnai’s bosom), [mūnṟām tiruvandādi 3](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2018/02/munram-thiruvandhadhi-3/) “*malarāl tanatullān*” (he dwells on the bosom of śrīdēvi), and tirucanda viruttam 57 “*sangu tangu mun kai nangai kongai tangaluṟṟavan*” (one who dwells on the bosom of the lady who has bangles on her forearms)”, it is only apt to refer to tiruvēngadam and tirumālirunjōla malai which are the permanent residences of emperumān

**varaik kongai** *–* *Since* the bosoms of noble women are hard, mountains are used for comparison.

**kaṇṇār kadal** – An ocean is something that fills the eyes of the beholder, viz., it is an ocean which is apt to be seen. Alternatively, *kaṇ* also means “place” and so it can be construed as an ocean that is full of place.

**kaṇṇār kadal udukkai** – The very beautiful ocean is like a blue coloured sari for bhūmippirātti.  Since the ocean is expansive, it can be draped as *mundānai* (front portion of a sari) and *koycakam* (plaited folds of a sari) for bhūmippirātti.

**kadal udukkai** – Ocean will be soft and cool, lending to the softness of the divine form \[of bhūmippirātti\]

**sīrār sudar sutti** – sun with its beautiful rays is an ornament for bhūmippirātti.

The term *sudar* refers to sun’s rays and the term *siṟar sudar* refers to sun. *sirār* is an adjective for the rays – being full of beauty. Alternatively, if the term *sudar* refers to sun himself. the term *sīrār* would be an adjective for sun. Eminence for sun is that he is the decorative ornament on the forehead of pirātti, who herself is a decorative ornament for emperumān just as it is mentioned in [tiruvāimoḻi 10-3-5](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2019/11/thiruvaimozhi-10-3-5-pani-mozhi/) *“aṇimigu tāmaraik kaiyai andō adicciyōm talaimisai nī aṇiyāy*” (you should place your hand which is like an attractive and well decorated lotus flower, on the head of ours, servitors). Just as it is mentioned in vikramōrvaśīyam *“ābharaṇasyābharaṇam . . .vapustasyā:”* (it is a decorative ornament for the ornament of her divine form), aren’t pirāttimārs (all the divine consorts of emperumān) those who add beauty to ornaments!

**sengaluḻip pērāṟṟup pērāramārvil** – pirātti’s divine bosom is the huge river here. If there is a bosom, the area below it should have been decorated with the paste of sandalwood and vermilion. Is it like that, here? Just as it is mentioned in tiruccandaviruttam 54 *“malaittap piṟandiḻindu vandu numdu sandanam kulaittiṟitteṟindu kungamakkuḻambinōdu alaittoḻugu kāviri “* (river kāviri originates from (sahya) mountain, flows from there rapidly, pushing sandalwood trees, damages vermilion creepers, carrying particles of vermilion which are let out from those creepers, creating waves), is this (river) too not formed atop the mountain, coming down carrying sandal, vermilion etc from the mountain and as a result, is reddish in colour? If it were a bosom, it would be decorated with chains of pearls, precious stones etc. Is it like that, here? periya tirumoḻi 3-8-3 says *“. . . .āḷariyāl alaippuṇḍa yānai maruppum agilum aṇimuttum veṇsāmaraiyōdu ponni malaippaṇḍam maṇḍatt tiraiyundu . . .*” (waves of river kāviri carry materials such as tusks of elephants which have been thrown by courageous lions, fragrant eaglevood, beautiful pearls, white covries as well as various materials which grow on mountains); periya tirumoḻi 5-4-1 says *“sandinōdu maṇiyum koḻikkum punaṟkāviri”* (kāviri river which has abundance of sandalwood and gems); periya tirumoḻi 5-4-9 says *“vēyin muttum maṇiyum koṇarndār* *punaṟkāviri*” (river kāviri has distinguished pearls and gems); śrī rangarājastavam 1-21 says *“sanjaccāmara candra candana mahāmāṇikya muktōtkarān kāvēvi laharīkarair vidadatī paryēti “* (kāviri is flowing down rapidly, carrying through its waves which act like hands materials such as covry (fly-flapper), fragrant camphor, sandalwood trees, distinguished gems and pearls). As stated in the above references, by tirumangai āḻvār and parāśara bhattar, isn’t kāviri flowing from the mountains, carrying pearls and precious gems. Just as the bosom is expansive, so is the river, expansively covering the earth. Even though it has been said generically as a river, just as the bosom of a noble lady keeps embracing the husband always, it is kāviri, who embraces śrī ranganātan through her two tributaries, who is referred to as the river here.

## 2. Perara marvil

*pērāra mārvil perumā maḻaikkūndalnīrāra vēli nilamangai ennum ip                                          2*

**Пословный перевод:**

pēr āram mārvil – being one decorated with distinguished chains on her chest; peru mā maḻai kūndal – having huge, dark clouds as her tresses; āram nīr vēli – having the āvaraṇa jalam (water around the periphery of universe) as her protection; nila mangai ennum – being called as śrī bhūmippirātti

**Перевод:**

**perumā maḻaikkūndal – She \[pirātti\] has huge, dark clouds, as her tresses. The pageant of monsoon clouds, heavily laden with water, is her locks. For the noble woman, vouldn’t the tresses be dense, dark, decked with a variety of flowers, cool (comforting) for her husband, be such that even if the husband touches them once to diśevel them and withdraws, make all her distress to disappear? In the same way, this \[cloud\] also is huge and dark. indradanuś (rainbow) and lightning are like a variety of flowers. It is pleasing to the eyes and once it pours down (as rain), it removes all the distress.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**perumā maḻaikkūndal** – just as it is mentioned in [tiruvāimoḻi 7-7-9](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2018/10/thiruvaimozhi-7-7-9-kolginra-kolirulai/) “*koḷginṟa kōliruḷaic cugirnditta koḻunjuruḷin uḷkoṇḍa neela nannūl taḻaigol anṟu māyan kuḻal*” (emperumān’s divine tresses are unlike the darkest of  tresses with a streak of bluish hued tresses on the inside), this pirātti is an apt beauty of tresses for the *kuḻalaḻagar* (emperumān, who is described as one with beautiful tresses).

Noble women will have stringent protection. Does she \[this pirātti\] have such protection? āḻvār says that she does, in the next words. . .

**nīrāra vēli** – *āram nīr vēli* – The āvaraṇa jalam which is surrounding this universe is referred to as *āranīr*. Another opinion is that *āvaraṇa nīr* has mutated into *āranīr*. Thus, the pirātti has the protection of the huge moat of *āvaraṇa nīr*.  Alternatively, (*nīrāravēli*) *āram nīr vēli* – sandalwood is called as *āram*.  *nīr* is simplicity. Thus, *āranīr* refers to the chastity of this pirātti, who is like the cool sandalwood. *vēli* refers to limit or boundary. This pirātti has her chastity as the limit – a boundary for her greatness. Chastity for her is being *vāmanan maṇ* \[earth on which emperumān, as vāmana had trodden\] – existing exclusively for emperumān. Since her chastity is cool like sandalwood, it is clear that it does not cause any heartburns for her consort. When does chastity cause heartburns? When the lady is so beholden to her consort that she will not tolerate if her husband is affectionate towards other wives. But this pirātti is such that when emperumān is with periya pirātti (śrī mahālakśmi), she will be like garland or sandalwood and co-exist. This is the boundary for her greatness.

Who is such a pirātti?

**nilamangai** – She is bhūmi dēvi. *mangai* – one who is complete with all the qualities of a noble lady, mentioned earlier.

**nilamangai ennum ippār** – Intending to mention about *puruśārtams* (goals), prior to discussing about the people who follow these, āḻvār is speaking about the abode where they inhabit. Since bhūmippirātti is the attribute for such bhūmi, āḻvār speaks about her.

## 3. Paror solappatta

*pārōr solappatta mūnṟanṟē – am mūnṟum ārāyil tānē aṟam poruḷ inbam enṟu                                   3*

**Пословный перевод:**

ip pārōr – by the people living in this world; solappatta – the puruśārtams (end goals) mentioned by them; mūnṟu anṟē – aren’t they three?; am mūnṟum ārāyil – if one were to analyse as to which these three are; aṟam poruḷ inbam enṟu tānē – they are darmam (righteousness), artam (wealth) and kāmam (love)

**Перевод:**

**ippārōr solappatta mūnṟanṟē – aren’t the puruśārtams (end goals) attained by the people of this world three in count? solappatta – being mentioned (by the people of the world) is representative of being attained too.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**pārōr** – without any distinction between young and old, between distinguished and lowly, the end goals attained by the people of the world are three only.

**anṟē** – hence, āḻvār denies the existence, apart from these three, of a  fourth *puruśārtam, mōkśam*.

**am mūnṟum ārāyil tānē** – if the people of this world were to analyse the three *puruśārtams* of *aṟam* (righteousness), *poruḷ* (wealth) and *inbam* (love) which they mention, it is *kāmam* (love) which is dear to me, which alone is the end goal. They will know that the other two, viz. righteousness and wealth, are created only to complement attaining that *puruśārttam* of *kāmam*. The term *tān****E*** is to signify that it is only *kāmam* which alone is there in his divine mind.

What is the certainty after analysing . . . . .

**aṟam porul inbam enṟu** – only righteousness, wealth and love are the puruśārtams \[which are to be attained\]. While it has been earlier emphasised that there are only three, the purpose of mentioning them explicitly as *aṟam, poruḷ and inbām* is to exclude the possibility of adding *mōkśam* (liberation \[from samsāram\]) which some people may interpret as being one among the three.

## 4. Arar ivarrin

*ārār ivaṟṟin idaiyadanai eyduvār sīrār irugalaiyum eyduvar – cikkenamaṟṟu                  4*

**Пословный перевод:**

ivarṟṟinidai – among these three puruśārtams; adanai eyduvār Ar Ar – who all would attain the puruśārtam of kāmam (love); sīr Ar iru kalaiyum eyduvar – such people are considered to have attained the other two puruśārtams of darmam (righteousness) and arttam (wealth)

**Перевод:**

**ārār ivaṟṟin idai adanai eyduvār – In the tamiḻ literary work called nāladiyār, aṟattuppāl, chapter 12, the tamiḻ poet says “naduvaṇadu eyda iru talaiyum eydum” (among the three puruśārtams of aṟam, poruḷ and inbam, the person who has attained the puruśārtam in the middle, poruḷ, will also attain the first and last puruśārtams of aṟam and inbam since wealth is the means for attaining the other two). While āḻvār’s pāsuram is similar to this poem in terms of the words, it is different from that in its meaning, since the puruśārtam in āḻvār’s mind is not wealth. avaṟṟinidai – among these three puruśārtams, adanai – the kāma puruśārtam (which I attained), eyduvār – those who attain**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**sīrār iru kalaiyum eyduvar** – āḻvār’s opinion is that those who attain the middle one are considered to have attained the other two puruśārtams of righteousness and wealth.

**ārār ivaṟṟin idai adanai eyduvār** – Since he is using the term *Ar Ar* (whosoever), he says that even if they are from lowly birth or lowly conduct, for the singular reason of having attained the *kāma puruśārtam*, they are considered to be distinguished people. This is just like [tiruvāimoḻi pāsuram 3-7-1](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2016/09/thiruvaimozhi-3-7-1-payilum-sudaroli/) “*payilum tiruvudaiyār evarēlum avar kaṇḍīr payilum piṟappidaidōṟu emai āḷum paramarē*” (all those who are deeply engaged (with enjoying the beauty of emperumān and are carrying out kainkaryam to him), irrespective of their lowliness of birth or character, will be my lord in all the births to come).

**sīrār iru kalaiyum eyduvar** – the great people who attain the middle goal of love, are considered to have also attained the other two goals of righteousness and wealth, which are a part of the middle one. Those who attain the possibility (love) ought to have attained the means for this possibility (namely righteousness and wealth), already. If a person sets out to reach a place and reaches it, would it not be true that he would have crossed the intervening places too?

## 5. Aranum undenbar

*ārānum uṇḍenbār enbadudān aduvum ōrāmaiyanṟē ulagattār sollum sol                                 5 ōrāmai ām āṟu adu uraikkēn kēḷāmē kārār puravi ēḻ pūṇḍa tani āḻi                                     6*

**Пословный перевод:**

ārānum – those with deficiency in knowledge; cikkena maṟṟu uṇḍu enbār – will say that there is another stable puruśārtam (of mōkśam, liberation); enbadudān aduvum – that which is mentioned (by them); ulagattār sollum sol – the word and the deed which is mentioned by the worldly people; ōrāmai anṟē – aren’t these mentioned without analysing properly; adu ōrāmai Am āṟu uraikkēn – I will clarify (to you) that it has been mentioned without analysing; kēḷ āmē – Could you listen to that (by lending your ears)? (Please listen!); kār Ar puravi ēḻ pūṇḍa tani āḻi – one which traverses the skies, reined by seven horses and having one wheel

**Перевод:**

**cikkena maṟṟu ārānum uṇḍenbār enbadudan aduvum – some people, who are not distinguished, say that there is another puruśārtam**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**cikkena** – being firm

**maṟṟu uṇḍu** – there is another *puruśārtam* which is distinct from these three *puruśārtams*; these three are very lowly; not permanent. Giving joy, just as mentioned in tiruvāimoḻi 10-9-11 “*andamil pērinbam*” (unceasing bliss), this *puruśārtam, mōkśam,* is very distinguished, and, it is permanent – all these are useless talk \[in the opinion of āḻvār\].

**uṇḍu enbār enbadudān aduvum** – there is none who has seen it. Their words are similar to the sound emanating from the ocean \[meaningless\].

**enbadudān aduvum** – while they talk as if it were like a deluge of ocean, in reality it is just a mirage.

**ārānum** – \[some people\] this implies that even their names cannot be mentioned; and that they are without knowledge. Not attaining emperumān has taken her (āḻvār in feminine \[pirātti\] mood) to the edge of atheism.

Does the *puruśārtam, mōkśam*, really not exist?

**ōrāmaiyanṟē ulagattār sollum sol** – aren’t people of the world saying that it \[mōkśam\] exists without analysing properly? In reality, it doesn’t exist at all. How could it be said that they said it without analysing?

**ōrāmiyāmāṟu adu uraikkēn** – I \[pirātti\] will explain how it was said without analysing. I will describe how their words were uttered without analysing the worldly behaviour. With my words, I will destroy their authentic sources \[which they would show to prove the existence of mōkśam\] as well as the *puruśārtam* of *mōkśam*.

**kēḷāmē** – All that you need to do is to lend your ears \[to what I am going to say\]. If you listen, you would also cross over to my side.

āḻvār elaborates further, after they agree to listen to him. . . .

**kārār puravi** – horses which move amidst clouds. You could see the clouds. Look for horses amidst those clouds.

**kārār puravi** – Is it possible for horses to move amidst clouds?

**puravi ēḻ** – It seems that sun has reined in seven horses on his chariot. What a fable is this! They are not horses with an even count such as two or four or six. It seems it is an odd number of horses, seven; what a mischief! Should only seven horses be reined in? Couldn’t they rein in two or four or six or eight or any such \[even\] number of horses?

## 6. Terar niraikadiron

*tērār niṟai kadirōn maṇḍalattaik kīṇḍu pukku ārā amudam angeydi adilninṟum                                                   7 vārādu oḻivadonṟu uṇḍē adu niṟka*

**Пословный перевод:**

tēr Ar – one who is fitting well in the chariot; niṟai kadirōn – sun, with complete set of rays; maṇḍalattai kīṇḍu pukku – speeding away, piercing through the sun’s orbit; angu – paramapadam which is at a long distance; ārā amudam eydi – attaining the supreme being who is like the most enjoyable nectar; adil ninṟum vārādu oḻivadu onṟu uṇḍē – is it proper to say that one who goes to that paramapadam will remain there forever, without returning \[to samsāram\]?; adu niṟka – let that matter be, for the present

**Перевод:**

**tani āḻittēr – chariot with a single wheel. How strange! We have seen in this world, chariots which run only with two or four wheels. It seems that this chariot of sun’s is running in the sky, with one wheel. Could there be anything greater than this?**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**tērār niṟai kadirōn** – is it possible for this lowly jīvātmā, who cannot look, without any strain, at a burning lamp  with his eyes, to attain sun who is full of boiling rays?

**maṇḍalattai kīṇḍu pukku** – it seems that this jīvātmā, after vorśipping the supreme being with devotion here, with the strength of having that King among kings as his support, goes through the sun’s orbit. When he goes like that, is the pathway covered by branches of trees on either side just as it is in tiruvarangam (śrirangam) so that he will be protected from the heat?  Is it possible to go through the solar orbit with its cruel heat?

**ārā amudam angu eydi** – While tirunaṟaiyūr \[a divine abode\], where ārāvamudam (emperumān) has taken residence, and tiruvāyppādi, where he runs amok, are here, it seems that one goes there to attain *ārā amudam* (the eternal nectar). What ignorance!

**angu eydi** – while *madal*, the means to attain emperumān, and tirunaṟaiyūr, which nurtures and protects him, are here, what is to be attained there, and with what means?

**adil ninṟum vārādu oḻivadu onṟu uṇḍē** – it seems that once someone attains that place \[paramapadam\], he cannot return here. I believe that this is *mōkśam*! What a laughable matter! One who goes to *naragam* to suffer since he is under the control of *karma* due to lacking in strength to act on his own, has the independence to return to this place \[samsāram\] but one who goes \[to *mōkśam*\] on his own, to enjoy the comforts there, cannot return. Who wants such *mōkśam*?

**onṟu uṇḍē** – Does it appear to you that such a *puruśārtam, mōkśam*, which is unfit in all the ways, could exist? parakāla nāyagi (tirumangai āḻvār, in feminine mood) tells the people to analyse this.

**adu niṟka** – The implied meaning is – let that mōkśam exist or not exist. Why argue about it unnecessarily?

## 7. Erar muyal

*ērār muyal vittuk kākkaip pin pōvadē                              8 ērār iḷa mulaiyīr enṟanakku uṟṟadudān*

**Пословный перевод:**

ēr Ar muyal vittu – leaving aside the beautiful rabbit; kākkai pin pōvadē – will anyone go behind (the useless) crow?; ēr Ar iḷa mulaiyīr – Oh women with beautiful, youthful bosoms; en tanakku uṟṟadudān – whatever happened to me (I will tell you; listen)

**Перевод:**

**ērār muyal vittu kākkai pin pōvadē – even if it (mōkśam) were assumed to exist, leaving arcāvatāram (emperumān in idol form in various divine abodes) and searching for it is similar to leaving aside the great rabbit and chasing a crow. arcāvatāram is like a rabbit on the ground; paratvam (supreme state of emperumān, just as he is seen in paramapadam) is like a crow sitting on a branch. It is easy to catch a rabbit while it is difficult to catch a crow.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**ērār muyal – kākkai** – āḻvār wonders that while the rabbit is easy to trap and is tasty to eat, would anyone go with a bow \[and arrow\] in hand, leaving the rabbit aside and trying to catch a crow, which doesn’t get caught easily, without any certainty that it could be caught, and even if caught, not fit for being cooked and consumed! He is asking whether it is the apt action of a wise person. arcāvatāram is like rabbit – easy to approach and enjoyable while paratvam is like crow – difficult to approach and not at all enjoyable.

**ērār muyal** – rabbit with the distinction of being easy to approach and being enjoyable.

**ērār iḷa mulaiyīr** – Oh friends with beautiful, youthful bosoms! This implies that the purpose of having a beautiful form is to enjoy the benefit of such beauty here \[in samsāram\] itself.  Don’t people like us say, as in nācciyār tirumoḻi 13-9 “*kommai mulaigaḷ idar tīrak kōvindaṟkōr kuṟṟēval immaip piṟavi seyyāmē inippōyc ceyyum tavandān en*” (instead of carrying out *kainkaryam* in this world, with this form itself, thus satiating its desire, what is the purpose in carrying out penance in paramapadam after leaving this body and reaching that place?) Instead of carrying out service to śrī rāma, kriṣṇa et al during the time of their incarnations and later to their arcā forms, what is the purpose in getting any benefit with another form and in another place?

**endanakku uṟṟadudān** – She \[parakāla nāyagi\] says “Let all these remain. Listen to what happened to me due to the arrow of *māṟan* (manmadan, cupid) or the glance of tirumāḷ”, just as it was mentioned by āṇḍāḷ in nācciyār tirumoḻi 13-3 “*kanjaik kāynda karuvilli kadaikkaṇennum siṟaikkōlāl nenjūduruva ēvuṇḍu nilaiyum taḷarndu naivēnai*” (having got involved with the glance of the one who killed the demon kamsan and who has eye-brows like a bow, I became ruined and suffered).

*kārār kuḻal eduttuk katti kadir mulaiyai                     9 vārāra vīkki manimēgalai tirutti*

**Пословный перевод:**

kār Ar kuḻal eduttuk katti – taking up the tresses and tying them up (so that they will not be diśevelled); kadir mulaiyai – the resplendent bosoms; vār Ara vīkki – binding them tightly so that they would fill out the corset; maṇimēgalai tirutti – properly wearing the waist cord which is decorated with gems

**kār Ar kuḻal eduttuk katti – If he \[emperumān\] were to see me tying my dark tresses, he will engage in madal to attain me!**

**kār Ar kuḻal eduttuk katti** – tying the tresses which are smooth like a bluish carbuncle and dark in colour, and which will remove the ennui of the beholder.

**eduttuk katti** – since the strands are dense and grow as a tight pack due to her youthful state, and look beautiful when not tied into a bundle, they are difficult to tie into a bundle.

**eduttuk katti** – if she were to set out \[to engage with madal\] with her tresses in a diśevelled condition, without tying the them, it would appear as if she could chain him with her tresses.

**kadir mulaiyai** – bosom is radiant due to her youthful state.

**vārāra vīkki** – tying the bosoms in such a way that they would fill out the corset. Due to the contact with her bosoms, the corset appears fulfilled. īsn’t she one who bestows beauty on ornaments! Alternatively, tying the bosoms with the corset very tightly. Just as a wild elephant which runs amok and kills people is tamed and decorated with caparison, if her bosoms are not tied well with the corset, they will make him to lose his balance and fall down; he will be bewildered on looking at them.

**maṇimēgalai tirutti** – properly wearing the waist cord, decorated with gems. śrī rāmāyaṇam ayōdyā kāṇḍam 55-16 says “*tatra śrīyamivācintyām rāmō dāśarati: priyām* I *īśat samlajjamānām tāmadyārōpayata plavam* īī” (cakravati tirumagan \[śrī rāma, son of emperor daśarata\] helped sītā, who was most beautiful like śrīdēvi, beyond one’s imagination, a bit shy, and who was sweet for him, on to the raft). Just as sītā, feeling a little shy when śrī rāma touched her, displayed all her beauty to him when he helped her to climb on to the raft, this parakāla nāyagi too made herself to look beautiful for him so as to display all her beauty when he beholds her.

## 9. Arar ayil

*ārār ayilvēṟkaṇ anjanattin nīṟaṇindu                          10 sīrār seḻum pandu koṇḍu adiyā ninṟēn nān*

**Пословный перевод:**

Ar Ar ayil vēl kaṇ – in the eyes which are beautiful and sharp like a spear; anjanattin nīṟu aṇindu – applying powdered black pigment \[mixed in oil\]; sīr Ar seḻu pandu koṇḍu – taking a beautiful ball in the hand; nān adiyā ninṟēn – I was playing with it! (during that time)

**Перевод:**

**ārārayil vēṟkaṇ – Ar indicates beauty. ārār indicates beauty which is complete \[without any fault\]. ayil vēl – sharp spear. ārār ayil vēl kaṇ – eyes which are beautiful and sharp.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**anjanattin nīṟaṇindu** – is there a need to add beauty to her eyes! She is thus applying the pigment to bring in a sense of auspiciousness.

**ayil vēṟkaṇ anjanattin nīṟaṇindu** – just like adding a few drops of ghee on a weapon which has been sharpened using an abrasive stone so that it does not rust over time, the black pigment is applied to the sharp eyes.

Each activity of hers is apt for her femininity. If she had set out without decorating herself like this, he \[emperumān\] would have had to engage with *madal* after seeing the overall beauty of her limbs.

**kārār kuḻal eduttuk katti** – \[further explanation for three verses seen in the previous part, starting with this verse\] if she had set out, without tying her tresses, he would have been compelled to engage with madal, after being bewildered with its beauty.

**kadir mulaiyai vārāra vīkki** – if I had not hidden the beauty of my bosoms with the corset (just like decorating the forehead of an elephant with caparison), I would have made him suffer just like I did.

**maṇimēgalai tirutti** – if I had not worn the waist cord with its gems properly, as well as the dress for the waist, my state (of sufferance) would have happened to him.

**ārār ayil vēṟkaṇ nīṟaṇindu** – just like stopping the flood from flowing downstream by keeping sluice gates at the origin of flood, hiding the beauty of eyes, where the flood of beauty is originating, by applying pigment. If she had not done this, he could not have escaped from her sharp eyes.

**sīrār seḻum pandu** – *sīr* is śrī, wealth. For anyone, wealth will accrue only due to contact with śrīdēvi, this pirātti. Hence, the ball which was touched by her becomes a wealthy ball. Just as it is mentioned in [tiruppāvai 18](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2015/01/thiruppavai-18-undhu-madhakalirran/) “*pandār virali*” (one who has a ball in her hands in order to play with kriṣṇa), since it is a ball which never separates from her hand, there is no shortcoming in having contact with her.

**seḻum pandu** – beautiful ball. Alternatively, splendorous ball. Just as the elephant *śatrunjayan* was nurtured by śrī rāma and the cows were nurtured by kaṇṇan (kriṣṇa) in gōkulam, this ball became splendorous since it was always in contact with her.

**koṇḍadiyā ninṟēn** – I stood playing with the ball. However, the real intention was not to play, but to wait for that supreme entity.

## 10. Nirar kamalam

*nīrār kamalam pōl sengaṇmāl enṟoruvan                        11 pārōrgaḷellām magiḻap paṟai kaṟangac*

**Пословный перевод:**

nīr Ar kamalam pōl sem kaṇ – one who has reddish eyes similar to lotus, which is present in abundance in water bodies; māl enṟa oruvan – the puruśa (supreme entity) who is called as tirumāl; pārōrgaḷ ellām magiḻa – such that all the people of the world are happy; paṟai kaṟanga – with the musical instrument paṟai (a kind of drum) reverberating

**Перевод:**

**nīrār kamalam poḷ . . .  – the activities which he had undertaken to attain me were such that those which I had undertaken to attain him paled in comparison.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**nīrāi kamal poḷ sengaṇmāl** – tirumāḷ (consort of śrī mahālakśmi) whose eyes were reddish like the lotus flower which has blossomed in the water body; the lotus has filled up the water body fully.

**nīrār kamalam** – His eyes occupy his entire form, just like only one lotus blossomed, occupies the entire water body. Since it says *kamalam pōl sem kaṇ,* this implies that the eye has the characteristics of the lotus such as coolness, softness, fragrance, freshness and colour.

**māl** – one who makes the other person to be bewildered, by manifesting his eyes.

**enṟoruvan** – the word *oruvan* implies that he is referred to as a unique person who would make the beholder look dazed even if that person looks at him superficially. This also implies that he cannot be described that ‘he is like this’ \[viz. he cannot be estimated to be like anything\]. Just as this āḻvār himself had mentioned in his periya tirumoḻi 10-10-9 “*sārngamudaiya adigaḷai innār enṟu aṟiyēn*” (I am unable to know the person who has the divine bow *sārngam* in his divine hand), he is unable to know the person even after identifying him.

**pārōrgaḷ ellām magiḻa** – without any distinction between young and old, between great and lowly, he appeared in such a way that all the people within the circle of his vision desired him. Since the term *pārōrgaḷ ellām* has been used, it implies that he made happy not only the greatest and the lowliest, but even those in between these two categories of people.

**paṟai kaṟanga** –  just as people beat drums in order to forewarn others about an elephant in exultation is coming to harm them, drums are beaten here too, before his arrival, to inform women that he is coming to harm them.

**sīr Ar kudam iraṇḍu ēndi** – What distinction did these pots get! Even my bosoms did not get that distinction – she expresses her desire arising out of her disease.

**iraṇḍu** – he has taken two pots in order to give up the ball that I carried in my hand.

**ēndi** – he was changing the pots from his hands, just as he was playing the game of *ammānai* (juggling), making it appear that one pot was in the air and the other pot, handled deftly by his hand.

**seḻum teruvē** – a street which was not short either in length or in breadth. Oh, those who have the vanity that you have been born as women! Those who could protect your modesty, do so”

**ādum adu kaṇḍu** – looking at the beauty of that dance with pots

**ērār iḷa mulaiyār** – those who have beautiful, youthful bosoms. After witnessing that dance with pots, just as it is mentioned in nācciyār tirumoḻi 5-6 “*irudīkēsan valiseyya muttanna veṇmuṟuval seyya vāyum mulaiyum aḻagaḻindēn nān*” (emperumān who controls the senses of those who see him, hid himself, making it difficult to see him; due to this, I lost the beauty of my bright smile which is like a pearl, my reddish lips and my bosoms, thus becoming contorted) unlike me who lost the beauty of my bosoms, those who still retained the beauty of their bosoms after seeing him dance.

**ennaiyarum ellārum** – *em annai* = *ennai* – my mothers and other friends. Those mothers who told me “Don’t go anywhere near him” and prevented me from approaching him, became engrossed with his dance and told me too to come and witness it.

## 12. Varayo enrarkku

*vārāyō enṟārkkuc cenṟēn en val vinaiyāl kārār maṇiniṟamum kaivaḷaiyum kāṇēn ṇan                  14*

**Пословный перевод:**

vārāyō enṟārkku – To those who said “Hey! Will you not come to see?” (in agreement); en val vinaiyāl – due to my cruel sins; senṟēn – I went (with them to that place); kār Ar maṇi niṟamum – the colour of bluish gem (that I had attained earlier when I was together with him); kai vaḷaiyum – the bangles which were on my hand; nān kāṇēn – I lost

**Перевод:**

**ennaiyarum ellārum vārāyō enṟārkku – mothers who say good things and friends who say sweet things invited in unison “Hey loser! You lost all along by not seeing this (dance). At least now, come and witness it”**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**vārāy O** – the long syllable *O* at the end expresses sadness at having lost the opportunity.

**vārāyō** – Will you not come? Even though they invited only once, they are inviting as if they are inviting a person who is not coming even after repeated invitations.

**vārāy O** – The term *O* is used to call someone who is not in sight or who is at a far distance. These people are keeping an eye on him (emperumān) who is dancing in the street and calling me aloud, who is inside the house.

**senṟēn** – I went since they are mothers who are to be venerated, and I have to do as per their bidding.

**en valvinaiyāl senṟēn** – I went because of my huge sin. The term *val vinai* (cruel sin) refers to her *druda* *bhakti* (unśakeable devotion). Sin is that which is inimical to what is desired. The comfort in being united with emperumān and the absence of discomfort in being separated from him is what is desired by her; devotion is the enemy for being without the discomfort of being separated from him. When he is unattainable, isn’t the sorrow of being separated from him multiplied several times by her devotion? Hence, devotion which is inimical to her desire is referred to as a sin here.

She describes further how her sorrow of separation grew hugely. . . .

**kār Ar maṇi niṟamum kai vaḷaiyum kāṇēn nān** – I have lost the hue which I had after being united with him and the bangles which I wore because of being with him.

**kār Ar maṇi niṟamum** – the colour which matches the cloud, like a gemstone. Alternatively, darkiś hue like a gem. Elsewhere in this pāsuram, there is a verse “*kār Ar maṇi niṟak kaṇṇanūr viṇṇagaram*” (the divine town of tiruviṇṇaga which is the residence of kaṇṇan (kriṣṇa) who has the hue of dark gem), she gets his healthy hue as a result of being united with him.

**kār Ar maṇi niṟam** – how is it apt to describe her golden hued colour as dark colour? Since her mothers and friends have often heard her exclaiming “How beautiful is the colour of dark gem!” and since she often keeps thinking of his dark colour, she thinks that her colour too is now in line with his and speaks highly of that colour.

**nān** – I, who am capable of seeing him before being separated.

**nān kāṇēn** – I lost my colour and my bangles, after attaining all the sufferings on being separated from him, just as he had attained when he was attempting to get me.

āren’t these (colour and bangles) the ones which are always dear to her, just as it has been mentioned in tirunedundāṇḍagam 23 “*oḷivaḷaiyum māniṟamum koṇḍār*” (he stole my fair complexion and my bangles)?

## 14. Erar kilikkilavi

*ērār kiḷikkiḷavi emmanaidān vandennaic cīrār seḻum puḻudik kāppittuc cengurunjit        16 tārār naṟumālaic cāttaṟkut tān pinnum*

**Пословный перевод:**

ēr Ar kiḷi kiḷavi em anai kaṇḍu irangi – my mother, who has a speech which is similar to that of a beautiful parrot, took pity on me, seeing me; tān vandu – came on her own (near me); ennai – for me; sīr Ar seḻum puḻudikāppu ittu – protecting me by applying the powder from the divine feet of great, distinguished bhāgavatas; sem kurinji tār Ar naṟu māllai sāttaṟku – for śāstā, another deity, who wears the fragrant flowers of kurinji (a hilly flower); tān pinnum – she, who hadn’t so far \[done\]

**Перевод:**

**kiḷik kiḷavi – my mother, who was hard-hearted, without any pity on her basic nature, and who  told me harshly “Do not have any affection towards him”, now took pity on me and spoke to me, just as a beautiful parrot would, with words which were very sweet for the ears.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**ērār kiḷik kiḷavi** – this would also imply that her speech was sweeter than a parrot’s speech. The mother’s speech was so sweet that one could say that the speech of a parrot would sound like the song of a demon.

What is the reason for the hard-hearted mother to take pity like this?

**emmanai** – since she is my mother. Though the mother was castigating the daughter all along saying “Your pouring affection towards him is not correct”, she will not speak harshly when the existence of the daughter is threatened. Hence, she took pity.

**tān vandu ennai** – seeing my suffering, she came on her own, without my calling out to her.

**sīrār seḻum puḻudi k kāppittu** – Just as it is mentioned in [tiruvāimoḻi 4-6-6](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2017/03/thiruvaimozhi-4-6-6-thaniyum-pozhudhillai/) “*māyan tamaradi nīṟu koṇḍu aṇiyamuyallil maṟṟillai kāṇḍīr ivvaṇangukkē*” (if the soil from under the divine feet of devotees of emperumān were to be applied, there cannot be a better remedy than this), since she was born in a clan of śrīvaiṣṇavas, the mother applied the soil from the divine feet of devotees of emperumān. The term *seḻum puḻudi* would refer to the soil from under the divine feet of emperumān and the term *sīrār seḻum puḻudi* would refer to the soil from under the divine feet of devotees of emperumān.

**sem kurinji tārār naṟu mālai sāttaṟku** –  for śastā, a deity who wears garland with reddish coloured, fragrant, *kurinji* flowers (a hilly flower).

**sāttan** – śastā; a deity who is referred to as *iyanār* or *iyappan*.

## 15. Neradana onru

*nērādana onṟu nērndāḷ adanālum                                17 tīrāden sindainōy tīrāden pēduṟavu vārādu māmai adukaṇḍu maṟṟāngē                             18 ārānum mūdaṟiyum ammanaimār solluvār*

**Пословный перевод:**

nērādana onṟu nērndāḷ – she carried out an anjali (cupping the palms together as a mark of worship) which she had never carried out; adanālum en sindai nōy tīrādu – despite that, my mental illness has not been cured; en pēduṟavu tīrādu – my distress \[bewilderment\] too has not been cured; māmai vārādu – the colour of my form, which I lost, has not returned; āngē ārānum mūdu aṟiyum ammanaimār – some old women who were there, familiar with stories of old \[happenings of past\]; adu kaṇḍu – looking at my state; solluvār – started speaking (to provide means for overcoming my disease)

**Перевод:**

**tān pinnum nērādana onṟu nērndāḷ – after this bewilderment (at my condition) , as well as before, she (my mother), who had not performed anjali (cupping the palms together in worship) even at emperumān as a means of attaining a goal, performed that act towards this deity.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**pinnum** – during the period after bewilderment

**nērādana onṟu** –  when carrying out an *anjali* towards emperumān with the thought of using it as a means for attaining a goal, it would lead to destruction of one’s *svarūpam* (basic nature of servitude). She, who believes firmly in this, performed that *anjali* as the means of a goal \[well-being of her daughter\] towards this deity. However, since this has been done considering the status of her daughter, this will not be taken as a *dōśam* (fault). Due to their affection for śrī rāma, the people of ayōdyā fell at the feet of all Gods for his well-being; sītāpiirātti prayed to *dikpālakas* (protectors of the eight directions) to protect śrī rāma; *hanumān* said “*namōstu vācaspatayē*” (salutations to the lord of speech) when crossing the ocean; nammāḻvār said “*deyvangāḷ*” (calling out to various deities); āṇḍāḷ said “*unnaiyum umbiyaiyum toḻudēn*” (I vorśipped you and your brother). Distinguished ācāryas said that such bewilderment which happens in matters relating to emperumān and his devotees cannot be considered as *dōśam* and that these which are caused due to uncontrolled love \[towards emperumān\] are apt only \[for one’s basic nature\].

**adanālum tīrādu en sindai nōy** – my mental disease which was quite severe already, became more severe after my mother prayed to a lowly deity.

**tīrādu en pēduṟavu** – my distress too has not got eliminated.

**vārādu māmai** – my previous complexion has not returned; healthy complexion would occur only when united with one’s beloved. Since there is no possibility for that to occur, what is the reason for her saying this? She thought that looking at her condition, even complexion (an insentient entity) would not remain without coming to her aid; but since even that is not coming, she is expressing her sorrow.

**adu kaṇḍu maṟṟāngē ārānum mūdaṟiyum ammanaimār solluvār** – after looking at my condition, though not invited, and not known to me earlier, some old women, who were there, are saying . .

**adu kaṇḍu** – these old women took pity the moment they saw her condition.

**ārānum** – unknown people.

**mūdaṟiyum ammanaimār solluvār** – the old women, who knew lot of old matters, are saying…The vyākyātā (periyacāccān piḷḷai, commentator for this prabandam) quotes an incident \[quoted down generations\] to show how much these old women know. One old woman asked of another “Do you know indra?” to which the other old woman responded “I know of ninety eight indras and their grandfather, brahmā, and you are asking me as to whether I know indra. If you are willing to listen, I will tell you. Listen!” Some old women, who knew as much as these old women, know of the diseases which afflict women, the reason for such diseases and the remedy for such diseases, and are saying . . .

## 16. Paror solappadum

*pārōr solappadum kattuppaduttirēl                                 19ārānum meyppaduvan enṟār – adu kēttukkārār kuḻaṟkoṇḍai kattuvicci kattēṟi                             20*

**Пословный перевод:**

pārōr solappadum kattuppaduttir ēl ārānum meyppaduvan enṟār – they (old women) said “There is a practice called as fortune-telling \[predicting as to what will happen in future\], among the people. If you carry this out, whoever (had created this disease) would be revealed truthfully”; adu kēttu – hearing those words which they were saying; kār Ar kuḻal koṇḍai – one with black tresses; kattuvicci – a hilly tribal woman who is an expert in fortune-telling; kattēṟi – possessed by a divine power

**Перевод:**

**pārōr solappadum kattuppaduttirēl – The old women said “Without any distinction between the ignorant and the knowledgeable, all the people in the world will say ‘If you go through the process of fortune-telling, you would know the reason for the disease‘. Do that “.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

What is the benefit of carrying out this activity?

**ārānum meyppaduvan enṟār** – whosoever is torturing her, will be revealed. Even if it is kaṇṇa (kriṣṇa) who gives troubles to women but will not manifest himself, not knowing the sufferings of a woman, he would be revealed.

**adu kēttu** – The old women said “Go by fortune-telling”; the mothers and the friends \[of parakāla nāyagi\] acceded to that. At that moment, even though they had not invited her, the hilly tribal woman \[who is an expert in fortune-telling\], finding that they are willing to check with fortune-telling, came on her own. In the special meaning for this narration, the tribal woman is ācāryan (preceptor). Once the ācāryas come to know that people are willing to listen to matters relating to emperumān, they will fall head over heels, out of their mercy, and explain the difficult meanings to the ones who are desirous to learn. Prior to the time of emperumānār (bhagavad śrī rāmānujar), ācāryas will explain the subtle meanings of matters relating to emperumān, only to a distinguished few (two or four persons); even that, they will reveal only after examining the disciples deeply, for their basic nature, real intention to know etc. emperumānār instructed the meanings to those who, in his opinion, had the desire to know about emperumān. This fortune teller is similar to bhagavad rāmānujar.

**kār Ar kuḻal koṇḍai** – more than the words spoken by the fortune-teller who had black coloured tresses, her tresses were enjoyable for this mother. In the special meaning for this verse, ācāryan’s *dēhaguṇas* (qualities of physical form) are more apt than his *ātmaguṇas* (qualities of soul).

**kattuvicci** – kattuvicci is the one who would be able to point out as to which deity has possessed her.

**kattuvicci kattēṟi** – the *kattuvicci* became a *dēvāviśtai*. In other words, without yawning when one is feeling sleepy or stretching oneself due to laziness \[signs that the person is not in his/her control\], through the influence of deities, she will speak, without being conscious, by herself.

## 17. Sirar sulagil

*sīrār suḷagil sila nel piditteṟiyāvērā vidirvidirā mey silirāk kaimōvā                                  21*

**Пословный перевод:**

sīr Ar suḷagil sila nel pidittu eṟiyā – picking a few grains of paddy from a beautiful winnow and throwing them in the front; vērā – perspiring; vidir vidirā – breaking down; mey silirā – having goose bumps; kai mōvā – smelling the hand

**Перевод:**

**sīrār suḷagil – on a beautiful winnow. Beauty for winnow is that even as paddy and chaff are mixed together, it is capable of removing the chaff from the grain. īsn’t this winnow also going to remove the other deities, which are the chaff and dust, and to give only \[emperumān\] the grain as mentioned in periya tirumoḻi 11-8-8 “maṇiyē maṇimāṇikkamē” (Oh one who has the form of a bluish gem stone! Oh one who has the greatness of a distinguished gem such as carbuncle!) Thus, it is referred to as a beautiful winnow.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**sila nel pidittu eṟiyā** – the fortune teller is celebrating the paddy grains too saying “āren’t they too some \[distinguished\] grains!”

**vērā vidir vidirā mey silirā** – as soon as she started talking about *bhagavat viśayam* (matter relating  to emperumān) the \[fortune-teller’s\] body crumbled; *vērā* – started perspiring; *vidir vidirā* – the body trembled; *mey silirā* – goose bumps formed all over the body. Has it not been said in perumāḷ tirumoḻi 2-9 “*moyttuk kaṇpani sōra meygaḷ silirppa*” (with tears of joy flowing and the body having goose bumps), in [periya tiruvandādi 34](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2018/11/periya-thiruvandhadhi-34/) “*kālāḻum nenjaḻiyum kaṇ suḻalum*”(legs became weak, heart became decrepit and eyes were rolling) and in vēdam “*āhlāda śīta nētrāmbu: puḷakīkruta gātravān* I *sadā paraguṇāviśtō draśtavya: sarvadēhibi*: īī” (having tears of joy, goose-bumps all over the body, being constantly engaged with the qualities of paramātmā (supreme being), the devotee is apt to be seen by all sentient entities)! When śrī vālmīki bhagavān started asking nārada bhagavān “*kōnyasmin sāmpratam lōkē guṇavān . . .*” (who is the one with great qualities among the people in this world, now) and further asked “*maharishi! tvam samartōsi jñātum ēvam vidam naram*” (Oh great sage! You are the one capable of knowing such a person), śrī nārada bhagavān responded saying “*munē! vakśyāmi aham bhuddvā tair yukta: śruyatām nara:*” (Oh sage! I will analyse and tell you; please listen as to who is such a person with such great qualities . . .). And he starts saying immediately, about śrī rāma. While nārada maharishi knew about śrī rāma all along and started talking about him immediately, what is the meaning of his saying that he will analyse and tell? As he started revealing the qualities of śrī rāma one after another, after vālmīki bhagavān asked him, nārada became totally involved with the qualities of śrī rāma and was in a state of stupor for a short time. To convey the meaning that he would respond after coming out of that stupefied state, nārada bhagavān said that he will analyse and tell him. The state of nārada is much similar to that of nammāḻvār when he said in [tiruvāimoḻi 9-6-2](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2019/08/thiruvaimozhi-9-6-2-ninaidhorum/) “*ninaidoṟum sollundoṟum nenjidindugum*” (whenever I think and speak (about the qualities of emperumān), the heart melts and crumbles). In [nānmugan tiruvandādi 78](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2018/09/nanmugan-thiruvandhadhi-78/) “*kaṇḍu vaṇanginārkku ennāngol kāmanudal koṇḍa tavattāṟku umai uṇartta vaṇḍalambum tāralangal nīṇmudiyān than peyarē kēttirundu angāralangal ānamaiyāl āyndu*”, the state of a person steeped in devotion is described. *kāman udal koṇḍa tavattāṟku* – Siva, who burnt *kāman* (cupid) since kā*man* united Siva and pārvati and Siva considered that as a shortcoming in *kāman*. To him, *umai uṇartta* – when umā (pārvati) nudged her husband Siva and told him as to who is the one with such great divine names, due to the stupefaction of hearing the divine names \[of emperumān\], Siva forgot the order of teacher – disciple and considered himself as disciple and umā as the teacher, to listen to the divine names. After hearing the divine names, was he able to sustain himself without becoming crumbled? *angāralangal ānamaiyāl āyndu* – Siva became like a withered garland \[due to becoming very weak, on hearing the divine names of emperumān\]. *kaṇḍu vaṇanginārkku ennāngol* – If this is the state of Siva who only heard the divine names of emperumān and went into a state of stupor, what will be the state of one who sees emperumān?

**kai mōvā** – Due to the deep thoughts about emperumān, vouldn’t the hands of the fortune teller also be fragrant, just as it has been said in [tiruvāimoḻi 10-6-7](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2020/01/thiruvaimozhi-10-6-7-kurai-kazhalgal/) “*virai kuḻuvu naṟunduavam meyninṟu kamaḻum*” (the divine tuḷasi, which is very fragrant, stayed firmly on my body and emitted fragrance)? Thus, she smells her hand.

## 19. Karar tirumeni

*kārār tirumēni kāttināḷ – kaiyaduvum                             22sīrār valampuriyē enṟāḷ – tiruttuḻāy*

**Пословный перевод:**

kār Ar tirumēni kāttināḷ – she showed (his) divine form which is like monsoon cloud, as a simile; kaiyaduvum – present in his divine hand; sīr Ar valampuriyē enṟāḷ – she showed through gestures that it is only the beautiful conch, curling to the right hand side

**Перевод:**

**pērttēyum kārār tirumēni kāttināḷ – by pointing out to the clouds which remove one’s ennui, she showed his \[emperumān’s\] divine form. Farther down in this tirumadal, parakāla nāyagi is going to say “kārār tirumēni kaṇḍaduvē kāraṇamā”, giving the reason for her suffering.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**kaiyaduvum sirār valampuriyē enṟāḷ** – She said that he has the opulent *pāncajanyam* conch on his hand. *sīr Ar* – being wealthy and in contrast with the colour of the divine form. She pointed out to the conch which is like the moon rising amidst darkness and which is wealthy. Alternatively, *sīr Ar valampuri* – conch which is opulent. We can construe this as mentioning about the opulence of conch as said divinely by āṇḍāḷ in her nācciyār tirumoḻi 7-4 “*indiranum unnōdu selvattukkēlānē*” (even indra, who is famous for being very rich, cannot stand in front of you, in terms of wealth), nācciyār tirumoḻi 7-7, “*sangaraiyā unselvam sāla aḻagiyadē*” (Oh pāncajanyam who is the best among conces! Your opulence is indeed very distinguished) nācciyār tirumoḻi 7-9 “*selvap perum sangē*” (Oh conch with a distinguished wealth) and nācciyār tirumoḻi 7-8 “*uṇbadu sollil*” (if we have to speak about your food intake .  . . (you consume the nectar from the divine mouth of the one who measured all the worlds)).

What is the distinguished opulence of his conch? āṇḍāḷ describes it in her nācciyār tirumoḻi as follows: *uṇbadu sollil ulagaḷandān vāyamudam* – it consumes the nectarean fluid from the divine mouth of emperumān as he feeds it; *kaṇpadai koḷḷal kadalvaṇṇan kaittalattē* – stealing glances at emperumān’s divine form, the conch sleeps turning itself on the right and left, on his divine hand, just like a lady who has worn her beloved’s garland, lies under his embrace; *peṇpadaiyār unmēl perum pūsal sāṟṟuginṟār* – nectar from his \[emperumān’s\] mouth is a common property for all women. Women, who are like armies in their numbers in śrī madurā {maturā) and in śrī āyppādi (gōkulam) are screaming that their wealth is being stolen and appealing whether this is fair; *paṇpala seyginṟāy* – you are carrying out unlawful acts. You are not doing what devotees who are dear to emperumān will do. They will enjoy emperumān along with other devotees, and never on their own; *pāncajanniyamē* – Your activity and the quality which is the reason for that activity are incongruent with the place where you have reached. They are more congruent with the place where you were born \[*pāncajanyam* was born in the body of a demonic person, *pancasanan*\].

## 20. Tarar narumalai

*tārār naṟumālai katturaittāḷ katturaiyā                     23 nīrēdum anjēnmin num magaḷai nōy seydān ārānum allan aṟindēn avanai nān                                  24*

**Пословный перевод:**

tiru tuḻāy tār Ar naṟumālai katturaittāḷ – she showed through gestures, the fragrant garland made with divine tuḷasi flowers; katturaiyā – thus, having shown all these through gestures (she said through spoken words); nīr ēdum anjēlmin – “You do not have to be afraid”; num magaḷai nōy seydān – the one who had caused (this) disease for your daughter; ārānum allan – he is not some lowly deity; avanai nān aṟindēn – I have found out as to who is that person (who had hurt parakāla nāyagi)

**Перевод:**

**tiruttuḻāy tārār naṟumālai katturaittāḷ – to reveal that he is the lord of both  the realms, has he not donned the fragrant tuḷasi garland which emits fragrance in both material realm and spiritual realm? She showed that incomparable garland \[through gestures\]. Just as it has been mentioned in periya tirumoḻi 6-5-10 “tāmat tuḷaba nīṇmudi māyan” (the supreme being who has the garland made of divine tuḷasi on his long crown), is it not on this emperumān, who is often spoken of by her, that she is waging a madal?**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

All the identifications described until now (starting from *vērā vidirvidirā),* have been shown by the fortune-teller through gestures. Now, she starts speaking . . .

**katturaiyā** – After speaking within herself, she is now speaking aloud. This should be taken till the 53<sup>rd</sup> verse which will be mentioned further “*nummagaḷait tīrā nōy seydān ena uraittāḷ*”. All the verses in-between have been spoken by the hilly tribal woman.

**nīr ēdum anjēnmin** – Don’t be afraid that some lowly deity such as *karuppan, ,kāttēri, muniyan* et al has possessed her. No other deity has possessed her. There is no need for you to be fearful.

**num magaḷai nōy seydān ārānumallan** – *num magaḷai* – once she became ill, you have forgotten her; is it possible for a daughter born of you to be troubled by any lowly deity?

**ārānum allan** – neither a dog nor a jackal has pounced on her. Just as it has been mentioned in śrīmad bhāgavatam “*gōmāyuvan mrugapatēr balim*” (just as a jackal seizes the kill of a lion) and in nācciyār tirumoḻi 1-5 “*kānidait tirivadōr nari pugundu kadappadum mōppadum seyvadoppa*” (a jackal which roams in the forest cannot seize or smell the lofty offering made to celestial entities), she has not gone down to the level of being troubled by a lowly deity.

**aṟindēn avanai nān** – Unlike trying to ascertain through one’s efforts and failing, just as it is mentioned in taittirīya upaniśat ānandavalli ”*yatō vācō nivartantē*” (from whom the words return without knowing about that entity), I learnt about him with the knowledge bestowed upon, by him.

## 21. Kurar verkannir

*kūrār vēṟkaṇṇīr umakkaṟiyak kūṟugēnō ārāl ivvaiyam adiyaḷappu uṇḍadudān                                              25*

**Пословный перевод:**

kūr Ar vēl kaṇṇīr – Oh women, who have eyes as sharp as a spear!; umakku aṟiya kūṟugēnō – Shall I inform you (about him)?; i vaiyam ārāl adi aḷappu uṇḍadu – by whom this earth was measured

**Перевод:**

**kūrār vēṟkaṇṇīr – since she has said that she has found out as to who it is, all the people around her look at her sharply as well as expectantly as to what she is going to say. Looking at their sharp eyes, she says “Oh those with sharp eyes similar to spear!”**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**umakku aṟiya kūṟugēnō** – I will tell you, who are bewildered to such an extent that you are willing to fall at the feet of other deities such as iyanār, so that you would know as to who is troubling her. Will you listen?

They were eager to listen to her. Hence she continues further . . . . .

**ārāl ivvaiyam adiyaḷappu uṇḍadudān** – By whom was the entire expanse of this earth brought under his foot? Since she is using the term *ārāl* \[by whom\], just as it is mentioned in [tiruvāimoḻi 2-2-2](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2016/01/thiruvaimozhi-2-2-2-epavam-parame/) “*īpāvam seydaruḷāl aḷippār yār*” (who will protect due to mercy after saying that all the sins have been removed?) she is readying herself to give the answer as “*he*”. āḷavandār (one of our important preceptors) said the same in his stōtra ratnam ślōkams [12](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2016/12/sthothra-rathnam-12/),[13](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2016/12/sthothra-rathnam-13/) and [14](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2016/12/sthothra-rathnam-14/) “*ka: Sr:I śrīya: paramasatvasamāśraya: ka: ka: puṇḍarīka nayana: puruśōtama ka: . . . . .*” (who is the one providing opulence to śrī mahālakśmi herself? Who is the one having total goodness in him? Who has lotus like eyes? Who is the best among all ātmās? . . . . .)

**ivvaiyam** – Just as it says in [tiruvāimoḻi 4-4-1](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2017/02/thiruvaimozhi-4-4-1-mannai-irundhu/) “*maṇṇaiyirundu tuḻāvi vāmanan maṇṇiduvennum*”, the person will wallow in the expanse of the earth, saying that this is the soil from the footprint of vāmana and smell it. When śrī rāma went to sage viśvāmitra’s hermitage in order to protect his *yāgam* (ritual with *agni*, the god of fire), he asked the sage as to whose hermitage was the one which they passed. The sage replied that it was vāmana’s when he had incarnated and that he had carried out penance there. Immediately, śrī rāma said that he would remain there itself for that reason and smell that soil. Thus, just like nammāḻvār and viśvāmitran said that this is vāmana’s soil, the fortune teller also said “this world” to denote that it was the world which carries emperumān’s footprints.

**ārāl ivvaiyam adiyaḷappu uṇḍadudān** –  tiruviruttam 69 says “*buvaniyellām nīṟēṟṟu aḷanda nediya pirān aruḷāvidumē*” (will the one with a tall form, who measured all the worlds after accepting water \[from māvali\] not shower his mercy on you?) Since he had taken to alms in order to retrieve his worlds, will such a person let go of her \[parakāla nāyagi\]? Among *maṇṇāsai* (desire for land), *peṇṇāsai* (desire for lady) and *ponnāsai* (desire for gold), desire for lady is superior to desire for land. Thus, the meaning implied here is that if he could retrieve his land, would he let his lady suffer?

## 22. Aral ilangai

*ārāl ilangai podipodiyā vīḻndadu maṟṟu ārālē kanmāri kāttadudān  āḻi nīr                                        26*

ilangai ārāl podipodi A vīḻndadu – by whom was lankā blown to smithereens and destroyed?; ārālē kal māri kāttadu – by whom was the hailstorm prevented

**Перевод:**

**ārāl ilangai podi podiyā vīḻndadu – how was lankā, which had abundance of strength of boons and physical strength \[attributes of its king, rāvaṇa\], reduced to dust?**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

ārāl – Was lankā reduced to powder because of dēvas such as brahmā et al, who gave boons to rāvaṇa without knowing their own strengths, who escaped without getting their necks cut, who lost everything except the dresses which they wore, and who prayed to emperumān with bowed heads and the dress which they wore? Did not these dēvas struggle after granting boons to rāvaṇa et al, just because they felt overly pleased by the worship of rāvaṇa et al, not realising the effects of the boons which they granted?

**ārāl ilangai** – It is implied through this that he, who suffered greatly to retrieve one lady, as mentioned in nācciyār tirumoḻi 11-7 “*uṇṇādu uṟangādu oli kadalai ūdaṟuttu peṇṇākkai āppuṇḍu*” (crossing the agitating ocean, without food and sleep, due to his desire for his beloved), would not have allowed her (parakāla nāyagi) to suffer.

**maṟṟārālē kal māri kāttadudān** – since he was denied the food which was offered to him by the people of gōkulam, indra showered hailstorm out of anger. Who protected \[the people of gōkulam\] from that hailstorm? Did the cattle and the cattle-herds protect themselves? This implies that he will remove the difficulties which happen because of him. Thus he would protect her \[parakāla nāyaki’s\] too, since she suffered because of him.

## 23. Aral kadaindu

*ārāl kadaindidappattadu avan kāṇmin ūrānirai mēyttu ulagamellām uṇḍumiḻndum                         27*

**Пословный перевод:**

āḻi nīr – ocean; ārāl kadaindidappattadu – was agitated by whosoever; avan kāṇmin – the distinguished person who carried out all those activities; Ur A nirai mēyttu – grazing all the herds of cattle in that place; ulagu ellām uṇḍu umiḻndum – swallowing the worlds (during the time of deluge) and spitting them out (during the time of creation)

**Перевод:**

**āḻinīr ārāl kadaidnhidaip pattadu – śrī rāmāyaṇam yudda kāṇḍam 19-31 says “apramēyō mahōdadi:” (ocean cannot be estimated by anybody). By whom was the ocean, which cannot be estimated by anyone, agitated? By whom was the essence of the ocean, its nectar, removed during the process of agitation, just as mentioned in nācciyār tirumoḻi 10-9 “kadalē! kadalē! unnaik kadaindu kalakkuṟuttu udaluḷ pugundu ninṟu uṟalaṟuttavaṟku” (Oh ocean! Despite being his mattress \[emperumān rests on tiruppāṟkadal, kśīrābdi\], he churned you after entering you \[with a mountain\] and took away the nectar, which is your essence)? Was the ocean churned by dēvas (celestial entities) and asuras (demonic entities) who gave up after starting the process of churning? tirumaḻisaippirān said in tiruccanda viruttam 21 “aranganē! tanranga nīr kalangavanṟu kunṟusūḻ marangaḷ tēya mānilam kulunga māsuṇam sulāy nerunga nī kadaindapōdu ninṟa sūrar enseydār” (due to the curse of sage durvāsa, when the celestial entities lost their wealth and took refuge under you, pleading with you to give them back their wealth, you churned the ocean with a mountain in such a way that the trees around the ocean started getting eroded. When you churned the ocean with vāsuki, a snake and the earth trembled, what were the highly capable warriors from both the celestial entities’ side and demons’ side doing?) nammāḻvār too mercifully said in tiruvāimoḻi 7-1-7 “anṟu dēvāsurar vānga alai kadal aravamaḷāvi ōr kunṟam vaitta endāy” (on that day when the celestial and demonic entities got hurt when they tried to churn the ocean for taking out nectar, you had churned the ocean with \[mantara\] mountain as shaft and the snake vāsuki as rope). Did he not churn the ocean after the celestial and demonic entities gave up (due to their inability), and after taking several forms to support the mountain on the bottom, sides and top? The implied meaning here is that whether he, who had taken so much of efforts and suffered, for prayajōnāntaparars (those who expect other benefits) would let this lady \[parakāla nāyagi\] who is like śrīdēvi (śrī mahālakśmi) to suffer. Just as it is mentioned in periya tirumoḻi 6-1-2, is he not like the one who had  “viṇṇavar amuduṇa amudil varum peṇṇamudu uṇḍa emperumān” (he is my lord who coveted śrī mahālakśmi who came from the ocean after he had taken out the nectar from it and given it to celestial entities for them to eat)?**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**avan kāṇmin** – The opinion here is: Will your daughter suffer in separation from this entity or will she suffer in separation from any other deity?

**ūrānirai mēyttu** – grazing all the herds of cattle in the town. It cannot be said that he was grazing cattle from the entire village since the number of cattle in his house was limited. Just as it has been mentioned in [tiruppāvai 21](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2015/01/thiruppavai-21-erra-kalangal/) “*vaḷḷal perum pasukkaḷ āṟṟappadaittān maganē aṟivuṟāy*” (Oh the son of one who has plenty of cows! Wake up) and in periya tirumoḻi 10-4-3 “*ungaḷ tam āniraiyellām vandu pugutarumbōdu*” (when all your cattle enter), vasn’t the wealth of cattle, which they had, such that people would cast evil eyes? Despite that, since the purpose of his being born in the clan of covherds was only to protect cows, he will graze the cattle present in the entire town. When a capable person engages in the activity of protecting others, even if the number of entities to be protected were high, would he not demand more of such entities! In the same way, he \[the person identified by the fortune teller\] also would graze cattle from the entire town, apart from grazing his own cattle.

**ulagellām uṇḍumiḻndum** – keeping all the words which were caught in the deluge, inside his divine stomach so that they do not suffer; unable to bear seeing them lying there itself, releasing them appropriately.

**ulagellām uṇḍumiḻndum** – there is not even an atom which can remain without entering his divine stomach.

**ūrānirai mēyttu ulagellām uṇḍumiḻndum** – since he is doing all these effortlessly, grazing the cows in the town as well as swallowing and spitting out the worlds appear similar to her (the fortune teller).

## 24. Arada tanmaiyanay

*ārāda tanamaiyanāy āngorunāl āyppādi*

**Пословный перевод:**

āngu ārāda tanmaiyan āy – being dissatisfied, having to be in paramapadam; oru nāḷ – on one fine day; āyppadi – in tiruvāyppādi (śrī gōkulam)

**Перевод:**

**ārāda tanmaiyanāy – even though he is protecting all the entities to be protected, he thinks that he has not done anything. “These ātmavastus (soul entities) which are my possessions are totally dependent on me. I have allowed them to suffer during deluge etc, and then protected them. What is the benefit in this? Since I have protected them after a lengthy delay, am I not responsible for their suffering?” Just as a person robs another who has come to take rest under the care of the former and then suffers due to the guilt, he also suffers \[due to the delayed protection\]. When the riśis in śrī daṇḍakāraṇya forest showed their bodies which had been tormented by demons to śrī rāma, as mentioned in śrī rāmāyaṇam āraṇya kāṇḍam 6-16 “ēhi paśya śarīrāṇi” (Oh rāma! See our bodies) and pleaded with him, did you \[the riśis\] not feel ashamed as you felt “There is someone to protect us” and you were tormented because of that? While you had the power to protect yourselves, like a womb, as mentioned in śrī rāmāyaṇam āraṇya kāṇḍam 1-21 “garbha bhūtās tapōdanā:” (we are to be protected by you like a womb) due to the strength of your penance, you did not do that because of your svarūpam (being totally dependent on the supreme being) and put up with the trouble. But śrī rāma said, as in śrī rāmāyaṇam āraṇya kāṇḍam 10-9 “hrīrēśā hi mamātulā” (my shame was incomparable) that he could not put up with his shame since had let them down to the extent that the riśis had not only to suffer but that they had to reveal it too, themselves. In the same way, he too could not tolerate the situation here \[says the fortune-teller\].**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

From where did he feel that he could not tolerate it?

**āngu** – from the place where he did not have the need to protect from danger.

**oru nāḷ** – Let the activities of annihilation and creation which he carried out from *paramapadam*  (śrīvaikuṇṭam) remain. *paramapadam* is the place where as it is said in mahābhāratam mōkśa parvam 25-9 “*na kāla: tatravai prabhu*:” (time is not an entity in paramapadam). Hear the activities in the place where things change because of the effect of time.

**oru nāḷ** – even that was for just one day.

**āyppādi** – In a way, āyppādi (gōkulam) is similar to paramapadam. Since the nitya muktas (permanent residents of paramapadam as well as those who reached there after being liberated from samsāram) do not have any birth and are with distinguished demeanour, there is no question of great clan or great demeanour. In the same way, in āyppādi, since all are born in the lowly cattle-herd clan and follow the cattle to the forest and have food there, they too do not have distinction of great clan or great demeanour. Thus āyppādi is in line with paramapadam.

## 25. Sirar kalaiyalgul

*sīrār kalaiyalgul sīradic cenduvar vāy                            29*

**Пословный перевод:**

sīr Ar kalai algul sīr adi sem tuvar vāy – one having a waist draped with a beautiful sari, having beautiful legs, having deep reddish mouth

**Перевод:**

**sīr Ar kalai algul – one having a wealthy garment and waist. The opulence for yaśōdā’s sari is that the turmeric powder applied on kaṇṇa’s divine form, the black pigment applied on his eyes and the dust which settles on him when he goes out to play, get transferred on to her sari. This is its opulence. She would decorate and dress herself well so that kaṇṇa will not be angry. However, in a short time, whatever is on his divine form will settle down on her divine sari.  This is the eminence for her sari. The term sīr Ar is also an adjective for her waist. The eminence for the waist is that it gets broken due to carrying kaṇṇa often, just as it is mentioned in periyāḻvār tirumoḻi 1-2-9 “eduttukkoḷḷil marungaiyiṟuttidum” (if yaśōdā takes kaṇṇa on her waist, he tries to get down from there, breaking her waist in the bargain). In the olden days, the preceptors would fold and hang out their clothes which they had worn on the day of festival in śrīrangam temple, on the clothesline, saying “This is the dress which had vorśipped emperumān on the auspicious day of festival”. They will hang out the dress till the festival returns the next year and keep vorśipping it. The opinion here is that connection with emperumān gives eminence to \[yaśōdā’s\] sari too.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**sīr adi** – divine feet which are wealthy. If he \[kaṇṇa\] indulges in mischief while being carried by yaśōdā on her waist, will she not push him down? When she pushes him, he embraces \[her\] legs. Has it not been mercifully said in perumāḷ tirumoḻi 5-1 “*arisinattāl īnṟa tāy agaṟṟidinum maṟṟavaḷ aruḷ ninaindē aḻum kuḻavi aduvē poḷ irundēnē*” (I am in the state of that child which cries out for the mercy of its mother even if she pushes it away, out of anger) and in [stōtra ratnam 26](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2017/01/sthothra-rathnam-26/) “*ruśā niraśtō’pi śiśustanandayō na jātu mātuścaraṇou jihāsati*” (even if the suckling child is kicked out by the mother in anger, the child will not let go of that leg (which kicked it))?

**senduvar vāy** – once he embraces her legs, she will try to shake him away. As she tries to shake him away, he will repeatedly embrace her legs. Looking at that, she will smile with her reddish mouth.

## 26. Varar vanamulaiyal

*vārār vanamulaiyāḷ mattārap paṟṟikkoṇḍu*

**Пословный перевод:**

vār Ar vana mulaiyāḷ – yaśōdā, who has beautiful bosoms, draped in corset; mattu Ara paṟṟikkoṇḍu – holding on to the churning-staff firmly

**Перевод:**

**vārār vana mulaiyāḷ – once she smiles, he desires to suckle, knowing that her anger has evaporated. In order to ensure that children do not indulge in eating excessively, elders would give a coat of a medicine over them. In the same way, the bosoms are covered by a corset. It would appear to challenge the child “If possible, untie”**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**mattārap paṟṟikkoṇḍu** – Is it possible to pass time, by continuously engaging with him? One has to carry out household work too! Hence, she is churning curd. āren’t there servants to look after household work? Is it that their wealth is limited such that she has to carry out the household work herself? No it is not like that – one who has cows in plenty cannot have his wealth limited. The reason for her to churn the curd herself is – since the product of churning \[butter\]  is something which her pet child desires to eat, she does the churning herself. Moreover, since it is the work of her clan \[the clan of herds-people\], she engages with it, herself. Just because one is wealthy, is it proper to engage servants to carry out *sandyāvandanam* \[saluting dēvatās during dawn, noon and dust\]? Thus, this is the profession of her clan \[to engage with churning\].

**mattārap paṟṟikkoṇḍu** – this describes her status of holding on to the churning staff after she churns a couple of times. Once she starts churning, due to the act of having to churn, her divine body, due to its soft nature, trembles and she has to hold on to the churning staff.

## 27. Erar idai

*ērār idai nōva ettanaiyōr pōdumāyc                          29 cīrār tayir kadaindu veṇṇey tiraṇḍadanai vērār nudal madavāḷ vēṟōr kalattittu                           30*

**Пословный перевод:**

ērār idai nōva – such that the beautiful waist gets hurt; ettanai ōr pōdum āy – for a very long time; sīr Ar tayir kadaindu – churning the great curd; vēr Ar nudal madavāḷ – that yaśōdā, who was perspiring from her forehead (due to the effort of churning); vēṟu ōr kalattu ittu – keeping it in another vessel

**Перевод:**

**ērār idai nōva – such that her beautiful waist suffers. Beauty for her waist is that it is decorated with kaṇṇa all the time.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**ettanaiyōr pōdumāy**  – due to the softness of her divine form, yaśōdā, who starts to churn the curd once the eastern sky clears up, will not stop even after the sun sets \[she churns for such a long time\].

**sīr Ar tayir kadaindu** – churning the eminent curd. What is the eminence for curd? It has kaṇṇa’s spittle \[*eccil* in tamiḻ\] before it is churned; it has his spittle while being churned \[when he dips his hands in it and eats the just generated butter\] and even after it has become butter, after churning, it has his spittle. īsn’t the greatness of any entity arising out of its exclusive existence for him alone!

**veṇṇey tiraṇḍadanai** – butter which gathers \[after churning\]. Once curd is churned, butter will gather automatically. What is the purpose then in saying explicitly “Butter which gathers”? Due to his craving for butter, it would appear as if butter gathered on its own (without even the need for churning the curd). Is he not the one who is spoken of as in [tiruvāimoḻi 7-7-2](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2018/10/thiruvaimozhi-7-7-2-attiyum-thurriyum/) “*īttiya veṇṇey uṇḍān*” (ate the butter which had gathered)?

**vērār nudal madavāḷ** – due to the effort of churning the curd, yaśōdā perspires from her forehead. Just as the celestial entities, once they started churning the ocean, became tired, this lady \[yaśōdā\] too became tired after churning the curd, and as a result was perspiring from her forehead. This indicates how soft she is.

**madavāḷ** – one who is complete with the qualities of being a lady. She is without any shortcoming in the qualities of her ātmā too as mentioned through the sounds of the words in the verse *sīradic cenduvarvāy vārār vanamulayāḷ*.

**vēṟōr kalattittu** – If she keeps the butter in the vessel in which curd was churned, kaṇṇa would know from the smell that it is there. Hence she filled it in a vessel which is used for filing with other materials.

## 28. Narar uri

*nārār uṟiyēṟṟi nangamaiya vaittadanaip pōrār vēṟkaṇ madavāḷ pōndanaiyum poyyuṟakkam                               31 ōrādavan pōl uṟangi aṟivuṟṟut*

**Пословный перевод:**

nār uṟi ēṟṟi – keeping \[the butter obtained after churning curd\] on a pot suspended by a network of ropes (such that even a finger cannot enter it); nangu amaiya vaittadanai – kept in the most protected way; pōr Ar vēl kaṇ madavāḷ – that yaśōdā who has (sharp) eyes which are like a warring spear; pōm tanaiyum – until she went out; ōrādavan pōl poy uṟakkam uṟangi – feigning sleep as if he knew nothing; aṟivu uṟṟu – waking up (soon after she left)

**Перевод:**

**nār Ar uṟi ēṟṟi – it is an uṟi \[a network of ropes suspended (from the ceiling in a room) to keep pots and other vessels out of reach of rodents etc\] which is full of strings / ropes. The strings were packed so tightly that one cannot insert even a finger through them.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**ēṟṟi** – just as one places the facade stone atop a *gōpuram* (temple tower) with lot of effort, yaśōdā placed the *uṟi*  with lot of effort.

**nangamaiya vaittadanai** – she kept the butter well protected in a pot \[in that *uṟi*) in such a way that if thieves enter to steal the butter, they will be trapped by the strings

**nangu vaittadanai** – she kept the butter in such a way that the butter that he would desire to eat was out of his reach. The fortune teller is despising yaśōdā, wondering if she is a mother!

**pōr Ar veṟkaṇ madavāḷ** – one who has eyes which engage in a war-like situation by darting in all directions. Even though she has kept the butter safely, if he (kaṇṇa) sees it, the butter cannot escape. Hence yaśōdā keeps looking in all directions before keeping the butter in the *uṟi*.

**pōndanaiyum** – until she left that place. Just as a day appears to his devotees, who are unable to attain him, to be 1000 *kalpa* (one kalpa is daytime in the life of brahmā) as mentioned in [tiruvāimoḻi 10-3-1](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2019/10/thiruvaimozhi-10-3-1-vey-maru-thol-inai/) “*oru pagal āyiram ūḻiyālō*” (the daytime when kaṇṇa goes to forest to graze the cows appears to be thousands of yugas to the covherd girls), each passing moment when he is unable to grab the material which has been touched by his devotees appears to be a thousand *kalpas.*

**pōndanaiyum** – since he is capable of playing a trick on her to steal the butter which he likes a lot and which she has kept in the pot, why is he waiting for her to move away from that place? Is it because of any lesser interest now in butter? The reason is that he has already stolen and eaten a lot of butter from several other places. Since he has already eaten plenty of butter, he is a bit sluggish and is waiting patiently.

**poy uṟakkam** – yaśōdā thinks that he is really sleeping. But in reality, he is very much awake. Just as his sleep during deluge is termed as *yōganidrā* (meditative sleep when the body is resting and mind is awake fully) or *kaḷḷaniddirai* (deceptive sleep) or *aṟituyil* (sleeping while remaining conscious of outer world), āḻvār calls this sleep of kaṇṇa’s as *poy uṟakkam* (deceptive sleep).

**ōrādavan pōl  uṟangi** – he lay down such that one would think that all that he knows is only sleep and that he would remain motionless even if woken up several times.

**aṟivuṟṟu** – just as wisdom dawns on certain *acētana* (insentient) entities, he suddenly came to life. After she (yaśōdā) left, he played out as if he were really sleeping by stretching his body, yawning etc.  Knowing his craftiness, if she were to re-enter suddenly, it would appear as if he were really sleeping.

## 29. Tarar tadam

*tārār tadam tōḷgaḷ uḷḷaḷavum kai nītti                       32 ārāda veṇṇey viḻungi arugirunda*

tār Ar tada tōḷgaḷ uḷ aḷavum kai nītti – getting the divine huge shoulders, which are adorned with a garland, to go right down to the bottom of the pot; ārāda veṇṇey viḻungi – eating butter, which does not satiate him (however much he eats); arugu irunda – lying beside (the pot of butter)

**tār Ar tadam tōḷgaḷ – due to the strong desire in butter, just as it is said in tiruvāimoḻi 1-9-7 “tōḷiṇai mēlum” ((tuḷasi garland) on the two divine shoulders) he dropped his hands inside the pot of butter such that the garland on his shoulders were pressed against the pot. ḍidn’t nammāḻvār too say in tiruvāimoḻi 1-8-5 “vaigalum veṇṇey kaikalanduṇḍān” (he always ate butter by inserting his hand to the full extent that it would reach inside the vessel)!**

## 30. Morar kudamurutti

*mōrār kudamurutti mun kidanda tānattē                                               33 ōrādavan pōl kidandānaik kaṇḍavaḷum*

**Пословный перевод:**

mōr Ar kudam – the pot which is full of buttermilk; urutti – making it to roll (to manifest his lack of desire in it); mun kidanda tānattē – in the same place where he was lying earlier; ōrādavan pōl – as if he did not know anything; kidandānai – kaṇṇa, who was lying down

**Перевод:**

**arugirunda mōr Ar kudam urutti – his childishness will not stop with eating the butter. Just as it is mentioned in periyāḻvār tirumoḻi 2-9-1 “veṇṇey viḻungi veṟungalattai veṟpidaiyittu adan ōsai kētkum kaṇṇapirān kaṟṟa kalvi” (after swallowing the butter (without leaving anything behind), kaṇna will break the empty pot on a stone and relish the sound of its breaking. Such is the trickery that he has learnt!) isn’t he the one who relishes the sound of the pot getting broken after he has eaten the butter inside and breaking it on a stone! In the distinguished meaning for this verse, empirān takes the ātmā which is inside the body, which is like the sweet butter and relishes getting rid of the body which is like the pot. In the same way, here too he eats butter which is the essence and discards the pot containing buttermilk which is the residue (after butter had been removed from it). Just as he cannot bear to see thankless people amidst his devotees, he cannot bear to see pot of buttermilk near the pot of butter and hence rolls it down. In the distinguished meaning this translates into his liking for those who are full of essence \[of being devoted to emperumān\] and relishing them and his hatred for those who are full of residue \[of not being devoted to him\] and despising them. Even among vēdas (sacred texts) has he not praised those who are engaged with the second part \[vēdāntam\] which is full of essence by exclaiming “brāhmaṇō mama dēvatā” (these intelligent people are my dēvatās \[highly respected entities\]) while despising those who are engaged with the first part \[dealing with mere activities\] which is not of essence by disclaiming, as in bhagavat gītā ślōkam 2-42 “avipaścita: – vēda vāda ratā:” (the ignorant people who engage in arguing about vēdam)! While bhattar  was reciting the siṟiya tirumadal from āḻvār maṇḍapam inside śrīrangam temple, he would say “brāhmaṇarudaiya danattaiyellām pāḻē pōkkinān”. The meaning for this is: the brāhmaṇas who are engaged in rituals as mentioned in the first part of vēdam would treat butter, milk, curd, buttermilk etc which are used in the rituals, as their wealth. emperumān, seeing that they are leaving him, who is the ultimate puruśārtam (benefit), and are considering these lowly puruśārtams as important, ate up all the butter, milk, curd etc and rolled down the pots of buttermilk, thus destroying their wealth.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**mun kidanda tānattē** – fearing that someone may be seeing \[him eating butter etc\], he fell down in anxiety. Fortunately for him, the place where he fell down happened to be the place where he was \[deceptively\] sleeping before.

**mun kidanda tānattē** – even if he is accused of stealing \[butter\] it would appear as if he had stolen by getting up with the bed and lying back \[after eating the butter\] with the face, legs and hands in the same places as they were before.

**ōrādavan pōl kidandānai** – if someone were to look at him, he would say “He has no thoughts about butter at all”. He lay down such that he was totally ignorant in this matter. Even as the buttermilk \[which he had kicked and made to spill on the floor\] had wet his mat, he lay down as if he knew nothing.

**kaṇḍavaḷum** – vasn’t it she \[yaśōdā\] who had given birth to him? From the way he lay on the mat, she knew that he had done \[the mischief\]

## 31. Varattan

*vārāttān vaittadu kāṇāḷ vayiṟadittingu                           34 ārār puguduvār aiyar ivar allāl nīrām idu seydīr enṟōr nedum kayiṟṟāl                                       35*

**Пословный перевод:**

avaḷum vārā kaṇḍu – that yaśōdā too, on reaching and seeing; tān vaittadu kāṇāḷ – not finding the butter that she had kept (in the uṟi); vayiṟu adittu – beating herself on her stomach (wondering if eating this amount of butter would lead to indigestion for kaṇṇa); aiyar ivar allāḷ – other than this great person; ingu puguduvar Ar Ar – who else can enter this place?; idu seydīr nīrām enṟu – saying ‘only you have done this’; tīrā veguḷiyaḷāy – with uncontrolled anger; ōr nedu kayiṟṟāl – with a short rope that her hands could reach out to

**Перевод:**

**vārāttān vaittadu kāṇāḷ – as soon as she came back, yaśōdā checked the uṟi and found that the butter that she had kept there was missing.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**vayiṟadittu** – she would not beat herself on the stomach if kaṇṇa had eaten the butter, saying that “I have lost the butter”; why then did she beat herself? It is either because she felt that it would cause trouble for his stomach \[due to indigestion, having eaten so much of butter\] or due to her concern “Today he has stolen butter; tomorrow, he will steal the girls; in the end where will it lead him?”

**ingu ārār puguduvār** – she had kept the butter well protected, just like lankā was protected. In such a place, who else could enter other than he?

**aiyar ivar allāl** – only the chief \[referring to kaṇṇa, derisively\] would indulge in such an act.

**nīrām idu seydīr** – did you engage in this act on your own or was there assistance from others also?

**enṟu** – saying so, she lifted him by his hand, even as he was \[feigning to be\] asleep.

**ōr nedungayiṟṟāl** – she had not kept a rope ready to tie him, anticipating that he would steal butter and that she would have to tie him. Hence, she caught hold of a rope which she could lay her hands on.

**negungayiṟṟāl** – when this āḻvār (tirumangai āḻvār) himself explicitly narrated the same incident in his periya tirumoḻi 11-5-5 “*kaṇṇikkuṟungayiṟṟāl kattuṇḍān*” (he was tied down with a small rope with several knots in it), since he had mentioned that it was with a small rope, the term *nedungayiṟu* (long rope) is a *viparītalakśaṇai* (saying the exact opposite of what was intended to be said). It is similar to calling a fool as a wise man. The correct interpretation is that he was tied with a small rope. Alternatively, the term *nedumai* need not refer to the long length of the rope but to its greatness. In other words, just as the ocean attained greatness due to its contact with emperumān as brought out in the [19](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2017/06/mudhal-thiruvandhadhi-19/)[<sup>th</sup>](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2017/06/mudhal-thiruvandhadhi-19/) [pāsuram of mudal tiruvandādi](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2017/06/mudhal-thiruvandhadhi-19/) “*mālum karungadalē ennōṟṟāy? vaiyagamuṇḍu ālinilaittuyinṟa āḻiyān kōlak karumēnic cengaṇmāl kaṇpadaiyuḷ tirumēni nī tīṇḍappeṟṟu*” (how fortunate are you, oh ocean! You are feeling the divine form of the supreme being who was sleeping on a banyan leaf after swallowing all the worlds and is reclining on you), this rope too acquired greatness due to its contact with emperumān and hence is referred to as *nedum kayiṟu*.

## 32. Urargal ellarum

*ūrārgaḷellārum kāṇa uralōdē tīrā veguḷiyaḷāyc cikkena ārttadippa                       36 ārā vayiṟṟinōdu āṟṟādān – anṟiyum*

ūrārgaḷellām kāṇa – (such simplicity) to be seen by all the people in the village; uralōdē – to a \[grinding\] mortar; cikkena ārttu adippa – to tie down firmly and beat; ārā vayiṟṟinōdu āṟṟādān – one who stood with sorrow and his stomach hurting; anṟiyum – further

**Перевод:**

**ūrāragaḷ ellārum kāṇa – haven’t all the people in tiruvāyppādi (śrī gōkulam) lost butter to kaṇṇa or their womenfolk to him! She (parakāla nāyagi) was waiting to engage in madal.   Just as he was beaten with everyone looking at him, she was waiting to engage in madal such that everyone would see it. I (the fortune-teller) was waiting to instruct that to everyone. All the people other than us were enjoying his divine forms and divine qualities which he was offering. Those who aren’t hungry are enjoying the feast. We, who are hungry, are fasting. In other words, those who thought “If we could, we will see him; otherwise, it doesn’t matter to us” enjoyed seeing him. We, who vouldn’t be able to sustain ourselves unless we see him even if we have to engage in madal to achieve that goal, haven’t seen him.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**uralōdē** – what an action did she perform! Instead of bringing the mortar to where he was, with his flower-like soft divine body, she dragged him to where the mortar was.

**tīrā veguḷiyaḷāy** – Since it was anger born out of love, the anger too was without an end, just like her love. Only if there is a limit to her love, since she thought “What ill will befall him since he has eaten so much of butter” would there be a limit to her anger at his eating butter!

**cikkena ārttadippa** – she tied him firmly to the mortar and she hit him firmly with the rope. What an act did she perform!

**ārā vayiṟṟinōdu āṟṟādān** – what did he do? Just like those, who have been put in prison during the time of *tiruvadyayana utsavam* (festival conducted during the month of *mārgaḻi*) in śrīrangam temple due to the order of the King, would lament that they have missed out on the festival, he felt unhappy that he was tied down here while butter and womenfolk were making merry, going around, like in a great flood.

**āṟṟādān** – he could not bear it. What is it that he could not bear? He could not bear his doubt as to where they have hidden butter and their womenfolk.

**anṟiyum** – \[the fortune teller says\] should you not listen to another great danger, further? What has been just now narrated was the way he was tied down to a mortar by the affectionate yaśōdā and beaten by her. The narration now talks about how he was tied down by kāḷiyan (a poisonous snake), his enemy. Since she (yaśōdā\] was his mother, who had the liberty to beat him and had affection for him, one could put up with being tied down by her hands. The fortune teller is asking whether they were willing to listen to the story of kāḷiyan.

## 33. Nirar nedum

*nīrār negungayattaic cenṟalaikka ninṟurappi                         37*

**Пословный перевод:**

nīr Ar nedu kayattai senṟu – going to a deep place in a huge pond which was full of water; alaikka ninṟu urappi – agitating it (along with thousand others, mischievous just like him), in such a way that water would flow out like a flood

**Перевод:**

**nīrār nedungayattai – Just as it has been mentioned in nācciyār tirumoḻi 14-1 “ittamāna pasukkaḷai inidu maṟittu nīrūtti vittukkoṇḍu viḷaiyāda” (making the cows which are his favourite, to drink water by hindering them in their way and forcing them towards water, thus playing with them), there was a pond with plenty of water, from which kaṇṇa would make the cows to drink water. However, kāḷiyan, a poisonous snake, contaminated the water with his poison. Thus he spoiled the water, rendering it useless for the cows, which are to be protected by kaṇṇa.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**senṟalaikka ninṟurappi** – he would go with a thousand other cow-herd boys who were as mischievous as he himself was, just as mentioned in periyāḻvār tirumoḻi 3-1-1 “*tannērāyiram piḷḷaigḷōdu taḷar nadai ittu varuvān*” (he will walk along with a thousand boys like himself, with a tottering gait), to the pond, indulge in mischief such as agitating the pond with his legs and hands, throwing stone on the snake etc, thus provoking it.

**senṟu alaikka ninṟu urappi** – This could be construed as kaṇṇa agitating the water such that it would flow out of the bund on all the sides. Alternatively, it could mean that he indulged in daring acts just like he had laid siege on lankā (during his incarnation as śrī rāma) and made the demons to get ready for battle.

While his childish activities thus far were enough to incite the snake to be furious, listen to his further childish acts, says the fortune teller.

## 34. Orayiram pana

*ōrāyiram paṇa vengōviyal nāgattai vārāy enakkenṟu maṟṟadan mattagattu                              38 sīrār tiruvadiyāl pāyndān tan sītaikku*

**Пословный перевод:**

ōr āyiram paṇa vengō iyal nāgattai – kāḷiyan, having thousand hoods and having a cruel nature like yama (the deity of righteousness); enakku vārāy enṟu – calling him out for a duel with him; maṟṟadan mattagattu – atop his head; sīr Ar tiruvadiyāl pāyndān – jumped and danced with his beautiful divine feet; than sītaikku – for sītā, his consort

**Перевод:**

**vem kō iyal nāgattai – he is not just anyone else; he is yama himself who has taken the form of a snake! vem iyal kō – yama who has a cruel nature**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**ōr āyiram paṇa nāgattai** – why is she saying that kāḷiyan has thousand hoods while periyāḻvār, in  his periyāḻvār tirumoḻi 3-9-7 says “*kāḷiyan poygai kalangap pāyndittu avan nīḷ mudi aindilum  ninṟu nadam seydu*” (jumping into the pond, agitating it, and dancing on top of  the five hoods of kāḷiyan’s which reached up to the sky) that kāḷiyan had five hoods? Since he was a small child, the moment kāḷiyan came to fight with him, it appeared to the fortune-teller that his five hoods became a thousand.

**vengōviyal nāgattai** – the term *kō* refers to yama; the phrase *kō iyal nāgam* could mean that it is a snake having the nature of yama; alternatively, it could mean that it created cruelty even in yama through his activity; or, as a non-existing simile, since the yama that we know of cannot equal this snake in cruelty, this snake could be assumed to possess the cruelty of another yama, different from this.

**vārāy enakkenṟu** – Just as each monkey in the army of monkeys kept saying that it wanted to destroy lankā, each of the thousand friends of kaṇṇa invited kāḷiyan to wage war with him. But kaṇṇa asked them to be quiet and called out to kāḷiyan, says the fortune teller. Since he cannot bear to see his friends being hurt, he stood upfront.

**maṟṟadan mattagattu** – on the hood of kāḷiyan. It appeared that he fell into the mouth of the snake.

**sīrār tiruvadiyāl pāyndān** – the divine feet are so soft that if periya pirātti (śrī mahālakśmi), who is as soft as a flower, keeps his divine feet on her bosom, she would be worried whether the feet will become sore. With such soft divine feet, he jumped on the hoods of kāḷiyan. Was the divine foot not described in [tiruvāimoḻi 9-2-10](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2019/06/thiruvaimozhi-9-2-10-koduvinaip-padaigal/) “*vadiviṇai illā malarmagaḷ maṟṟai nilamagaḷ pidikkum melladi*” (the soft foot which was massaged by śrīdēvi and bhūdēvi who are incomparable in their beauty) as being very soft!

## 35. Ner avan

*nērāvan enṟōr nisāsaridān vandāḷai                              39*

**Пословный перевод:**

nēr āvan enṟu – one who said “I am equal“; ōr nisāsari vandāḷai – one who came running, the demon sūrpaṇakā (asking śrī rāma to marry her)

**Перевод:**

**than sītaikku nērāvan . . . . silai kunittān – sūrpaṇakā came \[to citrakūtam\], saying “I am fully equal to sītā; I will marry śrī rāma”. He cut her ears and nose, disfiguring her. She ran to kara (a demon) and fell at his feet. This sequence captures the narration of how he killed kara and his brother dūśaṇa.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**than sītaikku** – sitā who was apt for him \[śrī rāma\] in all ways. Just as it is mentioned in the śrī rāmāyaṇa ślōkam “*tulya śeela vayōvruttām tulyābhijana lakśaṇām* I  *rāghavō’rhati vaidēhim tam cēyam asitēkśaṇā* īī” (only śrī rāma is apt for sītā who has equivalent qualities, age, conduct, as well as equivalent clan and beauty; in the same way, this (sītāp)pirātti only, who has black eyes, is apt for him) and in śrī rāmāyaṇam āraṇya kāṇḍam ślōkam 37-18 “*apramēyam hi tat tējō yasya sā janakātmajā*” (it is impossible to estimate that radiance called as śrī rāma to whom the daughter of King janaka belongs), sūrpaṇakā came saying that she is the equal to that sītā , whose glory śrī rāma himself desired.

**nērāvan enṟu** – she came, with her grotesque form, saying that she matches sītā, who was splendorous with all the defining characteristics of a lady.

**ōr nisāsari** – since she came with anger against sīrtāppirātti, saying that she matches sītāppirātti, who is the *jaganmātā* (mother  for the entire world), the fortune teller highlights that she comes from the demonic clan which eats corpse.

**tān** – she, who is no match for śrī rāma, calls herself as a match for sītā, who is superior to even śrī rāma.

**than sītaikku nērāvan enṟōr nisāsari tān vandāḷai** – just as it is mentioned in śrī rāmāyaṇam sundara kāṇḍam 16 – 14 “*trailōkyarājyam sakalam sītāyā nāpnuyāt kalām*” (the kingdoms of the three worlds and the one who rules them (śrī rāma) will not be a match for even a part of sītā), even if all wealth of śrī rāma and he himself were put on a balance, they cannot equal sītā. When such is the reality, sūrpaṇakā came saying that she is a match for sītā.

**vandāḷai** – as soon as sūrpaṇakā came to śrī rāma, he told her “I am already married and have taken a vow that I will have only one wife; go to lakśmaṇa, who is not married”; lakśmaṇa told her “I am his servitor; if you marry me, you have to do menial service to them both. Hence, only if you marry śrī rāma himself, could you get a title \[as his queen\] and sit on the same throne on which he sits”.  After being tossed a few times between the two of them, she decided that it was only sītā who was preventing her from realising her wish and hence pounced on her saying “I will eat you up”.

## 36. Kuraranda

*kūrārnda vāḷāl kodi mūkkum kādiraṇḍum*

**Пословный перевод:**

kūrārnda vāḷāl – with a sharp sword; kodi mūkkum kādu iraṇḍām – nose which is like a creeper and the two ears

**Перевод:**

**kūrārnda vāḷāl kodi mūkkum kādiraṇḍum īrā viduttu – while it has been mentioned \[in śrī rāmāyaṇam\] that it was lakśmaṇa who disfigured sūrpaṇakā, why is it mentioned here that śrī rāma had done that?   Since it has been mentioned in śrī rāmāyaṇam āraṇya Kāṇḍam 34-13 “rāmasya dakśiṇō bāhu:” (lakśmaṇa is śrī rāma’s right hand), with lakśmaṇa being śrī rāma’s right hand, there is nothing inappropriate in saying the he had done it when his right hand had done it. The meaning implied here is that to punish a person \[sūrpaṇakā\] who had carried out asahyāpacāram (deeply offending without a reason), instead of engaging another person, he did it with his right hand.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**kūrārnda vāḷāl** – with a sharp sword. While working on the rough body of sūrpaṇakā \[to disfigure her\], to ensure that the bad blood of sūrpaṇakā’s did not fall on him, he had used a sharpened sword.

**kodi mūkkum kādu iraṇḍām īrā viduttu** – since she had come claiming that she was a match for sītāppirātti, she had changed her nose and ears through her magical powers to resemble those of sītā. He cut those, which were the cause for her vanity.

## 37. Ira viduttu

*īra viduttu avatku mūttōnai vennaragam                                40 sērā vagaiyē silai kunittān – senduvarvāy*

**Пословный перевод:**

īrā viduttu – severed and drove \[sūrpaṇakā\]; avatku mūttōnai – kara, her elder brother; vem naragam – for him to go now to another cruel narakam (hell); sērā vagaiyē silai kunittān – he \[śrī rāma\] stood with his bow drawn, giving all sorts of troubles which kara would undergo in narakam such that he need not experience more troubles (in another narakam more cruel than this); sem tuvar vāy – one who has very reddish lips

**Перевод:**

**īrā viduttu – chased her away, after cutting her nose and ears. The sharpness of the sword was indicated in the term kūrārnda vāḷ. The roughness of the body, which made it difficult to cut, is indicated in the term īrā (cut).**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**īrā viduttu** – unlike the way when a girl leaves her parents’ place for her husband’s place, when she leaves her husband’s place for her parents’ place, isn’t it customary to decorate her well! In the same way, he \[śrī rāma\] decorated her \[sūrpaṇakā\] and sent to her parents’ \[here, brothers’\] place. Even though she desired the lord of all the worlds, since she was inimical towards pirātti, she lost her limbs. Thus, the implication here is that even if one has desire in matters related to bhagavān, if one commits an offence towards his devotees, what happened for sūrpaṇakā only will result. In śrī viṣṇu purāṇam 5-3-20 . . .22, the story of *cintayanti* (a girl in tiruvāyppādi, śrī gōkulam) has been narrated “*kāciccāvasatasyāntē stitvā druśtvā bahirgurūn* I *tanmayatvēna gōvindam dadyau meelita lōcanā* īī *taccintāvimalāhlādakhśīṇa puṇyacayā tadā* I *tadaprāptimahādu:kkavileenāśēśapātakā* īī *cintayanti jagatsūtim parabrahma svarūpiṇam* I *niruccvāsatayā muktim gatānyā gōpakanyakā* īī” (Another herds-girl, (on hearing the music from flute), seeing the elders in the way, stayed inside the house itself (out of fear) and meditated on kaṇṇa through her mind. At the same moment, since she felt unblemiśed happiness as she thought of the supreme being, all her *puṇya* (virtues) got eliminated. Since she could not see him in person, all her *pāpa* (sins) got eliminated. Since she kept meditating on the supreme entity, who is the cause for all the worlds, she stopped breathing (having got both her sins and virtues eliminated) and attained liberation). Though *cintayanti* too desired the same supreme entity whom sūrpaṇakā desired, since she did not commit any offence, she attained the highest benefit.

**viduttu** – letting her to go. Making her to fall at the feet of *kara* \[her brother\] exclaiming “*taruṇau rūpasampannau*” as mentioned in śrī rāmāyaṇam āraṇya kāṇḍam 19-14 (śrī rāma and lakśmaṇa are youthful and handsome), thus recounting their greatness.

**avatku mūttōnai** – in the case of favourable ones (emperumān’s devotees), just as it has been said in [tiruvāimoḻi 2-7-1](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2016/04/thiruvaimozhi-2-7-1-kesavan-thamar/) “*kēsavan tamar kīḻmēl emar ēḻēḻ piṟappum māsadiridu peṟṟu*” (those who are related to me and who are devotees of kēsavan, in seven generations gone by, seven generations including me and in seven generations coming after me, obtained greatness like this), emperumān’s mercy will not stop with his devotee alone, but will extend to those who are related to such devotee. In the same way, his anger towards the unfavourable ones (those who consider emperumān as their enemy) will not stop with them but extend to those who are related to them \[thus, the anger towards sūrpaṇaka for showing her cruelness towards sītāppirātti went towards her elder brothers too\].

**vennaragam sērā vagaiyē silai kunittān** – when *kara* \[incited by sūrpaṇakā\] entered the battlefield, he saw śrī ramapirān with his curved bow drawn, ready for battle. After seeing such a sight, there was no need to go to another hell and experience hardships. śrī rāma ensured that he suffered all the hardships here itself. When nanjīyar was in mēlakkōttai (present day tirunārāyaṇapuram), still in *gruhastāśramam* (leading a married life, with the name of śrī mādavan), before he came under the tutelage of śrī parāsara bhattar, there was a discussion about this prabandam. Some people interpreted the meaning for this verse as “śrī rāma drew his bow such that *kara* entered valorous heaven without having the need to enter narakam”. However, the context here is about how emperumān was dealing with those who are unfavourable to him. Both the verse before this (hurting sūrpaṇakā) and the verse following this (killing rāvaṇa) deal with hurting the unfavourable ones. Hence it is inappropriate that the verse in between the two could speak about sending someone to the valorous heaven. nanjīyar asked one of the disciples of bhattar who was there “How will bhattar mercifully explain this verse?” and that disciple responded by saying “śrī rāma had drawn the bow in such a way that for *kara* who saw śri rāma with drawn bow, there was no hell more cruel than that sight ”. Hearing this, the longing to see bhattar who had given such an explanation would increase in nanjīyar. In later years, nanjīyar did surrender to the divine feet of śrī parāsara bhattar.

## 38. Varar vanamulaiyal

*vārār vanamulaiyāḷ vaidēvi kāraṇamā                             41 ērār tadandōḷ irāvaṇanai – īraindu sīrār siram aṟuttuc ceṟṟuganda sengaṇmāl            42*

**Пословный перевод:**

vār Ar vana mulaiyāḷ – one having beautiful bosom, donning a corset; vaidēvi kāraṇam A – for sītāppirātti; ēr Ar tada tōḷ irāvaṇanai – rāvaṇa, who has beautiful, huge shoulders; sīr Ar siram aṟuttu ceṟṟu – severing the ten great heads, and killing; uganda – (having destroyed the enemy of sages), feeling happy in his divine mind; sem kaṇ māl – kaṇṇa, with lotus like eyes

**Перевод:**

**senduvar vāy . . . . – after kara was killed, akampana was the only survivor who could carry the narration, dressed as a female, to rāvaṇa. Soon, rāvaṇa, with the help of mārīca came to citrakūta and separated perumāḷ and pirātti on either side of the ocean. With this as the reason, śrī rāma killed rāvaṇa and this narration is described further.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**senduar vāy** – one with deep, reddish mouth. śrī rāmāyaṇam āraṇya kāṇḍam 64-78 “*purēva mē cārudatīmaninditām diśanti sītām yadi nānyamaitileem* I *sadēva gandarva manuśya pannagam jagat saśailam parivartayāmyaham* īī” (If the princess of mitilā, who is without any fault, is not returned to me with her smile as before, I will invert the earth with its mountains, dēvas, gandarvas, humans and snakes). sītā’s smile was such that it could make śrī rāma, who is the sustaining force for the world, like a shed for supplying water to travellers to quench their thirst, to take a vow to destroy the world. When the golden deer appeared in their hermitage, sitā requested śrī rāma, with a smile on her face, “Oh Lord! Please get me that deer”. śrī rāma said that he will destroy the entire world, much like severing a bunch of bananas, if that sītā is not back in his presence, with her smile.

**vārār vanamulaiyāḷ** – Just as it has been said in [mūnṟām tiruvandādi 3](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2018/02/munram-thiruvandhadhi-3/) “*malarāḷ tanattuḷḷān*” (emperumān resides in the bosom of śrīdēvi), isn’t it the place where śrī rāma dwells?

**vaidēvi** – isn‘t she having the pride of being spoken often by śrī rāma of having been born in the clan of *vidēha rāja*?

**kāraṇamā** – on her account, who has such a fame.

**ērār tadandōḷ irāvaṇanai** – hanuman, who will not bother about anyone other than śrī rāma and sītāppirātti, says of rāvaṇa, as in śrī rāmāyaṇam sundara kāṇḍam 49-17 “*ahō rūpam ahō dairyam ahō satvam ahō dyuti:* I *ahō rākśasa rājasya sarvalakśaṇa yuktatā* īī” (How amazing is his form, his courage, his strength and his radiance! How is it that he, who is the king of demons, has all the identities \[of a great person\] fitting well on him!) This explains how rāvaṇa has such qualities to be respected by even hanuman.

**īraindu sīrār siram aṟuttu** – if all the ten heads were severed at the same time, rāvaṇa cannot see how he appears with his heads severed. Hence to enable him to see that great sight and to feel distressed, śrī rāma severed five heads at a time.

**sīrār siram aṟuttu** – severing the great heads. Greatness of the severed heads is that even as they are getting severed and are falling down, just as it is mentioned in śrī rāmāyaṇam yudda kāṇḍam “*śatrō*: *prakyāta vīryasya ranjanīyasya vikramai*:” (an enemy who had a famed valour and who has to be praised for his activities of bravery, such rāma’s), they praise the bravery of śrī rāma with a smile on their faces. Alternatively, instead of fearing being severed, taking that \[being severed\] itself as a sign of bravery, is their greatness. In other words, even as the severed heads are falling prey to the eagles and vultures, the heads which are still on his neck are desiring to get severed and to fall down so that they too could get the fame of having fallen in an act of bravery.

**seṟṟu** – instead of killing the person \[rāvaṇa\] who came to fight with a single arrow, śrī rāma destroyed his divisions of chariots, elephants, horses and foot-soldiers, killed the horses which were drawing rāvaṇa’s chariot, killed the charioteer, destroyed the chariot, brought down the flag flying atop the chariot, removed rāvaṇa’s crown, as if tonsuring his head, hurt his shoulders, severed his heads, and making him suffer at all such ignominy, finally killed him.

**uganda sengaṇmāl** – *uganda* – feeling happy that he had destroyed the enemy of sages and crowned śrī vibhīśaṇāḻvān. īsn’t he one who carries out activities for the sake of others and feels happy about it! *sengaṇmāl* – showing in his divine eyes, his affection and motherly love towards his devotees, thinking “We have removed the enemies of our devotees and made them feel happy”. When the eyes are full of love and motherly affection, they will be reddish in colour. *māl* – one who has affection.

## 39. Porar neduvelon

*pōrār neduvēlōn ponpeyarōn āgattaic*

**Пословный перевод:**

pōrār neduvēlōn ponpeyarōn āgattai – hiraṇya kaśyapu (always) has the battle-ready long spear. That hiraṇya’s chest

**Перевод:**

**pōrār neduvēlōn . . .  – The earlier narration was about emperumān’s killing rāvaṇa for the sake of pirātti, after waiting till the end, trying to see if he could correct him. Now, the narration is about how he could not wait patiently since he had to appear the instant his devotee prahlāda told hiraṇya that emperumān is present in the pillar also. He had to take a half-human, half-lion form and destroy the enemy of his devotee.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**pōrār vēl** – the spear which is battle-ready. It has to constantly hurt the bodies of his enemies in the battle front and does not even have the time to take rest in the weapons storage yard. This spear is constantly surrounded by flesh, blood, intestine etc as well as birds of prey such as vultures and eagles.

**nedu vēl** – It is mentioned in śrī rāmāyaṇam yudda kāṇḍam 100-12 *ālikandamivākāsam avaśtabhya mahat danu*:“ (keeping a huge bow which extends up to the sky, pressed). Once śrī rāma draws an arrow on the bow, it will not stop until it carries out its task. In the same way, this spear, once it is released by hiraṇya on his enemy, will not stop even if he tries to control it. This is its greatness.

**vēlōn** – one who has that spear. The implied meaning is that even when he lay dead, he was holding on to the spear, making it appear that it was his identification.

**pon peyarōn** – he has as his name *gold,* which is the meaning for the term *hiraṇya*.

**āgattai** – his chest. For carrying out hunting in forests, people will rear pigs as bait and feed the pigs sumptuously to make them stout. In the same way, hiraṇya had made his chest huge with the strength of the boons that he had received from brahmā et al.

## 40. Kurarnda vallurgiral

*kūrārnda vaḷḷugirāl kīṇḍu kudal mālai                           43 sīrār tirumārbin mēl kattic cengurudi*

**Пословный перевод:**

kūr ārnda vaḷḷugirāl kīṇḍu – splitting with sharp, divine nails which were close together; kudal – (plucking out) the intestine of that demon; sīr Ar tirumārbin mēl mālai katti – wearing that as a victorious garland on the chest which is the dwelling place for the valorous lakshmi

**Перевод:**

**kūrārnda vaḷḷugirāl – with divine nails which were sharp and were close together. vaḷḷugir – dense nails \[nails which were close together\]. Alternative explanation: vaḷḷugir – benevolent nails. When emperumān engages with his consorts, they will be afraid whether his divine form will become sore just by their looking at him, since he would be so soft towards them, whereas when he engages with his enemies in a battle, he would have enough strength to destroy them. In the same way, these nails will be sharp towards hiraṇya while they will be benevolent towards his consorts for their enjoyment. Just as it has been mentioned in various pramāṇams (authentic sources) such as in the saying “candra bhāskara varcasam” (having splendour like moon and sun), “kadir madiyam pōl mugattān” (face similar to sun and moon) etc, don’t his divine body, body limbs and weapons behave in a friendly way towards those who are favourable to him and in an inimical way toward those who are unfavourable to him!**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**kīṇḍu** – as soon as hiraṇya saw *narasimha* (emperumān with human body and lion’s head) with his rage,  his body became withered, became supple just like a boar which has been roasted in fire, such that emperumān could tear it effortlessly.

**kudal mālai sīrār tirumārbin mēl katti** – due to excessive anger, narasimha plucked out hiraṇya’s intestine and wore it on his divine chest. In other words, he wore the intestine as a victorious garland on the chest where *vijayalakśmi* (lakshmi who grants victory) dances, since it is *sīrār tirumārvu*; *sīrār* refers to *vijayalakśmi* shining. Alternatively we can construe it as the residence of periya pirāttiyār (śrī mahālakśmi) and emperumān decorated that with this garland. Since she will not be with him if he does not have this anger with the enemies of his devotees, he garlanded her dwelling place with hiraṇya’s intestine.

## 41. Sorak kidandanai

*sōrāk kidandānaik kungumattōḷ kotti                         44 ārā eḻundān ari uruvāy – anṟiyum pēr vāmanāgiya kālattu mūvadi maṇ                             45*

**Пословный перевод:**

sem kurudi sōrāk kidandānai – that demon who was lying in a flood of blood; kungamattōḷ kotti – \[emperumān\] beating himself on his shoulders which were decorated with vermillion powder (as a symbol of having emerged victoriously); ārā eḻundān – one who stood up tumultuously; anṟiyum – apart from that; pēr vāman āgiya kālattu – when he took the divine incarnation as dwarfish vāmana

**Перевод:**

**sem kurudi sōrāk kidandānaik  kungumattōḷ kotti – hit the demon hiraṇya, who was lying in a flood of blood, having been torn asunder, against his shoulder which had been decorated with vermillion for embracing his consorts.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

The verse must be rearranged as *sengurudi sōrākkidandānai – kūrārnda  vaḷḷugirāl kīṇḍu kudal mālai sīrār tirumārbin mēl katti – kungumattōḷ kotti ārā eḻundān* to get the correct sequential meaning.

**ārā eḻundān** – got up tumultuously. Got up rousingly, by making uproarious sounds.

**ārā eḻundān ari uruvāy** – cāndōjña upaniśat 3-14 says of him “*avākyanādara*:” (one who does not speak; one who is without any prop). Since there is no one qualified enough to listen to what he says, he remains quiet. Such emperumān made uproarious sounds since he was ecstatic that he had destroyed his devotee’s enemy.

**anṟiyum** – if the person is evil like hiraṇya, he could be destroyed; but māvali (mahābali) is someone who had something good too, in him; thus defeating him is a bit difficult. The narration now talks about how he defeated him too. mahābali had the noble quality of being magnanimous. Hence he cannot be destroyed so rapidly as hiraṇya. However he had to be defeated for the sake of dēvas (celestial entities). Keeping in mind mahābali’s quality of magnanimity (that he will be happy in giving away largesse), emperumān disguised himself as a mendicant, accepted the water (symbolically water is poured by the giver, on the palm of the receiver, as a token of having given what was asked for) and gave the earth (which was given) back to indra. This is described now.

**anṟiyum pēr vāmanāgiya kālattu** – *pēr vāman* – huge vāmana. All the dwarfs would appear very tall when compared to this vāman; he was such short. ālternativey, those who are dwarfish do not keep a name ‘dwarf’ to signify their height; but emperumān, apart from being dwarfish, had the name too as *vāmana* \[dwarf\].

**kālattu** – this laments that it was a time when the supreme one lowered himself to the level of the lowly.

## 42. Taray enakkenru

*tārāy enakkenṟu vēndicc cattināl nīrēṟṟu ulagellām ninṟaḷandān māvaliyai                       46 ārāda pōril asurargaḷum tānumāy*

**Пословный перевод:**

māvaliyai – with mahābali; mū adi maṇ enakku tārāy enṟu vēṇḍi – asking him “Grant me three steps of land”; nīr ēṟṟu – accepting the water which mahābali poured on his hand; salattināl – with the deception of showing small foot and measuring with large foot; ninṟu – rising up; ulagu ellām aḷandān – measured and took possession of all the worlds; ārāda pōril – in the impossible-to-achieve battle between dēvas (celestial entities) and asuras (demons); asurargaḷum tānumāy – he and the asuras

**Перевод:**

**mūvadimaṇ tārāy – vāmana told mahābali “māvali! You have to give me three steps of land”; māvali also said “I will give”; vāmana told him “You cannot give excuses; give me immediately”. vyākyātā (commentator) for this prabandam, periyavāccān piḷḷai says that this firmness on the part of vāmana is similar to vaḷaippār. In malayāḷa dēsam (present day kēraḷā), if a person has taken money on loan and does not pay it back on time, the lender will accost the receiver and draw a line in front of him. He can cross that line only after paying back the lender, the money owed. This practice was called as vaḷaippu.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**enakkenṟu** – one who never says “For me” and always says “For the sake of my devotees”, now says “For me”, since he is taking alms for the sake of others (celestial entities).

**vēṇḍic calattināl ninṟaḷandān māvaliyai** – After he asked “Give me three steps of land”, māvali poured out water on to his hand. Immediately, he grew to a huge form and measured the worlds by deceptively measuring with his huge foot, after initially showing māvali his small foot. The verse should be rearranged as *māvaliyai mūvadi maṇ tārāy enakkenṟu vēṇḍi, nīrēṟṟu, salattināl ulagellām ninṟu aḷandān* to get the sequential meaning.

**ārāda pōril . . .**  – The next narration speaks about how he churned the ocean when the celestial and demonic entities could not accomplish the task when they set out to churn the ocean.

**ārāda pōril** – in the activity (of churning) which they could not accomplish. *pōr* – activity. When the celestial and demonic entities could not accomplish the task of churning the ocean, which they set out to do. *ārāda* – could not be fulfilled. Alternatively, *ārāda* – not feeling satisfied. emperumān does not feel satisfied despite doing how much ever that he did, for his devotees. We could construe this (*ārāda*) as – in the activity of churning in which he was not satisfied.

**asurargaḷum tānumāy** – instead of saying *dēvargaḷum asurargaḷumāy* (dēvas and asuras), why is he saying *asurargaḷum tānumāy* (demons and himself)? Since he is assuming the dēvas who are favourable to him to be a part of him, he is identifying himself with them, and hence he says this way.

## 43. Karar varai

*kārār varai nattu nāgam kayiṟāgap                                      47 pērāmal tāngik kadaindān tiruttuḻāyt tārārnda mārvan tadamāl varai pōlum                       48*

**Пословный перевод:**

kār Ar varai nattu – anchoring the mantara mountain (a celestial mountain), which has monsoon cloud (as the shaft for churning); nāgam kayiṟāga – using the snake vāsuki as the rope for churning; pērāmal tāngi – sustaining that mountain so that it does not shift sideways or below or above, \[during the churning process\]; tiru tuḻāy tār ārnda mārvan kadaindān – he churned the ocean donning a garland of tiruttuḻāy (tuḷasi) on his chest; tadam māl varai pōlum pōr ānai – an elephant in exultation, which was like a huge mountain

**Перевод:**

**kār Ar varai nattu – anchoring the mantara mountain, on which monsoon clouds had settled, as the shaft for churning the ocean. His capability was such that when he anchored the mountain, the clouds atop the mountain were not disturbed at all.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**nāgam kayiṟāga** – *nāgam* – snake. *kam* – *sukam* (joy); *akam* – *dukkam* (sorrow). *nāgam* – great joy. Since snake is a *cētana* (sentient entity) it would appear to us that it would suffer if it is wound around the mountain and used as a rope for churning the ocean. But it experienced a great joy on account of the supreme being touching it, and as a means of giving vent to its joy, it participated in the act of churning and sustained that joy.

**pērāmal tāngik kadaindān** – positioning himself as a tortoise below the mountain so that the mountain would not sink to the bottom of the ocean during churning operation, taking different forms to support the mountain on the sides as well as on top to support it, he churned the ocean.

**tiruttuḻāy tār ārnda mārvan** – when he was churning the ocean, he did it such effortlessly that not even a petal from the garland on his shoulder withered.

**tada māl varai pōlum** – the \[just concluded\] narration was about dēvas (celestial entities) who desire other benefits \[and not emperumān himself}. The narration to be made now describes how he came running helter-skelter to the tank bund to save the elephant, which considered emperumān himself as its ultimate benefit, destroyed the elephant’s enemy and accepted servitude from the elephant.

**tada māl varai pōlum pōrānai** – *tadavmāl varai* – mountain which is huge and tall. The elephant is like that mountain. What is the significance of mentioning the greatness of the elephant’s form? This is to show that the huge elephant’s trouble would also be huge, just like the old saying which says that the swelling on a finger would be proportional to the size of the finger.

## 44. Poranai poygaivay

*pōrānai poygaivāyk kōtpattu ninṟalaṟi nīrār malarkkamalam koṇḍōr nedum kaiyāl                                    49 nārāyaṇā ō! maṇivaṇṇā! nāgaṇaiyāy! vārāy en āridarai nīkkāy ena veguṇḍu                                 50*

**Пословный перевод:**

poygai vāy kōḷ pattu ninṟu alaṟi – feeling distressed after being caught in water, in the mouth of a crocodile; ōr nedu kaiyāl nīr Ar kamalam malar koṇḍu – with a long trunk, taking lotus flowers which had just then blossomed; O nārāyaṇā! maṇivaṇṇā! nāgaṇaiyāy! – “Oh nārāyaṇa! Oh one with blue gemstone like complex! Oh one who is lying on the serpent-bed!; vārāy en Ar idarai nīkkāy ena – you should come; you should mercifully remove my great sorrow” crying out loudly, like this; veguṇḍu – getting furious (on the crocodile)

**Перевод:**

**pōrānai – elephant which engages in war. This elephant, gajēndran, is a sātvic (purely good) entity; hence we cannot construe that he engages wantonly in battle with others. As a behaviour consistent with his creed, due to exultation, roaming around, hitting against mountains, is termed as engaging in war.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**poygai vāy** – it is capable of destroying its enemies if the fight is on land which is its natural ground. However, since it is in water, which is the favourite land for crocodile, it is suffering.

**kōtpattu** – being caught by its enemy, the crocodile.

**ninṟu** – while the crocodile was trying to pull it into water, the elephant was trying to drag the crocodile to the bank (of the water body). Since it was going on for a very long time, the elephant stood, gradually losing its strength.

**nīrār malarkkamalam koṇḍōr nedum kaiyāl** – except for its trunk in which it was holding the just blossomed lotus which he wanted to submit at the divine feet of emperumān, the elephant was fully immersed in water.

**nīrār malarkkamalam** – Since it was meant for emperumān, despite a long time having passed since it was plucked from water, the lotus appeared fresh, as if it were still in water.

**nārāyaṇā O!** – “Oh the one who is the means and the end benefit for the entire society of sentient entities! Am I not one among that society? If you do not protect me, is there any way for your *nārāyaṇatvam* (the nature of being the refuge for all) to be sustained?” When asked whether he wanted his life to be saved after destroying the crocodile, gajēndra says

**maṇivaṇṇā** – Am I not desirous of seeing your divine form which is like a bluish gemstone? Are you not the one who gives his divine form to quench the thirst of your devotees who are suffering from *tāpatrayam*? \[*tāpatrayam* is sufferance at the hands of nature, one’s own deeds and emperumān’s divine mind) Are you not the one manifesting your form to your devotees as mentioned in jitantē ślōkam “*bhaktānām*” (for the sake of devotees)? When such is the case, would you hide your form from me?

**nāgaṇaiyāy** – are you not the one lying on tiruvanantāḻvān (ādiśēśan) who desires to experience and enjoy your divine form?

**vārāy** – you should come, donning your beautiful form to remove all my sorrows.

**en Ar idarai nīkkāy** – *Ar idar* – huge obstacle. Here, the obstacle is not the one caused by crocodile. gajēndran wants only to submit the \[lotus\] flower, before it withers away, at the divine feet of emperumān. The hurdle to the kainkaryam at the divine feet of the ordained lord is the obstacle meant here. gajēndra’s words were “*nāham kaḷēbarasyāsya trāṇārtam madusūdana* I *karasta kamalānyēva pādayōrarpitum tava* īī” (Oh madusūdana! I am not praying for the sustenance of this body. I am praying only for submitting the flower in my hand at your divine feet)

**ena** – as gajēndran said in this way

**ena veguṇḍu** – As soon as he heard these words, the supreme being became very furious at the crocodile. parāśara bhattar says in his śrī rangarāja stavam “*bhagvatas tvarāyai nama*:” (Oh emperumān! I salute your anger).

**veguṇḍu** – Just as it is said in śrī rāmāyaṇam āraṇya kāṇḍam 24-34 “*kōpamāhārayat tīvram*” (if someone commits an offence on him, he puts on an act of anger) if the offence is committed on him, the anger will be something invited; if the offence is committed on his devotee, just as it is mentioned in śrī rāmāyaṇam yudda kāṇḍam 59-136 “*kōpasya vaśamēyivān”* (he comes under the influence of anger), anger will become his basic nature. In this case, since the offence was on his distinguished devotee, his anger was furious.

## 45. Tirada sirrattal

*tīrāda sīṟṟattāl senṟiraṇḍu kūṟāga īrā adanai idar kadindān emperumān                             51*

**Пословный перевод:**

tīrāda sīṟṟattāl senṟu – going (to the bank of that pond) with uncontrolled fury; iraṇḍu kūṟāga – as two pieces; īrā – split (that crocodile); adanai idar kadindān emperumān – emperumān who removed the sorrow of gajēndran

**Перевод:**

**tīrāda sīṟṟattāl – his anger could not be controlled even after protecting the elephant. His uncontrolled anger stemmed from the thought “What is the point in my letting the elephant be initially caught by the crocodile and then protecting him after he had been hurt?” After the mahābhārata war, it is said “nātisvastamanā yayau” (he left without his mental agony removed). He left for śrīvaikuṇṭam with the thought “I did not do anything after draupadi called out to me as ‘gōvinda’”**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**sīṟṟattāl senṟu** – it appeared that he went on the vehicle of anger and not on the vehicle *garuda*. Since he was under the control of anger, he went in the path showed by anger. He did not go as per his decision.

**iraṇḍu kūṟāga īrā** – if he had tried to pull the crocodile, the elephant would have got hurt more. Hence he split the crocodile into two, using his *cakrāyudam* (disc) such that the crocodie released its grip on the elephant’s leg.\`

**adanai idar kadindān** – he assuaged the hurt elephant by gently rubbing his divine hand on the place where the crocodile had gripped, blowing on that spot with his mouth, fomenting that spot with the soft end of his divine upper cloth etc. As per gajēndran, hurdle to his kainkaryam \[of submitting the blossomed lotus flower at the divine foot of emperumān\] is the reason for sorrow. As per emperumān, the hurt caused by crocodile is the reason for sorrow.

**emperumān** – the fortune-teller refers to him as “My Lord” as if she had obtained all the comforting acts of emperumān that the elephant had got.

## 46. Perayiram udaiyan

*pērāyiram udaiyān pēyppeṇḍīr num magaḷait tīrā nōy seydān ena uraittāḷ – cikkenamaṟṟu                     52*

**Пословный перевод:**

pēr āyiram udaiyān – one who has a thousand names (which reflect such innumerable activities); pēy peṇḍīr – Oh womenfolk, who have lost your sense of knowledge; tīrā nōy seydān ena uraittāḷ – (the fortune teller) ended her narration saying that only he caused such incurable disease

**Перевод:**

**pēr āyiram udaiyān – he has a thousand names in order to protect his devotees, by indulging in such noble work. The term thousand does not refer to the numeral one thousand but to an inestimable number, just as it has been said in puruśa sūktam 1 “sahasra śīrśa” (thousand heads). nammāḻvār too said “pērum ōr āyiram piṟa pala udaiya emperumān” (my svāmy (lord) who has a thousand names). Just as emperumān has innumerable qualities, forms, wealth etc for his devotees to experience deeply, he has thousands of names too for them to meditate on.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**pēyppeṇḍīr** – Even though I said that she has got this disease because she has not attained emperumān, didn’t you people doubt whether this would have come about because of other deities? You have lost your knowledge \[of what is correct and what is incorrect\] because of excessive love \[towards your daughter\].

**num magaḷai** – the one who was born in your womb. Will your daughter, who is a supreme devotee of emperumān, and who was born in your stomach, be afflicted by the supreme deity or by other deities?

**tīrā nōy seydān ena uraittāḷ** – she says that this disease will never be cured, to those who ask her as to when it will get cured. Just as it has been said in [tiruvāimoḻi 4-6-1](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2017/03/thiruvaimozhi-4-6-1-thirpparai-yam/) “*uṟṟa nalnōy*” (attained a good disease) since this disease of devotion towards bhagavān is good to be accepted, she says that this is an incurable disease when they ask with trepidation “What should we do if this disease is cured?” Since this will exist until ātmā (soul) exists and will continue even after the soul reaches śrīvaikuṇṭam, she says that this will not get cured ever. This is termed as a disease since it is separation from emperumān leading to sorrow.

**ena uraittāḷ** – since the words of the fortune-teller are like a gentle waft of breeze mixed with droplets of water falling on her, parakāla nāyagi is reiterating the words.

**uraittāḷ** – how beautifully did the fortune-teller describe these! It appears that just to hear her words, one can permanently be in this disease.

## 47. Aranum allamai

*ārānum allāmai kēttengaḷ ammanaiyum pōrār vēṟkaṇṇīr avanāgil pūnduḻāy                                             53 tārādoḻiyumē tannadicci allaḷē maṟṟu ārānum allanē enṟoḻindāḷ – nān avanai                                        54*

**Пословный перевод:**

engaḷ ammanaiyum – (further) my mother; maṟṟu ārānum allāmai cikkena kēttu – after hearing firmly that no other lowly deity is the reason for this disease; pōr Ar vēl kaṇṇīr – Oh friends who have eyes like a spear which is battle-ready \[implying that the eyes are sharp\]!; avanāgil – if it is emperumān (who caused this disease); pū tuḻāy tārādu oḻiyumē – will he not give his prasādam (mercy) of tuḷasi which she \[parakāla nāyagi\] so desires; than adicci allaḷē – (she is) his servitor; maṟṟu ārānum allanē enṟu oḻindāḷ – (the mother) left without any worry, after saying that there is surely no one else who has caused this disease; nān – I (meanwhile); avanai – his

**Перевод:**

**When the fortune teller said like this \[that it was emperumān who has thousands of names who caused this disease in parakāla nāyagi\], what did the mothers say?**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**cikkena maṟṟu ārānum allāmai kēttu engaḷ ammanaiyum** – the fortune teller said “this disease came about because of emperumān”. My mother asked repeatedly “Are you sure that there is no other deity who had any role in this?” After ascertaining this . . . .

**pōrār vēl kaṇṇīr avan āgil pūnduḻāy tārādu oḻiyumē** – *pōrār vēl kaṇṇīr* – Oh those who have eyes similar to a battle-ready spear! The opinion here is that seeing the palpable tension in them, the mother is asking them as to why they are feeling stressed.

**avanāgil pūnduḻāy tārādu oḻiyumē** – tiruviruttam 69 says “*nīrēṟṟu aḷanda nediya pirān aruḷāvidumē*” (Will the supreme entity who took water given by mahābali as a token of granting him what he had asked for and who measured all the worlds, not shower his mercy on us?) When we have submitted ourselves to him, to say that he will not fulfil our wishes is similar to saying “wet this \[article\] with fire” which is totally at variance \[like an oxymoron\]. Just as it is mentioned in śrī rāmāyaṇam ayōdyā kāṇḍam 18-30 “*rāmō dvir nābibhāśatē*” (śrī rāma will not speak with two different meanings) and “*ētat vratam mama*” (I have taken a vow to protect those who have surrendered to me), he will not deny her the tuḷasi which she \[parakāla nāyagi\] has desired. Hence, there is no need for you to get anxious.

**than adicci allaḷē** – Even if, by chance, he does not grant her what she desires, does she not have the relationship with him by which she can hold on to his divine feet and obtain what she desires? Hence it is certain that her wish will be fulfilled.

**maṟṟu ārānum allanē** – after ensuring that no other lowly deity has caused this disease in her, the mother felt a huge load off her chest and became clear in her mind, “There is no need for me to worry about her who has an everlasting relationship with emperumān”. What did she do?

**oḻindāḷ** – she took rest \[she got rid of her worries\]

**nān** – when I was playing with the bundle of flowers, I had attempted to make him engage in *madal* to attain me. But having lost out to him, after seeing him dancing with his pots, I am now preparing to engage in *madal* to attain him.

**avanai** – he manifested his beautiful form and made me to exist exclusively for him.

## 48. Karar tirumeni

*kār ār tirumēni kaṇḍaduvē kāraṇamāp pērāp pidaṟṟāt tiri taruvan pinnaiyum                       55 īrāp pugudalum ivvudalait taṇvādai*

**Пословный перевод:**

kār Ar tirumēni kaṇḍaduvē kāraṇamā – vorśipping his blackish form, as the reason; pērā pidaṟṟā pinnaiyum tiri taruvan – transforming (from tirumangai āḻvār, as a male, to parakāla nāyagi, as a female), talking incoherently, and furthermore, roaming around \[aimlessly\]; taṇ vādai – the cool breeze; ivvudalai īrāp pugudalum – entering my body after splitting it

**Перевод:**

**kārār tirumēni kaṇḍaduvē kāraṇamā – seeing his divine body, which is pleasing to the eyes of the beholder, as the reason. parakāla nāyagi’s mother is one who is fully engaged with the qualilties of basic nature \[of emperumān’s\] like riśis (sages) while the pirātti (parakāla nāyagi) is one who is fully engaged with the divine form and the characteristics of the divine form like āḻvārs. For those who are engaged with the qualities of basic nature, even if they get separated \[from emperumān\] they can sustain themselves with the knowledge of those qualities. However, there is nothing to sustain those who are engaged with the beauty of the divine form and who get separated from it. Since the mother had knowledge of the qualities \[of emperumān\] like the sages, she was able to sustain herself. For the pirātti, this became impossible as she was engaged with the divine form.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**pērā** – I transformed myself. I lost my form as the king of tirumangai, a male, and took the female form of parakāla nāyagi.

**pidaṟṟā** – I lost my quality of self-control as a female and started talking incoherently.

**tiri taruvan pinnaiyum** – once a woman loses her self-control, it is better that she dies. Instead of doing that, I roamed around with the body which tortured me, just like the *yātanā śarīram* (body which a soul takes in hell which will make it to endure all the pain inflicted during punishment in hell) which cannot be got rid of.

**īrāppugudalum** – when the northerly, cold wind enters the body and splits it, one can remain without suffering the torture for some time, at least. However, this wind, even as it entered the body, split it.

**ivvudalai** – two interpretations: 1) emaciated body which is so weak that once someone even touches it, the body will fall down and the blame for killing will fall on the one who touched it; 2) this body should have fallen the moment it got separated from emperumān; since it is still alive, this punishment is apt for this body.

**taṇvādai** – since it is the breeze which is coming from the snow covered mountains (north), being cold by its nature, it will be like a weapon for those who are boiling due to the pain of separation. Since it is chill, it appears to be like a poisoned weapon and is torturing further.

## 49. Sora marukkum

*sōrā maṟukkum vagai aṟiyēn sūḻ kuḻalār                      56 ārānum ēsuvar ennum adan paḻiyai vārāmal kāppadaṟku vāḷā irundoḻindēn                   57*

**Пословный перевод:**

sōrā maṟukkum – creating tiredness and troubling; vagai aṟiyēn – I am unable to describe the ways to trouble; sūḻ kuḻalār ārānum – any lady with abundant tresses; ēsuvar ennum adan paḻiyai – due to the blame from their abuse; vārāmal kāppadaṟku – to prevent from reaching; vāḷā irundu oḻindēn – I did not do anything

**Перевод:**

**sōrā maṟukkum – makes (one) to become unconscious; once one becomes conscious, it also causes trouble.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**vagai aṟiyēn** – I can only say that it bewilders me and  hurts me, but I cannot explain as to in which way it causes bewilderment and hurt.

**sūḻ kuḻalār** – those fortunate women whose tresses and beauty are not disturbed by this cold wind. The opinion is that those women have not fallen in love with this person \[emperumān\].

**ārānum** – among the people that I know of, there is none who will mistake my distinguished act \[of engaging in *madal*\]. Unknown to me, there may be a few people. They may be ignorant and may not be apt to be spoken of.

**ēsuvar ennum adan paḻiyai** – the blame which comes out of their abusing the *nāyaga* \[emperumān\] and parakāla nāyagi. **vārāmal kāppadaṟku** – to prevent any fault from occurring to his greatness and my simplicity

**vāḷā irundoḻindēn** –  just as a person starves when grains are available in plenty, without having to even assist in the process of cultivation, when he \[emperumān\] incarnated, manifested himself as a simple person to everyone and engaged in dancing with the pots, I remained quiet. Instead of engaging with *madal* amidst the people who were watching him on either side of the long street when he was dancing with the pots, I wasted time by fearing for the blame which would have come my way.

## 50. Varay madanenje

*vārāy madanenjē vandu maṇivaṇṇan sīrār tiruttuḻāy mālai namakkaruḷit                                          58 tārān tarum enṟu iraṇḍattil onṟadanai*

**Пословный перевод:**

mada nenjē – Oh mind which has lost its intellect!; vārāy – get up (to go as a messenger); vandu – going near that emperumān; maṇivaṇṇan – that emperumān who has a form similar to a bluish gem; namakku aruḷi – showering his grace on us; sīr Ar tiru tuḻāy mālai – distinguished tuḷasi garland; tārān tarum enṟu iraṇḍattil onṟadanai – a word, whether he will grant it or not (word which I sent him \[as a message through my mind\])

**Перевод:**

**vārāy – rise up, at least now**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**mada nenjē** – since the heart is lying inactive due to the pain of separation, this verse cannot be interpreted as obedient heart. It should be construed as – āḻvār calling out to his heart which has lost its intellect. Since we did not want to do anything which will lower his *svarūpam* (basic nature), we have suffered all along. Now, at least, rise up.

**vandu** – the opinion is “to go”. Wouldn’t reaching a place indicate going \[leaving\] from another place! Since the place where she must be is the place where he is, going to that place appears to be coming to that place, for her.

**maṇivaṇṇan** – one who has a divine form which will make the person who has separated from him to engage in *madal.*

**sīrār tiruttuḻāy mālai** – it is distinguished garland because it has been applied by a great person who does not desire other benefits \[but desires only emperumān himself\]. Just as it has been mentioned in periya tirumoḻi 11-1-9 “*mikka sīrttoṇḍaritta pūnduḷavin vāsamē*” (the sweet fragrance of tuḷasi flower which has been applied by a highly distinguished devotee), since it is a garland which has been applied by one who does not desire other benefits, it will have a distinguished fragrance like the one applied by nityasūris, as mentioned in tiruviruttam 21 “*sūttu nanmālaigaḷ*” (the distinguished garlands applied by nityasūris). The garland applied by those who desire lowly benefits, who are referred to in bhagavat gītā by the term “*kāmātmāna*:” will have the smell of burnt flowers, reflecting their hearts which desire other benefits from emperumān.

**namakku aruḷi** – when we were together in an affectionate way, he would give me his garlands even when I did not want them; after we were separated, despite seeing my distress, that he did not help me shows that he has lost his affection for me. Hence, if he takes pity on me, he may give me.

**tārān tarum enṟu iraṇḍattil onṟu adanai** – whether he gives after thinking about the earlier state and showers his mercy or due to his independent nature, he does not give, find out what he chooses to do and tell me \[parakāla nāyagi tells her heart\]. In other words, if he gives the tuḷasi garland, we will sustain and live. If he does not give, we will give up our life and get rid of the sorrow.

## 51. Aranum onnadar

*ārānum onnādār kēḷāmē sonnakkāl                                   59 ārāyumēlum paṇikēttu adanṟu enilum pōrādu oḻiyādē pōndidu nī enṟēṟku                          60*

**Пословный перевод:**

onnādār ārānum kēḷāmē sonnakkāl – when you tell him \[my words\] without anyone, who is not favourable to him, listening; paṇi kēttu – after hearing those words; ārāyumēlum – whether he enquires (affectionately); adu anṟu enilum – or he totally denies knowing about me; nī pōrādu oḻiyādē pōndidu – come back here, soon, without staying there itself; enṟēṟku – agreeing with my words

**Перевод:**

**ārānum onnādār kēḷāmē sonnakkāl – \[parakāla nāyagi tells her heart\] when you tell him my words, you should be very careful about one matter. There are many who wish to destroy his qualities. Ensure that you tell him without anyone among his enemies listening to your conversation. Even when she \[the nāyagi\] is about to engage in madal which will destroy his form, qualities and wealth, she cautions her heart, out of affection for him, to tell him, out of earshot of his enemies. At a time when she, unable to bear the separation, is destroying her svarūpam (basic nature) to engage in madal, she is showing affection for him, thus revealing her true basic nature. īsn’t affection \[towards emperumān\] the basic nature of these āḻvārs!**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**ārāyumēlum paṇi kēttu** – as soon as he sees you, even if he asks you “What all did she suffer? Is she still alive? Even if she were alive, she would have lost her complexion and only the body would be there; until I go back and unite with her, will she be alive? Will I attain her?” you hear those words well and tell him my condition. Alternatively, the words could be rearranged as *paṇikēttu ārāyumēlum* in which case the meaning would be – after hearing your words (which I had told you) if he analyses . . .

**adanṟu enilum** – instead of analysing like that, if he says like King duśyanta \[who denied knowing śakuntalā\], “I do not know anyone like that”

**pōrādu oḻiyādē** – even if he denies knowing me, do not wait by telling him again in such a way that his heart becomes aware and awaiting his response.

**pōndidu nī** – unlike the one who forgot me even after knowing my infirmity, you, at least, return to me.

**pōrādu oḻiyādē pōndidu nī enṟēṟku** – I showed this heinous heart the way to stay there. Holding on to my words, telling it “Do not stay there”, it perhaps thought “There is some gain in staying here“ and stayed there itself. The commentator for this prabandam, periyavāccān piḷḷai, narrates a simile for this incident, with an event in villages those days: A rich landlord, after coming to know that his farm hand was stealing his produce by hiding it in canals and on the ridges of fields, replaced the culprit farm hand with a new one. If he tells the new farm hand “You should not hide the grains in the ridges of fields and canals” it would tantamount to telling the new farm hand that hiding the grains in ridges and canals is the way to steal the produce. Much in the say way, hearing my counsel to it “Do not stay there itself” the heart now knows that *bhagavat viśayam* (matter relating to emperumān) is not something which one can see and return and decided to stay put there itself.

## 52. Karar kadal

*kārār kadal vaṇṇan pin pōna nenjamum vārādē ennai maṟandadudān valvinaiyēn                               61*

**Пословный перевод:**

kār Ar kadal vaṇṇan pin pōna – one which went near the dark ocean complexioned \[emperumān\]; nenjamum – my mind; vārādē – without any thought of returning; ennai maṟandadudān – stayed there itself, forgetting me; val vinaiyēn – I, the heinous sinner

**Перевод:**

**kār Ar kadal vaṇṇan pin pōna nenjamum vārādu ennai maṟandadu – my heart, which shed tears as it left me, looking at my distressful state, fully engaged with his beauty on seeing him, and forgot all about me. **valvinaiyēn** – I have committed such heinous sins that my heart will not help me. Just as bharata said in śrī rāmāyaṇam “*matpāpamēvātra nimittamāsīt*” (it is due to my sins that śrī rāma went to the forest) and sītāppirātti said “*mamaiva duśkrutam mahadasti*” (there is a huge sin of mine \[which is causing these difficulties\]), parakāla nāyagi is blaming herself.

## 53. Urar ugappade

*ūrār ugappadē āyinēn maṟṟenakku ingu ārāyvārillai aḻal vāy meḻugu pōl                                        62 nīrāy urugum en āvi nedum kaṇgaḷ*

**Пословный перевод:**

ūrār ugappadē āyinēn – I was in ruins such that the world celebrated; enakku ingu maṟṟu – no one else here, for me; ārāyvār illai – none with whom I could talk; en āvi – my ātmā; aḻal vāy meḻugu pōl nīrāy urugum – started melting like wax kept near fire; nedu kaṇgaḷ tān – the long eyes too

**Перевод:**

**ūrār ugappadē āyinēn – There are people who think that they have to take all the efforts to attain what they desire to get. Just as they wanted, I too started taking efforts to attain what I desired. Here the term ūrār refers to those who are not favourable to her. Earlier, she used to say “Only emperumān is the means to attain everything. There is nothing that we can attempt on our own in order to attain him”. They (the unfavourable ones) were waiting for an opportunity to admonish her; now, she has reached a state where they could celebrate by saying “Have you seen how she is suffering after thinking that she can attain him only through him? Those who think ‘He is the means’ will have to suffer like this only. All these people have to come to our way only”**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

Could you not sustain yourself by talking it over with someone when you get highly distressed?

**maṟṟenakku ingu ārāyvār illai** – when my heart is not helping me, who else is there for conversing? Since she says *maṟṟu illai* (there is no one else), only he has to come and examine her troubles; else, there is no other way. Haven’t these āḻvārs said as in [tiruvāimoḻi 9-9-6](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2019/09/thiruvaimozhi-9-9-6-avanudai-arul/) “*avvaruḷ alllana aruḷumalla*” (apart from emperumān’s grace, no one else’s mercy could be termed as grace) and as in [mudal tiruvandādi 15](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2017/06/mudhal-thiruvandhadhi-15/) “*vaiyagattup pallār aruḷum paḻudu*” (there is no use of the grace of many others’ in this world. Only his grace counts)?

**aḻalvāy meḻugu pōl nīrāy urugum ennāvi** – When asked “Could you remain indifferent like this? Should you not pull up your heart forcefully and sustain yourself?” she says “When my ātmā melts due to this separation just like wax melts when kept near fire, how could I pull up my heart forcefully?”

## 54. Urar urangilum

*ūrār uṟangilum tān uṟangā uttaman tan                   63 pērāyinavē pidaṟṟuvan pinnaiyum kārār kadal pōlum kāmattar āyinār                                 64 ārē pollāmai aṟivār adu niṟka*

**Пословный перевод:**

ūrār uṟangilum uṟangā – even when everyone else in that place sleeps, they \[the long eyes\] will not sleep; uttaman than – that supreme entity’s; pēr āyinavē – only his divine names; pidaṟṟuvan – I kept saying incoherently; mēlum – further; kārār kadal pōlum kāmattar āyinār – those who have unlimited lust, like the deep sea; ārē – who; pollāmai aṟivār – will know what is not in line with one’s basic nature; adu niṟka – let that remain

**Перевод:**

**nedum kaṇgaḷ uṟaṟ uṟangilum tān uṟangā – when asked, “Could you not forget this distress due to separation, by sleeping”, she says “Even if everyone else sleeps, my eyes do not sleep” just as it has been said in tiruvāimoḻi 2-1-1 “āyum amarulagum tunjilum nī tunjāyāl” (even if my mother and entities like the nityasūris, who are always awake and who constantly keep envisioning emperumān, sleep, you do not fall asleep).**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**nedum kaṇgaḷ** – the hugeness of the eyes, which were the reason for ensnaring him, is now a huge load on my head. Just as it has been said in śrī rāmāyaṇam sundara kāṇḍam 16-5 “*asitēkśaṇā*” (sītāppirātti with black eyes), when they were together, her eyes were capable of making him to be involved with her.

**ūrāruṟangilum tānuṟangā** –  those who are in the place where she resides are not aware of the concept of sleeping, just as it has been said in [tiruvāimoḻi 6-7-2](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2018/03/thiruvaimozhi-6-7-2-urum-nadum/) “*ūrum nādum ulagamum tannaip pōl*” (the place, country and the world were all alike her). Even if such people sleep, she says that her eyes will not sleep.

**uttaman** – one who has the greatness of being easily approachable. He enticed me by showing his simplicity and made me become sleepless.

**pēr āyinavē pidaṟṟuvan** – isn’t it better not to think about him when he does not help? Unable to do even this, I will keep reciting his names incoherently; I will recite them in a random way. For those who are deeply devoted to emperumān, is it possible to carry out anything in an orderly way?

**pinnaiyum** – They started saying a few comforting words, beyond that, such as “Did not the daughter of king janaka \[sītāppirātti\] wait patiently for ten months?”

**kār Ar kadal pōlum kāmattar āyinār ārē pollāmai aṟivār** – when the desire crosses all limits, just like a deep sea, is it possible to wait patiently, thinking that the people \[of the town\] will abuse? What does it matter to me as to who they were? I will consider their desire to be less than mine. For those like me who have uncontrolled desire to see the divine form of kaṇṇa (śrī kriṣṇa) which has the complexion of dark clouds, is it possible to control that with *svarūpajñānam* \[knowledge about one’s true nature, viz. one is totally subservient to the supreme being\]? If sītāppirātti could wait patiently with her *svarūpajñānam* saying “*tat tasya sadruśam bhavēt*” (it is apt for him (śrī rāma) to come here and take me along) I can only conclude that their desire is inferior to mine. I cannot be faulted for this.

**adu niṟka** – you do not have to say “You cannot establish that what you are doing is correct, because you are doing it”. There is authentic evidence to show that great people have done this. Distinguished women from the past have destroyed their femininity for the sake of love.

## 55. Aranum adanum

*ārānum ādānum allaḷ avaḷ kāṇīr                                        65 vārār vanamulai vāsavadattai enṟu ārānum sollappaduvāḷ avaḷum tan                                    66 pēr āyam ellām oḻiyap  perum teruvē*

**Пословный перевод:**

avaḷ ārānum ādānum allaḷ kāṇīr – she is not any ordinary person (she is very intelligent); vār Ar vana mulaiy vāsavadattai enṟu – she, who has covered her bosoms in a corset, is known as vāsavadattai; ārānum sollap paduvāḷ – she is praised by all; avaḷum – that vāsavadattai too; than pēr āyam ellām oḻiya – leaving aside all her friends; perum teruvē – in a huge street

**Перевод:**

**ārānum ādānum allaḷ – ārānum allaḷ – she is not having any deficiency in her intellect. ādānum allaḷ – she is not without intelligence like insentient entities.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**avaḷ kāṇīr** – Hear about her demeanour as I narrate.

**vārār vanamulai** – this talks about her youthful state where her bosoms cannot be left on their own and have to be covered in a corset. They should be supported by the beloved and if the beloved is not there, by her corset.

**vāsavadattai enṟu ārānum sollappaduvāḷ** – īsn’t she very famous? īsn’t she praised by all the distinguished persons? īsn’t she celebrated as a person without any deficiency in the assembly of sītāppirātti?

**avaḷum** – parakāla nāyagi says that it was not she alone who followed unacceptable means to attain the beloved; even she \[vāsavadattai\] did the same.

**than pērāyam ellām oḻiya** – I am saying all these words in order to make you people (parakāla nāyagi’s friends) agree with me since I do not want to let go of you. However, did she \[vāsavadattai\] not leave the large gathering of her friends in order to attain her beloved?  Just as a person cannot step on to fire, she could not stand amidst her friends; is she not more determined than I?

**perum teruvē** – did she seek a corner of the palace after leaving her friends? Despite being a princess, did she not walk on the major road?

## 56. Tar ar

*tār ār tadam tōḷ taḷaikkālan pin pōnāḷ                                    67ūrār igaḻnditappattāḷē maṟṟenakku inguārānum kaṟpippār nāyagarē nān avanai                                           68*

**Пословный перевод:**

tār Ar tada tōḷ talai kālan pin pōnāḷ – she went behind vatsarājan \[her beloved\] who has huge shoulders with garland decorating them, and who had his legs chained; ūrār igaḻnditappattāḷē – Was she spoken ill of, by the people? (no); ingu – in my present state; enakku – for me (who has this determination); maṟṟum kaṟpippār – those who counselled patience which is opposite of my determination; ārānum – whoever it may be; nāyagarē – can their words be binding on me? (I will not listen to them); nān avanai – I will, his \[emperumān’s\]

**Перевод:**

**tār Ar tadam tōḷ taḷai kālan pin pōnāḷ – she went behind her beloved person, vatsarājan who was chained by her father**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**tār Ar tadam tōḷ taḷaikkālan** – not bothered about the chain around his legs, she saw his shoulders and the garland on his shoulders; desiring him,  she followed him.

**ūrār igaḻndidap pattāḷē** – the people of the land only praised her and did not abuse her (this narration is mentioned in bhāsan’s drama as well as in the twelfth chapter of *katāsaritsāgaram* which is based on old books such as *bruhatkatai* and historical facts).

**maṟṟu enakku ingu ārānum kaṟpippār nāyagarē** – those who think of correcting me, by saying words against my determination, are not my lords. I went past the state mentioned in [tiruvāimoḻi 3-7-1](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2016/09/thiruvaimozhi-3-7-1-payilum-sudaroli/) “*payilum tiruvudaiyār evarēlum avar kaṇḍīr payilum piṟappidaidōru emmaiyāḷum paramarē*” (whoever is fully devoted to emperumān, irrespective of their clan, conduct etc, they will be my lord in any number of births in this materialistic world) and in periya tirumoḻi 7-4-1 “*tāḷ toḻuvār kāṇmin en tallai mēlārē*” (those who worship the divine feet of emperumān are those whose divine feet I will sustain on my head). Those who accept my determination will be my lord. Those who counsel me patience by saying “You will attain him in the course of time. Wait patiently until then” are not my lords; they are people who I do not need. Her state is much similar to that of parānguśa nāyagi as mentioned in [tiruvāimoḻi 5-3-6](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2017/06/thiruvaimozhi-5-3-6-annai-en-seyyilen/) “*annai en seyyilen Ur en sollilen*” (what does it matter to me as to what my mother does or what the people of the land tell about me).

**nān** – I, who have moved away from those who are not in agreement with my determination.

**avanai** – he, who made me reject everyone other than himself.

**nān avanai** – I who set out to play with bundles of flower and he who set out to dance with pots; this could be construed as “I set out to make him fall for my beauty, ended up with falling for that dancer and losing my heart to him”

## 57. Karar tirumeni

*kārār tirumēni kāṇum aḷavum pōyc cīrār tiruvēngadamē tirukkōvalūrē                                                        69*

**Пословный перевод:**

kār Ar tirumēni kāṇum aḷavum pōy – going from place to place \[one divine abode to another\] until I see his divine form which is like a dark cloud; sīr Ar tiruvēngadamē tirukkōvalūrē – the eminent tiruvēngadam and tirukkōvalūr

**Перевод:**

**kār Ar tirumēni kāṇum aḷavum pōy – did she not suffer like this only because she saw his divine form initially? When asked as to till when her determination to engage in madal will last, she says that it will be most appropriate if she could see him, she will then stop engaging it madal. Alternatively, when her friend says “You should unite with him” she responds “It is not necessary to unite with him. Earlier too, I suffered after seeing him; even now, it is sufficient if I see him”.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

When asked as to how she is going to see him, she says . . . .

**sīrār tiruvēngadamē . . . .**  – I will go to each place where he is manifesting each of his \[innumerable\] qualities, show my body which has been eaten away by separation, and by destroying his quality manifested in each of those places I will make him come running to me in despair; thus, I will see him. Now she talks about some of those divine abodes where he is manifesting such qualities:

**sīr Ar tiruvēngadamē** – great tiruvēngadam. amalanādipirān 3 says “*mandi pāy vadavēngada māmalai vānavargaḷ sandhi seyya ninṟān*” (the divine abode of tiruvēngadam, located in the northern direction, has monkeys jumping from one tree to another; emperumān has taken residence here so that nityasūris could come here and carry out worship); [tiruvāimoḻi 1-8-3](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2015/09/thiruvaimozhi-1-8-3-kannavan/) says “*kaṇṇāvān enṟum maṇṇōr viṇṇōrkkut taṇṇār vēngada viṇṇōṟ veṟpanē*” (one who has the ever-cool tiruvēngadam, which is nityasūri’s place, is the protector for both samsāris and nityasūris); [nānmugan tiruvandādi 45](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2018/08/nanmugan-thiruvandhadhi-45/) says “*taṇṇaruvi vēngadamē vānōrkkum maṇṇōrkkum vaippu*” (it is only tiruvēngadam, with its cool streams, which is the wealth for nityasūris and śrīvaiṣṇavas on earth). Thus we see that tiruvēngadam has the greatness of being equal to both nityasūris and to nityasamsāris. parakāla nāyagi says that by going there “I will tell all the people how indifferent such emperumān has been to me and ensure that he is not the lord of both *vibhūtis* (realms)”

**tirukkōvalūrē** – [mudal tiruvandādi 86](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2017/09/mudhal-thiruvandhadhi-86/) says “*nīyum tirumagaḷum ninṟāyāl kunṟeduttup pāyum pani maṟutta panbāḷā vāsal kadai kaḻiyā utpugā kāmar pūngōval idai kaḻiyē paṟṟi ini*” (Oh one who is simple and who held the gōvardana hill to block the torrential rain! How amazing is it that you and your pirātti are standing in the corridor of the hermitage (where the first three āḻvārs were standing) wherein no one could go out or no one could enter, and are showering your mercy!) emperumān is still standing there since it is the place where the first tre āḻvārs assembled together, enjoying the smell of that soil. parakāla nāyagi says that she will go to tirukkōvalūr, show her emaciated form and tell everyone how he gave up on his devotee.

## 58. Ure maditkatci

*ūrē madiḷ katcci ūragamē pēragamē pērā maṟudu iṟuttān veḷḷaṟaiyē vehkāvē                  70*

**Пословный перевод:**

madiḷ kacci ūragamē – ūragam, which is within the fortified kānci; pēragamē – the sannidi in appakkudattān, tiruppēr; pērā maṟudu iṟuttān veḷḷaṟaiyē – tiruveḷḷaṟai where kaṇṇa, who broke through the two maruda trees which were erect, has taken residence; vehkāvē – tiruvehkā

**Перевод:**

**madiḷ kacci ūragamē – the protective wall built around the town of kānci is appropriate to the strength and firmness of the supreme entity who has taken residence within in, at tiru ūragam. Here, emperumān stands as trivikrama, who measured all the worlds, and is gently massaging all the people, without any distinction between vaśiśta (learned person) and caṇḍāḷa (lowly person), just like a mother gently massages her child. parakāla nāyagi says that she will go there and displaying her physical form, will tell the people there that emperumān who touched everyone in his trivikrama incarnation, did not touch her alone.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**pēragamē** – in ūragam there is the protective wall which acts as a boundary to check people coming in. Without any such wall, lying between two rivers, emperumān is giving darśan to those who travel through that way, without any protection, and is displaying himself and his belongings. It is famous for this reason. She says that she will go to *tiruppēr*, show her form and tell everyone as to how he is granting everything to his devotee.

**pērā marudu iṟuttān veḷḷaṟaiyē** – the term *pērā* in *pērā marudu* could taken as an adjective for *marudu*; the meaning for *perāmarudu* would then be the *marudu* tree, inside which two demons had pervaded, which was very firm. Alternatively, the term *pērā* could be taken as *pērndu* (to be uprooted) and the meaning for *pērā marudu* would then be that kaṇṇa took baby steps towards the *marudu* tree and uprooted it by walking through it. Since the verse is *marudiṟuttān veḷḷaṟaiyē*, it would mean that unlike śrī gōkulam where demons had pervaded every single blade of grass in order to kill kaṇṇa, tiruveḷḷaṟai is a divine abode where people with lot of affection for emperumān live. These people are descendants of those who had lived there when periyāḻvār had sung the pāsuram as in periyāḻvār tirumoḻi 2-8-1 “*cadurargaḷ veḷḷaṟai ninṟāy! andiyam pōdu iduvāgum aḻaganē! kāppida vārāy*” (this is the time of dusk \[when bad elements would be roaming around\]. Oh one who is standing in *tiruveḷḷaṟai* where affectionate and intelligent people are residing! Come inside and be protected) and are hence very affectionate towards emperumān. \[parakāla nāyagi says\] I will go to such a divine abode, show my form to the people who are there and announce to them “See how affectionate has he been towards one who has been affectionate towards him”

**vehkāvē** – isn’t emperumān lying in the divine abode of tiruvehkā with the name *sonnavaṇṇam seyda perumān* (supreme entity who did as per the wishes of his devotees)? This has been amply manifested in his act of rolling his bed and going behind tirumaḻisai āḻvār when āḻvār told him to roll his bed and walk out of the temple along with him as mentioned in his pāsuram “*kaṇikaṇṇan pōginṟān kāmarupūngacci maṇivaṇṇa nī kidakka vēṇḍā tuṇivudaiya sennāppulavanum yān pōginṟēn nīyum un pannāgappāy suruttikkoḷ*” (Oh one with the form of gemstones! (my assistant) *kaṇikaṇṇan* is leaving this place; I, a tamil poet with firm tongue, too am leaving this place; you should not remain here anymore after we have left. You too roll up your mattress of ādiśēśan and follow us). Later, when tirumaḻisai āḻvār decided to return to kānci, he told emperumān to spread his mattress like before, which emperumān duly followed, as mentioned in the pāsuram “*kaṇikaṇṇan pōkkoḻindān kāmarupūngacci maṇivaṇṇa nī kidakka vēṇḍum tuṇivudaiya sennāppulavanum yān pōkkoḻindēn nīyum un painnāgap pāy virittukkoḷ*” (Oh one with the form of gemstones! *kaṇikaṇṇan* is returning to kānci; I too am returning; you should also return with us; hence you roll back your ādiśēśan mattress and follow us). In his haste to follow the instructions of his devotee tirumaḻisai āḻvār, emperumān interchanged the positions of his divine head and divine feet on his ādiśēśan bed and lay back in tiruvehkā itself. parakāla nāyagi says that she will go to such divine place and tell the people assembled there “See whether he has acted as per my words”, show them my emaciated form and destroy that emperumān’s name \[of acting as per the wishes of his devotees\].

## 59. Perali tankal

*pērāli taṇkāl naṟaiyūr tiruppuliyūr*

**Пословный перевод:**

pērāli taṇkāl naṟaiyūr tiruppuliyūr – The famous divine abode of tiruvāli nagar, tiruttaṇkāl, tirunaṟaiyūr, kutta nāttu tiruppuliyūr

**Перевод:**

**pērāli – the famous tiruvāli. Being the place where tirumangai āḻvār was born, it is famous for emperumān offering himself to āḻvār for his enjoyment. I will go there and tell the people “Look at the way he has offered himself to me for my enjoyment, by looking at my form. See and decide for yourself as to for how many days he has starved me”. While she (parakāla nāyagi) is living in tiruvāli, is it apt to say that she will go to tiruvāli and abuse emperumān? Even though she lives there, since it does not appear to exist for her, it does not appear to be close to her and hence she says that she will go to tiruvāli.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**taṇkāl** – Since he removes the difficulties of his devotees, like a cold breeze, the place became *taṇkāl*! I will go there and tell the people who have eyes which can see “Look at my form. Look at the way he has helped me to rid me of my difficulties”

**naṟaiyūr** – is it not an abode which is famous for emperumān’s giving up his independence and giving primacy to pirātti (śrī mahālakśmi)? I vil go there and tell the people “Look at the way he is listening to his pirātti by looking at me, who is her belonging”

**tiruppuliyūr** – Just as it is mentioned in [tiruvāimoḻi 8-9-7](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2019/05/thiruvaimozhi-8-9-7-mellilaich-chelva/) says “*mellilaic celva vaṇkodippulga vīngiḷandāḷ kamugin*“ (even insentient entities exchange affection as seen in the behaviour of betel leaf creeper hugging arecanut tree), it is the place where plants, men and women live united. I will go there and tell them “Look at the way I am united with my beloved”

## 60. Aramam sulnda

*ārāmam sūḻnda arangam kaṇamangai                        71*

**Пословный перевод:**

ārāmam sūḻnda arangam kaṇamangai – tiruvarangam, which is surrounded by beautiful gardens, tirukkaṇṇamangai

**Перевод:**

**ārāmam sūḻnda arangam – arangam (śrīrangam) which is surrounded by gardens. Here, the term ārāmam (gardens) refers to other divine abodes \[of emperumān\]. It is śrīrangam which is surrounded by other divine abodes. In the divine minds of āḻvārs, śrīrangam is the permanent dwelling place of emperumān from where he bestows \[his divine mercy\] on his devotees and the other divine abodes are like gardens where he takes rest during daytime when he goes for hunting. Don’t kings, when they go out for hunting to various places, rest for a while at those places during the noon-time and return to their palaces for resting at night? This is much similar to that. That emperumān protects both nityavibhūti (śrīvaikuṇṭam) and leelāvibhūti (samsāram) while residing at śrīrangam is brought out by kulasēkara āḻvār in his perumāḷ tirumoḻi pāsuram 1-10 “vanperu vānagam uyya amararuyya maṇṇuyya maṇṇulagil manisaruyya tunbamigu tuyaragala ayarvu onṟu illāc cugam vaḷara agamagiḻum toṇḍar vāḻa anbodu then tisai nōkkip paḷḷi koḷḷum aṇiyarangan tirumuṟṟattu . . . .” (in the courtyard of śrīrangam where tiruvarangan \[emperumān\] has taken residence, by lying down facing southern direction, for uplifting upper worlds and those who live there, for uplifting earth and those who are living on earth, uplifting his devotees so that they enjoy bliss without any trace of sorrow . . ). I will go to that place vherefrom he is ruling with his sceptre, show my form and ask “Is this the way he would protect his devotee?”**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**kaṇamangai** – tirumangai āḻvār himself has sung of this divine abode in his periya tirumoḻi 7-10-9 “*kaṇgaḷāraḷavum ninṟu kaṇṇamangaiyuḷ kaṇḍukoṇḍēnē*” (I saw that emperumān till my eyes became satiated, at tirukkaṇṇamangai). I expressed my desire to see him till my eyes became satiated. You could judge for yourself whether I saw him till my eyes became satiated, by looking at my form, says parakāla nāyagi.

## 61. Karar mani nira

*kārār maṇi niṟak kaṇṇanūr viṇṇagaram sīrār kaṇapuram cēṟai tiruvaḻundūr                                          72 kārār kudandai kadigai kadal mallai*

**Пословный перевод:**

**Перевод:**

**kārār maṇi niṟak kaṇṇanūr viṇṇagaram – leaving aside paramapadam where the residents are complete \[with their knowledge, qualitities, activities etc\], emperumān came to tiruviṇṇagar so that he could manifest himself to those who have shortcomings \[in their knowledge, qualities, activities etc\]. ḍidn’t nammāḻvār too mercifully mention in his tiruvāimoḻi 6-3-9 “tannoppār illappan tandanan tana tāḷ niḻalē” (emperumān who is non-pareil, gave me the shadow \[protection\] of his divine feet)! I will show them my form and ask them “Has he manifested himself to me who has shortcomings?”**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**sīrār kaṇapuram** – the great abode of tirukkaṇṇapuram which is the *pukkagam* (place of residence of husband) for parakāla nāyagi. I have expressed my excusive existence for emperumān, as mentioned in periya tirumoḻi 8-9-3 “*kaṇṇapuram onṟu udaiyānukku adiyēn oruvarkku uriyēnō*” (Will I, a servitor of emperumān who resides at tirukkaṇṇapuram, belong to any one else?) I will go to that abode, display my form and tell them “Look at the way he has made me suffer”

**tiruccēṟai** – emperumān residing at tiruccēṟai, is famous for not knowing the difference between the bosom of his mother \[yaśōdā} and others \[pūtanā, the demon sent by kamsan to kill him\] as a child. This has been brought out in periya tirumoḻi 7-4-1 pāsuram ”*kaṇ sōra vengurudi vandiḻiya vendaḻalpōl kūndalāḷai maṇsēra mulaiyuṇḍa mā madalāy*” (Oh infant who sucked the life out of pūtanā, the demon with red coloured hair which looked like fire, such that her eyes lost all energy and a flood of blood flowed out of her!) I will go to that place and tell them how he has shown partiality by not even touching me.

**tiruvaḻundūr** – isn’t this the divyadēsam (divine abode) where he manifested his quality of not sustaining himself if he cannot have butter which has been touched by his devotees? I will go there, tell the people to see my emaciated body and ask them whether he had sustained himself by touching me.

**kārār kudandai** – is invigorating kudandai not famous as the place where kaṇṇan, who is the servitor of his servitors, has taken residence? I will go there and prove to them by displaying my form that I, who am his servitor, am not cruel.

**kadigai** – this divine abode is famous for emperumān who is as sweet as a sugar candy fruit \[fruit which has grown in the plant, which was grovon from the seed of sugar candy, a non-existing entity\], as brought out by the pāsuram in periya tirumoḻi 8-9-4 “*kadigaittadangunṟin misaiyirunda akkārakkani*” (emperumān who is known as *akkārakkani* who has taken residence atop the huge mount of *kadigai*). I will go there and ask “For whom is he residing here with all his sweetness? Is it for his own enjoyment? Is the well-known term *bhaktānām* (for the sake of his devotees) as mentioned in *jitantē ślōkam* a lie?”

**kadal mallai** – Has he not earned fame in *tirukkadanmallai* (a divine abode, now known as māmallapuram) by leaving aside his mattress of *tirvandāḻvān* (ādiśēhan) and lying on the floor, in order to bless his devotee *puṇḍarīkar*? I will go there and ask the people “Is he lying on the floor to attain me or am I lying on the floor to attain him? Look at our forms and decide for yourselves”

## 62. Erar polil

*ērār poḻil sūḻ idavendai nīrmalai                                      73 sīrārum mālirunjōlai tirumōgūr*

**Пословный перевод:**

ērār poḻil sūḻ idavendai nīrmalai – tiruvidavendai which is surrounded by beautiful gardens, tirunīrmalai; sīrārum mālirunjōlai tirumōgūr – beautiful tirumālirunjōlai, tirumōgur

**Перевод:**

**ērār poḻil sūḻ idavendai – tiruvidavendai (a divyadēsam near present day māmallapuram) has beautiful gardens around it so that emperumān, who has taken the form of wild boar in this divyadēśam, can roam around, as an outlet, in line with his form. The emperumān is residing in this divine abode to reflect his infatuation with bhūmippirātti, just as mentioned in tirunedundāṇḍagam 18 “pārvaṇṇa madamangai pattar” (one who is infatuated with bhūmippirātti). I will go there, inform the people as to how he tortured me, a servitor of pirātti and ask them to decide for themselves whether the affection which he shows towards bhūmippirātti is real or an outward show, thus subjecting his love to a measurement.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**nīrmalai** – just as it has been said in tirunedundāṇḍagam 18 “*nīrvaṇṇan nīrmalaikkē pōvēn*” (I will go to tirunīrmalai itself where he is manifesting his characteristic of being simple, like water), tirunīrmalai is the divine abode which acts like shadow for those who have lost their self-restraint while trying to attain him so that they could take comfort. I will tell them to see in my form as to how I, who am engaging in madal after losing my self-restraint, am unable to enter that abode too.

**sīrārum mālirum sōlai** – emperumān remains without moving, like a tree, in the southern direction, which was not regarded highly in ancient days by *vaidikas* (those who follow *vēdams*) and manifests his simple and easily approachable qualities to lowly people too, as brought out in periyāḻvār tirumoḻi 5-3-3 “*punattinaik kiḷḷip pudu avi kātti un ponnadi vāḻga enṟu inakkuṟavar pudiyadu uṇṇum eḻil mālirunjōlai endāy*” (Oh one who is residing in tirumālirunjōlai and who is my lord! The hilly tribal people offer freshly plucked millets to your divine feet, and without asking for any other benefit, worship you and eat the offered millets). I will go there, show my form and establish that his qualities are one of supremacy (and not simplicity) and of being tough to approach and not easily to approach.

**tirumōgūr** – isn’t this the divine abode for emperumān who is blissful with his devotees, without any distinction, through the faculties of mind, body and speech, just as it has been mercifully mentioned in [tiruvāimoḻi 10-1-6](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2019/10/thiruvaimozhi-10-1-6-kuththan-kovalan/) “*tirumōgūr āttan*” (one who has taken residence at tirumōgūr and is trustworthy)? I will show how trustworthy he is, after going there.

## 63. Paror pugalum

*pārōr pugaḻum vadari vadamadurai                             74 ūrāya ellām oḻiyāmē nān avanai*

**Пословный перевод:**

pārōr pugaḻum vadari vadamadurai – śrī badarikāśramam, which is praised by all the people of the world, maturā which is in the northern direction; Ur āya ellām oḻiyāmē – without missing any of the divine abodes (entering all those places)

**Перевод:**

**pārōr pugaḻum vadari – isn’t this the abode which is praised by the people of this world as being the place where emperumān carried out penance for their well being? I will go there, show my form and ask the people “Oh those who can see! Look at me and then decide as to who carried out penance – is it emperumān or I?”**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**vada madurai** – initially when it was the hermitage of the great sages, it was the place where vāmana (one of emperumān’s incarnations) had carried out penance; in rāmāvatāram, it was the place where śrī śatrugna had defeated lavaṇāsura and ruled over the place; in kriṣṇāvatāram, it was the place where emperumān incarnated himself as kaṇṇa. Thus, maturā is the abode which did not lose its connection with *bhagavat viśayam* (matters relating to the supreme being) as brought out by the famous saying “*maturā nāma nagarī puṇyā pāpaharī śubhā*” (the town maturā is pious, removes sins and auspicious). I will go there and show them as to how he ran out of that place because I have reached there.

**ūrāyavellām oḻiyāmē** – will I say a few words in only a few places \[as mentioned in the few verses stated above\] and leave? I will enter all those places which I have not spoken of. It is not possible for me to say everything \[here\]. If you wish, you could follow me to all those places and make a note.

**nān** – I, who am capable of destroying all his qualities which he is boasting of, in all the places where he is entering to hide himself, being fearful of me

**avanai** – he, who is torturing me and who is trumpeting that he is under the control of *arcakas* (who are involved in carrying out timely *ārādana* (worship) of emperumān at various divine abodes)

## 64. Oranai kombu

*ōrānai kombosittu ōrānai kōḷ vidutta                       75 sīrānai sengaṇ nediyānait tēn tuḻāyt tārānai tāmaraipōl kaṇṇānai eṇṇarum sīrp                      76 pērāyiramum pidaṟṟip perum teruvē*

**Пословный перевод:**

ōr ānai kombu osittu ōr ānai kōḷ vidutta sīrānai – having attained the greatness of being partial by breaking the tusk of one elephant, kuvalayāpīdam and removing the suffering of another elephant gajēndran; sem kaṇ nediyānai – having reddish eyes and being unreachable for me; tēn tuḻāy – (being separated from me) tuḷasi with honey dripping from it; tārānai – donning the garland; eṇ aru sīr pēr āyiramum pidaṟṟi – incoherenty reciting a (new) sahasranāma which reflects his innumerable (curel) qualities; peru teruvē – through the main street

**Перевод:**

**Will I stop with destroying his greatness in arcāvatāram (emperumān in idol form) alone? She says that she will also destroy his great qualities in his incarnations.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**ōr ānai kombu osittu ōr ānai kōḷ vidutta sīrānai** – while both are elephants, have you seen the partiality that he has shown in killing kuvalayāpīdam and protecting gajēndran? From this itself, you could see that he has nectar in one eye and poison in the other.

**sīrānai** – he is similar to a sweet which is full of jaggery on the outer side and plain \[tasteless\] puffed rice on the inner. I will announce to the world that outwardly he would appear that he is protecting everyone but in reality he has inferior qualities.

**sengaṇ nediyānai** – what is the purpose in having lotus-like eyes which manifest his motherly forbearance? He is unreachable for me. He is like a cup of cool water with sweet fragrances added, but kept at a place unreachable for me.

**tēn tuḻāyttārānai** – It appears that the garland which is decorating him will drip with honey only when he separates from those who are dear to him. Though he is decked with honey dripping tuḷasi garland, he is one who is *tārān* i.e. does not give that.

**tāmarai pōl kaṇṇānai** – he displays new eyes everyday after separating from me.

**eṇṇarum Sir pērāyiramum pidaṟṟi** – he has a sahasranāmam (thousand divine names) to reflect his auspicious qualities. I will recite another sahasranāmam which will reflect his cruel qualities.

When told to recite the sahasranāmam which she was intending to recite, she says “Will I recite that from a corner which will be heard by just a few people?” What will you do then?

**perum teruvē** – I will recite it in the middle of the street, that too in the main street.

## 65. Urar igalilum

*ūrār igaḻilum ūrādu oḻiyēn nān                                       77vārār pūm peṇṇai madal*

**Пословный перевод:**

ūrār igaḻilum – even if all the people abuse (me); nān – I; vār Ar pū peṇṇai madal ūrādu oḻiyēn – I will not stop from engaging with long, beautiful madal

**Перевод:**

**ūrār igaḻilum – just as vāsavadattai was praised by the people, as mentioned in the 68th verse of this prabandam “ūrār igaḻndidappattāḷē”, the people will praise me too.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

**ūrādu oḻiyēn nān** – instead of praising like that, even if they abuse me, I will not stop from engaging with *madal*.

**vārār pūm peṇṇai madal** – even when they {śrī rāma et al\] had great astrams (weapons) such as brahmāstram, just like they felt inferior to opponents such as indrajit, when I am having my astram *madal* in my hand, will I let my desire remain unfulfilled?

**vārār pūm peṇṇai madal** – Haven’t you seen the long, beautiful *madal* in my hand? Haven’t you seen the brahmāstram in my hand?

There is a tanippādal (separate verse) at the end of the prabandam, attributed to the great tamiḻ poet kambar.

kambar’s song

*ūrādu oḻiyēn ulagaṟiya oṇṇudalīr*  
*sīrār mulaittadangaḷ sēraḷavum – pārellām*  
*anṟōngi ninṟaḷandān ninṟa tirunaṟaiyūr*  
*manṟōnga ūrvan madal*

Oh those with resplendent foreheads! Until I embrace the emperumān, who measured the worlds, with my bosom, I will not stop from engaging with *madal*, for the world to see. I will engage with *madal* at the junction of the main roads in the divine abode of tirunaṟaiyūr, where that emperumān has taken residence.
