# Taittiriya Upanisad \[BS\]

## Taittiriya Upanisad

## 1. Siksa-valli

## First Anuvāka

**Śānti-pāṭhaḥ**

*oṁ śaṁ no mitraḥ śaṁ varuṇaḥ*  
*śaṁ no bhavatv aryamā*  
*śaṁ na indro bṛhaspatṁḥ*  
*śaṁ no viñṇur urukramaḥ*  
*namo brahmaṇe namaste vāyo*  
*tvam eva pratyakñaṁ brahmāsi*  
*tvām eva pratyakñaṁ brahma vadiñyāmi*  
*ṛtaṁ vadiñyāmi satyaṁ vadiñyāmi*  
*tan mām avatu tad-vaktarām avatu*  
*avatu mām avatu vaktāram*  
*om śāntiḥ śāṇtiḥ śāntiḥ*

**Перевод:**

**May the sun give us auspiciousness! May Varuṇa give us auspiciousness! May Aryamā give us auspiciousness! May Indra and Bṛhaspati give us auspiciousness! May Viñṇu, endowed with great strides, give us auspiciousness! I offer respects to the Vedas. O Vāyu, I offer respects to you. You are directly Brahman. I will call you direct Brahman. I will speak proper words and the truth. Protect me and protect the speaker. Protect me and protect the speaker. Peace, peace, peace.**

**Комментарий:**

Mitra, Varuṇa and Aryamā are all names of Aditya (sun). May Viñṇu, endowed with great strides (urukramaḥ) grant us auspiciousness! I offer respects to the Vedas (brahmaṇe). I offer respects to Vāyu, who reveals the syllables of the Vedas. Since Vāyu gives life to beings in the universe and is spread everywhere, though he is actually different from Brahman, respects are offered to Vāyu as being directly Brahman. I will speak the words with proper grammar and pure words (ṛtam). I will speak the real meaning of the words (satyam). Protect me, who am studying Brahman. Protect the teacher also from obstacles. Repetition of the prayer shows great respect.

## Second Anuvāka

*oṁ śīkñāṁ vyākhyāsyāmaḥ varṇaḥ svaraḥ*  
*mātrā balam sāma santānaḥ ity uktaḥ śikñādhyayaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**I will describe śikñā. The scripture describing śikñā includes syllables, intonation, length of pronunciation, strength of pronunciation, the seven svāras, and combination of sounds.**

**Комментарий:**

The aṅga of the Vedas called śikñā is described. The word śikñā is taken as śīkña for metrical reasons. Varṇa refers to three, sixty and sixty-four syllables. Svāra refers to the intonation. Mātra refers to the length of the sound. Bala indicates the small or great amount of prāṇa in pronouncing the sounds. Sāma is pronunciation of seven tones. Santāna refers to the combination of sounds. Knowledge of all of these elements constitutes śikñā. This knowledge is helpful in brahma-vidyā. Ādhyāyaḥ indicates a special scripture. Use of śikñā scripture is described in this verse.

## Third Anuvāka

## 1.

*saha nau yaśaḥ saha nau brahma-varcasam*  
*athātaḥ saṁhitāyā upaniñadaṁ vyākhyāñyāmaḥ*  
*pañca-svadhikaraṇeñu*  
*adhilokam adhijyotiñam adhividyam adhiprajam adhyātmam*  
*tā mahāsaṁhitā ity ācakñate athādhilokam*  
*pṛthivṁpūrva-rūpam dyor uttara-rūpaṁ*  
*ākāśaḥ sandhiḥ vāyuḥ sandhānam ity adhilokam*

**Перевод:**

**May fame and the power of Brahman come to both of us. I will explain the Upaniñad in terms of combinations divided into five categories: planets, light, knowledge, offspring and body. This is the great combination. First I will explain the planets: earth is the first form, and sky is the later form. Ether is their combination and air is their connecting elment. This is called adhilokam.**

**Комментарий:**

The guru and disciple pray for results of knowledge. May there be power of Brahman and fame for both of us. I will explain teachings concerning the secrets of combining sounds (sandhi) in five categories (analogies) of planets, luminaries, knowledge, offspring and body. First I will speak concerning planets (adhilokam).

Since air spreads on earth and heaven, it is the connecting medium.

## 2.

*athādhijyotiñaṁ agniḥ pūrva-rūpam āditya uttara-rūpam*  
*āpah sandhiḥ vidyutaḥ sandhānam*  
*ity adhijyotisām*

**Перевод:**

**Now the combination concerning luminaries is explained. Fire is the first form, and the sun is the later form. Water is the combination and lightning is the connector. These are the combinations concerning the luminaries. This is called adhijyotiñam.**

**Комментарий:**

Related to oblations in sacrifice, fire and sun are the two forms. Agnau prāstāhutiḥ samyag ādityam upatiñṭhate: the oblation offered to fire goes to the sun. (Maitrayani Upaniñad) Lightning is dependent on fire and is thus the connecting medium.

## 3.

*adhādividyaṁ ācāryaḥ pūrva-rūpam atnevāsy uttara-rūpaṁ*  
*vidyā sandhiḥ pravacanaṁ sandhānam*  
*ity adhividyam*

**Перевод:**

**Concerning knowledge, the teacher is the first form and the student is the later form. Knowledge is the combination and instruction is the connector. This is called adhividyam.**

**Комментарий:**

Vidyā is the knowledge gained by the student. Pravacanam means instructing.

## 4.

*athādhiprajam mātā pūrva-rūpam pitottara-rūpam*  
*prajā sandhiḥ prajananaṁ sandhānam*  
*ity adhiprajam*

**Перевод:**

**Concerning offspring, the mother is the first form, the father is the later form, the offspring are the combination and procreation is the connector. This is called adhiprajam.**

**Комментарий:**

Prajananam means producing offspring.

## 5.

*athādhyātmam adharā hanuḥ pūrva-rūpam uttara hanur uttra-rūpam*  
*vāk sandhiḥ jihvā sandhanam*  
*ity adhyātmam*

**Перевод:**

**Concerning the body, the lower jaw is the first form, the upper jaw is the later form, formoation of words is the combination and the tongue is the connector. This is called adhyātmam.**

**Комментарий:**

Vāk means words.

## 6.

*itṁmā mahāsaṁhitāḥ*  
*ya etam etā mahāsaṁhitādhyakhyātā veda*  
*sandhṁyate prajayā paśubhir*  
*brahma-varcasenānnaḍyena*  
*suvargyeṇa lokena*

**Перевод:**

**These are the great combinations. The person who knows these great combinations is endowed with progeny, animals, spiritual power, food and the spiritual world.**

**Комментарий:**

Great combinations means combinations which are be respected. Sandhi or connection is the combination of sounds like a and u to produce o. Its favourable quality is called sandhāna. Thus there are four elements in each of the five categories. The word ucyate should be supplied at the end of each verse. The word atha in each verse introduces the subject.

## Fourth Anuvāk

### 1

*yaś chandasām ṛñabho viśva-rūpaḥ*  
*chandobyo ‘dhy amrtāt sambabhuva*  
*sa mendro medhayā spṛṇotu*  
*amrtasya deva dhāraṇo bhuyāsam*  
*śarṁraṁ me vicarñaṇam*  
*jihvā me madhumattamā*  
*karṇābhyāṁ bhūri viśruvam*  
*brahmaṇaḥ kośo ‘si medhayā pihitaḥ*  
*śrutaṁ me gopāya*

**Перевод:**

**You who are the best of the Vedas, a form of Viñṇu, have manifested from the Lord and are superior to the Vedas. Being most powerful, enliven me with knowledge. May I possess the Lord in my heart. May my body maintain health and beauty. May my tongue speak about Brahman. May I hear about Brahma with my ears. You are the receptacle of Brahman, and are covered with intelligence. Please preserve the meaning of what I have heard.**

**Комментарий:**

This is a prayer to praṇava (om) for gaining knowledge of Brahman. Praṇava which is the best (ṛñabhaḥ) of the Vedas (chandasām) is Viñṇu (viśva-rūpaḥ). Viśvaṁ viñṇuḥ: viśva is a name of Viñṇu. (Viśṇu-sahasra-nāma). He manifests with form (rūpaḥ) and that form is used for meditation and sādhana. Viśva also means everything. Praṇava is the form of everything.

Praṇava, being superior to all the Vedas, manifested from Paramātmā (amṛtāt). May praṇava, endowed with all powers (indraḥ), enliven (spṛṇotu) me (mā) with knowledge (medhayā). O shining one (deva)! May I be the possessor (dhārakaḥ) of the Lord (amṛtasya) in my heart! May brahma-jñāna reside there at all times. May my body remain beautiful with strength and health (vicarñaṇam). May my tongue always enjoy the sweetness of speaking about Brahman. May I hear plentifully with my ears scriptures which explain Brahman. You are the receptacle (koña) of the Lord, since you indicate the Lord. You are covered by intelligence, since you produce intelligence or are full of intelligence. Preserve the meaning of what was heard (śrutam gopāya).

### 2-3

*āvahantṁvitanvānā kurvāṇā ‘cṁram ātmanaḥ*  
*vāsāmsi mama gāvaś ca anna-pāne ca sarvadā*  
*tato me śriyam āvaha*  
*lomaśāṁ paśubhiḥ saha svāhā*  
*ā māyantu brahmacāriṇaḥ svāhā*  
*vi māyantu brahmcāriṇaḥ svāhā*  
*pra māyantu brahamcārinaḥ svāhā*  
*dam āyantu brahmacārinaḥ svāhā*  
*śam āyantu brahmcāriṇaḥ svāhā*  
*yaśo jane ‘sāni svāhā śreyaṇ vasyaso ‘sāni svāhā*  
*taṁ tvā bhaga praviśāni svāhā*  
*sa mā bhaga praviśa svāhā*  
*tasmin sahasraśathe ni bhagāhaṁ tvayi mṛje svāhā*  
*yathāpaḥ pravatā yanti yathā māsā aharjaram*  
*evaṁ māṁ brahmacāriṇaḥ dhātar āyantu sarvataḥ svāhā*  
*prativeśo ‘si pramā māhi pra mā padyasva*

**Перевод:**

**Since you supply and increase cloth, cows, food and drink for me quickly, please bestow wealth along with cows in plenty. May students endowed with vijñāna and prajñāna come to me. May students endowed with sense control and mind control come to me.**

**May I be famous among men and the best among the wealthy. O Lord! May I attain you. May you attain me. O Lord! Having attaining you, most powerful, may I remain pure. As water flows and months become years, may students come to me at all times. O Lord! You are near me. Protect me and make me surrender.**

**Комментарий:**

Mantras used in homas for gaining knowledge are described. This is a prayer to Lakñmī. Since you produce (āvāhantī) and increase (vitanvānā), always supply me with clothing, cows, food and drink and produce (āvaha) wealth along with plentiful (lomaśām) ingredients such as animals, for sacrifice. Svāhā is added since it is a mantra for homa. May students endowed with brahmacārya, suitable for brahma-vidyā come to me (mā āyantu). This is a prayer for a proper student.

May I be famous among people. May I be the best among the wealthy (vasyasaḥ). O person full of powers (bhaga). May I attain (praviśāni) you (tvā). O Lord! Please attain me. O Lord! Having attained you, full of powers (sahasraśāthe), may I remain pure (mṛje). O Lord (dhātār)! As waters flow from the directions, as months accrue to a person who ages by days (aharjaram) or as months produce the year, may qualified students come to me. You are a neibhor (prativeśaḥ).

Please protect me nicely (pra māhi). Please make me surrender (papadyasva).

## Fifth Anuvāk

(worship of the vyāhṛtis).

### 1

*bhur bhuvaḥ suvar iti vā etas tisro vyāhṛtayaḥ*  
*tāsām ha smaitām caturthiṁ māhā-camasyaḥ pravedayate*  
*maha iti tad brahma sa aṭmā*  
*aṅgāny anyā devatāḥ*

**Перевод:**

**The three vyāhṛtis are bhuḥ, bhuvaḥ and suvaḥ. Mahācamasya revealed the fouth vyāhṛti mahañ. This is Brahman or ātmā. All the devatās are the limbs of mahas or Brahman.**

### 2

*bhur iti vā ayaṁ lokaḥ bhuva ity anatrikñam*  
*suvar ity asau lokaḥ maha ity adityaḥ*  
*ādityena vāva sarve lokā mahīyante*

**Перевод:**

**Bhuḥ is earth, bhuva is antarikña. Suvaḥ is Svarga. Mahas is the sun. By the sun, all planets are worshipped.**

### 3

*bhur iti vā agniḥ bhuva it vāyuh*  
*suvar ity adityaḥ maha iti candramāḥ*  
*candramasā vāva sarvāṇṁjyotṁṁñi mahṁyante*

**Перевод:**

**Bhuḥ is fire, bhuvaḥ is air and Suvaḥ is the sun. Mahas is the moon. By the moon, all luminaries are worshipped.**

### 4

*bhur iti vā ṛcaḥ bhuva iti sāmāni*  
*suvar iti jajūṁñi maha it brahma*  
*brahmanā vāva sarve vedā mahiyante*

**Перевод:**

**Bhuḥ is the Ṛg Veda. Bhuva is the Sāma Veda, Suvas is the Yajur Veda. Mahas is Brahman. By Brahman, all the Vedas are worshipped.**

### 5

*bhur iti vai prāṇah bhuva ity apānaḥ*  
*suvar iti vyānaḥ maha ity annam*  
*annena vāva sarve prāṇā mahiyante*

**Перевод:**

**Bhuḥ is prāṇa, bhuva is apāna, Suvas is vyāna and mahas is food. Bu food all prāṇas are worshipped.**

### 6

*tā vā etāś catasraś caturdhā*  
*catasraś catasro vyāhrtayaḥ*  
*tā yo veda sa veda brahma sarve smai devāvalim āvahanti*

**Перевод:**

**These are the four vyāhṛtis with four types of worship. Thus there are sixteen vyāhṛtis. He who knows these vyāhārtis knows Brahman. All the devaṭās worship him.**

**Комментарий:**

Worship of the vyāhṛtis as part of vidyā is taught. Vai indicates that it is famous.

The sage Māhācamasya revealed the fourth syllable mahas. Meaning the greatest, mahas means Brahman or ātmā. The devatās are the Lord’s aṅgas. They are the body of the Lord since they do not have his power. Thus they cannot be called mahas. Mahas is described through the three other vyāhṛtis. Four types of worship are shown through the four syllables. One should visualize, earth, antarikña, Svarga and the sun when chanting the four vyāhṛtis. By the sun all planets are worshipped (mahīyante). Mahīyante means to be worshipped. Because of mahas, the Lord, they planets become worshippable. The four vyāhṛtis have four types of worship (caturdhā) as explained in the verse. The four syllables times four become sixteen (catasraḥ catasraḥ vyāhṛtiḥ). Thus there are sixteen vyāhṛtis. The result of knowing the vyāhṛtis is shown. He knows Brahman, the Lord. All the devatās worship him (balim āvahanti).

## Sixth Anuvāk

### 1

*sa ya eño ‘ntarhṛdaya ākāśaḥ tasmin ayaṁ puruño manomayaḥ*  
*amṛto hiranmayaḥ antareṇa tāluke*  
*ya esā staṇa ivālambate sendra-yoniḥ*  
*yatrāsau keśānte vivartate vyapohya śṁrña-kapāle*

**Перевод:**

**He who is in the space in the heart is the attractive Lord beyond saṁsāra. This is the Lord’s place , which hangs down like a lotus bud between the two palates, where the jīva dwells.**

### 2

*bhur ity agnau pratiñṭhati bhuva iti vāyau suvar ity āditye*  
*maha iti brahmani*

**Перевод:**

**Leaving the body the jīva pierces the skull and uttering bhur attains the planet of fire. Uttering bhuva he attains the planet of air. Uttering suvar he attains the planet of the sun. Uttering mahas he attains Brahman.**

### 3

*āpnoti svārājyam āpnoti mana-sampatim*  
*vak-patis cakñupatiḥ śrotra-patir vijñāna-patiḥ*  
*etat tato bhavati ākāśa-śarṁram brahma*  
*satyātmā prāṇārāmam mana ānandam*  
*śānti-samṛddhim amṛtam*  
*iti pracīna-yogyopāsva*

**Перевод:**

**He becomes free of karma and attains the wealth of the mind. He becomes the master of speech and the master of the eye, the master of the ear, the master of all desires. After this, he attains a spiritual body, which experiences pleasure like the Lord, who is the object of prema for the liberated souls. He develops bliss in which every desire is fulfilled. He develops the highest peace and is completely beyond saṁsāra. Worship in this way, dear student, Pracīna-yogya!**

**Комментарий:**

Having taught the limbs of para-vidyā, now he teaches para-vidyā.

There is an attractive person (ayam hiranmayaḥ puruñaḥ), beyond saṁsāra (amṛtaḥ), understood by the pure mind (manomayaḥ), dwelling in the space within the heart. He is to be worshipped. This is not the jīva. Vyāsa in Vedānta-sūtra explains that this person is Paramātmā.

*sarvatra prasiddhopadeśāt*

**The word manomaya refers to Brahman because teachings of these qualities which belong to the Lord are found everywhere in the scriptures. Vedānta-sūtra 1.2.1**

The path hangs down like the nipple of a cow (stana iva) in the midst of the tongue (tāluke). This is the path of leaving for the jīva endowed with power (indra). On that path (yatra), the Lord, cause of Brahmā and Śiva, exists as the object of attainment (vivartate).

Or this is the place of Paramātmā (sā indrasya yoniḥ) which hangs down like a nipple between the upper and lower palate. Satatam śirabhis tu lambhatyākośa-sannibham:

There the jīva, fine like the tip of a hair (keśante), resides in a special manner (vivartate). Hṛdi hy ayam ātmā: the jīva resides in the heart.

At the time of death, piercing the skull, uttering bhuḥ, one becomes fixed in the planet of fire. The vyāhṛti bhur allows one to attain the planet of Agni. Though persons go by the path of the sun, they stay for a period of time on the planet of fire etc.

The person becomes free of karma (svārājyam āpnoti). He develops all senses by his will (vijñāna-patiḥ) or he develops knowledge independent of the senses. Later (tataḥ) he develops a completely (ā) manifested (kāśa) form (śarīram) which is spiritual (brahma). He develops pleasures similar to those of the Lord (ātmā) who is the abode of prema (prāṇa) for the liberated souls (satya). He develops bliss in which the desires of the mind are completely satisfied (mana ānandam). Yam kāmayate so ‘sya saṅkalpāt samuttiñṭhati: what he desires manifests simply by his will. (Chandogya Upaniñad) He develops the highest peace and is completely beyond saṁsāra (amṛtam). O pracīna-yogya (name of student)! Please worship the fourth vyāhṛti. Thus the teacher instructed the student.

## Seventh Anuvāk

*pṛthivy antarikñaṁ dyaur diśo ‘vāntara-diśaḥ*  
*agnir vāyur ādityaś candramā nakñatrāṇi*  
*āpa oñadhayo vanaspataya ākāśa ātmā ity adhibhūtam*  
*athādhyātmam prāṇo ‘vyāno ‘pāna udānaḥ samānaḥ*  
*cakñuḥ śrotraṁ mano vāk tvak carma māṁsaṁ snāvasthi majjā*  
*etad adhividhāya ṛñir avocat*  
*pāṅktaṁ vā idaṁ sarvaṁ pāṅktenaiva pāṅktaṁ spṛṇotīti*

**Перевод:**

**The first group is earth, antarikña, heaven, the directions and sub-directions. The second group is fire, air, the sun, the moon and the constellations. The third group is water, herbs, trees, ether and the body. These all concern the elements. Concerning the self, there are three more groups as follows. The fourth group is prāṇa, vyāna, apāna, udāna, and samāna. The fifth group is eye, ear, mind, voice and skin. The sixth group is skin, flesh, bone, nerves and marrow. Gathering these, a sage spoke. One attains control of the whole world by worship using these groups of five.**

**Комментарий:**

The first group consists of earth etc. The second group consists of fire etc. The third group consists of water etc. These three concern the elements. The next groups concern the self. The fourth group consists of prāṇa etc. The fifth consists of eye etc. The sixth group consists of skin etc. thus there are six groups of five. A group of five is called a paṅkti. By the six groups of five one maintains the world (pāṅktim). One attains lordship over the world. Some sage said this.

## Eighth Anuvāk

*Om iti brahma om itīdaṁ sarvam*  
*Om ityetad anukṛtir ha sma vā apy oṁ śrāvayety āśrāvayanti*  
*Om iti sāmāni gāyanti*  
*Om śom iti śastrāṇi śaṁsanti*  
*Om ity adhvaryuḥ pratigaraṁ pratigṛnāti*  
*Om iti brahmā prasauti*  
*Om ity agni-hotram anujānāti*  
*Om iti brāhmaṇaḥ pravakñyann āha brahmopāpnavānīti*  
*brahmaivopāpnoti*

**Перевод:**

**Om is the Lord. Om is everything. One utters om to accept anything. That is well known. Chanting om śravāya, they begin chanting. They sing sāma verses chanting om. The hotṛs chant the mantras, starting with om śom. The adhvāryu chants om in response to the hotṛ. The brahmā priest chants om to give permission. The sponsor chants om to permit the sacrifice. The brāhmaṇa saying “Let me study the Vedas” chants om and studies the Vedas.**

**Комментарий:**

Om is Brahman since it represents Brahman and is used to meditate on Brahman during sādhana. Om is everything, since it is Brahman. Om is used to signify consent to all rules and prohibitions. Thus it is “imitation” (anukṛtiḥ). Ha sma vai indicates it is well known. The udgātṛs sing the sāma verses starting with om (śrāvaya). The hotṛ, utting oṁ, chants mantras. The adhvaryu responds (pratigrnāti) to the hotṛ (pratigaram), accepting by saying om. The brahmā priest gives permission (prastauti), uttering om. The sponsor gives permission (anujānāti) for the agni-hotra, uttering om. he brāhmaṇa chants om on beginning study of the Vedas, saying “Let me study the Vedas.”

## Ninth Anuvāk

*ṛtaṁ ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*satyaṁ ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*tapaś ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*damaś ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*śamaś ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*agnayaś ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*agni-hotraṁ ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*atithayaś ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*mānuñaṁ ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*prajā ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*prajanaś ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*prajātiś ca svādhyāya-pravacane ca*  
*satyam iti satya-vacā rāthṁtaraḥ*  
*tapa iti taponityaḥ pauruśiñṭḥiḥ*  
*svādhyāya-pravacane eveti nāko maudgalyaḥ*  
*tad dhi tapas tad dhi tapaḥ*

**Перевод:**

**One should speak the truth, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should see equally, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should perform austerities, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should control the senses, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should control the mind, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should perform household homa, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should perform nitya-karmas related to fire, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should welcome guests, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should worship the Lord, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should serve society, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should conceive children, study and teach the Vedas.**  
**One should produce offspring, study and teach the Vedas.**

### 1-2

*brahmavid āpnoti param tad eñā 'bhyuktā \|\| 1 \|\|*  
*satyaṁ jñānam anantaṁ brahma*  
*yo veda nihitaṁ guhāyāṁ parame vyoman*  
*so 'śnute sarvān kāmān saha brahmaṇā vipaściteti \|\| 2 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The worshipper of Brahman attains the supreme Brahman. This verse is uttered by students concerning Brahman: Brahma is existence beyond upādhis, is knowledge devoid of restriction and is unlimited in qualities and svarūpa. He who knows this Brahman situated in the heart experiences the auspicious qualities of the Lord along with experiencing the Lord, in the spiritual sky.**

**Комментарий:**

This section explains the supreme tattva (the Lord), the beneficial goal for the jīva. The worshipper who takes shelter of the supreme tattva (brahma-vit) attains Brahman, supreme among all things (param āpnoti). The word vit (knower) means a worshipper (meditator). Knowing and worshipping are taken as synonymous. This is explained in the commentary on āvṛrtir asakṛd upadeśāt: Sādhanas should be repeated since the scriptures teach that one must engage many times. (Vedānta sutra 4.1.1) According to tat-kratu-nyāya (one attains one’s object of worship), the person attains Brahman (param) since he meditates on Brahman (brahma-vit).

Four items are mentioned: Brahman, knowing Brahman, attainment of Brahman and what is attained. What is Brahman? What is knowing it (vit)? What is the attainment (āpnoti)? What is that which is attained (param)?

A mantra is given to explain this. This verse (eñā) is uttered by the students, after accepting Brahman as that which should be explained (abhi uktāḥ). The goal is accomplished by a mantra whose meaning is purified by being uttered by a brāhmaṇa. Thus it is present in sutra form. The statement defines the svarūpa of Brahman, the cause of the creation of the universe, as declared in janamādyasya and in the yato vā imāni adhikāraṇa, while discarding everything else.

The word satya means “endowed with existence devoid of upādhis.” This excludes matter, unconscious entities, which transform and also excludes the jīvas, conscious entities embraced by matter. Matter and jīva are not devoid of upādhis.

The word jñānam indicates unrestricted knowledge of the highest form. Liberated jīvas are excluded since they had restricted knowledge previously.

The word anantam indicates a svarūpa undivided by time, space or substance. Since the Lord has qualities, he is infinite in terms of qualities and svarūpa. The eternally liberated souls, who have extraordinary svarūpas and qualities, who are different from the conditioned souls and the souls which have become liberated, are also excluded by this word.

Brahman has been explained as satyam, jñānam and anantam. Now the knower (vit) is explained. He knows Brahman dwelling in the heart. The word āpnoti is explained with “he experiences in the supreme sky or abode (parame vyoman).” What is attained is explained with the words “he enjoys all auspicious qualities of the Lord as well as enjoying the svarūpa of Lord.” Here kāmān means all auspicious qualities. The liberated soul is detached from material enjoyment and thus has no desires except to please the Lord.

*atha ya ihātmānam anuvidya vrajanti etāṁs ca satyān kāmān teñāṁ sarveñu lokeñu kāmacāro bhavati*

**Those persons who depart from here while knowing ātmā and auspicious qualities wanders at will in all worlds. Chāndogya Upaniñad 8.1.6**

Vipaścit means the conscious being who sees various things, he who knows everything, without upādhis, independent of everything else. This quality excludes the nitya-muktas.

“Since knowledge means knowledge of an object, the word jñāna is a quality rather thant he svarūpa of Brahman.” When jñāna means self revealing, it refers to the svarūpa of Brahman, not ts quality. The word jñāna refers both to jñāna as svarūpa and jñāna as quality (Brahman is the shelter of knowledge). Similarly the word Brahman means the Lord is great, both in svarūpa and in qualities. In the statement satyaṁ jñānam Brahman has the quality of knowledge. However Brahman has a svarūpa of knowledge is the following statements.

*prajñāna-ghana evānandamayaḥ*

**The Lord is condensed knowledge and is concentrated bliss. Māṇḍukya Upaniñad**

The word anantam means unlimited in space. There is no limitation of an object which is one one place and not another place. Anantam also means unlimited in time, unlike objects which exist at a certain time and not at another time. Ananatam in reference to the Lord also means unlimited as an object (vastu). Objects are one thing and not another thing. Brahman is also unlimited in the sense of having excellent qualities which are unequalled.

*Nāntam guṇānāṁ gacchanti tenānanto yam ucyate*

**Because there is no end to his qualities he is called Ananta. Brahma Purāṇa**

The customary meaning of the word distinguishes the Lord from devatās. One should not object and say that the word is in the neuter, and thus cannot refere to Nārāyaṇa, who is masculine. Anantam here is the accusative case of the masculine. One should know the person residing in the heart, who is infinite. Taking the etymologically meaning, he who is unlimited in space, time and object is only Nārāyaṇa.“With Brahman” indicates experiencing the prominence of qualities of Brahman in comparison to Brahman. Saha brahmanā indicates that the Lord is in a secondary position. But this is not a fault since enjoyment of the qualities of the Lord ends in showing the excellence of the Lord himself. The meaning is “The jīva enjoys the Lord’s auspicious qualities along with enjoying the Lord.” In this case, there is no fault of making the Lord secondary at all.

*tasmād vā etasmād ātmana ākāśaḥ saṁbhūtaḥ*  
*ākāśād vāyum vāyor agnim*

**Комментарий:**

This section explains the infinite nature of Brahman, not limited by any substance, since he is the material cause and pervades everything. Tasmat refers to the supreme mentioned in brahma-vid āpnoti param. Etasmāt refers to satyam jñānam anantam. Ether arose from the Lord who is attained by the brahma-vit and who is satyam jñānam anantam. From ether arose air. Here ether actually means the Lord, of which ether is part of his body. This is explained in the adhikaraṇa tejo tas tathā hyāha (Vedānta-sūtra 2.3.9). Puruñam means the body.

## Second Anuvāk

*annād vai prajāḥ prajāyante \| yāḥ kāś ca pṛthivīṁ śritāḥ \| atho annenaiva jīvanti \|*  
*athainad apiyan ty antataḥ \| annaṁ hi bhūtānāṁ jyeṣṭham \| tasmāt sarvauṣadham ucyate \|*

*sarvaṁ vai te'nnam āpnuvanti \| ye'nnaṁ brahmopāsate \| annaṁ hi bhūtānāṁ jyeṣṭham \| tasmāt sarvauṣadham ucyate \| annād bhūtāni jāyante \| jātāny annena vardhante \| adyate'tti ca bhūtāni \| tasmād annaṁ tad ucyata iti \|*

*tasmād vā etasmād anna-rasa-mayāt \| anyo'ntara ātmā prāṇamayaḥ \| tenaiṣa pūrṇaḥ \| sa vā eṣa puruṣa-vidha eva \| tasya puruṣa-vidhatām anv ayaṁ puruṣa-vidhaḥ \| tasya prāṇa eva śiraḥ \| vyāno dakṣiṇaḥ pakṣaḥ \| apāna uttaraḥ pakṣaḥ \| ākāśa ātmā \| pṛthivī pucchaṁ pratiṣṭhā \| tad apy eṣa śloko bhavati \|\| 1 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**From food all beings who dwell on earth arise. By food they live. And at death, they merge into food. Food is the best for all beings. Therefore it is called all medicine. Whose who worship Brahman attain all medicine. From food all beings arise. Populace increases by food. Food is eaten by living beings and it devours all beings. Therefore it is called food.**

**Different from the annamaya puruña is the prāṇamaya ātmā, dwelling in the gross body. The gross body is pervaded by the prāṇamaya ātmā. It has the form of a human. This form imitates the gross annamaya form. Prāṇa is its head. Vyāna is its right side. Apāṇa is its left side. Akāśa is its ātmā. Earth is the foundation, the tail. There is a verse concerning this.**

**Комментарий:**

All living beings (prajāḥ) existing on earth arise from food. Having arisen, they live by food. At death they merge into (yānti) food (enat). Food is best for the living entities because it assists them. It is said to be all medicine since it prevents hunger and disease. Oñadhībyo ‘nnam: from plants (medicine) food arises. Since food is a transformation of plants (medicine) it is suitable to call food medicine. The Vedas teach this since food produces medicine.

Those who think of food as Brahman attain food as much as they desire. Those who see food as Brahman equate food with Brahman by similarity. Thus food is said to be the best for living beings. And ultimately Brahman is all medicine since it curse all disease (birth and death). Food is similarly to Brahman since it increases the population (jātāni).

Food is faultless like Brahman. It is eaten while a living being is alive. When the living being dies, it is the destroyer or eater since it is the cause of an opposite transformation and it causes death by not being available (starvation). Annaṁ mṛtyaṁ tam u jivātum āhuḥ: they say that food is life and death as well. (Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa) From Brahman arose ether etc. The Lord is the cause of the elements and body mentioned and is the antaryāmī of everything,

Brahman is the ātmā of all the factors from ether to the gross body previously mentioned and is also the material cause of all the factors. Who is that ātmā? To show this, first prāṇa which dwells in the gross body of food is shown to be the ātmā, in the manner of showing Arundhatī star. Considering that is it is impossible to show the smallest star at the beginning, first a larger star close to it is shown, while saying “This is Arundhatī.”

Different from this body made of food there is something made of prāṇa within the body. That is called ātmā, since the Lord is the antaryāmī of all the elements starting with ether.

First one thinks of prāṇa, which is superior to the gross body, as Paramātmā. Later, one thinks of the manomaya form as Paramātma, and then the vijñāmaya form and then the ānanda maya form as Paramātma. This is Rāmānujācārya’s explanation.

Since the form is filled with the five functions of prāṇa it is called prāṇa-maya. The anna-maya form is filled with the prāṇa-maya form (tena eña pūrṇaḥ). Prāṇa spreads through the whole body.

This prāṇamaya ātmā has the form of a person (puruña-vidhaḥ). This prāṇamaya puruña imitates the form described for the anna-maya puruña (tasya puruña-vidhatām anv ayam), with hands, feet etc. One should not mistake it for an actually human form with hands and feet. Ether is its ātmā.

*yathākāśa-sthito nitya vāyuḥ sarvatra go mahān:*

**air, situated in ether, goes everywhere. BG 9.6**

Ether is the ātmā since it is the maintainer of the prāṇas which are a transformation of air element.

Earth is the support (pratiñṭḥā). It is the tail since it supports ether.

## Third Anuvāk

*prāṇaṁ devā anu prāṇanti \| manuṣyāḥ paśavaś ca ye \| prāṇo hi bhūtānām āyuḥ \| tasmāt sarvāyuṣam ucyate \| sarvam eva ta āyur yanti \| ye prāṇaṁ brahmopāsate \| prāṇo hi bhūtānām āyuḥ \| tasmāt sarvāyuṣam ucyata iti \|*

*tasyaiṣa eva śārīra ātmā \| yaḥ pūrvasya \| tasmād vā etasmāt prāṇamayāt \| anyo'ntara ātmā manomayaḥ \| tenaiṣa pūrṇaḥ \| sa vā eṣa puruṣa-vidha eva \| tasya puruṣa-vidhatām anv ayaṁ puruṣavidhaḥ \| tasya yajur eva śiraḥ \| ṛg dakṣiṇaḥ pakṣaḥ \| sāmottaraḥ pakṣaḥ \| ādeśa ātmā \| atharvāṅgirasaḥ pucchaṁ pratiṣṭhā \| tad apy eṣa śloko bhavati \|\| 1 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The devatās, humans and animals depend on prāṇa for their lives. Prāṇa is the life of all beings. Therefore it is called the life of everyone. Those who worship prāṇa as Brahman attain all life. Prāṇa is the life of all binegs. Therefore it is called the life of everyone.**

**This is also the ātmā of the prāṇa maya puruña just as it was the ātmā of the annamaya puruña. Different from the prāṇa-maya puruña is the manomaya puruña, the self. The prāṇa-maya puruña is filled with this manomaya form. It takes the form of a human, imitating the previous form. Knowledge of Yajur Veda is its head. Knowledge of Ṛg Veda is its right side. Knowledge of the Sāma Veda is its left side. Orders are its mid section. Knowledge of the ātharvāṅirasa mantras is the tail, the foundation. There is verse concerning this.**

**Комментарий:**

Devatās, humans and animals are dependent on prāṇa (prāṇam anu prāṇanti). As long as prāṇa, with inhaling and exhaling, dwells in living beings, they maintain life. Therefore prāṇa is the life of all beings (prāṇo hi bhūtānāṁ āyuḥ). Those who worship prāṇa as Brahman attain all life (sarvam āyur yānti).

Stating that prāṇa-maya puruña was the ātmā of all things from ether to the annamaya puruña, that statement is then controverted. That which is the ātmā of the annamaya puruña (pūrvasya) is also the ātmā of the prāṇamaya puruña (tasyaiña ātmā), related to the body (śarīra). Prāṇa-maya puruña is not the ātmā of the annamaya puruña. Both have the same ātmā. Who is that? That answer is given.

Different from the prāṇa-maya puruña, dwelling within it, is the manomaya form, a self (ātmā), a form filled with mind--with its functions of mind, intelligence, ahaṅkāra and citta. By this the praṇamaya puruña is pervaded. It has a human form following the previous form.

Yajur veda is the head of this form. This means the head generates knowledge of the Yajur Veda. That Yajur Veda itself cannot be the head of the manomaya puruña since it does not deal with mind. A similar explanation is necessary for the other Vedas mentioned.

The order (ādeśaḥ) is the ātmā (middle portion). “Do this. Do not do this.” This means secret instructions in the form of rules and prohibitions. Ādeśa means the function of the antaḥkaraṇa to produce knowledge based on the orders. The mantras seen by Atharvāṅgiras and other sages are the tail, or basis. This refers to the function of mind which develops knowledge based on the mantras of Atharvāṅgiras

## Fourth Anuvak

*yato vāco nivartante \| aprāpya manasā saha \| ānandaṁ brahmaṇo vidvān \| na bibheti kadācaneti \|*

*tasyaiṣa eva śārīra ātmā \| yaḥ pūrvasya \| tasmād vā etasmānmanomayāt \| anyo'ntara ātmā vijṇānamayaḥ \| tenaiṣa pūrṇaḥ \| sa vā eṣa puruṣavidha eva \| tasya puruṣavidhatām \| anvayaṁ puruṣavidhaḥ \| tasya śraddhaiva śiraḥ \| ṛtaṁ dakṣiṇaḥ pakṣaḥ \| satyam uttaraḥ pakṣaḥ \| yoga ātmā \| mahaḥ pucchaṁ pratiṣṭhā \| tad apy eṣa śloko bhavati \|\| 1 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**He who knows the bliss of Brahman, which words and mind cannot approach, has no fear at all. There is an ātmā of this manomaya puruña which is the same ātmā of the prāṇamaya puruña.**

**Different from the manomaya puruña there is the vijñānamaya puruña or self. The manomaya puruña is pervaded by the vijñānamaya puruña. It has a human form, imitating the previous form. Faith is its head. Proper conduct is its right side. Truth is its left side. Yoga is its midsection. Mahas is its tail, its foundation. On this there is a verse.**

**Комментарий:**

The bliss of Brahman is such that words along with mind cannot comprehend it since it is infinite. Understanding it by the pure mind, however one has no fear.

*dṛśyate tv agryayā buddhyā:*

**Brahman is seen by the pure mind, by concentrated intellect. (Katha Upaniñad)**

Thus the bliss of Brahman can be understood by the pure mind.

That which was the ātmā of the previous form (prāṇamaya) is also the ātmā of the manomaya puruña. There is one ātmā for all three forms. What is that ātmā? It is vijñāna-maya. This means the jīva, rather than intelligence alone. Intelligence is not filled with (maya) vijñāna. The manomaya puruña is pervaded by the vijñāna-maya puruña. The words faith, ṛta and truth indicate types of knowledge.

Yoga is the mid section. Yoga means “being endowed with special knowledge.”

*Brahmaṇe tvā mahasa om ity ātmānaṁ yuñjīta:*

**one should endow the self with the great Lord. (Bṛhad-ārāṇyaka Upaniñad)**

Mahas is the tail. Mahas means power to destroy obstacles to meeting the Lord. Since faith etc. are well known qualities of the jīva, the ātmā in its svarūpa is described with these qualities as its limbs.

## Fifth Anuvāk

*vijñānaṁ yajññaṁ tanute \| karmāṇi tanute'pi ca \| vijñānaṁ devāḥ sarve \| brahma jyeṣṭham upāsate \| vijñānaṁ brahma ced veda \| tasmāc cen na pramādyati \| śarīre pāpmano hitvā \| sarvān kāmān samaśnuta iti \|*

*tasyaiṣa eva śārīra ātmā \| yaḥ pūrvasya \| tasmād vā etasmād vijñānamayāt \| anyo'ntara ātmā' ’nandamayaḥ \| tenaiṣa pūrṇaḥ \| sa vā eṣa puruṣavidha eva \| tasya puruṣavidhatām \| anv ayaṁ puruṣavidhaḥ \| tasya priyam eva śiraḥ \| modo dakṣiṇaḥ pakṣaḥ \| pramoda uttaraḥ pakṣaḥ \| ānanda ātmā \| brahma pucchaṁ pratiṣṭhā \| tad apy eṣa śloko bhavati \|\| 1 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The jīva performs sacrifices. He performs karmas. All the devatās worship the jīva, which is superior to matter. If one knows the jīva as Brahman, and if he does not stray during his life, on giving up sin in the body, he enjoys all types of auspicious qualities of the Lord.**

**The ātmā of the previous puruña is the ātmā of the vijñāna maya puruña. But there is another ātmā within the vijñāna maya puruña, ānandamaya. The vijñāna maya puruña is pervaded by the ānānda maya puruña. He has the form of a human like the previous puruña. His head is the happiness of seeing the Lord. His right side is the happiness of attaining the Lord. His left side is the happiness of experiencing interaction with the Lord. His midsection (ātmā) is bliss. His tail is Brahman, the basis. There is a verse concerning this.**

**Комментарий:**

The jīva denoted by vijñānamaya, is also denoted by vijñāna, since the ātmā’s svarūpa is self manifesting and denoted as knowledge. If the jīva is mere vijñāna, the following statements that vijñāna performs sacrifices and karmas would be meaningless. Or vijñāna means one who knows. In antaryāmī brāhmaṇa, the mādhyandina-pātha, or the kānva-pāṭha, the word vijñāna means the jīva. All the devatās worship the jīva (vijñānam) which is superior to Brahman (which means pradhāna or matter). This is explained in prajāpati vidyā. He who knows jīva as Brahman and does not become bewildered up till death (tasmāt), giving up sin in the body, still existing in his body, enjoys auspicious qualities of the Lord, destroying all previous and remaining karmas.

Happiness (priyam) generated by seeing the desire object is the head of the ānandamaya puruña. Happiness (modaḥ) generating by attaining the Lord is the right side. Happiness generated from the experience after attainment of the Lord (pramodaḥ) is the left side. Intense happiness (ānandaḥ) is the midsection. One should not claim that the bliss of the midsection and the Brahman of the tail are the same (since Brahmana is bliss), though one is the ātmā (midsection) and the other is the tail. The one Brahman (the Lord) takes the form of the tail as Brahman and takes the form of midsection as bliss.

“How can one call Brahman, the very form of bliss, anandamaya, full of bliss? It is impossible for bliss to be full of bliss. “In answer to this, Brahman which is the very form of bliss, can be filled with bliss, since head, sides, and midsection have the quality of intense bliss, with the names priyam, moda, and pramāda.

The word ānanda-maya refers to everything in this form, just as words anna-maya, prāṇa-maya, manomaya and vijñāna-maya referred to all the limbs of their respective puruñas. Thus the tail, Brahman is also ānanda-maya, full of bliss.

The section commenced with the sentence brahma-vit āpnoti param: the worshipper of the Lord attains the Supreme Lord. At the conclusion, ānanda is equated with Brahman, the subject of the discussion. The anandamaya puruña must be equated with Brahman.

## Sixth Anuvāk

*asann eva sa bhavati \| asad brahmeti veda cet \| asti brahmeti ced veda \| santam enaṁ tato vidur iti \| tasyaiṣa eva śārīra ātmā \| yaḥ pūrvasya \|*

*athāto'nupraśnāḥ \| utā vidvān amuṁ lokaṁ pretya \| kaścana gacchatī āho vidvān amuṁ lokaṁ pretya kaścit samaśnutā u \|*

*so'kāmayata \| bahu syāṁ prajāyeyeti \| sa tapo'tapyata \| sa tapastaptvā \| idaɱ sarvam asṛjata \| yad idaṁ kiñca \| tat sṛṣṭvā \| tad evānuprāviśat \| tad anupraviśya \| sac ca tyac cābhavat \| niruktaṁ cāniruktaṁ ca \| nilayanaṁ cānilayanaṁ ca \| vijñānaṁ cāvijñānaṁ ca \| satyaṁ cānṛtaṁ ca satyam abhavat \| yad idaṁ kiñca \| tat-satyam ityācakṣate \| tad apy eṣa śloko bhavati \|\| 1 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**If one thinks Brahman (ānanda maya puruña) does not exist, one remains in saṁsāra. If one knows that Brahman exists, they say that he attains liberation. The ātmā of the previous puruña is ātmā in this body.**

**There are questions in this regard. Does the wise man on leaving this world, go to the spiritual world? Does the wise man leaving this world experience Brahman as blissful in that world?**

**The Lord desired, “May I become many beings. May I be born as the elements.” He deliberated. After deliberating, he created the universe and whatever exists. Having created it, he entered it. Entering it, he became conscous and unconscious entities. He became the definable and undefinable. He became the shelter and the sheltered. He became knowledge and ignorance. He became the true and the false. The sages say that whatever exists is the Lord.**

**Комментарий:**

This verse concerning the ānanda maya puruña, including his tail which is Brahman, just as the previous subjects of the verse after “there is a verse concerning this” were the various puruñas, not just the tail. The word Brahman in asad brahmeti veda cet etc. signifies the whole ānanda maya puruña only.

By knowing about the ānandamaya puruña, one gains liberation (santam enam tato viduḥ) and by not knowing one remains in saṁsāra (asann eva sa bhavati). Brahman was said to be the antaryāmī of the various puruñas. Does the ānanda-maya puruña have an ātmā also different from ānanda maya puruña? The ātmā of the previous puruña (vijñāna maya) is the ātmā of the ānanda maya puruña. The previous sections described an inner ātmā of each puruña (tasmād vā etasmād vijñānamayāt\| anyo'ntara ātmā' ’nandamayaḥ etc.) This section has no such phrase. Thus this ātmā is the final one.

After describing the ānanda-maya Brahman, questions are asked to clarify the statement made at the beginning: the worshipper of Brahman attains the supreme.

The word athātaḥ indicates a cause, desiring to know what was previously explained. Utā has a prolonged vowel. So the phrase is utā vidvān not uta avidvān. Does the wise person who worships infinite Brahman who resides close by in the heart go to the spiritual sky (amum lokam described previously as parame vyoman) on dying? Or does he attain Brahman on earth without going anywhere?

The next question concerns the jīva as an experiencer. Does the person, endowed with a sense of I, experience Brahman as an object of pleasure, on going to that place? Or does he become one with Brahman? There are many questions (praśnān) concerning the specific destination and jīva and Brahman being one or different, based on various scriptures.

To answer the questions, knowledge of the Lord to be attained is given. He has various qualities which assist him in creating the universe. The ānanda-maya Brahman desired. May I become many in the form of devatās and humans (bahu syām). May I be born in the form of ether etc. for this purpose. He made a decision to creation conscioius and unconscious entities as individuals and as a totality. He performed austerity (tapas) in the form of deliberating on creation. Tapa means to deliberate.

Since Brahman is all pervading why would he enter into the creation? Just as cowness becomes particularized in the calf in its mother’s womb, so the all pervading Lord becomes particularized in visible conditions among all things. Though Brahman is unlimited, it was already stated that he rsides in the heart. Entering, he became sat and tyat. Sat means conscious entities always in the same form with no change. Tyat means unconscious entities which changes, giving up their previous state. Brahman becomes conscous and unconscious entities with names and forms.

He became the unconscious elements (niruktam), which are defined in terms of jāti, guṇa etc. He became conscious beings (aniruktam), not possible to define in terms of jāti, guna etc.

Nilayam means the conscious entities who are the support for the unconscious elements. Etā bhūtā-mātrāḥ prajñā-mātrāsv arpitāḥ: all the elements were offered to the jīvas. (Kauśitaki Upaniñad) Anilayam means all the unconscious elements which take shelter of the jīvas. Vijñaṇam means jīvas. Avijñānam means matter. Satyam belongs to the conscious beings, who do not change. Anṛtam refers to unconscious things. Brahman, satyam, who does not change, became all this.

“But Brahman, being the material cause, would then undergo change.” Brahman called satya entered into all conscious and unconscious elements as their ātmā.

The sages like Parāśara who saw through scriptures have said (ācakñate) that this universe consisting of conscious and unconscious elements is satyam or brahman.

*harer na kiñcid vyatiriktam asti*

**There is nothing except the Lord. Brahma Purāṇa**

*jyotīṁñi viñṇur bhuvanāni viñṇuḥ.*

**All the luminaries are Viñṇu. All the planets are Viñṇu. Viñṇu Purāṇa**

## Seventh Anuvāk

*asad vā idam agra āsīt \| tato vai sad ajāyata \| tad ātmānaɱ svayam akuruta \| tasmāt tat-sukṛtam ucyata iti \| yad vai tat sukṛtam \| raso vai saḥ \| rasaɱ hy evāyaṁ labdhvā 'nandī bhavati \| ko hy evānyāt kaḥ prāṇyāt \| yad eṣa ākāśa ānando na syāt \| eṣa hy evā''nandayāti \| yadā hy evaiṣa etasminn adṛśye'nātmye'nirukte'nilayane'bhayaṁ pratiṣṭhāṁ vindate \| atha so'bhayaṁ gato bhavati \| yadā hyevaiṣa etasminn udaram antaraṁ kurute \| atha tasya bhayaṁ bhavati \| tat tv eva bhayaṁ viduṣo'manvānasya \| tad apy eṣa śloko bhavati \|\| 1 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord alone existed before creation. Then he created the universe. He created himself (universe) by himself. Therefore he is called the person who makes expertly. He who expertly creates is rasa (bliss). The jīva, attaining the Lord, becomes blissful. If the Lord who is bliss did not exist who could live? He alone gives bliss. When the worshipper is fixed fearlessly in the Lord who is invisible, without material body, beyond description without support, he attains fearlessness. When the worshipper does not engage himself with full concentration in the Lord, there is great fear. There is a verse concerning this.**

**Комментарий:**

Brahman (idam), unmanifested in name and form (asat), having jīvas and matter as his gross body, existed previous to creation (agre). Then he became manifested with name and form as the universe (sat). “Since brahman or asat is the material cause, there must be another doer. There should be a nimitta as well as an upādāna.” In answer to this it is said “He by himself creates himself.” Accepting himself as the upādāna (ātmānam) he creates by himself (svayam). Since he himself is the effect (universe), he is a person whose effect (universe) was well done, being Brahman itself (sukṛtam). There was no difficulty also in producing this effect. In producing something different from oneself, difficulty arises. Brahman is the untroubled effect. Niḥśvasitam etan mahato bhūtasya: the world arose from the Lord’s breathing. (Bṛhad-ārāṇyaka Upaniñad)

The Lord is the producer of nice effects. But the Lord as the object of worship and object of attainment should be described (in answer to the questions). Brahman which is sukṛtam (producer of perfect effects) is rasa or bliss. If one attains him one becomes blissful. Because he is bliss, it is proper that he is the object of worship and attainment.

This is further explained. If the Lord who is infinite bliss did not exist (anyāt), who would attain happiness from material life or liberation? He produces bliss. Since he is the cause of all bliss, he should be the object of attainment.

Adṛśye means invisible to the eye. Anātmye means not having a material body. Anirukte means he who is not describable by jāti (type) or guṇa (quality). Anilayane means devoid of support. Abhayam should be abhayāya. He who attains steadiness in the form of constant meditation (pratiñṭhām) in the Lord who is beyond the eye, devoid of material body, and without material support, attains fearlessness.

Vyāsārya says that invisible distinguishes the Lord from matter. Anātmye (not pervaded or not the body) distinguishes the Lord from the bound jīvas. The jīva is pervaded by Paramātmā and has a material body produced by karma. Anirukta distinguishes the Lord from the liberated soul. The liberated soul is given names like deva while bound in the material world. Anilaya distinguishes the Lord from the eternally liberated soul. The Lord is their support.

The fault in breaking the meditation is described. When the worshipper (eñaḥ) even a little (daram) breaks (antaram kurute) his meditation on the Lord (etasmin), he produces fear. Antaram is connected with meditation of the previous statement. The meaning is not “when the worshippers sees difference (antaram) in Brahman.” What is that fear? For the worshipper of Brahman who has performed constant meditation, lack of concentration, with a desire for something other than the Lord, is fear. There is no other fear greater than this. The great sages have said:

*yan muhūrtaṁ kñaṇaṁ vāpi vāsudevo na cintyate*  
*sā hānis tan mahac-chidraṁ sā bhrāntiḥ sā ca vikriyā*

**Not thinking of Vāsudeva even for a moment, is destruction, a great fault, an error and a sin. Kātyāyana Saṁhita**

*varaṁ hutavaha-jvālā pañjarāntar-vyavasthitiḥ*  
*na śauri-cintā-vimukha-jana-saṁvāsa-vaiśasam*

**It is preferable to remain within a cage of blazing fire than to have the misfortune of living in association with persons who are opposed to thinking of the Lord. Kātyāyana Saṁhita**

## Eighth Anuvāk

### 1

*bhīṣā'smād vātaḥ pavate \| bhīṣodeti sūryaḥ \| bhīṣā'smād agniś cendraś ca \| mṛtyur dhāvati pañcama iti \| saiṣā''nandasya mīmāɱsā bhavati \| yuvā syāt sadhu yuvā'dhyāyakaḥ \| āśiṣṭho dṛḍhiṣṭho baliṣṭhaḥ \| tasyeyaṁ pṛthivī sarvā vittasya pūrṇā syāt \| sa eko mānuṣa ānandaḥ \| te ye śataṁ mānuṣā ānandāḥ \|\| 1 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Out of fear of the Lord, the wind blows. Out of fear, the sun rises. Out of fear of the Lord, Agni, and Candra function. Out of fear, the fifth one, death, flees.**

**This is the deliberation on bliss. Suppose there is a young man, who has properly studied the Vedas and qualified to teach other students. He is healthy, physically strong and mentally strong. The earth, full of wealth, becomes his. This is human bliss.**

**Комментарий:**

Brahman, full of intense bliss, the object of attainment has been described as the infinite entity endowed with qualities such as omniscience, which aid him in creation of the universe. Brahman is described as ānandamaya. Now the meaning of this word, “having the greatest abundance of bliss” is illustrated.

All chief devatās headed by Agni, Indra and Sūrya alertly perform their duties out of fear that what will happen if they were to disobey the order of the Lord. Thus all goals other than the Lord are useless, bringing great suffering. With the desire for the Lord, one has no fear of breaking the meditation.

This is a deliberation (mīmāṁsa) on the subject of bliss, the present subject of discussion.

There is a young student who has studied according to the rules, completely, or without omitting proper pronunciation or intonation of words (sadhu). He is young (yuvā). He has studied the Vedas in a fresh way. Or he is a teacher of students his age. He has more knowledge than them. He is quick to act (āśiñṭhaḥ) or he can eat well, being free of disease. Or he has been blessed, being attractive to all people. He has a strong mind (baliñṭhaḥ). He has a strong body (dṛdhiñṭhaḥ). Or he has a steady nature. Then baliñṭhaḥ can mean that in mind and body he is strong. The earth, full of all wealth becomes his. Qualities and powers of the human have been described. This is human bliss for this person.

“How can qualities or powers be bliss? Knoweldge is bliss and these are different from it?” They are favorable for bliss and thus they are called bliss. Thus qualities and powers may be called bliss. They are favorable objects because they are favorable for knowledge of bliss.

### 2

*sa eko manuṣya-gandharvāṇām ānandaḥ \| śrotriyasya cākāma-hatasya \| te ye śataṁ manuṣya-gandharvāṇām ānandāḥ \| sa eko deva-gandharvāṇām ānandaḥ \| śrotriyasya cākāma-hatasya \| te ye śataṁ deva-gandharvāṇām ānandāḥ \| sa ekaḥ pitṛṇāṁ cira-loka-lokānām ānandaḥ \| śrotriyasya cākāma-hatasya \| te ye śataṁ pitṛṇāṁ cira-loka-lokānām ānandāḥ \| sa eka ājānajānāṁ devānām ānandaḥ \|\| 2 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**If one multiplies the human bliss a hundred times, it becomes one unit of the bliss of a manuñya-gandharva and of a liberated soul. If one multiplies the manuyña-gandharva bliss a hundred times it becomes one unit of the bliss of a deva-gandharva and of a liberated soul. If one multiplies the deva-gandharva bliss a hundred times, it comes one unit of bliss of the Pitṛas on their long lasting planet, and of the liberated soul. If one multiplies the bliss of the Pitras by a hundred it becomes one unit of the bliss of the being born on devaloka.**

**Комментарий:**

śrotriyasya cākāma-hatasya means “of the person fixed in Brahman who is completely free of desire for material enjoyment.” Thus it refers to a liberated person. The joy of the human is also the joy of the liberated person, since his bliss includes that of the human. He experiences all levels of bliss.

Those humans who attain gandharva status by dint of knowledge and karmas, attaining hundreds of powers, such as power to be invisible, become manuñya-gandharvas. Deva-gandhāravas dwell in in the antarikña. Cira-loka means the place which remains for a long time. Ajāna means deva-loka. The devatās are called ajāna-jā. One is born in deva-loka by special smārta karmas.

### 3

*śrotriyasya cākāmahatasya \| te ye śataṁ ājānajānāṁ devānām ānandāḥ \| sa ekaḥ karma-devānāṁ devānām ānandaḥ \| ye karmaṇā devānapiyanti \| śrotriyasya cākāmahatasya \| te ye śataṁ karma-devānāṁ devānām ānandāḥ \| sa eko devānām ānandaḥ \| śrotriyasya cākāmahatasya \| te ye śataṁ devānām ānandāḥ \| sa eka indrasyā''nandaḥ \|\| 3 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**If one multiplies the bliss of the being born on devaloka by a hundred, it becomes one unit of the bliss a karma-devatā and of a liberated soul. If one multiplies the bliss of a karma-devatā by a hundred, it becomes one unit of the bliss of being devatā and of a liberated soul. One multiplies the bliss of a devaṭa by a hundred it becomes the bliss of Indra and of the liberated soul.**

**Комментарий:**

By actions of agni-hotra etc. one becomes a karma-deva, becoming similar to Agni, Indra and others. The devas are Vasu, Rudra and other others. They number thirty-three. They receive oblations. Indra and Bṛhaspati are famous devas.

### 4

*śrotriyasya cākāmahatasya \| te ye śatamindrasyā''nandāḥ \| sa eko bṛhaspaterānandaḥ \| śrotriyasya cākāmahatasya \| te ye śataṁ bṛhaspaterānandāḥ \| sa ekaḥ prajāpaterānandaḥ \| śrotriyasya cākāmahatasya \| te ye śataṁ prajāpater ānandāḥ \| sa eko brahmaṇa ānandaḥ \| śrotriyasya cākāmahatasya \|\| 4 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**If one multiplies the bliss of Indra by a hundred, it becomes the bliss of Bṛhaspati and of the liberated soul. If one multiplies the bliss of Bṛhaspati by a hundred, it becomes the bliss of Brahmā and the liberated soul. If one multiplies the bliss of Brahmā by a hundred, it becomes the bliss of Brahman and of the liberated soul.**

**Комментарий:**

Prajāpati means four headed Brahmā and not Dakña and other Prajāpatis, since it is in the singular. The word brahma refers to Brahman which was first stated in brahma-vid āpnoti param.

One should not think that by saying that the bliss of Brahman is a hundred times that of Brahmā one limits the bliss of Brahman. One may say “the sun, moving many yojanas, goes like an arrow.” That comparison is given only to indicate that the sun does not move slow. Thus the expression “a hundred times” only means that Brahman’s bliss is much more than Brahmā’s bliss, rather than saying that Brahman’s bliss is not more than a hundred times Brahmā’s bliss.

### 5

*sa yaś cāyaṁ puruṣe \| yaś cāsāv āditye \| sa ekaḥ \| sa ya evaṁ vit \| asmāl lokāt pretya \| etam anna-mayam ātmānam upasaṅkrāmati \| etaṁ prāṇamayam ātmānam upasaṅkrāmati \| etaṁ manomayam ātmānam upasaṅkrāmati \| etaṁ vijñāna-mayam ātmānam upasaṅkrāmati \| etam ānanda-mayam ātmānam upasaṅkrāmati \| tad apy eṣa śloko bhavati \|\| 5 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**He who is in the puruña and in the sun is this one entity. He who knows him, on departing from this world, attains the ātmā of annamaya puruña. He attains the ātmā of prāṇamaya puruña. He attains the ātmā of manomaya puruña. He attains the ātmā of vijñāna-maya puruña. He attains the Paramātmā, the ānanda-maya puruña. There is a verse concerning this.**

**Комментарий:**

What is the particular form of the person who is dwelling in the heart and being worshipped? What devatā is he?

He who is in the puruña and in the sun is one.

*ya eño ‘ntarāditye hiraṇmayaḥ puruño dṛśyate hiraṇya-śmāśrur hiraṇya-keña āpraṇakhāt sarva eva suvarṇaḥ tasya yatha kapyāsaṁ puṇḍarīkam evam akñiṇi tasyoditi nāma*

**He who is to be seen in the sun is a golden person, having a glowing mustache, glowing hair, glowing from the tip of his nails, and having eyes like a blossoming red lotus. He is called Ut. Chāndogya Upaniñad 1.6.6**

He is the pesont to be worshipped in the heart. He is different from the vijñanamaya puruña. He is Nārāyaṇa. One should meditate on the Lord in the heart with this particular form.

The Lord has cit and acit as his body (sat and tyāt). His svarūpa is satyam jñānam. The form dwelling in the sun and endowed with lotus eyes is also this svarūpa. This is explained in Vedānta-sūtra 1.1.32. Thus only one entity should be worshipped. Thus all these statements describe Brahman only in his svarūpa. He should be meditated upon as having the body of the enjoyer and having a body which assists the jīvas and matter, as in the statement tad anupraviñya sac ca tyāc cābhavat.

Having explained what is to be attained, the question of what the person who knows Brahman experiences is answered.

All the knowers of Brahman go by the path of the sun. The knowers attain the ātmā who is ānanda-maya. (The jiva experiences bliss.) This indicates that the jīva is the worshipper and is enjoyed and the Lord is worshipped and is the enjoyer in the liberated state. Jīva becoming Brahman in the liberated state is rejected. The word etam in the description of the five puruñas means Paramātmā. The Lord has the anna-maya puruña, prāṇa-maya puruña, manomaya puruña, and vijñāna-maya puruña as his body. Vyāsārya explains that on leaving the body the wise man, and experiencer, realizes Paramātmā who is intense bliss and who has the other four puruñas as his vibhūtis.

## Ninth Anuvāk

*yato vāco nivartante \| aprāpya manasā saha \| ānandaṁ brahmaṇo vidvān \| na bibheti kutaścaneti \| etaɱ ha vāva na tapati \| kim ahaɱ sādhu nākaravam \| kim ahaṁ pāpam akaravam iti \| sa ya evaṁ vidvān ete ātmānaɱ spṛṇute \| ubhe hy evaiṣa ete ātmānaɱ spṛṇute \| ya evaṁ veda \| ityupaniṣat \|\| 1 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The person who knows blissful Brahman, from which words and mind turn back, does not fear anything. He does not lament “Why did I not do puṇyas? Why did I commit sins?” That person who knows saves himself from puṇya and sins. He who knows saves himself from both puṇya and sin. This is the secret teaching.**

**Комментарий:**

A verse is cited to prove that one attains fearlessness on attaining the Lord. Speech and mind, trying to understand the bliss of Brahman, cannot do so. By worshipping Brahman filled with bliss one destroys all sufferings and fear. This is further explained. He does not lament, “Why did I not do sukrṭs to take me to Svarga? Why did I commit sin which leads to hell?” This is because he has no desire for attaining Svarga and all his sins have been destroyed by the fire of knowledge of Brahman.

One should meditate on the glories of such brahma-vidyā. He saves himself (spṛṇute) from puṇya and sin (ete). He does not experience the results of puṇya or sin. The statement is repeated to suggest constant meditation on destruction of puṇya and sin and to indicate the end of the section. This most secret teaching should be give to the qualified person.

## 3. Bhṛguvallī

## First Anuvaka

*auṁ saha nāvavatu \| saha nau bhunaktu \| saha vīryaṁ karavāvahai \| tejasvi nāvadhītamastu mā vidviṣāvahai \| auṁ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ \|\|*

*bhṛgur vai vāruṇiḥ \| varuṇaṁ pitaram upasasāra \| adhīhi bhagavo brahmeti \| tasmā etat provāca \| annaṁ prāṇaṁ cakṣuḥ śrotraṁ mano vācamiti \| taɱ hovāca \|*

*yato vā imāni bhūtāni jāyante \| yena jātāni jīvanti \| yatprayantyabhisaṁviśanti \| tad vijijñāsasva \| tad brahmeti \| sa tapo'tapyata \| sa tapas taptvā \|\| 1 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**May the mantra protect us. May it nourish us. May it give us power. May our studies have power. May we not develop hatred. Peace, peace, peace.**

**Bhṛgu, son of Varuṇa approached his father Varuṇa and said to him, “Teach me about Brahman, respected father!” His father spoke to him. “Food, prāṇa, the eye, the eaer, the mind and speech are Brahman.”**

**“Brahman is that from which all beings arise, by which they live and into whom they merge. You should know that this is Brahman.” He performed austerities.**

**Комментарий:**

Bhṛgu-valli commences to explain Brahman through other characteristics, and that the mind purified by tapas causes attainment of Brahman. Bhṛgu, son of Varuṇa, approached his father with the request, “Teach (adhīhi) me about Brahman, respected one (bhagavo)!” Or please remember for instructing about Brahman (adhīhi). Varuṇa said “Food, prāṇa, the eye, the ear, the mind and speech are Brahman.” He said this in order to purify his son’s mind. Seeing his son confused, thinking “Is everything Brahman? Is one on the list Brahman? Then which one is Brahman?” He spoke again.

That is Brahman from which all beings arise, by which all beings live, as ātmās, into which all beings enter or merge (saṁviśanti). Śam can mean to become one, as in saṁveśa, meaning to enter as one unit. Or prayanti can mean gaining liberation. And abhisaṁviśanti can mean “they are destroyed at pralaya.” These are not exclusive definitions of Brahman (he has other characterisitics as well) and for that reason tad brahman is not repeated with each statement.

The intention of the statement, though not exclusive in nature, is to extinguish opposite ideas abou Brahman. If Brahman were only stated to be the cause of everything, there would be doubt that another entity maintains and destroys the universe. Thus Brahman’s perfect nature could not be established. If Brahman as the cause of destruction were not mentioned, one may claim that another entity gives liberation, which is the ultimate type of destruction. Thus Brahman could not be established as the greatest, possessing qualities to give liberation and being the object of attainment. Though merely being the cause of all entities eliminates other entities as causes of maintenance or destruction, being the cause of creaton, maintenance and destruction are all mentioned to indicate the extreme greatness of Brahman.

The words yataḥ, yena and yad indicate the cause and the word tat indicates that Brahman is that cause. Thus Brahman as the cause is established. Tad vijijñāsasva does not indicate a rule to deliberate or worship. Knowledge composed of deliberation cannot be ordered. It arises from attraction. And knowledge composed of worship was already stated in brhama-vid āpnoti param. The request was for explaining about Brahman as tattva, not the method. Thus tad vijijñāsasva implies given attention to the meaning of what is taught and destroying all doubt. Thus this statement also indicates Brahman’s nature as the cause.

## Second Anuvāk

*annaṁ brahmeti vyajānāt \| annād dhy eva khalv imāni bhutāni jāyante \| annena jātāni jīvanti \| annaṁ prayanty abhisaṁviśantīti \| tad vijñāya \| punar eva varuṇaṁ pitaram upasasāra \| adhīhi bhagavo brahmeti \| taɱ hovāca \| tapasā brahma vijijñāsasva \| tapo brahmeti \| sa tapo'tapyata \| sa tapastaptvā \|\| 1 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Having performed austerity, he realized food as Brahman. All beings arise from food. By food all beings live. All beings enter into food at destruction. Understanding this Bhṛgu approached his father again. He said, “Please teach me about Brahman, respected father.” The father said, “KBow brahman by austerity. Austerity is Brahman.”**

**The son performed austerity.**

**Комментарий:**

Varuṇa taught his son, not by directly showing Brahman, like holding a cow by its horn, but by giving instructions on Brahman’s characteristics. The command vijijñāsasva indicates this. Discerning that the meaning was “After attaining knowledge of Brahman, know that Brahman is of this nature” Bhṛgu understood that he should perform sādhana in the form of controlling his senses (tapas) since brahma-vidyā requires a direct method. So the meaning of the command is “Know Brahman by controlling the senses.” By that tapas he realized food as Brahman.

## Tenth Anuvāk

### 1

*na kañcana vasatau pratyācakṣīta \| tad vratam \| tasmād yayā kayā ca vidhayā bahv annaṁ prāpnuyāt \| arādhy asmā annam ity ācakṣate \| etadvai mukhato'naɱ rāddham \| mukhato'smā annaɱ rādhyate \| etad vai madhyato'nnaɱ rāddham \| madhyato'smā annaɱ rādhyate \| edad vā antato'nnaɱ rāddham \| antato'smā annaɱ rādhyate \|\| 1 \|\|*

By some means, even forbidden means, one should obtain enough food to feed guests. Food should be prepared for the worshipper (asmai). Thus it is not a fault to use any means to earn for the food. He who knows this will attain plenty of food by some means.

### 2

Now meditation related to the devatās is explained. One should meditate on satisfaction in rain and and on strength in lightning.**

**Комментарий:**

Because speech is a means for preservation, one should meditate on speech as preservation.

### 3

**Комментарий:**

### 4

*tan-nama ity upāsīta \| namyante'smai kāmāḥ \| tad-brahmety upāsīta \| brahmavān bhavati \| tad-brahmaṇaḥ parimara ity upāsīta \| pary eṇaṁ mriyante dviṣantaḥ sapatnāḥ \| pari ye'priyā bhrātṛvyāḥ \| sa yaś cāyaṁ puruṣe \| yaś cāsāvāditye \| sa ekaḥ \|\| 4 \|\|*

**He who is in the puruña and in the sun is one.**

**Перевод:**

**He who knows him, on departing from this world, attains the ātmā of the annamaya puruña. He attains the ātmā of the prāṇamaya puruña. He attains the ātmā of the manomaya puruña. He attains the ātmā of the vijñāna-maya puruña. He attains the Paramātmā, the ānanda-maya puruña. Wandering in this world at will, fully satisfied, he sings this song.**

### 6

*Aham annam aham annam aham annam \| aham annādo ‘ham annādo āham annādaḥ \| ahaɱ śloka-kṛd ahaɱ śloka-kṛd ahaɱ ślokakṛt \| aham asmi prathama-jā ṛtāsya \| pūrvaṁ devebhyo'mṛtasya nābhāyi \| yo mā dadāti sa ideva mā ‘vāḥ \| aham annam annam adantam ā dmi \| ahaṁ viśvaṁ bhuvanam abhyabhavām \| suvarna jyotīḥ \| ya evaṁ veda \| ity upaniṣat \|\| 6 \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**The Lord is food, the Lord is food. The Lord is food. The Lord is the eater of food. The Lord is the eater of food. The Lord is the eater of food. The Lord is worthy of all praise. He is worthy of all praise. He is worthy of all praise.**

**The Lord matures the karmas of the jīvas. He is the navel of liberation, which existed previous to the devatās. The person who instructs a worthy student about the Lord attains the Lord. **Комментарий:**

Aham means Paramātmā. Food is the enjoyed object and the eater of food is the enjoyer. These refer to the Lord as well. Śloka-kṛt means he who is praised for creating the world etc. the Lord is he who makes the jīva’s karmas (ṛtasya) fructify at the time of creation (prathama-jā). The Lord is the navel (nābhāi) of liberation (amrtasya), which existed previous to the devatās. He who teaches (dadāti) a worthy student about me (mā) attains (avāḥ) me. Id eva has no meaning. I pervade (ādmi) matter (annam) and the enjoyers of matter (annam adantam). At the time of destruction I suprass the universe (abhyabhavām). He who knows this develops a shining body (svarṇa jyotiḥ).
