Inclusion means the exclusion of all other groups, such as Whites, Asians, and Indians, socially, economically, and politically. Racial, religious, sexual, and class identity is given greater importance under DEI while merit, qualification, and competence are downgraded.

DEI is the combination of several socio-political-economic-cultural trends over the last few centuries.

The colonial divide-and-rule policy used to incite the weaker religions against the stronger religions, to create violent conflicts between them, and portrayed the colonizers as the good guys brokering peace and stability in the colonized country.

The DEI program combines the essence of the above four movements—religious, class, gender, and racial war—into a single multifaceted all-out war between religions, classes, genders, and races.

But in another sense, the activism of the academic should not shock us since academics began in Europe under the control of the Church to teach Christianity, create rational arguments for the justification of Christian doctrines, and establish the hegemony of the Church over society at large. **Ideological Biases Pervade All Areas**

The ideological biases of the academic system can be seen in every area of academic study:

- In physics, the goal is to advance the ideology of mechanicism and reductionism and decry any organismic and holistic view of reality.
- In biology, the goal is to advance the ideology of genetic evolution and decry any theory that gives primacy to mind or consciousness.
- In economics, the goal is to advance monetary theories that make the rich richer and decry any theory that brings economic stability and prosperity for all.
- In philosophy, the goal is to justify the scientific method rooted exclusively in instrumentation and mathematics and decry any system of knowledge rooted in ordinary perception or spiritual investigation.
- In sociology, the goal is to advance the ideology of individualism and decry any social system that promotes any type of collectivism, including units of family, community, or tradition.
- In the case of psychology, the goal is to advance different computational theories of the mind and decry alternatives rooted in introspection or meditation.
- In management, the goal is to promote ideologies of bureaucratic machines governed by laws and processes and decry theories that treat them as organisms.
- In history, the goal is to promote theories of social transformation based on external factors like climate and food rather than internal factors such as a society’s morals and ideals.

When genuine inquiry and analysis go against the preselected ideology, academics rejects the analysis rather than the ideology. It then proceeds to marginalize, hound, and ridicule the proponents of any alternative, to drive them out of the academic system. The peer-review system of accepting papers that advance prevalent ideological dogmas and rejecting the papers that advance alternative ways of thinking ensures that challengers to the current dogmas never gain visibility. Rather, by creating a cyclical citation loop within an ideological echo chamber, existing ideas are strengthened and alternatives are destroyed.

The concept of academic freedom exists only so long as the academic works strengthen the preselected ideology. The concept is forgotten if the academic critiques the chosen ideology. Thus, academics try to monopolize the human mind with a predetermined set of ideas rather than the truth established after completing a comprehensive and irrefutable process of inquiry and analysis.

These ideological biases remain hidden so long as they benefit most people. The same ideological biases start ringing alarm bells when the ideology doesn’t match the thinking of most people. Such discrepancies can arise when the academic system shifts to new ideologies rather quickly. But that change would remain imperceptible and go unnoticed if this change was gradual and incremental.

People who have studied the methods of the systematic transformation of a society from within, talk about a gradual ideological change brought into the society over four generations, with each generation lasting about 25 years. In one century, society can be transformed so drastically by a new ideology that seeps into society slowly that it doesn’t notice its own transformation. At every stage, it considers the next progressive phase in the ideology a logical step forward from the previous ones. No matter how alien an ideology might have been at the start, it is embraced and endorsed by a population if it is introduced gradually and incrementally. Academics is the centerpiece of this indoctrination system as it affects nascent minds, who go out into the world to propagate and advance the same set of ideas.

However, the same process of social transformation raises alarms if it is done too quickly or if it affects many people adversely. Most people are afraid of the unknown. They prefer the known devil to the unknown devil. New ideas have a slow adoption rate. The exception to this rule is if the new idea solves a thorny problem and benefits most people quickly. In contrast, the unknown devil becomes a greater alarm for most people if its benefits cannot be demonstrated easily and quickly.

For instance, the DEI program has many opponents because it has moved very fast and offends many people. They will come to believe that being ruled by a global corporatist managerial class is just the progression of humanity.

**The Detrimental Effects of Ideological Bias**

As the saying goes, a lie repeated often enough becomes the truth. People who have grown up with the lie cannot think any differently. Even if truth is presented to them, they consider it false. But all false ideologies have a limited lifespan. They follow the Law of Diminishing Returns. After people accept a falsehood as a truth, they benefit from it initially. Then, the benefits decrease, but history is cited to increase the investment in the same falsehood. Eventually, the returns on investment turn negative. At this juncture, most people abandon the falsehoods and seek an alternative. The seeking minds can again be swallowed by yet another falsehood which will then follow its curve of Diminishing Returns.

A society driven by preselected ideologies, without establishing their truth, always jumps from one ideology to another until it comes back to the stage where it started. At every stage, the problems of the current ideology are obscured, marginalized, and postponed. The naked emperor asks servile courtiers to praise his invisible garments. The charade goes on until an innocent child states the obvious—i.e., that the emperor is naked. But those who have become accustomed to living by ideologies rather than the truth don’t change. They have been dulled by previous indoctrination to the point that they cannot think. They look for short-term benefits and opportunistically jump onto a new ideology uncritically.

The modern academic system has been advancing and teaching preselected ideologies for so long that people have come to believe that there is no truth, or even if it exists, it cannot be known. We must therefore try to get the next best thing to truth—namely an ideology that promises the best returns. If the returns aren’t good, then we can change the ideology and hope for the best. This cynicism about truth is the main reason that truth is never pursued. People are so exhausted by pursuing preselected ideologies that they have concluded that if all those weren’t the truth then the truth is unknowable.

**Moving Academics Toward the Truth**

The method of finding the truth involves asking deep and big questions. An ideology that doesn’t answer such questions will be short-lived because the neglected questions don’t disappear. In fact, by neglecting them, we go farther from the truth. When the distance between the ideology and the truth becomes too big, then the ideology collapses. And yet, academics has never used the method of asking and answering deep and big questions. In fact, it marginalizes such questions by calling them irrelevant to the day-to-day problems of life or by giving answers that don’t address all the big and deep questions. In both cases, academics relies on the students being ignorant and timid. Indoctrination rests on the tendency for short-term thinking, shallow solutions, and quick gratification prevalent in the ignorant and timid masses.

To get out of this system of indoctrination, it is important to emphasize the quest for truth. Young minds must ask deep and big questions. Every ideology must be evaluated against such questions. People have to be respected based on their capacity to answer deep and big questions. Unless we put the education to test against deep and big questions, we will perpetually remain victims of false indoctrination.

Finally, we must note that prestigious universities (by modern standards) are not centers of learning the truth. They are descendants of Convents and Madrasas and continue to be centers of indoctrination. Their degrees are certificates of indoctrination rather than education. The journey to the truth begins by asking big and deep questions. We know the truth when we can answer all such questions. The ideology that answers these questions is long-lived. All others are generally outdated within the lifetime of the person who learned them during their childhood. Unless the academic system accepts the genuine methods of finding the truth, it will remain a system of indoctrination rather than education.
