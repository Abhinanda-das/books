## Introduction

We understand that the four authorized Vaisnava *sampradayas* originated from four sources. These original sources are Lord Siva; the four Kumaras, sons of Brahma; Laksmi; and Brahma. Sri Visnu Svami was the principle *acarya* of the *sampradaya,* or the disciplic succession, coming from Lord Siva. The philosophy propounded by him is known as *suddhadvaita-vada*. Sri Nimbaditya was the principle acarya of the disciplic succession coming from the four sons of Brahma, headed by the sage Sanaka. The philosophy propounded by them is known as *dvaitadvaita-vada*. Sri Ramanuja was the principle acarya of the disciplic succession coming from Laksmi-devi, who resides in the heart of Narayana, the Lord of Vaikuntha. The philosophy propounded by him is known as *visistadvaita- vada*. Sri Madhvacarya was the principle *acarya* of the disciplic succession coming from Brahma. The philosophy propounded by him is known as *dvaita-vada*. There are differences of opinion about the dates of the appearances of these four *acaryas.* We present in this book whatever information is available.

It is written in *Sri* *Caitanya-caritamrta* that when Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu traveled to South India, He had a discussion with a Vaisnava of the Sri-sampradaya named Sri Venkata Bhatta, at Sri Rangam. The son of Venkata Bhatta left home and received the Gaudiya Vaisnava philosophy from Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu at Sri Purusottama-ksetra. Thereafter, on the order of Lord Caitanya, he spent his remaining days in Vrndavana in the association of the Lord’s dearest associates, Sri Rupa and Sri Sanatana, and was known as one of the six Gosvamis, Sri Gopala Bhatta Gosvami.

His great contribution was to collect and present scriptural evidence for *Hari-bhakti-vilasa*, which was compiled by Sri Sanatana Gosvami. It is because of him that some influence of the path of devotional service in regulative principles as followed by the Sri Ramanuja *sampradaya* entered the Gaudiya Vaisnava society. Sri Gopala Bhatta Gosvami’s uncle, Sri Prabodhananda Sarasvati, also resided in Vrndavana and composed many literatures, including *Sri Caitanya-candramrtam*, *Sri Navadvipa- dhama-satakam*, and *Sri* *Radha-rasa-sudha-nidhi.*

During Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s South Indian tour He visited Udupi, the headquarters of Sri Madhva *sampradaya*, and had a discussion with the *acaryas* there. Later on, while Lord Caitanya was at Jagannatha Puri, Sri Vallabhacarya went there for the second time and met Him. At that time, due to his pride, Vallabhacarya refused to accept Sridhara Svami’s commentary on *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, claiming that his own commentary was superior. However, Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu cut down his false pride.

Sridhara Svami was a supporter of Sri Visnu Svami’s philosophy of *suddhadvaita-vada.* Apart from this incident regarding Lord Caitanya and Vallabhacarya, we have no information of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s meeting any followers of Sri Visnu Svami or Sri Nimbaditya.

In his *Sat-Sandarbha*, Sri Jiva GosvamI frequently quoted portions of Sridhara Svami’s commentary on *Srimad- Bhagavatam*. Later on, Sri Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura quoted some portions of Sridhara Svami’s commentary in his own commentary on *Srimad-Bhagavatam*. Although his predecessor, Sri Baladeva Vidyabhusana Prabhu, in his *Govinda-bhasya* commentary on *Vedanta-sutra*, frequently quoted the writings of *acaryas* of the Sri Madhva *sampradaya,* his commentary was primarily based on the teachings of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu and His disciples, the six Gosvamis, which strongly establish the philosophy of *acintya-bhedabheda*.

Sri Baladeva Vidyabhusana Prabhu had first referred to the Gaudiya Vaisnava *sampradaya* as the Brahma-Madhva-Gaudiya *sampradaya* in his book *Prameya Ratnavali*. For this reason many people mistakenly think that Sri Baladeva Vidhyabhusana Prabhu is the one who first identified the Gaudiya Vaisnava *sampradaya* with the Madhva *sampradaya.* However, this is not a fact.

Sri Gadadhara Pandita’s disciple was Sri Gopala Guru Gosvami, and his disciple, Sri Dhyanacandra Gosvami, accepted the Brahma-Madhava-Gaudiya *sampradaya.* Although Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu is the combined form of Radha and Krsna, He took initiation from Sri Isvara Puri, a *tridandi sannyasi* of the Madhva *sampradaya*. Srila Vyasadeva had written in the *Padma Purana* that unless one formally accepts initiation in one of the four authorized *sampradayas,* his chanting of *mantras* would not yield any result. It is for this reason that the Gaudiya Vaisnavas don’t hesitate to identify themselves as members of the Brahma-Madhva-Gaudiya *sampradaya.*

The people of Bengal were generally ignorant of the four Vaisnava *sampradayas* until Sri Bhaktivinoda Thakura wrote some essays about them in his Sajjana-tosani magazine. After him, Sri Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Gosvami Thakura established Sri Caitanya Matha at Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu’s birthplace in Sridham Mayapur, and also sixty-four Gaudiya Mathas throughout India. Among them, the Matha at Dhaka was named Sri Madhva Gaudiya Matha. Moreover, in the original Sri Caitanya Matha, Sri Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati not only installed Deities of Sri Sri Radha-Krsna and Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, but in the four comers of the temple he also installed four deities of the four *acaryas*, as well as deities of their founders; Sri Laksmi, Sri Brahma, Sri Sadasiva, and Sri Catuhsana. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakur has elaborately discussed the four *sampradayas* in the essays that form the basis of this book.

## The Unique Position Of An Acarya

Many people think that the path of devotional service is covered with a soft bed of flowers. Blind faith, indiscipline, recklessness, a lack of power of discrimination, sentimentalism, and emotional outbursts are all counted by them as being in the category of devotional service. But from the scriptures and from the teachings of the *acaryas* we understand that, although the path of devotional service is the only means of attaining the ultimate goal of life, it is full of thorns. Especially in this age of Kali, or quarrel, it is covered with millions of thorns.

So that the travelers on the path of devotional service can safely and without disturbance reach the desire tree of Krsna’s lotus feet and thus relish the fruit of love of God, the causelessly merciful *acaryas,* who are sent to us by the Supreme Lord, warn those who are desirous of entering the path of devotional service of the obstacles they will surely encounter. This naturally selfless propensity of the *acaryas* is an example of their great compassion for the living entities.

Despite knowing that one’s hands may be injured while uprooting thorny bushes, and that wild animals or cruel snakes residing in the bushes may bite, one engaged in such work should not lose enthusiasm for performing his duty. Rather, his endeavor and enthusiasm for removing all the thorns will progressively increase as he continues to progress toward his goal. Similarly, increasing enthusiasm for removing the thorns on the path of devotional service is seen as one of the characteristics of an *acarya*.

Those who are selfish, being simply desirous of their own happiness, and who are idle and afraid of irreligious people, generally desist from doing good to others upon hearing the shouting of such atheists. They think, “All we need is to fulfill our self-interest by achieving fame and personal happiness. Why should we go to so much trouble for the benefit of others? Why should we risk being abused by others?”

Another group of people think, “Let us simply become *bhajananandis* (solitary worshipers) so that we won’t have to face all this trouble. In this way we won’t have to hear criticism from others.”

On the other hand, an *acarya* who becomes distressed upon seeing others’ suffering is not selfish, idle, or simply desirous of his personal happiness. He is not afraid of other people. He thinks, “Even if hundreds of people, even if all the godless people of the countless universes assemble and abuse me with harsh language, I will accept it and boldly continue to declare the truth; If the topics of reality enter the ears of even a single person among millions of people from millions of universes completely averse to Krsna, and thus remove the contamination of the cheating propensity from his heart, then I will think that I have been able to fulfill Mahapabhu’s mission.

“I know for certain that all living entities within the innumerable universes are wandering about in different species of life because of their aversion to Krsna. All living entities within the universe, beginning from Brahma, are averse to Lord Krsna, or the supreme Absolute Truth. Therefore, not everyone is going to hear about the truth. If we can find even one person who is interested in hearing the truth, that is sufficient. Such a person can first perfect his life and then preach the truth to others.

“Perhaps it is for this reason that Sri Bhaktivinoda Thakura said, ‘A preacher brings more benefit to the people of the world than those who, according to their own taste, neglect preaching work by absorbing themselves in the bliss of their own *bhajana*.’”

All those who preached the truth had to witness stiff opposition from people who are selfish, averse to God, and envious. Those who are idle and fond of sense gratification accept the conventional path of birth and death as being thornless while accepting support from their blind followers; or they have become fully absorbed in the bliss of their *bhajana* while maintaining an attitude of indifference regarding the welfare of the godless civilization: or they simply imitate genuine bhajananandis while desiring fame and sense pleasures, thus cheating themselves and others. Almost all these people are very lazy and unauthorized. Of the three classes of people mentioned above, the first and third are completely duplicitous, desiring sense gratification and being afraid of unrighteous people.

Such persons belong to a society of people who are averse to Krsna, while externally posing as devotees. For such persons, pleasing godless people is the aim of life and the object of their satisfaction. They never have to face harassment from ordinary people. They are simply busy trying to please everyone so as to earn wealth and attract women, while maintaining the *sakta* philosophy internally, the *saiva* philosophy externally, and the Vaisnava philosophy publicly.

As the habit of smoking is praised in the society of intoxicated people, so in a godless society, or in the newspaper columns presented by godless people, only glorification of people who desire personal happiness, who accept the conventional path of birth and death, and who make a business of the holy name, mantras, and recitation of *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, as well as the so- called renounced devotees who imitate *paramahamsa* Vaisnavas, is heard. The few real possession-less *paramahamsa* Vaisnavas (like Srila Vamsidasa Babaji Maharaja of Kulia), who, although appearing to belong to the second class as described above, but who are genuinely retired from all material activities, are not blasphemed by envious people. As a show of apparent kindness, such envious persons spare them because they think that they are bereft of their rightful share of material enjoyment, are discarded from society, are good for nothing, and are unable to utilize their natural cunningness.

An *acarya* does not expect, or care for, the cheap adoration of ordinary people that materialistic so-called devotees hanker for, nor does he engage in solitary *bhajana* while considering only his personal self-interest. Rather, because he follows in the footsteps of Sri Gaurasundara, he tries to deliver all the people of the world while setting the perfect example.

Those who are godless are naturally envious of genuine devotees. Therefore it is seen that preachers of the truth are often the targets of torture by atheistic and envious people. Although, to ordinary vision, such preachers are seen to be put into difficulty by malicious people, they are not actually disturbed because, like Lord Visnu, the Vaisnavas are transcendentally situated. Even Ravana could not actually disturb Lord Rama, for he could only kidnap an illusory Sita.

Although Prahalada was a devotee of Lord Visnu, before he took the position of a preacher, he was a favorite of the demons in general and Hiranyakasipu in particular. He was like the garland on their neck, like the nectar in their heart, for he was the object of their affection, loving care, and glorification. But from the very day he began to boldly speak the truth to the king of the demons, and began to preach devotional service to Visnu to his classmates, he made enemies.

Prahalada preached about the necessity for giving up family attachment and worshiping Lord Hari. He preached to his schoolmates that the service of attached family priests is useless, whereas service to *paramahamsas* who are completely engaged in the worship of Lord Hari, after giving up the association of those who are adverse to the Lord, is the true path of auspiciousness.

The mundane relationship between a father and son is so strong that, out of illusion, a father sees his dark son as fair, like molten gold, and sees his blind son as lotus-eyed. He sees his disgraced son as an ornament of his family because all of his son’s faults appear to him to be good qualities. All this is simply due to illusion.

The same thing was seen in the relationship of Prahlada with his father, but his great fault was that he spoke the truth. This hurt his bewildered father so much that this wonderful quality of his son appeared to him as a grave fault. Thus, even though his son was dearer to him than his own self, he finally wanted to kill him.

Even in this material world, has anybody heard of a case where, for his own self-interest, a father put his five-year-old gentle son, who is dearer to him than his own life, who has not yet learned to disregard his father by the influence of his wife, or who has not yet become an enemy of his father due to greed for a kingdom, like Emperor Aurangzeb, under the feet of an intoxicated elephant, or throw him from a hill top, or place him in a blazing fire, or administer him poison?

Prahalada’s only fault was that he preached the truth. There is no surprise when the selfish people of this material world become enemies of preachers of the truth. Even in Satya-yuga, when religious principles stood on four legs, a father did not hesitate to oppose his son who was a preacher of the truth. Even the naturally affectionate heart of a father became filled with hatred for his son. How glorious is Lord Visnu’s illusory energy that makes the impossible possible! The living entities are so stubborn and so averse to God, from their toenails to their hair, that they will not hear the truth that explains the essential characteristics of God, or Krsna. It’s as if they had taken a vow to oppose the truth.

When examples of disregard for the truth and envy directed at the preachers of the truth were found even in Satya-yuga, then it is not at all astonishing to see indifference to the truth and a combined effort to thwart the mission of the acaryas who are engaged in spreading truth in this age of Kali.

In the life of Sri Ramanuja, who was one of the four *acaryas* that preached transcendental religion, we find that in his childhood he protested the explanation of the phrase *kapyasam pundarikaksam* given by his spiritual master, and preached the truth without accepting subordination to such a false teacher. From that day onward, the harassment of Sri Ramanuja began. His so-called spiritual master,Yadavaprakasa, who was envious of Lord Visnu and the Vaisnavas, organized a conspiracy to kill him.

The worshipers of Sri Ranganatha at Sri Rangam would steal the ingredients meant for the Lord’s service and, instead of using the Lord’s wealth in His service, used it to deceive ordinary people by preaching that their wives’ service and satisfaction was as good as Lord Visnu’s service. Sri Ramanuja protested this theft and spirit of material enjoyment.

Wherever the truth is preached, people who are blinded by selfishness and thus opposed to the truth will become an enemy. This is inevitable. At first the priests at Sri Rangam administered poison to Sri Ramanuja, mixing it with rice. Fortunately, the simple-hearted wife of one of these priests informed Ramanuja of their evil intent by a hint and so the *acarya* gave the rice to a dog. The dog immediately died after eating the rice.

On another occasion, the priests of Lord Rariganatha mixed poison with *caranamrta* and gave it to Ramanuja. This material world is always ruled by those who are envious of Lord Visnu and the Vaisnavas, for they are the majority. Such an arrangement has been made by the supreme will of Lord Visnu, just to protect the rarely-attained and most confidential treasure of devotional service, and to bewilder the demons.

When Sri Ramanujacarya began preaching about the worship of Lord Visnu, the king of that province, who was a *smarta* attached to mundane fruitive activities, made a great effort to forcibly convert him into a *smarta saivite*. However, an attached mundane fruitive worker can never touch even a single hair of a transcendental Vaisnava *acarya*.

Let the attached *smartas* be the leaders of society, let them be the rulers of kingdoms, let them have the power to punish and kill, but a Vaisnava, and especially an *acarya*, is never subject to their control. The so-called Vaisnavas that lick the feet of *smartas* who are attached to fruitive activities may be afraid of incurring their anger, but a real Vaisnava doesn’t care for them. The Vaisnava *acarya*, who is free from the contamination of Kali, is never afraid of a *smarta’s* shouting and scolding.

For this reason Sri Ramanuja did not refrain from boldly speaking the truth, despite being shown the angry reddened eyes by the *smarta* King of Cola, who was envious of Lord Visnu and the Vaisnavas.

Sri Ramanuja’s ideal disciple, Kuresa, refused to accept the *smarta* philosophy, even though the Cola king, Krmikantha, plucked out his eyes. It has repeatedly been seen that whenever the truth was boldly preached in this world, unscrupulous people who are averse to Lord Visnu and the Vaisnavas tried their best to obstruct the attempt. Indeed, this is one of the symptoms of the truth. A theory supported by the godless people of the world must be an atheistic conclusion. Similarly, when it is seen that all the godless people of the world attempt to oppose the preaching of a philosophy, it can be concluded that it must contain the truth for that very reason.

When the *acaryas*, Sri Visnu Svami, Sriman Madhva, and Sri Nimbaditya preached the truth in this world, they also had to face stiff opposition. By studying their lives one can see how they simultaneously displayed anger at those who are envious of the devotees and set the ideal example for the benefit of others, without any personal motive. Simply by seeing such pure devotees, devotion for the maintainer of all living entities, Lord Visnu, becomes established within the heart.

All glories to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Visnu, the protector of *sanatana-dharma* and the ocean of absolute truth! Unlimitedly glorious are the transcendental *acaryas* who have been sent by Him, who are causelessly merciful, and who are therefore benefactors of the entire world.

Now let me say a few words about the crest-jewel of all *acaryas*, Sri Gaurasundara, as well as about His followers, who also acted as acaryas, before I begin narrating the history of the four *acaryas* of the four *sampradayas*. How distasteful a person becomes in the eyes of ordinary materialistic men when he preaches the truth can also be seen in the life of Sri Gaurasundara, the crest-jewel among all *acaryas.*

As long as Lord Visnu and the Vaisnavas continue to deceive the atheists, such godless people will praise them. When Sri Gaurasundara, for the welfare of the godless society, enacted His pastimes as a pious householder, a learned *brahmana*, an obedient student, and an erudite teacher; when He pacified His mother when she was afflicted with grief after the demise of her husband, when He displayed affection for His birthplace and relatives, when He performed the funeral rites of His father at Gaya, and when He enacted the pastime of drinking water that had washed the feet of a *brahmana*, just to manifest devotion for the demigods and *brahmanas*, the attached fruitive workers, without understanding the actual purport of the Lord’s activities, which bewilder the demons, praised Him, thinking Him to be an ordinary living entity like them.

However, such glorification of Lord Caitanya did not last long. After He returned from Gaya and revealed His true identity, fulfilling the desires of the pure devotees, like Sri Advaita and Srivasa, and began to preach the truth, the same *smarta* Hindus who had previously praised Nimai with a hundred mouths began to criticize Him from all directions. At this time, the blasphemy of Nimai started.

The *smarta* blasphemers said, “Nimai is no longer the good boy He once was. It appears that He now wants to destroy the religious principles of the Hindus. Now He preaches the glories of the Supreme Lord, while claiming that worshiping goddess Candi and goddess Manasa, and spending lavishly for such occasions, is entirely useless. He preaches the superiority of serving Vaisnavas and accepting the shelter of a spiritual master over performing the *sraddha* ceremony at Gaya.

“He also preaches that a non-devotee is untouchable and should not be spoken to, just like a dog-eater, whereas a devotee, even if he had taken birth in a family of dog-eaters, is worshipable and supremely pure. He even chases, with a stick in His hand, His schoolmates that are worshipers of five gods and are fond of mundane fruitive activities, when they disagree with His version of the truth.”

These verses from *Sri Caitanya-caritamrta* attest to this attitude of the blasphemers.

*purve bhala chila ei nimai pandita*  
*gaya haite asiya calaya viparita*

**“Nimai Pandita was previously a very good boy, but since He returned from Gaya, He conducts Himself differently.”**

*suni’ krodha kaila saba paduyara gana*  
*sabe meli’ kare tabe prabhura nindana*

**“Hearing of the incident, all the students became greatly angry and joined together in criticizing the Lord.”**

*saba desa bhrasta kaila ekala nimani*  
*brahmana marite cahe, dharma-bhaya nai*

**“‘Nimai Pandita alone has spoiled the entire country,’ they accused. ‘He wants to strike a caste** ***brahmana*****. He has no fear of religious principles.**

*punah yadi aiche kare mariba tahare*  
*kon va manusa hay a, ki karite pare*

**“‘If He again performs such an atrocious act, certainly we shall retaliate and strike Him in turn. What kind of important person is He, that He can check us in this way?’”**

Desiring to benefit the mundane fruitive *smartas* after witnessing their wicked mentality, and to distinguish Himself from the mundane *smarta* community, Nimai decided to accept the renounced order of life, *sannyasa*. By His personal example, the Lord showed that a Vaisnava is never under the control of *smarta* society. The *smartas’* acceptance of a Vaisnava as one of them only shows how deceived and unfortunate they are.

“O *smarta*, attached to ritualistic activities! Before you were deceived, thinking Sri Gaurasundara to be an attached householder like yourself while maintaining your offensive mentality of considering Lord Visnu and Vaisnavas as belonging to a particular caste. But today, to bestow His causeless mercy upon you, and to teach ordinary people, He has accepted the renounced order of life, giving up the bad association of mundane fruitive *smartas.*”

The Lord presented the ideal example of renouncing the worldly morality of maintaining a wife and serving a mother, even at the cost of fuelling the burning fire of lamentation in His mother, who was aggrieved due to separation from her husband. He gave up attachment for birthplace and relatives, and showed the futility of performing the *sraddha* ceremony at Gaya and traveling to holy places of pilgrimage, rather than engaging in the service of Lord Krsna under the shelter of a spiritual master. He demonstrated that drinking the water that had washed the feet of a *brahmana* is not an act meant for fruitive workers, but is rather a show of affection by the Lord for His servant, and proof of how exalted a Vaisnava is. The Lord even transgressed the mundane injunction that one should not accept *sannyasa* in Kali-yuga, a rule that is applicable to *smartas.*

After accepting *sannyasa* Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu rejected outright many prejudices found in the *smarta* understanding, which is materialistic by nature. The *smartas* consider the remnants of the Lord’s food to be contaminated under certain conditions, and they consider a Vaisnava as belonging to a particular caste. Both of these attitudes are offensive and thus were rejected by Lord Caitanya. This is seen in the pastime of the Lord ordering Advaita Acarya to sit with Mukunda and Haridasa, who was bom in a Muslim family, and accept *prasada*.

When Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu thus attacked the stereotyped conceptions of the *smartas*, who consider *maha- prasada* and Vaisnavas to be mundane, He was blasphemed by the Mayavadi *sannyasis* at Benaras, headed by Prakasananda Sarasvati, and even Ramacandra Puri, a disciple of Sri Madhavendra Puri. Thus it is to be concluded that an *acarya* who takes the responsibility of preaching the truth will invariably face many impediments while acting for the welfare of human society. We can only try to imagine the heart of an *acarya*, who feels unhappy by seeing others’ unhappiness.

The readers of Sri *Caitanya-caritamrta* have seen how, in the ninth chapter of the *Madhya-lila*, the Buddhists attempted to dishonor Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu because of His preaching against their principles. Whenever Vaisnava *acaryas* take up the responsibility of preaching the truth, godless people, who are the majority of the world’s population and are firmly determined to bring about their own ruination, vehemently try to oppose them.

If Sri Nityananda Prabhu had worshiped the Lord in a solitary place rather than preach the chanting of the holy name door to door, He would not have been attacked by Madhai with a clay pitcher. If Haridasa Thakura had kept to himself rather than preach Krsna consciousness, he would not have been beaten in twenty-two marketplaces. Because he preached the truth, even today, the attached *smarta* community commits a grave offense at the lotus feet of Haridasa Thakura by criticizing him for having accepted food after the *sraddha* ceremony of a *brahmana*. The *smartas* consider him a Yavana that disregarded the principles of *varnasrama-dharma*. Even today the *smartas* blaspheme Nityananda, saying that he ate food cooked by low- class people and transgressed the principles of *varnasrama-dharma* adhered to by them.

Hoping to deliver such attached fruitive workers from committing offenses at the lotus feet of Lord Visnu, the greatly merciful Vaisnava *acarya*, Srila Vrndavana dasa Thakura, used very strong language, saying, “I kick on the head of such an offender.” Still, some *smartas* criticize Vrndavana dasa Thakura for making such statements.

In due course of time, even sinful persons can be elevated to the platform of piety. And yet, even after five hundred years, some people openly, and others secretly, criticize the spiritual masters of the entire world, including Lord Caitanya, Lord Nityananda, Thakura Haridasa, and Thakura Vrndavana dasa. From this we can understand how degraded the people of this world are, and how compassionate are the *acaryas* towards the fallen conditioned souls. Even against such opposition, such greatly magnanimous personalities tirelessly endeavor to benefit those suffering in material existence.

## The Authorized Sampradayas

*sampradaya-vihina ye*  
*mantras te nisphala matah*

**"Unless you are initiated by a bona fide spiritual master in disciplic succession, the** ***mantra*** **that you might have received is without any effect."**

*sampradaya vihina ye mantraste viphala matah*  
*ata kalau bhavisyanti catvarah sampradayinah*  
*sri brahma-rudra-sanaka vaisnavah ksiti-pavanah*  
*catvaraste kalau bhavya utkale purusottamat*  
*ramanujam sri svi cakre madhvacaryah caturmukhah*  
*sri visnusvaminam rudran nimbaditya catuhsanah*

**"The holy formula (*****mantra*****) that is not received from an authorized disciplic succession will never bear fruit. This is why, in this age of Kali, four great souls will emerge to establish the four Vaisnava disciplic successions coming from Brahma, Rudra, Laksmi, and four Kumaras. These four great leaders will manifest their four authorized disciplic successions from the holy** ***dhama*** **of Purl. Laksmi-devi will give this sacred knowledge to Ramanujacarya, Lord Brahma to Sri Madhva, Rudra to VisnusvamI, and the four Kumaras to Nimbarkacarya. Thus, after being empowered with spiritual knowledge, these four will promulgate their disciplic successions."**

Srila Vyasadeva, the compiler of *Vedanta-sutra*, is a disciple of Narada Muni. In the *Padma Purana* he has stated that unless one accepts a *mantra* from an authorized *sampradaya,* it will not bear any fruit. For this reason, by the will of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Visnu, the protector of *sanatana-dharma*, the four principal *acaryas* appeared to inaugurate the four authorized *sampradayas* in Kali-yuga. Without being connected to one of these four authorized *sampradayas* there is no possibility of achieving perfection in the chanting of *mantras*. Conditioned souls are by nature restless because of their unsteady existence. A restless person cannot have fixed intelligence, and thus he attempts to follow various paths in the course of time. If we look at history, we will see that nobody has attained auspiciousness by opposing the authorized *sampradayas* in this land of Bharata-varsa, which is the best place for executing religious principles, Unfortunately, when Western philosophers exerted their influence within India, so-called educated people began opposing the authorized Vedic *sampradayas*. ## Acarya Sri Visnusvami

There was a powerful king named Pandyavijaya, or Panduvijaya, ruling Pandya-desa. He was bom around 300 B.C. Pandya-desa was an ancient kingdom situated on the shore of the ocean in South India, forming the southernmost part of ancient Dravida. Although he was the king, Panduvijaya steadily engaged himself in the worship of Lord Visnu and the Vaisnavas. His reign began about 300 years after the appearance of Lord Buddha. At the time of King Pandyavijaya, Vaisnava *dharma* had almost been forgotten due to the vigorous preaching of the Buddhists.

Powerful King Pandyavijaya drove the practice of Buddhism out of Pandya-desa because he was very eager to re-establish eternal Vaisnava *dharma* throughout his kingdom. King Pandyavijaya’s priest was a great devotee and staunch worshiper of Lord Visnu named Sri Devesvara. This devotee advised his Vaisnava king on a great variety of subjects. Being advised by Devesvara, the king of Pandya converted the people throughout his entire kingdom into devotees of Lord Visnu. With the help of Devesvara and his son, King Pandyavijaya delivered the Deities of Lord Jagannatha, Baladeva and Subhadra from the clutches of the Buddhists, who claimed that they were actually Deities of Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha.

The king placed these Deities on a chariot and took them to Sundaracala, and later They were re-established in the temple at Sri Nilacala. Even today, when Lord Jagannatha, Balarama and Subhadra climb upon Their chariots at the time of Ratha-yatra, this is known as Pahandi, or Panduvijaya, after King Pandyavijaya. When Lord Jagannatha returns from Sundaracala to Nilacala; this is called Punaryatra Indeed, the present worshipers of Lord Jagannatha are known as Pandas. Pandyavijaya’s priest, Devesvara, tried his best to free the people from the misconceptions preached by the followers of Lord Buddha, and he reinstated the worship of Lord Purusottama at Nilacala, according to the prescribed rules and regulations.

Lord Purusottama, who is very affectionate to His devotees, was very pleased by the devotional service of Devesvara. So that the eternal religion of devotional service to Lord Visnu would be preached throughout the world, Lord Purusottama empowered a suitable personality and had him take birth as Devesvara’s son. When Devesvara saw the great effulgence and influence of his son, he became very satisfied and named him Devatanu.

Devesvara engaged his son, Devatanu, in the service of Lord Visnu right from his childhood. Indeed, the boy showed no interest in any activity that was not in relation to the service of Lord Visnu. At a very young age, Devatanu exhibited his extraordinary capability for acquiring transcendental knowledge. Devatanu accepted *tridanda sannyasa* according to the rules and regulations prescribed for Vaisnava *sannyasa* as found in the Vedic literatures and was given the name Visnusvami.

Indeed, it was Visnusvami who revived the institution of Vedic *tridanda sannyasa* in Kali-yuga. In his disciplic succession, one hundred and eight names of Vedic *tridanda sannyasis* are accepted. There were approximately seven hundred *tridandi sannyasis* appearing in his disciplic succession. Due to fear that this book would become too voluminous, we will not list the names of these *sannyasis*. Those who are interested can find the one hundred and eight names of *tridandi sannyasis* in a book published by SrI Gaudiya Math named *Gaudiya Kanthahara*. The names of seven hundred *tridandi sannyasis* in the disciplic succession from Visnusvami are found in the book *Vaisnava Manjusa*.

The mistaken idea that the custom of awarding ten different names of *sannyasa* was first introduced by Sankaracarya will be removed by studying the history of the Visnusvami *sampradaya*. From such a study it will also be learned that the Vaisnava *sampradayas* did not come into existence after Sankaracarya, but have been current since time immemorial. The fact is that Devatanu Visnusvami appeared long before Sankaracarya.

After accepting the renounced order of life, Devatanu was known as Acarya Visnusvami throughout the world. Because two other Visnusvami's appeared later on and acted as *acaryas*, Devatanu is known as the original Visnusvami. Visnusvami was especially concerned about how the Buddhists, who were averse to the Vedas, were leading people away from *sanatana- dharma*. Visnusvami saw how some Buddhists engaged in destroying manuscripts of the scriptures, which are the source of Vedic evidence, and so he carefully protected the *Brahma- sutra*, or Badarayana-sutra, which are the essence of all Vedic literatures.

He thereafter composed a wonderful commentary on the *Brahma-sutra* so that the lost glory of eternal Vaisnava dharma would be firmly re-established within the world. This commentary is known in the society of learned scholars as *Sarvajna-sutra*.

Some people mistakenly accept Sarvajnatma Muni, a follower of the philosophy known as *kevaladvaita-vada*, as the original Visnusvami. Sarvajnatma Muni preached the philosophy of Mayavada, which is based on self-worship. The commentary on *Brahma-sutra* of Sri Visnusvami, who was also known as Sarvajna Muni, preaches the philosophy known as *suddhadvaita-vada*,i He preached that Lord Visnu is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the living entities are eternally subordinate to Him, the holy name of the Lord is worshipable, devotional service to the Supreme Lord is the eternal activity of the liberated souls, the Supreme Lord and His associates are eternal, and the Supreme Lord is the Absolute Truth. The conclusions presented in the commentary of Sri Visnusvami, or Sarvajna Muni, refute the unauthorized conclusions of *kevaladvaita-vada*, and are known as *suddhadvaita-siddhanta.*

Sri Visnusvami called himself a follower of Sri Rudra and a worshiper of Nrpancasya Visnu, the Lord of Sri Rudra. It is stated in the *Mahabharata* that the Rudra *sampradaya* was manifested in this world by the mercy of Lord Narayana during the second, or Caksusa, birth of Brahma. The great sages known as Balakhilyas, who are members of the Rudra *sampradaya*, then protected Vaisnava *dharma*. Sankaracarya's *sampradaya* is distinct from the Sivasvami *sampradaya*, which later on became known as the Lingayat *sampradaya*, and came into existence much later.

Apart from *Sarvajna-sutra*, we see the name of Sri Visnusvami, his worshipable Lord Nrpancasya Visnu, and his worship of Lord Nrsimhadeva, in a philosophical work known as *Rasesvara-darsana*, which was compiled by Sayana Madhva.

In *Rasesvara-darsana*, this verse is found.

*nrpancasya sarirasya nityatva*

*saccinnitya nijdcintya purna nandaika*  
*vigraham nrpancasyam aham vande*

**According to Visnusvami, the form of Nrpancasya Lord Visnu is eternal. The Supreme Lord is perfect and has a transcendental form. He is eternal, full of knowledge, always blissful, and inconceivable.**

Sri Devatanu, who appeared in the house of Devesvara Bhatta Desika of Madhura, during the rule of King Pandyavijaya, became famous throughout the land as the original Visnusvami. Later on, seven hundred *tridandi acaryas*, who were his followers, preached the worship of Lord Visnu as outlined in his book, *Sarvajna-sutra*. Many ignorant people who could not understand the difference between the philosophy of *suddhadvaita-vada* and the impersonal conception of *kevaladvaita-vada*, accepted the Sivasvami *sampradaya*, considering it as being Mayavada. ## The Siddhanta Preached By Sri Visnusvami

Sri Visnusvami preached *advaya-jnana*, or *advaita siddhanta*. Later on, when other philosophies that opposed this *siddhanta* became prominent, the Vaisnava *acaryas* called Sri Visnusvami’s *siddhanta “suddhadvaita-vada*,” and the unauthorized *siddhanta* of the Mayavadis was called *“kevaladvaita-vada,”* or *“viddhadvaita- vada.”*

*vastunomso jivah vastunah saktir maya ca vastunah karyam*  
*jagacca tat sarvam vasteva na tatah prthagiti*

**“According to Sri Visnusvami’s** ***suddhadvaita*** **philosophy, the** ***jivas*** **(living entities) are parts and parcels of the Absolute Truth,** ***maya*** **is the energy of the Absolute Truth, and the material world is an effect of the energy of the Absolute Truth. All of them are factual, and none of them are separate from the Absolute Truth.”**

## Sri Visnusvami’s Siddhanta I

Concerning the Living Entities

Acarya Visnusvami collected the following evidence from *Brahma-sutra* and other *sruti-sastras* to substantiate his claim that the living entities are parts and parcels of the Supreme Lord.

*amso nana vyapa desat (Brahma-sutra 2.3.42)*. “Since the living entities have been described by the *Vedas* sometimes as *‘Brahma*,’ sometimes as ‘ignorant,’ sometimes as ‘spirit,’ sometimes as ‘servant,’ and sometimes as ‘fragmental parts,’ it is to be concluded that they are parts and parcels of the Supreme Brahman.”

*mantra varnat (Brahma-sutra 2.3.43).* “The *sruti mantras* describe all living entities as being fragmental parts of the Supreme Soul.”

*api smaryayate* *(Brahma-sutra 2.3.44).* “The *smrti-sastras* confirm that the living entities are parts and parcels of the Supreme Lord.”

*prakasadivattu naivam parah (Brahma-sutra 2.3.45).* “Although the individual living entities are parts and parcels of the Supreme Lord, His plenary portions, such as Matsya, are not like the individual living entities. For example, the sun is fire and a spark is also fire, but there is a gulf of difference between the two. Another example is that although honey and wine are both liquids, there is a gulf of difference between the two. Similarly, although the individual living entities and the Matsya incarnation are both expansions of the Supreme Lord, there is a great difference between them.”

*yathagneh ksudra visphulinga vyuccaranti*  
*evam evasmadatmanah sarvani bhutani vyuccaranti*

**“Just as sparks are manifested from a great fire, the infinitesimal individual spirit souls are manifest from the complete spiritual whole.”**

*nityo nityanam cetanas cetananam eko bahunam yo vidadhati kaman.* “The Supreme Lord is the supreme eternally conscious person who maintains all other living entities.”

In the *smrti-sastras* also it is stated (Bhagavad-glta 15.7): *mamaivamso jiva-loke jlva-bhutah sanatanah*. “The living entities in this conditioned world are My eternal fragmental parts.”

As sparks from the fire are also fire because they have same quality (heat and light), the living entities are also Brahman because they have the same quality (*sac-cid-ananda*). Although both a great fire and its sparks are both fire, and thus share the qualities of heat and light, the distress caused by cold can only be alleviated by the fire, and not by the sparks. Similarly, although the Supreme Brahman and the individual living entities are one in quality; the Supreme Brahman is the complete whole whereas the minute individual living entities are parts and parcels of the complete whole, and not the complete whole themselves.

## Sri Visnusvami’s Siddhanta II

Regarding the Material World

Acarya Sri Visnusvami preached that the material world is an effect of the original cause of all causes, the Absolute Truth. The material world, which is manifested from the energy of the Supreme Lord, is factual and not an illusion. For example, gold is factual and so are all the ornaments, such as earrings, that are made of gold. Because the Supreme Brahman, who is the cause of all causes, is eternal and factual, the material world, which is an effect of the Supreme Lord’s energy, is also eternal and factual.

Someone may object: How can you say that the material world is factual and eternal? Since the material world has a beginning and an end, instead of saying it factual and eternal, we should consider it to be false.

Our reply is that if an object is not visible, it cannot be concluded that it does not exist. For example, sometimes we cannot see a vulture flying high in the sky, and what to speak of the Gandharvas. Sometimes we cannot see objects that are too close to our eyes, like the black ointment that anoints them; or objects too small, such as atoms. We cannot see the queen, who lives behind palace walls. We cannot see the stars during the day. We cannot see a drop of rain after it has fallen on the water of a lake. We cannot see the presence of ghee in yogurt, and we cannot see the salt in salty water.

Although these objects are not directly visible to our naked eyes, no one has a tinge of doubt that they exist. If someone attempts to prove the non-existence of an object simply because it is not visible, then let that person prove himself to be nonexistent while standing before a blind person) Just as an effect, such as a pot made of clay, is present in the cause (clay), so the form of this material world was present in the Supreme Brahman, the cause of all causes, before its creation.

When the Supreme Brahman manifests His energy, then the material world becomes clearly visible. The *Chandogya Upanisad (7.26.1)* describes two energies of the Supreme Lord by which He manifests and then annihilates everything. When the supremely independent, energetic Supreme Lord employs His energy, material objects become visible.

The Supreme Brahman is the immediate cause of creation, and also the ingredient cause. Someone may object: If you say that the Supreme Lord is the immediate cause; that is understandable. However, if you say that the Supreme Lord transforms Himself to become the ingredient cause and thus manifests the material world, then the fault of transformation will be attributed to Him.

Our reply is that there are two kinds of transformations— total transformation (*vikara*), and a kind of transformation where the effect leaves the cause unchanged. When an object is transformed and does not retain its original form, it is called *vikara*. For example, when milk is transformed into yogurt, it does not retain its original form as milk. As opposed to this, (he Supreme Lord transforms His energy, but personally remains unchanged. This is the case when the Lord’s energy is transformed to manifest the material world.

The Supreme Lord, by His supreme will, creates the material world by the transformation of His inconceivable energy, and yet remains unchanged. Before, during, and after this transformation creating the material world, the Supreme Lord remains as He is; eternal, full of knowledge, and bliss. Although gold is transformed into various ornaments, such as bangles and necklaces, these can be transformed back to their original condition as gold. The states of being bangles and necklaces are simply other states of being gold. This kind of transformation is not called *vikara*, or *bheda*. The Supreme Lord's transformation to become the material world is like this. Therefore, when it is said that the material world is an effect of the Supreme Lord, the cause of all causes, the fault of vikara cannot be attributed to Him.

## Sri Visnusvami's Siddhanta III

Regarding the Supreme Lord

*hladinya samvidaslistah sac cid ananda isvarah*  
*svavidya samvrto jivah samklesa-niharakarah*

**"That personality who is eternal, full of knowledge and bliss, and who is surrounded by His spiritual potencies, such as** ***hladini*** **and** ***samvit*****, is the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Those living entities who are conditioned by nescience and thus experience material miseries are the conditioned souls."**

*sa iso yadvase maya sa jivo yastayadditah*  
*svavir bhutah paranandah svavir bhutah suduhkhabhuh*

**"The living entities' degradation from their original state of pure knowledge of oneness with the Supreme Lord, based on their relationship as master and servant, into the state of identification with material designations, which causes them to be covered by ignorance and subject to material miseries, is the state of conditional life. The Supreme Lord is the master of** ***maya,*** **and the conditioned living entities are controlled by** ***maya*****. The Supreme Lord is self-manifest and full of transcendental bliss. The living entities are also self-manifest, being parts and parcels of the complete whole. Still, because of their contact with matter, they are put into an extremely miserable condition.”**

*isvarasya upadhi vasyata bhavena nitya muktatam sa gunam eva gunair abhibhutam sarvajnyam sarva saktim sarvesvaram sarvaniyantaram sarvopasyam sarva karma phala pradataram samasta kalyana guna nilayam saccidanandam bhagavantam srutayah pratipadayanti*

**“According to the** ***siddhanta*** **of the Visnusvami** ***sampradaya*****, the Supreme Lord is eternally liberated. He never becomes materially designated. He possesses all transcendental qualities in full. He is omniscient, omnipotent, the controller of everything, the master of everyone, supremely worshipable for all, the giver of the fruits of all activities, and the only source of all auspiciousness. He is eternal, full of knowledge and bliss. This is the opinion of Sri Visnusvami, as substantiated by the Vedic literatures.”**

Sri Visnusvami was a worshiper of Lord Nrsimhadeva under the subordination of Rudra. In his commentary *Saruajna-sutra* he proved that the body of the Supreme Lord is *sac-cid-ananda*. There is also clear evidence that he took complete shelter of the chanting of the Lord’s holy name. Sri Visnusvamipada accepts the eternity of worship, the worshiper, and the object of worship. This is confirmed in his commentary *Sarvajna-sutra* wherein he writes, *mukta api lilaya vigraham krtva bhagavantar, bhajante*, that “liberated souls worship the Supreme Lord i their spiritual bodies.”

In the Adi Visnusvami *sampradaya*, the *Nrsimha-tapa* *Upanisad*, the *Pancaratras*, and *Srimad-Bhagavatam* a considered most informative and authoritative. Among the literatures composed by Adi Visnusvami, *Sarvajna-sutra* is most widely accepted. The *acaryas* of Sri Visnusvami *sampradaya* have written commentaries on *Sri Visnu Purana*, *Sri Bhagavad-gita*, and *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, and have also written essays, such as *Nrsimha Paricarya*. The customs of accepting *tridanda sannyasa*, keeping one's *sikha* and sacred thread in the *sannyasa asrama*, and applying *tilaka* on the body, were always practiced in this *sampradaya.*

At present the Visnusvami *sampradaya* is practically extinct. The preaching of Sri Visnusvami's commentary, *Saruajna-sutra*, is very rarely seen. Although Sri Vallabhacarya advertised himself as a member of Sri Visnusvami *sampradaya,* there are differences between his opinions and the *siddhanta* preached by Sri Visnusvami.

## Acarya Sri Nimbaditya

In ancient times there was a city named Vaidurya Pattna in the country of Tailanga-desa. At present this city is known as Mungera Pattana, or Mungi Patana. In that city lived a pious sage named Aruni, who was a devotee of Lord Visnu and whose wife was named Jayanti-devi. It was said that he was a descendent of Aruna Muni, who came to the assembly of King Pariksit, as described in *Srimad-Bhagavatam* 1.19.11.

At the end of Dvapara-yuga, when the sky of *bhagavata- dharma* was covered by the fog of duplicity, so that people in general were distracted from the path of devotional service to the Supreme Lord, which is the constitutional duty' of all living entities, being overcome by petty material desires, the supremely merciful Lord Visnu, in order to protect pure *sanatana-dharma,* decided to send an empowered incarnation to the holy land of Bharata-varsa.

Thus a jewel-like child, as brilliant as the sun, appeared in this world in the evening of the full moon day of the month of Karttika as the son of Srimad Aruni and his wife, Sri Jayanti devi, who were both devoted to Lord Visnu, thus enhancing the happiness of the devotees. Aruni Muni dutifully performed all the prescribed Vedic *samskaras* for his jewel-like son, and then sent him to *gurukula* so that he could study the scriptures under the guidance of a qualified spiritual master. In a very short time the wonderful boy surprised everyone by displaying his genius in mastering all the *Vedas* and branches of the *Vedas*, and exhibiting his propensity for spiritual realization.

This bright sun-like personality' grew up while observing a vow of strict celibacy. Then, for the purpose of propagating eternal Vaisnava *dharma*, he accepted the renounced order of life according to prescribed rules and regulations. After accepting *sannyasa*, he set out for Nanda-grama in Vraja, hoping to have the *darsana* of Lord Krsna. There, at Nanda-grama, he composed a beautiful prayer consisting of twenty-five verses known as *Savisesa-nirvisesa-srikrsna-stava,* and offered it at the lotus feet of his worshipable Lord. He constructed a cottage near Govardhana Hill and exhibited the ideal example of how one should worship Lord Krsna by performing unalloyed devotional service. The place where he performed *bhajana* after constructing a cottage is at present known as Nimba-grama.

It is said that one day a *sannyasi* from a Jain *sampradaya* came to Mathura and invited all the learned scholars there to a debate on the scriptures. This Jain *sannyasi* scholar desired to conquer the entire country by proving the uselessness of Vedic religious principles. However, the Vaisnava *acarya* from Vaidurya Pattna effortlessly refuted all the arguments put forward by the Jain *sannyasi* by quoting scriptural evidence.

The Jain *sannyasi*, after being defeated, took shelter of the Vaisnava acarya. The Vaisnava *acarya* thus accepted him as a disciple and then instructed him in the true meaning of Vedic religious principles.

It is said that after the scriptural debate commenced between the Jain *sannyasi* and the Vaisnava *acarya*, it continued for a long while. Finally, the Vaisnava *acarya*, realizing that the sun was about to set, offered some remnants of Lord Visnu to his guest, who had come to his *asrama*, to relieve him of his fatigue'. Jain *sannyasis* are prohibited to eat anything in the evening or at night and so at first he refused to honor the *prasada*. Because of this, the Vaisnava *acarya* climbed a *neem* tree and held the sun above the horizon until the *sannyasi* could finish his meal. According to one rendition of this story, he climbed a *neem* tree and then invoked the Lord’s Sudarsana *cakra* in the sky. As the cakra, which was as effulgent as the sun, appeared, the *sannyasi* thought it to be the sun.

Because he climbed a *neem* tree and manifested *arka*, or *aditya* (the sun), he became known as Nimbarka, Nimbaditya, and Nimba Vibhavasu. At some places he was known as Aruneya, Niyamananda, and Haripriyacarya. Some people say that Nimbarka appeared in this world at the time when Krsna’s great-grandson, Vajranabha, was the king of Mathura. The disciplic succession of Sri Nimbarka is mentioned in a commentary on the eighth *sutra* of the third section of the first chapter of *Vedanta-darsana*:

*paramacaryaih sri kumararir asmad guruve sriman naradaya*  
*upadisto bhumatveva vijijnasitavya*

**‘The exalted acarya, Sri Sanat-kumara, was a great sage. His disciple was Sri Narada and Narada’s disciple was Sri Nimbarka.”**

Sri Nimbaditya’s commentary on Vedanta-sutra is known as *Vedanta-parijata-saurabha.* Sri Nimbarka’s disciple, Srinivasacarya, elaborated on that commentary by presenting another commentary called *Vedanta-kaustubha*. Kesava Kasmiri, who was a contemporary of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, accepted the Nimbarka *sampradaya* and composed an explanation of *Vedanta-kaustubha* called Kaustubha-prabha.

Apart from *Vedanta-parijata-saurabha*, the following literatures and commentaries were composed by Sri Nimbaditya, according to the members of his *sampradaya:* *Gita-bhasya, Sadacara Prakasa, Dasasloki, Savisesa-nirvisesa-srikrsnastotra, and Pratah-smarana-stotra (Vedanta-garbhita-stotram).* Among these six literatures, Vedanta-parijata-saurabha, Dasasloki, Savisesa- niruisesa-srikrsna stotra, and Pratah-smarana-stotra are said to be compiled by Nimbaditya by the present-day members of the Nimbarka *sampradaya*.

The four Kumaras, sons of Brahma, headed by Sanaka, imparted eternal religious principles to Narada Muni, who then instructed Srila Vyasadeva. From Srila Vyasadeva, through the disciplic succession extending through Sri Prahlada and others, Sri Nimbarka received instruction. To propagate *bhagavata-dharma*, as originally taught by Lord Narayana, Sri Nimbarka Svami established an authorized *sampradaya* in this age of Kali. This *sampradaya* is therefore known as the Nimbarka sampradaya.

## The Code Of Conduct Observed In The Nimbarka Sampradaya

Initiated followers of the Nimbarka *sampradaya* wear *tulasi* neck beads and decorate their forehead with Vaisnava *tilaka*. The *sannyasis* keep their *sikha* and sacred thread and wear saffron robes. They are supposed to undergo five purificatory rituals at the time initiation. The members of all four *asramas* are asked to renounce violence toward, other living entities with their body, mind, and speech. They should eat only food that has been offered to Lord Visnu. Worship of the demigods and honoring their remnants are prohibited Vows, such as Janmastami, Ekadasi, and Vyasa-puja are to be observed by following the prescribed rules and regulations. If an Ekadasi or Janmastami is polluted by the presence of another day (*tithi*), they should not be observed. Householder Vaisnavas should perform the *sraddha* ceremony according to Vaisnava system by offering the remnants of Lord Visnu to the departed soul. It is very detrimental to offer oblations to a departed soul in a *smartta* fashion, thinking that he has become a ghost.

## The Siddhanta Preached By Sri Nimbaditya

Acarya Sri Nimbaditya preached the *siddhanta* known as *cintya-dvaitadvaita.* Sri Nimbaditya accepted the *Vedas* as the crest-jewel of all evidences. He also accepted the supplementary Vedic literatures as evidence. The instructions that the four Kumaras, the sons of Brahma, imparted to Sri Narada Muni in the seventh *prapathaka* of *Chandogya* *Upanisad* were preached in this world by Acarya Sri Nimbaditya after he received them from Sri Narada in disciplic succession. The ekayana system of philosophy that was instructed by Srila Sanant-kumara to Sri Narada Gosvami in the seventh *prapathaka* of *Chandogya* *Upanisad* is mentioned in Acarya Sri Nimbaditya’s instructions (*Vedanta-parijata-saurabha* 7.1.2)

Other conclusions of Acarya Sri Nimbaditya are:

1\. The *Puranas* are the fifth *Veda* (7.1,4).

2\. Lord Visnu is the doer of everything (7.15.1).

3\. Advancement in devotional service depends on faith and steadiness (7.19-20.1).

4\. Nothing is equal or superior to love of God (7.23.1).

5\. The glories of the Lord’s eternal abode (7.24.1).

6\. The Supreme Lord is fully independent (7.24.2).

7\. Liberated souls are eternal associates of the Supreme Lord and they engage in eternal pastimes with the Supreme Lord in His transcendental abode (7.25.2).

8\. The Supreme Lord possesses the power to appear and disappear at will (7.26.1).

9\. Vaisnavas are eternal and transcendental (7.26.2).

10\. The glories of the Lord’s remnants (7.26.2).

In his book *Dasa-sloki*, Sri Nimbarka presented the following conclusion.

*sarvam hi vijnanam atho yatharthakam*  
*sruti smrtibhyo nikhilasya vastunah*  
*brahmatma katva diti vedavin matam*  
*trirupa tapi sruti sutra sadhita*

**“All objects are related to the Supreme Lord. The conclusion of those who are not actually well-versed in the** ***Vedas*** **is that temporary material objects cannot emanate from the eternal Absolute Truth. To know things in truth is real knowledge. This is confirmed in all the** ***srutis*** **and** ***smrtis*****. Sometimes the philosophy of oneness is stressed in the** ***Vedas*****, sometimes the philosophy of duality is stressed, and sometimes both are mentioned. Therefore the philosophy of** ***kevaladvaita-vada*** **has no standing. After carefully considering all the statements of the** ***Vedas*****, the philosophy of simultaneous oneness and difference will certainly be established within one’s mind. Thus the philosophy of** ***dvaitadvaita*** **should be accepted as the actual conclusion of the** ***sastra*****.**

## Sri Nimbaditya’s Siddhanta Regarding The Individual Living Entities

In Sri Nimbarka’s commentary on *Brahma-sutra* (2.3.42) the simultaneous oneness and difference between the living entities and the Supersoul has been established. In his commentary he has written:

*amsamsi bhavajjiva-paramatmanor bhedabhedau darsayati paramatmano jivo amsa jnajnau dvavajabi sanisabi - tyadi bheda vyapa desat tattvamasi tyadya bheda vyapa desacca api ca artha vanikah brahmadasa brahmadasa brahmakitava - iti brahmano hi kitavaditvam adhiyate*

Here the commentator explains that the individual spirit souls and the Supersoul are simultaneously one and different from each other. The Supreme Lord is the complete whole and the living entity is His part and parcel. The Supreme Lord is the knower of everything and the living entity in conditional life is ignorant. Both are unborn and eternal. All the Vedic statements bring one to the conclusion that there is eternally a difference between the Supreme Lord and the minute living entities. Of course, there are some Vedic statements, such as *tat tvam asi*, that declare that there is no difference between the Supreme Lord and the living entities. These should not be taken out of context, however. By careful consideration of all Vedic statements, it is seen that there is oneness and difference between the Supreme Lord and the living entities. The minute individual living entities are related to the complete whole as fragmental parts.

In the book *Dasa-sloki*, the following has been concluded. The living entities are eternal and fully cognizant. However, the living entities possess minute consciousness, whereas the Supreme Lord is supremely conscious. The living entities are always under the control of the supreme controller, and can never act independently. The minute living entities are unlimited in number. An individual soul is present in each and every material body. Because of being infinitesimal, the individual living entities are prone to either accept or reject material embodiment. When the living entities are conditioned by *maya* they are covered by gross and subtle bodies, but when they are liberated they become freed from these coverings.

The living entities are grouped into three classes—those who are eternally liberated, those who have become liberated, and those who are conditioned by *maya*. Those who are under the shelter of Lord Hari’s lotus feet are eternally liberated. Those who were previously conditioned by *maya*, but, by the mercy of the devotees and the spiritual master, have attained the favor of the Supreme Lord, have been liberated from their conditional state. And those who in the clutches of *maya* due to being averse to the service of the Supreme Lord are called conditioned souls. The eternally liberated souls are further divided into two categories—those who are direct associates of the Supreme Lord and those who are followers of the associates of the Supreme Lord.

All three classes of living entities are further classified according to their status. Those who have become liberated are also of two kinds—the associates of the Lord and the practitioners of devotional service. The conditioned souls are of many varieties, such as gross materialists without discrimination, those who can discriminate between what is to be done and what is not to be done, and those desiring liberation.

The illusory energy, *maya*, is eternal. The conditioned souls have been entrusted to *maya* due to their aversion to the service of the Supreme Lord. Therefore, only by the mercy of the Supreme Lord can the conditioned souls become liberated from the clutches of nescience.

## Sri Nimbaditya's Siddhanta Regarding The Material World

Inert matter is divided into two parts—the time factor and *maya*. The time factor is also in two divisions—transcendental time and material time. The transcendental time factor is beyond the jurisdiction of material nature and is fully spiritual, functioning by the will of the Supreme Lord, Time is eternal, but when the time factor functions in the material world, it is experienced in terms of past, present, and future.

The perverted reflection of the spiritual time factor is the material time factory Material nature, which is compared to a shadow, is a perverted reflection of the spiritual nature. Material nature is referred to by many names, such as *pradhana, avidya, nimitta*, and *upadana*. From the Vedic statement *ajarh ekarh lohita krsna suklam* it is understood that material nature consists of three modes. Although matter appears like spirit, in the sense that a shadow appears like the original object, it must be considered inferior. The entire material creation, including the gross and subtle bodies of the conditioned souls, is a product of inert matter.

## Sri Nimbaditya's Siddhanta Regarding The Supreme Lord

The Supreme Absolute Truth is spotless. The eighteen faults, including illusion, laziness, and the tendency to commit mistakes, do not exist in the Supreme Personality of Godhead. The Supreme Lord if full of unlimited auspiciousness. The Absolute Truth, or the Supreme Brahman, is Lord Krsna. He is the source of all beauty and sweetness. The quadruple forms in Goloka, the quadruple forms in Vaikuntha, and the other quadruple forms are His plenary portions.

Lord Krsna is constantly served by His internal potency, the daughter of King Vrsabhanu, Sri Radha, along with thousands of other *gopis*, who are expansions of Sri Radha’s transcendental body. Therefore it is concluded that Lord Krsna is the eternal object of worship for all living entities. He possesses an eternal transcendental body. Because He is devoid of material hands and legs, He is accepted as formless by materialistic people. Because He possesses a transcendental form, He appears as the Supreme Personality of Godhead before the eyes of devoted transcendentalists. He is supremely independent, omnipotent, the controller of all controllers, the possessor of inconceivable energies, and always worshiped by great demigods, beginning with Brahma and Siva.

## The Process Of Worship In The Nimbarka Sampradaya

It is the duty of all living entities to worship Lord Krsna, the supreme controller of all controllers, who is thus worshiped by the most exalted personalities, headed by Brahma and Siva. Those who worship the demigods rather than Lord Visnu are condemned, and are considered travelers on the path to hell. Worship, or devotion, is of two types—*apara-bhakti* manifested as *sadhana*, and *uttama-bhakti* manifested as *prema*. *Uttama-bhakti* performed in love of God is awakened by cultivating the nine types of *sadhana-bhakti*, beginning with hearing and chanting.

From Sri Nimbaditya, the Nimayet *sampradaya* became manifested. Some people mistakenly consider the Nimanandi *sampradaya* to be the Nimayet *sampradaya.* However, this is not a fact. Another name of Sriman Mahaprabhu was Nimai. Because the name Nimai was very dear to Lord Nityananda Prabhu, Sri Vakresvara Pandita’s disciple, Sri Gopala Guru GosvamI, referred to Lord Caitanya as Nimananda in his writings. Here is a verse written by him.

*tatah srikrsna caitanyah, prema kalpadruma bhuvi*  
*nimanandakhya yo sau vikhyatah ksitimandale*

**“Lord Sri Krsna Caitanya is the desire tree of love of God. He is celebrated in this world by the name Nimananda.”**

Those who abandon the disciplic succession from Sri Madhvacarya to Isvara Puri and create a new *sampradaya* identify themselves as belonging to the Nimananda *sampradaya*. However, the Nimananda *sampradaya* is distinct from the Nimayet *sampradaya*.

Some people say that the *sampradaya* introduced by Aruni Sri Nimbaditya after he received instruction from Sri Sanat-kumara’s disciple, Sri Narada Muni, long ago became extinct. That is why, even though the *siddhantas* preached by Sri Visnusvami, Sri Ramanuja, and Sri Madhva are mentioned in the *sarvadarsana sangraha* literature written by Sayana Madhava, there is no mention of Sri Nimbarka or his *siddhanta*. Therefore it is argued that the present Nimbarka *sampradaya* has resurfaced after the disappearance of Sriman Mahaprabhu, just like the Vallabhacarya sampradaya. It is also seen that there are many similarities between the *siddhantas* preached by Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu and those of the Nimbarka *sampradaya*.

Srila Jiva Gosvami Prabhu, who was a foremost *acarya* of the Gaudiya Vaisnava *sampradaya*, mentioned the names and *siddhantas* of Sri Visnusvami, Sri Ramanuja, and Sri Madhva in his sandarbhas and other works, but did not mention the name of Nimbarka. What could be the reason for this?

Many people presume that, after the compilation of *sarva darsana sangraha*, or even during the period when the Gaudiya Vaisnava Gosvamis lived in Vrndavana, the philosophy of the present-day Nimbarka *sampradaya* had not been spread very widely. Still, there is no doubt that Sri Nimbaditya was the perpetuator of the ancient authorized *dvaitadvaita sampradaya*. Although the philosophy of the present Nimbarka *sampradaya* was nourished by the *siddhanta* of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, the *acarya* of all *acaryas*, the *siddhanta* preached by Sri Nimbaditya can be understood from the instructions of Sri Sanat-kumara that are found in the seventh *prapathaka* of *Chandogya Upanisad*.

## Acarya Sri Ramanuja

A Dravida *brahmana* named Kesavacarya, who was a descendent of Harita, who had compiled *dharma-sastra*, lived around the year one thousand Saka era in a village named Sri Paramavattura, or Sri Mahabhuta Puri, which was about twenty- six miles west of Madras. Kesava and his wife, Kantimati, were very pious and possessors of all good qualities.

One day, with a desire to have a son, they bathed where the River Kairavini enters the sea and disclosed their heart’s desire to the Supreme Lord. At that time Lord Parthasarathi assured them that He would fulfill their desire.

During that period, Bharata-varsa, which is a land where *sanatana-dharma* is meant to be practiced, was flooded with people opposed to devotional service to the Lord. Desiring to deliver the people of Bharata-varsa from the great danger of misusing the valuable human form of life, Lord Hari empowered a suitable living entity with His potency of Sankarsana to stem the tide of anti-Visnu sentiment. By the will of the Supreme Lord, this empowered incarnation appeared in the village of Mahabhuta Puri at midday on a Thursday, during the period of Adra naksatra, on the fifth day of the fortnight of the waxing moon in the month of Caitra in the year 938 Saka era, from the womb of Sri Kantimati, who was a devotee of the Supreme Lord, fathered by her pious devotee-husband, Kesavacarya. According to another opinion, the son of Kesava appeared around 1200 A.D.

Kantimati was the sister of Sri Sailapurna, a disciple of Sri Yamunacarya who resided at Sri Rangam. When he heard that a jewel-like son had appeared in the house of Kantimati, Sri Yamunacarya came to see the child. Upon seeing how the child’s symptoms were similar to those of Sri Rama’s brother, Laksmana, he named the boy Laksmana.

From the very beginning of his childhood Laksmana exhibited an unprecedented genius, as well as unflinching attachment for devotional service to the Supreme Lord and the association of devotees and their service.

There was an exalted devotee named Kancipurna, who was an unalloyed servant of Lord Varadaraja at Kancipuram. Just to demonstrate that a Vaisnava should not be discriminated against because of having appeared in a low caste, Kancipurna appeared in a low-class *sudra* family. This exalted soul would travel from his birthplace, Punameeli, to Kanclpuram every day to worship Lord Varadaraja. While traveling to and from Kanclpuram, he had to pass by the house of Kesava. The young boy, Laksmana, understood Kancipurna to be a great Vaisnava the first time he saw him, and he became very eager to serve him.

One day, without taking permission from his parents, young Laksmana invited Kancipurna to his house for lunch. He personally fed him delicious food and then prepared to massage his feet. When Kancipurna saw this behavior of Laksmana, who was the son of a pious *brahmana,* he spoke to him with the utmost humility that is befitting a Vaisnava, informing him that he was from a low-class *sudra* family and thus should not be served by a *brahmana’s* son. Upon hearing these words, Laksmana felt extremely sad and replied, “My dear sir, a Vaisnava is never a *sudra*. Actually, a Vaisnava is the spiritual master of even a brahmana. Just remember how Tiruppana Aloyara was worshipable by *brahmanas*, despite his being bom in a *sudra* family.”

Thus, even in his childhood, Laksmana preached the truth that a Vaisnava is transcendentally situated, above the designations of *varna* and *asrama*. While in the prime of youth, this pride of a Dravida family was married at the behest of his parents. After thus establishing his son, Laksmana, in the *grhastha asrama*, Kesavacarya left this world.

After the death of his father, Laksmana lived with his mother for some time, along with his wife. At that time Laksmana became very eager to study the scriptures. He had heard of a teacher named Sri Yadavacarya of Kancipuram, who was reputed to be very well-versed in *Vedanta*, and so he approached him and began studying under his care. Kanclpuram is one of the seven Puris that award liberation, and was near Bhutapuri. At present Kanci is named Kanjivaram, and is twenty-four miles west of Madras. During the reign of the Cola kings, Kancipuram was a center for learning and Sarasvati worship, and considered to be the crest-jewel of South India.

While Sri Laksmana was studying the Vedic literatures with Yadavacarya at Kanci, the teacher, who was expert in the art of chanting *mantras*, was one day summoned to the palace of the king of Kanci in order to exorcise a ghost from the body of his daughter. As Yadavacarya fuciely attempted to drive away the *brahmana* ghost from the princess by the power of his mantras, the evil spirit began to condemn and frighten him. At that time the ghost informed Yadava that in his precious life he had been an iguana that unknowingly ate the remnants of a Vaisnava’s food and thus had next taken birth as a *brahmana*. He informed Yadava that if he were to receive some water that had washed his student’s, Laksmana’s, feet, he would leave the princess at once.

In this way Sri Laksmana Desika showed mercy to a *brahmana* ghost who possessed the body of the princess. Yadavacarya was very disturbed by the incident, however, and from that time onward he displayed envy and hatred toward his pupil, Laksmana Desika.

One day thereafter, early in the morning, as Sri Laksmana was massaging his master’s body with oil, another student of Yadavacarya came and inquired from his teacher about the meaning of the world *kapyasam*, from the Vedic *mantra, tasya yatha kapyasam* *pundarikam evam aksini*. In reply, Yadavacarya told this student that according to the explanation of the previous *acarya*, Sripada Sankara, the word *kapyasam* means “the anus of a monkey.” When the word was interpreted in this way, the meaning of that *sruti mantra* became: “The eyes of that effulgent personality are reddish, like the anus of a monkey.”

When Sri Laksmana Desika heard this explanation from his teacher, he felt very aggrieved so that tears began to flow from his eyes. Sri Laksmana Desika was engaged in serving his master at that time, and without his knowledge, a few warm teardrops fell on his body. When Yadavacarya saw that Laksmana’s eyes were filled with tears for no apparent reason, he inquired about this. Laksmana then explained that he had been extremely pained by his explanation of the word *kapyasam* that is found in the *sruti mantra*. He said, “It is certainly a great offence to compare Lord Visnu’s lotus-like reddish eyes with the anus of a monkey.”

When Yadavacarya heard these words of Laksmana, he became blinded by rage and chastised him, saying, ‘You are a fool because you find fault with the explanation of Sankaracarya. How can you dare do this? You are very arrogant. If you have the power, then give a better explanation of this *mantra*. ”

Thereafter Sri Laksmana explained how, instead of meaning “the anus of a monkey,” the word *kapyasam* meant “blossomed by the sun.” Thus, the meaning of the *mantra* became: “The eyes of Lord Visnu, who is situated within the sun globe, are like lotus flowers blossomed by the sunshine.”

When Yadava heard this explanation of Laksmana, he said, “This is not the primary meaning, but rather the secondary meaning.” Still, within himself he understood that the boy was not an ordinary human being, and that in future he would become a great opponent of the philosophy established by Sankaracarya.

Another day, while Yadavacarya was explaining a mantra from *Taittiriya* *Upanisad:* *satyam jnanam anantam brahma (Anandavalli 2)*, Laksmana pointed out some discrepancies, because his teacher’s explanation was based on the philosophy of impersonalism. By giving the correct meanings, Laksmana established that the Supreme Brahman is not impersonal, but is the Supreme Personality of Godhead. After being insulted by his disciple again and again in this way, Yadavacarya hatched a conspiracy to kill Laksmana, considering him to be an enemy of his *sampradaya.*

One day thereafter, Yadava proposed that they go to Prayaga, to bathe at the confluence of the three rivers there, and he practically forced Laksmana to agree. Thus Laksmana began the long journey with Yadava from Kanclpuram to Prayaga, so that they could bathe in the Ganges. Along the way, near the Vindhya Mountain, Laksmana’s cousin-brother and a disciple of Yadava named Govinda secretly informed Laksmana of his teacher’s evil plot to drown him, and advised him to run away if he hoped to save his life.

Laksmana left the main road and began to run as fast as possible along a smaller path. After some time he became exhausted and sat down under a tree to rest. At that time it suddenly started raining heavily, and so Yadava and his associates became drenched. When Yadavacarya saw that Govinda was alone, he inquired from him about Laksmana. Thereafter, although by the order of Yadava his disciples searched for Laksmana for a long time, they all returned disappointed. Finally Yadava concluded that Laksmana must have died in a mishap, and thus he felt relieved, as if his mission had been accomplished.

Meanwhile as Sri Laksmana Desika was lying under a tree, thinking about the Supreme Lord, he suddenly saw a hunter couple coming toward him. Laksmana understood that the couple was also traveling and so he accompanied them. Gradually the whole day passed and evening arrived. After taking shelter under a tree, the hunter’s wife requested her husband to fetch some drinking water. When Sri Laksmana said that he would bring some water, the hunter would not allow him to go anywhere in the dark.

Early the next morning the hunter asked Laksmana to bring some water and so he went down a well by the stairs inside it and thus fetched water three times for the hunter’s wife, carrying it in the palms of his hands. On the fourth trip, as Laksmana came out of the well, he could not see the hunter couple. And, rather than the dense and dark forest, he saw a beautiful locality with roads. When he inquired about this from passersby, he realized that he had arrived in his hometown, Kanclpuram.

When Laksmana narrated the story to Kancipurna, that exalted devotee informed him that Laksmi-Narayana had appeared as the hunter couple to save his life and accept his service. From that day onwards, Kancipurna ordered Laksmana to bring water from that well and use it for the service of Lord Varadaraja.

After some time, when Yadavacarya returned to Kanclpuram with his disciples, he was very surprised to see Laksmana. He pretended to be happy to see him, and requested him to resume his studies under his guidance. Laksmana agreed to this request of Yadava, intending to show him mercy in the future.

Gradually news of Laksmana spread everywhere so that the exalted devotee Sri Yamunacarya received it at Sri Rahgam. He could understand that Sri Laksmana Desika was a great Vaisnava that would single-handedly spread Vaisnava *dharma* in the future. After some time, Yamunacarya went to Kancipuram to see Lord Varadaraja, and there he met Laksmana in the company of Yadavacarya. Simply by seeing Laksmana from a distance, Yamunacarya felt transcendental pleasure and within his mind, He began to think of the means whereby he could instruct him.

At first Yamunacarya did not speak to Laksmana, or disclose his intention. He returned home and then sent his disciple Purnacarya to Kanclpuram with the order that he should recite the *Stotra-ratna* that he had composed in front of Lord Varadaraja. When Sri Laksmana heard these wonderful prayers composed by Yamunacarya from the mouth of Purnacarya in the temple of Lord Varadaraja, he became very eager to meet Yamuna Muni. Purnacarya obliged Laksmana and took him to Sri Rangam, but on the way they received the shocking news of Yamunacarya’s disappearance. So that the transcendental body of Sri Yamunacarya would not be touched by *smartas*, Purnacarya, despite suffering greatly in separation from his spiritual master, somehow managed to pacify himself, as well as Laksmana, and then quickly came to the place where the body of Yamunacarya was lying.

Sri Laksmana Desika saw that three fingers of Yamunacarya’s hand were closed and from this he understood that he had three wishes yet to be fulfilled for the welfare of the world. After some research, Laksmana became confident that he understood these three intentions and he boldly and publicly made this vow: “I will follow the path of Sri Vaisnavism. I will uplift those who are bewildered by ignorance by teaching them five *samskaras*, and I will engage them in the devotional service of the Supreme Personality of Godhead as received through disciplic succession.”

As soon as Laksmana said this, one of Sri Yamunacarya’s fingers opened. Then Laksmana Desika made a second vow: “I will compose a commentary on *Vedanta-sutra* known as *Sri-bhasya* after collecting all the necessary evidence, for the benefit of the people of the world.”

As soon as he said this, a second finger of Sri Yamunacarya unfolded. Laksmana then made a third vow: “I will compile a dictionary based on the foremost *Purana* that describes the nature and characteristics of the Supreme Personality of Godhead and the living entities, which was written by the great sage Parasara.”

The moment he finished speaking, Yamunacarya’s third finger unfolded. When the assembled people saw this extraordinary act of Laksmana they were struck with wonder, understanding that in the future this person would become the protector of the Vaisnava *sampradaya*.

Thereafter Laksmana returned to Kanclpuram, where he gradually developed great attachment for Yamunacarya’s disciple, Kancipurna. From his childhood, everyone saw that Laksmana had great respect for Vaisnavas, accepting them as transcendental and eternally perfect. Although Kancipurna had appeared in a *sudra* family, Laksmana wanted to take initiation from him and no one else. Hoping to attract his mercy, Laksmana tactfully invited Kancipurna to his house for lunch with the intention of obtaining some of the remnants of his food.

The great Vaisnava Kancipurna somehow thwarted this attempt by deceiving Laksmana’s wife, who followed *smarta* principles. Laksmana then inquired from Kancipurna about the best means for finding a bona fide spiritual master. Kancipurna told Laksmana that he would ask Lord Varadaraja about this and then let him know the Lord’s reply. Thereafter, Kancipurna received an order from Lord Varadaraja in a dream and then informed Laksmana that Sri Mahapurna alone was qualified to become his spiritual master.

After hearing this, Laksmana Desika set out for Sri Rangam a second time. On the way, by good fortune, he met Mahapurna in the village of Agrahara, near Mathura, and duly received the five *samskaras* from him. After initiating Laksmana, Purnacarya began to reside at Kancipuram, along with his family.

As previously mentioned, Laksmana’s wife was an attached fruitive *smarta*. One day, as she was collecting water from a well, a drop of water from the rope of Purnacarya’s wife happened to fall into her pitcher. Seeing this, Laksmana’s wife spoke very harsh words to the wife of her husband’s spiritual master, saying that she belonged to a noble family whereas the “offender” belonged to a low-class family.

When Mahapurna came to know about this incident, he immediately left for Sri Rangam without telling Laksmana, just to insure that such an unpleasant incident would never reoccur. Laksmana found out about his spiritual master’s sudden departure from Kanclpuram and firmly decided to give up the bad association of his wife, for she was envious of his spiritual master and other Vaisnavas.

A few days after this incident, a hungry *brahmana* came to Laksmana’s house and begged for some food, but his request was refused by Laksmana’s wife. The *brahmana* then approached Laksmana and complained about the ill treatment he had received from his wife. Taking advantage of this opportunity, Laksmana Desika gave the poor *brahmana* a letter, some turmeric, and a new cloth and said, “Go to my house and tell my wife that you have come from my father-in-law’s house to take her for the occasion of her brother’s marriage.”

Accepting this order of Laksmana, the *brahmana* again went to Laksmana’s wife and gave her the letter and other items. This time, Laksmana’s wife treated the *brahmana* very respectfully and fed him sumptuously. Meanwhile, Laksmana returned home at a suitable moment. When his wife told him of the *brahmana’s* proposal, he immediately gave his consent so that he could give up her bad association forever. He tactfully said to her, “Because you are going to your father’s house to attend your brother’s marriage, you should take all your clothes and ornaments with you.”

Not understanding the intention of her husband, Laksmana’s wife happily left with that *brahmana* for her father’s house, taking with her all her belongings. With his wife out of the picture, Laksmana felt happy and relieved, thinking that he was now free from the bad association of a person who is envious of the spiritual master and Vaisnavas. He went before Lord Varadaraja and said to Him after offering his respectful obeisances, “My dear Lord, from today onwards I surrender myself untoYou. I now belong toYou alone, and so please accept me.”

Thereafter Laksmana collected all the required paraphernalia for accepting *sannyasa*. Then, by the will of Lord Varadaraja, he accepted the order of *tridanda sannyasa* while remembering Sri Yamunacarya on the bank of Ananta- sarovara.

After Srila Ramanuja accepted the renounced order of life he began to receive disciples, one by one. His first disciple was his nephew, who was named Dasarathi. After him, a man named Kuranatha, or Kuresa, took shelter of his lotus feet. In the Ramanuja *sampradaya*, Sri Kuranatha is accepted as a partial expansion of Rama, and Dasarathi is accepted as a partial expansion of Bharata.

When the mother of Ramanuja’s previous teacher, Yadavacarya, named Yatindra, saw Sri Ramanuja’s power and opulence, she advised her son to take shelter of his lotus feet as atonement for his having blasphemed Lord Visnu and the Vaisnavas. Yadava had already realized to some extent the incorrectness of his philosophy, although he continued to propagate it just to maintain his reputation.

Finally, by the strong desire of his devoted mother, Yadava approached Sri Ramanuja and after a great deal of discussion, his heart became cleansed. By the causeless mercy of Sri Ramanuja, Yadava was delivered from the pit of Mayavada philosophy and duly purified by the performance of the five types of *samskaras* and then awarded *tridanda sannyasa*. After accepting the renounced order of life, Yadava was known as Sri Govinda dasa. By the order of Sri Ramanuja, he composed a literature describing the glories of Vaisnavas as a means of atonement for his previous offenses. Shortly thereafter, Sri Govinda dasa returned to the abode of Lord Visnu.

Ramanuja’s cousin, Govinda, worshiped Lord Siva under the shelter of Mayavada philosophy rather than worshiping Lord Visnu, which is the eternal constitutional duty of all living entities. Sri Ramanuja delivered Govinda from the ditch of Mayavada philosophy with the help of Yamunacarya’s disciple, Sri Sailapurna, and initiated him into Vaisnava *dharma*.

At this time Sri Ramanuja began collecting scriptures according to the instruction of Sri Yamunacarya and the previous *acaryas*, with the help of Sri Mahapurna, including *Nyasa-tattva, Purusa-nirnaya, Gitartha-sangraha, Vyasa-sutra*, and the *Pancaratras*. By the desire of Sri Mahapurna, Sri Ramanuja accepted Pundarikaksa (Sri Mahapurna’s son) as his disciple.

Sri Ramanuja came to know from Sri Mahapurna that a famous Vaisnava named Sri Gosthi Purna resided at Gosthipura village. He was very expert in the art of chanting *mantras,* and he was a knower of the truth. He was a dear disciple of Yamunacarya and so Mahapurna urged Ramanuja to hear about spiritual topics and the mystery of *mantras* from him.

At a suitable time, Sri Ramanuja approached Sri Gosthi Purna and begged for his mercy. In order to test Ramanuja’s determination and genuine desire for knowing the truth, Gosthi Purna refused his request eighteen times. Then, the nineteenth time, he awarded him mantras with their confidential meanings and forbade him to disclose this to anyone.

When Sri Ramanuja, who felt unhappy upon seeing the unhappiness of others, returned to Sri Rangam, he summoned his seventy-four disciples and simultaneously initiated them by loudly chanting the mantras received from Sri Gosthi Purna. Somehow Gosthi Purna came to know about this and so he became very displeased with Ramanuja. Another day, when he saw Ramanuja, Sri Gosthi Purna informed him that his destination would surely be hell.

In reply Sri Ramanujacarya said, “My dear lord, if by going to hell a condemned person like me can benefit thousands of men and women, by your mercy, then why should I simply consider my selfish interest?”

When Gosthi Purna heard this statement, he could understand what a compassionate soul Sri Ramanuja was, and so he ordered his dear son, Saumya Narayana, to take initiation from him.

To establish in this world the understanding that the confidential purport of the scriptures can be understood only by approaching a bona fide spiritual master, inquiring from him submissively, and rendering service unto him, and that the purport of the scriptures is never revealed to one who is puffed-up with the pride of vast learning and noble birth, Sri Ramanuja disclosed the confidential purport of Sri Bhagavad- Gita to his disciple Sri Kuresa, who constantly engaged in his service without duplicity. But when he saw that another disciple, Dasarathi, had become a little proud of his wisdom and high birth, he engaged him in cooking for the Vaisnavas. In this way he removed his disciple’s pride by engaging him in a humble service. Thereafter, Sri Ramanuja also disclosed to Dasarathi the confidential purport of Sri Bhagavad-Gita.

Kanci Purna, Maha Purna, Gosthi Purna, Maladhara, and Vararanga—these five exalted personalities were very intimate disciples of Sri Yamunacarya. Sri Ramanuja accepted all of them as his instructing spiritual masters, although he was like a second manifestation of Sri Yamuna Muni.

Sri Ramanujacarya became well established as the sole acarya of the authorized sampradaya at Sri Rangam. Naturally, some selfish people could not tolerate Sri Ramanuja’s exalted position and reputation as a Vaisnava. Although they repeatedly tried to diminish his prestige as an acarya by various unfair means, they failed every time. Finally, when they ran out of such plans, they decided to kill Ramajuna. They somehow bribed Lord Ranganatha’s pujaris and instructed them to mix poison with the Lord’s remnants of food that the acarya would honor. They incited the pujaris by saying that the acarya frequently indulged in blaspheming them. They claimed that because of Ramanuja’s preaching, common people no longer respected the pujaris as before. They said that everybody respected Ramanuja more than the pujaris, and that all the rich people of Sri Rangam were spending money lavishly for his service.

Thus a conspiracy was hatched, and then the head priest of Sri Rangam revealed the plan of killing the acarya to his wife, ordering her to help implement it. The heart of this simple brahmana lady trembled with fear, for she abhorred the idea of violence toward any living entity. When the time came, she placed the poisoned prasada before Ramanuja. Then, on the pretext of offering obeisances, she made some gesture with her fingernail on the feet of Ramanuja, thereby creating suspicion within his mind. The greatly intelligent king of sannyasis then tested the prasada by giving a portion to a dog. When he saw the effect, he became convinced that the pujaris were trying to kill him.

Another day, with an intention to kill Ramanuja, a priest of Rangadeva personally mixed poison with Sri Rangadeva’s caranamrta and offered it to him. But by the unlimited mercy of Sri Ranganathadeva no harm befell him. During this period, a Mayavadi sannyasi named Yajnamurti challenged Ramanuja to a debate, hoping to hamper his reputation. Acarya Sri Ramanuja, by the order of Lord Ranganatha, easily defeated this learned sannyasi by putting forth arguments explained in the book entitled Siddhi-traya written by Sri Yamunacarya.

After being defeated, when Yajnamurti surrendered himself to Ramanuja, he was advised to atone for removing his sikha and sacred thread. Ramanuja ordered him to again wear a sikha and sacred thread, and purified him by performing the five kinds of samskaras. Ramanuja then awarded tridanda sannyasa to Yajnamurti, who from then on became known as Devarata, or Devanatha. Sri Ramanuja had built a large temple, and he put Devarata in charge of the management.

After some time Sri Ramanuja went to visit Sri Saila, or Tirupati, in the company of his disciples. On the way he stopped at a village called Astasahasra and accepted the hospitality offered by two of his disciples, named Varadacarya and Yajnesa. By doing so, he set the ideal example of how a devotee should honor and serve his spiritual master and the Vaisnavas.

Varadacarya’s wife was a very chaste lady who had sincere devotion for her spiritual master. Indeed, she was even prepared to serve the spiritual master and the Vaisnavas by selling her body. By the mercy of Varadacarya’s wife, a rich and sinful businessman had a change of heart and took shelter of Ramanujacarya’s lotus feet.

Upon arriving at Sri Saila, which is considered to be Vaikuntha on earth, Sri Ramanuja did not climb the hill but rather took darsana of it from its base. The king of that country, Vitthal Rao, also took shelter of Sri Ramanuja’s lotus feet and donated a huge tract of land known as “Ilamandiya” to him as guru daksina. Sri Ramanuja distributed that land among the Vaisnavas and brahmanas.

From Tirupati Sri Ramanuja went to visit Ghatikacala, where he had the darsana of Lord Nrsimhadeva. From there he returned to Kanclpuram, after visiting Paksitirtha and bathing there. Some time later on, Govinda accepted tridanda sannyasa from Sri Ramanujacarya.

Thereafter Ramanuja remembered his previous promise to Yamunacarya and decided to compose the Sri-bhasya commentary on Vedanta-sutra. With the intention of writing the commentary based on the teachings of the previous acarya, Bodhayana, he, along with his disciple, Kuresa, went to Nrsimha Pitha in Kashmir to acquire them. The literature in which those teachings were recorded was not widely known because it was suppressed by the followers of impersonal monism. They kept the book hidden, thinking that if it were preached then their philosophy would be exposed and their reputation would be diminished by the strong evidence and reasoning described therein.

When Sri Ramanuja arrived at Sarada-pitha and asked to see the teachings of Bodhayana, the followers of monism there informed him that the book no longer existed. This news made SrI Ramanuja despondent, and he revealed his mind to Lord Laksmi-Narayana. That very night Lord Nrsimhadeva personally delivered the desired literature into the hands of Ramanuja and ordered him to leave that place as soon as possible.

After some days, the followers of advaita-vada at Sarada- pitha discovered that the literature Bodhayanavrtti was missing, and so they concluded that Ramanuja had stolen it. They sent many strongly-built men in search of Ramanuja. After looking for about one month in many places, they finally found Ramanuja with Kuresa and forcibly took the “stolen” literature and returned home. This made Ramanuja very aggrieved as he thought about how all his hard labor had gone in vain.

At that time Kuresa pacified his spiritual master, saying that he had memorized the entire Bodhayanavrtti by reading it every night during the last month, and that he would write a new manuscript very quickly. Kuresa, who could remember anything after hearing it only once, wrote the entire Vrtti within five or six days, from memory. Ramanuja’s happiness knew no bounds while witnessing this extraordinary talent of Kuresa, and so he engaged him as his scribe while composing his Sri-bhasya.

After composing Sri-bhasya, Sri Ramanuja compiled some other literatures before setting out to conquer the four directions, accompanied by his disciples. In the course of his preaching tour he visited many places. From Kanclpuram he first visited Kumbhakonam, after taking permission from Lord Varadaraja. From there he went to Madura, which was the capital of the Pandya country. From there he went to Kurukapuri, and then Kurunga-nagari, Kerala, Malabara, and its capital, Tiruanathapuram, or Trivandrum.

From South India, Ramanuja went northward, visiting many places, including Dvaravati, Mathura, Vrndavana, Haridvara, Badarikasrama, Puskara, Naimisaranya, Kuruksetra, Ayodhya, Prayaga, Deva Prayaga, Magadha, and Gaya. Sri Ramanuja also went to Sri Nrsimha Pitha in Kashmir for the second time. It is said that, at that time, Lord Nrsimhadeva became so pleased by Sri Ramanujacarya’s explanations that he awarded him the title “Bhasyakara. ” Even during this second visit of Ramanuja, the followers of the philosophy of monism, as well as the smarta panditas of Kashmir, tried to obstruct his preaching in various ways.

Thereafter Ramanuja traveled to Varanasi before heading south. On the way to south he came to Purusottama-ksetra, where he preached the philosophy of the Pancaratras. From Purusottama-ksetra he went to Kurma-ksetra, and then Simhacalam before arriving at the Ahovala Nrsimha temple on the Garuda Mountain. At that temple he introduced the worship of Lord Nrsimhadeva according to the Pancaratra system. He constructed a matha there, thus establishing a center for the propagation of his Visistadvaita philosophy.

From the Ahovala Nrsimha temple he went to Venkatacala, or Tirupati, and settled the on-going quarrel between the followers of Lord Siva and Lord Visnu as to whether the Deity at Tirupati was of Lord Visnu or Lord Siva. He revealed to everyone that this Deity was of Lord Visnu and in this way He engaged everyone in the service of Lord Visnu.

Thereafter Ramanuja returned to Kancipuram. After having the darsana of Viranarayanapura, the birthplace of Natha Muni, he went to Sri Rahgam. During this period, Sri Rangam became glorious as the headquarters from which the preaching of eternal religious principles spread all over India under the victory flag of Sri Ramanujacarya’s Vaisnava siddhantha, known as visistadvaita-vada. The king of sannyasis, Ramanuja, initiated the two sons of Kuresa and a nephew of Govinda, awarding them the names Parasara, Vedavyasa and Parankusa, respectively. He then decorated their bodies with the symbols of Lord Visnu.

In this way the lives of many people became transformed by the mercy of the acarya. There was a powerful wrestler named Dhanurdasa who was born in a sudra family, but by the mercy of Ramanuja, he became very advanced in spiritual life and was thus respected as an exalted brahmana.

During that period, when Sri Mahapurna once treated a disciple of Sri Yamunacarya, named Maranerinambi, who was born in a sudra family, as being as good as a brahmana, the smarta community criticized him. Mahapurna then explained to these smartas that a Vaisnava is never to be considered as a member of any caste or family. It is the statement of the scriptures that those who consider Vaisnavas as belonging to a particular caste possess a hellish mentality. Mahapurna urged the smartas to remember how Lord Ramacandra performed the funeral rites of the bird, Jatayu, and how King Yudhisthira worshiped Vidura, who was bom of a sudra mother.

When Krmikantha, the King of Cola, who was a devotee of Siva and a follower of smarta traditions, saw that the philosophy of Vaisnavism was being preached very strongly everywhere by Sri Ramanujacarya, he sent a few strongly-built men to arrest him. At that time a staunch disciple of Ramanuja named Kuresa, understanding the evil intention of Krmikantha, dressed himself just like his spiritual master without informing him and went to the royal court in the company of the king’s men.

In the royalassembly, Kuresaidentified himself as Ramanuja. There, the sinful followers of Krmikantha forced Kuresa to debate with them and while doing so, they attacked him with abusive language. When Kuresa refuted some misconceptions of the mundane fruitive smarta philosophy, as well as the Saiva philosophy preached under the shelter of Mayavada, then, by the order of Krmikantha, his cruel associates blinded Kuresa. After this incident, Kuresa somehow managed to return to Sri Rangam with the help of a beggar.

After a few days, Krmikantha became afflicted by a dreaded disease and soon thereafter gave up his life. When Sri Ramanuja heard the news of Krmikantha’s death, he became jubilant. Meanwhile, by the mercy of Lord Varadaraja, Kuresa received transcendental vision enabling him to see Lord Hari, his spiritual master, and the Vaisnavas.

Thereafter, a king named Vallala Rao, a follower of the Jain religion who lived in Kadanura, took shelter of Sri Ramanuja’s lotus feet after witnessing his extraordinary influence. The king took initiation and became known as Visnuvardhana. Along with him, many followers of Buddhism also accepted Vaisnava dharma.

Sri Ramanujacarya reinstated the discontinued service of the Deity of Lord Visnu at Yadava Hill. He also arranged for the construction of a temple there and engaged one of his disciples to perform the daily worship according to the Pancaratra system. He visited the place named Cenagami, and after preaching his philosophy vigorously, he constructed a matha there.

Acarya Ramanuja spent the last sixty years of his life at Sri Rahgam, engaging his disciples in the propagation of Vaisnava dharma. During this period, (some of Ramanuja’s disciples requested permission to install a deity of the acarya and he agreed. Thus his deity was installed at Sri Rahgam during his manifested pastimes.

One day thereafter, the acarya summoned all his disciples and expressed his desire to leave the material world. He carefully instructed them about how they should lead their lives after his departure. After entrusting various responsibilities of preaching to his qualified disciples, Acarya Ramanuja returned to Vaikuntha at noon on a Saturday, the tenth day of the fortnight of the waxing moon in the month of Magha in the year 1059 Saka era. His disciples continued to preach whatever they had learned from their spiritual master, pushing on his mission with great enthusiasm.

The names of the most prominent books composed by Sri Ramanujacarya are found in this verse:

*vedantasaro vedantadipo vedartha sangrahah*  
*gadya-gitabhasya-sutrabhasya-nityakrama iti*

**Vedantasara, Vedantadipa, Vedartha Sangraha, Essays, commentary on Bhagavad-gita, commentary on Vedanta Sutra, and Nityakrama.**

Apart from these, there were other excellent literatures that are considered to be compiled by Ramanuja. They are: Vedanta Tattvasara, a commentary on Visnu-sahasra-nama, Visnu-vigraha-samsana-stotra, commentaries on the Isa, Prasna, Mundaka, and Svetasvatara Upanisads, Kuta Sandoha, and Divyasuri-prabhava-dipika.

Many people mistake the Ramanuja sampradaya as being the Ramanandi sampradaya. Although the Ramanandis identify themselves as belonging to the Ramanuja sampradaya, there is a gulf of difference between the two philosophies. Ramananda was the fourteenth acarya in the disciplic succession from Ramanuja.

The Sri sampradaya is divided into two parts—Vadagalai and Tengalai. This division was made at the time of Varavara Muni. Varavara Muni and Tengalai were both acaryas. A description of their philosophies is found in the Tamil literature known as Padanadai Vilakkam. The differences between these two sampradayas are in relation to their conceptions, codes of conduct, style of tilaka, and so on.

The Vaisnavas of the Sri sampradaya are purified by the performance of five samskaras. According to them, a person who has not undergone these five samskaras cannot be considered a Vaisnava. Sri Ramanuja said that even if a person who has not undergone these five samskaras is celebrated as a highly qualified brahmana, one should still not associate with him, knowing him to be untouchable and not fit to converse with, like a dog-eater (candala). And, even if a Vaisnava who has been purified by undergoing the five kinds of samskaras had appeared in a family of out castes, he is capable of purifying the three worlds.

In the Ramanuja sampradaya every initiated Vaisnava is duty-bound to decorate his body with branded symbols of the Lord and marks of tilaka, accept a name of Lord Visnu or a Vaisnava that indicates that he is their servant, chant the Narayana mantra, and worship the salagrama-sila. Wearing unauthorized tilaka and worshiping the demigods rather than Lord Visnu is absolutely prohibited. The followers of Ramanuja say that even if one is about to be devoured, by a tiger he should not take shelter in a demigod’s temple to save his life. The Vaisnavas of the Sri sampradaya completely reject the smarta religious principles.

In the scriptures it is stated that there is no more grave offense than to consider a Vaisnava to be an ordinary human being. Those who consider the spiritual master and the Vaisnavas as belonging to a particular caste will certainly traverse the path to hell. From the manner in which Sri Ramanuja’s spiritual master, Sri Yamunacarya, even though he had appeared in a prestigious brahmana family, showed great respect to Sri Sathakopa dasa, who was bom in a sudra family, one can clearly understand the distinction between the understanding of the Sri Vaisnavas and that of the mundane fruitive smartas.

Sri Yamunacarya wrote in his Stotra-ratna:

*mata-pita yavatayas tanaya vibhutih*  
*sarvam yadeva niyamena madanvayanam*

*srumat tadanghri yugalam pranamami murddha*

**“I offer my respectful obeisances unto the lotus feet of our family spiritual master and acarya, Sri Bakulabhirama. His lotus feet are the only asset of all my descendents and disciples. Their father, mother, wife, children and wealth are the lotus feet of Sri Sathakopa.”**

In the Sri Visistadvaita sampradaya, accepting Vedic tridanda sannyasa is standard practice. Tridandi sannyasis do not give up their sikha and sacred thread, like the ekadanda sannyasis. In this regard Sri Ramanuja quoted this verse from the Puranas'.

**A sannyasi should not give up his sikha, sacred thread, water pot, loin cloth and thread as long as he is alive.**

## The Holy Places of the Sri Sampradaya

The Vaisnavas of the Sri sampradaya have one hundred and eight holy places known as astottara-sata visnu mukhya-sthana. Inquisitive readers who wish to know their names and exact locations should refer to Vaisnava Manjusa-samahrti, Volume two, published by Sri Gaudiya Math.

## The Disciplic Successions of the Visistadvaita Sampradaya

Acarya Sri Ramanuja preached the philosophy known as Visistadvaita. The Visnavas of the Sri sampradaya believe that since time immemorial the Visistadvaita philosophy has been present within the hearts of truly saintly persons. According to them, Srimad Krsna Dvaipayana Vedavyasa propagated the Visistadvaita philosophy in his Brahma-sutra. In due course of time, when a need for an elaborate commentary arose, the great sage Baudhayana presented a commentary on the sutras based on the Visistadvaita philosophy.

When the impersonalists preached the philosophy of hevaladvaita vada to counteract the philosophy of Buddhism, they unlawfully attacked the visistadvaita philosophy preached by Baudhayana. Indeed, they even tried to destroy all copies of his book, Baudhayana-vrtti. To refute these Mayavadis, Yamunacarya composed three literatures, called Atmasiddhi, Samvit-siddhi, and Svaprakasa-siddhi.

Just prior to the extinction of Baudhayana vrtti, two great acaryas named Dramida and Tanka appeared and nourished the philosophy of visistadvaita. Apart from them, a few other followers of the visistadvaita philosophy, such as Guhadeva and Bharuci, preached that philosophy by writing some books on Vedanta. Therefore the conclusion is that the visistadvaita philosophy did not come into existence at the time of Sri Ramanuja. This fact is supported by statements found in Sri Ramanuja’s Sri-bhasya and Sruta Prakasita commentaries.

In the beginning of his Sri-bhasya Sri Ramanuja wrote:

*tagavat baudhayana krtam vistirnam brahma suta vrttim purvacaryah sanciksipuh tan matanusarena sutraksarani vyakhyasyante.*

**“Previous acaryas, such as Dramida, presented elaborate commentaries on Brahma-sutra in full agreement with what was written by the sage Baudhayana, in a condensed form. I will try to explain the codes of Brahma-sutra by following in their footsteps.”**

In this disciplic succession, the first acarya is Parasara’s son, Vyasadeva, and then Baudhayana, Guhadeva, Bharuci, Brahmanandi, Dramidacarya, Parankusanatha, and finally Yamunacarya. The ninth acarya was Sri Ramanuja. All of them preached the philosophy if Visistadvaita. When the teachings of the previous acaryas were almost forgotten, due to the attacks of the Buddhists and the impersonalists, who were like covered Buddhists, SrI Laksmana Desika, who was an empowered incarnation of Lord Sankarsana, vigorously preached that ancient philosophy throughout the world. The philosophy that was accepted by saintly persons since time immemorial, and which was supported by the Pancaratras and Srimad-Bhagavatam, was propagated victoriously by Acarya Sri Ramanuja.

## The Visistadvaita Philosophy

In this philosophy the Supreme Brahman has been accepted as one without a second. Non-dual Brahman is full of transcendental qualities. Matter and spirit are His energies. Matter and spirit are divided into two—gross matter and subtle matter, and gross spirit and subtle spirit. Subtle matter and spirit in the state of cause transforms into gross matter and spirit in the state of effect. Because the non-dual Supreme Brahman alone is the cause and affect, all qualities found in the effect are present in Him. Such qualities are certainly adjectives of the possessor of qualities. Both spirit and matter are qualities of the effect of the Supreme Brahman, who is their cause.

The body is meant to be regulated, for it is the shelter of the spirit soul. Both spirit and matter, which are under the shelter of the non-dual Brahman, are to be enjoyed by Him, are controlled by Him, and as an effect they reveal the identity of the Supreme Brahman, who is the cause. In their constitutional positions there are no distinctions between spirit souls based on material designations, such as human being, demigod, and so on. It is the soul, who, according to his karma, receives a particular type of body for enjoyment and thus identifies himself accordingly. Therefore, the designations “demigod” and “human being” are merely symptoms of the soul’s different karmas.

As caste and other designations are assigned to human beings, different species of life, of which the human being is one, are impositions on the pure spirit souls, meant for selfish purposes and thus manifestations of their perverted nature. That the material body is dependent upon the spirit soul is realized when one sees its uselessness as soon as the soul leaves it.

The material body has come into existence simply so that the conditioned soul can enjoy the fruits of his actions. Even if it is said that a soul is a demigod or a human being, it should be understood that such bodies of a demigod or human being are adjectives of the soul. If the soul were devoid of adjectives, or qualities, it would be difficult to explain the existence of the material body.

The material body is fit to be enjoyed by the spirit soul. But the identity of the spirit soul is not complete in and of itself because the spirit soul is a minute particle of consciousness. This minute consciousness is an indicator of supreme consciousness. Just as the material body is controlled and enjoyed by the individual spirit soul, and thus it indicates the presence of the soul, so the individual spirit soul is an indicator of the supremely conscious Supersoul.

The material body and the spirit soul are both indirectly and directly connected to the Supersoul. However, this does not indicate that the body and soul are identical with the Supersoul in all respects. For example, there is a mantra in the jyotistoma in which it is said, “One should buy soma with a cow that is brown, one year old, and has yellowish eyes.”

In this sentence the words “brown,” “one year old” and “yellowish eyes” are adjectives of the cow by which soma can be purchased. Similarly, both spirit and matter are adjectives, or indicators, of the Supersoul. As there is a relationship between the body and the soul, and that relationship is like a noun and its adjective, or as there is a relationship between the controller and the controlled, or the enjoyer and the enjoyed, so the spirit soul is eternally related to the Supersoul.

In the Visistadvaita conclusion of Sri Ramanuja, the complete oneness, the complete difference, as well as formal oneness and difference are all refuted.

## Sri Ramanuja’s Philosophy in Brief

In the opinion of Acarya Sri Ramanuja, one should revive his relationship with the Supreme Lord by spiritual cultivation. One should worship, offer obeisances to, and meditate upon the lotus feet of the Supreme Personality of Godhead with love and devotion while remaining fixed in the principles of varnasrama-dharma.

To attain the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord is the ultimate goal of life. This is confirmed in Sri Ramanuja’s book Vedartha Sangraha:

*jiva paramatma yathatmya jnana purvaka vamasrama dharmoti kartavyataka parama purusa carana yugala dhyanarccana pranamadi rati artha priyastat prapti phalah*

Cit, acit, and Isvara. These three truths have been accepted in the visistadvaita siddhanta. The word cit refers to the spirit soul, acit refers to matter, or the cosmic manifestation, and Isvara refers to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Narayana, who is the controller of both spirit and matter.

## Sri Ramanuja’s Siddhanta Regarding Spirit

Cit, or the spirit soul, is distinct from material objects, such as the material body, senses, mind, and life air. He is self-illuminated, full of bliss, eternal, infinitesimal, and not perceived by the blunt material eyes that can normally see objects like pots, etc. The spirit soul cannot be cut or pierced, like a material object. It is unchangeable, the shelter of knowledge, controlled by the supreme controller, fully dependent on the Supreme Lord, and meant to be enjoyed by the Lord.

There are three kinds of souls. 1. Those that are conditioned by maya, or materialists 2. Those that have attained liberation from material bondage, and 3. The eternal associates of the Supreme Lord, such as Garuda.

These three types of living entities are all unlimited in number. In the constitutional position of the living entities, there are no distinctions made, as there are in material conditioning, where the living entities are categorized as demigods, human beings, and so on. The spirit soul is full of transcendental bliss. When he gives up all material designations, which are the results of his previous karma, he is situated in his original position, which is beyond the jurisdiction of mundane perception and words.

In the liberated state, all spirit souls have equal qualities and nature. This is confirmed in the Vedartha Sangraha, where it is said:

*jivatmanah svarupam deva manusyadi prakrti parinama vesesa rupa nanavidha bheda rahitam jnananandaika gunam tasyai tasya karmakrta devadi bhede vidhvaste svarupa bhedo vacama gocarah sva samvediah jnana svarupam ityetava deva nirdesyam tacca sarvesam atmanam samanam*

**In the opinion of Sri Ramanuja, the living entities are the minute parts and parcels of the Supreme Lord. The supreme controller is the complete whole and the living entities are His fragmental parts and parcels. In his Sri-bhasya, Sri Ramanuja uses the words anu and sruyate as follows.**

*eso’nuratma cetasa veditavya yasmin pranah pancadha samvivesa (Mundaka Upanisad 3.1.9)*

*uddhrtya manam unmanam anu sadrsam vastudhrtya tanmanatvam jivasya suyate and balagra-sata-bhagasya satadha kalpitasya ca bhago jivah sa vijneyah (Svetasvatara Upanisad 5.9)*

*aragra-matro hy aparo’pi drstah* (Svetasvatara Upanisad 5.8) and *ato nurebayam atma* (2.3.23)

*prakasadivat jivah paramatmanomsah, yatha agnyadityader bhasvatobha rupah prakasomso bhavati, yatha gavasva-sukla krsnadinam gotradi visistanam vastunam gotvadini visesananyamsah, yatha va dehino deva manusyadir dehomsah tadvat.*  
*ekavastveka desatvam hymsatvam visistasekasya vastuno visesanamamsa eva.*  
*tathaca vivecakah viseste vastuni visesanamsoyam, visesyamsoyam iti vyapadisanti.*  
*visesena-visesyayor amsamsitve pi svabhava vailaksanyam drsyate, evam jiva parayor visesana visesyayor amsamsitam, svabhava bhedas copapadyate.*  
*yadidamucyate “naivam parah" iti.*  
*yathabhuto jivah na tathabhutah parah, yathaiva hy prabhayah prabhavan anyathabhutah, tatha prabhasthaniyat svamsajjivat amsi paropythantarabhut ityarthah. evam jiva parayor visesana vissyatva krtam svabhava vailaksanyam asritya bheda nirdesah pravartante.*  
*abheda nirdesastu prthak siddhanyarha visesananam visesya paryantvam asritya mukhyatvena upapadyante*

**“One must realize the presence of the minute spirit soul within the body, in which the life-air has entered after being divided into five aspects, by one’s purified consciousness.”**

In this context the word anu (minute) has been used with regards to the living entities. The word unmana means “to measure.” The living entities are measured in comparison to a minute object.

This is stated in the Vedas as follows.

*balagra-sata-bhagasya*  
*satadha kalpitasya ca*  
*bhago jivah sa vijneyah*

**“When the upper point of a hair is divided into one hundred parts and again each of such parts is further divided into one hundred parts, each such part is the measurement of the dimension of the spirit soul.”**

Although the spirit soul is very powerful, it is understood to be very subtle and infinitesimal, like the point of a needle. There is no doubt that the spirit soul is very tiny. As light is the energy of fire, and thus not separate from it, so the spirit souls are parts and parcels of Supersoul, and non-different from Him. This is how learned transcendentalists define the parts and the complete whole. Although a noun and its adjectives are related to each other as the whole and its parts, there are differences between their natures and characteristics. Even though the spirit souls and the Supersoul are related to each other like a noun and its adjectives, there are differences between them. Their natures and positions are different, as the complete whole is distinct from its parts.

Considering all this, it must be concluded that the Supersoul and the individual spirit souls are not completely identical. As light cannot be separated from its source, the living entities, who are parts and parcels, are inseparably related to the Supersoul, who is their source. In this way, by the example of a noun and its adjectives, the difference between the Supersoul and the individual spirit souls has been ascertained. The conception of oneness between them exists because an adjective, or quality, cannot exist independently of the noun it qualifies.

## Sri Ramanuja’s Siddhanta Regarding Matter

Acit, or matter, is inert, devoid of knowledge, and changeable. Matter is of three kinds - suddhasattva, misrasattva, and sattvasunya. Suddhasattva is devoid of a mixture of raja and tama. It is eternal, full of knowledge and bliss, and devoid of fruitive activities. By the will of the Supreme Lord, it manifests as airplanes, courtyards, palaces, and interiors in Vaikuntha. Suddhasattva is full of effulgence. Some people refer to suddhasattva as “matter,” and some people refer to it as “anti­matter.” The reason it is sometimes referred to as anti-matter is that it is self-manifested and not manifested before the senses of materialistic people.

Misrasattva acit is a combination of the three modes of material nature - goodness, passion, and ignorance. It covers the real knowledge and happiness of those whose consciousness it covers.

Another name of sattvasunya acit is kala, or time. It is a perverted form of material nature and material objects. Kala transforms into material objects, such as wood, but it is eternal and assists the pastimes of the Supreme Lord. Both suddhasattva acit and misrasattva acit are meant to be enjoyed, by the Supreme Lord and the conditioned soul, respectively. They manifest as ingredients (such as eyes), as well as places (such as the fourteen worlds) for their enjoyment.

## Sri Ramanuja’s Siddhanta Regarding the Supreme Lord

The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Narayana, is the original cause of the creation, maintenance, and destruction of the material worlds. He is the director of the cosmic manifestation, free from all abomination, the abode of all auspiciousness, extremely pleasant, the proprietor of everything material and spiritual, as well as all qualities, unique, full of unlimited transcendental qualities, the life and soul of everyone, the Supreme Brahman, supremely effulgent, the Supreme Absolute Truth, the Supersoul, and the ultimate goal of all Vedic literatures. He is the indwelling Supersoul within all living entities. He is the form of unlimited bliss devoid of contamination, who is full of transcendental knowledge and the possessor of unlimited potencies. He is the only shelter for the four kinds of pious people - those who are distressed, those who are inquisitive, those who seek wealth, and those who have genuine knowledge. He awards the four objective of life. Lord Narayana possesses a transcendental body and is the master of the potencies known as Sri, Bhu and Lila. This is confirmed by Sri Ramanuja in his Vedartha Sangraha:

*evamvidha cidacidatmaka prapanca udbhavasthiti pralaya samskara nivarttanaika hetubhutah samasta heya pratyanikataya nanta kalyanaikatanataya ca svetara samsato vastu vilaksana svarupo navadhikatisaya sankhyeya kalyana gunah sarvatma parabrahma parajyotih paratattva paramatma sadadi sabda bhedair nikhila vedanta vedyo bhagavan narayanah purusottama iti antaryami svarupam*

The forms of the Supreme Lord are divided into five categories - para, vyuha, vibhava, antaryami, and arcavatara. The word para refers to the supreme controller. He is the ever-existing, original, and effulgent Personality of Godhead, Vasudeva.

The word vyuha refers to Sankarsana, Pradyumna, and Aniruddha, who are in charge of creation, maintenance, and dissolution, who protect the manifested creation, and who bestow mercy upon Their worshipers.

The word vibhava refers to the incarnations, such as Rama and Krsna.

The word antaryami refers to two manifestations of the Lord. One is the Supersoul situated within the hearts of all living entities, and the second is the supremely attractive Lord Narayana, who is present along with Laksmi within the heart of a fully devoted person who always thinks, “Lord Vasudeva is my life and soul.”

The word arcavatara refers to the Deity form of the Supreme Lord, who manifests His transcendental name and form just to provide an opportunity for His neophyte devotees to serve Him. Although the Deity form appears out of His own sweet will, He looks to materialistic men like a dull stone. Although He is omnipotent, He appears helpless. Although He is fully satisfied in Himself, He appears to be dependent upon others. Although He is the protector of the world, He appears to be in need of protection, and although He is the supreme controller, He enjoys being controlled by His devotees.

By nature the Supreme Personality of Godhead is faultless. Even though the entire material creation consisting of gross and subtle objects is His external body, He is completely aloof from the reactions of karma. For example, it is our practical experience that those who follow the king’s orders are favored by him, whereas those who oppose him are punished. As a result of their activities, some people receive the king’s favor and some receive his wrath, while the king himself is unaffected. (Sri-bhasya 1.2.14)

## The Understanding of parinama-vada According to Sri Ramanuja

Gross and subtle matter and spirit are forms of the Supreme Brahman, *suksma cida acida vastu sarirasayaiva brahmanah sthula cida acida vastu sariratvena karyatvat* (Sri-bhasya 1.1.1). Before the creation, the subtle material energy remains within the Supreme Brahman, like a green bird merged within a forest. It remains there as distinct from Brahman, without any name, form, and so on. At the time of creation, the subtle material energy once again emanates from the body of Brahman. After being divided into names and forms, it transforms into the gross elements. As a spider manifests its web from its body, while remaining unchanged, the Supreme Brahman remains unchanged even after expanding His material and spiritual energies.

The purport is that the Supreme Brahman does not change even while transforming Himself to manifest the material world and the living entities, This is an example of the Supreme Lord’s natural and uninterrupted opulence. In Sri-bhasya (1.4.27) it is stated: natropadisya manasya parinamasya parasmin brahmani dosavahatvam svabhavah parantu nirankusa aisvaryatya vahatvam eveti abhiprayah

## The Process of Worship According to Sri Ramanuja

*evamvidha parabhaktirupa jnana visesasya utpadakah purvokta haraharupadyamana jnana purvaka karmanugrhita bhakti yoga eva, yathoktam bhagavata pardsarena – varnasrama iti. yathoditakrama parinata-bhaktyekalabhya eva bhagavad bodhayana-tanka-dramida-guhadeva-kaparddi bharuci prabhrtya vigita-sista parigrhita-puratana-veda-vedanta vyakhyana suvyaktyartha sruti nikara-nidarsitoyam panthah.*

Here it is said that devotional service is the sublime process for worshiping the Supreme Lord and it is the ultimate goal of life Kntowledge and detachment from matter are automatically attained by engagement in pure devotional service, The Supreme Lord is served by pure living entities through the rendering of devotional service. The devotees attain the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord by engaging themselves in the process of devotional service. One who has actually become perfect in the acquisition of knowledge engages in the devotional service of the Lord, utilizing all activities for His pleasure while constantly remembering his relationship with Him.

Parasara Muni has confirmed this in a verse beginning with the words varnasrama caravata. The path of perfection is nothing more than activities performed for the pleasure of the Supreme Lord. This path is to be traversed step by step, it is obtained only by engagement in devotional service, and is approved by great authorities, inclding Bodhayana, Tanka, Dramida, Guhadeva, Kaparddi, and Bharuci. This path can be ascertained by a thorough study of ancient literatures, such as the Vedas and Vedanta-sutra.

## The Five Types of Worship

1\. Abhigamana (cleansing the Lord’s temple, courtyards, and procession path, and smearing it with cow dung.)

2\. Upadana (collecting the ingredients for worship, like sandalwood and flowers.)

3\. Ijya (offering worship to Lord Visnu)

4\. Svadhyaya chanting mantras while meditating on the meanings, reciting prayers and Vaisnava suktas, chanting the holy name of the Lord, and discussing literatures that ascertains the Absolute Truth.

5\. Bhagavad-anusandhana (Cultivation of devotional service to the Supreme Lord)

## The Ultimate Goal of Life According to Sri Ramanuja

In the opinion of Sri Ramanuja, Lord Narayana, who is the naster of the spiritual sky and the husband of Laksmi, alone is the Supreme Personality of Godhead. He could not appreciate the wonderful excellence of the worship of Sri Sri Radha- Krsna. According to Sri Ramanuja, the perfectional stage of life is attained by pleasing the Supreme Lord by the execution of devotional service. One attains the direct service of Laksmi-Narayana in the mood of unalloyed dasya rasa, by maintaining the conviction that the lotus feet of Laksmi-Narayana are one’s all-in-all, either while living or after death. This is the ultimate goal of life for Sri Ramanuja and his followers. The king of the sannyasis, Sri Ramanuja, has clearly discussed these confidential topics in his book, Gadyatraya. Here we quote a passage from this book.

*kadaham bhagavantam nardyanam mama natham mama kuladaivatam mama kula dhanam mama bhogyam mama matararam mama pitaram mama sarvam saksat karavani caksusa, kadaham bhagavat padambujadvayam, sirasa sangrahisyami, kadaham bhagavat padambujadvaya paricaryasaya nirasta samastetara bhoga sopahata samasta samsarija svabhavahprabuddha nitya niyamya nitya dasaika rasatmaka svabhavastat padambujadvayam pravaksyami, kadaham bhagavat padambujadvaya paricaryakarana yogas tadeka bhogas tatpadau paricarisyami, kada mam bhagavan svakiya ati sitalaya drsavalokya snigdha-gambhira-madhuraya gira paricaryayai mamajnapayisyati iti bhagavat paricaryamasam varddhayitva tayaivasaya tat prasadopa vrmhitaya bhagavatam upetya duradeva bhagavatam sesa bhoge sriya sahasinam vainetyadibhih sevyamanam samasta paribaraya cremate narayanaya nama iti pranamya utthaya utthaya punah punah pranamyatyanta sadhvasa vinayavanato bhutva, bhagavat parsada gana nayakai dvarapalakaih krpaya sneha garbhaya drsavalokitah.*

**“When will I directly see the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Narayana, who is my Lord, my family Deity, my family treasure, the object of my enjoyment, my mother, my father, and my all-in-all? When will I touch my head to the lotus feet of that Supreme Lord? When will I manifest a desire to serve the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord? When will I glorify those lotus feet, which destroy all types of desires for material enjoyment, remove all kinds of mundane conceptions, are the source of all knowledge, are the eternal controller, and are the object of eternal dasya rasa? When will I serve the Supreme Lord’s lotus feet, which are meant to be worshiped and are the source of all pleasures? When will the Supreme Lord cast His merciful, soothing, affectionate glance upon me, and sweetly order me to serve Him? In this way may my desire for serving the Supreme Lord constantly increase. I earnestly seek His mercy.**

**“I offer my most resepectful obeisances unto Lord Narayana, who is seated with His consort, Laksmi, on the bed of Ananta Sesa, and is respectfully served by His eternal associates, like Garuda. I fall down to the ground again and again and offer my obeisances with utmost humility May the Lord’s associates and doorkeepers glance at me with compassion.**

## Acarya Sri Purnaprajna

**Madhvacarya**

There was a high mountain named Vimanagiri on the bank of the Papanasini River, which flows about seven miles south-east of Udupi-ksetra, In ancient times Lord Parasurama shattered this mountain and created four kundas, named Parasutirtha, Dhanustirtha, Vanatirtha, and Gadatirtha. There is a large temple of Yogamaya at the foot of Vimanagiri Hill and the Deity was installed by Parasurama. Yogamaya resides in that temple and is worshiped by a Vaisnava brahmana. The holy tirtha, Dhanustirtha, which is one of the four tirthas established by Parasurama, is about one mile east of Vimanagiri Hill. The area around Dhanustirtha is known as Pajakaksetra.

At the present time some people explain the word pajaka as follows: *pati iti pa, na jayate it aja, pascaso ajasceti pajah, pajat ham (jalam) yasmin tat pajakam*. This means “the place where the unborn Lord Visnu-Parasurama created holy tirthas, including Dhanustirtha, is known as Pajakaksetra, and is located on the bank of the Papanasini River."

In Pajakaksetra there lived a poor brahmana who belonged to the Madhyageha family. He was well-versed in the Vedic literature and observed the codes of good conduct. In ancient times King Ramabhoja brought one hundred and twenty brahmanas that were his relatives from the province of Ahicchatra to Parasurama-ksetra. These brahmanas then resided at different locations in that area. Among those one hundred and twenty brahmanas, the one who constructed his residence in the middle of them all was called Madhyageha.

Similarly, the descendents of the brahmanas who constructed their houses at Pugavana, Likucavana, and other places, identified themselves with the names of those places and thus became known as Pugavana-vamsa, Likucavana- vamsa, Madhyageha-vamsa, and so on.

In the Kannada language the word madhyageha is translated as naddantillaya, which means nad (middle) anta (within) illaya (one who has a house). The name of that pious brahmana of Pajakaksetra who hailed from the Madhyageha family was Narayana Bhatta. (This name is found in the book Anumadhva- carita, written by Sri Madhva’s disciple, Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha. However, this name is not found in the book Madhva Vijaya, where only the name Madhyageha is found.)

Narayana Bhatta lived with his wife, Vedavati (or Vedavidya), and worshiped his family Deity of Lord Visnu, who lies on the bed of Sesa. In due course of time Vedavati gave birth to two sons, but both died prematurely. Then, the brahmana couple, being bereft of the happiness of having a son, performed severe austerities for twelve years while subsisting only on milk, with the desire of having an immortal son. The Supreme Lord, who sleeps on the bed of Sesa, became inclined to reward them for their severe austerities.

At that time there was a scarcity of pure worship of the Supreme Lord all over Bharata-varsa, which is traditionally the abode of sanatana-dharma. Atheism in the form of covered Buddhism was forcefully pushing the people towards an existence of dense ignorance, taking them away from their eternal religion of devotional service to Lord Visnu. Whenever there is a decline in religious principles, either Lord Visnu, whose body is composed of pure goodness, personally appears, or else He sends an empowered representative, for the welfare of the world.

To remove the fog of covered Buddhism from the atmosphere of Bharata-varsa, and to infuse life into the almost dead conditioned souls, Vayu, the wind-god, decided to appear in this world by the will of Lord Visnu. Just as previously, in the Treta-yuga, Mahavira Bajranga (Hanuman) appeared from the womb of Kesari’s wife, Anjana, to preach the glories of Lord Ramacandra, and as, in Dvapara-yuga, the foremost Vaisnava, Bhimasena, jappeared from the womb of Pandu’s wife, Kunti, to propagate the actual purport of all Vedic literatures compiled by Lord Sri Vedavyasa, Vayu appeared during Kali-yuga, from the womb of Vedavati, the wife of Narayana Bhatta, of the Madhyageha family of Pajakaksetra.

Sri Narayana Bhatta named his son Vasudeva. There is a difference of opinion regarding the time of Vasudeva’s appearance. According to the Tattvavadis., the time of Sriman Madhvacarya’s appearance has been ascertained by Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha, a direct disciple of Madhvacarya, from a statement of Mahabhdrata-tatparya-nirnaya. In the Moksadharma section of the Santi-parva of Mahabharata, Bhisma told the five Pandava brothers that they would re-appear, four thousand years after the beginning of Kali-yuga.

According to this prediction of Bhisma, it is said in the Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya:

*catuh sahasre trisatottare gate*  
*samvatsaranantu kalau prthivyam.*  
*jatah punah vipratanuh sa bhimo*  
*daityera nigudham haritattva mahi”*

**“After four thousand three hundred years of Kali-yuga had passed, Bhimasena again appeared in this world as a brahmana and preached the science of Lord Visnu, which was covered by demoniac people.”**

In accordance with this statement, Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha, a direct disciple of Madhvacarya who was in charge of the original matha known as Palamara, which is one of the eight mathas at Udupi, wrote in his book Anumadhvacarita:

*trisatabdottara catusahasrabdebhyo uttare*  
*ekona catvarimsabde vilambi parivatsare*  
*asvija-sukladesami-divase bhuvi pavane*  
*pajakakhye suciksetre durgaya cabhiviksite*  
*jato madhahna-velayam budhavare maruttanuh*  
*bhusurendropanito yah tatah ekadasabdake*  
*saume jagraha bhagavan turiyasramam uttamam*  
*madhvanama jigayayam vadino vadakausali*  
*ekonasiti varsani nitva manusa drstigah*  
*pingalabde maghasuddha navamyam vadarim yoyau*

If we accept Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha’s calculation, then Sriman Madhvacarya’s date of appearance was the year 4339 Kali era. At present, according to the opinion of the Tattvavadi’s calendar, it is 5029 Kali era . According to that calendar, Kali- yuga commenced at the time when Yudhisthira was enthroned after Bhima defeated Duryodhana in a duel with clubs. If we accept the calculation of Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha regarding the date of Sri Vasudeva’s appearance, which is 4339 of the Kali era, then it is to be understood that Sri Madhva appeared about 690 years ago.

In his Anumadhvacarita, Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha writes that Narayana Bhatta’s son, Vasudeva, appeared at noon on a Wednesday, the tenth day in the fortnight of the waxing moon in the month of Asvin in the year 4339 of the Kali era at Pajakaksetra.

Sri Krsnasvami Ayara and Dr, Bucanan have ascertained that the year of Sri Madhvacarya’s appearance was 1121 of the Saka era. However, some people present evidence against this conclusion. Padmanabhacarya of Adamara Matha maintained yet another opinion in his book “Life and Teachings of Madhva.” Most of the present Tattvavadis of the eight mathas consider the opinion of Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha to be reasonable.

From his very childhood Vasudeva astonished his friends and relatives by performing many wonderful pastimes. Within a very short time Vasudeva completed his primary education and he received the sacred thread at the age of eight. After receiving the sacred thread, he approached a brahmana from the Pugavana family who resided at Dandatirtha, which was about six miles west of Pajakaksetra, hoping to study the Vedas under his tutelage, Still, Vasudeva became busy playing with his class friends so that he was not at all attentive to his study of the Vedas.

One day, the teacher chastised Vasudeva severely for always playing and thus neglecting his studies. In response, Vasudeva recited non-stop discourses on many subjects of the Vedas and other scriptures, both what the teacher had taught him and much that he had not taught. Thus the teacher became struck with wonder. Within a very short time Vasudeva mastered all the Vedic literatures and so he returned home.

One day, Vasudeva picked up a stick and approached his father, He said, ‘‘My dear father, now I have finished studying the scriptures and so I will preach the Vaisnava conclusions, refuting the philosophy of impersonalism.”

Upon hearing these words of his child, Narayana Bhatta replied, “If an ordinary boy like you can refute Mayavada and propagate the Vaisnava Siddhanta throughout the world, then it must also be possible for this stick in your hand to transform into a huge tree.”

The father was in effect saying, “As it is impossible for a dry stick to become a living tree, it is impossible for a small boy like you to preach Vaisnava Siddhanta, thus refuting the widely propagated philosophy, Mayavada.

After hearing this, Vasudeva said, “My dear father, it is not at all impossible for this stick to become a huge tree by the mercy of the Supreme Lord, Similarly, by the Lord's mercy, a boy like me can establish Vaisnava Siddhanta and refute Mayavada.”

After saying this, Vasudeva stuck the dry stick into the ground and immediately, much to everyone’s surprise, it grew into a giant banyan tree. Even today one can see this banyan tree at Pajakaksetra, reminding everyone of the glories of Sriman Madhvacarya.

Upon seeing Vasudeva’s uncommon enthusiasm for preaching Vaisnava dharma and refuting the unauthorized philosophy of the impersonalists, as well as his unwavering self-confidence, Narayana Bhatta could understand that his soh would not want to enter family life in the future. Finally, he decided to keep Vasudeva at home by arranging his marriage. However, the intelligent boy understood the intention of his parents. Who in this world could entangle a lion-like personality who was eager to liberate everyone from material bondage, who was prepared to establish the Vaisnava Siddhanta within the world while condemning all unauthorized literatures, and who was empowered by Lord Visnu?

The common practice is that one should take permission from one’s parents before doing something important. Some people say that without the permission of one’s parents, it is improper to practice religious principles, and so what to speak of accepting the renounced order of life, sannyasa. According to such persons, great personalities like Sankaracarya had accepted the sannyasa order after somehow securing the permission of their parents. However, that this is a materialistic conception bom within the imagination of sense enjoyers that are averse to Krsna is clearly evident from the behavior of Sri Vasudeva.

Every godless living entity, beginning from Brahma down to the insignificant ant, is averse to the worship of Lord Hari on account of his spirit of material enjoyment. Such persons are also opposed to others’ worship of Lord Hari. The spirit of enjoyment flows incessantly from parents to son, son to parents, wife to husband, husband to wife, brother to brother, relative to relative, and friend to friend. Therefore, whenever anyone among them shows an interest in the worship of Lord Hari, others will put obstacles on his path, thinking that their object of enjoyment will be dedicated to the Supreme Lord forever. Indeed, it is as if they think that someone is snatching a morsel of food meant for them.

As long as parents can keep their son under their control and enjoy him by engaging him in their service they consider him to be a “devotee” and give him permission to worship the Supreme Lord. But when they understand that their son wants to dedicate himself for Krsna’s enjoyment, instead of remaining an object of their own enjoyment, they do not hesitate to shake heaven and earth while placing obstacles on his path of worshiping Lord Hari. It is not that this principle applies only to parents towards their sons. It also applies to a person who has forgotten his constitutional position and thus identifies himself as someone’s son. Such a son will also try to impede the devotional spirit of his parents.

The wild dancing of such a spirit of enjoyment is found everywhere in this world, and is also seen in wives, husbands, relatives, and friends. The unlimitedly wise Vasudeva knew this very well and so, without waiting for anyone’s permission, not even his parents, relatives, and friends, and without disclosing his intention to anyone, he accepted the sannyasa order from Sri Acyuta Preksa of Sri Rajatapithapura at the age of twelve.

According to the opinion of the followers of Madhva at Rajatapithapura, Lord Narayana in the form of Hamsa initiated four-headed Brahma into the worship of Lord Visnu. Brahma then instructed the four Kumaras, who then instructed the sage Durvasa. Durvasa instructed Paratirtha, who then instructed Satyaprajna. Satyaprajna initiated Prajnatirtha into the worship of Lord Visnu in disciplic succession.

Trivikramacarya’s son, Narayana Pandita, a disciple of Madhva and the compiler of the book Manimanjari, says that when Sankaracarya was departing this world, some of his disciples, including Padmapada, inquired from him about their duties. At that time Sankaracarya ordered his disciples to work hard to establish the philosophy of kevaladvaita-vada. He also said that if somehow or other Prajnatirtha Yati, who was a learned scholar of dvaita-vada and thus a great enemy of kevaladvaita-vada, was not converted to the understanding of kevaladvaita-vada, then the propagation of kevaladvaita-vada would be hampered.

After receiving this order and thus realizing the intention of their spiritual master, Padmapada and the others tried their best to convince Prajnatirtha to accept the hevaladvaita philosophy. At that time Prajnatirtha was in charge of a matha at Nandigrama, and was being served by a disciple named Acyutapreksa.

In an attempt to forcibly convert Prajnatirtha Yati to their understanding, the followers of kevaladvaita-vada set his matha on fire and thus succeeded in destroying many manuscripts preaching the dvaita Siddhanta. They even took Prajnatirtha Yati’s sannyasa danda and water pot, forced him to apply tilaka like the hevaladvaitavadis, and ordered him to chant the mantra “so ham”. Being harassed by the hevaladvaitavadis, Prajnatirtha externally adopted their behavior and dealings, while internally not budging even an inch from the worship of Lord Visnu,

Acyutapreksa also, just to deceive the hevaladvaitavadis, and act for their future benefit, externally imitated the behavior of his spiritual master while internally remaining engaged in the worship of Lord Visnu. From the time of Vayu’s incarnation as Vasudeva in the house of Sri Narayana Bhatta of Pajakaksetra, the hearts of devotees like Acyutapreksa became filled with joy and fearlessness.

Acyutapreksa attended the temple of Lord Anantesvara at Rajatapithapura, where he worshiped Lord Visnu, who lies on the bed of Sesa. Narayana Bhatta’s son, Vasudeva, at the age of ten, glorified the dvaita Siddhanta to Sri Acyutapreksa for one full year in the temple of Anantesvara at Rajatapithapura while serving him. And at the age of twelve, in the opinion of Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha, in the year 4350 Kali era, Vasudeva accepted the sannyasa order.

Vasudeva’s sannyasa name was Ananda Tirtha, or Tatparyaya Madhva. The explanation of the word madhva is found in the following verse written by Madhvacarya in his book Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya:

*madvityananda uddista variti jnanam ucyuate madhva anandatirthasyat trtiya maruti tanuh*

**“The word madhu means ‘happiness’ and the letter va means ‘knowledge.’ Therefore the word madhva means ‘Ananda Tirtha,’ or ‘blissful knowledge.’ The word tirtha also means ‘knowledge.’”**

Anandatirtha was the third incarnation of Vayu. Even today the followers of Sri Madhva sampradya chant this mantra while introducing Sri Madhvacarya:

*svasti srimate paramahamsa parivrajakacarya varyatvadyaneka. guna ganalankria pada vakya pramana paravara- parangata sarva tantra svatantra. srimad bhaismi-satyabhdma-sameta-srigopala-krsna pada padmaradhaka-srimaddvaita-vaisnava-siddhanta-pratisthapanacarya. srimad anandatirthdpara-namaka-sriman madhvacaryah*

**The practice of introducing the acarya in this manner is still current in all the eight mathas at Udupi, as well as other mathas that are branches of the original mathas.**

After accepting sannyasa from Sri Acyutapreksa at the age of twelve, Sri Vasudeva was known as Sriman Madhvacarya. After accepting the renounced order of life, Sriman Madhvacarya began to act like an acarya and began preaching while carefully following all scriptural rules and regulations. By his personal code of conduct, Sriman Madhva even surprised his own spiritual master. He set out to preach the science of devotional service to Lord Visnu everywhere.

Sriman Madhva defeated a learned logician named Vasudeva by quoting scriptural injunctions, and secured a “victory certificate” from him. He thus gained the respect of all the devotees by refuting the unauthorized conclusions of sinful logicians and staunch Mayavadis, including Vadisimha and Buddhisagara.

After some time, Madhva arrived at Ananatasayana-ksetra (Trivandrum) while traveling throughout South India on the pretext of visiting Sri Ramesvaram.

At the temple of Lord Anantasayana, the acarya countered Sankaracarya’s philosophy while explaining Vedanta-sutra. According to the principal followers in the Madhvacarya sampradaya, including Pandita Sri Narayana Bhatta, Sankaracarya appeared for the first time before the appearance of Sriman Madhvacarya at a village named Kaladi in the state of Kerala. Sankaracarya’s second appearance, in the village of Kudupustura, near Kumbhakonam, during the time of Sriman Madhvacarya, is mentioned in the book Sankara Vijaya. The place where Sankaracarya appeared for the second time has a matha of the Sankara sampradaya, named Kamakotimatha, even today.

At the time when Sriman Madhvacarya went to Ramesvara- ksetra and explained the Brahma-sutra according to the dvaita siddhanta, Sankaracarya also came there from Kudupustura village and said that without composing his own commentary, Madhvacarya had no right to preach Brahma-sutra based on dvaita siddhanta. Sriman Madhvacarya replied, saying that this was not a valid principle, and he challenged Sankaracarya to refute the dvaita siddhanta if he had the power to do so. Sankaracarya then tried to refute Madhvacarya’s dvaita philosophy with the support of his concocted ideas. However, Madhvacarya defeated Sankaracarya in the debate by showing the inconsistency of his conclusions. In this way Madhva began to beat the victory drum of dvaita siddhanta everywhere.

After visiting Ramesvara, Sri Madhva went to Sri Rangam and other holy places, where he refuted many unauthorized philosophies. Thereafter he arrived at the shore of the Payasvini River and participated in a meeting of brahmanas that were well-versed in the Vedic literatures. While there he refuted the smarta and Mayavada philosophies, which are both against Vaisnava principles, and thus he became celebrated as Sarvajna Yati.

Thereafter, with a few disciples, Sriman Madhvacarya went to Badarikasrama, where he instructed them on his commentary of Bhagavad-gita. One day, as the disciples were listening to this commentary near Badarikasrama, they saw an effulgent entity approach from the sky and enter the mouth of Sriman Madhvacarya. When he regained his composure, Sriman Madhvacarya understood that he had been brought to Badarikasrama by Vedavyasa. Alone he approached Vedavyasa and received instructions concerning the authorized conclusions of the Vedas, Vedanta-sutra, Mahabharata, and Srimad Bhagavatam.

Thereafter, Sriman Madhvacarya visited Sri Nara-Narayana Rsis’s asrama, where he had the darsana of Sri Narayana. He then returned to his disciples after receiving permission from Vedavyasa and Nara-Narayana Rsis. Thereafter, the acarya descended from the Himalaya mountains, along with his disciples, and traveled to Dvaraka, Kuruksetra, Naimisaranya, Prayaga, Kasi, Gaya, and other holy places related to Lord Visnu, where he preached, establishing his Siddhanta while refuting the unauthorized siddhantas of learned scholars in those places.

When Sriman Madhvacarya returned to Ananda Matha from Badarikasrama, he finished his commentary on Vedanta- sutra. His companion and disciple, Satya Tirtha, wrote down the entire commentary on behalf of his spiritual master. It is said that Sriman Madhvacarya established his own siddhanta in his commentary on Vedanta-sutra by refuting twenty-one unauthorized commentaries. While returning from Badarikasrama, Sriman Madhvacarya visited many places around the Godavari River in the province of Ganjama. There he met two prominent scholars, named Sobhana Bhatta and Svami Sastri. These two later became known as Padmanabha Tirtha and Narahari Tirtha in the Madhva parampara.

One day, after returning to Udupi, Sriman Madhvacarya composed five chapters of a collection of prayers dedicated to Lord Krsna while going to bathe in the sea. After arriving at the seashore, he saw that a boat full of merchandise was struck in the sand and, despite all efforts of the crew, could not be moved. The name of the ship’s captain was Maina. He was from the village of Yermal, near Adamara village, which was 14 miles south of Udupi.

Sriman Madhvacarya made some kind of gesture with his hands, as if moving the boat, and to everyone’s astonishment, it suddenly began to move across the surface of the water. The captain of the boat was overjoyed, and felt highly indebted to the powerful sannyasi. As an expression of his gratitude, he requested the sannyasi to take something from his cargo. Sriman Madhvacarya only wanted a large piece of gopi candana that had been taken from Gopi-sarovara at Dvaraka. While Madhvacarya was bringing the large block of gopi candana home, it broke on the way at a place called Badabhandesvara, and within it was found an enchanting Deity of Balakrsna.

It is said that at the end of Dvapara-yuga, Lord Krsna’s great grandson, Vajranabha, installed three Deities of Lord Krsna at Vraja-mandala. Long before that, during the manifested presence of Lord Krsna, the Deity of Balakrsna was personally installed by Lord Krsna at Dvaraka. At the end of Krsna’s manifested pastimes in this world, Sri Arjuna placed that Deity in Gopi-sarovara, by the seashore. Then, in the age of Kali, when Madhvacarya went to bathe in the sea, the Deity of Lord Krsna that was covered by a huge block of gopi candana once again appeared.

In one hand the Deity of Balakrsna holds a grinding stick and in His other hand He holds a grinding rope. After discovering the Deity of Lord Krsna, Sriman Madhvacarya immediately composed the remaining seven chapters of his famous Dvadasa stotra prayers.

When thirty strongly-built men were unable to even lift the Deity, Sriman Madhva, who was an incarnation of Hanuman, Bhimasena, and Vayu of the spiritual world, effortlessly picked Him up and took Him to his matha. He bathed the Deity, who was covered with gopi candana, in a large lake at Udupi and then installed Him there.

To facilitate the worship of the Deity of Lord Balakrsna that he had installed, and the propagation of his siddhanta, Madhva awarded sannyasa to his eight senior brahmacaris. He then entrusted them with the responsibility of maintaining the service of Lord Balakrsna, along with their study of the scriptures. He then awarded sannyasa to a householder disciple and gave him the name Padmanabha Tirtha. He instructed that the service of Lord Balakrsna would be conducted by his eight sannyasis for a period of two years each, in rotation.

Visnu Tirtha was Sriman Madhvacarya’s younger brother. Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha wrote in his Anumadhva-carita that even today, Sri Visnu Tirtha is alive and performing bhajana at Kumaradhara Mountain in the province of Subramanya- ksetra.

Sriman Madhvacarya had inscribed all the literature composed by him on copper leaves and buried them at Katatila-ksetra, which is about 50 miles from Udupi-ksetra. At this spot he installed a copper Deity of Lord Krsna holding a pot of butter. That place is known as Vyasa-tirtha. Even today a black snake lives on that altar, protecting the literature of the acarya. This is the opinion of the local people.

This sacred place, Vyasa-tirtha, is daily worshiped by the sannyasis and brahmacaris of the Madhva sampradaya. The sannyasis are allowed to touch the Deity of Lord Krsna while worshiping Him, but the brahmacaris are not allowed to do so. They are required to worship the Deity from a distance. It is said that every day, at the time of worship, the black snake goes away, but if there is any mistake in the process of worship, or if the prescribed etiquette is not observed, then the black snake returns.

When the worshiper humbly prays to the snake and promises to worship the Lord according to the prescribed rules and regulations, then only does it leave. There is a matha here named Katatila Matha. According the Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha, when mundane literature became prominent by the influence of Kali, so that the literature of Madhvacarya was hardly read, Madhva’s disciple, Visnu Tirtha, dug up all the literature of Sriman Madhva that was inscribed on copper leaves from the pit known as Vyasa-tirtha at Katatila-ksetra. After doing this, Visnu Tirtha was able to defeat the unauthorized philosophers and revive the preaching of Sri Madhva’s literature.

Sriman Madhvacarya visited Badarikasrama a second time and in the course of his journey, he passed through Maharastra. At that time a king named Mahadeva was digging a pond with the help of his servants for the benefit of his subjects. When the king ordered his followers to force Madhva and his disciples to help with the digging, Sriman Madhvacarya tactfully engaged the king in that work and then continued his journey.

Thereafter Madhva arrived at the shore of the Ganges. On one side of the river there was a kingdom ruled by a Mohammedan and on the other side there was a kingdom ruled by a Hindu. Due to the great enmity between the two kings, there were no boats available to cross the river, and indeed, it was forbidden to cross the river by any means. Still, Sri Madhva did not care for the danger and he swam across the river while holding the hands of his disciples. When Madhva came to the far side of the river, the soldiers of the Muslim army began to harass him, considering him to be an offender.

Madhva then personally went before the king and informed him of his mission. The Mohammedan king became so overwhelmed with emotion while seeing Madhva’s attractive form, and while listening to his speech, that he expressed his desire to hand over half of his kingdom to him. Sriman Madhvacarya refused this offer.

Later on, in the course of his journey, Madhva was attacked by a band of plunderers, but he easily routed them. Once Satya Tirtha was attacked by a tiger and Sriman Madhvacarya drove it away after a fierce fight. In Badarikasrama, Madhva personally met Vyasadeva and received eight salagrama-silas from him. After this meeting, Sriman Madhvacarya composed his Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya.

Gradually the news of Sriman Madhvacarya’s extraordinary wisdom and devotion for the Supreme Lord spread throughout Bharata-varsa. As a result, the person in charge of Sankaracarya’s Srngeri Matha became filled with anxiety. Realizing that the reputation of their institution was being diminished, the followers of Sankara kept themselves busy harassing Madhva. They endeavored to isolate the followers of Madhva from society and prove that his philosophy was unauthorized and not approved by sastra.

The impersonalist Padma Tirtha, with the help of Pundarikaksa Puri, a learned scholar who knew the Sankara philosophy in all details, challenged Madhvacarya to a debate and then conspired to steal all his literature. But after a great struggle these literatures were recovered with the help of Jai Singha, the king of Kumbhala (situated near Katva-tirtha).

Sriman Madhvacarya defeated the famous scholar Trivikramacarya, who belonged to the Likuca family and was a resident of Visnu Mangala, and then made him a disciple. The son of Trivikramacarya was a great poet named Sri Narayanacarya, who was the author of some well-known books, including Sumadhva Vijaya and Mani-manjari.

There was no limit to Sri Purnaprajna’s physical strength. There was a powerful man named Kadanjari who boasted that he possessed the strength of thirty men. One day, the acarya dug his toe into the ground and ordered that extraordinarily powerful man to lift it. Despite mustering all his great strength, Kadanjari could not lift the acarya’s toe.

This verse is found on a stone slab at Mudageri village in the district of Kadura: sri madhvacaryair eka hastena dniya sthapita sila. There was large tract of land that was eroding because of the flooding of a river. To check the flow of the river, thousands of people tried to carry a large stone slab, but because they were unable to carry it further, they left it on the road. Then Sriman Madhvacarya effortlessly picked up the stone slab with one hand and placed it where the people directed.

What to speak of others, even the demigods were surprised and greatly pleased to hear the scriptural conclusions presented by Sriman Madhvacarya, the third incarnation of Maruti. One day, all the demigods headed by Rudra appeared in the sky above Rajatapithapura and then came in front of the temple of Sri Anantesvara. At that time Sriman Madhvacarya was explaining Aitareya Upanisad to his disciples in the temple. The demigods were so pleased to witness Sriman Madhvacarya’s skill in explaining the scriptures that they showered celestial flowers, like the mandara and parijata.

Sri Madhvacarya disappeared form this world while explaining his commentary on Aitareya Upanisad in the temple of Anantesvara on the 9thday in the fortnight of the waxing moon in the month of Magha. Sriman Madhvacarya remained in this world for seventy-nine years. The famous scholar and acarya of the Madhvacarya sampradaya, Sri Vadiraja Svami, wrote in his book Sarasabharativilasa that Sriman Madhvacarya is present invisibly at Udupi, and visibly at Badarikasrama.

There are many prominent personalities, including Asvatthama, Bali, Vyasa, Hanuman, Vibhisana, Krpa, and Parasurama, who will remain in this world until the end of the Vaivasvata manvantara, and then return to their respective spiritual abodes. Sriman Madhvacarya will reside at Badarikasrama until the end of Vaivasvata Manu’s reign, and then enter the body of Vayu. According to the opinion of Sri Hrsikesa Tirtha, Sriman Madhvacarya disappeared from Udupi on the ninth day of the waxing moon in the month of Magha, at the end of year 4418 Kali era.

## The Disciplic Succession and Mathas

He also awarded sannyasa to a householder disciple and engaged him in preaching the dvaita Siddhanta.

This person who renounced his family life and accepted sannyasa from Srimad Ananda Tirthapada is known in the Madhva-Gaudiya disciplic succession as SrI Padmanabha Tirtha. The authors of Sri Gaur-ganoddesa-dipika and Sri Govinda-bhasya have described Sri Padmanabha Tirtha as a disciple of Srimad Ananda Tirthapada in their parampara.

## The Glorious Intellect Displayed by the Acaryas in the Disciplic Succession from Sri Madhva

Acarya Srimad Purnaprajna Anandatirtha compiled many literatures that establish his dvaita philosophy while refuting all opposing philosophies, especially those preached by the impersonalists. After Madhvacarya’s disappearance, many acaryas in his disciplic succession also compiled many literatures establishing the supremacy of Lord Visnu and refuting the unauthorized impersonalist philosophies. Although these literatures are not known to the common people, they are painstakingly studied by the learned scholars of the Madhva sampradaya.

The unalloyed followers of the Madhva sampradaya do not show much interest in discussing literature of other sampradayas. They also hesitate to preach the teachings of their sampradaya to those who are not their followers.

In the Madhva *sampradaya,* two divisions were made, named Dasakuta and Vyasakuta. Those who are more inclined towards chanting and worshiping the Deity, rather than discussing Sanskrit literature, are considered as belonging to the Dasakuta group.

The Dasakutas are sometimes referred to as *bhajananandis.* It is not that those belonging to the Dasakuta group are ignorant of the scriptures. They are simply more interested in performing *bhajana* than discussing the philosophy of their *sampradaya.* There are many literatures in the Dasakuta *sampradaya,* but they were written in the native language of Kannada, and mostly in the form of prose. Many respectable members of the Madhva *sampradaya,* such as Sri Kanaka dasa, were celebrated as belonging to the Dasakuta *sampradaya.* And, many devotees of the Vyasakuta *sampradaya* wrote books in the Kannada language. For example, Vadiraja Svami, although a very distinguished and learned scholar of the Vyasakuta *sampradaya,* wrote many books about *bhajana.*

The members of the Vyasakuta *sampradaya* are sometimes referred to as *gosthanandis* because they teach the Sanskrit literature of their *sampradaya.*

The following are some of the most celebrated scholars and *acaryas* of the Madhva *parampara,* and the literature composed by them. (Although we technically belong to Sri Madhva sampradaya, because it was accepted by Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, we do not concern ourselves very much with Sriman Madhva Muni, or his sampradaya. Many of these previous acaryas, such as Sri Padmanabha Tirtha, Sri Narahari Tirtha, and Sri Jaya Tirtha, were greatly learned scholars of Udupi.

1\. Sri Padmanabha Tirtha (a disciple of Madhva, from Uttaradi Matha). His literature includes the book Sannyasa-ratnavali.

2\. Sri Narahari Tirtha (a disciple of Madhva, from Uttaradi Matha). His literature includes a brief commentary on the books written by Madhva. At present this book is not found but the contents can be seen in the books written by Sri Jaya Tirthapada.

3\. Sri Jaya Tirtha, or Tikacarya (from Uttaradi Matha). His literature includes Nyaya-sudha, Tattva-prakasika, Dasa- prakarana-tlka, Satprasna-tika, Isavasya-tika, Gitabhasya- tika, Gita-tatparya-nirnaya-tika, Bhagavat-tatparya-tika, Rgbhasya-tika, Nyaya-vivarana-tika, Pramana-padhhati, and Vadavali.

Sri Jaya Tirthapada’s Nyaya-sudha is a famous book in the Madhva sampradaya. Unless one is very knowledgeable in the philosophy of the Madhva sampradaya, one cannot understand this literature, however great a scholar he may be. To understand how knowledgeable one is about the Madhva sampradaya, the members often ask, “My dear sir, how many times have you drunk the sudha?” According to the opinion of the members of the Madhva sampradaya, the more one studies Nyasa-sudha, the more he is to be considered a learned scholar. The Nyasa- sudha was printed only once, and now it is unavailable.

4\. Trivikrama Panditacarya (a householder disciple of Sri Madhvacarya). His literature includes Tattva pradipah, Sutra-bhasya-tika, Vayu-stutih, Visnu-stutih, and Usaharana- kavyam.

5\. Narayana Panditacarya (the son of Trivikrama Pandita). His literature includes Madhva-vijaya, Madhva-vijaya-tika, Bhava-prakasika, Anu Madhva-vijayah, Mani-manjari, Nrsimha-stutih, Siva-stutih, Nayacandrika, and Sangraha Ramayanam.

6\. Sri Vijayadhvaja Tirtha (from Pejavar Matha, the seventh descendent from Sri Madhva). He was the compiler of Padaratnavali-tika, which is an elaboration of Bhagavat-tatparya written by Sriman Madhvacarya. In the mangalacarana of this famous commentary, Sri Vijayadhvaja Tirtha has described his guru parampara while offering obeisances to his spiritual master.

7\. Vyasa Tirtha (a sannyasi from Vyasaraya Matha. He was the fourteenth descendent in the disciplic succession from Sriman Madhva. His disciple was Sri Laksmipati Tirtha, whose disciple was Sri Madhavendra Puri.) His literature includes Nyayamrtam, Tatparya-candrika, Tarka Tandvah, Bhedojjivanam, Khandana-trayamandara-manjari, and Tattva-viveka-mandara-manjari.

The book Nyayamrtam, written by Sri Vyasa Tirtha, is a greatly respected book in the society of the students of Vedanta. It refutes all opposing and unauthorized opinions. It demolishes the status of unauthorized literature, like a second pasupata weapon; it protects the greatly powerful devotees of Lord Visnu and awards them supreme happiness; and it illuminates the truth like a second Sudarsana cakra in the hand of Lord Visnu.

By the wonderful influence of this excellent literature, Nyayamrtam, the propagation of impersonalism by the Mayavadi sampradaya was crippled. This literature is unfathomable by even the best of scholars from the Mayavadi sampradaya, so that they are unable to even begin to understand the subject matter.

As a challenge to the Sudarsana-cakra-like Nyayamrtam, Sri Madhusudana Sarasvati, an advaitavadi, wrote the famous book Advaita-siddhi. Still, he could not even begin to refute the philosophical conclusions presented in Nyayamrtam.

Sri Ramacarya Tirtha of the Madhva sampradaya wrote a book named Tarangini, in which he refuted the opinions set forth by Sri Madhusudana Sarasvati’s Advaita-siddhi.

In response, a book entitled Brahmanandiya was written by someone in the kevaladvaita sampradaya as a refutation of the book Tarangini. Yet even that book was considered completely lusterless beside the wonderful brilliance of the Sudarsana-cakra-like Nyayamrtam. This can be seen in the book Vanamala Misriya, which was presented by the Madhva sampradaya as a refutation of Brahmanandiya. There is no doubt that if a scholar carefully studies these five literatures, he will clearly appreciate Sri Vyasa Tirtha's extraordinary wisdom, learning, philosophical conclusions, networks of unprecedented arguments, and power to refuting all contrary philosophies.

8\. Sri Vadiraja Tirtha (the sixteenth descendent in the disciplic succession of Sode Matha from Sriman Madhvacarya. Sri Vadiraja Tirtha appeared about three hundred years after Sriman Madhvacarya’s visit to Badarikasrama. He is celebrated as “the second Madhvacarya” in the Madhva sampradaya In terms of preaching Vaisnava siddhanta and refuting unauthorized philosophies, no other such unlimitedly powerful personality appeared in the sampradaya after the disappearance of Madhvacarya.

Sri Vadiraja Tirtha was born in a poor brahmana family in the village of Hubinaka, twenty-six miles north of Rajatapithapura. After seeing the attractive form of that poor brahmana boy, Bagisa Tirtha of Sode Matha became attracted to him and accepted him as a disciple. He then awarded him the sannyasa order, after which he became known as Sri Vadiraja Tirtha.

Although, as was the tradition at Sode Matha, he was engaged in the worship of Lord Varahadeva, Sri Vadiraja Tirtha became especially attracted to the Hayagriva form of Lord Visnu. Indeed, he had so much love and devotion for Lord Hayagriva that every day, the Lord would place His two front legs on Sri Vadiraja Tirtha's arms and eat sweetened chick peas from a plate that he had placed on his head. After eating, the Lord would disappear, leaving aside some remnants. To reciprocate Vadiraja’s worship and faith, Lord Hayagriva give him darsana in this manner every day.

Although Sri Vadiraja Svami was very eager to refute all kinds of misconceptions and unauthorized doctrines, he was especially determined to refute the siddhantas of the Shaivites and Jains. Once, he defeated a very powerful Jain sannyasi in a debate and as a sign of victory, accepted his stick and crown. Even today that crown and stick can be seen at the samadhi of Sri Vadiraja Tirtha, which is at the Trivikrama temple at Soda village in north Kannada.

In the Soda village, Vadiraja Svami constructed two temples—Trivikrama-devalaya and Prana-devalaya. He also constructed a lake there called Dhavala Ganga.

Starting from Rajatapithapura, Vadiraja Svami traveled all over India, visited all the holy places of pilgrimage, including holy rivers, mountains, and temples, and then composed a book based on his tour of India. The name of this book is Tirthaprabanda, and it is written in prose form. It contains narrations of many interesting incidents and discussions.

Vadiraja Svami had revived and enhanced many codes of etiquette and good conduct that had been established by Sriman Madhvacarya. He created an institution at Rajatapithapura that assisted in the service of the Sri Krsna temple at Udupi.

After a few generations, many devotees in Sriman Madhvacarya’s sampradaya became somewhat indifferent toward preaching the literatures of their predecessors. On the other hand, Vadiraja Svami, in order to preach Madhva’s Siddhanta among the fallen souls of his country, composed many poems in the Kannada language, and in those poems he presented the glories of the Supreme Lord and the authorized scriptural conclusions. So that these poems would be discussed every day, he organized a harinama-sankirtana-sampradaya in the temple of Lord Krsna at Udupi, so that they would be sung congregationally.

The members of the Madhva sampradaya say that it was Vadiraja Svami who made special arrangements for the performance of harinama-sankirtana in the temple of Lord Krsna every day. Even today the members of the Dasakuta faction of the Madhva sampradaya regularly perform kirtana and recite glorification of and prayers to the Supreme Lord written in the Kannada language by Vadiraja Svami.

Vadiraja Svami introduced the singing of Dvadasa- storta, composed by Sriman Madhvacarya, to the accompaniment of musical instruments. There is a story told among the followers of Madhva that Vadiraja Svami had collected so much gold by defeating scholars in debate that even if he covered the entire temple of Lord Krsna with gold, there would be a seemingly unlimited quantity remaining.

When Vadiraja Svami decided to cover Lord Krsna’s temple with gold, the Lord appeared in his dream and instructed him that to build a golden temple in Kali-yuga is catastrophic, because it would attract the attention of atheistic plunderers who are greedy and averse to the Supreme Lord. After receiving this instruction in a dream, Vadiraja Svami gave up this idea and buried all his gold somewhere on the north side of Lord Krsna’s temple and installed a snake on top of it. Even today, Subhramanya is worshiped at that place.

Vadiraja Svami converted many people to Madhva sampradaya, he compiled many literatures, and he personally preached their contents. His literature includes Yuktimallika, Sudhatippani, Tattva- prakasikatippani, Samagramahabharata-iika, Laksalankarah, Sarasabharativilasah, Pasandamatakhandnam, Adhikarananamavalih, Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-tika, Rukminisa-vijayakavyam, Tirtha-prabandah, and Jainamata- khandanam.

9\. Sri Raghavendra Tirtha (a sannyasi of Mantralaya Matha). His literature includes Sudha- parimala, Tattvaprakasika-bhavadlpah, Tantradipika, Mantrarthamanjari, Purusasukta-tika, Dasopanisad- khandarthah, Gitavivrtih, Dasaprakarana-tika-tippani, and Paddhati-tippani.

10\. Sri Visvapati Tirtha (a sannyasi of Pejavara Matha). His literature includes Madhvavijaya-tika, Manimanjari-tika, Tirthaprabanda-tika, Rukminisavijaya-tika, Pancastuti-tika, Sangraharamayana-tika, and Ramasandesa-tika.

## Ascertaining the Date of Madhvacarya’s Appearance

The various evidences pointing to Sriman Madhvacarya’s date of appearance are not accepted by everyone. In this book we quote six principal evidences.

1\. The listings of the previous *mathas* found by Sri Bhandarkar, and evidence from the *Vayu* *Purana*. Bhandarkar says that apart from *Barhaspatya-varsa-nirupana*, there is no mention of any year of the Saka era in the lists of ancient *mathas*. Thus the year of the Saka era has been guessed by considering the information provided in the lists of *mathas.*

Sri Padmanabha Acarya of Adamara Matha tried to convince the learned scholars of Udupi of his conclusion that Madhvacarya was born in the *vilambi-varsa* by quoting evidence from the *Vayu Purana* and other authorized sources. According to the verse quoted from the *Vayu Purana*, Madhva was born on the seventh day of the fortnight of the waxing moon in the month of Magha in *vilambi-varsa*. According to another verse, he was born on Vijaya-dasami of the same year.

2\. The lists of mathas prepared by the persons in charge of the eight mathas at Udupi, and by Tirthasvami Mahodaya, who was in charge of Uttaradi Matha. According to the book Satkatha, written in the Kannada language by Bhimarao Svamirao and printed by Prasada Raghava Press at Dharwad, the time of Madhya’ s appearance was in the vilambi-varsa of 1040 Saka era. The learned scholars of the Madhva sampradaya respect this statement very highly and have no suspicion of its validity.

3\. In his book Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya, Sriman Madhvacarya mentioned the time of his appearance in two places:

*prayaso raksasascaiva tvai krsnatvam agate*  
*sesa yasyanti tacchesa astavimse kalau yuge*  
*gate catuh sahasrabde tamogasti satottare*  
*(Chapter 9, Text 100)*

*catuh sahasre trisatottare gate sambat saranantu kalau*  
*prthivyam jatah punar vipratanuh sa bhimo daitai*  
*nighudam haritattvam aha*  
*(Chapter 32, Text 131)*

According to these passages, Sri Madhva Muni appeared some time during the year 4300 Kali era. By converting the date 4300 Kali era into the corresponding year of the Saka era, Sri Krsnasvami Iyer concluded that the year 1121 of the Saka era was the true date of Sri Madhva’s advent.

Dr Buchanan had visited Mysore, Kannada, and Malabar in the year 1799 AD, or 1721 Saka era, and he then invited all the learned scholars of Udupi and announced that the year 1121 of the Saka era was the acarya’s date of appearance. However, he did not provide much evidence for establishing this conclusion.

4\. Sri Gopinath Rao found the following two verses from the second Gabesana-khanda of the book Daksinapathe Sri Vaisnavadharmera Laghu Itivrtya, which talks about the date of Sri Madhva’s appearance.

*kalau pravirte bauddhadi matam ramanujam tatha*  
*sake hy ekona pancasad adhikabde sahasrake*  
*nirakartum mikhya vayuh sammata sthapanaya*  
*ca ekadasa sake cake vimsatasta yuge gate*

Uddhvacarya, a son of Balacarya and resident of Bai-ksetra, which is located on the bank of the Krsna River, has written in the introduction of the book Sarvamula compiled by Srimad Purnaprajna:

*utsannamnayam punar nirupayitum*  
*raupya pithe supithe madhyageha sugehe*  
*avirasa bhagavan dasa satatama saka satake*  
*srimat purnaprajnah suprajnah uktam*  
*etacchalari nrsimhacarya krta smrtiarthasagare*

According to the opinion of Nrsimhacarya, Sri Madhvacarya appeared in the year 1100 Saka era.

5\. The archeology department found these inscriptions on three stone slabs that were in the possession of Sri Narahari Tirtha: “From the year 1186 Saka era to the year 1215 Saka era, Tirtha Svami (Sri Narahari Tirtha) propagated the glories of Sisuraja of the Kalinga kingdom. Narahari Tirtha was initiated by Ananda Tirtha, a sannyasi disciple of Purusottama Tirtha.

“Ananda Tirtha punished many misguided followers of Vyasadeva, bringing them back to the path of auspiciousness. Those who carry out the orders of Ananda Tirtha obtain the lotus feet of Lord Hari. The words of Ananda Tirtha are very dear to Lord Visnu and they are capable of awarding one His lotus feet.”

This message was inscribed on the stone slabs in year 1203 of the Saka era. Professor Kilaharna ascertained the date of the stone inscriptions as the 29thMarch, 1281 A.D. A stone inscription at Chlkakola, near Kurmacala, and another stone inscription in the temple of Simhacalam, confirm Narahari Tirtha’s presence at those places.

Vidyaranya Bharati had acquired land for his Srngagiri Matha from the king of Vijayanagara in the year 1268 of the Saka era. He was a contemporary of Aksobhya Tirtha, who was in the fourth generation from Sri Madhva. It is said:

*asina tattvam asina parajiva prabhedina*  
*vidyaranyam aranyam aksobhya muniracchinat*

Moreover, Vedantadesika lived during the thirteenth century of the Saka era and at the request of the king of Vijayanagara, he acted as a judge during the debate between Vidyaranya and Aksobhya. This incident is mentioned in the book known as Vedantadesika-vaibhavaprakasika.

In the book Jayatirthavijaya, it is mentioned that Jaya Tirtha had a meeting with Vidyaranya Bharati. In addition, Vidyaranya, in his book, cited evidence from Jaya Tirtha’s commentary. Therefore there is no doubt that Vidyarana, Jaya Tirtha, Aksobhya, and Vedantadesika were contemporaries.

There are many speculations and a variety of conclusions regarding the date of appearance of Sri Madhva because all this evidence points in different directions.

## Sri Madhva is the Third Incarnation of Vayudeva

From the evidence that Acarya Sri Purnaprajnapada had presented regarding the forms of Vayudeva is his commentary on the thirteenth sutra of the fourth pada of the second chapter of Sri Brahma-sutra, it is understood that in Vayuloka the principal Vayu, or mukhya-prana is present. The mukhya- prana has five forms—prana, apana, vyana, udana and samana. They had five sons bom from the womb of goddess Bharati. The names of the five sons are the same as those of the fathers. Among the five, Prana is also called Nasikya Vayu. Nasikya Vayu is one of the predominating lords of the ten directions. From Nasikya Vayu, innumerable other Vayus have emanated. Among these innumerable Vayus, forty-nine are prominent.

Mukhya-prana, from whom the five kinds of Vayus have come, is the source of all the forms of Vayudeva. They appear as expansions of the pradhana Vayu in every yuga. It is also said that in some particular yugas, pradhana Vayu incarnates in three principle forms. The incarnation of Vayudeva in Treta-yuga was Hanuman, in Dvapara-yuga Vayu incarnated as Sri Bhimasena, and in Kali-yuga Vayu incarnated as Sri Madhvacarya. Therefore Sri Madhvacarya was the third incarnation of pradhana Vayu, or Mukhya-prana.

Sriman Madhvacaryapada confirmed this in his books, including Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya, Sutra-bhasya, Taittiriya- bhasya, Aittareya-bhasya, and Anuvyakhyana. Apart from this, many acaryas of the Tattvavadi sampradaya, especially Vadiraja Svami, who was celebrated as being a second Madhvacarya, collected evidence from the Vedic literature confirming that Sriman Madhvacarya was the third incarnation of Vayudeva. Vadiraja Svami, in verses 498 to 720 of the Phala-saurabha section of his book Yuktimallika, has presented this evidence.

Here we present only a few quotes form the Vedic literature as evidence that Sriman Madhvacarya was an incarnation of Vayudeva.

In the Rg Veda, all the sutras, beginning from the sixteenth varga of the seventh chapter of the sixth astaka, up to the end of the eighth chapter, and then the first five chapters of the seventh astaka, are known as Pavamana-sukta. This sukta begins with the words svadistayamadistaya. The word pavamana refers to Vayu, according to the Amarakosa dictionary.

The following are a selection of sutras from the Rg Veda;

*1. pradhara madhvo agriyo mahirapo vigahate harirhavisu vandyah*

**“The great personality, Madhvacarya, who was like an offering to Lord Sankarsana into the water of devastation at the time of annihilation, is worshipable by the demigods, being on the level of their spiritual master. He initiates the flow of transcendental knowledge and he is the bestower of ultimate liberation.”**

Another interpretation of this sutra: “Madhvacarya, who visited Badarikasrama, being invited by Vyasadeva, and who was worshiped as the spiritual master by innumerable disciples, plunged into the currents of the sacred River Ganges.”

*2. asmabhyaminda vidrayur madhvah parasya dharaya parjjanyo vrstimam iva*

**“O bestower of all auspiciousness! O Vayudeva! You help re-establish the forgotten relationship between the supremely opulent Lord Visnu and the pious living entities. Your name is Madhva. Like a cloud during the rainy season, you shower spiritual knowledge upon us and thereby purify our existence.”**

*3. sa purvyah pavate yam divaspari syeno mathaya disitas tirorajah sa madhva ayuvate vevijana iti krsanorastur manasa ha vibhyusa*

**“You are eternal and spread throughout the bodies of the living entities. You are situated in your original spiritual form, you are present in the spiritual world in the liberated state, and you are eternally subordinate to the all-blissful personality, Lord Visnu. You are dear to the devotees, you remove the dust of material contamination, and your presence is perceived by the movements of the trees.”**

Another meaning of this sutra: “You are guided by the all- blissful personality, Lord Visnu, and you are sought after by those who are pious. You are Vayu’s incarnation, Madhvacarya. You refuted all unauthorized philosophies born of the modes of passion and ignorance, and for the benefit of the devotees you have elaborated five distinctions between the Supreme Lord, the living entities, and the material world. Just as Sriman Madhvacarya brought peace to this world by dispelling all unauthorized philosophies, so at the time of the dissolution of the universe, Vayudeva will assume a formidable form and extinguish the blazing fire of annihilation.”

*4. unmadhva urmirvanana atisthadapo vasano mahisi vigahate raja pavitra ratho vajamaruhat sahasra bhrstir jayati sravo wrhat*

**“You have appeared within this world, and you are the most learned scholar and the foremost of all pious living entities. You are Madhvacarya. You propagate those Vedic literatures that help one obtain the lotus feet of Lord Visnu, the supremely worshipable Lord of lords. The all-attractive supreme personality, Lord Narayana, who carries the Sudarsana cakra, is the master of Madhvacarya. Lord Visnu is very dear to everyone, just like food grains.**

**“O Vayudeva, you are gloriously present within this world as Madhvacarya, engaged in the service of hearing the scriptures from the mouth of Srila Vyasadeva.”**

Madhvacarya’s hearing of the scriptures from Srila Vyasadeva in disciplic succession is most relishable, and it nourishes one’s devotional service, gives one complete satisfaction, and mitigates one’s hunger. When Madhvacarya heard the scriptures, Lord Visnu in the form of His Sudarsana cakra would remain present at that place. Thus, in the system of oral reception in disciplic succession, there is no possibility of unauthorized interpretation, or the influence of maya. When such bona fide hearing and chanting is in progress, the Supreme Lord comes with His Sudarsana cakra and remains present at that place in the form of transcendental sound. By hearing the Vedic wisdom in disciplic succession, all living entities achieve auspiciousness.

*5. saptasvasrrarusir vavasano vidvan madhva ujjabharadrse kam, antaryene antarikse puraja icchan vavrima vidvat pusanasya.*

**“Sriman Madhvacarya, who directly engages in the devotional service of supremely effulgent and blissful Lord Visnu, is the bestower of transcendental qualities that counteract mundane qualities, beginning with lust and anger. At the time of annihilation, he protected the seven bodies of knowledge—Rg, Yajuh, Sama, Atharva, Pancaratra, Purana and Mahabharata—for the welfare and enlightenment of all living entities. Before the creation of the material world, Madhvacarya received transcendental knowledge in the spiritual sky after attaining the mercy of Lord Visnu, who is full of six transcendental opulences, and in turn imparted that knowledge into the hearts of saintly persons.”**

*6. vistambho divo dharunah prthivyah visva utaksitayo haste asya, asatta usto grnate niyutvan madhvo amsuh pavate indriyaya.*

**“O Vayudeva, you are the support of the heavenly planets, the sustainer of the earth, always eager to glorify the Supreme Lord, and always engaged in the service of Lord Hari. Madhvacarya was an expansion of your original form. Madhva offered prayers to the Supreme Lord, who is beyond the understanding of the intelligence of those who are sinful. He moves within the bodies of all living entities, inducing their senses to serve the Supreme Lord, the master of the senses, and he travels from place to place in order to inspire those who are pious to glorify the Supreme Lord. Sri Madhvacarya is the spiritual master of the whole world.”**

*7. simham nasanta madhvo ayasam hari marusam divo asya patim, suro yutsuh prathamah prcchate ga asya dhaksasa paripatyuksa.*

**“The foremost of human beings, Sri Madhvacarya, a valiant hero in the art of debate, learned the Vedic conclusions from Lord Nrsimhadeva, the deliverer of transcendental knowledge to the pious souls, who is very compassionate to His devotees, who appeared from a pillar, whose nostrils are flared, and who is the annihilator of the demons. Having received the mercy of Lord Nrsimhadeva, Madhvacarya strengthened the realizations of saintly persons by preaching the knowledge he had received. ”**

*8. idam te patram sanavittam indra pivasomamena satakrato, puma ahavo madirasya madhvo yam visva idabhi harsanti devah*

**“O unlimitedly knowledgeable and supremely opulent Lord, Madhva, who was empowered with detachment, transcendental knowledge, devotional service, and a charitable attitude, has a heart that is a fit place for Your residence. Please drink the soma beverage offered by Madhva, who was enriched with complete knowledge of the Vedas. He instructed spiritual knowledge to the devotees. In order to relish the mellows of transcendental knowledge, those who are actually pious take shelter of him.”**

*9. madhvo vo nama marutam yajatrah prajnesu savasa madanti, ye rejayanti rodasi cidurvi pinvanttyat sam yadaya surugra*

**“In the Marat-sukta the demigods offer prayers to Vayu’s incarnation as follows: ‘O incarnations of powerful Vayudeva, now that you have appeared in the material world, kindly shower your mercy upon those who are enthusiastic for your service, and give them all protection. The incarnations of Vayu are present both in the heavenly planets and on the earth. Madhva is one of those incarnations. The devotees satisfy Madhvacarya, the incarnation of the principle Vayu, by offering him prayers, and his followers become very joyful by studying his explanations, which are called brahmana khandartha, presented in his book, Karma-nirnaya. ”**

*10. tadasya priyamabhipatho asyam naro yatra devayavo madanti, urukramasya sahi bandhurittha visnoh parame madhva utsah*

**“I, who have attained a human birth, wish to drink the water that has washed the lotus feet of Lord Trivikrama Visnu, who is very dear to the devotees. Even the demigods headed by Brahma felt joyful by accepting the spiritual water that penetrated the coverings of the universe when Lord Vamana stretched His lotus feet. Madhvacarya, who is very eager to serve the lotus feet of Lord Visnu, is very dear to Lord Trivikrama, as are the demigods headed by Brahma.**

*11. balittha tad vapuse dhayi darsatam devasya bhargah sahaso yato jani, yadim upahurate sadhate mati rtasya dhena anayanta sasrutah*

**“Just as Pradhana Vayu, or Mukhya Prana, who emanated from Lord Visnu, is endowed with the strength of transcendental knowledge, as well as physical strength, so the incarnation of Vayudeva possesses great strength of knowledge, as well as bodily strength. In other words, those qualities possessed by the source of incarnations are to be found in the incarnations as well.”**

By this statement, Sri Hanuman, the first incarnation of Vayu, is glorified. Hanuman is particularly wise among the soldiers of Lord Rama. He always remains very humbly by the side of Lord Ramacandra, assisting Him in His mission. It was Hanuman who conveyed Lord Ramacandra’s nectarean words that nourish the devotees, to Sita.

*12. prtkso vapuh pitumannitya asaye dvitiyama sapta sivasu matrsu*

In this verse Vayu’s second incarnation, Bhimasena, is glorified.

**“Bhimasena, who destroyed the Kaurava soldiers, is the second incarnation of Vayu. He was a voracious eater, but eternally knowledgeable. He propagates the seven kinds of Vedic knowledge that award all auspiciousness.”**

*13. trtiyam asya rsabhasya dohase dasa pramatim janayanta yosanah, niryadim budhnan mahisasya varpasa isanasah savasa kranta surayah, yadimanu pradivo madhva adhave guhasantam matarisva mathayati*

In this verse Vayu’s third incarnation, Madhvacarya, is glorified.

**"Sri Durgasakti, who is the embodiment of the Vedas, arranged for the advent of Purnaprajna so that he could propagate transcendental knowledge in this world. In this way, the glories of the appearance of Purnaprajna, the third incarnation of Vayu, is sung in the Vedas. The demigods, headed by Siva, heard about the transcendental qualities of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Visnu, from Madhvacarya with utmost humility, honest inquiry, and a service attitude. Madhvacarya, an incarnation of Vayu, preached to establish the supremacy of Lord Visnu, who is situated within the hearts of all living entities, after collecting evidence from the Vedic literature.**

It is said in the *Vayu Purana*:

*vayor divyani rupani padmatraya yutani ca*  
*trikoti murttir sanyuktas tretayam raksantakah*  
*hanuman iti vikhyato rama karya dhurandharah*  
*sa vayur bhimasenobhud dvaparante kurudvahah*  
*krsnam sampujayamasa hatva duryodhanadikan*  
*dvaipayanasya sevartham vadaryam tu kalau yuge*

*vayusca yatirupena krtva duhsastra khandanam tatah kaliyuge prapte trtiyo madhva namakah bhurekha daksinebhage manimad garva santaye dhikurvan tatprabham sadyo vatirnotra dvijanvaye*

**“The principal manifestation of Vayu, whose form is fully transcendental, appears like three lotus flowers. The first incarnation of Vayu was Hanuman, who, in Treta-yuga, was the servant of Lord Rama, the destroyer of the demoniac family that governed thirty million followers. Vayudeva next appeared in the Kuru dynasty at the end of Dvapara-yuga as Bhimasena. He killed sinful persons, headed by Duryodhana, and worshiped Lord Krsna with great devotion.**

**“Thereafter, when the Kali-yuga commenced, the third incarnation of Vayu, known as Madhva, appeared in the Sivalli family of South India, and then visited Badarikasrama as a sannyasi. He served Srila Vyasadeva by refuting the unauthorized scriptures and philosophies that had sprouted in Kali-yuga. To destroy the pride of the demon Maniman and minimize his genius, Madhva appeared in Kali-yuga as the third incarnation of Vayu.”**

## Sri Madhva’s Siddhanta in Brief

*sriman-madhva-mate harih paratarah satyam jagat tattvato bhedo jivaganah hareranuchara nicoccha bhavam gatah*  
*muktirnaija sukhanubhutir amala bhaktisca tatsadhanam hyaksadi tritayam pramanam akhilamnayaika vedyo harih*

In this verse the doctrine of Sriman Madhva is clearly and briefly explained. This verse is often quoted in the literature of the acaryas of Sri Madhva sampradaya as being the condensed form of Sri Madhva’s conclusions. Sri Jaya Tirthapada, an acarya of the Madhva Gaudiya sampradaya, has also quoted this verse in his literature. Sri Vadiraja Svami, who is accepted as the most distinguished scholar of the Tattvavada sampradaya has also quoted this verse in one of his books.

Some people say that this verse was written by Sri Madhva’s disciple, Srimad Trivikramacarya. The meaning of the verse is this: “According to the conclusion of Srimad Madhvacarya, Lord Visnu alone is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the material world is real, there are five kinds of eternal differences between the Supreme Lord, the living entities, and the material world, the living entities are Lord Hari’s servants, all living entities are distinguished by their qualifications, the manifestation of the living entities’ constitutional duties is known as mukti, and pure, or causeless, devotional service is the only process by which the living entities can revive their constitutional position. There are three types of evidence— that which is received by aural reception, hypothesis, and direct perception. Lord Hari is the only object of all Vedic knowledge. Therefore, realization of the Supreme Lord is possible only by cultivating the spiritual knowledge that comes down in disciplic succession.”

Madhva Gaudiya Vedantacarya Sripada Baladeva Vidyabhusana Prabhu included the following verse in his book Prameya Ratnavali:

*sri-madhvah praha visnum paratamam akhilamnaya-vedyam ca visvam satyam bhedam ca jivam hari-carana-jusas taratamyam ca tesam moksam visnv-anghri-labham tad-amala-bhajanam tasya hetum pramanam pratyaksadi-trayam cety upadisati harih krsna-caitanyacandrah*

According to Sri Madhva:

1\. Lord Visnu alone is the Supreme Absolute Truth.

2\. Lord Visnu is the object of all Vedic knowledge.

3\. The material world is real.

4\. The living entities are separate from Lord Visnu.

5\. The living entities are the servants of Lord Hari’s lotus feet.

6\. The living entities are of two kinds—the liberated living entities and the conditioned living entities.

7\. Attainment of Lord Visnu’s lotus feet alone is liberation for the conditioned souls.

8\. Pure worship of Lord Visnu is the cause of the conditioned souls’ liberation.

9\. Direct perception, hypothesis, and the Vedas are the three pramanas.

Lord Sri Krsna Caitanyacandra also taught these nine truths presented by Sri Madhva.

It is clearly understood from this statement of Madhva Gaudiya Vedantacarya Sripada Baladeva Vidyabhusana Prabhu that Lord Sri Krsna Caitanyadeva accepted the disciplic succession of Sriman Madhva. That is why Sri Gaudiya-sampradaya is known in the society of saintly persons as the Madhva-Gaudiya or Brahma-madhva-gaudiya-sampradaya.

It was the opinion of Sriman Madhvacarya that Lord Visnu alone is the Supreme Personality of Godhead. According to him (Tattvaviveka adi-sloka):

*svantatra paratantranca*  
*prameyam dvividham matam*  
*svatantro bhagavan visnor*  
*nirdosakhila sadgunah*

**“There are two types of truth—independent truth and dependent truth. Only Lord Visnu is a supremely independent personality. He is unlimited, faultless, and full of transcendental qualities. He is the reservoir of unlimited transcendental qualities. He is omnipotent, fully independent, the controller of all moving and non-moving living entities, His entire body, beginning from His toenails up to His hair, is full of transcendental bliss and knowledge. He is** ***sac-cid- ananda vigraha*****. There is no difference between His body and His soul. His names, forms, qualities, and pastimes are all transcendental. Indeed, there is no distinction between His names, forms, qualities, and pastimes. He is eternal, and the master of everyone, beginning from Brahma, Siva, and Laksmi. He is the controller of all controllers.”**

## According to Madhvacarya

(*Tattvaviveka Adi-sloka*):

*sarvatrakhila sacchaktih*  
*svatantro sesa darsanah*  
*nitya tadrsa cicceti*  
*antesto no ramapatih*

**“In all circumstances and at all times He is the energetic personality and the proprietor of all energies. He is supremely independent, the omniscient regulator of all moving and non- moving living beings. That husband of Rama is our worshipable Lord. ”**

In his *Gita-tatparya* (Ch. 2, Text 25) Madhvacarya wrote:

*matsa kurmadi rupanam*  
*gunanam harmanamapi*  
*tathaiva vayavananca*  
*bhedam pasyati yah kvacit*  
*bhedabhedau ca yah pasyet*  
*sa yati tama eva tu*  
*pasyed bhedame vaisam*  
*vabhusu purusastatah”*

**“Those who differentiate between the forms, qualities, pastimes, and characteristics of the incarnations of the Lord, such as Matsya and Kurma, will certainly be forced to enter the darkest regions of hell. Therefore, those who desire their own welfare always see Lord Visnu’s names, forms, qualities, and pastimes as non-different from one another.”**

In Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya (1.11) Madhvacarya wrote:

*nirdosa purna guna vigraha atmatantra*  
*nischetanatmaka sarira gunaisca hinah*  
*ananda matra kara pada mukhodaradih*  
*sarvatra ca svagata bheda vivarjitatma*

**“Lord Hari is devoid of faults. He is full of transcendental qualities and possesses a transcendental body. He is completely independent. His body and qualities are all spiritual, for there is no tinge of matter in His existence. He possesses a form with hands, legs, mouth, and stomach, and all His bodily limbs are manifestations of His blissful nature. He is the Supreme Absolute Truth.”**

In Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya (1.12) Madhvacarya wrote:

*kalacca desa guna tosya na cadiranto*  
*vrddhi ksayau na tu parasya sadatanasya*  
*naitradrsah kvaca babhuba na caiva bhabyo*  
*nastyattarah kimuparat paramasya visnoh*

**“Lord Hari is fully spiritual and eternal. His appearance, growth, and disappearance are not caused by the influence of material time, place, or circumstance. There has never been, nor will there ever be, nor is there at present any truth superior to Lord Visnu. Because there is no one comparable to Lord Visnu at present, nor was there in the past, nor will there be in the future, who could be superior to Him?”**

In Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya (1.13) Madhvacarya stated:

*sarvajna isvaratamah sa ca sarvasaktih*  
*purnavyatma balacit sukha virya sarah*  
*yasyajnaya rahita amindiraya sametam*  
*brahmesa purvakam idam na tu kasya cesam*

**“He is the controller of everything. By His independent will, Lord Visnu creates, maintains, and annihilates the material world. All these activities are His pastimes.”**

In his Anuvyakhyana (Ch. 1, section 1, sutra 2) Madhvacarya wrote:

*srsthih sthitisca sangharo*  
*niyatir jnanam avrttih*  
*bandha moksava pihyasu*  
*sruti yukta hareh sada*

**“It has been described in all these Vedic statements that everything—creation, maintenance, dissolution, the fate of the living entities, their knowledge, their coverings, their bondage, and their liberation—is made possible only by Lord Visnu.”**

Lord Visnu eternally exists as the origin of the living entities, all of whom are His separated parts and parcels. In the spiritual world, innumerable living entities are present in their original sac-cid-ananda forms. All of the conditioned souls, beginning from Brahma on down to the tiniest insects, possess original spiritual forms that are manifest in their constitutional positions. All of the individual living entities are eternal, full of knowledge and bliss, possessing varieties of forms that are pure reflections of Lord Visnu. The purport is that all kinds of variegatedness have emanated from Lord Visnu alone.

Lord Visnu manifests unlimited eternal forms in the spiritual worlds. The individual living entities are also present in Vaikuntha as reflections of the transcendental forms of the Supreme Lord. Had Lord Visnu not assumed various sac- cid-ananda forms, beginning from Brahma down to the most insignificant insect, there would have been no possibility of the living entities assuming any form. All animals, aquatics, trees and other creatures in Vaikuntha are pure living entities and are eternal, full of knowledge and bliss. They are pure reflections of the pure original object, Lord Visnu. The Mayavadis consider the living entities to be reflections of the Supreme Brahman, but with material designations.

Madhvacarya’s conclusion is distinct from that of the impersonalists, however. He says that in Vaikuntha there are varieties of living entities, including birds, deer, human beings, and trees. The Supreme Lord is always present in His pure spiritual form as the origin of all those varieties of living entities. There is a similarity between the form and quality of the pure Supreme Lord and individual living entities. The difference between the individual living entities and the Supreme Lord is that the living entities’ knowledge and bliss are partial whereas the knowledge and bliss of the Supreme Lord is full.

Whatever varieties of forms are in existence, including those of the demons, have their origin in the Supreme Lord. Nothing can exist unless it is in the Supreme Lord. Some living entities in the spiritual world have bodies resembling those of demons. Since their bodies are eternal, they are free from material designations. These bodies are made of the eternal mode of ignorance. The Supreme Lord is also the origin of these bodies of demons, but there is no tinge of ignorance in the Supreme Lord.

It is to be understood that at present the conditioned souls have received, and will continue to receive, varieties of bodies as results of their karma. All these gross bodies are not pure reflections of the Supreme Lord. At present a person may have a human body, but his constitutional body may be that of a monkey. A conditioned soul now having the body of a fish may have a transcendental human body in his eternal constitutional position. One can not presume someone’s eternal body by seeing his present gross body. The gross and subtle bodies of the conditioned souls are coverings of their original spiritual bodies.

The spiritual body is eternal and free from material designations. It can be of different shapes. That is why the pure living entities that are reflections of the Supreme Lord have been addressed as pure spirit souls. The source of all these pure living entities is the unlimitedly powerful sac-cid-ananda forms of the Supreme Lord. This is the conclusion of Madhvacarya, as stated in his book Paingi-srutih (Ch. 2, section 3, sutra 501)

*dvirupavamsakau tasya*  
*paramasya harerbibhoh*  
*prativimvamsakas catha*  
*svarupamsaka eva ca*  
*prativimvamsaka jiva*  
*pradurbhavah pare smrtah*  
*prativimvesu alpasamyam*  
*svarupanita ranitviti*  
*sopadhir anupadhisca*  
*prativimva dvidheyate*  
*jiva isyanupadhir indra*  
*capo yatha raventi*

**“The almighty supreme controller, Lord Hari, has two kinds of expanded forms, His reflected parts and parcels and His plenary portions. The innumerable individual living entities are His reflected parts and parcels and the incarnations, such as Matsya, are His plenary portions. The Supreme Lord Hari shares some similarities with the living entities that are His parts and parcels, as well as with the incarnations. The reflections are of two types—those that are materially designated and those that are completely pure. The living entities are pure reflections of the Supreme Lord, whereas a rainbow in the sky is a designated reflection of the sun, and is therefore temporary.”**

At the beginning of the day of Brahma, Lord Visnu, out of His own sweet will, manifests Himself as four different forms— Vasudeva, Sankarsana, Pradyumna and Aniruddha—for the purpose of creation. In the form of Pradyumna, the Supreme Lord creates the universe. The name of Pradyumna’s consort is Krti. In the form of Anirudha, the Supreme Lord maintains the universe. The name of Aniruddha’s consort is Santi. In the form of Sankarsana, the Supreme Lord annihilates the universe. The name of Sankarsana‘s consort is Jaya. In the form of Vasudeva, the Supreme Lord awards the living entities their goal of life. The name of Vasudeva’s consort is Maya.

In Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya (Ch. 1, texts 6-8), Madhvacarya has written:

*ittham vicintya param satu vasudeva*  
*nama vabhuva nijamukti pada pradata*  
*tasyajnayaiva niyatatha ramapi rupam*  
*vabhre dvitiyamapi yat pravadanti mayam*  
*sankarsanasca sa vabhuva punah*  
*sunityah sanhara karana vapustanujnayaiva*  
*devi jayetyanu vabhuva sa srsti hetoh*  
*pradyumnatam upagatah krtitanca devi*  
*sthityai punah sa bhagavan aniruddha nama*  
*devi ca santir abhavaccharadam sahasram*  
*sthitva svamurttir amubhir acintya saktih*  
*pradyumna rupaka imamscaramatmane dat*

**“I will manifest the spirit souls who are resting within my abdomen so that they can have the chance to realize their constitutional positions.’ Thinking like this, the Supreme Lord and supreme controller, Lord Hari, appeared in the form of Vasudeva, who awards liberation to His devotees. By His order, His consort, Rama-devi, also assumed a second form. Learned authorities address this consort of Vasudeva as Maya.**

**“Thereafter, the eternal Supreme Lord manifested His form as Sankarsana, which brings about the destruction of the universe. By His order, Laksmi-devi manifested as Krti. When the Supreme Lord, Visnu, appeared as Aniruddha, to maintain the universe, Laksml-devT appeared as Santi. In this way, the Supreme Lord remained for one thousand samvatsara years in the forms of Vasudeva, Sankarsana, Pradyumna, and Aniruddha. Thereafter, the inconceivably powerful Lord Pradyumna entrusted the aggregate of living entities to Aniruddha for their maintenance.”**

## Creation and Destruction

These two functions are carried out either by the demigods, who are empowered by Lord Visnu, or by a great soul who is accepted by the Lord as His representative. Lord Visnu in the form of Pradyumna invests the energy to create in four-headed Brahma, and Lord Visnu in the form of Sankarsana invests the energy to annihilate in Rudra. ’The Supreme Lord in His form as Aniruddha maintains the creation, and in His form of Vasudeva, He awards liberation.

These four forms of the Supreme Lord remain present in the material creation until the end of Brahma’s lifetime. The incarnations of the Supreme Lord, such as Matsya and Kurma, appear in this world from time to time, and then return to the non-manifested spiritual world. Lord Visnu, in twelve different forms, such as Kesava, and twelve other forms, such as Vasudeva, controls the twenty-four demigods that are in charge of the twenty-four elements.

As Vasudeva, Sankarsana, Pradyumna, Aniruddha, and Narayana, the Supreme Lord is the regulator of the five gross elements. In the form of the universe, power, wisdom, and transcendence, the Lord controls the living entities’ four states of existence—wakening, dream, deep sleep, and liberation. In the forms of the spirit soul and Supersoul, the Supreme Lord controls the gross body and the constitutional body, respectively. The Supreme Lord controls the living entities by remaining present in their bodies as the Supersoul. In this way He inspires both the demigods and the senses. Still, Lord Visnu is not responsible for the sins and pious acts of the conditioned souls, for these are products of the misuse of their minute independence.

The Supreme Lord is the director and the living entities are directed. As stated in the Bhavisya Purana:

*punya papadiham visnuh*  
*karayet purva karmana*  
*anaditvat karmanasca*  
*na virodha kathancana*

This is confirmed by Caturveda-sikha, Sutrabhasya, (Ch. 2, section one, sutras 36 and 37):

*na karayet puny am athapi papam*  
*na tavata dosa vanisitapi*  
*iso yato guna dosadi sattve*  
*svayam paromadiradih prajanam*

**“In the character of the Lord Visnu there is no fault of hatred because He allows the living entities to perform pious and sinful activities as reactions to their previous karma. Since the Supreme Lord inspires the living entities to act sinfully or piously according to the mentality generated by their perpetual karma, there is no fault of unfair discrimination or hatred in Him. Lord Visnu helps the living entities engage in sinful or pious activities according to their desires, for He has endowed them with minute independence. Therefore Lord Visnu should never be considered guilty, because He is the master of all qualities and faults. His supremacy and greatness is not dependent upon others. He is beginningless and the origin of all living entities.”**

When Lord Visnu incarnates in various forms, such as Matsya and Kurma, or even in human-like forms, His forms, birth, and activities are not products of matter, Still, to the mundane perception of ignorant people, He appears like a pious conditioned soul. Lord Visnu is naturally devoid of any kind of fault, although He deceives foolish and demoniac people in this way. The Supreme Lord sends those who disregard Him without understanding His true spiritual position and therefore blaspheme the process of devotional service to the darkest regions of hell. And He awards those who serve Him without duplicity, and who take shelter of Him and worship Him with devotion, the supreme goal of life.

The Lord allows those who are between these two extremes, who neither oppose the Lord nor engage in His devotional service, but simply perform fruitive activities, to live in the material world perpetually. By nature these people are eternally bound to materialistic life.

The Supreme Lord manifests His transcendental pastimes just to please His devotees. By such pastimes, the Lord facilitates the devotional service of the devotees, while opposing those who are envious. All the incarnations of Lord Visnu can be grouped into two classes—jndndvatdra and balavatara. In some incarnations the Supreme Lord preaches spiritual knowledge, and these are called jnandvataras, and include Vyasadeva, Kapiladeva, and Dattatreya. In some incarnations the Supreme Lord simply exhibits His incomparable prowess, and these are called balavatara, and include Nrsimha, Vamana, and Ramacandra.

Because He is the origin of all incarnations, Lord Krsna possesses the qualities of the both categories and is therefore known as ubhayavatara, or the incarnation that encompasses both.

In his Mahabharata-tatparya nirnaya (ch. 2 text 25), Madhvacarya has written:

*krsnardmadi rupesu*  
*balakaryo janardanah*  
*data vyasadi rupesu*  
*jnanakdryas tatha prabhuh*

**“Janardana Lord Hari manifests His supreme prowess in the forms of Lord Krsna and Rama, and propagates transcendental knowledge in the forms of Vyasadeva and Dattatreya.”**

In incarnations like Dhanvantari the Supreme Lord simply displays His compassion upon His devotees. The eternal abode of Lord Visnu is Vaikuntha. In the beginning of creation, two abodes—Svetadvlpa and Anantasana—manifest in this world. The fourteen worlds are surrounded by seven coverings—the five gross elements, the mahat-tattva, and ahankara. Beyond these coverings is the eternal abode of Vaikuntha. The entire spiritual world of Vaikuntha is surrounded by the River Viraja.

The abode of Svetadvlpa is located in the middle of the universe and the abode of Anantasana is at the bottom. The Supreme Lord resides in Vaikuntha, along with His liberated associates. Lord Visnu’s ornaments, garments, decorations, bed, and throne are expansions of Laksmi. In each of the abodes of Vaikuntha, Svetadvlpa, and Anantasana, there are two sections—Muktasthana and Amuktasthana. In the first section the liberated associates reside, and in the second section the conditioned associates reside. Everyone in the latter section remains there until the end of Brahma’s life. When the lifetime of Brahma is over, everyone goes to Muktasthana, along with Brahma. In other words, all living entities attain liberation along with Brahma.

## The Conclusion Regarding Laksmi

Laksmi-devi is the very dear queen of Lord Visnu. She possesses an eternal body that is full of knowledge and bliss. Like Lord Visnu, she is transcendental to material conditions, such as being forced to live within the womb of a mother. She is always present with Lord Visnu. She is all-pervading and enjoys the association of Lord Visnu in His unlimited varieties of forms.

Whenever Lord Visnu incarnates, Laksmi accompanies Him to enjoy pastimes with Him. Like Lord Visnu, Laksmi has eternal names and forms, including Sri, Bhu, Durga, Maya, Jaya, Krti, Santi, AmbhranI, Sita, Daksina, andjayanti. Laksmi- devi is the controller of the three modes of material nature in her forms of Sri, Bhu, and Durga. In the form of Sri she enhances the mode of goodness and deceives the demigods. In the form of Bhu she enhances the mode of passion and bewilders human beings, and in the form of Durga she enhances the mode of ignorance and bewilders the demons.

Laksmi-devi is under the control of Lord Visnu and she superintends all departments of knowledge. She is far superior to four-headed Brahma. **Sri Madhvacarya’s Conclusion that the Material World is Real**

Sriman Madhvacarya says that in the beginning of creation, Lord Visnu, by employing His eternal material nature as ingredients, manifests the three modes of material nature, the mahat-tattva, ahankara, the five gross elements, subtle elements, the universes, the fourteen planetary systems, oceans, mountains, deserts, hills, the Ganges, Yamuna, and other rivers, rocks, vegetation including medicinal herbs, food grains, fruit, flowers, the nine precious jewels, gold, iron, and so on. AlI these objects are temporary as the effects, but eternal as the cause. Although these are temporary as effects, they are not unreal like the horn of a hare, flowers in the sky, the hair of a tortoise, or a city of the Gandharvas seen in the sky. They are not false, like accepting a rope as a snake or an oyster as silver. Because they are short-lived, they are called temporary, but they are neither unreal nor false.

Although objects such as pots are temporary, in the form of the cause they are eternal. The followers of Buddhism do not accept this conception. For example, suppose a clay pot is broken into two pieces, then the two pieces are broken into four pieces, and then they are smashed into powder and merged with the soil of the earth. In this way, beginning from the pot up to the state of merging, all these situations are effects. They are temporary, but in the form of the ingredient cause that became the pot and then powder, they are eternal.

Beginning from the time of creation, innumerable objects are manifested by the combined effort of cause and effect, and at the end of a kalpa, they remain in a subtle form known as prakrti, and so are not false. The Mayavadis say that when one’s knowledge of impersonal Brahman becomes mature, the material world naturally vanishes, like a drop of water placed on a red-hot sheet of iron. But this is only the imagination of a child.

Lord Visnu purposefully creates this universe as a manifestation of His pastimes. Thereafter, He intentionally annihilates it. At that time, the material world remains in the form of its cause? The material world created by the wisdom of Lord Visnu is not an element of *maya*. According to the destinies of the living entities, the Supreme Lord creates the universe in various ways. When the destinies of the living entities have been sufficiently awarded, He annihilates the universe. At that time, the universe remains present in the form of its cause. While creating the universe, there is a sequence of cause and effect, and when the universe is wound up, that sequence proceeds in reverse order. To substantiate all this, Sriman Madhvacarya has given various logical explanations, arguments, and evidence from the scriptures in his literature.

Regarding Sriman Madhvacarya’s conclusion about the reality of the material world, we herein present some of the evidence from the *Vedas* and *Puranas* that he quoted. In his *Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya* (Ch. 1, Text 69), Sriman Madhvacarya wrote:

Pious living entities address Lord Visnu as Satyakarma because the Lord created this world as factual. Some persons also call Him Nityakarma, because He is constantly engaged in the act of creation.

## Five Categories Of Distinction

Sriman Madhvacarya accepted five kinds of distinction.

1\. The distinction between the living entities and the Supreme Lord.

2\. The distinction between one living entity and another.

3\. The distinction between the Supreme Lord and the material world.

4\. The distinction between the living entities and the material world, and

5\. The distinction between the different objects of the material world.'

In this connection, Sri Acaryapada has written in his *Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya* (verses 70 and 71 of the first chapter):

*jivesayorbhida caiva*  
*jivabhedah parasparam*  
*jadesayor jadanam ca*  
*jada jiva bhida tath*  
*pancabheda ime nityah*  
*saruavasthasu nityasah*  
*muktananca na hlyante*  
*taratamyam ca sarvada*

## 1. The distinction between the living entities and the Supreme Lord.

Lord Visnu is eternally distinct from the individual living entities. “When materially designated, the Supreme Brahman appears as the living entities.” This is an unauthorized opinion. Brahman is supreme and unlimited, whereas the individual living entities are infinitesimal. Brahman is free of all faults, and the conditioned living entities are full of faults. Brahman possesses transcendental qualities to the fullest degree, whereas the individual living entities can possess transcendental qualities only to a certain extent. Brahman is eternally liberated, whereas the conditioned living entities are entrapped in the cycle of repeated birth and death.

Considering all this, there is no scope for attributing simple oneness to the individual living entities and the Supreme Lord. Even in the liberated state there is an eternal distinction between the living entities and the Supreme Lord. Even when liberated, the individual living entities remain distinct individuals that engage in the eternal service of Lord Visnu.

## 2. The Distinction Among Living Entities

**A. The Conditioned Living Entities**

In the material world, some people are happy and some are unhappy. Some are poor and some are rich. Thus each individual living entity suffers and enjoys the results of his fruitive activities.

**B. The Liberated Living Entities**

After liberation, all living entities enter the one transcendental realm, where they engage in a variety of services to Lord Visnu, according to their qualifications. Thus there are distinctions in the various services and feelings of happiness of the liberated souls.

However, sometimes in the *sastra* a statement like sarve ekibhavanti is found. The actual purport of such a statement is not oneness in all respects. For example, if it were said that in the evening, all the cows entered the cowshed, the word “entered” would not indicate complete oneness. Rather, it would indicate a gathering at one place. This understanding should be applied to the condition of liberated souls.

Or, if it were said that a royal family, although previously quarreling, had now become one, this would not indicate total oneness. Indeed, to imagine such a meaning would be the height of foolishness. It should simply be understood that the members of the royal family have reached an agreement. In the same way, the liberated living entities engaged in the devotional service of Lord Visnu while maintaining their individuality.

## 3. The Distinction Between the Supreme Lord and the Material World

The Supreme Lord is the embodiment of knowledge, and He is eternal and unchangeable. On the other hand, matter is inert and thus devoid of knowledge, and is perishable and changeable. Objects with such contradictory qualities can never be one and the same.

## 4. The Distinction Between the Living Entities and the Material World

The living entities are conscious entities. Therefore, dead matter, which is without consciousness, cannot be identical with the living entities.

## 5. The Distinction Between Products of Matter

Poison causes death, whereas nectar awards life. Poison is bitter, whereas sugar is sweet. Such contradictory natures are found in material objects. Therefore, objects with opposing qualities can never be considered one and the same.

These five distinctions are eternal—at all times and at all places. Even if the opposing qualities are lost, the distinction is not lost. For example, a clay pot may be smashed to pieces, but even though it loses its gross form, it remains clay.

These distinctions are factual and eternal. If there were an origin of these distinctions, then there would also be an end to them. However, these distinctions never disappear. These distinctions are not imaginary. Otherwise, such imagination would one day be dispelled. Therefore, only ignorant people conclude that there are ultimately no distinctions.”**

## Sri Madhva’s Siddhanta Regarding the Living Entities

The living entities are eternal servants of Lord Hari. According to Sriman Madhva’s *siddhdnta*, there are two kinds of truth. 1. Independent truth and 2. Dependent truth. Lord Visnu is independent truth and dependent truth is further divided into two classes—*bhava* and *abhava*. *Bhava* is also of two kinds. 1. Consciousness, or spirit, and 2. Dead matter. *Abhava* is of jour kinds. 1. *Pragabhava* 2. *Pradhvamsabhava* 3. *Atyantabhava* and 4. *Anyonydbhava*.

If someone says, “Tomorrow we will make a pot,” this is an example of *pragabhava*. When the pot has been destroyed, this is an example of *pradhvamsabhava*, because it no longer exists. That which has no existence in the past, present or future is called *atyantabhava*. Examples of this are the horn of a hare, the hair of a tortoise, and so on.

Consciousness, or spirit, is further divided into three categories—*sattvika*, *rajasa*, and *tamasa*. Dead matter is of many varieties. Countless conditioned souls remain within the abdomen of Lord Visnu when the cosmic manifestation is unmanifest after annihilation, and they are divided into these categories.

In *Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya* (Ch. 1, Text 4), Sriman Madhvacarya wrote:

*drstva sa cetanagnnan jathare sayanan anandamatra vapusah sati vipramuktan dhyanamgatan srtigatamsca susupti samsthana brahmadikan kaliparan manujams tathaiksat*

**“After the dissolution of the universe, countless living entities remain within the abdomen of Lord Visnu. These conditioned living entities are divided into three classes.**

**1. The demigods headed by Brahma.**

**2. Human beings.**

**3. Demons.”**

The conditioned living entities are entangled in the wheel of *karma* since time immemorial and are therefore full of ignorance. The liying entities in the mode of goodness are eligible for liberation, those in the mode of passion are fit to live in the world of birth and death, and those in the mode of ignorance are to remain in darkness.

The demigods, headed by Brahma and Indra, the Gandharvas, the sages, the forefathers, the fourteen Manus, the eight Vasus, kings, and pious human beings are *sattvika* jivas. Fallen human beings, who are fruitive workers, are *rajasika* *jivas*. Kali, Kalanemi, Jarasandha, Madhu-Kaitabha, Sambara, Vrtra, Tripura, Kalakeya, Paulama, the Raksasas and the Danavas are tamasika jivas.

The forms of the *sattvika jivas* are full of knowledge and bliss, and the forms of the *rajasika jivas* are a mixture of knowledge and ignorance, as well as happiness and distress. The forms of the tamasika jivas are full of ignorance and distress.

The constitutional qualities of the *sattvika* living entities are truthfulness, purity, compassion, self-control, peacefulness, detachment, and devotion to God. In the constitution of the *rajasika* living entities both religiosity and irreligiosity are present. The qualities of the *tamasika* living entities are falsehood, cruelty, impurity, pride, restlessness, attachment to sense gratification, and adversity toward the spiritual master, Vaisnavas, and Lord Hari.

In *Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya* (Ch.1, Texts 87 to 92), Sri Madhva states that the three classes of conditioned souls are eligible for three destinations.

*trividha jiva sanghastu*  
*deva manusa danavah*  
*tatra deva mukti yogya*  
*manusesu uttamas tath*  
*madhyama manusa ye tu*  
*sati yogyah sadaivahi*  
*adhama niryayaiva*  
*danavastu tamolayah*  
*muktir nitya tamaiscaiva*  
*navrtti punaretayoh*  
*devanam nirayo nasti*  
*tamascapi kathancana*  
*nasuranam tatha muktih*  
*kadacit kenacit kvacit*  
*manusanam madhyamanam*  
*naivaitad dvayamapyate*  
*asurdnam tamah praptis*  
*tada niyamato bhavet*

**“The conditioned living entities are divided into three classes—the demigods, human beings, and demons. The demigods and pious human beings are eligible for liberation. Human beings that are neither very pious nor very sinful are destined to continue living in the material world, and those who are very fallen and wretched are eligible to go to hell. The demons are destined to go to the hell called Andhatanisra.**

**Liberation and hellish conditions of life are both eternal. They are not created again and again. The demigods never go to hell, or come under the influence of ignorance. Similarly, the demons never attain liberation at any time or under any circumstance. Human beings that are neither very pious nor very sinful neither attain liberation nor go to hell. Therefore the destination of Andhatanisra hell is only for those who are demoniac.”**

*gunaika matra rupastu*  
*deva, eva sadd matah*

**“The demons possess only faults as their eternal natural propensity.** ***Rajasika*** **human beings naturally possess faults and qualities simultaneously. The demigods and saintly persons who worship the Supreme Lord are full of eternal natural good qualities.”**

## Sri Madhvacarya’s Siddhanta Regarding the Constitutional Position of the Living Entities

In his *Brahma-sutra-bhasya* (Ch. 2-3, sutra 32), Sri Madhvacarya wrote:

*nityananda jnana bala*  
*deva naivam tu danavah*  
*duhkhopalabdhi matraste*  
*manusastu bhayatmakah*  
*tesam yadanyatha drsam*  
*tadudhikrtam matam*  
*vijnanera atma yogyena*  
*nijarupe vyavasthitih*  
*samyag jnanantu devanam*  
*manusyanam vimisritam*  
*viparitantu daityanam*  
*jndnsaivam vyavasthitih*

**“The demigods are endowed with eternal bliss, eternal knowledge, and eternal strength, but the demons are not empowered in this manner. They simply suffer various miseries. Human beings are generally fearful, although there are some exceptions. Sometimes a person’s natural tendency is changed as a result of a benediction or curse.**

**“By cultivating the science of self-realization, everyone is capable of regaining their original constitutional position. The knowledge of the demigods is perfect, the knowledge of human beings is mixed, and the knowledge of the demons is perverse.**

The spiritual body of every conditioned soul is covered by a subtle body, which is eternal.

**“The subtle body is covered by a gross material body that is called** ***karmadeha*****. These gross material bodies are manifested by Lord Aniruddha in every** ***kalpa*****. According to the last deeds that they had performed in the previous** ***kalpa*****, the conditioned living entities are awarded material bodies in the next** ***kalpa*** **by the Supreme Lord. Between the** ***kalpas*****, the conditioned living entities remain within the abdomen of the Supreme Lord as He lies in mystic slumber, and when they are again manifested at the beginning of the next** ***kalpa*** **they are awarded different bodies according to their previous** ***karma*****. Thus even when the conditioned souls are unmanifest, without possessing gross bpdies, the results of their fruitive activities remain with them.”**

In his *Mahabharata-tataparya-nirnaya* (Ch. 2, Text 9), Sri Madhvacarya wrote:

*nirdehakan sa bhagavan aniruddha nama*  
*jivan svakarma sahitanudare nivesya.*  
*cakretha deha-sahitan kramasah svayambhu*  
*pranatmasesa garudesa mukhan samagran*

**Lord Aniruddha creates the living entities’** ***karmadeha*** **so that they can perform activities in every** ***kalpa*****. According to their respective desires for karma, Lord Aniruddha withdraws the living entities within Himself at the time of the dissolution of the universe, and then, at the beginning of the next creation, He awards them suitable bodies, beginning with Brahma, Vayu, Sesa, and Garuda.”**

## Sri Madhvacarya’s Siddhanta Regarding the Number of Living Entities

In his *Visnu-tattva-nirnaya*, Sri Madhvacarya wrote:

*anagata atitasca yavantah sahitah krsna*  
*atitanagatascaiva yavantah paramanavah*  
*tatopyananta gunita jivanam rasayah prthak*  
*paramanu pradesepi hynantah prani rasayah*  
*suksmatvadisa saktaiva sthula api hy sansthitah*

**"The living entities are unlimited in number—more than all the atoms of the universe, and more than all the fractions of seconds of the past, present, and future. Even within the atoms there are an unlimited number of living entities. Although they have gross bodies, by the inconceivable power of the Supreme Lord, they are able to remain in that condition."**

## Sri Madhvacarya's Siddhanta Regarding the Living Entities' Bondage to the Law of Karma

In his *Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya* (Ch. 32, Texts 110, 78, and 85), Sri Madhvacarya wrote:

*brahmaparoksepi vikarma sucakam*  
*prarabdha papasya visasanam*  
*prarabdha karmanase hy*  
*pateddehopya papinah*  
*nahi papaphalam muktau*  
*dehapata kathancana*  
*kintu karmaksayadeva*  
*tatha sarvatra niscitah*

**”Just as when a person drinks poison, it indicates the fructification of his previous** ***karma*****, so despite having direct knowledge of the Supreme Lord, when one engages in sinful activities, it is an indication that his** ***karma*** **has fructified. When unfructified karma is exhausted, so that one becomes sinless, that person still has to give up his gross body at the time of death. Thus, at the time of liberation, one gives up his body not as a result of sin, but because of the exhaustion of his** ***karma*****. This is a fact.”**

In his *Bhagavata-tatparya-nirnaya* (2.9-33), Sri Madhvacarya wrote:

*vidyante hy tada jivah*  
*kala karmadikarh tatha*  
*kvanyatha hy punah srstih*  
*purva karmanusani*

**“The living entities are created according to their previous karma. Even before the creation of the living entities, the time factor, the results of activities, and other causes are present. Otherwise, how could there be creation once again? Therefore, the conclusion is that creation takes place simply as a result of previous fruitive activities.”**

## Sri Madhvacarya’s Siddhanta Regarding the Nature and Qualifications of the Living Entities

In his *Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya* (Ch. 22, Texts 84 to 86), Sri Madhvacarya wrote:

*jivanam svabhava yogyatadikam*  
*svabhavdkhya yogataya hathakhya*  
*yonadi siddha sarvajivesu nitya*  
*sakaranam prathamantu*  
*dvitiyam anadi karmaiva*  
*tatha trtiyah jiva prayatnah*  
*paurusakhyas tadetat trayam*  
*visnor vasagam saruadaiva*  
*hathascasau taratamyasthito hy*  
*brahmanam drabhya kalisca yavat.*

**“Nature and qualification are eternally present in all living entities. They are the primary causes of all their endeavors. Although** ***karma*** **is perishable, the flow of karma has been continuing since time immemorial. Beginningless previous** ***karma*** **is the second cause of all endeavors. The immediate endeavor itself is the third cause. All these causes are under the control of the supremely independent Lord Visnu, who is the master of** ***maya*****. In other words, by employing these three causes, the Supreme Lord awards the living entities their respective destinations. The individual living entities have no control over the Supreme Lord, who is the proprietor of maya. The qualifications of the living entities are proportionately present in the topmost living entity, Brahma, down to the mos’ degraded living entity, Kali.”**

If someone asks, “When there are unlimited living entities within the abdomen of the Supreme Lord, then why have only a handful of us been created? Why haven’t the others been created?”

The answer is that only those living entities that are eligible for entering the next creation as a result of their previous karma are placed within the creation. The *karma deha* of the living entities within the abdomen of the Lord is the second covering of their spiritual bodies. The living entities have their original spiritual bodies, and these are covered by the subtle body, and then the *karma deha*. Thus all conditioned living entities possess three kinds of bodies—the spiritual body, subtle body, and gross body. The spirit soul is eternal, full of knowledge and bliss, but in conditional life it accepts various temporary forms.

The difference between presently visible gross body (*karma deha*) and the *karma deha* of the living entities within the Lord’s abdomen is that the *karma deha* within the Lord’s abdomen is extremely subtle, whereas the material body visible in this manifested world is gross. When the living entity’s gross body is destroyed, he moves to heaven or hell, according to his *karma*, with the help of his subtle body. In the course of his transfer from one body to another he gets temporary bodies called yatana deha to enjoy his destined happiness and distress.

Undi one’s subtle body is dismantled, one can not revive his original constitutional position. Just to help them dismantle their subtle bodies, the Supreme Lord sends the living entities into the creation. The living entities are freed from their subtle bodies at the end of each *kalpa*, along with Brahma, by worshiping the spiritual master, by cultivating knowledge of the scriptures, and by executing devotional service to the Supreme Lord.

Those who are influenced by the mode of passion can also be freed from their subtle bodies at the end of each *kalpa* by performing their prescribed duties and avoiding prohibited activities while experiencing a mixture of happiness and distress. People in the mode of ignorance engage in lusty, prohibited, abominable activities that harass Lord Hari, the spiritual master, and the Vaisnavas, thus experiencing only distress and ignorance, before finally being freed from their subtle bodies.

In his *Bhagavata-tatparya* (2.2.30), Sri Madhvacarya wrote:

*ajnayaiva hareh kecid*  
*purtteh kecidanjasa*  
*vihrtaivanya lokesu*  
*mucyante brahmana saha*

**“Some persons, after perfecting their sadhana, go to another planet on the order of Lord Hari, and some persons are transferred to another planet because they were unable to attain perfection. But ultimately all of them are liberated along with Brahma.”**

In his *Brahma-sutra-bhasya* (Ch. 4, Part 3, Texts 10 and 11),

Sri Madhvacarya wrote:

*teha brahmanam abhisaripadya yadaitad*  
*viliyatetha saha bramana param abhigacchanti*  
*iti soparna srute maha pralaye tadadhyaksena*  
*brahmana saha gacchanti. brahma saha te sarve sanprapte prati sancare parsyante paratmanah pravisanti param padam.*

**‘“At the time of dissolution, all the living entities approach Brahma and then ascend to the abode of Lord Visnu along with him.’ According to this statement of** ***sauparna sruti*****, all living entities attain Lord Visnu at the time of the final dissolution of the universe, along with the creator, Brahma. At the end of four-headed Brahma’s life, all living entities attain the supreme destination, along with Brahma.**

According to *Brahma-sutra-bhasya* (Ch. 4, Section 4, *sutra* 19), the liberated living entities also travel to various places.

*atmetyeva param devam*  
*upasya harim avyayam*  
*kecid atraiva mucyante*  
*notkramanti kadacana*  
*kecit svarge maharaloke*  
*jane tapasi capare*  
*kecit satye mahajnana*  
*gacchanti ksirasagaram*  
*tatrapi karma yogena*  
*jnanadhikyat samipagah*  
*salokyam ca svarupatvam*  
*samipyam yoga eva ca*  
*imam arabhya sarvatra*  
*yavat su ksira sdgarae*

Some people attain the state of liberation in this world by worshiping the infallible Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Hari, with unalloyed devotion. They never fall down again. Some attain the state of liberation in Svargaloka, some in Maharloka, some in Janaloka, some in Tapolaka, and some in Satyaloka. Those who are highly advanced in understanding transcendental knowledge go to the ocean of milk? There, according to their advancement in spiritual knowledge, they gradually attain proximity to the Supreme Lord. Those who travel from the earth to the ocean of milk attain the four kinds of liberation—*salokya, samipya, sarupya* and *sajujya*—in an equal proportion.

In his Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya (Ch. 1, Texts 136 and 137 and Ch 32, Text 129), Sri Madhvacarya wrote:

*asurah kalir paryanta*  
*evam duhkhottora uttrah*  
*kalir duhkhyadhikas tesu*  
*tepepyevambrahmavada ganah*  
*talhanyepya surah sarue*  
*gana yogyataya sada*  
*brahmaiva tesamiha taratamyam*  
*kaleh param duhkham ihakhilacca*  
*yatha virincasya varam sukham syat*  
*muktau haridvesa krto visesah*

**“The superior the demigods are, the better facility for enjoyment they receive. Similarly, the more wretched the demons are, the more they suffer. Among all demons, Kali is the most miserable. As there are followers of the various demigods, beginning with Brahma, there are also followers of the various demons, like Kali. Other demons, such as Kalanemi and Vipracitti also have their followers, according to their qualifications. There are also varying degrees of suffering experienced by the demons, who must enter the darkest regions of hell. As Brahma, being the most qualified, enjoys the most happiness, so demons, like Kali suffer more than the others because of their degraded mentality. The primary cause of all varieties of happiness and distress is one’s inclination toward, or envy of, Lord Hari.”**

Among the *sattvika* living entities, four-headed Brahma is the foremost. Below him is Sarasvati, then Sesa, then demigods such as Garuda, then the sages, then the forefathers,

then the emperors of the earth, and then the best of human beings. There is not much information about the order of *rajasika* living entities. Among the *tamasika* living entities, Kali is the foremost. As Brahma is the most advanced among the *sattvika* living entities, Kali is the most degraded among the *tamasika* living entities. Below Kali is Kalanemi, then Jarasandha, and so on.

There are more *rajasika* living entities than *sattvika* living entities. The number of *tamasika* living entities is even more, than that of *rajasika* living entities. Beginning from the most ordinary living entities, up to Brahma, each class progressively feels one hundred times more happiness. For example, the kings of the earth enjoy one hundred times more happiness than ordinary human beings. Similarly, the forefathers enjoy one hundred times more happiness than kings. The same principle applies to the sages and demigods. The potency of their *sadhanas* similarly progresses by a factor of one hundred.

An elaborate description of the various qualities of the living entities, and their degrees of advancement, is found in Sri Madhavacarya’s commentary on the Fifth *brahmana*, Chapter three of *Brhad-aranyaka* *Upanisad*.

Four-headed Brahma alone attains *sajujya-mukti*. Sriman Madhavacarya’s conception of *sajujya-mukti* is not like those of the other *sampradayas*. According to Madhavacarya, *sajujya* mean to enter the body of Lord Visnu, just as a ghost may enter a person’s body, or as a piece of iron can be placed in a fire.

When a ghost enters a person’s body, he enjoys all the happiness created by that person, but he is not the actual doer of activities, and he can separate himself whenever he desires. Although a piece of iron is placed in a fire, it is separate from the fire, and it may be taken out of the fire at any time. In the same way, Brahma, who is a pure reflection of the original ideal form present in Lord Visnu, enters that ideal form by his own will. If Brahma wishes, he can exist separately from his ideal, spiritual form, or he can assume it if he chooses. Therefore the conventional theory of oneness, generally known as *brahma-sajujya*, or *isvara-sajujya*, is completely different from the sajujya-mukti attained by Brahma, according to Sriman Madhavacarya.

According to Sriman Madhavacarya, some of the other liberated persons attain the liberation of being an associate of the Supreme Lord, and some attain the liberation of residing on the same planet as the Supreme Lord. In this understanding there is a difference of opinion between the followers of Ramanuja and the followers of Madhva.

Thefollowers of Ramanuja say that after liberation, everyone obtains a four-armed form just like that of the Supreme Lord. But the followers of Madhva say that for everyone, there is original spiritual form present in the Supreme Lord’s eternal existence. When a living entity attains liberation, he is awarded that ideal spiritual form. Among the liberated souls, some are four-armed, some are two-armed, some are animals, some are birds, and some are grass. These liberated souls, by their own will, travel to different planets, such as Vaikuntha, Anatasana, the heavenly planets, and Satyaloka, and engage in glorifying the Supreme Lord and meditating upon Him while enjoying transcendental happiness.

During the night of Brahma, when there is no manifested creation, these liberated souls reside in the abode of Vaikuntha. Liberated souls that reside in this world after perfecting their *sadhana* reside on the planet called Santanlka (a portion of Janaloka) until the end of Brahma’s kalpa, by the order of the Supreme Lord, engaging in His service.

All the liberated living entities enter Vaikuntha, along with four-headed Brahma, at the end of his life. There they receive bodies that are full of knowledge and bliss and engage eternally in the service of the Supreme Lord. They also serve the servants of the Supreme Lord and offer obeisances with humility to them, for they are their superiors. These living entities do not take birth again in the material world.

Those who are predominantly influenced by the mode of passion and who are thus perpetually engaged in fruitive activities either live in this world or in the heavenly planets, or in the space between them. When they attain liberation, they also are freed from the cycle of birth and death.

Those who are primarily influenced by the mode of ignorance are generally envious of the spiritual master, Lord Hari, and the Vaisnavas, and after death they enter the darkest regions of hell. Such people never become free from their entanglement in matter.

The living entities that enter the creation during a particular *kalpa* do not again enter the creation in a future kalpa.

In his *Aitareya-bhasya* (Ch. 2, *mantra* 3), Sriman Madhavacarya wrote:

*bhunjate purusam prapya*  
*yathadeva grahadayah*  
*tatha muktabutta mayam*  
*visnum avisva bhunjate*

**“As the predominating deities of the planets can enter the body of a human being and enjoy that person’s happiness and distress, so when the living entities attain** ***sajujya-mukti*****, they enter Lord Visnu, who is the origin of their original, ideal forms, and enjoy pastimes with Him.”**

In *Anuvyakhyana, Atapa* 4, it is stated:

*visnorvasasca te sarve*  
*sarvada duhkha varjjitah*  
*na tu visnu gunan sawan*  
*bhunjate te kadacana*  
*bahyabhogan bhunjate ca*  
*taratamyena kamscana*  
*visitor dehad bahiscapi*  
*nirgacchanti yathestah*  
*vimuktikale praqvisyantyabhiksanam bhogamsca taddehagatah prabhunjate. anandasu vyaktir amutra tesam bhavati taas cestata eva niggatah. kridanti bhuyasca samavisanti taneva sayujyam idam vadanti. sayujya hinastu laye tu sarve proktena margena visanti srstau. bahisca niryanti tatonyad api sayujya bhajam bhavati pravesah*

**“The liberated souls’ entrance into the body of the Supreme Lord is not like the demigods’ entrance into the bodies of human beings. The living entities that have become detached from matter attain** ***sajujya-mukti*** **under the control of Lord Visnu. They enter the Lord’s body by His will and enjoy eternal bliss without any tinge of material distress. But they can never possess all the qualities of Lord Visnu, who is the reservoir of unlimited transcendental qualities. They enjoy a certain degree of transcendental happiness afforded by their constitutional position within the body of Lord Visnu, and if they so desire, they can come out from Lord Visnu’s body and assume their individual forms. After engaging in pastimes of individuality, they can again enter the Lord’s body.**

This kind of entrance into the Lord’s body and enjoyment of constitutional happiness with the Lord is said by learned scholars to be *sajujya-mukti*. Apart from these liberated souls, there are others who are liberated at the time of the final dissolution of the universe and then again, at the time of creation, they are manifest by their own will. Those who achieve sajujya-mukti enter Lord Visnu’s body, either at the time of creation, or at the time of dissolution.

## Sri Madhva’s Siddhanta Regarding Mukti, or Liberation

We have already discussed Sriman Madhvacarya’s Siddhanta regarding *mukti* while discussing the living entities’ constitutional position. The Supreme Lord helps the living entities perform activities, according to their qualifications. The living entities engage in activities according to their qualifications, the results of their previous *karma*, and present endeavor. The Supreme Lord awards them the fruits of their activities.

Surrendering to the spiritual master, hearing the scriptures, meditating on the Supreme Lord, and glorifying the Lord are all parts of devotional service and are included in the category of present endeavor. The Supreme Lord helps the living entities realize their constitutional position by means of this *sadhana.*

When persons in the mode of goodness realize their eternal constitutional position by the cultivation of the devotional service, they attain liberation. Liberation is not a condition imposed upon the individual living entities. It is the living entity’s constitutional position. The constitutional position of the living entities is covered in two ways, called *jivavarana* and *paravarana*.

*Jivavaran* is the covering of ignorance. As fire is unseen when it is covered by ashes, when a pure spirit soul is covered by nescience, it is in a dormant condition, hidden from view. Paravarana is the covering caused by the Supreme Lord’s illusory energy, *maya*. She puts a curtain around the vision of the conditioned souls so that they cannot see the Supreme Lord, who is situated in their hearts.

When the Supreme Lord is pleased, He completely withdraws both coverings. At that time the living entity has the *darsana* of the Supreme Lord residing in his heart. When the living entity obtains the *darsana* of the Supreme Lord, he no longer has any tinge of *karma*. When the living entity sees the transcendental form of Lord Visnu with his spiritual eyes, even once, from that moment he begins wandering about alone as a mendicant while thinking about his most wonderful worshipable Lord and chanting His holy names.

Although such a realized soul may be seen begging alms door to door as a matter of habit, he is always eager to engage in the service of the Supreme Lord and is fully satisfied with the happiness derived from cultivating Krsna consciousness. He remains fully absorbed in the happiness of seeing the Supreme Lord so that sometimes he laughs, sometimes he cries, sometimes he walks like a madman, and sometimes he remains inert, like a dullard. His friends receive the credit for whatever pious activities he performs after being enlightened in transcendental knowledge, and his enemies receive the punishment for whatever sinful activities he may perform out of habit.

Even after being enlightened with transcendental knowledge, four-headed Brahma continues to engage in the acts of creation, by the will of the Supreme Lord. Liberated souls that are enriched with spiritual knowledge perform activities for the welfare of the world by the will of the Supreme Lord. For example, great personalities like Sukadeva and Narada preach the glories of Lord Hari throughout the world. Even in the liberated stage there are distinctions in the constitutional positions of every individual soul. Thus their knowledge and feelings of happiness are also different from one another.

## Pure Devotional Service is the Means to Attain Liberation

Devotional service is of three kinds. 1. Ordinary devotional service. 2. Supreme devotional service. 3. Constitutional devotional service.

Ordinary devotional service refers to the devotional service awakened before the commencement of hearing the sastra from the spiritual master. Some people approach a bona fide spiritual master and, rather than acquiring from him transcendental knowledge coming down through disciplic succession, they beg for wealth, children, domestic animals, a house, and other kinds of opulence. Such so-called devotion is not even to be considered ordinary devotional service, for it is lower than that. Such acceptance of the spiritual master can never help one attain transcendental knowledge and liberation.

Devotional service that is awakened after one has acquired transcendental knowledge, or has received the darsana of the Supreme Lord, is called supreme devotional service. This devotional service is also called pure devotional service because it is devoid of a desire for fruitive activities and sense gratification. By supreme devotional service one achieves the supreme mercy of the Supreme Lord. Since it awards liberation, supreme devotional service is included in the category of *sadhana-bhakti*. Only when one obtains the supreme mercy of the Lord can one achieve liberation.

After liberation, eternal devotional service performed in one’s constitutional position is called constitutional devotional service. Devotional service is the essence of all kinds of *sadhana* performed for the attainment of perfection. There is no alternative to devotional service if one wishes to attain the Supreme Lord’s mercy. The knowledge that awards liberation mentioned everywhere in the Vedas is nothing but transcendental knowledge of the Supreme Lord. Impersonal knowledge, which is concocted from the mode of ignorance, is the goal of those who are demoniac. Only persons cultivating the mode of goodness can attach themselves to unalloyed devotional service to the Lord.

Although demons like Sisupala, Dantavakra, Karhsa and Jarasandha sometimes attain the Lord’s darsana and some understanding of His glories because they have no devotion for Him, their so-called knowledge simply fuels their opposition to Him. According to the statements of the scriptures, one acquires pious merit by seeing and touching a cow. However, a tiger does not earn pious merit by seeing or touching a cow, rather this increases his envy. The demons’ darsana of the Supreme Lord is like that.

Sriman Madhvacaryapada has given this definition of bhakti, which he quoted from *Brahma-tarka* and found in his book, *Mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya* (Ch. 1, Text 86).

*mahatmya jnana purvastu*  
*sudrdha sarvato dhikah*  
*sneho bhaktiriti proktas*  
*taya muktir na canyatha*

**“Pure, uncontaminated, and strong affection, which is only directed toward the Supreme Lord and nowhere else, and which is awakened after hearing the Supreme Lord’s glories, is called bhakti in the scriptures. Only by such devotional service can one achieve liberation, and not by any other means.”**

Sriman Madhvacaryapada described the following progression of sadhana in his *Anuvyakhyana* (Ch. 3, Text 4)

*bhaktya jnanam tato bhaktis*  
*tato drstis tatasca sa*  
*tato muktis tato bhaktih*  
*saiva syat sukharupini*

**“In the beginning, one devotedly receives knowledge of the glories of the Supreme Lord from saintly persons and** ***sastra*** **with** ***sraddha*** **(faith). Gradually one comes to the stage of transcendental knowledge, then supreme devotional service, then liberation at the lotus feet of Lord Visnu, and finally constitutional devotional service, which is the supremely blissful state.”**

In his *Gita-tatparya* (Ch. 2, Test 11), Sriman Madhvacarya writes:

*muktopi tadvaso nityam*  
*bhuyo bhakti samanvitah*  
*sadhyananda svarupaiva*  
*bhaktir naivatra sadhanam*

**“Even liberated souls are always fully under the control of the Supreme Lord and always engaged in constitutional devotional service, which is the embodiment of transcendental bliss and distinct from** ***sadhana-bhakti*****”**

## The Three Kinds of Evidence

According to Sri Madhva’s *siddhanta* there are three kinds of evidence. 1. Direct perception 2. Hypothesis, and 3. The *Vedas*. Direct perception is further divided into seven categories—the witness (the spirit soul, the knower), mind, eyes, ears, nose, tongue, and skin. The spirit soul directly witnesses everything. Thinking, feeling, and willing are functions of the mind. It also acquires knowledge and indirectly experiences happiness and distress. The mind, with the help of the senses, indirectly feels everything.

The evidence of the witness is faultless, but at the same time there is a possibility of discrepancies in the accuracy of the senses. Direct perception can also be classified in four divisions—isa-pratyaksa; laksmi-fratyaksa; Brahma and other yogis pratyaksa; and human beings, birds, animals, and so on pratyaksa.

The evidence of anumana (hypothesis) is described by the following words—hetu, upapatti, yukti and linga. This evidence is generally nothing more than guessing. Various faults, like virodha, satpratipaksa, asiddhi and badha create inaccuracy in the act of anumana. Therefore, only hetu, which is devoid of all these faults, can give one the correct information. Any anumana that is in agreement with pratyaksa and the Vedas can be accepted as evidence. Anything contradictory is not acceptable.

Vedic pramanas are of two kinds. 1. apauruseya and 2. pauruseya. Apauruseya evidence is derived from the Vedas, which include the Rg Veda, Upanisads, Mantras, Brahmanas, and Parisista-bhaga. Pauruseya evidence is collected from histories, Puranas, Pancaratras, and so on. The purport of the Vedas is explained in the Brahma-sutra. When there is a doubt about the purport of the Vedas, one has to accept the purport of the Vedic statements as explained by the Puranas, and so on.

The purport of the scripture should be ascertained by six procedures—upasamhara, abhyasa, apurvata, phala, arthavada and upapatti. Each is progressively stronger than the one preceding it.

There are three classes of Puranas—sattvika, rajasika and tamasika. The Siimad-Bhagavatam is a sattvika Purana. If portions of the rajasika Puranas are in agreement with the statements of the sattvika Puranas, they can be accepted as evidence. If there is any portion of the sattvika Puranas that are opposed to the mode of goodness, it is to be understood that it is meant for bewildering the demons. Therefore they should not be accepted by sattvika persons. The tamasika Puranas were compiled simply to bewilder the demons. Any Purana that supports the conclusions of the sattvika Puranas can be accepted as evidence.

**Some instructions of Purnaprajna Tirtha (The name Madhva received after accepting sannyasa at the age of twelve.)**

The supremacy of the Vaisnavas (Krsnamrta-maharnavam, 5)

*sa nama sukrti loke*  
*kulam tenabhyalankrtam*  
*adharah sarva bhutanam*  
*yena visnuh prasaditah*

**“Only he who has pleased Lord Visnu is the most accomplished in this world. Such a person is the ornament of his family and the shelter of all living entities.”**

Also in (*Krsnamrta-maharnavam* 7)

*kalau kalimala dhvamsi*  
*sarva papa haram harim*  
*yerccayanti nara nityam*  
*te pi vandya yatha haraih*

**“In the age of Kali, those who regularly worship Lord Hari, the dispeller of all sinful reactions and the contamination of Kali, are to be worshiped like Lord Hari.”**

Also in (*Krsnamrta-maharnavam* 14)

*samasta-loka nathasya*  
*deva devasya sanigtnah*  
*saksad bhagavato visnoh*  
*pujanam janmanah phalam*

**“The perfection of taking birth is to worship Lord Visnu alone, for He is the Lord of all living entities and the master of the demigods.”**

The glories of the Lord’s holy names (Krsnamrta- maharnavam 36)

*namnosti ydvati saktih*  
*papa nirharane hareh*  
*tavat kartum na saknoti*  
*patakam patakijanah*

**“A sinful person cannot commit as many sins as the holy name of the Supreme Lord can destroy by its supreme power.”**

Also in (*Krsnamrta-maharnavam* 44)

*hrdi rupam mukhe nama*  
*naivedyam udare hareh*  
*padodakanca nirmalyam*  
*mastake yasya socyutah*

**“One who meditates on Lord Hari’s form, who honors Lord Hari’s remnants of food, who chants Lord Hari’s holy names, and who holds on his head the water that has washed Lord Hari’s lotus feet and the flowers that have been offered to Lord Hari, is non-different from the Lord.**

Also in (*Krsnamrta-maharnavam* 72)

*kuruksetrena kim tasya*  
*kim kasya puskarena kim*  
*jihvagre varttate yasya*  
*haririti aksaradvayam*

**“What is the need for a person to visit Kuruksetra, KasI, Puskara, or other holy places, when, on the tip of his tongue, the two letters ha-ri are present?”**

Also in (Krsnamrta-mahdrnavam 74)

*sa jihva ya harim stauti*  
*taccittam yatta darpanam*  
*taveva kevalau slaghau yau*  
*tat pujakarau karau*

**“It is a real tongue that glorifies Lord Hari and it is a real heart that has been offered to Lord Hari. Those hands are glorious that have been engaged in the worship of the Lord.”**

Worship of the demigods is prohibited (Krsnamrta- mahdrnavam 115)

*svadharmantu parityajya*  
*paradharamam cared yatha*  
*tatha harim parityajya*  
*yonyam devam upasate*

**“Worship of the demigods while avoiding the worship of the Supreme Lord is like giving up one’s occupation to accept another’s.”**

Worship of Lord Visnu is the only duty (Krsnamrta- maharnavam 121)

*yavat svasthyam sariresu*  
*karanesu ca patavam*  
*tavadarccaye govindam*  
*ayusyam sarthakam kuru*

**“As long as one is in good health and his senses are active, he should engage in the worship of Lord Hari and thus make his life perfect.”**

Refuting the smarta philosophy (Krsnamrta-maharnavam 129)

*svadrtau pancagavyanca*  
*dasamya dusitam tyajet*  
*ekadasim dvijasresthah*  
*paksayor ubhayorapi*

**“The foremost of those who are twice-born should reject the Ekadasls of both fortnights if they are contaminated by the presence of Dasami, just as one rejects milk products kept in a pot made of dog skin.”**

Also in (*Krsnamrta-maharnavam* 150-152)

*athava mohanarthaya*  
*mohinya bhagavan harih*  
*arthitah karayamasa*  
*vyasarupi janardanah*  
*dhanadarcca vivrddhartham*  
*mahavitt layasya ca*  
*asuranam mohanartham*  
*pasandanam vivrddhaye*  
*atma svarupa vijnaptai*  
*svaloka praptaye tatha*  
*evam viddham parityajya*  
*dvadasyam upavasayet*

**“Or, being requested by MohinI, the Supreme Lord, Hari, in the form of Vyasadeva, made such a provisionjust to bewilder lusty people, to induce them to work hard for acquiring wealth, to ruin their spiritual life, to bewilder the demons, to conceal His true identity to the atheists, and to block their path to the abode of Lord Visnu. Therefore, one should give up such a contaminated Ekadasi and fast only on Dvadasi.”**

Also in (*Krsnamrta-maharnavam* 180)

*varam svamatrgamanam*  
*varam gomamsa bhaksanam*  
*varamhatya surapanam*  
*ekadasyanna bhaksanat*

**“It is more harmful and abominable to eat grains on Ekadasi than having sex with one’s mother, eating beef, or drinking wine.”**

Also in (*Krsnamrta-maharnavam* 221)

*tiryak pundram na kuravita*  
*samprapte marane piva*  
*na canya nama vivruyat*  
*parannarayan adrte*

**“Never apply tilaka in a crooked manner and never chant the names of the demigods instead of those of Lord Narayana, even if following this injunction causes one to lose his life.”**

It is an offense to consider a Vaisnava as belonging to a particular caste (Krsnamrta-maharnavam 223)

*urddhava pundaram rjum saumyam*  
*lalate yasya drsyate*  
*sa candalo pi suddhatma*  
*pujya eva na samsaya*

**“One who decorates his forehead with marks of simple, beautiful tilaka is a pure soul, even if he was bom in a family of dog-eaters. Indeed, such a person is worshipable.”**

The importance of Vaisnava seva (Mahabharata-tatpraya- nirnaya Ch. 29, Text 21)

*visnor bhagavatananca*  
*pratipasya krtih sada*  
*paraspara virodhe tu*  
*visistasya anukulata*  
*priyam visnos tadiyanam*  
*api sarvam samacaret*  
*dharma api priyam tesam*  
*naiva kincit samacaret*  
*hari bhakta vanuccas tu*  
*varnoccdpi na pujyate*

**“One should never impede or harm Lord Visnu or the Vaisnavas. If there is a dispute between two Vaisnavas, then simply follow the order of the senior Vaisnava. Always act in a way that pleases Lord Visnu and His devotees. If the observance of certain religious principles is not pleasing to the Vaisnavas, then discard such practices. A devotee of Lord Hari is always worshipable, even if he was born in a low-class family, and a person bom in a higher caste family is not worshipable if he is not a devotee of Lord Hari.”**

A brahmana should be accepted only if he is qualified (Chandogya-bhasya)

*arjjavam brahmane saksat*  
*sudro narjjava loksanah*  
*gautamas tu iti vijnaya*  
*satyakamam upanayat*

**“Simplicity is the primary qualification of a brahmana and crookedness is the primary qualification of a sudra. Following this rule, Haridrumata Gautama performed the sacred thread samskara for Satyakama.”**
