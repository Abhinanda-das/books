# Tiruelukurrirukkai

## 1. 1

*oru pērundi iru malar tavisil oru muṟai ayanai īnṟanai*

**Пословный перевод:**

iru – (from the) big; tavisil – seat (also petals of the flower); undi – (that is your) divine nābhi (navel); malar – (which is like a lotus) flower; pēr – (that is having the) greatness (of); oru – having no equals; oru muṟai – at one time (during creation); īnṟanai – you created; ayanai – brahmā

**Перевод:**

**Due to your causeless mercy (nirhētuka krupai), when the whole world did not exist after praḷayam, you made them all come into existence. You created the worlds, and created brahmā from your lotus like navel that is big and which has been there forever; and staying as antaryāmi inside him, you created sentient and non-sentients including humans. For you who is the cause of all these, is it hard to do the favor (of giving mōkśam) to them?**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

*‘oru’ pēr undi* – shows the supremacy of the navel (nābhi) of [emperumān](https://granthams.koyil.org/2013/03/12/thathva-thrayam-iswara-who-is-god/) which created brahmā. ‘*pēr*’ implies old – been there for long time (ever).

Since brahmā was created without the union of man and woman, he is called ‘*ajan*‘, or, *ayan* in tamiḻ.

‘*oru muṟai*‘ – once upon a time, (long ago) – since he creates brahmā after every praḷayam, this is a continuous occurrence.

By talking about creation of brahmā it is implied about the creation of other things before that. That is, when everything was indistinguishable and lacked any name or shape, and were together with [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/), and referred to as ‘avibhakta tamas’, He changed them to be distinguishable and referred to as ‘vibhakta tamas’, in manifested state, and then created small parts of states out of it like ‘akśaram’, ‘avyaktam’, and then after that, created mahān, ahankāram, and then from that ahankāram created five objects of senses (sabda (sound), sparsa (touch), rūpa (form), rasa (taste), ganda (smell)), and created panca bhūtas – 5 great elements – ākāsa (ether), vāyu (air), agni (fire), āpa/jala (water), prutvi (earth), and created the worlds from the panca bhūtas. These are called samaśti sruśti which he created by his sankalpam (divine will). Then the vyaśti sriśti: You created brahmā in order to create beings of four types, dēvas (celestial beings), humans, animals (tiryak), and plants (stāvaram), while being an antaryāmi inside brahmā. *(These are also explained in* [*piḷḷai lōkācariār*](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/09/18/pillai-lokacharyar/)*’s* [*tatva trayam*](http://ponnadi.blogspot.in/p/thathva-thrayam.html)*, and* [*maṇavāḷa māmunigaḷ*](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/09/23/azhagiya-manavala-mamunigal/) *has written an eloquent vyākhyānam for that grantam).*

There is no difference between sentients (including brahmā) and non-sentients regarding being subservient to [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/). The difference arises since we (including brahmā) *know and appreciate* the fact that [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) is inside us and making us be.

[tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) is saying these to imply that like how when a farmer plants something, it is he who would take care to remove the weeds and protect the planted, “Is it difficult for you to provide greatness (mōkśam) to these beings, for you who created all these so easily and for you who controls our existence?”.

## 2. 2

*āfter te above (first phrase), next, āḻvār says, like hov a farmer vould plant and he himself vould remove te veeds, emperumān destroys te rākśasās vho destroy te vorld He created.*

**Пословный перевод:**

iru sudar – moon and sun; mīdinil iyangā – would not span above; ilangai – lankāpuri; oru muṟai – even once (due to fear); mum madiḷ – (lankā) that is covered by three kinds of protection, by water, mountain, and forest; attanai – (you) burned and destroyed (such lankā); oru silai – (using your) unparalleled bow (sārngam); iru kāl vaḷaiya – with its two ends curved; vāḷiyin – using the arrows; onṟiya īr eyiṟu – that are fit into the bow, and having 2 teeth; aḻal vāi – and which have got the mouth that spits fire

**Перевод:**

**Using the unparalleled bow that is curved on both ends, you shot arrows that have two teeth and with the mouth the spits fire, and destroyed lankā, where even the sun and moon were afraid to go above of it.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

Unlike your creation of brahmā which you did with just your sankalpam ([*ayanai īnṟanai*](https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/index.php/2015/06/thiruvezhikurrirukkai-1/)), you physically stood in front of the enemy in the battle ground and destroyed him using your bow and arrows.

It was not pirātti (sītā) who destroyed the demon who separated Her from You, it is you who fought and destroyed such demon. Likewise, I cannot remove the ones that separate me from You, that are, this samsāram and my avidyā, karma, vācanā, ruci (lack of knowledge about nature of self, etc., long lasting impressions of my pāpams, and the taste for other things, which I still have got left in me); only You please remove such demons that separate me from You.

‘*bhīśō dēti sūrya:*’ (sun rises because of his fear (obedience) of paramātmā). But when rāvaṇan was there in lankā the sun was afraid of Him and did not show his strength in that place (*nainam sūrya: pratapati* – srī rāmāyaṇam).

Same can be applied to the moon also, so [āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) describes rāvaṇan in this way.

ilangai – like a wasp’s nest all these rākśasas were having a ball in this place (*ammaṇa kūttadikkum /* *அம்மணக்கூத்தடிக்கும்*), the rākśasas who were a terror to anyone who would even just think of them – isn’t this the kind of place that He destroyed?

How did He do that?

iru kāl vaḷaiya oru silai onṟiya ireyiṟṟu aḻal vāi vāḷiyin attanai – using the unparalleled bow that is curved on the two ends, using the arrows that is fixed on such a bow, the arrows having two teeth and which spit fire, you destroyed the place.

aḻal vāi vāḷi – when placing it in the bow it would look like an arrow, and when it strikes the enemy it would be like a fire.

vāḷiyin attanai – as opposed to ‘*ayanai īnṟanai’* above, where You created brahmā by your sankalpam, here you stood on the battlefield with your bow and arrows and tormented rāvānan.

oru muṟai … attanai – you are the one who destroyed the demon who separated pirātti from you, not she. Likewise it is you who has to destroy the samsāram and karma, etc., that separate me from you.

Here it gives a detailed explanation of how this neatly fits with mudal tiruvantādi’s 59<sup>th</sup> pāsuram of [poigai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/10/22/mudhalazhwargal/),

*adainda aruvinaiyōdu allal nōi pāvam*  
*midaindavai mīṇḍoḻiya vēṇḍil – nudangidaiyai*  
*mun ilangai vaittān muraṇ aḻiya mun oru nāḷ*  
*than vil am kai vaittān saraṇ.*                    \[mudal tiruvantādi *59*\]

in which he describes that for the ātmā it is not in-built with these karmās and its effects; like how iron would get the heat and color when it gets in contact with fire, ātmā due to contact with acit (nonsentient, this body), makes us believe that this body is us, that is the avidyā, and this is all due to karmam (accumulated good and bad effects), vāsana (trace of previously acquired bad karmas), ruci (continued interest in such things). These are *‘aru vinai’ –* very hard for us to destroy*.*

*mīṇḍu oḻiya vēṇḍil –* If you wish to destroy these with all its trace, in one sweep, what do you need to do? Surrender to the one who can destroy such hard things very easily :

*nudangidaiyai* – having waist that is tired and suffering due to separation from perumāḷ.

*mun ilangai vaittān muraṇ aḻiya –* once upon a time rāvaṇan jailed such a pirātti; and perumāḷ destroyed him,

*mun oru nāḷ than vil angai vaittāṇ caraṇ* – by touching his bow; he is the means for us for destroying our hard-to-destroy connection with this samsāram, etc.

*muraṇ aḻiya vil angai vaittān* – ((angai = am kai : beautiful hand)) – to destroy the enemies he does not even have to set the arrow in the bow and shoot it – just his touching the bow with his beautiful hands would make the enemies lose their strength and get destroyed.

*nudangidaiyai mun ilangai vaittān* – that is, our thinking that this ātmā is ours, whereas it is subservient to [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/), is equivalent to rāvaṇan’s act of separating [pirātti](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) from [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/).

Also means that those who decided that [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) is the means for destroying their hurdles, emperumān would take care of that, like how he took care of the enemy of pirātti.

Also to let us know that we hold relationship with [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) that is equal to [pirātti](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/)’s relationship with [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/).

Also to imply to us that when we go with [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) as the means, we must get [pirātti](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) as the mediator/connector (puruśakāram), so the mudal tiruvantādi pāsuram uses the phrase starting *nudangidaiyai* that refers to pirātti.

So far in tiruveḻukūṟṟirukkai, [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) shows us the following:

Starting with *oru pēr undi* in this prabandam (first phrase): you created everything from the stage of ‘non-existent’, so it is not difficult for you to help me who am in the stage of existing.

Starting from *oru muṟai* in this prabandam (second phrase which we are enjoying now): you destroyed rāvaṇan who was the hurdle for Your being together with [pirātti](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/), so it is not difficult for you to remove my hurdles for reaching you.

## 3. 3

*mūvadi nānilam vēṇḍi muppuri nūlodu mānuri ilangu mārvinil iru piṟappu oru māṇāgi oru muṟai īradi mūvulagu aḷandanai*

**Пословный перевод:**

oru muṟai – once upon a time; muppuri nūlodu – with pūṇūl (yajñōpavītam) and; mān uri – deer skin; ilangu mārvinil – adorned in your chest; iru piṟappu oru māṇ āgi – as unparalleled Brahmin bachelor; vēṇḍi – (you) begged for; mū adi – three steps of land; nānilam – in this earth that has four kinds of areas; aḷandanai – (and) you spanned; mū ulagu – the three worlds; īr adi – with (your) two divine feet

**Перевод:**

**Next, tirumangai āḻvār shows us that when it is not possible to do the task using the bow like in destroying of rāvaṇan, He would do it using his beauty and kindness.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

World as represented by four types of areas (forest, mountain, etc.) was spanned by your two steps, and the third step was on the head of mahābali. For this, you came as a bright and beautiful vāmanan (dwarf) wearing pūṇūl and deer skin, and begged for 3 steps of land for a low level wish of īndiran to get the lands.

Can you not come for me who wish for You only?

mūvadi … – You who performed what is required by indran who is after other benefits, can you not do what is required for me who is considering only You as my benefit?

nānilam mūvadi vēṇḍi – World as represented by four types of areas – mullai (forest and related areas), kurinji (mountain and related areas), marudam (town and related areas), neidal (ocean and related areas) – these are not of much importance (compared to you) – but you spanned them with your two steps; with the aim of capturing him with your third step, you begged for three steps of land.

(since these four types of land would turn in to desert when there is not enough water or due to heat), only the four types of land are talked about here – this is similar to how other poets have done, and also by [nammāḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/18/nammazhwar-english/) in tiruviruttam 26 – *nānilam vāik koṇḍu*…

muppuri nūlodu mān uri ilangu mārvinil – like the brightness of a lightning in dark clouds, your chest is having the pūṇūl and deer skin as a bramhacāri.

iru piṟappu – Him looking fresh with newly done upanayanam which is a second birth;

oru māṇ āgi – being an unparalleled brahmacāri. To get the land that indran begged Him to get back, he begged for the land in the form of a vāmanan (dwarf), like a brahmacāri who has had a long time experience in begging, where the sweet expression in such a person’s face would not be different whether the other party grants the alms or does not grant the alms. He is so beautiful in this form, that there is no parallel, and even if He Himself wants to be born like this, it would not be possible for him – such was vāmanan’s beauty.

oru muṟai īradi mūvulagu aḷandanai – at one time itself you measured using two steps, the three worlds. By this, [āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) says, “Like how for īndran you got him back the kingdom of the three worlds, for me please give me what I wish for – being sēśan (subservient) to you”. You took these efforts for those who wished for other benefits, can you not take some efforts for me who only wish You as the benefit? indran lost some inconsequential land only, whereas I have lost me and You both.

(When spanning the worlds) You had touched the heads of everyone including those who were not interested in You; can you not give your tiruvadi (divine feet) to me who wishes for you?

Unlike indran with his lowly interest in land, and unlike mahābali who was proud of his character of being the best in donating to others, am I not interested only in getting You? I wonder whether you will do the deeds, even by going as a beggar, only to those who wish for things other than You. Would you not do the deeds for those who wish only for You?

This is similar to wishes of other [āḻvārs and ācaryās](https://guruparamparai.wordpress.com/) – only interested in [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/)’s tiruvadi on their head.

## 4. 4

*nāl tisai nadunga anjiṟaip paṟavai ēṟi nālvāi mummadattu irusevi orutani vēḻattu arandaiyai orunāḷ irunīr maduvuḷ tīrttanai*

**Пословный перевод:**

oru nāḷ – once upon a time; nāl tisai nadunga – (got an anger such that) people in all four directions were scared; am siṟai paṟavai ēṟi – (and you) got onto the garudan who has got beautiful feathers; iru nīr maduvuḷ – (and went to the shore of) the pond having deep waters; tīrttanai – and removed; arandaiyai – the suffering of; nāl vāi – the one having a hanging mouth; mummadam – which lets out mada (intoxicated) water out of three places; iru sevi – and which has got two ears; oru tani vēḻattu – that is the unparalleled elephant gajēndran who was alone

**Перевод:**

**tirumangai āḻvār says that it is not just those like indran who are great in jñānam and śakti, you helped even an elephant; whereas you calmly created and destroyed the worlds, in this case you rushed losing your temper, to the astonishment of brahma and all other beings that are on all four sides of the universe;**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

[āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) describes the beauty of elephant the [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) rescued. This is to show how [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) considered the elephant so dear to him that he appreciates his beauty after rescuing him.

Like āṇḍāḷ sang in nāciār tirumoḻi asking [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) to come rescue her, [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) is also implying that He should come rescue him from the samsāram and the five faculty/senses that have been dangerous to him from time immemorial.

Now to the question of whether He would help only those like indran who possess jñānam and śakti, [āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) points out that if there is faith in Him, and if there is little bit of danger, then too He has come rushing (arai kulaiya, talai kulaiya – a saying in tamiḻ to roughly mean that He would rush without regard to whether the clothes worn by self are in proper position, and whether the hair style is getting disturbed) to help even animals which are lower in birth and conformity (ஒழுக்கம்).

*nāl tisai nadunga* – All the brahma and people who know that when creating the worlds He did so just by his thoughts, and when destroying the worlds He did so without any anger or efforts, now they are witnessing Him rushing without regards to his self and with so much anger; they are terribly afraid to see this, thinking something very untoward might happen.

*am siṟaip paṟavai ēri* – Like the dark clouds moving across the golden mountain mēru, He climbed on the shoulders of garudāḻvān who possesses beauty and speed, and rushed to help gajēndrāḻvān. [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) then describes why He is rushing like this:

*nāl vāi mummadattu iru sevi oru tani vēḻattu arandaiyai* – (nāl vāi) because of having raised its trunk it is having the mouth that his hanging; from three places of its body it is having mada (intoxicated) water pouring; having two (or, big) ears; not having any other source of protection; after having lost its strength, and not having any one to go to for help; having a big sorrow (arandai – arati – sorrow)

*oru nāḷ iru nīr maduvuḷ tīrttanai* – on that unparalleled time, when it was a foreign land for the elephant that was your devotee, and which was a familiar land for the crocodile that was its enemy, (you came to such a) shore of the lake having deep waters – seeing the weight of that danger you came rushing and removed the sorrow of the elephant.

oru nāḷ – [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) is celebrating such a day when [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) took to rushing to rescue an elephant.

āḻvār means that like how you rescued the elephant from the clutches of the crocodile, you please rescue me from the clutches of this samsāram. Like it is said, “elephant is pulling towards the shore; crocodile is pulling towards the water; this war continued with their equal strengths for 1000 dēva years (*viṣṇu darmam 69*)” – for elephant the enemy was one crocodile, for me the enemies are the 5 crocodiles which are the 5 faculty (indriyas/senses); that one is a strong elephant – I am weak; the elephant suffered for a short duration – I have been suffering from time immemorial; don’t You have to run to me and rescue?

*vēḻattu arandaiyai iru nīr maduvuḷ tīrttanai* – Like it is said in *viṣṇu darmam*, – *grāham cakrēṇa mādava:*, He rushed in to the pond, came to the shore tugging the elephant and the crocodile, and without hurting even a nail in the foot of the elephant He very carefully used sudarśaṇa chakra and killed the crocodile. Does He have to tug/hug the crocodile that was a bhāgavata virōdi (enemy to His devotee), when coming back to the shore? [bhattar](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/09/11/parasara-bhattar/) says, “Like how a servant, who uses his hands and body and fights with the prince every day during the practice for wrestling, would get related food when the prince is fed, the crocodile that fought with the elephant also got the benefit of [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/)’s hug”.

*nālvāi mumadattu iru sevi oru tani vēḻattu* – why is [āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) describing so much the body parts and the overall makeup (samudāya sōbhai) of the elephant? When people rescue a baby that fell into a well, they would start describing the beauty of that baby, “Oh! see this baby’s ears, aren’t they so beautiful? The shape (vāḷippu) of its neck! Its legs are beautiful! Its head is so beautiful! Not able to describe its overall beauty! By good grace we saved such a beautiful child!”, and so they would keep talking about the beauty of the body parts of the baby and its overall beauty; in the same way this phrase of the prabandam talks about [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/)’s involvement towards the elephant after He rescued him;

*vēḻattu arandaiyai* – unlike the danger of something small-bodied, it was a big danger like its big body; “*paramāpadam āpanna: manasā{S} cintayat harim”* \[viṣṇu darmam\] – said riśi showing the greater danger of the elephant – this is by saying ‘*parmāpad*’**;** now, by ‘*manasā cintyāt*’ it is implied that gajēndrāḻvān lost his ability due to weakness to even open the mouth, but just called [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) in his mind only.

*oru nāḷ … tīrttanai* – the [woman](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/12/16/andal/) who was born to [periyāḻār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/20/periyazhwar/) who is dear to [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/), knew her ways to get emperymān’s attention, and so sang – *kālaik kaduviduginṟa kayalodu vāḷai veruvi* \[nāciār tirumoḻi 3-5\] – when you could not tolerate a worm that lives in the water, can you tolerate when there are two fish (that live in the water)?. In the same way [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) asks [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/), “When you did not tolerate a crocodile that gave trouble to the strong gajēndran for some duration, can you tolerate when five faculty (indriyas/senses) have been troubling me forever?”

## 5. 5

*muttī nānmaṟai aivagai vēḷvi aṟu toḻil andaṇar vaṇangum tanmaiyai*

**Пословный перевод:**

andaṇar vaṇangum tanmaiyai – (you are of the nature who is) prayed by the brāhmaṇas using; muttī – three types of agni (fire), and; nāl maṟai – four types of vēdas, and; aivagai vēḷvi – five types of yajñas (rituals), and; aṟu toḻil – six types of karmas

**Перевод:**

**You are of the nature by which the brāhmaṇas who follow the darmas, perform the very difficult karma yōgam in order to attain you. It talks about the ways of the brāhmaṇas – they perform hōmam and maintain three types of agni, know four type of vēdas, do five types of yāgams, and perform six types of work. This goes to show the difficulty of doing karma yōgam which they perform for attaining him. It implies that emperumān goes along and helps such brāhmaṇas also and gives them what they needed.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

[tirumangai āḻvār](http://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/thirumangai-azhwar/) says ‘*vāḷiyin attanai*’ (used arrows to remove the enemy) for [emperumān](http://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/sriman-narayanan/) helping **His dear** pirātti; says ‘*maduvuḷ tīrttanai*’ for Him helping indiran who is after **other benefits**;

In the next few phrases of the prabandam, [āḻvār](http://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/thirumangai-azhwar/) talks about the greatness of emperuman, that is, He goes along and helps those who use Him and also **other ways as means** and gets them the benefits they wanted.

muttī … – As said by “*yōgō yōgavidām nētā”* \[viṣṇu sahasra nāmam 18,19\], He is the one who is the means for everything, and those who follow other means to get Him to help with what they needed, He is amicable to achieve that for them; To begin with, [āḻvār](http://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/thirumangai-azhwar/)  talks about the first of such other means that is karma yōga which is followed by those brāhmaṇas who follow the righteous ways.

*muttī* – Like how it would be hard for her to take care if she gets three hungry babies in one delivery itself, brāhmaṇas try to satisfy the three types of agni (which do not get satisfied how much ever firewood is given to it); the three types are gārhapatyam, āhavanīyam, dakśiṇāgni.

*nānmaṟai* – In order to do that karma yōga properly, they practice/recite all the four vēdas, rig, yajur, sāmam, and atarvaṇam.

*aivagai vēḷvi* – dēva yajñam, pitru yajñam, bhūta yajñam, manuśya yajñam, and brahma yajñam \[taittiriya Upanishad, Ara – 2-10\] are the five types of yajñams that the brāhmaṇas  perform every day.

*aṟu toḻil* – brāhmaṇas perform six types of work – yajanam (doing yāgam for self), yājanam (performing yāgam for others), aḍhyayanam (reciting vēdas), aḍhyāpanam (teaching others to recite vēdas), dānam (giving others what they need), pratigraham (obtaining from others what is needed). For other varṇams only some of these are applicable. By this, it talks about the extreme difficulty of performing karmya yōgam. Since it talks about the brāhmaṇas who are fully possessing the greatness of them, they are considered to be following the karma yōgam along with the jñānam (knowledge) of doing such karmas.

*andaṇar vaṇangum tanmaiyai* – by such karma yōgam, they want to reach You, and so they surrender to you and pray to you; you are of such nature.

## 6. 6

*aimpulan agattinuḷ seṟuttu nānugudan adakki mukkuṇattu iraṇḍavai agaṟṟi onṟinil onṟi ninṟu āngu iru piṟappu aṟuppōr aṟiyum tanmaiyai*

**Пословный перевод:**

aimpulan agattinuḷ seṟuttu – (without letting roam around onto other bad influences outside) they control the five senses to stay inside; nāngu udan adakki – eating, sleeping, fearing, enjoying other pleasures – are the four things they nullify; mukkuṇattu – out of the three characteristics, satvam, rajas, and tamas; agaṟṟi – they avoid; iraṇḍu avai – rajas and tamas; onṟi ninṟu – and stay involved; onṟinil – only in satva guṇam; āngu – by such a bhakti yōgam; iru piṟappu aṟuppōr – upāsakars (worshipers/followers who use their own efforts) avoid two types of births; aṟiyum tanmaiyai – You are of such a nature that they can reach You (by their own efforts as the means)

**Перевод:**

**In the previous phrase tirumangai āḻvār started talking about those who try by their own efforts to reach emperumān. It talked about karma yōgam (including jñāna yōgam). In this phrase he talks about those who do very difficult and almost impossible bhakti yōgam which some use as the means for attaining mōkśam and reaching emperumān.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

Those who perform bhakti yōgam have to control their outer five senses (body, mouth, eye, nose, and ears) from roaming around to enjoy the five benefits (sound, touch, form, taste, and smell (sabda, sparsa, rūpa, rasa, ganḍha)); they have to control their mind; they have to stay focused on having satva guṇam (tranquillity), and avoid two other guṇams (rajas, tamas) which can pull us down;

After doing such highly difficult tasks on their own, they avoid the two types of births – good and bad births (samsāram), and may attain [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/).

[āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) says that putting in these efforts which they consider as the means for attaining You, is against the nature of [ātmā](https://granthams.koyil.org/2013/03/06/thathva-thrayam-chith-who-am-i/) which is supposed to depend on [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) for everything; even then a few such beings are able to reach You, whereas adiyēn who considers You as the means for reaching You is still not able to attain You!.

Now, [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) talks about bhakti yōgam which has got karma yōgam as its part (angam), which is even more difficult (than karma yōgam), and which is followed by upāsakars (those who resort to meditation/recitation/worshiping) who are scared looking at this samsāram and wish to attain mōkśam.

*aimpulan agattinuḷ seṟuttu* – body, mouth, eye, nose, and ears are the five outer senses that they control from roaming around the outside influences of sound, touch, form, taste, and smell (sabda, sparsa, rūpa, rasa, ganḍha).

*nāngu udan adakki* – mind which is an inner sense is usually present in four states – **1)** when involved in thinking (mananam) it is ‘manam’; **2)** when it gains knowledge/understands – it is ‘budḍhi’; **3)** when reflecting, it is ‘cittam’; and **4)** when thinking of self it is ‘ahankāram’. This phrase points out that they have to control all these four states at the same time, which is hard and rare to do.

*nāngudanadakki* – can also understand this as that they have to control the four common activities of all beings – eating, sleeping, fearing, and sexual intercourse.

ōr, 1) the wealth of being able to distinguish between nityam (which is there forever), anityam (which is destroyed/changed), 2) control of inner and outer senses; 3) being not interested in benefits in this world or in other worlds, and 4) interested in attaining mōkśam – are the four means of mōkśam that they possess within them.

*mukkuṇattu iraṇḍavai agaṟṟi* – out of the three primordial – satvam, rajas, and tamas – they avoid with great effort and difficulty the rajas and tamas which pull us down,

*onṟinil onṟi ninṟu* – stay focussed on the one remaining, that is, satva guṇam.

āngu – by the bhakti yōgam that is reached through karma yōgam

iru piṟappu aṟuppōr –  upāsakars break the chain of the type type of births – good and bad – which are due to good and bad karmās (puṇayam and pāpam);

aṟiyum tanmaiyai – and You are of the nature whose guṇams are being known by such upāsakars based on their upāsanai.

By ‘birth’, it refers to this samsāram.

We can combine to the phrase of ‘āngu iru piṟappu aṟuppōr’ to mean ‘they get rid of births by bhakti yōgam’. ōr, ‘āngu aṟiyum’ to understand it as ‘upāsakars who know You by doing bhakti yōgam’.

So, starting from the previous phrase ‘muttī’, till the current phrase, following is explained:

Their following of the very hard to do karma (including jñānam), bhakti yōgams for getting rid of this samsāram and enjoy [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) – is the same as how gajēndrāḻvān tried to win the crocodile by his own efforts and put flowers in [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/)’s tiruvadi (lotus feet to enjoy [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/)). Mostly, they would miss out, is what would happen (பெரும்பாலும் இழந்தேபோமித்தினை). So, he emphasizes that we should survive (out of this samsāram) only by considering [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) only as the means, and he says “even when a few of them who followed other means that are very hard to do, which are against the nature of [ātmā](https://granthams.koyil.org/2013/03/06/thathva-thrayam-chith-who-am-i/) (that is supposed to depend on [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) only for everything), which in reality are Not the means, have been able to attain You with their impossible efforts, here I am who depends on You who is the true means for attaining mōkśam and reaching You – and I am not able to get You!”

Thus these are the thoughts [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) has provided us in the phrases from “muttī” till “aṟiyum tanmaiyai”.

## 7. 7

*mukkaṇ nāl tōḷ aivāi aravōdu āṟupodi sadaiyōn aṟivaruntanmaip perumaiyuḷ ninṟanai — ēḻulagu eyiṟṟinil koṇḍanai*

**Пословный перевод:**

mukkaṇ – (rudran who is) having three eyes; nāl tōḷ – four shoulders; aivāi aravōdu – having the snake which has got five mouths; āṟu podi sadaiyōn – and having river gangā in the plaits of his hair; aṟivu aru – cannot know you; tanmai – you are of that nature; perumaiyuḷ ninṟanai – you are having such a greatness; eyiṟṟinil koṇḍanai – (by srī varaham) lifted into your trunk (dantam); ēḻulagu – all the worlds

**Перевод:**

**Now, in the previous phrase tirumangai āḻvār said that upāsakars are able to understand emperumān, but it is only to the extent that they see based on what they meditate on, and not that they can fully comprehend His guṇams/characteristics.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

To show this, [āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) points out that perumān is such, that even rudran who is greater in knowledge and strength cannot identify [emperumān’](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/)s guṇams fully well.

mukkaṇ …. – the phrase says that rudran who has got additional eyes (eyes holding the inner meaning to imply knowledge/jñānam), and who is mentioned as “īśvarāt jñānamanviccēt” \[should wish to get knowledge from sivan\], is proud of such knowledge; and with that if he tries to fully determine about [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/), even such a rudran cannot achieve that – such is the nature of [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/).

*mukkāṇ* – one having three eyes; having more eyes implies having more knowledge.

*nāl tōḷ* – seeing sarvēśvaran having four shoulders, he (rudran) is also having four shoulders; also implies having more strength (śakti); As said by *“onṟiraṇḍu kaṇṇinānum unnai ētta vallanē”* \[tiruccanda viruttam 7\], thus holding excess knowledge and strength he still cannot understand [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/), so there is no chance for others to know [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) is what [āḻvar](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) is letting us know.

*ai vāi aravōdu āru podi sadaiyōn –* he is holding the snake that has got five mouths, and holding gangā in one part in his hair plaits; this also shows his excess of strength. By saying “*aravōdu āṟu podi*” it is saying that both are in his hair; or, by ‘*aravōdu’* it implies that the snake is in his body;

*aṟivarum tanmaip perumaiyuḷ ninṟanai – Even with all these* knowledge and strength, You are unreachable – such is your grand nature; by this, “If this is the situation for rudran who has got such great knowledge and strength, it goes without saying that for those with negligible knowledge and strength, like adiyēn (this humble servant), it is impossible to use such things to know/reach You; (so) It is You who has to help us with your causeless mercy” – this is what [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) shows with this phrase.

—

*ēḻulagu eyiṟṟinil koṇḍanai* – when there was danger to this world due to praḷayam, such rudra, etc., did not help, but only You helped.

*W*hen the praḷayam caught everyone including rudran who possess knowledge and strength, and us the opposite (who lacks knowledge and strength); You took the form of mahā varāham that does not hesitate about water or mud, when all the worlds were stuck to the walls of the spheres of the universe; and You entered into and pried out and kept the world shining in the tip of your white tusks – like a bee in a lotus; like a blue diamond fixed in a silver ornament;   by this, [āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) is letting [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) know that “like you saved the earth that got into the praḷayam, please have mercy save me from the praḷayam that is this samsāram”. By “*ēḻulagu”* it is implied the seven worlds above and the seven below, that is, all the worlds.

## 8. 8

*kūṟiya aṟu suvaip payanum āyinai — sudar vidum aimpadai ankaiyuḷ amarndanai sundara nāḷ tōḷ munnīr vaṇṇa*

**Пословный перевод:**

kūṟiya aṟu suvaip payanum āyinai – six types of tastes (mentioned in the sāstras) is You who is all such tastes for me; am kaiyuḷ – In your beautiful divine hands; amarndanai – you hold; sudar vidum ai padai – the five weapons that are bright; sundara nāl tōḷ – having four beautiful shoulders; munnīr vaṇṇa – Oh emperumān, having the beauty like an ocean

**Перевод:**

**tirumangai āḻvār considers emperumān as all the tastes specified in the śastrās;**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

—

[āḻvar](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) enjoys the beauty of [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/)’s four shoulders, which define the word beauty, and which give beauty to the ornaments worn; [āḻvar](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) asks [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) to let him fully enjoy such beauty.

*kūṟiya aṟu suvaipp payanum āyinai* – sāstras talk about six tastes that the people in this world use – salty, sour, saline/astringent, sweet, hot, and bitter (uppu, puḷippu, tuvarppu, inippu, kārppu, kaippu (kasappu); You are these tastes for us. [nammāḻvār](http://ponnadi.blogspot.in/2012/10/archavathara-anubhavam-nammazhwar.html) too sang “*aṟu suvai adisil enkō”* \[tiruvāimoḻi 3-4-5\]. You become the six tastes for the people and benefit them, you please be the six tastes – shad’rasānnam (aṟusuvai adisil – food with all six tastes) and give Yourself to us. This is the meaning [tirumangai āḻvar](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) shows in this phrase.

—–

For him, it is the five divine weapons that [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) holds, and the beautiful hands that holds them, are the food of six tastes – is what [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) shows in the next phrase.

*sudar vidum aimpadai ankaiyuḷ amarndanai* – having wonderful appearance of śankam (conch), cakram (sudarsana wheel), gadai (mace), sārngam (bow), nāndakam (sword); even if they are not present, your hands are so beautiful, which require removing the casting of others’ (evil) eyes; with such beautiful hands you provided your grace to us by holding those weapons, like they are ornaments for such beautiful hands.

*sundara nāl tōḷ munnīr vaṇṇa* – Shoulders that define the word beauty; that word beauty split in to four parts, that are your four shoulders; Like it is said “*sarva bhūśaṇa bhūśārhā: bhāhava*:” \[ramāyanam – kiśkinta kāndam? 3-15\], \[shoulders fit for decorating all the ornaments\], without having to use any ornaments to add beauty, the shoulders themselves are so beautiful; they create beauty; in reality they give beauty to the ornaments; One having such shoulders**;** river water, spring (ūṟṟu) water, and rain water – all the three get mixed (munnīr) in to oceans, and when people look at the ocean it their fatigue would be removed and feel fresh; in the same way you remove our fatigue by your beautiful form; [āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) implies that He should make him fully enjoy this beauty.

## 9. 9

*nin īradi onṟiya manattāl oru madi mugattu mangaiyar iruvarum malar(a/e)na am kaiyin muppoḻudum varuda aṟi tuyil amarndanai*

**Пословный перевод:**

oru madi mugattu mangaiyar iruvarum – the two pirāttis, srīdēvi and bHūdēvi (tirumadantai, maṇmadantai) with unparalleled divine face like the moon; onṟiya manattāl – (with their) mind immersed in; nin īr adi – your two divine feet; muppoḻudum – (they) always; varuda – press/caress (your divine feet); malar ena am kaiyin – using their beautiful hands that can be said as flowers; aṟi tuyil amarndanai – while you are immersed in meditating sleep (yōga nidrā)

**Перевод:**

**nin īradi . . . – tirumangai āḻvār shows that for getting the benefit of enjoying his beauty, there is also support of able mediation (puruśakāra balam).**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

*nin īradi onṟiya manattāl* – with their mind focused on dēvarīr’s divine feet; by this it shows their ananya bhōjñatvam (not enjoying anything other than [emperumān](https://guruparamparai.wordpress.com/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/));

*oru madi mugattu* – unlike the moon seen in this world, their faces are like a complete moon without any blemish, and their faces are pleasant and show how they are drinking the beauty that is [emperumān](https://guruparamparai.wordpress.com/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/).

*mangaiyar iruvarum* – Like said by “*tulyaśeelavayō vruttām”* \[rāmāyaṇam – sundara kāṇḍam 16-5\] \[she possesses equivalent character, age, and actions (as [emperumān](https://guruparamparai.wordpress.com/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/))\], and by “y*uvatiśca kumāriṇī”* \[she is near the end of young girlhood and near the beginning of womanhood \], they with their suitable age can make [emperumān](https://guruparamparai.wordpress.com/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) their bhakta and a mad lover (like said in “*pār vaṇṇa mada mangai* ***pattar*** ***pittar*** *pani malar mēl pāvaikku*” \[tiruneduntāṇḍakam 18\] ) – such are the two pirāttis – srīdēvi and bhūdēvi (tirumadantai and maṇmadantai).

*malar ana am kaiyin* – with beautiful hands, as if they touch with flowers; phrase is recited differently too as ‘*malar* ***e****na am kaiyin’* ; if we try to compare with flowers, their hands are even softer and more beautiful than the flowers; since their hands are soft, colorful and fragrant, it is not possible to provide a comparison for them, is the concept here. //  Like said by: “*candra kāntānanam rāmam atīva priyadarśanam”* \[rāmāyaṇam ayōdyā kāṇḍam 3-29\] \[sweet like the moon, appearing sweeter than the moon, that is rāman\]

*muppoḻudum varuda* – During all the three times – future, current, and past (edir, nigaḻ, kaḻivu), they caress [emperumān’s](https://guruparamparai.wordpress.com/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) divine feet with so much hesitation (to avoid disturbing the delicate and beautiful lotus feet) as said in “*vadivu iṇai illā malar magaḷ maṟṟai nila magaḷ pidikkum mel adi*” \[tiruvāimoḻi 9-2-10\].

*aṟi tuyil amarndanai* – As You keep thinking while lying down, “how to save the whole world, especially how to save his devotees?”,  you are in such a state that you would not get up even if arrows are thrown at You!

“*nin īradi … mangaiyar iruvarum … adi varuda”* – by this it implies [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) saying, “It is not possible for adiyēn to not get the benefit (You) due to any lack of mediators/recommendation (puruśakāram) (since both pirāttis are there).”

“*aṟi tuyil amarndanai*” – “It is not possible for adiyēn to not get the benefit (You) because of any lack in Your part of thinking of saving your devotee (since you are always thinking about saving everyone)”.

## 10. 10

*kaṇṇan fighting te seven bulls to get nappinnai pirātti*

**Пословный перевод:**

nāl vagai varuṇamum āyinai – You control all four varṇas (category of births); neṟi muṟai – who conduct themselves according to sāstra; mē tagum ai perum pūtamum nīyē – You are the antaryāmi of all the five elements (bhūtams) into which ātmās can enter into and get set into them; aṟupadam muralum kūndal kāraṇam – For nappinnai pirātti to whose hair the bees (six legs) come buzzing (for enjoying the honey); ēḻ vidai – the seven bulls; adanga ceṟṟanai – (which you) crushed them together; After talking in the previous phrase about yōga nidrā (aṟi tuyil – meditation) for protecting, it now talks about how He protects us, lets us be; The above is common to every being, and tirumangai āḻvar mentions a special and specific case next – as how He crushed the bulls that prevented Him from getting nappinnai pirātti who is beautiful, that bees would buzz around her hair that is having honey, flowers

**Перевод:**

**After talking in the previous phrase about yōga nidrā (aṟi tuyil) for protecting, it now talks about how He protects us.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

*neṟi muṟai nāl vagai varuṇamum āyinai* – You are the four varṇas (brāhmaṇa, kśatriya, vaiśya, and śūdra) who conduct themselves within the respective limits set by the sāstras. Says that these four varṇas are under your control. Since you gave these four varṇas to the ātmās, and since you are the target of the conduct/worship of these varṇas according to their ways, it can be said that ‘You are the four varṇas’, is the opinion.

‘*aham hi sarva yajñānām bhōktā ca praBhurēva ca*’ \[gītā – 9-24\] (I am the one who enjoys all the yajñas (that are performed for me); I am the benefactor who gives benefits for them), and

“*varṇāśrama ācārvatā puruśēṇa para: pumān \| viṣṇur ārāḍhayatē pantā nānyas tattōśakāraka: \|\|*” \[viṣṇu purāṇam 3-8-9\] (viṣṇu is worshiped only by those men who live according to the dharma of their varṇāśramam; He does not get happiness when done in other ways), and

*“cātur varṇyam mayā sruśtam” \[gīta 4-13\]* (all the four varṇas are created by me)

– these are the meanings of what sāstras have provided, isn’t it?

——

*mētagum aimperum pūtamum nīyē* – The elements of the body into which ātmās enter and get set – earth, water, fire, air, space – all these grand elements are You only. That is, they are controlled by You.

mētagu – enter and settled well into; that is, as said in ‘*yādānum ōr ākkaiyil pukku angu āppuṇḍum”* \[tiruviruttam 95\] – the ātmās enter the bodies of dēvas, humans, etc., and they get set into them to the extent of making them think “*dēvōham manuśyōham”* (I am a dēva, I am a human), and they become eligible to follow the karmās according to those bodies they entered into.

Saying “*nālvagai varuṇamum āyinai; aim perum pūtamum nīyē” ,* [*āḻvar*](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) *shows the meaning,* “for my body and its ways, the existence, being, and activities (sattā, stiti, pravrutti) are under your control, so is there anyone to protect me other than You?”

—–

In yōga nidrā, [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) thought of the protection of beings, and the previous phrase talked about the common aspects of creation, existence, etc. Now it talks about a special case of Him protecting nappinnai pirātti.

*aṟupada muralum kūndal –* bees, which have six legs, are buzzing around and settling into the hair of nappinnai pirātti, for the sumptuous honey ; for the reason of getting such a nappinnai..

*ēḻ vidai adangac ceṟṟanai* – without missing any of the sever bulls you crushed their necks and killed; the bulls which were preventing you from hugging such a pirātti;

By this, “Like how you removed the hindrances for getting nappinnai pirātti, please grace me and remove the hindrances that prevent **You** from **getting** **me”** is the idea that [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) shows in this phrase.

## 11. 11

*aṟu vagaic camayamum aṟivaru nilaiyinai aimpāl ōdiyai āgattu iruttinai — aṟamudal nāngavaiyāy mūrtti mūnṟāi iruvagaip payanāi onṟāi virindu ninṟanai*

**Пословный перевод:**

aṟu vagai camayamum – Six type of other philosophies; aṟivu aru – cannot know/understand; nilaiyinai – You; such is your nature; aimpāl ōdiyai – pirātti whose hair is identification of five ways of hair; āgattu iruttinai – have placed her in your divine chest; aṟam mudal nāngu avai āy – You are the one who grants the four goals – aṟam (dharma), poruḷ (things/wealth), inbam (pleasure), vīdu (srīvaikuṇṭam); mūrty mūnṟu āy – as antaryāmi for the three mūrtys; iru vagai payan āy – You are the one who creates happiness and sadness (based on karmās); onṟu Ai virindu ninṟanai – just the self (in the beginning), and then expanded as the whole world

**Перевод:**

**These phrases talk about the aiśvaryam (paratvam) of emperumān.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

It is impossible for those denying the [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/), that is those who believe in other six philosophies to reach/know [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/).

You have placed srīdēvi pirātti in your divine chest, where she stays at all times, to do puruśakāram (recommendation).

You are the grantor of the four goals of beings; you are the antaryāmi of the three mūrtys; You are the one who controls the happiness and sadness of beings according to their karmas; You who was the only One existing during praḷayam (annihilation), expanded into many, and gave names and shapes to things, and You are responsible for their being as the antaryāmi.

So it is not possible for me to lose you because of any lack of greatness/aiśvaryam on Your part, says [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/).

*aṟuvagaic camayamum aṟivaru nilaiyinai* – It is not possible to know You by those who do not accept you, that is, cārvākar, bouttar, samaṇar, naiyāyika’vaiśēśikar (tārkkikar), sānkyar, pāśupadar;   by this, āḻvar implies the meaning, “When you are beyond reach for those who do not accept you, you are beyond reach even for me who accepts you!”

*aimpāl ōdiyai āgattu iruttinai –* You hold periya pirātti in your divine chest; periya pirātti having the five identifications of a good hair – being curled, shining, fragrant, dense, and soft.

*āgattu iruttinai –* tiruvadi (hanuman) was corrected by pirātti, *“pāpānām vā śuBhānām vā vaḍhārhāṇām plavangama \| kāryam karuṇamāryēṇa  na kaścit nāparāḍhyati \|\|“* \[rāmāyaṇam yudda kāṇḍam 116-44\] (hey vānara! whether one has done sins or good deeds, even if they are to be killed, even in their matters a just person should show kindness. There is no one (in this world) who has not done any mistakes!); such pirātti is kept in Your divine chest such that she is always available for puruśakāram (recommendation);

![⚠ картинки нет на сайте: pApAnAm\_va](<https://divyaprabandham.koyil.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/pApAnAm_va.png>)When saying “*mangaiyar iruvarum varuda”*   in a previous phrase, it referred to both srīdēvi and bhūdevi as doing puruśakāram, but here it mentions the importance of srīdēvi who is always present in the divine chest of [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/); so this is not insignificant. ōr, it can be taken as this referring to srīdēvi here to imply that [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) gets His aiśvarya, (paratva), soulabhyam, beauty etc., due to pirātti only. This idea is used in the upcoming phrases.

To begin with it talks about His aiśvaryam.  
—  
aṟam mudal nāngavaiyāy – You are the four goals (puruśārtam) – that are, aṟam (dharma/charity), poruḷ (things/wealth), inbam (pleasure/happiness) and vīdu (srī vaikuṇṭam); “*caturṇām puruśārttānām dātā dēva: caturBhuja:”* \[nārāyaṇan who has got four arms, is the one who grants the four puruśārtas\].

*“dēvēndras triBhuvanam arttamēkapinga:*  
*sarvardḍhim triBhuvanagām ca kārttavīrya: \|*  
*vaidēha: paramapadam prasādya viṣṇum*  
*samprāpta: sakala pala pradō hi viṣṇu: \|\|”*   \[viṣṇu darmam 43-47\]

\[ By worshiping viṣṇu, devēndra got three worlds, kubēran the wealth, kārtta vīryan the fame that spread to the three worlds, and janaka mahārājan the paramapadam; such is viṣṇu bhagavān who grants what one wishes.\] – so since he is the one who can give the four goals, He himself is those goals, so says [āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/).

*mūrty mūnṟāi –* Three mūrtys, that are: brahmā, rudra, and indra**;** [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) is the antaryāmi in them; ōr, if considering the three – brahmā, viṣṇu, rudra, then it talks about how [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) stands between the two and does the protection himself, and does the creation and destruction as antaryāmi in brahmā and rudra.

“*sruśti stiti antakaraṇīm brahma viṣṇu śivātmikām \|*  
*sa samjyām yāti Bhagavān ēka Eva janārtdana: \|\|”* \[viṣṇu purāṇam 1-2-66\]

\[creation, protection, and destruction are the three activities for which janārtdanan takes the three names (and forms) of brahmā, viṣṇu, and shiva\], so said srī parasara riśi.

*iruvagaip payanāi –* happiness, sadness are the two types of states due to karmas – he is the one who controls them.

*onṟāi virindu ninṟanai* – during praḷayam (annihilation), all the things would be without any name or form and stick together with [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/), so he is said as ‘*sadēva*” \[cāndokya upanishad 6-2-1\], that is, He is the only one existing; then during creation, he decides “*bahu syām*” \[cāndokya Upanishad 6-2-3\] (I shall become many/multiply), and expands to many things that get name and form.

ōr, instead of creation, and praḷaya (annihilation) times, it can also mean that during all times, all the things are parts of Him, so we can say “sentient and non-sentient things are parts of the body of [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/); such [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) is the one truth” – he is such.

And it can be considered as follows. Even though He is having these things as his body, He is not affected by the blemishes of those things.

Starting from ‘*aṟamudal*” till current phrase, His aiśvaryam (paratvam) has been mentioned. From this, [āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) has implied, “It is not possible for me to lose (You) because of any lack of aiśvaryam on Your part”

Note: With this the phrases that follow a numbering pattern ends. In the next pāsuram of this prabandam, [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) surrenders to tirukkudandai [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/).

## 12. 12

*kunṟā madumalarc cōlai vaṇkodip padappai varupunal ponni māmaṇi alaikkum sennel oṇ kaḻanit tigaḻvanam udutta kaṟpōr purisai kanaka māḷigai nimirkodi visumbil iḷampiṟai tuvakkum selvam malgu ten tiruk kudantai antaṇar mantira moḻiyudan vaṇanga ādaravu amaḷiyil aṟituyil amarnda parama nin adi iṇai paṇivan varum idar agala māṟṟō vinaiyē.*

**Пословный перевод:**

kunṟā madu – having unlimited honey; malar cōlai – (from the) groves full of flowers; vaṇ kodi padappai – and with gardens having beautiful creepers; ponni – with cāuvēry river; varu punal – always having proliferating water; mā maṇi – and the best gems; alaikkum – great in number thrown by its waves; sennel oṇ kaḻani – having fields that are beautified by rice of yellowish hue; tigaḻ vanam udutta – surrounded in all the four sides by wilderness / grove / forest; kaṟpōr purisai – town inhabited by the learned; kanakam māḷigai nimir kodi – flags fluttering upward from the golden palaces/mansions; visumbil – in the sky; tuvakkum – touching/caressing; iḷa piṟai – the young moon; selvam malgu – (it is the) wealthy and; then tiru kudantai – beautiful tiruk kudantai; ādu aravu amaḷiyil – (where you are leaning) in the bed of ādi sēśan with its open hood; aṟi tuyil amarnda – and involved in doing yōga nidrai (meditating sleep); antaṇar – (that is suitable for) brāhmaṇas; mantiram moḻiyudan vaṇanga – to recite vēda suktas; parama – hey paramēśvara!; nin adi iṇai paṇivan – am surrendering to your two divine feet; varum idar agala – for the removal of hurdles that may come in the way (of reaching You); māṟṟu vinai – please remove those hurdles by your mercy; In this concluding pāsuram, tirumangai āḻvār surrenders to the lotus feet of tirukkudantai emperumān. He describes the wealth and nature of the place, how river cauvēry with its rich water base brings in gems and other valuables, how the learned ones living there – like tirumaḻisai āḻvār whose fame spread in all the eight directions – worship the emperumān with their vēdantā sūktis, and so on

**Перевод:**

**Now, “kidandavāṟu eḻundirundu pēsu” (~ please get up and converse) \[tirccanda viruttam 61\], (sung by tirumaḻisai āḻvār), that is, if devotees request/command, He would do as requested/commanded, so tirumangai āḻvār considers the sovlabhyam (neermai / easiness of attainment) of, and where beauty resides, that is āravamudāḻvār, and surrenders to Him similar to nammāḻvār surrendering to Him.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

*kunṟā madu malarc cōlai* – Having groves with ever increasing sumptuous honey, and the flowers that keep adding such honey; if they were fed with manure/fertilizer and water, then those flowers of honey might not grow – since these groves are growing by the flood of nectar that is the divine sight of aravamudāḻvār, they grow as during ever existing spring season.

*vaṇkodip padappai* – Having beautiful aquatic lands (or gardens) of posts of creepers (or the creepers), and having straws (padappai) and such various creepers, such gardens**;** or it is talking about aquatic lands having beetel leave plants. ‘*vaṇ*’ refers to richness of it and the beauty that shows because of it.

*varupunal ponni māmaṇi alaikkum* – Description of the river Cauvery. Having overflowing richness of water, that brings along and accumulates precious gems; (and the river as described in various texts): “*canjcaccāmara chandra candana maHa māṇikya muktōtkarān kāvērī laharīkarair vidadatī” \[rangaraja stavam 1-21\]* ( *kāvēri that comes carrying* attractive cāmaram (white hair from the yak tail – used in Fan/whisk), green camphor, sandal, best gems, pearls); “*āḷariyāl alaippuṇḍa yānai maruppum agilum aṇimuttum veṇsāmaraiyōdu ponni malaippaṇḍam maṇḍat tiraiyundu*” \[periya tirumoḻi 3-8-3\]; *candinōdu maṇiyum koḻikkum punal kāviri*” \[periya tirumoḻi 5-4-1\], “*vēyin muttum maṇiyum koṇarndu Ar punal kāviri*” \[periya tirumoḻi 5-4-9\], “*tisai vil vīsum seḻu māmaṇigaḷ sērum tirukkudantai*” \[tiruvāimoḻi 5-8-9\]”**—**  so made are the pāsurams on kāvēri, isn’t it?

*sennel oṇ kaḻaṇi* – due to kāviri the rice fields are growing in abundance and they prosper, and so they look beautiful; the place is having such beautiful fields;

*tigaḻ vanam udutta* – due to the ever flowing water, abundant forests are surrounding the place;

kaṟpōr purisai – In the place where it was said “*tisai vil veesum seḻumāmaṇigaḷ*” \[tiruvāimoḻi 5-8-9\], that talked about great personalities who lived in the city (tirukkudantai), like [tirumaḻisai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/16/thirumazhisai-azhwar/) and others whose fame spread in all eight directions, *“purisai”* stands for puri – city/place. ōr, when read as “puri sei” it implies the city that was made; ōr, “purisai” as the protective wall”, like “kaṟpu ōr purisai” where the divine walls have nice designs made in them.

*kanaka māḷigai* – in the palaces/mansions made of gold,

*nimir kodi visumbil iḷam piṟai tuvakkum* – the flags that are mast in such palaces, are long and tall and such that they, up in the sky, caress the young moon with their hoods – shows the greatness of the palaces; tuvakkum – can consider as touching it by surrounding the moon, or as stopping it.

*selvam malgu then tirukkudantai* – In tiruk kudantai that is of the best wealth and sweetness. ‘*selvam malgu’* to show the greatness of wealth, ‘*then’* to show its sweetness.

*andaṇar mantira moḻiyudan vaṇanga* – brāhmaṇas who consider only You as their desire, who are well versed in the vēdas, go unto you and recite the vēdānta sūktis which they safeguard from being heard by those not qualified/eligible, as said in “*mantram yatnēna gōpayēt”* \[try hard and hide/safeguard the mantras\].

*ādu aravu amaḷiyil aṟi tuyil amarnda parama –* expanded due to the contact of [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/)’s divine body, He lies down on that tiruvanantāḻvān (ādisēśan) as the bed, and thinks deep about helping the world**;** and due to that You appear apt to be said as “You are The [sarvēśvaran](https://granthams.koyil.org/2013/03/12/thathva-thrayam-iswara-who-is-god/)”!

*ādu aravu –* snake that dances with its hood open. Also can say that as tiruvanantāḻvān inhales and exhales, he is like a sleeping cradle as he shrinks and expands his body.

*nin adiyiṇai paṇivan –* am surrendering in your divine feet. Even if You shun me, those divine feet would accept me, and so am with determination have taken your divine feet as the means. *{pirāttiyum avanum vidiul tiruvadigaḷ vidādu, tiṇ kaḻalāi irukkum – mumukśuppadi}*

*varum idar agala –* For the enemies that appear in this world which prevent me from enjoying your divine feet and also from enjoying your nature, appearance, and character (svarūpa rūpa guṇam), which is ..

māṟṟō vinaiyē – the connection with the samsāram, You please rid me of that biggest sin/trouble.

*adiyiṇai paṇivan … maṟṟō vinai –* adiyēn surrendered to you as the means as per my (ātmā’s) nature; You please rid me of the sadness because of connection with the samsāram, as per Your nature. Like [nammāḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/18/nammazhwar-english/) who said “*tariyēn ini un caraṇam tandu en sanmam kaḷaiyāyē*” \[tiruvāimoḻi 5-8-7\], [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) thus surrenders to Him with divine tiruvadi as the means for getting rid of unwanted and for achieving the wanted (aniśta nivrutti, iśta prāpti).

## 13. 13

*idam koṇḍa nenjattu iṇangik kidappana enṟum tadam koṇḍa tāmarai sūḻum malarnda taṇ pūnkudantai vidam koṇḍa veṇ pal karum tutti cenkaṇ taḻal umiḻ vāi padam koṇḍa pāmbaṇaip paḷḷi koṇḍān tiruppādankaḷē*

**Пословный перевод:**

kudantai – In tirukkudantai; sūḻum – that is surrounded; ponni – by kāvēri; tāmarai koṇḍa tadam – and by the ponds having lotuses; taṇ pū – full of cool/nice/pleasant flowers; malarnda – that have blossomed; paḷḷi koṇḍān – ārāvamudāḻvār is lying down; padam koṇḍa pāmbu aṇai – in the bed that is tiruvanantāḻvān (ādi sēśan) who has opened his hood; vidam koṇḍa – who is having venom; veṇ pal – and white teeth; karum tutti – dark dots (in the hood); sem kaṇ – and reddish eyes; taḻal umiḻ vāi – and with mouth spitting fire; tiru pādangaḷē – (such ārāvamudāḻvār’s) beautiful divine feet (only); enṟum iṇangik kidappana – is always felt in; nenjattu – (tirumangai āḻvār’s) heart; idam koṇḍa – (heart that is) wide and deep

**Перевод:**

**ponni – tāmarai koṇḍa tadam sūḻum : Should read the meaning as: Surrounded by the river cauvery, and by ponds having lotus flowers, taṇ pūnkudantai – is tirukkudantai which is full of pleasant and cool flowers; in that place, emperumān is having sayanam.**

**Комментарий. Автор Periyavāccān Piḷḷai:**

*(Introduction): This song that is said to be authored by the poet kambar is recited at the end of tiruveḻukūṟṟirukkai. In this, kamba nāttāḻvār describes the greatness of tirumangai āḻvār.*

Describes the bed in which He is lying down by saying: *vidam koṇḍa … pāmbaṇai .*

*vidam koṇḍa veṇpal –* Having venomous white teeth to bite the asuras and rāskśasas

*karum tutti* – having black dots in the hood

*sem kaṇ* – As he is always alert to protect [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/), he is having reddish eyes due to fear/doubt.

*taḻal umiḻ vāi –* As said in “*āngu āravāram adu kēttu aḻal umiḻum pūnkār aravaṇai”* \[nānmugan tiruvandādi 10\], due to fear/doubt (about enemies trying to harm emperumān), he is having mouth that spit fire.

*padam koṇḍa pambaṇaip paḷḷi koṇḍān –* As [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) himself sang in tiruveḻukūṟṟirukkai “*ādu aravu amaḷiyil aṟi tuyil amarnda parama”*, starting from “*veṇ pal”* till here, kambar immerses in the beauty of ārāvamudāḻvār’s bed that is tiruvanantāḻvān’s whiteness of teeth, blackness of the dots (in the hood), redness of the eyes, and the color of flames from the mouth, whiteness of the hood, bluish hue of the body – that is kambar is enjoying the beauty of mixture of colors of tiruvanantāḻvān.

*tiruppādangaḷē –* only the two beautiful divine feet,

*idam koṇḍa nenjattu iṇangik kidappana* – As [tirumangai āḻvār](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/) himself graced the words “*veḷḷattān vēnkatattānēlum kalikanṟi uḷḷattinuḷḷē uḷan kaṇḍāi*” \[periya tirumoḻi 11-5-10\], as [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) loves the big and deep heart of [tirumangai āḻvar](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2013/01/23/thirumangai-azhwar/), His two beautiful divine feet always reside in one part of his heart.

As said in ubhaya vēdantas (samskrtm and tamiḻ): “*viśvasya āyatanam mahat”* \[nārāyaṇa suktam\] (it is the heart that is a big temple of sarvēśvaran), and “*nenjamē neeḷ nagarāga irunda en tanjanē!”* \[tiruvāimoḻi 3-8-2\], the srīvaiṣṇavas’ hearts are talked as grand temples and wide places for Him to reside.

{  
In srīvacana bhūśaṇam, piḷḷai lokācāriar says (with rough translation):

“*ankuttai vāsam sādanam, inkuttai vāsam sādyam –* His stay in temples is the means; His stay in srīvaiṣṇavas’ hearts is the goal.

*… idu siddittāl avaṟṟil ādaram mattamāi irukkum” –* īF [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) gets a place here (heart), then he would try to neglect that place (temple).

“*iḷam kōyil kai vidēl enṟu ivan prārttikka vēṇḍumpadiyāy irukkum*” – the devotee would have to plead to [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) not to neglect so.

“*prāpya prīti viśayatvattālum, krutajñataiyālum, pinbu avai abhimatangaḷai irukkum”* – Due to emperumān’s love towards the srīvaiṣṇavar, and due to [emperumān](https://acharyas.koyil.org/index.php/2012/08/17/divya-dhampathi/) being grateful to the temple for getting Him these hearts, he would then continue to reside in the temples.
