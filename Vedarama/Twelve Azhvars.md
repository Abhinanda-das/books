## An Introduction to the Twelve Azhvars

The Azhvars, twelve in number, had pure love for Sri Visnu and led their lives endowed with deep faith and devotion for Him. They inspired others as well and guided them on the righteous path.

The Azhvars were the partial incarnations of Sri Visnu or His associated accessories as given below:

Name of the Azhvar-Incarnation (Amsa)

Poygai Azhvar-Conch (Pancajanya)

Bhutatt Azhvar-Mace (Kaumodaki)

Pey Azhvar-Sword (Nandaka)

Tirumazhisai Azhvar-Discus (Sudarsana)

Nammazhvar-Visvaksena (Army chief)

Madhurakavi Azhvar-Ganadhipar (Kumudar)

Kulasekhara Azhvar-Jewel (Kaustubha)

Periyazhvar-Vehicle (Ratha)

Sri Andal-Bhumi Devi

Tondaradippodi Azhvar-Garland (Vaijayanti)

Tiruppan Azhvar-Divine Mark (Srivatsam)

Tirumangai Azhvar-Bow (Sarrigam)

The teachings of the Vedas are difficult to understand by common man, both due to the Sanskrit language and the philosophical import. To help even the layman to enjoy the bliss, of divine knowledge, the Azhvars embodied the essence of Vedic teachings in their Tamil hymns, which were later compiled as the *'Nalayira Divya Prabandham'* ('Four Thousand Sacred Hymns'). Composed in sweet and poetic Tamil language, the *Nalayira Divya Prabandham* is called *'Tamil Veda* or *'Dravida Veda'* by the Vaisnavas.

The life-stories of the Azhvars are really inspiring because of their earnest devotion and total surrender to Sri Visnu. The hymns composed by them, called *Divya Prabandham*, are also very beautiful. These hymns remained unknown for quite some time till they were unearthed and popularized by the great Acarya Nathamuni.

Although the Azhvars are generally considered to be twelve in number, others, who are deeply devoted to Sri Visnu, are also known as Azhvars in Sri Vaishnavism. For example, the disciple of Sri Ramanuja, Kuresa, is called 'Kurattazhvar' or 'Azhvar'. The platform where the Salagram (a symbol of Sri Visnu) is placed is called 'Kovilazhvar'. The sacred place where the Tulasi (basil) plant grows is called 'Tirutuzhay Azhvar'. The tamarind tree under which the great Nammazhvar lived is known as 'Tiruppuli Azhvar'. Garuda, the celestial bird and the vehicle of Sri Visnu, is popularly known as 'Garudazhvar'.

## The Great Acarya Nathamuni

Redeems

Divya Prabandham

*jyeste'nurddhdsambhutam vtrandrdyane pure I*  
*gajavaktramsamacdryamadyam nathamunim bhaje II*

**I adore Acarya Nathamuni, the greatest among the preceptors, who was born at Viranarayanapuram in the month of Jyestha (June-July) under the influence of the asterism Anuradha (seventeenth stellar mansion) as an incarnation of Gajavadana (a courtier of Visvaksena).**

The *Divya Prabandham* composed by the great Azhvars was redeemed for our sake by the great Acarya Nathamuni. In Sri Vaisnava tradition, the Acarya lineage starts with Sri Visnu, followed by Goddess Mahalaksmi and Visvaksena; with Nathamuni as the first Acarya. Books would be lost if there were none to take interest in preserving them. It so happened that the number of people studying and teaching the *Divya Prabandham* composed by the Azhvars diminished and hence during that period the real tenets of Sri Vaishnavism became unknown.

Sri Mahavisnu, as Kattumannar, graced the place called Viranarayanapuram. Isvaramuni, a devotee of the Lord living in that town, was fortunate enough to give birth to the great Nathamuni.

Nathamuni became well-versed in the Vedic scriptures at a young age. He got duly married and left for northern India with his family. In Govardhan, he was attracted to the Lord there and decided to stay there permanently to serve Him.

One day, Sri Kattumannar appeared in Nathamuni's dream and directed him to get back to Viranarayanapuram and he accordingly did so. Once, he went to Kumbhakonam to have the darshan of Sri Sarngapani. At that time, he was attracted to some devotees who were singing a hymn from Nammazhvar's *Prabandham* called *Aravamudhe*. At the end of the eleventh Pasuram, the wordings go like this: *'Or Ayirattul Ippattum'*, meaning these ten hymns form a part of one thousand *Pasurams*. Immediately, Nathamuni wanted to hear the remaining 990 hymns. Alas! The devotees said that they knew only those ten hymns. However, they suggested that he can go to Tirukkurugur, the place where Nammazhvar was born, and may get some clue about the remaining hymns.

Nathamuni wanted to know about the fate of the remaining hymns and desired to possess the same somehow. Immediately, he proceeded to Tirukkurugur and wandered hither and thither in vain. Finally, he came across an elderly scholar called Pararikusadasa. He gave another eleven hymns called the *Kanninun Siruttambu* written by Madhurakavi Azhvar. Further, he said that one who studied these hymns with full faith and devotion, recited them twelve thousand times, would definitely have the darshan of Nammazhvar.

Nammazhvar took his divine birth in Tirunagari and was in the state of Yoga for sixteen years under a tamarind tree. This tree is known as *'Tiruppuli Azhvar'*. Nathamuni sat under the tamarind tree and recited those eleven hymns (given by Pararikusadasa) twelve thousand times with great devotion and faith. Finally, he had the darshan of the great Nammazhvar. Nammazhvar blessed Nathamuni and gave the hymns rendered by the other Azhvars also, apart from his own thousand *Pasurams*, altogether four thousand in number. Thus, the four thousand sacred hymns, *Nalayira Divya Prabandham*, were brought back to the limelight by the great Nathamuni. He also set apt music for these *Pasurams*.

Apart from compiling and setting to tune the *Nalayira* *Divya Prabandham*, Nathamuni also wrote two valuable books — *Nydyatattvam* and *Yoga Rahasyam*. After rendering devoted service to Sri Kattumannar, he finally attained the lotus feet of his Acarya.

The main hymn which caused the *Nalayira Divya Prabandham* to come to limelight, *Aravamudhe*, is in fifth decad, eighth *Tiruvaymozhi* (given in Annexure I).

## 1. Poygai Azhvar

*tulayaiii sravane jatam kancyam kancanavarijat I*  
*dvapare pdncajanydmsam saroyoginamdsraye II*

**I take refuge in Saroyogi, who was born of a golden lotus in the month of Asvina (October-November) under the influence of the asterism Sravana (twentysecond stellar mansion) in the Dvapara Yuga in the city of Kancipuram, who is the incarnation of Paricajanya (the conch of Sri Visnu).**

Poygai Azhvar was born in the month of Asvina and Sravana asterism at Tiruvekka. Kancipuram is one of the seven sacred places of salvation, the others being Ayodhya, Mathura, Maya, Kasi, Avanti and Dvarakapuri. There are eighteen small localities in Kancipuram and Tiruvekka is one among them. In one of the holy tanks, Poygai Azhvar appeared in this world on a gold-like lotus. Conch, discus, mace, bow and sword are the five famous weapons of Sri Visnu. Poygai Azhvar was the incarnation of the conch, the 'Pancajanya'.

Poygai Azhvar lead a holy ascetic life endowed with Jnana, Bhakti and Vairagya. His attention was wholly on God without any worldly diversions. He visited the places of worship of Sri Visnu (Divya Desams), offering his prayers in the form of divine hymns. For several days, he stayed at Srirangam. He then visited Tiruvinnagaram, Tirukkovalur, Tirupati and other holy places.

Since he believed that his entire life was to be given exclusively to serve God, he was called 'Bhutatt Azhvar'.

As a staunch devotee, he paid holy visits to Srirangam, Tirumalirunjolai, Tirukkudandai, Tirukkottiyur, Tirupati and other places (Divya Desams) and offered his devoted service to Sri Visnu.

## 3. Pey Azhvar

*tulasatabhisag jatam mayurapurikairavat I*  
*mahantam mahadakhyatam vande srinandakamsakam II*

**Him I adore, that celebrated Mahatma, who was born out of a water-lily blossoming within a well in Mayurapuri in the month of Asvina (October-November) under the influence of the asterism Satabhisak (twenty-fourth stellar mansion) as an incarnation of Nandaka (the sword of Sri Visnu).**

Pey Azhvar'S holy birth was on a waterlily in a well at Adi Kesava Perumal temple in Tirumayilai (Mylapore) during Asvina-Satabhisak.

Pey Azhvar was the incarnation of sword Nandaka of Sri Visnu. He was a highly learned person. He had deep love for Sri Visnu and enjoyed visiting His temples (Divya Desams). Whenever his devotion matured, he would behave like a mad man; sometimes he would laugh continuously, or cry loudly, or dance and sing delightfully. On seeing his strange behaviour, everyone thought he was possessed by a ghost. Hence he was called Pey Azhvar.

Tirumazhisai Azhvar was the contemporary of Pey Azhvar. There is a version that Tirumazhisai Azhvar was previously a Saiva and was known as Sivavakya. Both of them were neighbours. On noticing that Sivavakya manifested the qualities of a Vaisnava, Pey Azhvar gradually taught him about Sri Vaishnavism z and the greatness of Sri Visnu. Finally, Sivavakya was converted into a Vaisnava, became the disciple of Pey Azhvar, and came to be known as Tirumazhisai Azhvar.

## The Meeting of the First Three Azhvars

Poygai Azhvar, Bhutatt Azhvar and Pey Azhvar are called the first three Azhvars (*Mudalazhvargal*). Though the three were staunch devotees of Sri Visnu, wandering over the Divya Desams\> they lived independently, unknown to one another. Sri Visnu decided to shower His blessings on these three great souls, wanting them to come together and sing hymns in praise of Him, which came to be known as *Divya* *Prabandham*.

Once, Poygai Azhvar went to Tirukkovalur, a sacred place situated near the Pennai river. Sri Visnu is known by the name Ulagalanda Perumal in that place. Sudarsana Cakra which is usually held by the right hand of Sri Visnu is held by His left hand in this place. Bhakta Prahalada and Namuchi, the son of Mahabali, are seen at the lotus feet of Sri Ulagalanda Perumal.

When Poygai Azhvar reached this holy place, it was raining heavily. He could not move anywhere. Fortunately, he saw the ashram of Mrkandu rishi, rushed inside, and closed the door behind him. He laid himself down in the front pial (raised platform) thinking of Sri Visnu.

As a divine play of Sri Visnu, a little later, Bhutatt Azhvar reached the same place. Trembling in the chilly rainy weather, he knocked at the door and requested to be let in. Poygai Azhvar enquired of the stranger. Bhutatt Azhvar replied that he was a devotee of Sri Visnu. Greatly surprised, Poygai Azhvar immediately sprang up and opened the door.

Since the place inside was small enough for only one person to lie down, both of them sat side by side and went on talking about the greatness of Sri Visnu.

Oh! What a surprise! Not before long, another devotee of Sri Visnu, Pey Azhvar, approached the ashrama and knocked at the door gently with a request to be let in. Astonished beyond bounds, Poygai Azhvar opened the door for Pey Azhvar, another great devotee of Sri Visnu, and invited him inside. Then Poygai Azhvar said, 'When I first entered, with great difficulty I managed to lie down. As soon as he (Bhutatt Azhvar) entered, both of us adjusted and were able to sit side by side. Ah! Now you are here! My dear brother, don't worry! Let us stand and talk about our God till dawn.'

Suddenly, they realised that someone else, a fourth person, had entered there without their knowledge. Neither were they able to stand comfortably nor breathe easily in that small pial. The fourth person was none else than Sri Ulagalanda Perumal. Unable to understand this, these three Azhvars were wondering about the strange presence. To remove the darkness that was making them uncomfortable, Poygai Azhvar sang the first verse of the *Mudal Tiruvantadi* praying to God for light.

The first verse was:

*vaiyam tagaliya vdr kadale ney dga*  
*veyya kadiron vdakku dga - seyya*  
*cudar azhiyan adikke cuttinen solmalai*  
*idar azhi nmgugave endru.*

**He was followed by Bhutatt Azhvar who sang a hymn in praise of Sri Visnu requesting Him to remove the inner darkness and to light the lamp of Jnana.**

This came to be called the *Irandam* *Tiruvantadi*:

*anbe tagaliya arvame ney aga*  
*inbu urugu sindhai idu tiriya - nanburugi*  
*jhanac cudar vilakku etrinen naranarku*

**Both the inner and outer darknesses were spontaneously removed by the holy lamps lit by Poygai Azhvar and Bhutatt Azhvar. Immediately, all the three had a wonderful darshan of Sri Visnu in all His majesty and splendour.**

It was then the turn of the third devotee, Pey Azhvar, to sing the *Mundram Tiruvantadi*:

*tirukkanden ponmeni kanden tigazhum*  
*arukkan ani niramum kanden - cerukkdarum*

**Sri Visnu appearing in a luminous form holding conch, discus, mace, sword and bow in the hands said, I' enacted this holy play only to bring all of you together.' He showered His blessings upon them and disappeared.**

With great joy, the first three Azhvars began visiting various temples (Divya Desams) together and finally reached Tirumazhisai. According to one version, they met another Azhvar called Tirumazhisai Azhvar and engaged in holy discussions about Sri Visnu.

Poygai Azhvar, Bhutatt Azhvar and Pey Azhvar lived long with their yogic strength and finally went to Tirukkovalur and attained *Paramapada* (the lotus feet of Sri Visnu). They are called *'Mudalazhvargal'* since they paved the way for others to follow. They are also known as *'Paley Tamizhar'*.

## 4. Tirumazhisai Azhvar

*maghdyam makare mdse cakramsam bhdrgavodbhavam I*  
*mahisarapuradhisam bhaktisaramaham bhaje II*

**I worship Bhaktisara who was born as the son of Bhargava in Mahisarapura (Tirumazhisai) in the month of Pausa (January-February) under the influence of the asterism Magha (tenth stellar mansion) as an incarnation of the discus (Sudarsana).**

Once upon a time, the great Rishis—Atri, Vasistha, Bhrgu, Bhargava, Pulastiya and Angirasa went to Sri Brahma in Satya Loka, prostrated before Him, and enquired about the most suitable place on earth for meditation and austerities.

Sri Brahma immediately asked Visvakarma, the architect of the celestials, to bring a weighing machine. He then requested Visvakarma to put Tirumazhisai on one side and the other places on the other side. Tirumazhisai weighed heavier. Hence Sri Brahma declared that Tirumazhisai was the best place on earth to perform austerities. The Rishis bowed down and took leave of Sri Brahma and proceeded towards Tirumazhisai.

One among them, Bhargava, did an elaborate Yaga called *'Dirgha Satram'*. After the completion of this Yaga, he began to do great austerities and engage himself in deep meditation to gain darshan of Sri Visnu. His meditation was so deep that Devendra (Indra) was afraid that the sage might attain his status. Therefore, he started troubling him by sending the celestial dancers like Rambha, Urvasi, Menaka and others to divert his mind from meditation. In spite of their great efforts, they felt embarassed that they could not win him over.

However, one among them known as Kanakarigi was able to attract the mind of Rishi Bhargava who instantly fell in love with her. Because of their union, during Pausa under Magha asterism, Kanakangi gave birth to a lump of flesh without any form. It was the incarnation of Sudarsana Cakra, the discus of Sri Visnu. Since nothing could be done with this formless child, the dejected parents threw this lump into a bush and left the place.

Goddess Bhumi Devi took care of this lump. Because of her affectionate care, the lump slowly took shape and turned into a normal child. Sri Visnu gave a wonderful darshan to this child for an instant and then disappeared. The child began to cry to have the darshan of the Lord again.

At that time, a person named Tiruvalar belonging to the Velalar community passed that way. Seeing a child crying helplessly, he took it to his house. His wife Pankayachelvi brought up the child with love, affection and great care.

This child later became Tirumazhisai Azhvar. From childhood he was different from Others. He would not drink milk, nor sleep, nor even answer nature's calls. Right from young age, he had the characteristics of a yogi. Several people visited Tiruvalar's house to see this peculiar child!

One fine morning, an aged couple came by and fed the child with milk-rice. To the great surprise of others, the child ate the milk-rice. They continued to feed the child regularly. One day, after taking some milk-rice, the child returned the rest to the aged couple. As soon as they ate that they turned into a young couple. They were also blessed with a child. Being devotees of Sri Visnu, the couple named the child Kanikannan. Later, Kanikannan became a devotee of Tirumazhisai Azhvar.

Up to the age of seven, Tirumazhisai Azhvar lived with the Velalar couple. He then moved out to learn and try different paths to realise God. In course of time, he became a Saiva with the name Sivavakya.

Sivavakya was living in solitude immersed in the devotion z z z of Sri Siva. Since he was born as a partial incarnation of Sri Visnu's Sudarsana Cakra, Pey Azhvar appeared on the scene to convert him into a Vaisnava. Finally, under the influence of Pey Azhvar who described the infinite qualities and glories of Sri Visnu, Sivavakya became a Vaisnava, and came to be known as Tirumazhisai Azhvar. Thereafter, taking leave of Pey Azhvar, he returned to Tirumazhisai to contemplate on Sri Visnu.

Tirumazhisai Azhvar has referred to this phase of his life in his own composition called *Nanmugan Tiruvantadi*:

*matrut tozhuvdr oruvaraiyum yan inmai*  
*katraic cadaiyan karikanday (hymn 26)*  
*pitirum manam Hen pinnjagan tannodu*  
*edirvan avan enakku neran (hymn 84)*

**In appreciation of his devotion to Sri Visnu, Sri Siva Himself appeared before him and blessed him with the title 'Bhaktisara'.**

Once, Tirumazhisai Azhvar was in trance. At that time, Suktiharan, a man with supernatural powers, shot up the sky seated on a tiger. However, when he was Tirumazhisai Azhvar, he had to stop as he could not move further. While trying to understand the reason, he was amazed to see a yogi sitting below. He came down to test him. Through his power, he brought one silk cloth and requested Azhvar to wear it discarding his worn out cloth. The Azhvar, who could read his mind with his superior power, brought forth an excellent armour made of gems and handed it over to him. On seeing its brightness like the rays of the sun, Suktiharan felt ashamed. He removed his pearl garland and earnestly requested the Azhvar to treat the same as Japamala. The Azhvar then showed the garland of Tulasi and lotus beads he was wearing which were shining like precious gems. supernatural pure golden Tirumazhisai right above

Regretting his act, Suktiharan praised the Azhvar, 'So far I used to win everyone with my own supernatural power. Now, you have won me with your superior powers. I am sure there is nobody in this world superior to you/ Saluting the Azhvar he left.

One day, another Siddha named Kongana Siddha met the Azhvar. He showed some medicinal pills which could transform baser metals into gold and urged the Azhvar to accept the same and thereby lead a happy life. Immediately, Tirumazhisai Azhvar collected dirt from his own body, handed it over to Kongana Siddha, and said that the dirt would transform stones into gold, thereby enabling him to enjoy his own life! The Siddha experimented and found it to be true. Feeling embarrassed, he bowed to the Azhvar and left the place.

Tirumazhisai Azhvar's life demonstrates that a real devotee has no attraction for worldly power or material things. To be away from such petty intrusions, the Azhvar moved to a cave in a nearby hill and sat in contemplation of the Lord.

According to one version, the first three Azhvars, namely, Poygai Azhvar, Bhutatt Azhvar and Pey Azhvar, came into the cave attracted by a divine light. There they saw Tirumazhisai Azhvar in a yogic state. Intuitively they understood who he was. He too understood who they were. All the four spent their time in ecstatic joy discussing about the infinite glories of Sri Visnu. All the four then proceeded to Tirumayilai and stayed there for a while. From there, the first three Azhvars took leave of him and proceeded on their own way.

Tirumazhisai Azhvar then went on a pilgrimage and visited Kumbhakonam. There he composed two *Prabandhams—Nanmugan* *Tiruvantddi* and *Tiruchandaviruttam*. After having darshan of Sri Visnu at some more places, he returned to Tirumazhisai.

Once, Tirumazhisai Azhvar could not get the sacred white earth (*Tiruman*) which was used to put the Vaisnava mark on the forehead. He searched everywhere in vain and felt sad for not getting it in Tirumazhisai. Sri Visnu appeared in his dream and instructed him to proceed to Tiruvekka near Kancipuram where he could get the sacred earth in the Potramarai tank. Immediately, the Azhvar left for Tiruvekka and felt extremely happy finding the sacred white earth. Immersed in the divine love for God, he settled there. His disciple Kanikannan also stayed with him in Tiruvekka to serve him.

There lived a old lady rendering service like cleaning, washing and decorating the floor with rangoli in the place where the Azhvar stayed. The Azhvar was so pleased with her service that he blessed her to become a young and beautiful woman.

The local king fell in love with this young woman and married her. When he came to know the reason behind her beauty, he too wanted to become young. He requested Kanikannan to bring his Acarya to the palace. Kanikannan replied that his Acarya would not come to the palace. Finding no alternative, the king decided to go to the Acarya. Kanikannan said that it too would be fruitless. Seeing the king getting dejected, the ministers said to him that Kanikannan himself was a great devotee and performed several miracles, and that the king can attain what he wanted if he requested him. Accordingly, the king requested Kanikannan the next day to sing a song so that he could become young. But Kanikannan strongly refused to do so saying that except in praise of Sri Narayana he would not sing in praise of any ordinary mortal. Being compelled by the king, Kanikannan finally sang a song, but in praise of Kancipuram:

*adavargaj eniianakalvar arul surandu*  
*padagamum liragamum pambanaiyum - nidiyamal*  
*nindrdn irundan kidandan iduvandre*  
*mandrdr pozhirkacchi manbu.*

**On listening to this, the king got angry at Kanikannan for singing in praise of Kancipuram instead of singing in praise of him. And he banished Kanikannan from Kancipuram.**

When the Azhvar came to know of this, he decided to follow disciple. Desiring that the Lord should also go along with them, he urged the Lord in Tiruvekka through the following hymn to quickly get ready to proceed with them:

*kanikannan pogindran kdmaru piinkacchi*  
*manivanna m kidakka venda - tunivudaiya*  
*sennap pulavanum selgindren myum undan*  
*painnagap pay curuttik kol.*

**Kanikannan is leaving the place. I am also going along with him. You too join us by rolling your snake-mat.**

At once, the Lord obeyed and followed him with His snakez z mat. Sri Mahalaksmi also joined Sri Visnu. On seeing this, the entire team of celestial gods joined them and left Kancipuram. The whole town of Kancipuram became dark and desolate.

Realising his grave error, the king went in search of the Azhvar in a place called *'Or Iravu Irukkai'* (now called 'Orikkai') where the Azhvar was resting with others. He prostrated before the Azhvar and begged his pardon. He requested the Azhvar to return to Kancipuram. The Azhvar was pleased and sang the following hymn:

*kanikannan pdkkozhindan kamaru punkacchi*  
*manivanna ni kidakka vendum - tunivudaiya*  
*sennap pulavanum pokkozhinden niyum undan*  
*painnagap pay paduttuk kol.*

**Kanikannan is not going anywhere. I am also not going. You too need not go anywhere. Spread your snake-mat and lie down!**

Along with Kanikannan he returned to Tiruvekka. The Lord with Sri Mahalaksmi and other Devas too returned to Tiruvekka. Since the Lord had adhered to His ardent devotee, at Tiruvekka, He is known as '*Yathoktakari—Sonna Vannam Seyda Perumdl'* ('the Lord who acted according to His devotee's behest').

In order to remind the future generations about this incident, Sri Visnu reversed His lying posture. Everyone praised the Azhvar's devotion. The king began to give due respect to the devotees, Kancipuram became prosperous. People led their lives happily.

Tirumazhisai Azhvar later proceeded to Kumbhakonam. On his way, while he was in the village called Perumpuliyur, he rested for a while on the pial in front of a house. At that time, some scholars were chanting the Vedas. Seeing the Azhvar, they abruptly stopped the chanting. When they wanted to continue, they lost the continuity. The place where they actually stopped was at *'Krsnanam vrihinam nakha nirbhinnam'* (*Yajur* *Veda* 1.8.9). The meaning goes like this: Rice attained by peeling out the black paddy using the nails. Tirumazhisai Azhvar, realising their struggle, picked up a black paddy from the floor and removed the outer portion with his nails just to remind them from where they should proceed further. The scholars at once felt embarrassed and repentantly prostrated before the Azhvar.

The Azhvar then proceeded towards the temple. Surprisingly, the main Deity turned towards the Azhvar in whichever way he moved! The priests were astonished to see this wonder to other devotees. All of them and also informed Perumpuliyur Adigal and told gathered who was about this to see this performing a Yaga. He invited the Azhvar and honoured him in the Yaga with the prime offering.

People who did not relish this due to jealousy began to criticise the Azhvar. Perumpuliyur Adigal felt distressed about their shameful act. To ease his distress, the Azhvar sang hymn in praise of Sri Visnu:

*akkarangal akkarangal endrum avadenkolo*  
*ikkurumbai mkki ennai isanakka vallaiyel*  
*chakkaram koi kaiyane sadangar vay adangida*  
*utkidanda vanname puram posindu kattide.*

**O Narayana, the holder of Sudarsana Cakra! Please come out and reveal Yourself to shut the mouth of those who are jealous.**

Astounding everyone, Sri Visnu revealed Himself, with Sri Devi and Bhu Devi, in the heart of Tirumazhisai Azhvar, as lying on the snake-mat. Everyone, including those who spoke ill of him, surrendered at the holy feet of the Azhvar. The Azhvar thereafter reached Kumbhakonam.

Once, Tirumazhisai Azhvar placed the palm leaves containing hundreds of hymns written by him (*Prabandham*) on the Kaveri river. To the surprise of those gathered there, two of his collections, namely, *Tiruchandaviruttam* and *Nanmugan* *Tiruvantddi*, moved upstream and reached the Azhvar safely. They understood that these two alone were sufficient to enlighten the devotees.

The Azhvar then visited the temple of Sri Aravamudha Perumal, one of the main Deities of Kumbhakonam, and had

His darshan. The Deity was in lying posture like Sri Rariganatha. Addressing Him, the Azhvar sang a beautiful hymn:

*ncidanda kalgal nondavo? nadunga jiidlam enamdy*  
*idanda mey kulungavo? ilangumal varaicchuram*  
*kadandakal paranda kavirik karaik kudandaiyul*  
*kidandavarezhundirundu pesn vazhikesane!*

**I presume that You are lying down because of the severe leg pain due to running hither and thither in search of Slta Devi in Your incarnation as Sri Rama. I am here only to have Your blessings. Will You not talk to me?**

On hearing the devotee's sincere request, Sri Aravamudha Perumal started getting up from the snake-mat. Immediately the Azhvar stopped Him and said, 'You need not get up. You may kindly speak to me as You are.' Even today, we can have the darshan of the Deity with the head raised above.

Usually, the devotees would be expected to serve the Lord as per His instructions. However, the Lord obeyed this devotee's behest! Because of this, the main Deity Aravamudha Perumal is popularly known as ' Aravamudhazhvan' and Tirumazhisai Azhvar is known as 'Tirumazhisaipiran'.

After living in Kumbhakonam for sometime, the Azhvar continued his holy yatra to Srirangam, Anbil, Tirupati, Tiru Evvul etc. and returned to Kumbhakonam. For several years, he stayed in divine communion and finally attained *Paramapada*.

Let us bow and pray at the lotus feet of Tirumazhisai Azhvar, the pure and sincere devotee of Sri Visnu.

*tan oppan tanay ulan kan tamiyerkum*  
*en opparkku isan imai.*

## 5. Nammazhvar

*vaisakhe tu visakhayam kurukapurikdrijam I*  
*pandyadese kaleradau satharim sainyapam bhaje II*

**I worship Sathari, the incarnation of the commander-inchief (Visvaksena) of the army of Sri Visnu, who was born in the beginning of Kali Yuga as a son of Kari at Kurukapuri in the land of Pandya in the month of Vaisakha (May-June) under the influence of the asterism Visakha (sixteenth stellar mansion).**

Nammazhvar is considered the foremost among the Azhvars. His holy birth was in the southern part of Tamil Nadu. His divine wisdom shone brilliantly like the rays of the sun. He is celebrated as the leader of Sri Vaishnavism and his compositions as Tamil Veda (*Tamizh Marai*). Sri Ramanuja's philosophy was inspired by Nammazhvar's hymns.

Nammazhvar was born to Maran Kariar in Velalar community on Vaisakha-Visakha, full moon day, at Tirukkurugur in Pandya kingdom. His mother, Udaya Nangaiyar, belonged to Tiruvenparisaram in Chera kingdom.

The word 'Satha-vayu' denotes 'evil air'. Karma, the results of past deeds, envelop the baby as 'evil air' in the womb of the mother. That is why the baby cries as soon as it comes out of the mother's womb. When Nammazhvar was born, he angrily pushed away the 'evil air'. Hence he is known as 'Sathakopan'.

He is said to be an incarnation of Tirukkurungudi Perumal. When Mahalaksml did penance to get united with the Lord, She was instructed to be a garland of *Magizham* flower so that the Lord could always wear the garland on His chest. Nammazhvar too used to wear the *Magizham* garland. Hence, he is called Vakulabaranar, one who wears *Magizham* (*Magizha malai anintha Piran*). He is also considered to be an incarnation of Visvaksena, the chief of Sri Visnu's army.

His original name was Maran, the name of his grandfather, and as the meaning suggests, his behaviour was unnatural. In his early days, Nammazhvar never used to open his mouth and talk. His parents were naturally worried. They prayed earnestly to Sri Visnu in the temple at Azhvar Tirunagari.

Adisesa is the umbrella, seat and bed for Sri Visnu. When the Lord took the form of Nammazhvar, he too came down and waited on the Lord in the form of a tamarind tree. One fine morning, Nammazhvar, as a child, slipped down from his mother's lap, crawled slowly towards that tamarind tree, and sat comfortably inside a hollow portion at the bottom of the tree. Due to its association with the Azhvar, the tree itself came to be known as 'Tiruppuli Azhvar'. Nammazhvar stayed there in silence like a dumb person for sixteen years. The Lord sent Visvaksena to teach Nammazhvar the Vedic scriptures. That is how Nammazhvar came to be well-versed in the Vedic lore.

At that time, Madhurakavi, who later became one of the twelve Azhvars, was residing in Ayodhya. He suddenly saw a bright light in the southern direction. Following that light he reached the Tiruppuli Azhvar at Azhvar Tirunagari.

He tried to call the Azhvar sitting quietly under the tree. There was no response. He clapped his hands. Even then there was no response. He then dropped a big stone in front of the Azhvar. He was happy to see the Azhvar graciously opening his eyes on hearing the sound. He made up his mind to talk to the Azhvar somehow or other.

Madhurakavi asked Nammazhvar a puzzling question—*'Settadin vayitril siriyadu piranddl ettait tindru edge kidakkum*?' (If the little one takes birth in the womb of the dead, what would it eat and where would it rest?) The inner meaning of the question goes like this: If there is a connection between Atman and the insentient body, what would it experience and what would it hold as a support.

For the first time, the mute Nammazhvar opened his mouth and replied, *'Attait tindru arige kidakkum'* (It would eat that and stay there). The inner meaning is: The Atman would experience joy and sorrow through the body it has taken, unaware of anything higher. Madhurakavi, stunned by this reply, prostrated straight at the feet of Nammazhvar. He earnestly begged Nammazhvar to take him as his disciple. Nammazhvar accepted him in spite of the disciple being elder to him. Madhurakavi Azhvar recorded the hymns of Nammazhvar on palm leaves.

History has it that the scholars sitting on the *sangappalagai* (a divine seat) at Tamil Sangham in Madurai were thrown off into the water the moment the following hymn of Nammazhvar's *Tiruvaymozhi* was placed on the seat:

*kannan kazhalinai nannum manamudaiyir*  
*ennum tirunamam tinnam naraname!*

We would see a more elaborate version of this incident in the life-story of Madhurakavi Azhvar.

Nammazhvar thought of returning to his celestial world after completing thirtyfive years of yeoman service to mankind. At that moment, Sri Visnu with Sri Mahalaksmi appeared on the celestial mount Garuda along with other Devas and Nammazhvar disappeared in holy light.

Madhurakavi Azhvar built a temple for Nammazhvar at Tirukkurugur and offered his daily prayers.

Nammazhvar was known by different names. Since he was like an elephant goad (*Ankusa*) to other religions, he was known as Parankusar. Since he wore a garland of Magizham flowers, he was known as Vakulabaranar. As his birth took place at Tirukkurugur, he was called Kurugaikkavalar. He had other names like Tiruvaymozhi Peruman, Sathakopan, Vedam Tamizh Seyda Maran and Tiruvazhuthi Valanadar. Since Sri Visnu Himself gave the name 'Nammazhvar'—'he is My own Azhvar'—he came to be popularly known as Nammazhvar.

There is an interesting story about Nammazhvar and Kamban. After the completion of *Ramayana* in Tamil, when Kavi Cakravarti Kamban tried to publicize it, Sri Visnu, through the temple priest, raised the question as to whether he has included any verse in praise of Sathakopan (Nammazhvar). Immediately, Kamban composed, on the spot, an *Antadi* (last word of the previous stanza becomes the first word of the next stanza) in praise of Nammazhvar, which is popularly known as *Sathakopar Antadi*.

With the gracious intention of being available to one and all, Nammazhvar took the form of sandals (*Paduka*) of Sri Visnu. That *Paduka* is known as Sathari and is customarily placed on the heads of the devotees visiting the Lord's temple, thereby connecting them with the Azhvar.

Several great Acaryas have written elaborate commentaries on Nammazhvar's compositions—*Tiruviruttam*, *Tiruvaciriyam*, *Tiruvaymozhi* and *Periya Tiruvantadi*.

Let us earnestly bow our head at the lotus feet of Nammazhvar who propagated the real and true meaning of the sacred Vedas in Tamil!

*—Tiruvaymozhi, 1.1.5*

## 6. Mathurakavi Azhvar

*caitre citrasamudbhutam pandyadese ganamsakam I*  
*sriparankusasadbhaktam madhuram kavimdsraye II*

**I take refuge in him, who was born in the month of Caitra (April-May) under the influence of the asterism Citra (fourteenth stellar mansion) as an incarnation of Garuda, the king of birds, and who had great devotion to Pararikusa.**

Tirukkurugur is a village located on the banks of the sacred river Tamirabarani where the great Nammazhvar took birth. There is another sacred place called Tirukkolur situated close to Tirukkurugur. Madhurakavi took his birth at Tirukkolur as the partial incarnation of Garudazhvar during the month of Caitra, asterism Citra, in a Vaisnava brahmana family who were following the *Sama Veda*.

It is remarkable that both Acarya Nammazhvar and his disciple Madhurakavi Azhvar were born in places very close to each other. Like a dawn before the sunrise, Madhurakavi took birth before Nammazhvar. Madhurakavi learnt Vedic scriptures at an early age. He also became a very good singer and earned the name Madhurakavi.

Madhurakavi Azhvar undertook pilgrimage to different Visnu temples (Divya Desams) and reached Ayodhya, the birth place of Sri Rama, in North India and stayed there. Though he was staying there, he never forgot to bow his head towards the south and pray to his own Tirukkolur Perumal.

One day, he saw a bright light coming from the southern direction. Thinking that some miraculous event must have taken place down south, he immediately began his journey in that direction. Finally, he reached Tirukkurugur. He understood that the light emanated from Nammazhvar sitting under a tamarind tree.

Madhurakavi bowed and earnestly requested Nammazhvar to bless him. But there was no reply from Nammazhvar, who was in a yogic trance. He tried several methods to attract Nammazhvar's attention, but in vain. Finally, he took a big stone and dropped it in front of Nammazhvar thereby creating a big noise. Distracted by that sound, Nammazhvar opened his eyes. Instantly, Madhurakavi Azhvar prostrated before him with full faith and devotion.

Madhurakavi asked Nammazhvar a complicated question— *'Settadin vayitril siriyadu pirandal ettait tindru edge kidakkum*?' (If the little one takes birth in the womb of the dead, what would it eat and where would it rest?) The inner meaning of the question goes like this: If there is a connection between Atman and the insentient body, what would it experience and what would it hold as a support.

For the first time, the mute Nammazhvar opened his mouth and replied, *'Attait tindru adge kidakkum'* (It would eat that and stay there). The inner meaning is: The Atman would experience joy and sorrow through the body it has taken, unaware of anything higher. Madhurakavi, stunned by this reply, once again prostrated straight at the feet of Nammazhvar.

He decided to accept Nammazhvar as his spiritual Acarya. Though Nammazhvar was younger to Madhurakavi, his intelligence, devotion, perseverance, discrimination and vast knowledge attracted Madhurakavi. Madhurakavi not only considered him as his spiritual Guru, but also worshipped him as his Chosen Deity. He required no other deities for worship. He constructed a temple for Nammazhvar and performed daily worship.

Madhurakavi Azhvar never composed hymns in praise of Sri Visnu. Since he had strong belief that Nammazhvar was his only god, he sang hymns in praise of him. Madhurakavi

Azhvar composed eleven hymns with the first hymn beginning with *'Kanninun* *Siruttambinal*...' These eleven hymns are at the middle of *Ndldyira Divya Prabandham*. There is a belief that Nammazhvar himself will appear before the devotee who recites these eleven hymns for 12000 times with earnest devotion and faith. We have seen earlier that Nathamuni had recited thus and had had the darshan of Nammazhvar.

After Nammazhvar's demise, Madhurakavi Azhvar regularly worshipped his portrait in Tirukkurugur. Being instrumental in referring to Nammazhvar as Kari Marar, Sathakopar, Parangusar, Tirukkurugur Nambi, Tiruvazhuthi Valanadar, Tirunagari Perumal, Tiruvaymozhi Perumal, Natkamazh Magizhmalai Marbinar, Vedam Tamizh Seyda Perumal etc., he sang and propagated Nammazhvar's works across the country.

Realizing the popularity these hymns were gaining among the people, poets of Tamil Sangham got jealous. They objected to the use of epithets, given to Nammazhvar unless he was able to defeat them in debate and adorn the *Sarigappalagai*.

*Sarigappalagai* was a kind of divine bench which would float on the Potramarai tank. Poets used to sit on the bench and render their compositions. The bench would expand or contract automatically to provide enough space for any number of poets. If there were no mistakes in their compositions, the bench would support the poets. If there were any mistakes, it would tilt and push them into the water.

Madhurakavi Azhvar gladly accepted the challenge, but said that since Nammazhvar was not alive then, he would place one of his hymns from *Tiruvaymozhi* on the bench. If the divine bench accepted it, then the poets of Tamil Sangham should also give due respect to his Acarya, Nammazhvar. The poets accepted the proposal.

Madhurakavi Azhvar then placed on the sacred divine bench the palm leaf containing the following hymn composed by Nammazhvar:

*kannan kazhalinai nannum manamudaiyir*  
*ennum tirunamam tinnam naraname!*

As was customary, all the three hundreds poets sat on the divine bench along with the palm leaf given by Madhurakavi Azhvar. What a wonder! The divine bench pushed the entire lot of poets into the water retaining only the palm leaf! Thereafter, the poets wholeheartedly accepted the works of Nammazhvar. As an atonement for their disrespect, some of them even composed eulogistic poems in honour of Nammazhvar. One such goes like this:

*semam kurugaiyo seyya tirupparkadalo*  
*namam parahkusamo naranamo-daman*  
*tulavo vagulamo tolirando nangum*  
*idavo perumdn unakku.*

**Realizing that Sri Visnu Himself had incarnated in the form of Nammazhvar, they began to publicly sing glories of Nammazhvar.**

One such song is as follows:

*iyaduvado garudarku edire iravikkedir minmini aduvado*  
*nay oduvado urumip pulimun nari kesari mun nadaiyaduvado*  
*pey aduvado azhagu urvasimun perumdn vagulabharanan arid kiirndu*  
*oduvadu urai ayiramamaraiyin oru sorperumo ulagil kaviye!*

**Like a small fly trying to fly in front of the king of birds, Garuda, like a firefly trying to show its light in front of the rays of the sun, like a dog trying to bark in front of a fierce tiger, like a fox trying to walk in front of a lion, like a ghost trying to dance in front of the beautiful celestial dancer Urvasi, our songs too remain so in comparison with even to a single word of Tamil Veda of Nammazhvar, the Vakulabaranar.**

Let us salute the lotus feet of Madhurakavi Azhvar who worshipped Acarya Nammazhvar as God Himself, eulogised him, and finally through his grace attained the final beatitude.

*pukka kadal adimaip payanandre.*  
*-Kanninun Siruttambu, 9*

## 7. Kulasekhara Azhvar

*kumbhe punarvasau jatam kerale colapattane I*  
*kaustubhamsam dharadhisam kulasekharamdsraye II*

**I take refuge in King Kulasekhara (the ruler of Kerala), who was born in Cholapattana (Tiruvanjikkulam) of Kerala in the month of Magha (February-March) under the asterism Punarvasu (seventh stellar mansion) as the incarnation of the Kaustubha (the gem of Sri Visnu).**

Kulasekhara Azhvar was born in Tiruvanjikkulam of Chera kingdom on asterism Magha-Punarvasu. His father was King Dhridavratan. Kulasekhara Azhvar was the incarnation of Kaustubha, the gem worn by Sri Visnu on his chest. Apart from being a learned scholar, he also was a great warrior. He ruled the kingdom after his father in a good and just manner. He had one daughter called Cherakulavalli, an incarnation of Nila Devi, and a son named Dhridavratan.

Kulasekhara was very much devoted to Sri Rama and z Sri Krsna, the divine incarnations of Sn Visnu. He was devoted to the Deities of Divya Desams like Srirarigam, Tirupati, Tirukkannapuram, Tirucitirakkudam and Tiruvitthuvakkodu. He arranged discourses on *Ramayana* at his court every day. He enjoyed hearing them with great faith and devotion. Moreover, he eagerly invited and entertained Vaisnavas.

One day, *Ramayana* recital was going on as usual. The narrator was depicting the episode of the battle between Sri Rama on one side and fourteen thousand demons on the other, instigated by Surppanaka who had her nose cut. The narration was so beautiful and natural that Kulasekhara suddenly got up in a rage and declared, 'Here I am to help Rama along with my army.' He immediately drew his sword and began to march on. The narrator, frightened by this sudden development, hurriedly narrated that Sri Rama himself killed all the fourteen thousand demons single handedly and returned as a hero.

It was only after hearing that was Kulasekhara pacified and withdrew his march. From then on, the narrator dealt with Sri Rama's victories in great detail and glossed over his defeats.

On another day, hearing that Ravana had abducted Sita he rose fuming with rage. 'I would right away go and kill Ravana and rescue Sita,' he exclaimed and called for his army. Nobody knew what to do. Kulasekhara went straight up to the sea and jumped into the water to swim across. At that moment, pleased with the devotion of Kulasekhara, Sri Rama appeared before him along with Sita and Lakshmana. He pacified the king by explaining that He had already killed Ravana and rescued Sita. Kulasekhara Azhvar was deeply moved and his joy knew no bounds. He felt Sri Rama's delight or distress as his own. Hence, he was called 'Perumal'. Because of his ardent devotion, he saw the devotees as the Lord Himself and treated them with great honour.

On hearing about the greatness of Sri Rariganatha, Kulasekhara Azhvar desired to have the darshan of Sri Rariganatha. He also fixed a date for his departure. His ministers knew very well that he would never return to his kingdom after the darshan of Sri Rariganatha. They therefore hit upon a plan. Just before his departure on the scheduled day, the ministers sent some Vaisnavas meet him. The king was very much pleased to meet them and invited them for a feast. He postponed his trip to Srirarigam. Using the same ploy, the ministers kept on postponing his trip to Srirarigam.

Finding that the Vaisnavas had a lot of freedom to move freely in the palace, some jealous ministers wanted to poison the king's mind against them. On the day of Sri Ramanavami, when the Deity was bathed, some of these ministers hid a costly pearl necklace meant for the Lord. The priests reported about the missing ornament to the king. The king ordered the ministers to find the thief immediately. The ministers, using this opportunity, accused the Vaisnavas of this misdeed.

Kulasekhara Azhvar felt sad about this untoward suspicion towards those true devotees. He asserted that the devotees of Sri Visnu would never indulge in such a mean act. Swearing on the Lord, he said he would prove this to all of them. He then instructed to bring a snake in a pot. He put his hand inside the pot saying, 'If any one of the devotees had committed this theft, let the snake bite my hands.' The snake, bound by truth, did no harm to the king. The ministers realized their fault and begged his pardon. Saying that it would not be enough if he pardoned them, he ordered them to serve the devotees of the Lord. They accordingly served the devotees till the end of their lives and finally attained Mukti.

Having lost interest in ruling the kingdom, Kulasekhara Azhvar transferred all the responsibilities to his son Dhridavratan and proceeded to Srirarigam fulfilling his long-cherished desire.

Kulasekhara Azhvar stayed at Srirarigam for long years. The z z festival Deity at Srirarigam is known as Sri Azhagiya Manavalan. Cherakulavalli, the daughter of Kulasekhara Azhvar, was considering Sri Azhagiya Manavalan as her Divine Husband.

Therefore, Kulasekhara Azhvar gave his daughter Cherakulavalli, the incarnation of Nila Devi, in marriage to Sri Azhagiya Manavalan. Even now, on Sri Ramanavami at Srirangam, Sri Azhagiya Manavalan and Cherakulavalli give darshan to the devotees.

Kulasekhara Azhvar constructed the third corridor to the temple of Sri Rariganatha. Even now it is called *'Kulasekharan Vidi'*. He spent his time serving the devotees there.

Later on, he went on a pilgrimage to Tirupati, Tirucitrakkudam, Tirukkannapuram, Tirumalirurichblai, Tiruvitthuvakkodu, Ayodhya and other places of worship (Divya Desams) and sang in praise of God. Then he proceeded to Tirukkurugur, prayed to Nammazhvar, and stayed at Mannarkoil and did service to Sri Rajagopalan. He finally attained the Paramapada at the age of sixtyseven.

His compositions are known as *'Perumal Tirumozhi'*. His lullaby on Sri Rama is very popular even today. He composed the *Ramayana* in a simple Tamil language.

Kulasekhara Azhvar said, 'O Lord! I am not at all interested inattaining virtue, wealth or passion. Let anything happen to me according to my fate. But I earnestly request you to bless me with a boon. I should never lose my devotion to Your lotus feet in the births to come.'

Let us offer our humble pranams at the lotus feet of Kulasekhara Azhvar.

## 8. Periyazhvar

*jyesthe svdtibhavam visnurathamsam dhanvinah pure I*  
*prapadye svasuram visnorvisnucittam purahsikham II*

**I take refuge in Visnucitta (whose mind would ever remain permeated with Visnu-consciousness) who was born at Dhanvinahpura (Srlvilliputtur) under the influence of the asterism Svati (fifteenth stellar mansion) in the month of Jyestha (June-July) as the incarnation of Visnu's chariot, who was reputed as the father-in-law of Visnu (his daughter being married by Narayana Himself) and who belonged to the brahmana community called Purahsikha (who had tufts on their foreheads).**

Periyazhvar took birth in Srlvilliputtur Pandya kingdom in a Vaisnava brahmana family during the month of Jyestha and asterism Svati. His parents were Mukundacarya and Padhumaiyar. He was the fifth son to them. He was the incarnation of the Lord's Chariot which is also a symbol of the Vedas. His father named him Visnucitta. He performed the sacred thread ceremony for his son at the right time. He also got him educated in the Vedic literature and got him married at an appropriate age.

Sri Visnu is known by the name Sri Vatapatrasayi in Srivilliputtur. Visnucitta wanted to serve Sri Vatapatrasayi befittingly and gave a thought to it. In the life-story of Sri Krsna, it is said that when He was walking along the streets of Mathura with His brother Balarama, He met a garland seller who used to regularly supply garlands to Kamsa. He asked him for a garland and wore it with great pleasure. After reading this episode, Visnucitta wanted to offer best flower garlands to the Lord. He started plucking and collecting various flowers and making beautiful big garlands with great devotion offered them to Sri Vatapatrasayi.

During that period, King Vallabhadeva was ruling the Pandya kingdom with Madurai as his capital. While he was moving about his capital in disguise, he saw a man sleeping on a platform. He woke him up and enquired about him. That man replied that he had been on a pilgrimage to Divya Desams (sacred places of Sri Visnu throughout India) and also had had a bath in Ganges river. While conversing thus, the king requested him to tell a verse. The stranger told the following:

*varsartham astau prayateta masan nisartham artham divasam yateta I*  
*vdrddhakya hetorvayasd navena paratra hetoriha janmana ca II*

**One should preserve things required for the four months of the rainy season during the (remaining) period of eight months. One should procure things needed for the night during the daytime. One should struggle and work hard to earn enough money during young age for a comfortable living in the later part of life. To attain a blissful life after death and in the next birth, one should take care of this life.**

King Vallabhadeva contemplated on the verse and its meaning throughout that night. At dawn, he realized that he had the means for the first three requirements, but regretted that he had none for the fourth.

He immediately proceeded to meet Selvanambhi who was well-versed in Vedic \| scriptures and requested him to tell him what he should do to attain bliss in afterlife. Selvanambhi thought that the king would not accept his views and advised the king to put his question to the scholars and follow their advice. Accepting the suggestion, the king got a bag containing gold (*Porkizhi*) tied in the palace hall. He then declared that this bag of gold would be given as a gift to one who answers his question.

Sri Vatapatrasayl wanted to enact a divine play through Visnucitta. So, He appeared in his dream and ordered him to get the prize money. Visnucitta replied that it was the privilege of learned scholars and not for him who was engaged in the humble service of making flower garlands for his Lord. To that Sn Visnu said, 'Do you think you are doing this service with your own capacity? Therefore, do not worry on this account. It is I who am going to reveal the Truth through you. So go and participate.'

Visnucitta was contemplating on this dream even on the next day. During that time, the Lord's servants gathered in front of his humble dwelling with decorated palanquin, banners and trumpets. They informed Visnucitta that Sri Visnu had instructed them to take him in the palanquin to Madurai. Visnucitta felt happy at the blessings of God and proceeded to the palace at Madurai.

King Vallabhadeva had already come to know about Visnucitta through Selvanambhi. On seeing his luminous face, the king got up from his throne and warmly welcomed him, prostrated at his feet, and eulogised him as 'Bhattar Piran'.

There were many learned scholars of different faiths in the king's court. They resented that the king offered his respects to an inerudite, overlooking the presence of many erudite scholars assembled there. Noticing this, Selvanambhi requested Visnucitta to explain the Vedantic conception of God to the king.

By the grace of Sri Visnu, Visnucitta started describing about the Lord to the assembled scholars with the help of the Vedas, Upanisads, Itihasas and Puranas. Finally, he stressed that one should totally surrender at the lotus feet of Sri Visnu to get rid of repeated births and deaths.

When Visnucitta established that Sri Narayana was the Supreme Reality, the bag containing gold lowered down of itself in front of Visnucitta. Praising the mysterious ways of the Lord, he accepted it. Everyone was amazed. Everyone, including the king, prostrated at his feet with reverence.

As a great honour, King Vallabhadeva put him on his own royal elephant, proclaiming the titles *'Vedappayan kollavalla Visnucittar* *vandar* ('Visnucitta, the recipient of the gift of Vedas, is coming'), *'Meyndthan vandar'* ('the true head of spiritual knowledge is coming'), *'Meyyadiydr vandar’* ('the true devotee is coming') etc. The king himself led the procession with great enthusiasm. The scholars followed him holding things like umbrella, flag, flyflapper and fan.

When Visnucitta was taken in a procession, to witness it Sri Visnu and His consorts appeared in the sky seated on Garuda. Visnucitta, however, feared that evil may befall the Lord when seen by wicked eyes.

Ringing the bells on the elephant to keep his beats, Visnucitta sang 'Pallandu' to ward off all evil:

*pallandu pallandu pallayirattandu*  
*palakddi nurayiram*  
*mallanda tinthoj manivanna!*  
*up sevadi sevvi tirukkappu!*

**To wipe out his fear, Sri Visnu showed his broad shoulders, hands which killed the Asuras, Pancajanya, the Conch, and Sudarsana, the Discus. On seeing these, he further trembled and sang one more** ***'Pallandu'*****. Sri Visnu, moved by his affection, gave him the title ‘*****Periyazhvar'*** **and disappeared.**

Thereafter, Periyazhvar taught the king that rendering service to God, singing praises of Him, and dedicating one's life for Him and His devotees are the means to attain eternal bliss. This forms the essence of *'Pallandu'*.

With the gold he got, Periyazhvar served Sri Vatapatrasayi in various ways. He established a beautiful garden and grew many flower plants. He planted his dear Tulasi (Basil) plant and nurtured it as his own life. Using varieties of flowers, he would make garlands for the Lord and offer them, singing praises of the Lord.

Bound by the affection and devotion of the Azhvar, Goddess Bhuma Devi incarnated in the form of a child in the Tulasi grove. Periyazhvar found the child, gave her the name Goda, and brought her up with great affection.

Periyazhvar's hymns are called *Periyazhvar Tirumozhi*. He lived up to the age of eightyfive. Finally, while serving the Lord at Tirumalirunjolai, he attained the *Paramapada*.

Let us salute the lotus feet of Periyazhvar who lived a sacred life serving God by hymns and garlands, who had Goddess Bhuma Devi as his child Andal, and who became the father-in-law of Sri Rariganatha.

## 9. Sri Andal

*asddhe puruaphalgunyam tulasikdnanodbhavdm I*  
*pandye visvambharam goddm vande srirangandyikdm II*

**I worship Sri Ranganatha's consort (Andal) who was born in a Tulasi grove in the month of Asadha (July-August) under the influence of the asterism Purvaphalguni (eleventh stellar mansion) in Pandya kingdom as an incarnation of Bhu Devi (supporter of the Universe).**

Srivilliputtur in Pandya kingdom had a beautiful flower garden nurtured by Periyazhvar. He used to prepare different types of garlands from varieties of flowers and offered the same to Sri Visnu with great devotion.

One fine morning while plucking flowers, Periyazhvar was taken aback hearing a child cry. Exploring, he was surprised to see a divinely beautiful girl-child in the Tulasi grove. As an incarnation of Bhuma Devi, the child came to the world on the day of Asadha-Purvaphalgum. Periyazhvar was delighted to have this child as a blessing and named her 'Goda'.

Right from childhood, Goda had unflinching devotion to Sri Krsna. She considered Srivilliputtur as Vrindavan, and herself a Gopika. She would wear the garlands (meant for the Lord) and would look at the mirror and ask herself whether she was a match for the Beautiful Krsna. Then she would remove the garland and place it back without the Azhvar's knowledge. This went on for several days. Unaware of all this, Periyazhvar would offer the garland to the Lord. The Lord too would accept that with great delight.

One day, Periyazhvar happened to see his daughter Goda wearing the garland meant for the Lord. Distressed to see this, he lamented that a grave offence had been committed in his service and that his own daughter had been the cause for it. He chided his daughter for her 'misdeed'. He prepared another fresh garland and offered it to the Lord. He became very sad seeing the Lord's face turn gloomy. He concluded that it was only because of his daughter's act. Periyazhvar began his description—from Sri Ranganatha to Sri Vaikunthanatha. It is through Periyazhvar that we have come to know of many Divya Desams and their importance.

Andal felt attracted to Sri Ranganatha. She has beautifully described her dreams about her marriage in the *Varanamayiram* songs.

But Periyazhvar was wondering how to get her married to Sri Rariganatha! One night, the Lord appeared in the dream of the Azhvar and commanded him to bring Andal to Him as a bride. Similarly, He appeared in the dreams of King Vallabhadeva and the temple authorities and commanded them to bring Andal as a bride to the temple.

Next day, the king accordingly sent the palanquin and other retinue. As per the wish of Sri Rariganatha, Periyazhvar took the bride Andal and presented her to Him in the temple. A flash of light appeared, and while everyone was watching, Andal merged into Sri Rariganatha.

The Lord graciously said, 'O Azhvar! Since you married your daughter to Me, from now on you are My fatherin-law. Continue your service to Me in Srivilliputtur and finally attain Me.'

Although the Azhvar felt sad at his separation from his daughter, he came around and reconciled himself.

As she offered garlands to the Lord after wearing them, she is known as '*Chudikkodutta Chudarkodiyal'*. And since she spiritually rules over the people through her songs, she is called 'Andal'.

Let us salute the lotus feet of Sri Andal who appeared in this world as an incarnation of Goddess Bhuma Devi, who offered garlands to the Lord after wearing them herself, who devoted z her entire life for the sake of Sri Rariganatha, who composed the great *Tiruppavai* and *Nacciyar Tirumozhi* and finally held the hand of Sri Rariganatha as Sri Rariganayaki.

## 10. Tondaradippodi Azhvar

*kodande jyesthanaksatre mandarigudipurodbhavam I*  
*colorvyam vanamalamsam bhaktarighrirenumasraye II*

**I take refuge in that greatest of devotees known by the name "Dust-of-the-Devotees' Feet" who was born under the influence of the asterism Jyestha (eighteenth stellar mansion) in the month of Agrahayana (December-January) as an incarnation of Sri Visnu's Vanamala (the garland made of forest flowers) at Mandangudipura (near Tiruchirapalli) in the kingdom of Chozhas.**

Tondaradippodi Azhvar took birth as the incarnation of Sri Visnu's Vaijayanti mala. He was born in the month of Agrahayana and asterism Jyestha in the Vaisnava brahmana community at a place called Tirumandangudi in Chozha kingdom situated on the bank of Kaveri river. His original name was Vipranarayana. He was devoted to Sri Visnu right from young age.

Vipranarayana lived in Srirarigam. He established and maintained a beautiful flower garden there. He used to serve Sri Ranganatha by making garlands with the flowers grown in that garden. As he was unmarried, everyone held him in great regard.

On the northern side of Srirangam, there was a place called Tirukkarambanur. There lived a woman called Devadevl. One day, while returning with her elder sister after getting a lot of gifts from the Chozha king for dancing and singing, she was attracted to the beautiful garden of Vipranarayana and entered it. Devadevi removed her costly dress and jewels and handed them over to her elder sister. As usual, he continued his service of offering garlands to Sri Visnu. Devadevi patiently waited for a right opportunity to distract his mind.

One day, it was raining heavily. Seeing Devadevi getting drenched in rain, he took pity on her. He invited her inside and gave her his own cloth to wear. Devadevi was very happy that the time had come to allure Vipranarayana and was successful in her attempt. Alas! Vipranarayana, losing himself, became entrapped in her beauty. He even forgot his daily service of offering garlands to Sri Rariganatha. Months passed thus.

Devadevi, satisfied that she had fulfilled her promise, returned home without even taking leave of Vipranarayana. Unable to be separated from Devadevi, Vipranarayana went in search of her. At last he could locate her, but she turned him away because he was bereft of any wealth. However, Vipranarayana continued to hover around her house.

Sri Ranganatha took pity on His devotee and wanted him to realize his fault and to continue his servise to Him. He took a golden plate from the temple and went in disguise to the house of Devadevi. Introducing Himself as a disciple of Vipranarayana, He gave the plate to her. Devadevi, much pleased to see the golden gift, opened the door and invited Vipranarayana to enter inside.

Next day, the priests noticed the theft and informed the king. The king ordered for a thorough search for the missing golden plate. It came to light through a servant of Devadevi that the plate was in her hause. The enraged king imprisoned Vipranarayana who had given her the plate.

He washed the feet of the devotees with water and consumed that water to purify himself. Realizing that it was through the service of the devotees of the Lord that he could attain salvation, he called himself Tondaradippodi.

Continuing his service like before, he attained the *Paramapada* at the age of one hundred and five.

Tondaradippodi Azhvar used to sing only in praise of Sri Ranganatha, which were compiled as *Tiruppalli Ezhucchi* and *Tirumalai*.

Let us adore and worship the feet of Tondaradippodi Azhvar who served Sri Ranganatha through garlands of flowers and garlands of hymns.

## 11. Tiruppan Azhvar

*kartike rohimjatam snpanam niculapure I*  
*srivatsamsam gayakendram munivahanamasraye II*

**I take refuge in Munivahana known as Sri Pana, who was born in Niculapura (Uraiyur) in the month of Kartika (November-December) under the influence of the asterism Rohini (fourth stellar mansion), who is the incarnation of (Visnu's) Srivatsa, and who is the foremost among singers (of the Lord's glory).**

Tiruppan Azhvar, the incarnation of Sri Visnu's Srivatsam, was born in a place called Uraiyur situated in Chozha kingdom during the month Kartika and asterism Rohini.

He appeared near a bush in a paddy field. One kind hearted Panar (a minstrel) saw him there and brought him home. As he was also known as Panar, he was not allowed to enter the Srirarigam temple due to customary caste prohibitions.

Panar used to get up early in the morning and take bath in the Kaveri river. Then he used to sit on the Kaveri bank with *Yazh* (a stringed musical instrument) in one hand and sing hymns with his sweet voice in praise of Sri Rariganatha with great devotion. He used to do this regularly as a sacred service. Listening to his melodious and heartfelt hymns in praise of the Lord, every one used to call him as Tiruppan Azhvar.

One of the priests, Lokasarariga Muni, used to perform worship to Sri Rariganatha with great devotion. He would bring water from Kaveri river for the Abhiseka (Tirumarijanam or ritualistic bath of the deity) of the Lord.

One day, as usual, when Lokasarariga Muni was on his way to the temple carrying water from Kaveri, Panar was singing the glories of Sri Rariganatha in an ecstatic mood. He did not notice Lokasarariga Muni coming in the opposite direction. Immersed in divine contemplation, he could not hear Lokasarariga ordering him to move away from his path. In a fit of anger, the priest threw a stone at him. It struck the forehead of Panar and he began to bleed. Startled, Panar opened his eyes. Panar, without minding his own injury, begged his pardon and hurried away.

Lokasarariga then went to the temple. To his surprise, the sanctum sanctorum was locked from inside. He tried to open the door, but in vain. All the priests gathered there were filled with fear and wonder. Lokasarariga thought that he should have committed some grave offence which had displeased the Lord. He earnestly prayed to the Lord begging His pardon. At that moment, a divine voice said from the sanctum sanctorum, 'Lokasarariga! The person who was sitting and singing with the Yazh is My staunch devotee. You had hit him with a stone and injured his forehead. You should carry him on your shoulder, go around the temple, and bring him to Me. Only then will the door be opened.' Like an insane man, Lokasarariga ran towards the bank of Kaveri river. He saw Tiruppan Azhvar there, prostrated before him, and begged his pardon. Seeing this, Tiruppan Azhvar felt embarrassed and pleaded that it was his fault.

Ignoring Tiruppan Azhvar's words, Lokasarariga lifted and placed him on his shoulders, and carried him to the temple. Since the priest became his vehicle, Tiruppan Azhvar came to be known as 'Muni Vahanar' ('One who had the priest as his vehicle'). Soon after reaching the main entrance of the sanctum sanctorum, Lokasarariga gently placed Tiruppan Azhvar down. Immediately the door opened and Sri Rariganatha gave darshan. Tiruppan Azhvar shed tears of ecstatic joy. Beholding Sri Rariganatha from foot to head, he started singing ten hymns starting with *'Amalanadipiran'* in praise of Him.

The Azhvar did not want to see anything else after having had the darshan of his beloved Sri Rariganatha. Suddenly a bright divine flame appeared and Tiruppan Azhvar merged in it. Everyone was wonderstruck seeing this.

Let us salute at the lotus feet of Tiruppan Azhvar who attained to great heights of spirituality because of his devotion to the Lord.

## 12. Tirumarigai Azhvar

*kartike krttikajatam catuskavisikhdmanim I*  
*satprabandhakrtam sarrigamurtim kaliyamasraye II*

**I take refuge in Kaliyan (Tirumarigai) who was born in the month of Kartika (November-December) under the influence of the asterism Krttika (third stellar mansion) as an incarnation of Sri Visnu's bow, Sarriga, and who was the crest-jewel among the poets accomplished in four kinds of poetry and the composer of six Prabandhams.**

Tirumarigai Azhvar, the incarnation of Sri Visnu's bow, Sarrigam, came to this world during the month of Kartika on Krttika asterism at Tirukkuraiyalur, a village near Tiruvali (Tirunagari) in Chozha kingdom. He belonged to Kallar community.

Since Azhvar was dark in complexion, he was called Nilan. He was brought up according to his community and culture. He was well trained in warfare. He served as an army commander of the Chozha king and won many battles. Hence he was known as Parakalar (Yama, the god of death) for his enemies. Honouring his valour, the king made him a vassal of Tirumarigai. Because of this great honour, he was called Tirumangai Mannan (the king of Tirumangai). Tirumangai was very much interested in dance, music and drama of Tamil culture.

In his territory, there was a lotus tank in a place called Tiruvellakkulam. Once, a celestial damsel came to have a bath in that tank. At that time, a devotee known as Maruthavan was there near the tank. He adopted that damsel as his own daughter. Since she was holding a kumuda flower in her hand, he named her Kumudavalli.

Hearing about the beauty of Kumadavalli, Tirumangai Mannan wanted to marry her. Kumudavalli belonged to the brahmana community, whereas Tirumangai Mannan belonged to Kallar community. The intelligent father Maruthavan left the choice to his daughter and said that if she was willing to marry Tirumangai Mannan he would have no objection.

Kumudavalli, however, was firm that she would marry only a Sri Vaisnava sanctified with the panca-samskaras. When Tirumangai Mannan was informed about this, he directly proceeded to meet Tirunaraiyur Nambi and requested him to sanctify him as a Sri Vaisnava. After the panca-samskaras, he returned to Kumudavalli and said that he had become a sanctified Sri Vaisnava and pleaded her to marry him.

Kumudavalli, however, laid one more condition that every day he should serve food to a thousand Sri Vaisnava devotees (*Thadiyaradhanam*) continuously for a year. He promised to do so. Both of them then got married.

When he started feeding a thousand Vaisnavas daily, his wealth gradually dwindled. He started using the tax money meant for the Chozha king. Coming to know of this, the king got furious and sent soldiers to collect the money. Tirumangai Mannan assured them that he would pay in due course, but failed to do so. Once again the king sent his soldiers to warn him that if he failed to pay the money he would be imprisoned.

Tirumangai Mannan, however, mounted his horse called 'Adal Ma' and fought with them. Gaining victory, he drove them away.

Sri Visnu, awaiting the right moment to bless Tirumangai Mannan by putting him on the right path, appeared in his dream and directed him to go to Kancipuram and find a treasure there. Trusting the dream, he requested the king to send his minister with him to Kancipuram so that he could hand over the money and clear his debts. The king agreed to his request and sent his minister to accompany him to Kancipuram. As per the Divine Will, Tirumangai Mannan traced a huge treasure near the bank of Vehavati river in Kancipuram.

Extremely delighted, Tirumarigai Mannan cleared his debts and continued his *Thadlyarddhanam* with the rest of the money. That wealth also eventually dwindled.

Since he wanted to keep his promise made to his wife at any cost, and since he could not find any other way to earn money, he returned to his own hereditary profession of thieving. Four of his close friends joined him. The first friend possessed a miraculous power of walking on water. Hence he was called 'Nirmel Nadappan'. The second one could hide himself in the shadow of any person without his knowledge. Hence he was known as 'Nizhalil Oduhguvan'. The third one could easily open the lock of any door just by blowing air through his mouth. He was called 'Thaludhuvan'. The fourth one, like an able advocate, could win any court case. Hence he was known as 'Tholavazhakkan'. With the help of those four friends, Tirumarigai Mannan used to waylay the merchants and spend that money for *Thadiyaradhanam*. This went on for many days.

Once, a young couple bedecked with costly jewellery crossed the place called Tirumanarikollai. Tirumarigai Mannan, who was then hiding under a pipal tree, came out and encircled them with his four friends. Threatening them with his sword, he ordered them to remove all the jewellery. As if frightened, the young couple removed their jewels and placed them on a cloth spread on the ground. Inspecting them from head to foot, Tirumarigai Mannan noticed that one of the rings on the second toe of the bridegroom was not removed. He ordered him to quickly remove that also.

When the man tried to remove it in vain, Tirumangai Mannan himself tried to remove the ring and being unable to do so, used his teeth to pull it out. The bridegroom laughed at this and referred to him as Kaliyan ('the mighty'). Thenceforth, Tirumangai Mannan was also known as Kaliyan.

Tirumangai Mannan bundled the valuables and tried to pick it up. But he could not lift the bundle off the ground. Although he used his full strength, he was unable to even move it.

Perplexed, Tirumangai Mannan accosted the bridegroom whether he was a sorcerer. The bridegroom mischievously accepted that he was a sorcerer and invited him to come closer to learn the magical formula. When he came near, the stranger uttered the Astaksara Mantra (eight syllable mantra) into his ears. Next moment, both the bridegroom and the bride disappeared and revealed themselves as Sri Mahavisnu and Sri Mahalaksmi. Tirumangai Mannan got transformed into Tirumangai Azhvar.

As a result of getting the instruction of the mantra from the Lord Himself, and having had the darshan of His divine and auspicious form, the ignorance of Tirumangai was dispelled and he began to immediately sing several hymns beginning with *Vadinen Vadi* in praise of Sri Visnu.

He worshipped the Lord in several Divya Desams and whenever he would be overwhelmed with devotion, he would sing and dance in ecstasy. His disciples therefore called him as ' Nalukaviperumal', 'Arulmari', 'Arattamukkhi', 'Adayar Siyam', 'Ali Nadar' etc.

It is said that the Azhvar and the Saiva saint Tirujnanasambandhar happened to meet once and that the Saiva saint was so impressed with the Azhvar's devotion, poetic skills and service to the devotees that he presented a trident to him.

Tirumangai Azhvar had a desire to construct the Vimana and surrounding walls for the Srirangam temple. He went in search of the required money. When he came to know that there was a big treasure in Nagapattinam, he went there and brought it. With that wealth, he fulfilled his desire by erecting halls and walls for the Srlrarigam temple.

Tirumahgai Azhvar had composed six *Prabandhams*, namely, *Periya* *Tirumozhi*, *Tirukkuruntandagam*, *Tiruneduntdndagam*, *Siriya Tirumadal*, *Periya Tirumadal* and *Tiruvezhukutrirukkai*. All these six *Prabandhams* are considered equivalent to the six *Vedahgas*. He also had composed a *Citrakavi* called *Rathabandham*. Apart from these, he had also composed wonderful songs like *Asukavi* and *Madhurakavi*.

Filled with delight on hearing his *Tiruneduntdndagam*, Sri Rariganatha asked him to seek a boon. The Azhvar replied that he would like to listen to the Tiruvdymozhi hymns composed by Nammazhvar. Following the instruction of Sri Rariganatha, Nammazhvar came to Srlrangam. He recited the complete *Tiruvaymozhi* in just ten days. *Tirumahgai* Azhvar was overwhelmed hearing it. He was amazed at the grace of the Lord. Fie then paid appropriate respects to Nammazhvar and saw him off.

Finally, Tirumahgai Azhvar reached a place called Tirukkururigudi and attained the *Paramapada*. Even today one can see the temple of Tirumahgai Azhvar at that place.

Let us salute at the lotus feet of Tirumahgai Azhvar who advised us to think about Sri Narayana when we are in distress, and to recite His Name wholeheartedly with complete faith to ward off the effects of evil fate.
