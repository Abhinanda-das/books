Ujjvala-nilamani is a supplement to Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu, which describes all the rasa, yet the conjugal one only in brief. Ujjvala-nilamani describes the conjugal rasa in great detail, following the general pattern of analyzing vibhavas, anubhavas, vyabhicari-bhavas, sattvika-bhavas and sthayi-bhavas. Parakiya and svakiya forms of love are discussed. Jiva Goswami in his commentary justifies svakiya whereas Visvanatha Cakravarti accepts parakiya as stronger.

# Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi-kiraṇa \[BS\]

## Chapter 1 - Nāyaka-bheda. Different Types of Heroes

Now I will discuss ujjvala-rasa. In this rasa the chief hero (*nāyaka*) is Kṛṣṇa. He manifests himself threefold: as most perfect in Gokula, more perfect in Mathurā and perfect in Dvārakā. Each of these forms has four varieties: *dhīrodātta, dhīralalita, dhīroddhata* and *dhīraśānta*. The *dhīrodātta* is grave, tolerant, and respectful to all people according to what they deserve. Rāmacandra is an example. The *dhīralalita* hero is clever, blossoming with new youth, free of worries, and controlled by his lover. An example is Cupid. The *dhīroddhata* is arrogant, angry, cheating, and boastful, like Bhīma. The *dhīraśānta* acts according to scripture, controls his senses and follows rules of dharma, like Yudhiṣṭhira.

Each of these types may be divided into two types: the husband (*pati*) and man attracted to a woman without marriage (*upapati*). These may be further divided into four types: favorable (*anukūla*), compliant (*dakṣiṇa*), deceitful (*śaṭha*) and impudent (*dhṛṣṭa*). The hero attracted to only one heroine is called *anukūla.* The hero who sees everything equally is called *dakṣiṇa*. The deceitful lover (*śaṭha*) is he who acts without affection while speaking pleasing words to his lover. The *dhṛṣṭa* is he who lies without fear, while having marks of enjoying with another woman. This makes ninety-six types of hero.

## Chapter 2 - Nāyikā-vibhāga. Divisions of Heroines

The heroine is the āśraya. There are two types: married to Kṛṣṇa (*svakīya*) and not married to Kṛṣṇa (*parakīya*). Among the girls who performed Kātyāyanī vrata those who married Kṛṣṇa by mutual agreement (gāndharva marriage) are considered married lovers (*svakīya*) in Vraja. The unmarried girls such as Dhanyā (who did not make such vows) are parakīya. Rādhā and her group are the parakīyās who had husbands. Though some gopīs in Gokula are married to Kṛṣṇa (by gāndharva vows), out of fear of their parents they act as if they are parakīya. In Dvārakā, Rukmiṇī and others are married heroines.

There are three types of heroines in relation to love of a man: mild (*mugdhā*), moderate (*madhyā*) and bold (*pragalbhā*). The moderate heroine is of three types: calm during anger (*dhīrā*), uncontrolled during anger (*adhīrā*) and sometimes controlled or uncontrolled during anger (*dhīrādhīrā*). She who is calm during her anger scolds her lover with crooked words. The moderate heroine who is uncontrolled during anger out of wrath scolds her lover harshly. The heroine who is a mixture of both of these tendencies (*dhīrādhīra-madhya*) is Rādhā.

Those who are bold (*pragalbha*) are also of three types: controlled during anger, uncontrolled during anger and mixed. The bold heroine with controlled anger such as Pālikā, Candrāvalī or Bhadrā, hides her anger and is neutral during love affairs. The bold heroine who is uncontrolled during her anger, such as Śyāmalā, is cruel and beats Kṛṣṇa with a lotus or ear ornament, and scolds him. The bold heroine with mixed tendency during anger, such as Maṅgalā, scolds him a little after hiding her anger.

The mild heroine is of only one type: she remains silent in extreme anger. Thus there are three types of moderate heroines, three types of bold heroines and one type of mild heroine in anger, totaling seven types. All these may be either married to Kṛṣṇa (*svakīya)* or married to another man (*parakīya*). Thus there are fourteen types in all. The mild heroines also have one variety which is unmarried (kanyā). This makes fifteen varieties of heroines.

These heroines act in eight different ways:

- *abhisārikā (*going for an appointment*),*
- *vāsa-sajjā* (decorating herself)*,*
- *unkanthitā* (anxiety because of delay),
- *khaṇḍitā* (devastated)*,*
- *vipralabdhā* (feeling cheated)*,*
- *kalahāntaritā* (separation by quarrel)*,*
- *proṣita-preyasī (*great separation*)*
- *svādhīna-bharṭrkā* (controlling the lover)

The heroine who personally goes to meet Kṛṣṇa is called abhisarikā. *Vāsaka-sajjā* is the heroine who, desirous of enjoying with Kṛṣṇa, prepares garlands, betel nut, and a pleasure bed in the grove. The *virahotkaṇṭhitā* heroine longs for Kṛṣṇa in separation when he delays in coming. The *vipralabdhā* heroine is she whom Kṛṣṇa promises to meet but then avoids meeting. The *khaṇḍitā* heroine is she who looks at Kṛṣṇa with anger when he comes in the morning marked with signs of enjoyment with another woman. The *kalahāntarita* heroine is she who repents after her anger. The *proṣita-bhatṛkā* is she who suffers when Kṛṣṇa goes to Mathurā. The *svādhīna-bhartṛkā* heroine is she who orders Kṛṣṇa how to dress after enjoying with him. Multiplying these eight by the fifteen types mentioned previously makes one hundred and twenty types of heroine. Multiplying this by three types *of uttama, madhyama* and *kaniṣṭha* (intensity of prema) makes three hundred and sixty types.

Among these types of heroines in Vraja some are nitya-siddhas like Rādhā and Candrāvalī. Some are sādhana-siddhas. Among the sādhana-siddhas some were previously sages and some were śrutis. Some heroines were devatā women.

## Chapter 3 - Nāyikā-svabhāva. The Natures of Heroines

The nature of the heroine is various. Some are bold in speaking (*prakhara*) like Śyāmalā and Maṅgalā. Others are moderate (*madhyā*) like Rādhā and Pālī. Others are soft in speaking (*mṛdvī*) like Candrāvalī. There are four types of heroines according to relationship: those in the same group, those in a friendly group, those in a neutral group and those in an enemy group. Lalitā and Viśākhā are in Rādhā's group. Śyāmalā is head of a friendly group. Bhadrā is in a neutral group and Candrāvalī is in the enemy group.

Some heroines are stubborn and some are compliant. Rādhikā is stubborn (*vāmā*) and moderate (*madhyā*). She wears blue and red cloth. Lalitā is harsh (*prakharā*) and wears cloth the color of a peacock's feather. Viśākhā is stubborn (*vāmā*) and moderate, wearing cloth with groups of stars on it. Indulekhā is stubborn and rough (*prakharā*), with pink cloth. Raṅgadevī and Sudevī are stubborn and rough (*prakharā*) and wear red cloth. All have golden complexion. Campakalatā is a stubborn madhyā with blue cloth. Citrā is compliant (*dakṣiṇā*) and mild (*mṛdvī*) with blue cloth. Tuṅgadevī is compliant (*dakṣiṇā*) and bold speaking *(prakharā*), with white cloth. Śyāmalā is a bold speaking heroine with compliant and stubborn tendencies, with red cloth. Bhadrā is a compliant and *mṛdv*ī with multi-colored cloth. Candrāvalī is compliant and *mṛdvī* with blue cloth. Her friend Padmā is compliant but bold speaking. Śaivyā is a compliant and *mṛdvī.* They wear red cloth.

## Chapter 4 - Dūtī-bheda. Different Types of Messengers

There are two types of messengers: the heroine acting as messengers for themselves and persons who assume that role for the heroine. Those who assume the role for others are of three types: self-appointed, appointed, and message bearers. The self-appointed (amitārthā) delivers messages on her own, even by signs, without speaking. The appointed messenger performs actions on orders from her leader. The message bearer simply delivers messages or sees that they get delivered. The messengers may be craftswomen, fortune tellers, servants, nurses, ascetics, sakhīs or forest spirits. In Vraja Vīrā, Vṛndā and the flute are the three messengers of Kṛṣṇa. Vīrā is bold in speech. Vṛndā speaks in a pleasing manner. The flute accomplishes all tasks.

## Chapter 5 - Sakhī-bheda. Different Types of Sakhīs

There are five types of sakhīs: sakhī, nitya-sakhī, prāṇa-sakhī, priya-sakhī and parama-preṣṭha-sakhī. Among them, some have equal affection for Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa and others have unequal affection for them. One who has more affection for Kṛṣṇa is called a sakhī. Vṛndā, Kundalatā, Vidyā, Dhaniṣṭhā, Kusumikā and Kāmadā, Ātreyī have this sakhī bhāva.

The one with greater affection for Rādhā is called nitya-sakhī. Examples are Kasturī, Manojñā, Maṇimañjarī, Sindūrā, Candanavatī, Kaumudī and Madirā.

The chief sakhīs who have more affection for Rādhā are called prāṇa-sakhīs. Tulasī, Kelikandalī, Kādambarī, Śaśimukhī, Candrarekhā, Priyaṁvadā, Madonmadā, Madhumatī, Vasantī, Kalabhaṣiṇī, Ratnāvalī, Mālatī, and Karpūra-latikā are examples.

Those who are almost one in form with Rādhā (identifying with her), numbering in the tens of millions ,are gopīs like Mālatī, Candralatikā, Guṇacūḍā, Varāṅgadā, Mādhavī, Candrikā, Prema-mañjarī, Tanu-madhyamā, and Kandarpa-sundarī. They are called priya-sakhīs.

The chief of these are called parama-preṣṭha-sakhīs . These are Lalitā, Viśākhā, Citrā, Campakalatā, Raṅgadevī, Sudevī, Tuṅgavidyā and Indu-lekhā.

Though they have equal love for Rādhā and Kṛṣna they side with Rādhā.

## Chapter 6 - Vayo-bheda. Different Age Groups

Now age will be discussed. Marginal youth, fresh youth, manifested youth and complete youth are the ages. Kalāvatī and others are marginal youths. Dhanyā is in fresh youth. Radhā is in manifested youth. Candrāvalī is in complete youth. Padmā is also in complete youth. These persons (Kṛṣṇa and gopīs in various manifestations) act as ālambana-vibhāva (viṣaya and āśraya).

## Chapter 7 - Uddīpana-vibhāva. Stimulants of Love for Śrī Kṛṣṇa

The uddīpana portion of vibhāva consists of many things: his qualities, his names, his dancing, his flute music, milking the cows, his ornaments, songs, signs on his feet, his fragrance, garlands, his peacock feather, his guñja ornaments, black clouds, and seeing the moon.

## Chapter 8 - Anubhāva. Signs of Ecstasy

Now the anubhāvas will be discussed. There are twenty ornaments: bhāva, hāva, helā, śobhā, kānti, dīpti, mādhuryam, pragalbhatā, audāryam, dhairyam, līlā, vilāsa, vicchiti, vibhrama, kilakiñcitam, moṭṭāyitam, kuṭṭamitam, bibokam, lalitam and vikṛtam. Bhāva is the first external indication of internal love in the heart which had not previously experienced such love. Hāva means indications of that love by wide eyes, movement of the brows and tilted neck. When the movements clearly indicate conjugal love, with trembling breasts, hairs standing on end and belt and cloth becoming slack, it is called helā. Śobhā refers to decoration of the limbs with beauty and enjoyment of love. Śobhā becomes kānti when youth becomes most prominent. Kānti become dīpti when the limbs are endowed with specific time and place. Looseness of the limbs from dancing or other physical acts is called mādhuryam. Pragalbhatā means opposition to enjoyment. Audāryam means to manifest gentleness even when angry. Dhairyam means steadiness in prema even when circumstances are painful. Līlā means imitating the actions of the lover. Vilāsa means blossoming of the face when in association with the lover. Vicchitti is beauty even when dressing up only a little. Vibhrama means to plāce ornaments in the wrong places out of bewilderment in going to a meeting. When pride, desire, weeping, smiling, envy, fear and anger manifest together because of joy, as when Kṛṣṇa blocks Rādhā's path, these symptoms are called kilakiñcita.

The appearance of desire in the heart caused by *sthāyi-bhāva* on hearing about or remembering the beloved is called moṭṭāyitam. Showing pain though experiencing bliss when the lover bites her lip or scratches her breast is called kuṭtamitam. Bibboka is showing disrespect to articles of the lover out of pride, after being cheated by him. Lalitam is actions such as brushing away bees, while frowning and moving the limbs. Vikṛtam is not indicating one's intentions with words but only by hints of actions, out of shyness.

There are two additional ornaments. Maugdhyam is asking something as if ignorant, though one knows. Cakitam is showing fear in front of the lover on seeing a bee etc.

There are other anubhāvas as well such as loosening of the belt, upper cloth or hair, stretching the limbs, yawning, widening the nostrils and breathing heavily.

## Chapter 9 - Sāttvika. Bodily Transformations of Ecstasy

The sattvika-bhāvas such as perspiration and being stunned manifest levels of dhūmāyita, jvalita, dīpta and sūddīpita.

## Chapter 10 - Vyabhicari-bhava. Transitory Ecstasies

The thirty-three vyabhicārī bhāvas manifest.

## Chapter 11 - Bhavotpatti. The Awakening of Bhāva

There are four stages of vyabhicārī-bhāvas. Bhāvotpatti is the initial appearance of the bhāva. Bhāva-sandhi is mixing with another bhāva. Bhāva-śāvalyam is conflict between bhāvas which first appear and bhāvas which appear later. Bhāva-śānti is the disappearance of the vyabhicārī-bhāva.

## Chapter 12 - Rati-bheda. Different Types of Amorous Attraction

The sthāyī-bhāva is madhura-rati. It has three types: sādhāraṇī, samañjasā and samarthā. Sādhāraṇī, like an ordinary gem, manifests in persons like Kubjā. Samañjasā, like a cintāmaṇi, manifests in the queens of Dvārakā. Samarthā, like the Kaustubha gem, manifets in the women of Vraja. Sādhāraṇī rati aims at one's own happiness, with ordinary bhāva. Samañjasā, filled with sentiments of a wife, aims at Kṛṣṇa and one's own happiness. Samarthā rati, with thoughts of belonging to another man, aims at only Kṛṣṇa's happiness.

## Chapter 13 - Samarthā-rati. Affection Capable of Controlling Kṛṣṇa

Now samarthā rati will be discussed. In its first stage of rati it is like a seed. It is like sugar cane at the stage of prema, like juice at the stage of sneha, like jaggery at the stage of māna, like sugar candy at the stage of praṇaya, like powdered sugar at the stage of rāga, like refined sugar at the stage of anurāga and like rock candy at the stage of mahābhāva.

Rati means attachment of the mind to Kṛṣṇa with affection, from impressions of previous lives or from hearing about and seeing Kṛṣṇa. When the love does not decrease in spite of obstacles it is called prema. Sneha causes the heart to melt. Ghṛta-sneha means the affection (sneha) is mixed with respect and mixed with other bhāvas, while thinking oneself the possession of Kṛṣṇa. It is liquid like ghee. It is found in Candrāvalī. Madhu-sneha is devoid of respect, with the thought that Kṛṣṇa is one's possession. It is naturally soft like honey and is found in Rādhā and others.

Māna is crookedness arising from great sneha, either without cause, or because of anger or because of auspicious or auspicious events. It appears gentle (udātta) in Candrāvalī with a mood of compliance and it appears crooked (lalita) in Rādhā with a trace of stubbornness.

Māna becomes praṇaya when feelings of intimacy develops with a sense of oneness of senses and body of the two lovers. It has two types, sakhyam ( no fear) and maitram (with modesty).

Nīla-rāga covers other bhāvas attached to it (such as vyabhicārī bhāvas). It is found in Candrāvalī. Śyāma-rāga, which takes a long time to reach perfection, is found in Bhadrā and others. Mañjiṣṭha-rāga is independent, not covered by other bhāvas and is found in Rādhā. Kusumbha-rāga, somewhat dependent, and which quickly becomes fixed, is found in Śyāmalā and others.

With anurāga, Kṛṣṇa is expereienced as newer and newer at all times. One desires to be born as a nonliving entity, experiences imaginary separation (prema-vaicittya) and experiences Kṛṣṇa internally during separation (sphūrti).

Mahābhāva has two types: rūḍḥa and adhirūḍha. In rūḍḥa one worries that Kṛṣṇa is in pain even when he is happy and one cannot tolerate a second of separation. In adhirūḍha, a particle of the joy experienced during union cannot compare to all the happiness in ten millioin universes and a particle of the suffering experienced during separation cannot compare with the suffering of being bitten by all the scorpions and snakes in the world.

Adhirūḍha has two forms: modana and madana. In modana the agitation of Kṛṣṇa and his lovers reaches the most astonishing level and sūddīpta sattvika-bhāvas manifest. Modana appears only in Rādhā and her group. In separation, modana becomes mohana. By this state, Kṛṣṇa faints even while being embraced by his queens, out of pain of separation. In this state the whole universe becomes disturbed and even animals weep. Generally it appears only in Rādhā. A special form of mohana is divyonmada in which there are citra-jalpa (chattering nonsense) and udghurṇā (unpredictable actions). Madana, the highest condition of all, appears only in Rādhā. In this state, unlimited bhāvas appear, she criticizes the garland, praises the Pulindas, and describes the good fortune of the jasmine touching the tamāla tree.

## Chapter 14 - Āśraya-nirṇaya. Deliberation on the Abodes of Prema

Now the āśraya nature of the various heroines is discussed. The sādhāranī rati of Kubjā reaches up to prema. The samañjasā rati of the queens reaches up to anurāga. Among the queens, Satyabhāmā and Lakṣmaṇā correspond to Rādhā, and Rukmiṇī corresponds to Candrāvalī in bhāva. Other queens reach the anurāga of the priya-nārma sakhīs of Vraja. The samarthā ratī of the women of Vraja and Subala reaches to mahābhāva. However adhirūḍha appears only in Rādhā's group and not in others. Mohana appears only in Rādhā, Lalitā and Viśākhā. Madana appears only in Rādhā.

## Chapter 15 - Sthāyī-bhāva. Permanent Ecstasy

The varieties of śṛṅgāra will be discussed. There are two types: in separation (vipralambha) and in union (sambhoga). There are four types of separation: pūrva-rāga, māna, prema-vaicittyam and pravāsa. Pūrva-rāga is rati filled with longing before any direct contact of the heroine with Kṛṣṇa. It has ten stages: lālasa (greed), udvega (agitation), jāgrya (insomnia), tānavam (becoming thin), jaḍimā (being stunned), vaiyagryam (perplexity), vyādhi (sickness), unmāda (insanity), moha (unconsciousness), and mṛtyu (death symptoms).

Māna has two types: with cause and without cause. Causeless anger dissipates on its own. Anger with a cause dissipates by suitably by use of *sāma* (soothing words), *bheda* (division), *kriyā* (action), *nati* (offering respects), *upekṣā* (disrespect) and *rasāntara* (change of *rasa*). *Sama* means speaking in a pleasing manner. *Bheda* means Kṛṣṇa showing his intelligence through wit and sakhīs scolding their friends for mistreating Kṛṣṇa. *Dāna* is giving garlands or cloth. *Nati* is offering respects. *Upekṣa* is showing indifference. *Rasāntaram* is an opportunity to the change the rasa using fear, danger etc. The signs that māna has dissipated are tears and smiles.

Prema-vaicittyam occurs when a person experiences separation though Kṛṣṇa is near, because of intense anurāga.

Pravāsa is separation at some distance and at a very great distance. Separation at some distance occurs daily when Kṛṣṇa goes to herd the cows. It occurs at great distance when Kṛṣṇa goes to Mathurā. The ten states become very strong at this time.

Sambhoga has four types. After pūrva-rāga there is saṅkṣipta or abbreviated union, with sparse injury to the lips and scratches by nails. Saṅkīrṇa after māna is mixed with some hatred and envy. Sampūrṇa-sambhoga after separation at some distance is distinct in manifestation. Samṛddhimān after separation at a great distance is most distinct.

Sambhoga consists of unlimited activities such as seeing, touching, speaking, blocking the path, playing in the forest, playing in water, stealing the flute, playing in a boat, taking taxes, hiding, and drinking intoxicants.

May Ujjvala-nīlamāṇī-kiraṇa illuminate the person devoted to the feet of the Lord, who has not studied grammar.
