# Visnu Purana 2

## Book Two: The Word

## Chapter 1 - Priyavrata distributes the continents

## \|\| 2.1.1 \|\|

*maitreya uvāca*  
*bhagavansamyagākhyātaṃ mamaitadakhilaṃ tvāyā \|*  
*jagataḥ sargasambandhi yatpṛṣṭosi guro mayā \|\| 1 \|\|*

Maitreya:

You’ve told me all about the creation of the universe in answer to my question, holy guru. 1

## \|\| 2.1.2 \|\|

*yoyamaṃśo jagatsṛṣṭisambandho gaditastvayā \|*  
*tatrāhaṃ śrotumicchāmi bhūyopi munisattama \|\| 2 \|\|*

But there’s an aspect of creation you’ve recounted, best of sages, about which I’d like to hear some more. 2

## \|\| 2.1.3 \|\|

*priyavratottānapādau sutau svāyambhuvasya yau \|*  
*tayoruttānapādasya dhruvaḥ putrastvayoditaḥ \|\| 3 \|\|*

Svāyaṃbhuva had two sons, Priyavrata and Uttānapāda. Regarding these, you mentioned that Uttānapāda’s son was Dhruva, 3

## \|\| 2.1.4 \|\|

*priyavratasya naivoktā bhavatā dvija santatiḥ \|*  
*tāmahaṃ śrotumicchāmi prasanno vaktumarhasi \|\| 4 \|\|*

But you’ve said nothing about Priyavrata’s descendants, brahmin. I’d like to hear about them, if it pleases you to tell me. 4

## \|\| 2.1.5 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*kardamasyātmajāṃ kanyāmupayeme prayivrataḥ \|*  
*samrāṭ kukṣi ca tatkanye daśaputrastathāpare \|\| 5 \|\|*

Priyavrata married the daughter of Kardama. They had ten sons and two daughters, Samrāj and Kukṣi. 5

## \|\| 2.1.6 \|\|

*mahāprajñā mahāvīryā vinītā dayitāḥ pituḥ \|*  
*priyavratasutāḥ khyātāsteṣāṃ nāmāni me śṛṇu \|\| 6 \|\|*

The sons of Priyavrata were said to be wise, brave, gracious and dear to their father. Listen while I tell you the names by which they’re known: 6

## \|\| 2.1.7 \|\|

*āgnīdhraścāgnibāhuś ca vapuṣmāndyutimāṃs tathā \|*  
*medhā medhātithirbhavyaḥ savanaḥ putra eva ca \|\| 7 \|\|*

Āgnīdhra, Agnibāhu, Vapuṣmant, Dyutimant, Medhas, Medhātithi, Bhavya, Savana and Putra. 7

## \|\| 2.1.8 \|\|

*jyotiṣmāndaśamasteṣāṃ satyanāmā suto 'bhavat \|*  
*priyavratasya putrāste prakhyātā balavīryataḥ \|\| 8 \|\|*

Jyotiṣmant, the ‘Shining One’, was tenth among them and was as brilliant as his name suggests, but all of Priyavrata’s sons were famed for strength and courage. 8

## \|\| 2.1.9 \|\|

*medhāgnibāhuputrāstu trayo yogaparāyaṇāḥ \|*  
*jātismārā mahābhāgāna rājyāya mano dadhuḥ \|\| 9 \|\|*

Three—namely, Medhas, Agnibāhu and Putra—were devoted to yoga. Remembering their former lives, these fortunate men didn’t set their hearts on sovereignty. 9

## \|\| 2.1.10 \|\|

*nirmalāḥ sarvakālantu samastārtheṣu vai mune \|*  
*cakruḥ kriyāṃ yathānyāyamaphalākākṣiṇo hi te \|\| 10 \|\|*

They remained ever selfless in all matters, sage, and performed sacrifices in accordance with propriety, not thinking of reward. 10

## \|\| 2.1.11 \|\|

*priyavrato dadau teṣāṃ saptānāṃ munisattama \|*  
*saptadvīpāni maitreya vibhajya sumahātmanām \|\| 11 \|\|*

Priyavrata divided the world into seven continents, best of sages, and gave one to each of his other seven great sons, Maitreya. 11

## \|\| 2.1.12 \|\|

*jambūdvīpe mahābhāga sognīdhrāya dadau pitā \|*  
*medhātithes tathā prādātplakṣadvīpaṃ tathāparam \|\| 12 \|\|*

Āgnīdhra received Jambūdvīpa from his father, fortunate seer. To Medhātithi, he gave another continent, Plakṣadvīpa. 12

## \|\| 2.1.13 \|\|

*śālmale ca vapuṣmantaṃ narendramabhiṣiktavān \|*  
*jyotiṣmantaṃ kuśadvīpe rājānaṃ kṛtāvānprabhuḥ \|\| 13 \|\|*

Vapuṣmant was anointed king of Śālmala, and holy Priyavrata made Jyotiṣmant king of Kuśadvīpa. 13

## \|\| 2.1.14 \|\|

*dyutimantaṃ ca rājānaṃ krauñcadvīpe samādiśat \|*  
*śākadvīpeśvaraṃ cāpi bhavyaṃ cakre priyavrataḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

Priyavrata also appointed Dyutimant as king of Krauñcadvīpa and made Bhavya lord over Śākadvīpa. 14

## \|\| 2.1.15 \|\|

*puṣkarādhipatiṃ cakre savanaṃ cāpi sa prabhuḥ \|*  
*jambūdvīpeśvaro yastu āgnīdhro munisattama \|\| 15 \|\|*

That holy one made Savana king of Puṣkara. Āgnīdhra, who was king of Jambūdvīpa, best of sages, had nine sons, equal to the patriarchs: 15

## \|\| 2.1.16 \|\|

*tasya putrā babhūvuste prajāpatisamā nava \|*  
*nābhiḥ kiṃpuruṣaścaiva harivarṣa ilāvṛtaḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

Nābhi, Kimpuruṣa, Harivarṣa, Ilāvṛta, Ramya, Hiraṇvant was the sixth, along with Kuru and Bhadrāśva. The other was Ketumāla, a king of virtuous conduct. 16

## \|\| 2.1.17 \|\|

*ramyo hiraṇvānṣaṣṭhaś ca kurubhadrāśva eva ca \|*  
*ketumālastathaivānyaḥ sādhuceṣṭo 'bhavantṛpaḥ \|\| 17 \|\|*

Hear how Āgnīdhra divided Jambūdvīpa among his sons, brahmin: Nābhi was given the southern region of Himāhva by his father. 17

## \|\| 2.1.18 \|\|

*janbūdvīpavi bhāgāṃś ca teṣāṃ vipra niśāmaya \|*  
*pitrā dattaṃ himāhvaṃ tu varṣaṃ nābhestu dakṣiṇam \|\| 18 \|\|*

Āgnīdhra gave Hemakūṭa to Kimpuruṣa. The third region, Naiṣadha, he gave to Harivarṣa. 18

## \|\| 2.1.19 \|\|

*hemakūṭaṃ tathā varṣaṃ dadau kiṃpuruṣāya saḥ \|*  
*tṛtīyaṃ naiṣadhaṃ varṣaṃ harivarṣāya dattavān \|\| 19 \|\|*

He gave the region centred on Mount Meru to Ilāvṛta, and the lands around Mount Nīla his father gave to Ramya. 19

## \|\| 2.1.20 \|\|

*ilāvṛtāya pradadau meruryatra tu madhyamaḥ \|*  
*nīlācalāśritaṃ varṣa ramyāya pradadau pitā \|\| 20 \|\|*

Hiraṇvant was given the region to the north of that, known as Śveta. 20

## \|\| 2.1.21 \|\|

*śvetaṃ yaduttaraṃ tasmātpitrā dattaṃ hiraṇvate \|\| 21 \|\|*

The lands to the north of Śṛṅgavant he gave to Kuru, and he gave the region that lies east of Meru to Bhadrāśva. 21

## \|\| 2.1.22 \|\|

*yaduttaraṃ śṛṅgavato varṣa tatkurave dadau \|*  
*meroḥ purveṇa yadvarṣa bhadrāśvāya pradattavān \|\| 22 \|\|*

The region of Gandhamādana he gave to Ketumāla. This is how that king divided the regions among his sons. 22

## \|\| 2.1.23 \|\|

*gandhamādhanavarṣa tu ketumālāya dattavān \|*  
*ityetāni dadau tebhyaḥ putrebhyaḥ sa nareśvaraḥ \|\| 23 \|\|*

After that sovereign had enthroned his sons in each of these, Maitreya, he retired to the most auspicious site of Śālagrāma to undertake austerities. 23

## \|\| 2.1.24 \|\|

*varṣeṣveteṣu tānputrānabhiṣicya sa bhūmipaḥ \|*  
*sālāgrāmaṃ mahāpuṇyaṃ maitreya tapase yayau \|\| 24 \|\|*

In the eight regions given to Kimpuruṣa and the others, great sage, achievement was spontaneous and happiness was effortless. 24

## \|\| 2.1.25 \|\|

*yani kiṃpuruṣādīti varṣāvyaṣṭau mahāmune \|*  
*teṣāṃ svābhāviki siddhiḥ sukhaprāyā hyayatnataḥ \|\| 25 \|\|*

They were free from misfortune and the woes of age and death. There was no distinction between vice and virtue, or between best, worst and middling. 25

## \|\| 2.1.26 \|\|

*viparyayo na teṣvasti jarāmṛtyubhayaṃ na ca \|*  
*dharmadhamau na teṣvāstāṃ nottamādhamamadhyamāḥ \|\| 26 \|\|*

These eight regions never knew the cycle of ages. The region of Himāhvaya was ruled by the great Nābhi. With his queen, Merudevī, he had a brilliant son, Ṛṣabha. 26

## \|\| 2.1.27 \|\|

*na teṣvasti yugāvasthā kṣetreṣvaṣṭasu sarvadā \|*  
*himāhvayaṃ tu vai varṣaṃ nāberāsīnmahātmanaḥ \|*  
*tasyarṣabho 'bhavatputro merudevyāṃ mahādyutiḥ \|\| 27 \|\|*

Ṛṣabha himself had a hundred sons, of whom Bharata was the eldest. Having ruled wisely and performed varied sacrifices, 27

## \|\| 2.1.28 \|\|

*ṛṣabhādbhārato jajñe jyeṣṭhaḥ putraśatasya saḥ \|*  
*kṛtvā rājyaṃ svadharmeṇa tathaiṣṭvā vividhānmakhān \|\| 28 \|\|*

King Ṛṣabha placed his son heroic Bharata upon the throne, then that fortunate man retired to the ashram of Pulaha to undertake austerities. 28

## \|\| 2.1.29 \|\|

*abhiṣicya sutaṃ vīraṃ bharataṃ pṛthivīpatiḥ \|*  
*tapase sa mahābhāgaḥ pulahasyāśramaṃ yayau \|\| 29 \|\|*

Following the prescription for those who retire to the forest, that resolute king duly performed austerities and instituted sacrifices. 29

## \|\| 2.1.30 \|\|

*vānaprasthavidhānena tatrāpi kṛtaniścayaḥ \|*  
*tapastepe yathānyāyamiyāja sa mahīpatiḥ \|\| 30 \|\|*

Wasted by his practices, he grew thin, his veins distended. Naked Ṛṣabha placed a pebble in his mouth and took the path of heroes. 30

## \|\| 2.1.31 \|\|

*tapasā karṣitotyarthaṃ kṛśo dhamanisantataḥ \|*  
*nagno vīṭāṃ mukhe kṛtvā vīrādhvānaṃ tato gataḥ \|\| 31 \|\|*

Because this region was given to Bharata by his father when he retired to the forest, it’s known throughout the threefold worlds as Bhārata. 31

## \|\| 2.1.32 \|\|

*tataś ca bhārataṃ varṣametallokeṣu gīyate \|*  
*bharatāya yataḥ pitrā dattaṃ prātiṣṭhatā vanam \|\| 32 \|\|*

Bharata had a most virtuous son, Sumati. After Bharata had successfully completed the required sacrifices, he bestowed the kingdom on his son. 32

## \|\| 2.1.33 \|\|

*sumatīrbharatasyābhūtputraḥ paramadhārmikaḥ \|*  
*kṛtvā samyagdadau tasmai rājyamiṣṭamakhaṃ pitā \|\| 33 \|\|*

King Bharata, who delighted in the practice of yoga, sage, passed responsibility to his son and drew his final breath at Śālagrāma. 33

## \|\| 2.1.34 \|\|

*putrasaṃkrāmita śrīstu bharataḥ sa mahīpatiḥ \|*  
*yogābhyāsarataḥ prāṇānsālagrāme 'yajanmune \|\| 34 \|\|*

In time, he was reborn a brahmin in a brilliant line of ascetics. I’ll recount his exploits for you later, Maitreya. 34

## \|\| 2.1.35 \|\|

*ajāyata ca viprosau yogīnāṃ pravare kule \|*  
*maitreya tasya caritaṃ kathayiṣyāmi te punaḥ \|\| 35 \|\|*

Brilliant Sumati had a son, Indradyumna. His son was Parameṣṭhin, and his son was Pratihāra. 35

## \|\| 2.1.36 \|\|

*mumatestejasastasmādindradyumno vyajāyata \|*  
*parameṣṭhī tatastasmātpratihārastadanvayaḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

He had a son named Pratiharta. His son was Bhuva. Next came Udgītha. King Prastāva was his son. 36

## \|\| 2.1.37 \|\|

*pratiharteti vikhyāta utpannastasya cātmajaḥ \|*  
*bhavastasmādathodgīthaḥ prāstavistatsuto vibhuḥ \|\| 37 \|\|*

Then came Pṛthu, followed by Nakta. Nakta’s son was Gaya. Nara was the son of Gaya. He had a son, Virāj. 37

## \|\| 2.1.38 \|\|

*pṛthustatastato nakto naktasyāpi gayaḥ sutaḥ \|*  
*naro gayasya natayastatputrobhūdvirāṭ tataḥ \|\| 38 \|\|*

His son was heroic Dhīmant. Then came Mahānta, whose son was Manasyu. 38

## \|\| 2.1.39 \|\|

*tasya putro mahāvīryo dhīmāṃstasmādajāyata \|*  
*mahāntastatsutaścābhūnmanasyustasya cātmajaḥ \|\| 39 \|\|*

Then came Tvaṣṭṛ. Tvaṣṭṛ’s son was Viraja, and his son was Raja. His son was Śatajit, who had a hundred sons, sage. 39

## \|\| 2.1.40 \|\|

*tpaṣṭā tvaṣṭuś ca virajo rajastasyāpyabhūtsutaḥ \|*  
*śatajidrajasastasya jajñe putraśanta mune \|\| 40 \|\|*

Of these, Viśvagjyoti was the eldest. They all contributed to the growth of the population and shone like ornaments in the ninefold region of Bhārata. 40

## \|\| 2.1.41 \|\|

*viṣvagjyotipradhānāste yair imā vardhitāḥ prajāḥ \|*  
*tair idaṃ bhārataṃ varṣaṃ navabhedamalaṅkṛtam \|\| 41 \|\|*

In former times, their descendants had possession of the land of Bhārata for seventy-one cycles of the ages, each consisting of the Kṛta, Tretā and the others. 41

## \|\| 2.1.42 \|\|

*teṣāṃ vaṃśaprasūtaiś ca bhukteyaṃ bhāratī purā \|*  
*kṛtatretādisargeṇa yugākhyāmekasaptatim \|\| 42 \|\|*

This was the period of creation under Manu Svāyaṃbhuva, who presided over the first Manvantara in the era known as Vārāha, sage, when this whole world was populated. 42

## Chapter 2 - Jambūdvīpa, ‘Land of the Rose-Apple Tree’

## \|\| 2.2.1 \|\|

*śrīmaitreya uvāca*  
*kathito bhavatā brahmansargaḥ svāyambhuvaś ca me \|*  
*śrotumicchāmyahaṃ tvattaḥ sakalaṃ maṇḍalaṃ bhuvaḥ \|\| 1 \|\|*

Brahmin, you’ve told me about creation during the period of Svāyaṃbhuva. Now I’d like to hear from you about the entire circle of the earth, 1

## \|\| 2.2.2 \|\|

*yāvantaḥ sāgara dvīpās tathā varṣāṇi parvatāḥ \|*  
*vanāni satitaḥ puryo devādīnāṃ tathā mune \|\| 2 \|\|*

Including oceans, lands, regions, mountains, forests, lakes and the cities of the gods and other beings, sage, 2

## \|\| 2.2.3 \|\|

*yatpramāṇamidaṃ sarvaṃ yadādhāraṃ yadātmakam \|*  
*saṃsthānamasya ca mune yathābadvaktumarhasi \|\| 3 \|\|*

As well as the dimensions, support, nature and state of each. It befits you, sage, to describe all this. 3

## \|\| 2.2.4 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*maitreya śrūyatāmetatsaṃkṣepādgadato mama \|*  
*nāsya varṣaśatenāpi vaktuṃ śakyo hi vistaraḥ \|\| 4 \|\|*

Listen while I explain it all in brief, Maitreya, because I couldn’t give you all the details even in a hundred years. 4

## \|\| 2.2.5 \|\|

*jambūplakṣāhvayau dvīpau śālmaliścāparo dvija \|*  
*kuśaḥ kraiñcas tathā śākaḥ puṣkaraścaiva saptamaḥ \|\| 5 \|\|*

Jambū, Plakṣa, Śālma, Kuśa, Krauñca and Śāka, with Puṣkara as the seventh, brahmin: 5

## \|\| 2.2.6 \|\|

*ete dvīpāḥ samudrais tu saptasaptabhir āvṛtāḥ \|*  
*lavaṇekṣusurāsarpirdadhigugdhajalaiḥ samam \|\| 6 \|\|*

These are the continents surrounded by seven oceans, of saltwater, syrup, wine, ghee, whey, milk and water, respectively. 6

## \|\| 2.2.7 \|\|

*sanskrit not found \|\| 7 \|\|*

Jambūdvīpa is in the middle of these, and the golden mountain, Meru, is at the centre of that continent, Maitreya. 7

## \|\| 2.2.8 \|\|

*sanskrit not found \|\| 8 \|\|*

Mount Meru stands eighty-four thousand leagues in height. 8

## \|\| 2.2.9 \|\|

*praviṣṭaḥ ṣoḍaśādhastāddvātriṃśanmūrdhri vistṛta \|*  
*mūle ṣoḍaśasāhasro vistārastasya sarvaśaḥ \|\| 9 \|\|*

It descends below the surface of the earth for a further sixteen thousand leagues, its summit is thirty-two thousand leagues across and the base of the mountain is fully sixteen thousand leagues wide. 9

## \|\| 2.2.10 \|\|

*bhūpadmasyāsya śailosau karṇikākārasaṃsthitaḥ \|\| 10 \|\|*

The world resembles a lotus flower with Mount Meru as the seed head at its heart. 10

## \|\| 2.2.11 \|\|

*himāvānhemakūṭaś ca viṣadhaścasya dakṣiṇe \|*  
*nīlaḥ śvetaś ca śṛṅgī ca uttare varṣaparvatāḥ \|\| 11 \|\|*

The mountain ranges Himavant, Hemakūṭa and Niṣadha are to the south of Meru, while Nīla, Śveta and Śṛṅgin lie northward. 11

## \|\| 2.2.12 \|\|

*lakṣapramāṇo dvau madhyau daśahīnāstathāpare \|*  
*sahasradvitayocchrāyās tāvadvistāriṇaś ca te \|\| 12 \|\|*

The two ranges closest to the centre are one hundred thousand leagues in length, while each of the others is successively ten thousand leagues shorter. They are all two thousand leagues high and two thousand wide. 12

## \|\| 2.2.13 \|\|

*bhārataṃ prathamaṃ varṣaṃ tataḥ kipuruṣaṃ smṛtam \|*  
*harivarṣaṃ tathaivānyanmerordakṣiṇato dvija \|\| 13 \|\|*

To the south of Meru, the first region is Bhārata, Kimpuruṣa is next, then comes another, Harivarṣa, brahmin. 13

## \|\| 2.2.14 \|\|

*ramyakaṃ cottaraṃ varṣaṃ tasyaivānu hiraṇmayam \|*  
*uttarāḥ kuravaścaiva yathā vai bhārataṃ tathā \|\| 14 \|\|*

To the north of Meru is the region Ramya, followed by Hiraṇmaya and the Northern Kurus, whose position mirrors that of Bhārata. 14

## \|\| 2.2.15 \|\|

*navasāhasramaikaikam eteṣāṃ dvijasattama \|*  
*ilāvṛtaṃ ca tanmadhye sauvarṇo merurucchritaḥ \|\| 15 \|\|*

Each of these regions is nine thousand leagues wide, excellent brahmin, including Ilāvṛta, which has the towering golden mountain, Meru, at its centre. 15

## \|\| 2.2.16 \|\|

*meroścaturdiśaṃ tattu navasāhasravistṛtam \|*  
*ilāvṛtaṃ mahābhāga catvāraścātra parvatāḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

From the four sides of Meru, Ilāvṛta extends nine thousand leagues in each direction, fortunate brahmin, and there are four mountains in this region. 16

## \|\| 2.2.17 \|\|

*viṣkaṃbhāracitā meroryojanāyutamucchritaḥ \|*  
*navaiḥ sarobhiś ca samaṃ dikṣvete keśavācalāḥ \|\| 17 \|\|*

Arranged like pillars around Mount Meru, each stands ten thousand leagues high. To the east is Mandara by name, to the south Gandhamādana, Vipula is on the western side and Supārśva is to the north. 17

## \|\| 2.2.18 \|\|

*purveṇa mandaro nāma dakṣiṇe gandhamādanaḥ \|*  
*viplalaḥ paścime pārśve mupārśvaścottara smṛtaḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

On each of these stands a tree, eleven hundred leagues across—a kadamba, a jambū, a pippala and a vaṭa, respectively—like banners on a mountain top. 18

## \|\| 2.2.19 \|\|

*kadambasteṣu jambūś ca pippalo vaṭa eva ca \|*  
*ekādaśaśatāyāmāḥ pādapā giriketavaḥ \|\| 19 \|\|*

Jambūdvīpa is named for this jambū, a rose-apple tree, great sage, the fruits of which are as large as elephants. 19

## \|\| 2.2.20 \|\|

*jambūdvīpasya sā jambūr nāmahetur mahāmune \|*  
*mahāgajapramāmāni jambvāstasyāḥ phalani vai \|*  
*patanti bhūbhṛtaḥ pṛṣṭhe śīryamāṇāni sarvataḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

When they fall down upon the mountain, they split open and their juices form a river, the Jambūnadī, from which the residents of that region drink. 20

## \|\| 2.2.21 \|\|

*rasena teṣāṃ prakhyātā tatra jāmbūnadīti vai \|*  
*saritpravartate cāpi pīyate tannivāsibhiḥ \|\| 21 \|\|*

As a result of drinking from the river, the people never sweat, smell bad, grow old or lose their senses, and their minds are always sharp. 21

## \|\| 2.2.22 \|\|

*na svedo na ca daurgandhyaṃ na jarā nendriyakṣayaḥ \|*  
*tatpānātsvacchamanāsāṃjanānāṃ tatra jāyate \|\| 22 \|\|*

When the mud on the riverbank, coming into contact with the juice, is dried by pleasant breezes, it becomes the kind of gold that’s known as jāmbūnada and is used in making jewellery for the siddhas. 22

## \|\| 2.2.23 \|\|

*tīramṛttadrasaṃ prāpya sukhavāyuviśoṣitā \|*  
*jāmbūnadākhyaṃ bhavati suvarṇaṃ siddhabhūṣaṇam \|\| 23 \|\|*

The region of Bhadrāśva lies east of Meru, Ketumāla is to the west, best of sages, and Ilāvṛta lies between them. 23

## \|\| 2.2.24 \|\|

*bhadrāśvaṃ pūrvato meroḥ ketumālaṃ ca paścime \|*  
*varṣe dve tu muniśreṣṭha tayormadhyamilāvṛtaḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

The Caitraratha forest is also east of Meru, and the forest of Gandhamādana is to the south. Similarly, Vaibhrāja is to the west and Nandana to the north. 24

## \|\| 2.2.25 \|\|

*vanaṃ caitrarathaṃ pūrve dakṣiṇe gandhamādanam \|*  
*vaibhrājaṃ paścime tadvaduttare nandanaṃ smṛtam \|\| 25 \|\|*

The four lakes of Aruṇoda, Mahābhadra, Asitoda and Mānasa are always enjoyed by deities. 25

## \|\| 2.2.26 \|\|

*aruṇodaṃ mahābhadramasitodaṃ samānasam \|*  
*sarāṃsyotāni catvāri devabhogyāni sarvadā \|\| 26 \|\|*

The mountain ranges Śītāmbha, Kumanda, Kurarin, Mālyavant, with Vaikaṅka as the foremost, extend eastwards from Meru. 26

## \|\| 2.2.27 \|\|

*śītāmbhaś ca kumundaś ca kurarī mālyavāṃs tathā \|*  
*vaikaṅkaprasukhā meroḥ pūrvataḥ kesarācalāḥ \|\| 27 \|\|*

The Trikūṭa, Śiśira, Pataṅga, Rucaka, Niṣadha and other ranges lie south. 27

## \|\| 2.2.28 \|\|

*trikūṭaḥ śiśiraścaiva pataṅgo rucakas tathā \|*  
*niṣadādyā dakṣiṇatastasya kesaraparvataḥ \|\| 28 \|\|*

Śikhivāsas, Savaiḍūya, Kapila, Gandhamādana, with Jārudhi as the main one, are to the west. 28

## \|\| 2.2.29 \|\|

*śikhivāsāḥ savaiḍūryaḥ kapilo gandhamādanaḥ \|*  
*jārudhipramukhāstadvat paścime kesarācalāḥ \|\| 29 \|\|*

Śaṅkhakūṭa, Ṛṣabha, Haṃsa, Nāga, Kālañjana and others lie northward. These are situated in the regions around Mount Meru, including Jaṭhara. 29

## \|\| 2.2.30 \|\|

*meror anantarāṅgeṣu jaṭharādiṣvavasthitāḥ \|*  
*śaṃśakūṭotha ṛṣabho haṃso nāgas tathā paraḥ \|*  
*kālañjanādyāś ca tathā uttare kesarācalāḥ \|\| 30 \|\|*

On the summit of Mount Meru is Brahmā’s wondrous city, fourteen thousand leagues across, Maitreya, and renowned throughout the heavens. 30

## \|\| 2.2.31 \|\|

*caturdaśasahasrāṇi yojanānāṃ mahāpurī \|*  
*merorupari maitreya brahmaṇaḥ prathitā divi \|\| 31 \|\|*

On all sides of that city, at the four cardinal points and the four intermediate ones, are the famed and excellent cities of Indra and other world-protecting deities. 31

## \|\| 2.2.32 \|\|

*tasyāḥsamantataścāṣṭau diśāsu vidiśāsu ca \|*  
*indrādilokapālānāṃ prakhyātāḥ pravarāḥ puraḥ \|\| 32 \|\|*

Here, the River Gaṅgā, which flows across the foot of Viṣṇu, having first bathed the face of the moon, cascades from the heavens on every side of Brahmā’s city. 32

## \|\| 2.2.33 \|\|

*viṣṇupādaviniṣkrāntā plāvayitvendumaṇḍalam \|*  
*samantādbrahyaṇaḥ puryāṃ gaṅga patati vai divaḥ \|\| 33 \|\|*

After it has descended there, it flows in four directions as the Sītā, Alakanandā, Cakṣu and Bhadrā rivers, respectively. 33

## \|\| 2.2.34 \|\|

*sā tatra patitā dikṣu caturdhā pratipadyate \|*  
*sītā cālakanandā ca cakṣurbhadrā ca vai kramāt \|\| 34 \|\|*

The Sītā flows eastwards through the sky from mountain to mountain, then crosses the eastern region of Bhadrāśva to the ocean. 34

## \|\| 2.2.35 \|\|

*pūrveṇa śailācchītā tu śailaṃ yātyantarīkṣagā \|*  
*tataś ca pūrvavarṣaiṇa bhadrāśvainaiti sārṇavam \|\| 35 \|\|*

Similarly, the Alakanandā flows south to Bhārata, before dividing into seven streams and entering the ocean, great sage. 35

## \|\| 2.2.36 \|\|

*tathaivālakanandāpi dakṣiṇenaitya bhāratam \|*  
*prayāti sāgaraṃ bhūtvā saptabhedā mahāmune \|\| 36 \|\|*

The Cakṣu flows west past all the mountain ranges and, having reached the western region of Ketumāla, there joins the ocean. 36

## \|\| 2.2.37 \|\|

*cakṣuścaṃ paścimagirīnatītya sakalāṃstataḥ \|*  
*paścimaṃ ketumālākhya varṣaṃ gatvaiti sāgaram \|\| 37 \|\|*

Likewise, the Bhadrā crosses the northern mountain ranges and the land of Northern Kurus to reach the ocean in that direction, great sage. 37

## \|\| 2.2.38 \|\|

*bhadrā tathottaragirīnuttarāṃś ca tathā kurūn \|*  
*atītyottaramambhodhiṃ samabhyeti mahāmune \|\| 38 \|\|*

Meru stands amid the Nīla, Niṣadha, Mālyavant and Gandhamādana mountains, like the seed head at the centre of a lotus. 38

## \|\| 2.2.39 \|\|

*ānīlaniṣadhāyāmau mālyavadgandhamādanau \|*  
*tayormadhyagato merūḥ karṇikākārasaṃsthitaḥ \|\| 39 \|\|*

The Bhāratas, Ketumālas, Bhadrāśvas and the Kurus, like the petals of the lotus-world, lie beyond the limits of the mountains. 39

## \|\| 2.2.40 \|\|

*bhāratāḥ ketumālāś ca bhadrāśvāḥ kuravas tathā \|*  
*patrāṇi lokapadmasya maryādāśailabāhyataḥ \|\| 40 \|\|*

Jaṭhara and Devakūṭa are two ranges that form a barrier extending north to south, meeting the Nīla and Niṣadha mountains. 40

## \|\| 2.2.41 \|\|

*jaṭharo hemakūṭaś ca maryādāparvatāvubhau \|*  
*tau dakṣimottarāyāmāvānīlaniṣadhāyatau \|\| 41 \|\|*

Gandhamādana and Kailāsa stretch east and west, eighty leagues in width, from sea to sea. 41

## \|\| 2.2.42 \|\|

*gandhamādanakailāsau pūrvavaccāyatāvubhau \|*  
*āśīti yojanāyāmāvarṇavāntavyavasthitau \|\| 42 \|\|*

The two ranges Niṣadha and Pāriyātra also form barriers to the west of Meru, as do those on the east. 42

## \|\| 2.2.43 \|\|

*niṣadhaḥ pariyātraś ca maryādāparvatāvubhau \|*  
*meroḥ paścimadigbhāge yathā pūrve tathā sthitau \|\| 43 \|\|*

Triśṛṅga and Jārudhi, two regional ranges in the north, also extend east and west from sea to sea. 43

## \|\| 2.2.44 \|\|

*triśṛṅgo jārudhiścaiva uttarau varṣaparvatau \|*  
*pūrvapaścāyatāvetāvarṇavāntarvyavasthitau \|\| 44 \|\|*

Thus, I’ve described for you, best of sages, the mountain ranges of Jaṭhara and the rest, which, in pairs, form barriers on the sides of Meru. 44

## \|\| 2.2.45 \|\|

*ityete munivaryoktā maryādāparvatāstava \|*  
*jaṭharādyāḥ sthitā merosteṣāṃ dvaudvau caturdiśam \|\| 45 \|\|*

The chains of mountains on each side of Meru that I’ve mentioned, such as Śītā, are exceedingly delightful, sage. The valleys found between these ranges, inhabited by siddhas and cāraṇas, are pleasant, as are the forests and the cities. 45

## \|\| 2.2.46 \|\|

*mero ścaturdiśaṃ yetu proktāḥ kesaraparvatāḥ \|*  
*śītāntādyā mune teṣāmatīva hi manoramāḥ \|*  
*śailānāmantare droṇyaḥ siddhacāraṇasevitāḥ \|\| 46 \|\|*

There the excellent sanctuaries of Lakṣmī, Viṣṇu, Agni, Sūrya and other deities, best of sages, are frequented by the foremost kiṃnaras. 46

## \|\| 2.2.47 \|\|

*suramyāṇi tathā tāsu kānanāni purāṇi ca \|*  
*lakṣmīviṣṇvagnisūryādidevānāṃ muni sattama \|*  
*tāsvāyatanavaryāṇi juṣṭāni varakinnaraiḥ \|\| 47 \|\|*

Gandharvas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, Daityas and Dānavas disport themselves both day and night in those splendid valleys. 47

## \|\| 2.2.48 \|\|

*gandharvayakṣarakṣāṃsi tathā daiteyadānavāḥ \|*  
*krīḍanti tāsu ramyāsu śailadroṇīṣvaharnisam \|\| 48 \|\|*

These regions are like heaven on earth and are the abode of the righteous, sage. Evildoers may not approach them, even after a hundred rebirths. 48

## \|\| 2.2.49 \|\|

*bhaumā hyete smṛtāḥ svargā dharmiṇāmālayā mune \|*  
*naituṣu pāpakarmāṇo yānti janmaśatair api \|\| 49 \|\|*

In the region of Bhadrāśva, Lord Viṣṇu dwells in the form of horse-headed Hayaśiras, brahmin. He takes the form of a boar in Ketumāla and a tortoise in Bhārata. 49

## \|\| 2.2.50 \|\|

*bhadrāśve bhagavānviṣṇurāste hayaśirā dvija \|*  
*varājaḥ ketumāle tu bhārate kūrmarūpadhṛk \|\| 50 \|\|*

Govinda takes the form of a fish in the land of Kurus, but Janārdana of universal form is in fact everywhere, and Hari, the universal being, is master of all. 50

## \|\| 2.2.51 \|\|

*matsyarūpaś ca govindaḥ kuruṣvāste janārdanaḥ \|*  
*viśvarūpeṇa sarvatra sarvaḥ sarvatrago hariḥ \|\| 51 \|\|*

He is the foundation of everything, Maitreya, and lies at the heart of all. 51

## \|\| 2.2.52 \|\|

*sarvasyādhārabhūtosau maitreyāstekhilātmakaḥ \|\| 52 \|\|*

The other eight regions beginning with Kimpuruṣa are free from pain, fatigue, anxiety, hunger, calamities and other woes, great sage. 52

## \|\| 2.2.53 \|\|

*yāniṃ kiṃpuruṣādini varṣāṇyaṣṭau mahāmune \|*  
*na teṣu śauko nāyāso nodvegaḥ kṣudbhayādikam \|\| 53 \|\|*

Their inhabitants are healthy, fearless, free from suffering and live for ten or twelve millennia. 53

## \|\| 2.2.54 \|\|

*svasthāḥ prajā nirātaṅkāḥsarvaduḥkhavivarjitāḥ \|*  
*daśa dvādaśavarṣāṇāṃ sahasrāṇi sthirāyuṣaḥ \|\| 54 \|\|*

The rain god Indra has no need to provide showers in those regions as there is sufficient water on the ground, nor are they subject to the cycles of the ages, Kṛta, Tretā and the others. 54

## \|\| 2.2.55 \|\|

*na teṣu varṣate devo bhaumānyambhāṃsi teṣu vai \|*  
*kṛtatretādikaṃ naiva teṣu sthāneṣu kalpanā \|\| 55 \|\|*

In each of these regions are seven mountain ranges, best of brahmins, where hundreds upon hundreds of rivers rise. 55

## Chapter 3 - The land of Bhārata

## \|\| 2.3.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*uttaraṃ yatsamudrasya himādreścaiva dakṣiṇam \|*  
*varṣaṃ tadbhārataṃ nāma bhāratī yatra santatiḥ \|\| 1 \|\|*

The region north of the ocean and south of the Himālaya, known as Bhārata, is inhabited by Bharata’s descendants. 1

## \|\| 2.3.2 \|\|

*navayojanasāhasro vistārosya mahāmune \|*  
*karmabhūmiriyaṃ svargamapavargaṃ ca gacchatām \|\| 2 \|\|*

Nine thousand leagues across, great sage, it’s where those who reach heaven or liberation do their deeds. 2

## \|\| 2.3.3 \|\|

*mahendro malayaḥ sahyaḥ śuktimānṛkṣaparvataḥ \|*  
*vindhyaś ca pārīyātraś ca saptātra kulaparvatāḥ \|\| 3 \|\|*

Mahendra, Malaya, Sahya, Śuktimant, Ṛkṣparvata, Vindhya and Pāriyātra are the seven mountain ranges. 3

## \|\| 2.3.4 \|\|

*ataḥ saṃprāpyate svargo suktimasmātprayānti vai \|*  
*tiryaktvaṃ narakaṃ cāpi yāntyataḥ puruṣā mune \|\| 4 \|\|*

From this region one may ascend to heaven or win liberation, sage, but folk are also born as animals or descend to hell. 4

## \|\| 2.3.5 \|\|

*itaḥ svargaś ca mokṣaś ca madhyaṃ cāntaś ca gamyate \|*  
*na khlavanyatra martyānāṃ karmabhūmau vidhīyate \|\| 5 \|\|*

From Bhārata, heaven, liberation, somewhere in between or an end are possible. No other region is given to the deeds of mortals. 5

## \|\| 2.3.6 \|\|

*bhāratasyāsya varṣasya navabhādānnaśāmaya \|*  
*idradvīpaḥ kaseruś ca tāmraparṇāṃ gabhastimān \|\| 6 \|\|*

Listen to the nine divisions of the land of Bhārata: Indradvīpa, Kaśeru, Tāmraparṇa, Gabhastimant, 6

## \|\| 2.3.7 \|\|

*nāgadvīpas tathā sāmyo gandharvastvatha cāruṇaḥ \|*  
*ayaṃ tu navamastaṣāṃ dvīpaḥ sāgārasaṃvṛtaḥ \|\| 7 \|\|*

Nāgadvīpa, Saumya, Gāndharva and Vāruṇa. The ninth of these is Dvīpa, surrounded by the ocean. 7

## \|\| 2.3.8 \|\|

*yojanānāṃ sahasraṃ tu dvīpoyaṃ dakṣiṇottarāt \|*  
*pūrve kirātā yasyānte paścime yavanāḥ sthitāḥ \|\| 8 \|\|*

Dvīpa is a thousand leagues from north to south. At the eastern edge of Bhārata are the Kirātas and in the west the Yavanas. 8

## \|\| 2.3.9 \|\|

*brāhmaṇāḥ kṣatriyā vaiśyā madhye śabdrāś ca bhāgaśaḥ \|*  
*ijyāyudhavaṇijyādyair vartayanto vyavasthitāḥ \|\| 9 \|\|*

In the centre are brahmins, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras, engaged in their respective occupations of sacrifice, warfare, commerce and service. 9

## \|\| 2.3.10 \|\|

*śatadrūcandrabhāgādyā himayatpādanirgatāḥ \|*  
*vedasmṛtimukhādyāś ca pārīyātrodbhavā mune \|\| 10 \|\|*

The Śatadrū, Candrabhāga and other rivers flow from the foot of the Himalāya. Others, most importantly the Vedasmṛti, arise in the Pāriyātra ranges, sage. 10

## \|\| 2.3.11 \|\|

*narmadā murasādyāś ca nadyo vindhyādrinirgatāḥ \|*  
*tāpīpayoṣṇīnirvindhyā prasūkhā ṛkṣasaṃbhavāḥ \|\| 11 \|\|*

The Narmadā, Surasā and others flow from the Vindhya mountains, while the Tāpī, Payoṣṇī and Nirvindhyā are the major rivers issuing from the Ṛkṣa ranges. 11

## \|\| 2.3.12 \|\|

*godāvarī bhīmarathī kṛṣṇaveṇyādikās tathā \|*  
*sahyapādodbhavā nadyaḥ smṛtāḥ pāpabhayāpahāḥ \|\| 12 \|\|*

The Godāvarī, Bhīmarathī, Kṛṣṇaveṇī and other streams that arise at the foot of the Sahya mountains assuage the sins of people and their fears. 12

## \|\| 2.3.13 \|\|

*kṛtamālā tāmraparṇiprasukhā malayodbhavāḥ \|*  
*trisāmā carṣikulyādyā mahendraprabhavāḥ smṛtāḥ \|\| 13 \|\|*

The Kṛtamālā and Tāmraparṇī are the largest rivers flowing from the Malaya ranges. The Trisāmā, Cāryakulyā and others arise in the Mahendra mountains. 13

## \|\| 2.3.14 \|\|

*ṛṣikulyākumārādyāḥ śuktimatpādasambhavāḥ \|*  
*āsāṃ nadya upānadyaḥ santyanyāś ca sahasraśaḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

The Ṛṣikulyā, Kumārā and others flow from the foot of the Śaktimant ranges. There are thousands of other streams besides, both great and small. 14

## \|\| 2.3.15 \|\|

*tāsvime kurupāñcālā madhyadeśādayo janāḥ \|*  
*pūrvadeśādikāścaiva kāmarūpanivāsinaḥ \|\| 15 \|\|*

The Kuru, Pāñcāla and other peoples inhabit the central districts. The easterners include the residents of Kāmarūpa. 15

## \|\| 2.3.16 \|\|

*puṇḍrāḥ kaliṅgā magadhā dakṣiṇādyāś ca sarvaśaḥ \|*  
*tathāparāntāḥ saurāṣṭrāḥ śūrābhīrāstathārbudāḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

Puṇḍras, Kaliṅgas, Magadhas and others live in the southern reaches. To the west are the Saurāṣṭras, Śūras, Bhīras and Arbudas. 16

## \|\| 2.3.17 \|\|

*kārūṣā mālabāścaiva pāriyātra nivāsinaḥ \|*  
*sauvīrāḥ saindhavā hūṇāḥ sālvāḥ kosalavāsināḥ \|\| 17 \|\|*

The Kārūṣas and Mālavas inhabit the Pāriyātra mountains. The Sauvīras, Saindhavas, Hūṇas, Sālvas and the inhabitants of Śākala, 17

## \|\| 2.3.18 \|\|

*mādrārāmāstathāmbaṣṭhā pārasīkādayas tathā \|*  
*āsāṃ pibanti salilaṃ vasanti sahitāḥ sadā \|*  
*samīpato mahābhāga hṛṣṭapuṣṭajanākulāḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

The Madras, Rāmas, Ambaṣṭhas, Pārasīkas and others drink the waters of these rivers and reside permanently in their vicinity, enjoying great prosperity. These rivers are populated by peoples, affluent and happy. 18

## \|\| 2.3.19 \|\|

*catvāri bhārate varṣe yugānyatra mahāmune \|*  
*kṛtaṃ tretā dvāparañca kaliścānyatra na kvacit \|\| 19 \|\|*

Here in the region of Bhārata, the fourfold ages of Kṛta, Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali are experienced, great sage, but nowhere else. 19

## \|\| 2.3.20 \|\|

*tapastapyanti munayo juhvate cātra yajvanaḥ \|*  
*dānāni cātra dīyante paralokārthamādarāt \|\| 20 \|\|*

Ascetics perform austerities, the devout conduct sacrifices and make donations with piety, all for the sake of the next world. 20

## \|\| 2.3.21 \|\|

*puruṣair yajñapuruṣo jambūdvīpe sadejyate \|*  
*yajñair yajñamayo viṣṇuranyadvīpeṣu cānyathā \|\| 21 \|\|*

In Jambūdvīpa, people always worship Viṣṇu, the spirit of the sacrifice and practice itself, with such rituals, but on other continents, he’s revered in other ways. 21

## \|\| 2.3.22 \|\|

*atrāpi bhārataṃ śreṣṭhaṃ janbūdvīpe mahāmune \|*  
*yato hi karmabhūreṣā hyatonyā bhogabhūmayaḥ \|\| 22 \|\|*

Furthermore, in all of Jambūdvīpa, Bhārata is the perfect region, great sage, because it alone is fit for sacrificial action, the others being set on pleasure. 22

## \|\| 2.3.23 \|\|

*atra janmasahasrāṇāṃ sahasrair api sattama \|*  
*kadācillabhate janturmānuṣyaṃ puṇyasaṃcayāt \|\| 23 \|\|*

A being is reborn in Jambūdvīpa in human form, best of sages, from merit won in a thousand thousand lifetimes. 23

## \|\| 2.3.24 \|\|

*gāyanti devāḥ kila gītakāni dhanyāstu te bhārata bhūmibhāge \|*  
*svargāpavargāspadamārgabhūte bhavanti bhūyaḥ puruṣāḥ suratvāt \|\| 24 \|\|*

The deities themselves have sung this song: ‘More fortunate than the gods are those who live in Bhārata, the land that leads to heaven and beyond. 24

## \|\| 2.3.25 \|\|

*karmāṇyasaṃkalpitatatphalāni saṃnyasya viṣṇo paramātmbhūte \|*  
*avāpya tāṃ karmamahīmanante tasmillayaṃ ye tvamalāḥ prayānti \|\| 25 \|\|*

Dedicating all their practice, never thinking of reward, to Viṣṇu, the highest spirit, and having reached that land of sacrificial action, they go forth unstained to liberation. 25

## \|\| 2.3.26 \|\|

*jānīma naitatkva vayaṃ vilīne svargaprade karmaṇi dehabandham \|*  
*prāpsyāma dhanyāḥ khalu te manuṣyā ye bhārate nendriyaviprahīnāḥ \|\| 26 \|\|*

We do not know where we’ll be born when the karma that gave us heaven has expired. Fortunate indeed are those who are born in Bhārata with all their senses sharp.’ 26

## \|\| 2.3.27 \|\|

*navavarṣaṃ tu maitreya jambudvīpamidaṃ mayā \|*  
*lakṣayojanavistāraṃ saṃkṣepāt kathitaṃ tava \|\| 27 \|\|*

I’ve described in brief to you, Maitreya, the land of Jambūdvīpa, with its nine regions, one hundred thousand leagues across. 27

## \|\| 2.3.28 \|\|

*jambūdvīpaṃ samāvṛtya lakṣayojanavistaraḥ \|*  
*maitreya valayākāraḥ sthitaḥ kṣārodadhirbahiḥ \|\| 28 \|\|*

Jambūdvīpa is ringed by an ocean of salty water, Maitreya, as wide as the land it girds. 28

## Chapter 4 - Plakṣadvīpa and the other continents

## \|\| 2.4.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*kṣārodena yathā dvīpo jambūsaṃjño 'bhiveṣṭitaḥ \|*  
*saṃveṣṭya kṣāramudadhiṃ plakṣadvīpas tathā sthitaḥ \|\| 1 \|\|*

Just as the land called Jambūdvīpa is ringed by an ocean of salty water, that ocean is surrounded by the land of Plakṣadvīpa, the ‘Land of the Pipal Tree’. 1

## \|\| 2.4.2 \|\|

*jambūdvīpasya vistāraḥ śatasāhasrasaṃmitaḥ \|*  
*sa evaṃ dviguṇo brahman plakṣadvīpa udāhṛtaḥ \|\| 2 \|\|*

Jambūdvīpa is one hundred thousand leagues across, brahmin, and Plakṣadvīpa is said to be twice as wide again. 2

## \|\| 2.4.3 \|\|

*sapta medhātitheḥ putrāḥ plakṣadvīpeśvarasya vai \|*  
*jyeṣṭha śāntahayo nāma śiśirastadanantaraḥ \|\| 3 \|\|*

Medhātithi, lord of Plakṣadvīpa, had seven sons, the eldest of whom was Śāntabhaya, followed by Śiśira, 3

## \|\| 2.4.4 \|\|

*sukhodayastathānandaḥ śivaḥ kṣemaka eva ca \|*  
*dhruvaś ca saptamasteṣāṃ plakṣadvīpeśvarā hi te \|\| 4 \|\|*

Sukhodaya, Ānanda, Śiva and Kṣemaka, with Dhruva as the seventh among them. All became rulers of Plakṣadvīpa. 4

## \|\| 2.4.5 \|\|

*pūrvaṃ śāntahayaṃ varṣaṃ śiśiraṃ ca sukhaṃ tathā \|*  
*ānandaṃ ca śivaṃ caiva kṣemakaṃ dhruvameva ca \|\| 5 \|\|*

The region named for its sovereign Śāntabhaya is the first, followed by Śiśira, Sukhada, Ānanda, Śiva, Kṣemaka and Dhruva. 5

## \|\| 2.4.6 \|\|

*maryādākārakāsteṣāṃ tathānye varṣaparvatāḥ \|*  
*saptaiva teṣāṃ nāmāni śṛṇuṣva munisattama \|\| 6 \|\|*

The borders of each region are formed by seven ranges. Listen to their names, best of sages: 6

## \|\| 2.4.7 \|\|

*gomadeścaiva candraś ca nāradau dundubhis tathā \|*  
*somakaḥ mumanāścaiva vaibhrājaścaiva saptamaḥ \|\| 7 \|\|*

Gomeda, Candra, Nārada, Dundubhi, Somaka and Sumanas, with Mount Vaibhāja as the seventh. 7

## \|\| 2.4.8 \|\|

*varṣācaleṣu ramyeṣu varṣeṣveteṣu cānaghāḥ \|*  
*vasaṃti devagandharvasahitāḥ satataṃ prajāḥ \|\| 8 \|\|*

In these pleasant ranges and the regions dwell blameless inhabitants, always in the company of deities and gandharvas. 8

## \|\| 2.4.9 \|\|

*teṣu puṇyā janapadāścirācca mriyate janaḥ \|*  
*nādhayo vyādhayo vāpi sarvakālasukhaṃ hi tat \|\| 9 \|\|*

In these locales are auspicious settlements whose residents enjoy long lives, free from pain and illness, always living happily. 9

## \|\| 2.4.10 \|\|

*teṣāṃ nadyastu saptaiva varṣāṇāṃ ca samudragāḥ \|*  
*nāmatastāḥ pravakṣyāmi śrutāḥ pāpaṃ haranti yāḥ \|\| 10 \|\|*

The seven regions each have a river flowing to the sea. I’ll tell you all their names, as hearing them will wash away your sins: 10

## \|\| 2.4.11 \|\|

*anutaptā śikhī caiva vipāśā tridivāklamaḥ \|*  
*amṛtā sukṛtā caiva saptaitāstatra nimnagāḥ \|\| 11 \|\|*

Anutaptā, Śikhī, Vipāśā, Tridivā, Kramu and Amṛtā, with Sukṛtā as the seventh—such are the rivers found there. 11

## \|\| 2.4.12 \|\|

*ete śailās tathā nadyaḥ pradhānāḥ kathitāstava \|*  
*kṣudraśailās tathā nadyastatra santi sahasraśaḥ \|*  
*tāḥ pibandi sadāhṛṣṭā nadīrjanapadāstute \|\| 12 \|\|*

I’ve told you about the mountains and the major rivers, but there are thousands of lesser chains and streams from which the inhabitants always delight in drinking. 12

## \|\| 2.4.13 \|\|

*apasarpiṇī na teṣāṃ vai na caivotsarpiṇī dvija \|*  
*na tvevāsti yugāvasthā teṣu stāneṣu saptasu \|\| 13 \|\|*

In those seven regions, lifespans never shrink or grow, brahmin, as the cycle of four ages is unknown. 13

## \|\| 2.4.14 \|\|

*tretāyugasamaḥ kālaḥ sarvadaiva mahāmate \|*  
*plakṣadvīpādiṣu brahmañ śākadvīpāntikeṣu vai \|\| 14 \|\|*

There, it’s always like the Tretā age, great sage. The inhabitants of the five continents between Plakṣadvīpa and Śākadvīpa, brahmin, 14

## \|\| 2.4.15 \|\|

*pañca varṣasahasrāṇi janā jīvantyanāmayāḥ \|*  
*dharmaḥ pañcasvathaiteṣu varṇāśramavibhāgaśaḥ \|\| 15 \|\|*

Live healthy lives for five millennia. On these five continents, occupations are determined by one’s community and stage of life. 15

## \|\| 2.4.16 \|\|

*varṇāś ca tatra catvārastānnibodha vadāmi te \|\| 16 \|\|*

There are four orders in Plakṣadvīpa; listen while I name them for you: 16

## \|\| 2.4.17 \|\|

*āryakāḥ kurāraścaiva vidiśyā bhāvinaśce te \|*  
*viprakṣatrivaiśyāste śūdrāś ca munisattama \|\| 17 \|\|*

The Āryakas, Kuraras, Viviṃśas and Bhāvins are like our brahmins, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras. 17

## \|\| 2.4.18 \|\|

*jmabūvṛkṣapramāṇastu tanmadhye sumahāṃstaruḥ \|*  
*plakṣastannāmasaṃjñoyaṃ plakṣadvīpo dvijottama \|\| 18 \|\|*

There’s one huge tree in the middle of Plakṣadvīpa, best of sages, as big as the jambū, called the plakṣa or pipal, hence its name. 18

## \|\| 2.4.19 \|\|

*ijyate tatra bhagavāṃstair varṇair āryakādibhiḥ \|*  
*sīmarūpi jagatstraṣṭā sarvaḥ sarveśvaro hariḥ \|\| 19 \|\|*

All the communities there, beginning with the Āryakas, worship Lord Hari, the universal creator of the world and master of all, in the form of Soma the Moon. 19

## \|\| 2.4.20 \|\|

*plakṣadvīpapramāṇena plakṣadvīpaḥ samāvṛtaḥ \|*  
*tathaivekṣuramodena pariveṣānukāriṇā \|\| 20 \|\|*

Plakṣadvīpa is girt by a circular sea of syrup, as wide as the continent itself. 20

## \|\| 2.4.21 \|\|

*ityevaṃ tava maitreya plakṣadvīpa udāhṛtaḥ \|*  
*saṃkṣepeṇa mayā bhūyaḥ śālmalaṃ me nisāmaya \|\| 21 \|\|*

I’ve briefly described the land of Plakṣadvīpa for you, Maitreya. Now you’ll hear about Śālmala from me. 21

## \|\| 2.4.22 \|\|

*śālmamasyeśvaroḥvīro vapuṣmāṃstatsutāñchṛṇu \|*  
*teṣāṃ tu nāmasaṃjñāni saptavarṣāṇi tāni vai \|\| 22 \|\|*

The lord of Śālmala is the hero Vapuṣmant. Listen to the names of his sons, which are also the names of the seven regions: 22

## \|\| 2.4.23 \|\|

*śvetotha haritaścaiva jīmūto rohitas tathā \|*  
*vaidyuto mānasaścaiva suprabhaś ca mahāmune \|\| 23 \|\|*

Śveta, Harita, Jīmūta, Rohita, Vaidyuta, Mānasa and Suprabha, great sage. 23

## \|\| 2.4.24 \|\|

*śālmalena samudrosau dvīpenekṣurasodakaḥ \|*  
*vistāradviguṇenātha sarvataḥ saṃvṛtaḥ sthitaḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

The sea of syrup is ringed by the land of Śālmala, twice as wide as the sea itself. 24

## \|\| 2.4.25 \|\|

*tatrāpi parvatāḥ sapta vijñeyā ratnayonayaḥ \|*  
*varṣābhivyañjakā ye tu tathā sapta ca nimnagāḥ \|\| 25 \|\|*

This continent also has seven ranges, the source of jewels, which divide the regions, in addition to seven rivers: 25

## \|\| 2.4.26 \|\|

*kumudaścennataścaiva tṛtīyaś ca balāhakaḥ \|*  
*droṇo yatra mahauṣadhyaḥ sa caturtho mahīdharaḥ \|\| 26 \|\|*

Kumuda, Unnata, Balāhaka is the third, Mount Droṇa the great source of healing plants is fourth, 26

## \|\| 2.4.27 \|\|

*kaṅkastupañcamaḥ ṣaṣṭhī mahiṣaḥ saptamas tathā \|*  
*kakudmānparvatavaraḥ sarinnāmāni me śṛṇu \|\| 27 \|\|*

Kaṅka is the fifth, Mahiṣa the sixth and Kukudmant the seventh splendid mountain. Now you’ll hear the rivers’ names from me: 27

## \|\| 2.4.28 \|\|

*yonitoyo vitṛṣṇā ca candrā muktā vimocanī \|*  
*nivṛttiḥ saptamī tāsāṃ smṛtāstāḥ pāpaśāntidāḥ \|\| 28 \|\|*

Yonī, Toyā, Vitṛṣṇā, Candrā, Śuklā and Vimocanī, with Nivṛtti as the seventh—all of which are thought to wash away one’s sins. 28

## \|\| 2.4.29 \|\|

*śvetañca haritaṃ caiva vaidyutaṃ mānasaṃ tathā \|*  
*jīmūtaṃ rohitaṃ caiva suprabhaṃ cāpi śobhanam \|*  
*saptaitāni tu varṣāṇi cāturvarṇyayutāni vai \|\| 29 \|\|*

Śveta, Harita, Jīmūta, Rohita, Vaidyuta, Mānasa and especially the lovely Suprabha are the seven regions. There are also four communities. 29

## \|\| 2.4.30 \|\|

*śālmale ye tu varṇāś ca vasantyete mahāmune \|*  
*kapilāścāruṇāḥ pītāḥ kṛṣṇāścaiva pṛthak pṛthak \|\| 30 \|\|*

Those who inhabit Śālmala, great sage, are the Kapilas, Aruṇas, Pītās and Kṛṣṇas, like our brahmins, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras. 30

## \|\| 2.4.31 \|\|

*brahmaṇāḥ kṣatrayā vaiśyāḥ śudrāścaiva yajantitam \|*  
*bhagavantaṃ samastasya viṣṇumātmānamavyayam \|*  
*vāyubhūtaṃ makhaśreṣṭhair yajvano yajñasaṃsthitim \|\| 31 \|\|*

The pious population worships everlasting Viṣṇu, heart of all who constitutes the sacrifice, in the form of Vāyu, god of winds, with finest sacrifices. 31

## \|\| 2.4.32 \|\|

*devānāmatra sānnadhyamatīva sumanohare \|*  
*śālmaliḥ sumahānvṛkṣo nāmnā nirvṛtikārakaḥ \|\| 32 \|\|*

Here in this very pleasant land, whose denizens enjoy the company of gods, there is a great śālmali, a cottonwood tree, bestowing perfect bliss. 32

## \|\| 2.4.33 \|\|

*eṣa dvīpaḥ samudreṇa surodena samāvṛtaḥ \|*  
*vistārācchālmalasyaiva samena tu samantataḥ \|\| 33 \|\|*

Śālmala is ringed by a sea of wine as wide as the land itself. 33

## \|\| 2.4.34 \|\|

*surodakaḥ parivṛtaḥ kuśadvīpena sarvataḥ \|*  
*śālmalasya tu vistarāddviguṇena samantataḥ \|\| 34 \|\|*

This sea of wine is ringed by the land of Kuśadvīpa, twice as wide as Śālmala. 34

## \|\| 2.4.35 \|\|

*jyotiṣmataḥ kuśadvīpe sapta putrāñchṛṇuṣva tān \|\| 35 \|\|*

In Kuśadvīpa, King Jyotiṣmant had seven sons. Listen to their names: 35

## \|\| 2.4.36 \|\|

*udbhido veṇumāṃścaiva veratho lambano dhṛtiḥ \|*  
*prabhākarotikapilastannāmā varṣapaddhatiḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

Udbhida, Veṇumant, Svairatha, Lambana, Dhṛti, Prabhākara and Kapila. These are also the names of the seven regions there. 36

## \|\| 2.4.37 \|\|

*tasminvasanti manujāḥ saha daite yadānavaiḥ \|*  
*tathaiva devagandharvayakṣakiṃpuruṣādayaḥ \|\| 37 \|\|*

In that land, people dwell with deities, Daityas, Dānavas, gandharvas, yakṣas, kimpuruṣas and the rest. 37

## \|\| 2.4.38 \|\|

*varṇāstatrāpi catvāro nijānuṣṭhānatatparāḥ \|*  
*daminaḥ śuṣmiṇaḥ snehā mandehāś ca mahāmune \|\| 38 \|\|*

Here are also four communities, each devoted to their duties: Damins, Śuṣmins, Snehas and Mandehas, great sage, 38

## \|\| 2.4.39 \|\|

*brahmaṇāḥ kṣatriyā vaiśyāḥ śūdrāścānukramoditāḥ \|\| 39 \|\|*

Corresponding to our brahmins, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras. 39

## \|\| 2.4.40 \|\|

*yathoskakarmakartṛtvātsvādhikārokṣayāya te \|*  
*tatraiva taṃ kuśadvīpe brahmarūpaṃ janārdanam \|*  
*yajantaḥ kṣapayanty ugram adhikāraphalapradam \|\| 40 \|\|*

Because they undertake prescribed responsibilities, and in order to escape their earthly tasks, there on Kuśadvīpa, while worshipping Janārdana in the form of Brahmā, they abandon deeds that would otherwise result in further tedious duties. 40

## \|\| 2.4.41 \|\|

*vidrumo hemaśailaś ca dyatimān puṣpavāṃs tathā \|*  
*kuśaśayo hariścaiva saptamo mandarācalaḥ \|\| 41 \|\|*

Vidruma, Hemaśaila, Dyutimant, Puṣpavant, Kuśeśaya and Hari, with Mount Mandata as the seventh— 41

## \|\| 2.4.42 \|\|

*varṣācalāstu saptaite tatra dvīpe mahāmune \|*  
*nadyaś ca sapta tāsāṃ tu śṛṇu nāmānyanukramāt \|\| 42 \|\|*

These are the seven ranges that divide the regions of that land, great sage. Now listen to the names of the seven streams in turn: 42

## \|\| 2.4.43 \|\|

*dhūtapāpā śivā caiva pavitrā saṃmatis tathā \|*  
*vidyudambhā mahī cānyā sarvapāpa harāstvimāḥ \|\| 43 \|\|*

Dhūtapāpā, Śivā, Pavitrā, Saṃmati, Vidyudambhā, Mahī and Sarvapāpaharā, 43

## \|\| 2.4.44 \|\|

*anyāḥ sahasraśastatra kṣudranadyastathācalāḥ \|*  
*kuśadvīpe guśastambaḥ saṃjñayā tasya tatsmṛtam \|\| 44 \|\|*

In addition, there are thousands of lesser streams and mountains. Kuśadvīpa is thought to be named for a clump of kuśa-grass that grows there. 44

## \|\| 2.4.45 \|\|

*tatpramāṇena sa dvīpo ghṛtodena samāvṛtaḥ \|*  
*ghṛtodaś ca samudro vai krauñcadvīpena saṃvṛtaḥ \|\| 45 \|\|*

It’s ringed by a sea of ghee as wide as the land itself. That sea is then enclosed within the land of Krauñcadvīpa. 45

## \|\| 2.4.46 \|\|

*krauñcadvīpo mahābhāga śruyatāñcāparo mahān \|*  
*kuśadvīpasya vistārā ddviguṇo yasya vistaraḥ \|\| 46 \|\|*

Now you’ll hear about that other continent, Krauñcadvīpa, fortunate sage, twice as wide as Kuśadvīpa. 46

## \|\| 2.4.47 \|\|

*krauñcadvīpe dyutimataḥ putrāstasya mahātmanaḥ \|*  
*tannāmāni ca varṣāṇi teṣāṃ cakre mahīpatiḥ \|\| 47 \|\|*

On Krauñcadvīpa, King Dyutimant had seven great sons, after whom he named its regions: 47

## \|\| 2.4.48 \|\|

*kuśalo mallagaścoṣṇaḥ pīvarothāndhakārakaḥ \|*  
*muniś ca dundubhiścaiva saptaite tatsutā mune \|\| 48 \|\|*

Kuśala, Manuga, Uṣṇa, Pīvara, Andhakāraka, Muni and Dundubhi—such were his seven sons, sage. 48

## \|\| 2.4.49 \|\|

*tatrāpi devagandharvasevitāḥ sumanoharāḥ \|*  
*varṣācalā mahābuddhe teṣāṃ nāmāni me śṛṇu \|\| 49 \|\|*

There are also pleasant mountain ranges between each region, inhabited by gods and gandharvas. Listen to their names: 49

## \|\| 2.4.50 \|\|

*krauñcaś ca vāmanaścaiva tṛtīyaścāndhakārakaḥ \|*  
*caturtho ratnaśailaś ca svāhinī hayasannibhaḥ \|\| 50 \|\|*

Krauñca, Vāmana, Andhakāraka is the third, the fourth is jewel-bearing Svāhinī, which resembles a horse, Divāvṛt is the fifth, then comes Puṇḍarīkavant, 50

## \|\| 2.4.51 \|\|

*divāvṛtpañcamaścātra tathānyaḥ puṇḍarīkavān \|*  
*dundubhiś ca mahāśailo dviguṇāsta parasparam \|*  
*dvīpādvīpeṣu ye śailā yathā dvīpāni te tathā \|\| 51 \|\|*

And then the great mountain Dundubhi. Just as each land is twice as wide as the one before it, each of these mountains is twice as high as the preceding one. 51

## \|\| 2.4.52 \|\|

*varṣeṣveteṣu ramyeṣu tathā śailavareṣu ca \|*  
*nivasaṃti nirātaṅkāḥ saha devagaṇaiḥ prajāḥ \|\| 52 \|\|*

In all these pleasant regions, and in the ranges that divide them, dwell communities free from worry and attended by hosts of deities. 52

## \|\| 2.4.53 \|\|

*puṣkarāḥ puṣkalā dhanyāstitikhyāś ca mahāmune \|*  
*brahmaṇāḥ kṣatriyā vaiśyāḥ śudrāścānukramoditāḥ \|\| 53 \|\|*

Their Puṣkaras, Puṣkalas, Dhanyas and Tiṣyas, great sage, are like our brahmins, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras. 53

## \|\| 2.4.54 \|\|

*nadīrmaitreya te tatra yāḥ pibanti śṛṇuṣva tāḥ \|*  
*saptapradhānāḥ śataśas tatrānyāḥ kṣudranimnagāḥ \|\| 54 \|\|*

Now, Maitreya, you’ll hear about the seven major rivers from which they drink, but there are also smaller rivers by the hundred: 54

## \|\| 2.4.55 \|\|

*gaurī kumudvatī caiva sandhyā rātrirmanojavā \|*  
*kṣāntiś ca puṇḍarīkā ca saptaitā varṣanimnagāḥ \|\| 55 \|\|*

Gaurī, Kumudvatī, Saṃdhyā, Rātri, Manojavā, Khyāti and Puṇḍarīkā are the seven rivers of those regions. 55

## \|\| 2.4.56 \|\|

*tatrāpi viṣṇurbhagavānpuṣkārādyair jānārdanaḥ \|*  
*yāgau rudrasvarūpaś ca ijyate yajñasannidhau \|\| 56 \|\|*

Here, too, Lord Janārdana is worshipped by the Puṣkaras and other orders in the form of Rūdra, with offerings made in sacrifices. 56

## \|\| 2.4.57 \|\|

*krauñcadvīpaḥ samudreṇa dadhimaṇḍodakena ca \|*  
*āvṛtaḥ sarvataḥ krauñca dvīpatulyena mānataḥ \|\| 57 \|\|*

Krauñcadvīpa is ringed all round by a sea of whey as wide as the land itself. 57

## \|\| 2.4.58 \|\|

*dadhimaṇḍodakaścāpi śākadvīpena saṃvṛtaḥ \|*  
*krauñcadvīpasya vistārāddviguṇena mahāmune \|\| 58 \|\|*

This sea is surrounded by Śākadvīpa, sage, which is twice as wide as Krauñcadvīpa. 58

## \|\| 2.4.59 \|\|

*śākadvīpeśvarasyāpi bhavyasya mumahātmanaḥ \|*  
*saptaiva tanayāstaṣāṃ dadau varṣāṇi sapta saḥ \|\| 59 \|\|*

Bhavya, the overlord of Śākadvīpa, had seven sons, to whom he gave the regions of his land: 59

## \|\| 2.4.60 \|\|

*jaladaś ca kumāraś ca mukumāro marīcakaḥ \|*  
*kusumodaś ca maudākiḥ saptamaś ca mahādrumaḥ \|\| 60 \|\|*

Jalada, Kumāra, Sukumāra, Maṇīcaka, Kusumoda and Sumodāki, with Mahādruma as the seventh. 60

## \|\| 2.4.61 \|\|

*tatsaṃjñānyeva tatrāpi sapta varṣāṇyanukramāt \|*  
*tatrāpi parvatāḥ spata varṣavicchedakāriṇaḥ \|\| 61 \|\|*

The seven regions, all named after these seven sons, are divided by seven ranges: 61

## \|\| 2.4.62 \|\|

*pūrvastatrodayagirirjalādhārastathāparaḥ \|*  
*tathā raivatakaḥ śyāmastathaivāstagirirdvija \|*  
*āmbikeyas tathā ramyaḥ kesarī parvatottamaḥ \|\| 62 \|\|*

The first is Udayagiri, then Jaladhāra, Raivataka, Śyāma, Ambhogiri, Āmbikeya and delightful Kesarī, that excellent mountain, brahmin. 62

## \|\| 2.4.63 \|\|

*śākastatra mahāvṛkṣaḥ siddhagandharvasevitaḥ \|*  
*yatratyavātasaṃspārśādāhlādo jāyate paraḥ \|\| 63 \|\|*

On that continent is a giant śāka—teak—enjoyed by siddhas and gandharvas. The touch of the breeze that passes through its leaves yields excellent refreshment. 63

## \|\| 2.4.64 \|\|

*tatra puṇyā janapadāścāturvarṇyasamanvitāḥ \|*  
*nadyaścātra mahāpuṇyāḥ sarvapāpabhayāpahāḥ \|\| 64 \|\|*

There are pleasant settlements wherein dwell the four communities, and auspicious rivers that wash away all sins: 64

## \|\| 2.4.65 \|\|

*sukumārī kumārī ca nalinī dhenukā ca yā \|*  
*ikṣuś ca veṇukā caiva gabhastī saptamī tathā \|\| 65 \|\|*

Sukumārī, Kumārī, Nalinī, Veṇukā, Ikṣu and Dhenukā, with Gabhastī as the seventh. 65

## \|\| 2.4.66 \|\|

*anyāś ca śataśastatra kṣudranadyo mahāmune \|*  
*mahīdharās tathā santi śataśotha sahasraśaḥ \|\| 66 \|\|*

There are also tens of thousands of lesser streams, great sage, and myriad mountains. 66

## \|\| 2.4.67 \|\|

*tāḥ pibanti mudāyuktā jaladādiṣu ye sthitāḥ \|*  
*varṣaṣu te janapadāḥ svargādabhyetya medinīm \|\| 67 \|\|*

The residents of Jalada and other regions descend from heaven to the earth to drink from these very rivers and, having done so, are refreshed. 67

## \|\| 2.4.68 \|\|

*dharmahānirna teṣvasti na saṃgharṣaḥ parasparam \|*  
*maryādāvyutkramo nāpi teṣu deśeṣu saptasu \|\| 68 \|\|*

Virtue is never lacking in these seven lands, nor are the inhabitants jealous of one another, nor do they transgress the boundaries of propriety. 68

## \|\| 2.4.69 \|\|

*vaṅgāś ca māgadhāścaiva mānasā mandagās tathā \|*  
*vaṅgā brahmaṇabhūyiṣṭhā māgadhāḥ kṣatriyās tathā \|*  
*vaiśyāstu mānasāsteṣāṃ śūdrasteṣāṃ tu mandagāḥ \|\| 69 \|\|*

The Magas, Māgadhas, Mānasas and Mandagas are the four communities. The Magas are in general like our brahmins, the Māgadhas our kṣatriyas, the Mānasas our vaiśyas and the Mandagas śūdras. 69

## \|\| 2.4.70 \|\|

*śākadvīpe tu tair viṣṇuḥ sūryarūpadharo muneṃ \|*  
*yathoktaurijyate samyakkarmabhir niyatātmabhiḥ \|\| 70 \|\|*

In Śākadvīpa, the communities I mentioned worship Viṣṇu in the form of Sūrya the Sun, sage, with perfect rituals, while holding themselves in check. 70

## \|\| 2.4.71 \|\|

*śākadvīpastu maitreya kṣirodena samāvṛtaḥ \|*  
*śākadvīpapramāṇena valayeneva veṣṭitaḥ \|\| 71 \|\|*

Śākadvīpa is ringed by a sea of milk as wide as the land itself, Maitreya, as if by a bracelet. 71

## \|\| 2.4.72 \|\|

*kṣirābdhiḥ sarvato brahmanpuṣkarākhyena veṣṭitaḥ \|*  
*dvīpena śākadvīpāttu dvīguṇena samantataḥ \|\| 72 \|\|*

The sea of milk is girt by the land called Puṣkara, brahmin, which is twice as wide as Śākadvīpa. 72

## \|\| 2.4.73 \|\|

*puṣkare savanasyāpi mahāvīro 'bhavatsutaḥ \|*  
*dhātukiś ca tayostatra dve varṣe nāmacihnite \|*  
*mahāvīraṃ tathaivānyaddhātukīkhaṇḍasaṃjñitam \|\| 73 \|\|*

On Puṣkara, King Savana had but a pair of sons, Mahāvīra and Dhātika, and he named the regions of the continent after them: one Mahāvīra, the other Dhātakī. 73

## \|\| 2.4.74 \|\|

*ekaścatra mahābhāga prakhyāto varṣaparvataḥ \|*  
*mānāsottarasaṃjño vai madhyato valayākṛtiḥ \|\| 74 \|\|*

The land has a single range of mountains dividing the regions, known as Mānasottara, blessed sage, which rings the centre of the continent like a bangle. 74

## \|\| 2.4.75 \|\|

*yojanānāṃ sahasrāṇi ūrdhvaṃ pañcāśaducchritaḥ \|*  
*tāvadeva ca vistīrṇaḥ sarvataḥ parimaṇḍalaḥ \|\| 75 \|\|*

This circular range, fifty thousand leagues in height and fifty thousand wide, 75

## \|\| 2.4.76 \|\|

*puṣkaradvīpavalayaṃ madhyena vibhajanniva \|*  
*sthitosau tena vichinnaṃ jātaṃ tadvarṣakadvayam \|\| 76 \|\|*

Forms a ring in the middle of Puṣkara and divides the island down its centre, sage, being located in such a way that it separates the regions. 76

## \|\| 2.4.77 \|\|

*valayākāramekaikaṃ tayorvarṣaṃ tathā giriḥ \|\| 77 \|\|*

Thus, each region, as well as the range itself, is shaped like a bracelet. 77

## \|\| 2.4.78 \|\|

*daśavarṣasahasrāṇi tatra jīvanti mānavāḥ \|*  
*virāmayā viśokāś ca rāgadveṣādivarjitāḥ \|\| 78 \|\|*

This continent’s inhabitants live for ten millennia, free from sorrow and disease, knowing neither enmity nor passion. 78

## \|\| 2.4.79 \|\|

*adhamottamau na teṣvāstāṃ na vadhyavadhakau dvija \|*  
*nerṣyāsūyā bhayaṃ dveṣo doṣo lobhādiko na ca \|\| 79 \|\|*

They have no concept of worse or better, nor are there killers or killed, brahmin, nor is there jealousy, envy, anger, greed or malice. 79

## \|\| 2.4.80 \|\|

*mahāpītaṃbahirvarṣaṃ dhātakīkhaṇḍamantataḥ \|*  
*mānasottaraśaulasya devadaityādisevitam \|\| 80 \|\|*

The region of Mahāvīra lies beyond the Mānasottara range, while the Dhātakī region, inhabited by deities, Daityas and the rest, lies within it. 80

## \|\| 2.4.81 \|\|

*satyānṛtena tatrāstāṃ dvīpe puṣkarasaṃjñite \|*  
*na tatra nadyaḥ śailā vā dvīpe varṣadvayānvite \|\| 81 \|\|*

Ideas of right and wrong remain unknown in the land called Puṣkara, nor are there rivers or other ranges in this land of just two regions. 81

## \|\| 2.4.82 \|\|

*tulyapeṣāstu manujā devāstatraikarūpiṇaḥ \|\| 82 \|\|*

All its people resemble deities, 82

## \|\| 2.4.83 \|\|

*varṇāśramācārahīnaṃ dharmācaraṇavarjitam \|*  
*trayīvārtādaṇḍanītiśuśrūṣārahitañca yat \|\| 83 \|\|*

And need not follow the codes of practice otherwise prescribed for every order and stage of life, but are free from the need to practise virtue. The threefold Vedas, governance, trade and servitude are equally unknown. 83

## \|\| 2.4.84 \|\|

*varṣadvayaṃ tu maitreya bhaumaḥ svargoyamuttamaḥ \|*  
*sarvartusukhadaḥ kālo jarārogādivarjitaḥ \|*  
*dhātakīkhaṇḍasaṃjñe 'tha mahāvīre ca vai mune \|\| 84 \|\|*

These two regions are like the highest heaven on earth, Maitreya, and seasons are always pleasant, sage, as Puṣkara, Dhātakī and Mahāvīra are free from the woes of age and sickness. 84

## \|\| 2.4.85 \|\|

*nyagrodhaḥ puṣkaradvīpe brahmaṇaḥ sthānamuttamam \|*  
*tasminnavasati brahmā pūjyamānaḥ surāsuraiḥ \|\| 85 \|\|*

The banyan tree that grows on Puṣkara is the preferred abode of Brahmā. There he dwells while worshipped by gods and demigods alike. 85

## \|\| 2.4.86 \|\|

*svādūdakenodadhinā puṣkaraḥ pariveṣṭitaḥ \|*  
*samena puṣkarasyaiva vistārānmaṇḍalaṃ tathā \|\| 86 \|\|*

Puṣkara is ringed by a sea of fresh water as wide as the land itself. 86

## \|\| 2.4.87 \|\|

*evaṃ dvīpāḥ samudraiś ca saptasaptabhir āvṛtāḥ \|*  
*dvīpaścaiva samudraś ca samāno dviguṇau parau \|\| 87 \|\|*

Each of the seven continents is thus surrounded by an ocean, and each continent and ocean is twice as wide as the one that it encloses. 87

## \|\| 2.4.88 \|\|

*payāṃsi sarvadā sarvasamudreṣu samāni vai \|*  
*nyūnātiriktatā teṣāṃ kadācinnaiva jāyate \|\| 88 \|\|*

The volume of all these oceans is unchanging, and is never more or less, 88

## \|\| 2.4.89 \|\|

*sthālīsthamagnīsaṃyogādudrekisalile yatha \|*  
*tathenduvṛddhau salilamaṃbhodhau munisattama \|\| 89 \|\|*

But just as water in a cauldron seems to rise when it begins to seethe, best of sages, so, too, the waters of the oceans rise with the waxing of the moon. 89

## \|\| 2.4.90 \|\|

*anyūnānatiriktāś ca vardhantyāpo hrasaṃti ca \|*  
*udayāstamaneṣvindoḥ pakṣayoḥ śuklakṛṣṇayoḥ \|\| 90 \|\|*

The waters rise and fall without changing in their volume, in response to the rising and setting of the moon during the light and darker fortnights. 90

## \|\| 2.4.91 \|\|

*daśottarāṇi pañcaiva hyaṅgulānāṃ śatāni vai \|*  
*apāṃ vṛddhikṣaṇo dṛṣṭau sāmudrīṇāṃ mahāmune \|\| 91 \|\|*

A tidal range of five hundred and ten inches has been observed in these oceans, great sage. 91

## \|\| 2.4.92 \|\|

*bhojanaṃ puṣkaradvīpe tatra svayamupasthitam \|*  
*ṣaḍrasaṃ bhuñjate vipra prajāḥ sarvāḥ sadaiva hi \|\| 92 \|\|*

On the continent of Puṣkara, all inhabitants partake of food of sixfold flavours, brahmin, which arises of its own accord. 92

## \|\| 2.4.93 \|\|

*svādūdakasya parito dṛsyate lokasaṃsthitiḥ \|*  
*dviguṇā kāñcanī bhūmiḥ sarvajantuvivarjitā \|\| 93 \|\|*

Beyond the ocean of fresh water is the land at the end of the world. It’s twice as wide as the ocean within it, but this realm, made of gold, is quite bereft of life. 93

## \|\| 2.4.94 \|\|

*lokālokastataḥ śailo jojanāyutavistṛtaḥ \|*  
*ucchrāyeṇāpi tāvanti sahasrāṇyacalo hi saḥ \|\| 94 \|\|*

The Lokāloka mountain range is ten thousand leagues in length and ten thousand high. 94

## \|\| 2.4.95 \|\|

*tatastamaḥ samāvṛtya taṃ śaulaṃ sarvataḥ sthitam \|*  
*tamaścāṇḍakaṭāhena samantātpariveṣṭitam \|\| 95 \|\|*

The space beyond the range is cloaked in utter darkness, and beyond the darkness everything lies within the shell of the cosmic egg. 95

## \|\| 2.4.96 \|\|

*pañcaśatkoṭivistārā seyamurva mahāmune \|*  
*sahaivāṇḍakaṭāhena sadvīpābdhimahīdharā \|\| 96 \|\|*

This universe, including the outer shell, the lands, the seas and ranges, sage, is five hundred million leagues across. 96

## \|\| 2.4.97 \|\|

*seyaṃ dhātrī vidhātrī ca sarvabhūtaguṇādhikā \|*  
*ādhārabhū6tā sarveṣāṃ maitreya jagatāmiti \|\| 97 \|\|*

It’s the mother and the nursemaid of every host of beings, Maitreya, and foundation of all the worlds. 97

## Chapter 5 - The lower realms; The serpent Śeṣa

## \|\| 2.5.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*vistāra eṣa kathitaḥ pṛthivyā bhavato mayā \|*  
*saptatistu sahasrāṇi dvijocchrāyopi kathyate \|\| 1 \|\|*

Parāśara:

I’ve described the upper realms for you, brahmin, which rise for seventy thousand leagues, they say. 1

## \|\| 2.5.2 \|\|

*daśasāhasramekaikaṃ pātālaṃ munisattama \|*  
*atalaṃ vitalaṃ caiva nitalaṃ ca gabhastimat \|*  
*mahākhyaṃ sutalaṃ cāgryaṃ pātālaṃ cāpi saptamam \|\| 2 \|\|*

Each of the lower realms descends ten thousand leagues: Atala, Vitala, Nitala, Gabhastimat, Mahākhya and Sutala, best of sages, with the foremost one, Pātāla, as the seventh. 2

## \|\| 2.5.3 \|\|

*śuklākṛṣṇāruṇāḥ pītāḥ śarkarāḥ śailakāñcanāḥ \|*  
*bhūmayo yatra maitreya paragrāsādamaṇḍitāḥ \|\| 3 \|\|*

Those realms, whose surfaces are white, black, brown, yellow, sand, stone and gold, are beautified with gracious mansions. 3

## \|\| 2.5.4 \|\|

*teṣu dānavadaiteyā yakṣāś ca śataśas tathā \|*  
*nivasanti mahānāgajā tayaś ca mahāmune \|\| 4 \|\|*

Clans of Dānavas and Daityas dwell there in the hundreds, great sage, as well as tribes of mighty nāgas. 4

## \|\| 2.5.5 \|\|

*svarlokādapi ramyāṇi pātālānīti nāradaḥ \|*  
*prāha svargasadaṃ madhye pātālābhyāgato divi \|\| 5 \|\|*

Nārada, who had visited the lower realms, declared amid the gods’ assembly that the underworlds were even more magnificent than those above: 5

## \|\| 2.5.6 \|\|

*āhlādakāriṇaḥ śubhrā maṇayo yatra suprabhāḥ \|*  
*nāgābharaṇabhūṣāsu pātālaṃ kena tatsamam \|\| 6 \|\|*

‘What can compare with Pātāla, where the nāgas wear jewellery made with lovely gems that shine so brightly? 6

## \|\| 2.5.7 \|\|

*daityadānavakanyābhir itaścaitaś ca śobhite \|*  
*pātāle kasya na prītirvimuktasyāpi jāyate \|\| 7 \|\|*

Who wouldn’t delight in Pātāla, ornamented here and there by Dānava and Daitya girls? Even a liberated being would revel in it.’ 7

## \|\| 2.5.8 \|\|

*divārkaraśmayo yatra prabhāṃ tanvanti nātapam \|*  
*śaśiraśmir na śītāya niśi dyotāya kevalam \|\| 8 \|\|*

The rays of the sun shed light, not heat, by day, and at night the moon is bright yet isn’t cool. 8

## \|\| 2.5.9 \|\|

*bhakṣyabhojyamahāpānamuditair api bhogibhiḥ \|*  
*yatra na jñāyate kālo gatopi danujādibhiḥ \|\| 9 \|\|*

Danu’s offspring never notice time is passing, addicted as they are to entertainments, delighting there in food and drink and other pleasures. 9

## \|\| 2.5.10 \|\|

*vanāni nadyo ramyāṇi sarāṃsi kamalākarāḥ \|*  
*puṃskokilābhilāpāś ca manojñānyambarāṇi ca \|\| 10 \|\|*

There are forests, rivers, lovely lakes and lily-pools where the pleasant air resounds with calls of koel cocks. 10

## \|\| 2.5.11 \|\|

*bhūṣaṇānyatiśubhrāṇi gandhāḍhyaṃ cānulepanam \|*  
*vīṇāveṇumṛdaṅgānāṃ svanair apūritāni ca \|\| 11 \|\|*

There are brilliant decorations, rarest scents and lotions, brahmin, and the sounds of lute and pipe, clarion and tabour. 11

## \|\| 2.5.12 \|\|

*etānyanyāni codārabhagyabhogyāni tānavaiḥ \|*  
*daityoragaiś ca bhujyante pātālāntaragocaraiḥ \|\| 12 \|\|*

These and other noble pleasures are enjoyed by Dānavas, Daityas and nāgas who inhabit Pātāla. 12

## \|\| 2.5.13 \|\|

*pātālānāmadhaścāste viṣṇoryā tāmasī tanuḥ \|*  
*śeṣakhyā yadruṇānvaktuṃ na śaktā daityadānavāḥ \|\| 13 \|\|*

Below the lower realms lies the darker form of Viṣṇu known as Śeṣa, whose qualities even Dānavas and Daityas can’t describe. 13

## \|\| 2.5.14 \|\|

*yonantaḥ pāṭhyate siddhair daivo devarṣipūjitaḥ \|*  
*sa sahasraśirā vyaktasvastikāmalabhūṣaṇaḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

The siddhas call him Ananta (‘Endless’), and the gods and heavenly sages worship him. He has a thousand heads and bears the swastika, the purest transcendental mark. 14

## \|\| 2.5.15 \|\|

*phaṇāmaṇisahasreṇa yaḥ sa vidyotayandiśaḥ \|*  
*sarvānkaroti nirviryān hitāya jagato 'surān \|\| 15 \|\|*

On his hood myriad jewels shine forth in all directions as he robs the demons of their power, to benefit the world. 15

## \|\| 2.5.16 \|\|

*madāghūrṇitanetrosau yaḥ sadaivaikakumḍalaḥ \|*  
*kiriṭī sragdharo bhāti sāgniḥ śveta ivācalaḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

His eyes are always glowing with excitement, he sports a single earring, a diadem and a garland, and blazes like a hill of silver flames. 16

## \|\| 2.5.17 \|\|

*nīlavāsā madotsiktaḥ śvetahāropaśobhitaḥ \|*  
*sābhragaṅgāpravāhosau kailāsādririvāparaḥ \|\| 17 \|\|*

Clad in blue and wild with bliss, Śeṣa wears a pearly necklace and rears up like cloud-capped Kailāsa with the river Gaṅgā flowing down its flanks. 17

## \|\| 2.5.18 \|\|

*lāṅgalāsaktahastāgro bibhranmusalamutamam \|*  
*upāsyate svayaṃ kāntyā yo vāruṇyā ca mūrtayā \|\| 18 \|\|*

Wielding mace and ploughshare in his hands, he’s attended by the goddess Vāruṇī, who embodies his own splendour. 18

## \|\| 2.5.19 \|\|

*kalpānte yasya vaktrebhyo viṣānalaśikhojjvalaḥ \|*  
*saṃkarṣaṇātmako rūdro niṣkramyātti jagattrayam \|\| 19 \|\|*

At the end of every aeon, Rudra, who’s one with Saṃkarṣaṇa, ablaze with fire and venom, emerges from the mouths of Śeṣa to devour the threefold worlds. 19

## \|\| 2.5.20 \|\|

*sa bibhracchekharībhūtamaśeṣaṃ kṣitimaṇḍalam \|*  
*āste pātālamūlasthaḥ śeṣo 'śeṣasurārcitaḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

Wearing this whole universe as a crown upon his head, Śeṣa rests beneath Pātāla while all the deities worship him. 20

## \|\| 2.5.21 \|\|

*tasya vīryaṃ prabhāvaś ca svarūpaṃ rūpameva ca \|*  
*na hi varṇayituṃ śakyaṃ jñātuṃ ca tridaśair api \|\| 21 \|\|*

Even the thirteen gods themselves cannot describe or grasp his splendour, strength, form or nature. 21

## \|\| 2.5.22 \|\|

*yasyaiṣā sakalā pṛthvī phaṇāmaṇiśikhārūṇā \|*  
*āste kusumamāleva kastadvīryaṃ vadiṣyati \|\| 22 \|\|*

Who can speak of the might of one who wears the universe, glowing in the brilliance of his jewelled hood, as if it were a garland? 22

## \|\| 2.5.23 \|\|

*yadā vijṛmbhatenanto madāghūrṇitalocanaḥ \|*  
*tadā calati bhūreṣā sābdhitoyā sakānanā \|\| 23 \|\|*

When Ananta yawns and flashes his eyes with fury, the earth, mountains, waters and forests tremble. 23

## \|\| 2.5.24 \|\|

*gandharvāpsarasaḥ siddhāḥ kinnaroragacāraṇāḥ \|*  
*nāntaṃ guṇānāṃ gacchanti tenānantoyamavyayaḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

Gandharvas, apsarases, siddhas, kiṃnaras, nāgas and cāraṇas never reach the end of praising his qualities. That’s why he’s called imperishable endless one. 24

## \|\| 2.5.25 \|\|

*yasya nāgavadhūhastair laṃbitaṃ hari candanam \|*  
*muhuḥ śvāsānilāpāstaṃ yāti dikkṣodavāsatām \|\| 25 \|\|*

Sandal powder, ground at the hands of nāga women, is always carried by his breath and scents the air in all directions. 25

## \|\| 2.5.26 \|\|

*yamārādhya pūrāṇarṣirgargo jyotīṃṣi tattvataḥ \|*  
*jñātavānsakalaṃ caiva nimittapaṭhitaṃ phalam \|\| 26 \|\|*

Having worshipped Śeṣa, the ancient sage named Garga gained true knowledge of astrology and all the benefits to be gained from understanding omens. 26

## \|\| 2.5.27 \|\|

*teneyaṃ nāgavaryeṇa śirasā vidhṛtā mahī \|*  
*bibharti mālāṃ lokānāṃ sadevāsuramānuṣām \|\| 27 \|\|*

With his serpent strength, he bears the world upon his head, while wearing realms of gods and demigods and mortals like a garland. 27

## Chapter 6 - The hell realms

## \|\| 2.6.1 \|\|

*parāśara uvāca*  
*tataś ca narakā vipra bhuvodhaḥ salilasya ca \|*  
*pāpino yeṣu pātyante tāñchṛṇuṣvamahāmune \|\| 1 \|\|*

Now hear about the hell realms, brahmin, which lie beneath the earth and oceans, and into which the wicked fall, great sage. 1

## \|\| 2.6.2 \|\|

*rauravaḥ sūkaro rodhastālo viśasanas tathā \|*  
*mahājvālastaptakumbho lavaṇetha vilohitaḥ \|\| 2 \|\|*

Raurava, Sūkara, Rodha, Tāla, Viśasana, Mahājvāla, Taptakumbha, Lavaṇa, Vilohita, 2

## \|\| 2.6.3 \|\|

*rūdhirāmbho vaitaraṇiḥ krimiśaḥ krimibhojanaḥ \|*  
*asipatravanaṃ kṛṣṇo lālābhakṣaś ca dāruṇaḥ \|\| 3 \|\|*

Rudhirāmbhas, Vaitaraṇī, Kṛmiśa, Kṛmibhojana, Asipatravana, Kṛṣṇa, Lālābhakṣa, Dāruṇa, 3

## \|\| 2.6.4 \|\|

*tathā pūyavahaḥ pāpo vahnijvālo hyadhaḥ śirāḥ \|*  
*saṃdaṃśaḥ kālasūtraś ca tamaścāvīcireva ca \|\| 4 \|\|*

Pūyavaha, Pāpa, Vahnijvāla, Adhaḥśiras, Saṃdaṃśa, Kṛṣṇasūtra, Tamas, Avīci, 4

## \|\| 2.6.5 \|\|

*śvabhojanothāpratiṣṭhaścāpraciś ca tathā paraḥ \|*  
*ityevamādayaścānye narakā bhṛśadārūṇāḥ \|\| 5 \|\|*

Śvabhojana, Apratiṣṭha and another called Avīci—these are just the first few hells of dreadful suffering, but there are others. 5

## \|\| 2.6.6 \|\|

*yamasya viṣaye ghorāḥ śastrāgnibhayadāyinaḥ \|*  
*patanti yeṣu puruṣāḥ pāpakarmaratāstu ye \|\| 6 \|\|*

Those who delight in wickedness fall into Yama’s realm, filled with fearsome fires and weapons. 6

## \|\| 2.6.7 \|\|

*kūṭasākṣī tathāsamyakpakṣapātena yo vadet \|*  
*yaścānyadanṛtaṃ vakti sa naro yāti rauravam \|\| 7 \|\|*

A dishonest witness who lies because of partiality and one who utters any other falsehood goes to Raurava, ‘Fearful’. 7

## \|\| 2.6.8 \|\|

*bhrūṇahā purahantā ca gaughnaś ca munisattama \|*  
*yānti te narakaṃ rodhaṃ yaścocchvāsanirodhakaḥ \|\| 8 \|\|*

One who performs abortions, best of sages, or kills his guru or a cow goes to the hell of Rodha, ‘Suppression’, as does the strangler. 8

## \|\| 2.6.9 \|\|

*surāpo brahmahā hartā suvarṇasya ca sūkare \|*  
*prayānti narake yaś ca taiḥ saṃsargamupaiti vai \|\| 9 \|\|*

One who drinks liquor, kills a brahmin or steals another’s gold goes to Sūraka, ‘Swine’, along with his accomplices. 9

## \|\| 2.6.10 \|\|

*rājanyavaiśyahantā ca tathaiva gurutalpagaḥ \|*  
*taptakuṇḍesvasṛgāmī hanti rājabhaṭāṃś ca yaḥ \|\| 10 \|\|*

One who kills a kṣatriya or a vaiśya goes to Tāla, the ‘Palm Tree’, as does one who violates his guru’s marriage bed. One who molests his sister or kills a royal emissary goes to Taptakumbha, the ‘Heated Cauldron’. 10

## \|\| 2.6.11 \|\|

*sādhvīvikrayakṛdbandhapālaḥ kesarivikrayī \|*  
*taptalohe patanty ete yaś ca ca bhaktaṃ parityajet \|\| 11 \|\|*

Purveyors of virtuous women, prison guards and horsetraders fall into Taptaloha, ‘Red-Hot Iron’, as does one who forsakes his devotees. 11

## \|\| 2.6.12 \|\|

*snuṣāṃ sutāṃ cāpi gatvā mahājvāle nipātyate \|*  
*avamantā gurūṇāṃ yo yaścākroṣṭā narādhamaḥ \|\| 12 \|\|*

A man who violates his daughter or daughter-in-law is cast into Mahājvāla, the ‘Great Inferno’, as is the lowly wretch who disrespects or scorns his gurus. 12

## \|\| 2.6.13 \|\|

*vedadūṣayitāyaś ca vedavikrayakaś ca yaḥ \|*  
*agamyagāmī yaś ca syātte yānti lavaṇaṃ dvija \|\| 13 \|\|*

One who reviles or sells the Vedas, brahmin, or has improper sexual contacts goes to Lavaṇa, the ‘Salty Hell’. 13

## \|\| 2.6.14 \|\|

*coro vilohe patati maryādādūṣakas tathā \|\| 14 \|\|*

The thief and he who crosses moral boundaries fall into Vimoha, ‘Confusion’. 14

## \|\| 2.6.15 \|\|

*devadvijapitṛdveṣṭā ratnadvaṣayitā ca yaḥ \|*  
*sa yāti krimibhakṣe vai krimiśe ca duriṣṭakṛt \|\| 15 \|\|*

One who despises deities, brahmins or his father and one who damages any gemstone go to Kṛmibhakṣa, ‘Worms-for-Food’. The practitioner of black magic goes to Kṛmiśa, ‘Insect Hell’. 15

## \|\| 2.6.16 \|\|

*pitṛdevātithīṃs tyaktvā paryaśnāti narādhamaḥ \|*  
*lālābhakṣe sa yāty ugre śarakartā ca vedhake \|\| 16 \|\|*

The wretch who eats before his father, deities or guests goes to frightful Lālābhakṣa, ‘Saliva-for-Food’, while arrow-makers go to Vedhaka, ‘Piercing’. 16

## \|\| 2.6.17 \|\|

*karoti karṇano yaś ca yaś ca khaṅgādikṛnnaraḥ \|*  
*prayāntyete viśasane narake bhṛśadaruṇe \|\| 17 \|\|*

Those who manufacture missiles with barbs, swords or other weapons fall into the terrible hell of Viśasana, ‘Vivisection’. 17

## \|\| 2.6.18 \|\|

*asatpratigṛhītā tu narake yātyadhomukhe \|*  
*ayājyayājakaścaiva tathā nakṣatrasūcakaḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

One who accepts improper gifts goes to Adhomukha, ‘Upside-Down’, as do those who sacrifice to unworthy beings or point at constellations. 18

## \|\| 2.6.19 \|\|

*vegī pūyavahe caiko yāti maṣṭānnabhuṅgaraḥ \|\| 19 \|\|*

Headstrong men and those who gorge on delicacies alone end up in Pūyavaha, ‘River of Pus’. 19

## \|\| 2.6.20 \|\|

*lākṣāmāsarasānāṃ ca tilānāṃ lavaṇasya ca \|*  
*vikretā brahmaṇo yāti tameva narakaṃ dvija \|\| 20 \|\|*

A brahmin selling cochineal, or meat, or wine, or sesame or salt goes to that same hell realm, brahmin. 20

## \|\| 2.6.21 \|\|

*mārjārakukkuṭacchāga śvavarāhavihaṅgamān \|*  
*poṣayannarakaṃ yāti tameva dvijasattama \|\| 21 \|\|*

Those who feed cats, chickens, goats, horses, pigs or birds, best of brahmins, also go to the ‘River of Pus’. 21

## \|\| 2.6.22 \|\|

*raṅgopajīvī kaivartaḥ kumḍāśī garadas tathā \|*  
*sūcī māhiṣakaścaiva parvakārī ca yo dvijaḥ \|\| 22 \|\|*

Actors, fishermen, those who marry bastards, poisoners, informers, men who prostitute their wives, brahmins who have sex on days of abstinence, 22

## \|\| 2.6.23 \|\|

*agāradāhī mitraghnaḥ śākunirgramayājakaḥ \|*  
*rudhirāndhe patantyete somaṃ vikrīṇate ca ye \|\| 23 \|\|*

Arsonists, those who murder friends, fortune tellers and those who sacrifice for rustics fall into Rudhirāndha, the ‘Well of Blood’, as do those purveying soma. 23

## \|\| 2.6.24 \|\|

*makhahā grāmahantā ca yāti vaitaraṇīṃ naraḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

All those who destroy a beehive or a village go to Vaitaraṇī, ‘Hard-to-Cross’. 24

## \|\| 2.6.25 \|\|

*retaḥ pātādikartāro maryādābhedino hi ye \|*  
*te kṛṣṇe yāntyaśaucāś ca kuhakājīvinaś ca ye \|\| 25 \|\|*

Trespassers and those obsessed by wealth or youth go to Kṛṣṇa, the ‘Black Hell’, as do debauchees and liars. 25

## \|\| 2.6.26 \|\|

*asipatravanaṃ yāti vanacchedī vṛthaiva yaḥ \|*  
*aurabhriko mṛgavyādho vahnijvāle patanti vai \|\| 26 \|\|*

Those who cut down trees for no good purpose go to Asipatravana, the ‘Sword-Leaf Forest’, while shepherds and deer-hunters fall into Vahnijvāla, the ‘Blazing Fire’. 26

## \|\| 2.6.27 \|\|

*yāntyete dvija tatraiva ye cāpākeṣu vahnidāḥ \|\| 27 \|\|*

The potter who fires a kiln, brahmin, goes to that same end. 27

## \|\| 2.6.28 \|\|

*vratānāṃ lopako yaścasvāśramādvicyutaś ca yaḥ \|*  
*sandaṃśayātanāmadhye patatastāvubhāvapi \|\| 28 \|\|*

One who breaks his vows descends to torment in Saṃdaṃśa, the ‘Hell of Pincers’, as does one who shirks the duties of his stage of life. 28

## \|\| 2.6.29 \|\|

*divā svapne ca skandante ye narā brahmacāriṇaḥ \|*  
*putrair adhyāpitā ye ca te patanti śvabhojane \|\| 29 \|\|*

Students of religion who ejaculate while napping and those who learn the scriptures from their children fall into Śvabhojana, ‘Dogs-for-Food’. 29

## \|\| 2.6.30 \|\|

*ete cānye ca narakāḥ śataśotha sahasraśaḥ \|*  
*yeṣu duṣkṛtakarmāṇaḥ pacyante yātanāgatāḥ \|\| 30 \|\|*

These and others are among the hundreds and thousands of hell realms where evildoers are cooked and otherwise tormented. 30

## \|\| 2.6.31 \|\|

*yathaiva pāpānyetāni tathānyāni sahasraśaḥ \|*  
*bhujyante tāni puruṣair narakāntaragocaraiḥ \|\| 31 \|\|*

Just as these and other misdeeds number in the myriad, there are myriad hell realms to be suffered by the guilty. 31

## \|\| 2.6.32 \|\|

*varṇāśramaviruddhaṃ ca karma kurvanti ye narāḥ \|*  
*karmaṇā manasā vācā nirayeṣu patanti te \|\| 32 \|\|*

Those who perform actions contrary to the duties of their order or their stage of life, whether in thought or word or deed, all go to hell. 32

## \|\| 2.6.33 \|\|

*adhaḥ śirobhidṛśyante nārakair divi devatāḥ \|*  
*devāścādhomukhānsarvānadhaḥ paśyanti nārakān \|\| 33 \|\|*

Those condemned to hell look up at gods in heaven, then hang their heads, while gods look down on all those suffering beings. 33

## \|\| 2.6.34 \|\|

*sthāvarāḥ krimayobjāś ca pakṣiṇaḥ paśavo narāḥ \|*  
*dhārmikāstridaśāstaddhanmokṣiṇaś ca yathākramam \|\| 34 \|\|*

Immobile things, insects, fish, birds, animals, humans, sages, the thirteen great deities and liberated beings— 34

## \|\| 2.6.35 \|\|

*sahasrabhāgaprathamā dvitīyānukramās tathā \|*  
*sarve hyete mahābhāgā yāvanmuktisamāśrayāḥ \|\| 35 \|\|*

Each successive stage is a thousand times as good as the one before, fortunate sage. All beings must pass through these until attaining liberation. 35

## \|\| 2.6.36 \|\|

*yāvanto jantavaḥ svarge tāvanto narakaukasaḥ \|*  
*pāpakṛdyāti narakaṃ prāyāścittaparāṅmukhaḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

There are as many beings in heaven as there are in hell, and the evildoer who neglects atonement will surely finish there. 36

## \|\| 2.6.37 \|\|

*pāpānāmanurūpāṇi prāyaścittāni yadyathā \|*  
*tathā tathaiva saṃsmṛtya proktāni paramarṣibhiḥ \|\| 37 \|\|*

After consideration, the greatest sages pronounced atonements fit for every sin. 37

## \|\| 2.6.38 \|\|

*pāpe gurūṇi guruṇi svalpānyalpe ca tadvidaḥ \|*  
*prāyaścittāni maitreya jaguḥ svāyaṃbhuvādayaḥ \|\| 38 \|\|*

Svāyaṃbhava and other experts in such matters proclaimed dire punishments for major crimes and lesser ones for minor sins. 38

## \|\| 2.6.39 \|\|

*prāyaścittānyaśeṣāṇi tapaḥkarmatmakāni vai \|*  
*yāni teṣāṇaśeṣāṇāṃ kṛṣṇānusmaraṇamparam \|\| 39 \|\|*

But setting one’s heart on Kṛṣṇa is superior to every other penitence, including austerity and sacrifice. 39

## \|\| 2.6.40 \|\|

*kṛte pāpe 'nutāpo vai yasya puṃsaḥ prajāyate \|*  
*prāyaścittaṃ tu tasyaikaṃ harisaṃsmaraṇaṃ param \|\| 40 \|\|*

For one who rues a deed he’s done, remembering Hari is alone the best atonement. 40

## \|\| 2.6.41 \|\|

*sanskrit not found \|\| 41 \|\|*

One who sets his heart on Nārāyaṇa morning, noon and night, and at dawn and dusk, is at once absolved of all his sins. 41

## \|\| 2.6.42 \|\|

*prātarniśi tathā sandhyāmadhyāhnādiṣu saṃsmaran \|*  
*nārāyaṇamavāpnoti sadyaḥ pāpakṣayannaraḥ \|*  
*viṣṇusaṃsmaraṇātkṣīṇasamastakleśasañcayaḥ \|*  
*muktiṃ prayāti svargāptistasya vighnonumīyate \|\| 42 \|\|*

Recalling Viṣṇu, all accumulated sins are shed and one reaches liberation. For such a person, even heaven is accounted an impediment. 42

## \|\| 2.6.43 \|\|

*vāsudeve mano yasyajapahomārcanādiṣu \|*  
*tasyāntarāyo maitreya devendratvādikaṃ phalam \|\| 43 \|\|*

For one whose heart is set on Vāsudeva while praying, sacrificing or worshipping, Maitreya, even rewards including sovereignty over gods become an obstacle. 43

## \|\| 2.6.44 \|\|

*kva nākapṛṣṭhagamanaṃ punarāvṛttilakṣaṇam \|*  
*kva japo vāsudeveti muktibījamanuttamam \|\| 44 \|\|*

‘On one hand is ascent to highest heaven whence one must fall to earth again; on the other, one may pray to Vāsudeva’—such is the excellent seed that leads to liberation. 44

## \|\| 2.6.45 \|\|

*tasmādaharniśaṃ viṣṇu saṃsmaranpuruṣo mune \|*  
*na yāti narakaṃ martyaḥ saṃkṣīṇākhilapātakaḥ \|\| 45 \|\|*

Hence, a person recalling Viṣṇu day and night avoids the realms of hell, sage, and is pure, as all his sins are swept away. 45

## \|\| 2.6.46 \|\|

*manaḥ prītikaraḥ svargo narakastadviparyayaḥ \|*  
*narakasvargasaṃjñe vai pāpapuṇye dvijottama \|\| 46 \|\|*

Heaven is that which delights the mind, and hell its opposite, best of brahmins, and deeds both good and bad are simply forms of those two realms. 46

## \|\| 2.6.47 \|\|

*vastvekameva duḥkhāya sukhāyeṣryāgamāya ca \|*  
*kopāya ca yatastasmādvastu vastvātmakaṃ kutaḥ \|\| 47 \|\|*

Because a single thing may cause both pain and pleasure, rage and envy, where is its essential nature? 47

## \|\| 2.6.48 \|\|

*tadeva prītaye bhūtvā punarduḥkhā ya jāyate \|*  
*tadeva kopāya yataḥ prasādāya ca jāyate \|\| 48 \|\|*

That which renders pleasure now gives rise to future pain. That which makes us angry now may later bring us peace. 48

## \|\| 2.6.49 \|\|

*tasmādduḥkhātmakaṃ nāsti na ca kiñcitsukhātmakam \|*  
*manasaḥ parimāmoyaṃ sukhaduḥkhādilakṣamaḥ \|\| 49 \|\|*

Nothing is in essence painful or enjoyable; only responses in our minds are marked by such sensations. 49

## \|\| 2.6.50 \|\|

*jñānameva paraṃ brahma jñānaṃ bandhāya ceṣyate \|*  
*jñānatmakamidaṃ viśvaṃ na jñānādvidyate param \|\| 50 \|\|*

Knowledge alone is the highest Absolute, but knowledge also binds us. This whole world consists of that, to which nothing is superior. 50

## \|\| 2.6.51 \|\|

*vidyāvidyeti maitreya jñānamevopadhāraya \|\| 51 \|\|*

Understand that knowing and not knowing are both aspects of knowledge, Maitreya. 51

## \|\| 2.6.52 \|\|

*evametanmayākhyātaṃ bhavato maṇḍalaṃ bhuvaḥ \|*  
*pātālāni ca sarvāṇi tathāva narakā dvija \|\| 52 \|\|*

I’ve thus described the universe for you, brahmin, with its lower realms and hells. 52

## \|\| 2.6.53 \|\|

*samudrāḥ parvatāścaiva dvīpā varṣāṇi nimnagāḥ \|*  
*saṃkṣepātsarvamākhyātaṃ kiṃ bhūyaḥ śrotumicchasi \|\| 53 \|\|*

The oceans, mountains, regions, lands and rivers have all been described in brief. What would you like to hear of next? 53

## Chapter 7 - The upper spheres; The power of Viṣṇu

## \|\| 2.7.1 \|\|

*maitreya uvāca*  
*kathitaṃ bhūtalaṃ brahmanbhamaitadakhilaṃ tvayā \|*  
*bhuvarlokādikāṃl lokāñ chrotumicchāmyahaṃ mune \|\| 1 \|\|*

Maitreya:

You have indeed described all this to me, brahmin, but now I wish to hear about Bhuvarloka, sage, and the other upper realms. 1

## \|\| 2.7.2 \|\|

*tathaiva grahasaṃsthānaṃ pramāṇāni yathā tathā \|*  
*samācakṣva mahābhāga tanmahyaṃ paripṛcchate \|\| 2 \|\|*

I’d also like to hear in detail about the nature of the planets and their dimensions, blessed seer. Kindly answer these questions of mine. 2

## \|\| 2.7.3 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*ravicandramasoryāvanmayūkhair avabhāsyate \|*  
*sasamudrasaricchailā tāvatī pṛthivī smṛtā \|\| 3 \|\|*

The region touched by rays of sun and moon is the earthly realm, with its rivers, seas and mountains. 3

## \|\| 2.7.4 \|\|

*yāvatpramāṇā pṛthivī vistāraparimaṇḍalāt \|*  
*nabhastāvanpramāṇaṃ vai vyāsamaṇḍalato dvija \|\| 4 \|\|*

The realm of the sky is the same shape and size as that of the earth, brahmin. 4

## \|\| 2.7.5 \|\|

*bhūmeryo janalakṣe tu sauraṃ maitreya maṇḍalam \|*  
*lakṣāddivākarasyāpi maṇḍalaṃ śaśinaḥ sthitam \|\| 5 \|\|*

The sun is a hundred thousand leagues above the earth, Maitreya, and the moon is an equal distance above the sun. 5

## \|\| 2.7.6 \|\|

*pūrṇo śatasahasre tu yojanānāṃ niśākarāt \|*  
*nakṣatramaṇḍalaṃ kṛtstramupariṣṭātprakāśate \|\| 6 \|\|*

All the lunar mansions lie a hundred thousand leagues beyond the moon. 6

## \|\| 2.7.7 \|\|

*dve lakṣe cottare brahman budho nakṣatramaṇḍalāt \|*  
*tāvatpramāṇabhāge tu budhasyāpyuśanāḥ sthitaḥ \|\| 7 \|\|*

Prudent Mercury lies two hundred thousand leagues above the lunar mansions, brahmin, and hasty Venus is that same distance beyond Mercury. 7

## \|\| 2.7.8 \|\|

*aṅgarakopi śukrasya tatpramāṇe vyavasthitaḥ \|*  
*lakṣadvaye tu bhaumasya sthito devapurohitaḥ \|\| 8 \|\|*

Glowering Mars lies just as far beyond Venus, and Jupiter, the deities’ own ministrant, is two hundred thousand leagues beyond that planet. 8

## \|\| 2.7.9 \|\|

*saurirbṛhaspateścordhvaṃ dvilakṣe samavasthitaḥ \|*  
*saptarṣimaṇḍalaṃ tasmāllakṣamekaṃ dvijottamā \|\| 9 \|\|*

Saturn, the Sun’s own son, is two hundred thousand leagues beyond Jupiter, and the Seven Sages are another hundred thousand beyond that. 9

## \|\| 2.7.10 \|\|

*ṛṣibhyastu sahasrāṇāṃ śatādūrdhvaṃ vyavasthitaḥ \|*  
*meḍhībhūtaḥ samastasya jyotiścakrasya vai dhruvaḥ \|\| 10 \|\|*

One hundred thousand leagues above the Seven Sages is Dhruva, the Pole Star, the hub around which the wheel of heavenly bodies turns. 10

## \|\| 2.7.11 \|\|

*trailokyametatkathitamutsedhena mahāmune \|*  
*ijyāphalasya bhūreṣā ijyā cātra pratiṣṭhitā \|\| 11 \|\|*

I’ve described the threefold spheres in terms of elevation, sage. This earthly sphere is the Bhūrloka, where rewards of sacrifice are won, as this is where the rituals are performed. 11

## \|\| 2.7.12 \|\|

*dhruvādūrdhvaṃ maharloko yatra te kalpavāsināḥ \|*  
*ekayojanakoṭistu yatra te kalpavāsinaḥ \|\| 12 \|\|*

Ten million leagues above Dhruva is Maharloka, where sages live for an aeon—that’s why it’s called ‘Unmoving’. 12

## \|\| 2.7.13 \|\|

*dve koṭī tu jano loko yatra te brahmaṇaḥ sutāḥ \|*  
*sanandanādyāḥ prathitā maitreyāmalacetasaḥ \|\| 13 \|\|*

Twenty million leagues higher is Janaloka, Maitreya, where Sanandana and Brahmā’s other pure-minded sons are said to live. 13

## \|\| 2.7.14 \|\|

*caturgaṇottare cordhvaṃ janalokāttapaḥ sthitaḥ \|*  
*vairājā yatra te devāḥ sthitā dāhavivarjitāḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

Four times higher than Janaloka is Tapoloka, the sphere of austerities, inhabited by the Vairāja gods, whom fire cannot burn. 14

## \|\| 2.7.15 \|\|

*ṣaḍguṇenatapolokānsatyaloko virājate \|*  
*apunarmārakā yatra brahmaloko hi saṃsmṛtaḥ \|\| 15 \|\|*

Six times higher than Tapoloka is Satyaloka, the sphere of truth, whose denizens never die again. This is thought to be the realm of Brahmā. 15

## \|\| 2.7.16 \|\|

*pādagamyantu yatkiñcidvastvasti pṛthivīmayam \|*  
*sa bhūrlokaḥ samākhyāto vistaro 'sya mayoditaḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

Anywhere that can be reached on foot is defined as earth, or Bhūrloka, whose dimensions I described before. 16

## \|\| 2.7.17 \|\|

*bhūmimūryāntaraṃ yacca siddhādimunisevitam \|*  
*bhuvarlokastu so 'py ukto dvitīyo munisattama \|\| 17 \|\|*

The region between the earth and sun, inhabited by siddhas and other sages, is the Bhuvarloka, the second sphere that I described, best of sages. 17

## \|\| 2.7.18 \|\|

*dhruvasūryāntaraṃ yacca niyutāni caturdaśa \|*  
*svarlokaḥ so 'pi gadito lokasaṃsthānacintakaiḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

The space between the sun and Dhruva, fourteen hundred thousand leagues across, is called Svarloka by those who contemplate these realms. 18

## \|\| 2.7.19 \|\|

*trailokyamatatkṛtakaṃ maitreya paripaṭhyate \|*  
*janastapas tathā satyamiti cākṛtakaṃ trayam \|\| 19 \|\|*

These three spheres are transitory, Maitreya, but the outer ones of Janaloka, Tapaloka and Satyaloka are enduring. 19

## \|\| 2.7.20 \|\|

*kṛtakākṛtayormadhye maharloka iti smṛtaḥ \|*  
*śunyo bhavati kalpānte yotyantaṃ na vinaśyati \|\| 20 \|\|*

Between the transitory and enduring spheres lies Maharloka. At the end of every aeon it’s vacated, but not entirely destroyed. 20

## \|\| 2.7.21 \|\|

*ete sapta mayā lokā maitreya kathitāstava \|*  
*pātālāni ca saptaiva brahmāṇḍasyaiṣa vistaraḥ \|\| 21 \|\|*

I’ve told you about these seven spheres, Maitreya, the seven lower realms and the dimensions of the universe. 21

## \|\| 2.7.22 \|\|

*etadaṇḍakaṭāhena tiryak codhvamadhas tathā \|*  
*kapitthasya yathā bījaṃ sarvato vai samāvṛtam \|\| 22 \|\|*

These are enclosed above, below and on all sides by the shell of the cosmic egg, just as the wood-apple’s outer rind conceals the seed within. 22

## \|\| 2.7.23 \|\|

*daśottareṇa payasā maitreyāṇḍaṃ ca tadvṛtam \|*  
*sarvombuparidhānosau vahninā veṣṭito bahiḥ \|\| 23 \|\|*

The cosmic egg is surrounded by water ten times as wide as the egg itself, and the water is ringed by fire. 23

## \|\| 2.7.24 \|\|

*vahniś ca vāyunā vāyurmaitreya nabhasā vṛtaḥ \|*  
*bhūtādinā nabhaḥ so 'pi mahatā pariveṣṭitaḥ \|*  
*deśottarāmyaśeṣāṇi maitreyaitāni sapta vai \|\| 24 \|\|*

The fire is surrounded by wind, Maitreya, and the wind by space. Space is surrounded by the fundamental elements, and they are enclosed within mahat, the elemental principle of Greatness. Each of these seven layers is ten times larger than the one within, Maitreya. 24

## \|\| 2.7.25 \|\|

*mahāntaṃ ca samāvṛtya pradhānaṃ samavasthitam \|*  
*anantasya na tasyāntaḥ saṃkhyānaṃ cāpi vidyate \|\| 25 \|\|*

Mahat is enclosed within primal Matter, which is infinite, endless and immeasurable. 25

## \|\| 2.7.26 \|\|

*tadanantam asaṃkhyāta-pramāṇaṃ cāpi vai yataḥ \|*  
*hetubhūtamaśeṣasya prakṛtiḥ sā parā mune \|\| 26 \|\|*

Because it’s thus, it’s the underlying cause of all phenomena. This, sage, is supreme prakṛti, primal Substance. 26

## \|\| 2.7.27 \|\|

*aṇḍānāṃ tu sahasrāṇāṃ sahasrāṇyayutāni ca \|*  
*īdṛśānāṃ tathā tatra koṭikoṭiśatāni ca \|\| 27 \|\|*

There are millions and tens of millions and quadrillions of cosmic eggs identical to this one. 27

## \|\| 2.7.28 \|\|

*hāruṇyagniryathā tailaṃ tile tadvatpumānapi \|*  
*pradhāne 'vasthito vyāpī cetanātmātmavedanaḥ \|\| 28 \|\|*

Just as fire is latent in wood, and oil is latent in a sesame seed, so, too, all-pervasive puṃs, elemental Spirit, is inherent in Matter in the form of self-manifesting consciousness. 28

## \|\| 2.7.29 \|\|

*pradhānaṃ ca pumāṃścaiva sarvabhūtātmabhūtayā \|*  
*viṣṇuśaktyā mahābuddhe vṛtau saṃśrayadharmiṇau \|\| 29 \|\|*

Both Matter and Spirit are mutually dependent, wise sage, and are enveloped by the power of Viṣṇu, which is at the heart of all creation. 29

## \|\| 2.7.30 \|\|

*tayoḥ saiva pṛthagbhāvakāraṇaṃ saṃśrayasya ca \|*  
*kṣobhakāraṇabhūtā ca sargakāle mahāmate \|\| 30 \|\|*

That power is the cause of their separation and their interdependence, sage. It’s also the cause of their initial stirring at the moment of creation. 30

## \|\| 2.7.31 \|\|

*yathā saktaṃ jale vāto bibharti kaṇikāśatam \|*  
*śaktiḥ sāpi tathā viṣṇoḥ pradhānapuruṣātmakam \|\| 31 \|\|*

Just as wind raises ripples by the hundred on the surface of the water, in that same way the power of Viṣṇu agitates both Matter and Spirit. 31

## \|\| 2.7.32 \|\|

*yathā ca pādayaur mūlaskandhaśākhādisaṃyutaḥ \|*  
*ādibījātprabhavati bījānyanyāni vai tataḥ \|\| 32 \|\|*

A tree, consisting of roots, trunk, branches and so on, grows from a seed in the beginning, which releases other seeds. 32

## \|\| 2.7.33 \|\|

*prabhāvantitatastebhyaḥ sambhavantyapare drumāḥ \|*  
*tepi tallakṣaṇadravyakāraṇānugatā mune \|\| 33 \|\|*

These grow into further trees, which share its character, nature and ultimate origin, sage. 33

## \|\| 2.7.34 \|\|

*evamavyākṛtātpūrvaṃ jāyante mahadādayaḥ \|*  
*viśaṃṣāntā statastebhyaḥ saṃbhavantyasurādayaḥ \|*  
*tebhyaś ca putrāsteṣāṃ ca putrāṇāmapare sutāḥ \|\| 34 \|\|*

In the same way, mahat and the other elemental phenomena arise in the beginning from this Unseen principle. These phenomena then give rise to gods and other beings, who have sons, and those sons have offspring of their own. 34

## \|\| 2.7.35 \|\|

*bījādvṛkṣapraroheṇa yathā nāpacayastaroḥ \|*  
*bhūtānāṃ bhūtasargeṇa naivāstyapacayas tathā \|\| 35 \|\|*

Just as the growth of a new tree from a seed does not diminish the parent plant, in the same way, the creation of beings has no effect on the primal elements. 35

## \|\| 2.7.36 \|\|

*sannidhānādyathākāśakālādyāḥ kāraṇaṃ taroḥ \|*  
*tathaivāpariṇāmeva viśvasya bhagavānhariḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

As a combination of space, time and other factors influences a tree’s growth, Lord Hari, though himself unchanging, is the cause of everything. 36

## \|\| 2.7.37 \|\|

*vrīhibīje yathā mūlaṃ nālaṃ patrāṅkurau tathā \|*  
*kāṇḍakoṣastu puṣpaṃ ca kṣīraṃ tadvacca taṇjulāḥ \|\| 37 \|\|*

The root, stalk, leaf, shoot, stem, bud, flower, sap, ear, 37

## \|\| 2.7.38 \|\|

*tuṣāḥ kaṇāś ca santo vai yāntyāvirbhāvamātmanaḥ \|*  
*prarohahetusāmagryamāsādya munisattama \|\| 38 \|\|*

Chaff and further grains are all latent in a single grain of rice, best of sages, and appear when the conditions for growth arise. 38

## \|\| 2.7.39 \|\|

*tathā karmasvanekeṣu devādyāḥ samavasthitāḥ \|*  
*viṣṇuśaktiṃ samāsādya prarohamupayānti vai \|\| 39 \|\|*

Similarly, the gods and other beings, engaged in their own activities, arise when they encounter Viṣṇu’s energy. 39

## \|\| 2.7.40 \|\|

*sa ca viṣṇuḥ paraṃ brahma yataḥ sarvamidaṃ jagat \|*  
*jagacca yo yatra cedaṃ yasmiṃś ca layameṣyati \|\| 40 \|\|*

Viṣṇu is the supreme Absolute from whom all this arises. The world abides in him and is absorbed in him. 40

## \|\| 2.7.41 \|\|

*tadbrahma tatparaṃ dhāma sadasatparamaṃ padam \|*  
*yasya sarvamabhedena yataścaitaccarācaram \|\| 41 \|\|*

The Absolute is the supreme abode of Viṣṇu, a state beyond existence and nonexistence. Viṣṇu is one with this whole world of moving and unmoving things. 41

## \|\| 2.7.42 \|\|

*sa eva mūla prakṛtirvyaktarūpī jagacca saḥ \|*  
*tasminneva layaṃ sarvaṃ yāti tatra ca tiṣṭhati \|\| 42 \|\|*

He is the manifest form of both the root and the nature of the world. He is the world. In him the world concludes, on him the world relies. 42

## \|\| 2.7.43 \|\|

*kattā kriyāṇāṃ sa ca ijyate kratuḥ sa evatatkarmaphalaṃ ca tasya \|*  
*strugādiyatsādhanamapyaśeṣaṃ harerna kiñcivdyatiriktamasti \|\| 43 \|\|*

He performs the sacrifice. He is the sacrifice that’s offered. He is the sacrificial outcome. He is the ladle and utensils with which it’s carried out. Nothing is apart from Hari! 43

## Chapter 8 - The movement of the sun; Time; The heavenly paths

## \|\| 2.8.1 \|\|

*prāśara uvāca*  
*vyākhyātamaitadbrahmaṇḍasaṃsthānaṃ tava suvrata \|*  
*tataḥ pramāṇasaṃsthāne sūryādīnāṃ śṛṇuṣva mai \|\| 1 \|\|*

Parāśara:

I’ve described the nature of the cosmic egg to you, sage of rigid vows. Now hear about the size and nature of the sun and other bodies. 1

## \|\| 2.8.2 \|\|

*yojanānāṃ sahasrāṇi bhāskarasya ratho nava \|*  
*īṣādaṇḍastathaivāsya dviguṇo munisattama \|\| 2 \|\|*

The carriage of the sun is nine thousand leagues in length, best of sages, and its shaft is twice as long again. 2

## \|\| 2.8.3 \|\|

*sārdhakoṭis tathā sapta niyutānyadhikāni vai \|*  
*yojanānāṃ tu tasyākṣastatra cakraṃ pratiṣṭhitam \|\| 3 \|\|*

The axle is more than fifteen million and seven hundred thousand leagues in length, to which a wheel is fixed. 3

## \|\| 2.8.4 \|\|

*trinābhimati pañcāre ṣaṇṇeminyatrayātmake \|*  
*saṃvatsaramaye kṛtstraṃ kālacakraṃ pratiṣṭhitam \|\| 4 \|\|*

This eternal wheel, with three hubs, six fellies and five spokes, forms a year, and on it rests the entire cycle of time. 4

## \|\| 2.8.5 \|\|

*hayāś ca saptacchadāṃsi teṣāṃ nāmāni me śṛṇu \|*  
*gāyatrī ca bṛhatyuṣṇigjagatī triṣṭubheva ca \|*  
*anuṣṭuppaṅktirityuktā chandāṃsi harayo raveḥ \|\| 5 \|\|*

The carriage’s second axle is forty-five thousand and five hundred leagues in length, wise sage. 5

## \|\| 2.8.6 \|\|

*catvāriṃśatsahasrāṇi dvitīyo 'kṣo vivasvataḥ \|*  
*pañcānyāni tu sārdhāni syandanasya mahāmate \|\| 6 \|\|*

The two halves of the yoke are the same length as the two axles, respectively. The carriage’s shorter axle and the shorter half of the yoke rest on the Pole Star. The longer axle, to which the wheel is fixed, rests on Mount Mānasa. 6

## \|\| 2.8.7 \|\|

*ākṣapramāṇamubhayoḥ pramāṇaṃ tadyugārdhayo \|*  
*hrasvokṣastadyugārdhena dhruvādhāro rathasya vai \|*  
*dvitīyekṣe tu taccakraṃ sasthitaṃ mānasācale \|\| 7 \|\|*

The carriage’s seven horses are the Vedic metres. Listen to their names: Gāyatrī, Bṛhatī, Uṣṇih, Jagatī, Triṣṭubh, Anuṣṭubh and Paṅkti. These are said to be the horses of the sun. 7

## \|\| 2.8.8 \|\|

*mānasottaraśailasya pūrvato vāsavī purī \|*  
*dakṣiṇe tu yama syānyāpratīcyāṃ varuṇasya ca \|*  
*uttareṇa ca somasya tāsāṃ nāmāni me śṛṇu \|\| 8 \|\|*

Indra’s city lies on the eastern side of Mount Mānasottara, Yama’s city lies southward, Varuṇa’s to the west and Soma’s to the north. Listen to their names: 8

## \|\| 2.8.9 \|\|

*vasvaukasārā śakrasya yāmyā saṃyamanī tathā \|*  
*purī sukhājaleśasya somasya ca vibhāvarī \|\| 9 \|\|*

Mighty Indra’s city is called Vasvokasārā, Yama’s is Saṃyamanī, that of Varunā, lord of waters, is Sukhā and Soma’s is Vibhāvarī. 9

## \|\| 2.8.10 \|\|

*kāṣṭhāṃ gato dakṣiṇataḥ kṣipteṣuriva sarpati \|*  
*maitreya bhagavānbhānurjyotiṣāṃ cakrasaṃyutaḥ \|\| 10 \|\|*

During its southern passage, the lordly sun flies like an arrow, Maitreya, attended by the wheel of stars. 10

## \|\| 2.8.11 \|\|

*ahorātravyavasthānakāraṇaṃ bhagavānraviḥ \|*  
*devayānaḥ paraḥ panthā yogināṃ kleśasaṃkṣaye \|\| 11 \|\|*

The sun is the cause of the difference between day and night. This divine vehicle is the highest resort for ascetics who’ve transcended worldly woes. 11

## \|\| 2.8.12 \|\|

*divasasya varirmadhye sarvākālaṃ vyavasthitaḥ \|*  
*sarvadvīpeṣu maitreya niśārdhasya ca sanmukhaḥ \|\| 12 \|\|*

The sun is always overhead at midday on one continent, Maitreya, while on the others, it is midnight. 12

## \|\| 2.8.13 \|\|

*udayāstamane caiva sarvakālaṃ tu saṃmukhe \|*  
*vidiśāsu tvaśeṣāsu tathā brahman diśāsu ca \|\| 13 \|\|*

It always rises and sets at opposing cardinal and intermediate points on the horizon, Maitreya. 13

## \|\| 2.8.14 \|\|

*yair yatra dṛśyate bhāsvānsa teṣāmudayaḥ smṛtaḥ \|*  
*tirobhāvaṃ ca yatraiti tatraivāstamanaṃ raveḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

Those who see the sun appear think that it is rising, and when it disappears from sight, they believe that it is setting. 14

## \|\| 2.8.15 \|\|

*naivāstamanamarkasya nodayaḥ sarvadā sataḥ \|*  
*udayāstamanākhyaṃ hi darśanādarśanaṃ raveḥ \|\| 15 \|\|*

In fact, it doesn’t rise or set, and what we call its rising and its setting are merely its appearance and disappearance. 15

## \|\| 2.8.16 \|\|

*śakrādīnāṃ pure tiṣṭhan spṛśatyeṣa puratrayam \|*  
*vikoṇau dvau vikoṇasthastrīn koṇāndve pure tathā \|\| 16 \|\|*

When the sun is directly over mighty Indra’s city, for example, it illuminates three cities and two intermediate points of the compass. When it’s located above one of the intermediate points, it shines on three other points and two cities. 16

## \|\| 2.8.17 \|\|

*udito vardhamānābhirāmadhyāhnāttapanraviḥ \|*  
*tataḥ paraṃ hrasantībhir gobhir astaṃ niyacchati \|\| 17 \|\|*

When the sun has risen, its rays, growing ever stronger, become hotter until midday, after which they weaken as it nears its setting place. 17

## \|\| 2.8.18 \|\|

*udayāstamanābhyāṃ ca smṛte pūrvāpare diśau \|*  
*yāvatpurastāttapati tāvatpṛṣṭhe ca pārśvayoḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

The directions of east (pūrva, ‘before’) and west (apara, ‘after’) are named for the rising and the setting of the sun. The distance it shines towards the east is the same as the distance it shines westwards and on both sides. 18

## \|\| 2.8.19 \|\|

*ṛte 'maragirermerorupari brahmaṇaḥ sabhām \|*  
*yeye marīcayorkasya prayānti brahmaṇaḥ sabhām \|*  
*tete nirastāstadbhāsā pratīpamupayānti vai \|\| 19 \|\|*

(The only exception is the palace of Brahmā on the summit of divine Mount Meru, where the rays of the sun that reach that edifice are outshone by the city’s brilliance and are driven back.) 19

## \|\| 2.8.20 \|\|

*tasmāddiśyuttarasyāṃ vai divārātriḥ sadaiva hi \|*  
*sarveṣāṃ dvīpavarṣāṇāṃ seruruttarato yataḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

As a result, there’s always a distinction between day and night in northern parts, since Meru stands northward of the continents and other regions. 20

## \|\| 2.8.21 \|\|

*prabhā vivasvato rātrāvastaṃ gacchati bhāskare \|*  
*viśatyagnimato rātrau vahnirdūrātprakāśate \|\| 21 \|\|*

In the evening when the sun has set, its brilliance enters fire, and that’s why fire is brighter in the night. 21

## \|\| 2.8.22 \|\|

*vahnoḥ prabhā tathā bhānurdineṣvāviśati dvija \|*  
*atīva vahnisaṃyogādataḥ sūryaḥ prakāśate \|\| 22 \|\|*

During the day, one-quarter of fire’s brilliance joins the sun, brahmin, and, resulting from this union, the sun shines even brighter. 22

## \|\| 2.8.23 \|\|

*tejasī bhāskarāgneye prakāśoṣṇasvarūpiṇī \|*  
*parasparānupraveśādāpyāyete divāniśam \|\| 23 \|\|*

These two forms of brightness, the light and heat of the sun and fire, are strengthened during day and night, respectively, as they merge with one another. 23

## \|\| 2.8.24 \|\|

*dakṣiṇottarabhūmyardha samuttiṣṭhati bhāskare \|*  
*āhorātraṃ viśatyambhastamaḥ prākāśyaśīlavat \|\| 24 \|\|*

When the sun is above the horizon, night enters the waters, filling them with darkness, and, after it has set, daylight fills the waters, imbuing them with light. 24

## \|\| 2.8.25 \|\|

*ātāmrā hi bhavatyāpo divānaktapraveśanāt \|*  
*dinaṃ viśati caivāṃbho bhāskarestamupeyuṣi \|*  
*tasmācchuklā bhavantyāpo naktamahnaḥ praveśanāt \|\| 25 \|\|*

The ocean appears dark by day because it absorbs the night. Daylight imbues the waters when the sun goes down, and that’s why the ocean is brighter at night: it’s filled with light. 25

## \|\| 2.8.26 \|\|

*evaṃ puṣkaramadhyena yadā yāti divākaraḥ \|*  
*triṃśadbhāgastu medinyāstadā mauhūrtikī gatiḥ \|\| 26 \|\|*

When the sun is in the middle of Puṣkara, the time taken to transit for one lunar mansion—one-thirtieth part of the circumference of the celestial globe—is one muhūrta. 26

## \|\| 2.8.27 \|\|

*kulālacakraparyante bhramanneṣa divākaraḥ \|*  
*karotyahas tathā rātriṃ vimuñcanmedinīṃ dvijaḥ \|\| 27 \|\|*

Turning as if on the edge of a potter’s wheel, the sun brings day, brahmin, freeing the world from night. 27

## \|\| 2.8.28 \|\|

*ayanasyottarasyādau makaraṃ yāti bhāskaraḥ \|*  
*tataḥ kumbhaṃ ca mīnaṃ ca rāśe rāśyantaraṃ dvija \|\| 28 \|\|*

During its passage to the north of the celestial equator, the sun first traverses Capricorn, then Aquarius and Pisces, brahmin, moving from one house to the next. 28

## \|\| 2.8.29 \|\|

*triṣveteṣvatha bhukteṣu tato vaiṣuvatīṃ gatim \|*  
*prayāti savitā kurvannahorātraṃ tataḥ samam \|\| 29 \|\|*

After it has passed through these three, the sun reaches the point of the equinox, making day and night of equal length. 29

## \|\| 2.8.30 \|\|

*tato rātriḥ kṣayaṃ yāti vardhate 'nudinaṃ dinam \|\| 30 \|\|*

The nights become thereafter shorter as the days grow longer. 30

## \|\| 2.8.31 \|\|

*tataś ca mithunasyānte parāṃ kāṣṭhāmupāgataḥ \|*  
*rāśiṃ karkaṭakaṃ prāpya kurute dakṣiṇāyanam \|\| 31 \|\|*

Then when the sun reaches the end of Gemini, it takes a different course, and, reaching the sign of Cancer, begins its southern passage. 31

## \|\| 2.8.32 \|\|

*kulālacakraparyanto yathāśīghraṃ pravartate \|*  
*dakṣiṇaprakrame sūryas tathā śīghraṃ pravartate \|\| 32 \|\|*

Just as the outer edge of the potter’s wheel moves more quickly, the sun hastens during this passage. 32

## \|\| 2.8.33 \|\|

*ativegitayā kālaṃ vāyuvegabalāccaran \|*  
*tasmātprakaṣṭāṃ bhūmiṃ tu kālenālpena gacchati \|\| 33 \|\|*

On account of its speed, it proceeds as quickly as the wind and traverses a great distance in but little time. 33

## \|\| 2.8.34 \|\|

*sūryo dvādaśabhiḥ śaighryān muhūrte dakṣiṇāyane \|*  
*trayodaśārdhamṛkṣāṇāmahnā tu carati dvija \|*  
*muhūrtais tāvadṛkṣāṇi naktamaṣṭādaśaiś caran \|\| 34 \|\|*

Because of its haste, while on its southern passage, the sun takes twelve muhūrtas to traverse an extent equivalent to thirteen and a half lunar mansions during the day, brahmin, but during the night it takes eighteen muhūrtas to travel the same distance. 34

## \|\| 2.8.35 \|\|

*kulālacakramadhyastho yathā mandaṃ prasarpati \|*  
*tathodagayane sūryaḥ sarpate mandavikramaḥ \|\| 35 \|\|*

Just as a point near the centre of a potter’s wheel moves more slowly, so does the sun during its northern passage. 35

## \|\| 2.8.36 \|\|

*tasmāddīrgheṇa kālena bhūmimalpāṃ tu gaccati \|*  
*aṣṭādaśasuhūrtaṃ yaduttarāyaṇapaścimam \|\| 36 \|\|*

For this reason, it traverses less space in longer time. When the sun reaches the end of its northern path, the length of the days is again eighteen muhūrtas. 36

## \|\| 2.8.37 \|\|

*aharbhavati taccāpi carate mandavikramaḥ \|\| 37 \|\|*

Here, too, the sun is in the sky for a longer time. 37

## \|\| 2.8.38 \|\|

*trayodaśārdhamahnā tu ṛkṣāṇāṃ carate raviḥ \|*  
*muhūrtais tāvadṛkṣāṇi rātrau dvādaśabhiścaran \|\| 38 \|\|*

Its path is as long as thirteen and a half mansions during the day, and the same distance is traversed in twelve muhūrtas at night. 38

## \|\| 2.8.39 \|\|

*ato mandataraṃ nābhyāṃ cakraṃ bhramati vai yathā \|*  
*mṛtpiṇḍa iva madhyastho dhruvo bhramati vai tathā \|\| 39 \|\|*

Just as a lump of clay at the centre of a potter’s wheel turns more slowly still, this is the case with Dhruva, which stands at the celestial pole. 39

## \|\| 2.8.40 \|\|

*kulālacakranābhis tu yathā tatraivavartate \|*  
*dhruvas tathā hi maitreya tatraiva parivartate \|\| 40 \|\|*

And just as the hub of a potter’s wheel turns on the spot, Maitreya, so it is with that star. 40

## \|\| 2.8.41 \|\|

*ubhayoḥ kāṣṭhayormadhye bhramato maṇḍalāni tu \|*  
*divā naktaṃ ca sūryasya mandā śīghrāca vai gatiḥ \|\| 41 \|\|*

The relative length of day and night is a function of the length of the arc traversed by the sun between the points at which it rises and sets—that is, the length of time it appears in the sky. 41

## \|\| 2.8.42 \|\|

*mandāhni yasminnayane śīghrā naktaṃ tadā gatiḥ \|*  
*śīghrā niśi yadā cāsya tadā mandā divā gatiḥ \|\| 42 \|\|*

When, during its journey, the daytime passage of the sun is long, the nights are short, and when its passage is long at night, the days are short. 42

## \|\| 2.8.43 \|\|

*eka pramāṇamevaiṣa mārgaṃ yāti divākaraḥ \|*  
*ahorātreṇa yo bhuṅkte samastā rāśayo dvija \|\| 43 \|\|*

The actual course of the sun is the same in either case, brahmin, because all signs of the zodiac appear to pass overhead in one day and night. 43

## \|\| 2.8.44 \|\|

*ṣaḍeva rāśīn yo bhuṅkte rātrāvanyāṃś ca ṣaḍdivā \|\| 44 \|\|*

Six signs pass at night and the other six by day. 44

## \|\| 2.8.45 \|\|

*rāśipramāṇajanitā dīrghahrasvātmatā dine \|*  
*tathā niśāyāṃ rāśīnāṃ pramāṇair laghudīrghatā \|\| 45 \|\|*

The length or shortness of a day is the result of the variation in the width of the signs of the zodiac, and, similarly, the length or shortness of the night is determined by the measure of the signs during that time. 45

## \|\| 2.8.46 \|\|

*dināderdirghahrasvatvaṃ tadbhogenaiva jāyate \|*  
*uttare prakrame śīghrā niśi mandā gatirdivā \|\| 46 \|\|*

The long and short durations of day and night are the result of the width of the signs. During its northern passage, the sun moves more quickly at night and slower by day. 46

## \|\| 2.8.47 \|\|

*dakṣiṇe tvayane caiva viparītāṃ vivasvataḥ \|\| 47 \|\|*

During its southern passage, the opposite is the case. 47

## \|\| 2.8.48 \|\|

*uṣā rātriḥ samākhyātāvyuṣṭiścāpyucyate dinam \|*  
*procyate ca tathā saṃdhyā uṣāvyuṣṭyoryadantaram \|\| 48 \|\|*

The night is called uṣā, and the day is known as vyuṣṭi. The interval between day and night is called sandhyā, ‘twilight’. 48

## \|\| 2.8.49 \|\|

*saṃdhyākāle ca saṃprāpte raudre paramadāruṇe \|*  
*mandehā rākṣasā ghorāḥ sūryamicchinti khāditum \|\| 49 \|\|*

When the cruel and pitiless twilit hours arrive, terrible rākṣasas known as mandehas attempt to swallow the sun. 49

## \|\| 2.8.50 \|\|

*prajāpatikṛtaḥ śāpasteṣāṃ maitreya rakṣasām \|*  
*akṣayatvaṃ śarīrāṇāṃ maraṇaṃ ca dine dine \|\| 50 \|\|*

Brahmā, lord of creation, pronounced a curse on these rākṣasas, Maitreya, such that they must die each day, but their bodies aren’t destroyed. 50

## \|\| 2.8.51 \|\|

*tataḥ sūryasya tair yuddhaṃ bhavatyatyantadāruṇam \|*  
*tato dvijottamā stoyaṃ saṃkṣipanti mahāmune \|\| 51 \|\|*

As a result, there’s a frightful daily struggle between the sun and them. That’s why eminent brahmins sprinkle water at such times, great sage, 51

## \|\| 2.8.52 \|\|

*oṅkārabrahmasaṃyuktaṃ gāyatryā cābhimantritam \|*  
*tena dahyanti te pāpā vajrībhūtena vāriṇā \|\| 52 \|\|*

While chanting the syllable Oṃ, consecrated with the Gāyatrī mantra. This water becomes thunderbolts and destroys those wicked beings. 52

## \|\| 2.8.53 \|\|

*agnihotre hūyate yā samantrā prathamāhutiḥ \|*  
*sūryojyotiḥ sahasrāṃśu samā dīpyati bhāskaraḥ \|\| 53 \|\|*

The first offering, accompanied by mantras, made into the sacred fire each morning causes the brilliant light-giving sun of a thousand rays to shine. 53

## \|\| 2.8.54 \|\|

*oṅkāro bhagavānviṣṇustridhāmā vacasāṃ patiḥ \|*  
*taduccāraṇataste tu vināśaṃ yānti rākṣasāḥ \|\| 54 \|\|*

The syllable Oṃ is Lord Viṣṇu, essence of the threefold Vedas and sovereign of each form of speech. By reciting it, those rākṣasas meet their doom. 54

## \|\| 2.8.55 \|\|

*vaiṣṇuvoṃśaḥ paraḥ sūryo yontarjyotirasaṃplavam \|*  
*abhiyāyaka oṅkārastasya tatprerakaṃ param \|\| 55 \|\|*

The sun is the highest aspect of Viṣṇu, whose brilliance never fades. The syllable Oṃ is expressive of the sun and causes it to move. 55

## \|\| 2.8.56 \|\|

*tena saṃpreritaṃ jyotir oṃkāreṇātha dīptimat \|*  
*dahatyaśeṣarakṣāṃsi mandehākhyānyaghāni vai \|\| 56 \|\|*

The light brought forth by Oṃ begins to blaze and incinerates all those wicked mandehas. 56

## \|\| 2.8.57 \|\|

*tasmānnollaṅghanaṃ kāryaṃ saṃdhyopāsanakarmaṇaḥ \|*  
*sa hanti sūryaṃ sandhyāyā nopāstiṃ kurute tu yaḥ \|\| 57 \|\|*

That’s why the sandhyā twilight rituals must never be neglected. One who fails to perform them at that time is guilty of murdering the sun. 57

## \|\| 2.8.58 \|\|

*tataḥ prayāti bhagavānbrāhmaṇair abhirakṣitaḥ \|*  
*vālakhilyādibhiścaiva jagataḥ pālanodyataḥ \|\| 58 \|\|*

Protected by the brahmins and the Vālakhilya sages, the lordly sun then journeys on its way for the welfare of the world. 58

## \|\| 2.8.59 \|\|

*kāṣṭhā nimeṣā daśa pañca caiva triṃśacca kāṣṭhā gaṇayetkalāṃ ca \|*  
*triṃśatkalaścaiva bhavenmuhūrtastais triṃśatā rātryahanī samete \|\| 59 \|\|*

Fifteen nimeṣas or ‘blinks of the eye’ make one kāṣṭhā. Thirty kāṣṭhās make one kalā. Thirty kalās make a muhūrta, and thirty muhūrtas, one day and night. 59

## \|\| 2.8.60 \|\|

*hrāsavṛddhī tvaharbhāgordivasānāṃ yathākramam \|*  
*sandhyāmuhūrtamātrā vai hrāsavṛddhau samā smṛtā \|\| 60 \|\|*

The periods into which a day is divided may be longer or shorter, as the days progress, but twilight always lasts one muhūrta. Even though the days are long or short, its duration stays the same. 60

## \|\| 2.8.61 \|\|

*rekhāprabhṛtyathāditye trimuhūrtagate ravau \|*  
*prātaḥ smṛtastataḥ kālo bhāgaścāhnaḥ sa pañcamaḥ \|\| 61 \|\|*

The first three muhūrtas after sunrise are called morning, one-fifth part of the day. 61

## \|\| 2.8.62 \|\|

*tasmātprātastanātkālāttramuhūrtastu saṅgavaḥ \|*  
*madhyāhnastrimuhūrtastu tasmātkālāttu saṅgavāt \|\| 62 \|\|*

The three muhūrtas after morning are the forenoon, and the three murhūrtas after forenoon are midday. 62

## \|\| 2.8.63 \|\|

*tasmānmādhyāhnikātkālādaparāhna iti smṛtaḥ \|*  
*traya eva muhūrtāstu kālabhāgaḥ smṛto budhaiḥ \|\| 63 \|\|*

Following midday comes the afternoon. The wise consider that this also lasts for three muhūrtas. 63

## \|\| 2.8.64 \|\|

*āparāhne vyatīte tu kālaḥ sāyāhna eva ca \|*  
*daśapañca muhūrtaṃ vai muhūrtāstraya eva ca \|\| 64 \|\|*

When the afternoon is over, the next period is called the evening. In this manner, a day lasts for fifteen muhūrtas and consists of periods of three muhūrtas each. 64

## \|\| 2.8.65 \|\|

*daśapañca muhūrtaṃ vai aharvaiṣuvataṃ smṛtam \|\| 65 \|\|*

But only at the equinox does the day actually last for fifteen muhūrtas. 65

## \|\| 2.8.66 \|\|

*vardhate hraseta caivāpyayane dakṣiṇottare \|*  
*ahastu grasate rātriṃ rātrirgrasati vāsaram \|\| 66 \|\|*

The days grow longer or shorter according to the sun’s southern or northern passage, when the day swallows part of the night or the night swallows part of the day. 66

## \|\| 2.8.67 \|\|

*śaradvasantayormadhye viṣuvaṃ tu vibhāvyate \|*  
*tulāmeṣagate bhānau samarātridinaṃ tu tat \|\| 67 \|\|*

The equinoxes occur in mid-autumn and mid-spring, and, when the sun reaches Libra and Aries, then night and day are equal. 67

## \|\| 2.8.68 \|\|

*karkaṭāvasthite bhānau dakṣiṇāyanamucyate \|*  
*uttarāyaṇamapyuktaṃ makarasthe divākare \|\| 68 \|\|*

When the sun enters Cancer, it begins its southern passage. Its northern passage starts when it enters Capricorn. 68

## \|\| 2.8.69 \|\|

*triṃśanmuhūrtaṃ kathitamahorātraṃ tu yanmayā \|*  
*tānipañcadaśa brahman pakṣa ityabhidhīyate \|\| 69 \|\|*

As I said, thirty muhūrtas make one day and night. Fifteen days and nights are called a half-month, brahmin. 69

## \|\| 2.8.70 \|\|

*māsaḥ pakṣadvayenokto dvau māsau cārkajāvṛtuḥ \|*  
*ṛtutrayaṃ cāpyayanaṃ dve 'yane varṣasaṃjñite \|\| 70 \|\|*

Two half-months make a month and two months make a season. There are three seasons during each passage or ayana and two passages a year. 70

## \|\| 2.8.71 \|\|

*saṃvatsarādayaḥ pañca caturmāsavikalpitāḥ \|*  
*niścayaḥ sarvakālsaya yugamityabhidhīyate \|\| 71 \|\|*

There are five kinds of years, made up of four kinds of months. The aggregate of all these divisions of time is called a yuga or a cycle. 71

## \|\| 2.8.72 \|\|

*saṃvatsarastu prathamo dvitīyaḥ parivatsaraḥ \|*  
*idvatsarastṛtīyastu caturthaścānuvatsaraḥ \|*  
*vatsaraḥ pañcamaścātra kāloyaṃ yugasaṃjñitaḥ \|\| 72 \|\|*

The first year is the saṃvatsara, the second is parivatsara, idvatsara is the third and the fourth is the anuvatsara, with vatsara as the fifth. These five make one cycle. 72

## \|\| 2.8.73 \|\|

*yaḥ śvetasyottaraḥ śailaḥ śṛṅgavāniti viśrutaḥ \|*  
*trīṇi tasya tu śṛṅgāṇi mairayaṃ śṛṅgavān smṛtaḥ \|\| 73 \|\|*

The mountain to the north of Śveta is known as Śṛṅgavant, so called as it has three peaks (śṛṅga): 73

## \|\| 2.8.74 \|\|

*dakṣiṇaṃ cottaraṃ caiva madhyaṃ vaiṣuvataṃ tathā \|*  
*śaradvasaṃtayormadhye tadbhānuḥ pratipadyate \|*  
*meṣādau ca tulādau ca maitreya viṣuvaṃ smṛtam \|\| 74 \|\|*

A southern, a northern and a central one. The central peak is also called the equinoctial summit, because the sun reaches this point at the equinoxes in the middle of autumn and spring, when it first enters Aries and Libra, Maitreya. 74

## \|\| 2.8.75 \|\|

*tadā tulyamahorātraṃ karoti timirāpahaḥ \|*  
*daśapañca muhūrtaṃ vai tad etad ubhayaṃ smṛtam \|\| 75 \|\|*

At such times, the sun, dispeller of the darkness, makes day and night of equal length, both being fifteen muhūrtas. 75

## \|\| 2.8.76 \|\|

*prathame kṛttikābhāge yadā bhāsvāṃstadā śaśī \|*  
*viśākhānāṃ caturthe 'ṃśe mune tiṣṭhatyasaṃśayam \|\| 76 \|\|*

When the sun is in the first segment of the lunar mansion of Kṛttikā, and the moon stands in the fourth segment of Viśākhā, sage, 76

## \|\| 2.8.77 \|\|

*viśākhānāṃ yadā sūryaścaratyaṃśaṃ tṛtīyakam \|*  
*tadā candraṃ vijānīyātkṛttikāśirasi sthitam \|\| 77 \|\|*

Or when the sun is in the third segment of Viśākhā and the moon is at the head of Kṛttikā, 77

## \|\| 2.8.78 \|\|

*tadaiva viṣuvākhyo 'yaṃ kālaḥ puṇyo 'bhidhīyate \|*  
*tadā dānādi deyāni devebhyaḥ prayatātmabhiḥ \|\| 78 \|\|*

It’s known as the time of the auspicious equinox, when self-restrained individuals make offerings to the deities, 78

## \|\| 2.8.79 \|\|

*brahmaṇebhyaḥ pitṛbhyaś ca mukhametattu dānājam \|*  
*dattadānastu viṣuve kṛtakṛtyobhijāyate \|\| 79 \|\|*

Brahmins and ancestors, for at this time, donations give rise to an opening to the gods. One fulfils one’s obligations by making offerings at the equinox and at the following times: 79

## \|\| 2.8.80 \|\|

*ahorātrārdhamāsā tu kalāḥ kāṣṭhāḥ kṣaṇās tathā \|*  
*paurṇamāsī tathā jñeyā amāvāsyā tathaiva ca \|*  
*sinīvālī kuhūścaiva ekā cānumatis tathā \|\| 80 \|\|*

During the day, night, half-month, kalā, kāṣṭhā and kṣaṇa of the full moon, new moon, the day of its first appearance, the first day of the first quarter, the actual day of full moon and one day before that. 80

## \|\| 2.8.81 \|\|

*tapastapasyai madhumādhavau ca śukraḥ śuciścāyanamuttaraṃ syāt \|*  
*nabhonabhasyau ca iṣastathorjaḥsahaḥ sahasyāviti dakṣiṇaṃ tat \|\| 81 \|\|*

The sun’s northern passage occupies the months of Tapas, Tapasya, Madhu, Mādhava, Śukra and Śuci. Nabhas, Nabhasya, Iṣa, Ūrja, Sahas and Sahasya constitute its southern passage. 81

## \|\| 2.8.82 \|\|

*lokā lokaś ca yaḥ śailaḥ prāg ukto bhavato mayā \|*  
*lokapālāstu catvārastatra tiṣṭhanti suvratāḥ \|\| 82 \|\|*

I’ve already told you about Mount Lokāloka, where the four guardians of the world, firm in their vows, reside: 82

## \|\| 2.8.83 \|\|

*sudhāmā śaṅkhapāccaiva kardamasyātmajau dvija \|*  
*hiraṇyaromā caivānyaścaturthaḥ kekumānapi \|\| 83 \|\|*

Kardama’s two sons Sudhāman and Śaṅkhapād, brahmin, as well as Hiraṇyaroman, with Ketumant as the fourth. 83

## \|\| 2.8.84 \|\|

*nirdhandvā nirabhīmānā nistandrā niṣparigrahāḥ \|*  
*lokapālāḥ sthitā hyete lokā loke caturdiśam \|\| 84 \|\|*

Indifferent to extremes, free from pride, vigilant and unencumbered, these global guardians dwell on that mountain, facing the four directions. 84

## \|\| 2.8.85 \|\|

*uttaraṃ padagastyasya ajavīthyāś ca dakṣiṇam \|*  
*pitṛyānaḥ sa vai panthā vaiśvānarapathādbāhiḥ \|\| 85 \|\|*

North of Canopus and south of Aries, beyond the path of Vaiśvānara, lies Pitṛyāna, the path of ancestral spirits. 85

## \|\| 2.8.86 \|\|

*tatrāsate mahātmāna ṛṣayo ye 'gnihotriṇaḥ \|*  
*bhūtāraṃbhakṛtaṃ brahma śaṃsanto ṛtvigudyatāḥ \|*  
*prārabhante tu ye lokāsteṣāṃ panthāḥ sa dakṣiṇaḥ \|\| 86 \|\|*

There reside great sages who maintain the sacred fires and eulogise the Vedas that began the process of creation, intent upon their priestly duties. The path of sages who cause the creation of the world lies southward. 86

## \|\| 2.8.87 \|\|

*calitaṃ te punarbrahma sthāpayanti yugeyuge \|*  
*santatyā tapasā caiva maryādābhiḥ śrutena ca \|\| 87 \|\|*

In every age, they reestablish Vedic tradition, which has been interrupted, with its lineages, austerities, social limits and oral teachings. 87

## \|\| 2.8.88 \|\|

*jāyamānāstu pūrve ca paścimānāṃ gṛheṣu vai \|*  
*paścimāścaiva pūrveṣāṃ jāyante vidhaneṣviha \|\| 88 \|\|*

The sages of the east are born in residences of the west, and the sages of the west are born in eastern mansions. 88

## \|\| 2.8.89 \|\|

*evamāvartamānāste tiṣṭhanti niyatavratāḥ \|*  
*saviturdakṣiṇaṃ mārgaṃ śritā hyacandratārakam \|\| 89 \|\|*

Alternating in this way, they remain upon this path until the destruction of the world. Their path lies southward of the sun and will endure as long as the moon and stars. 89

## \|\| 2.8.90 \|\|

*nāgāvīthyuttaraṃ yacca saptarṣibhyaś ca dakṣiṇam \|*  
*uttaraḥ savituḥ panthā devayānaś ca sa smṛtaḥ \|\| 90 \|\|*

North of Aries and Taurus, south of the Great Bear and north of the sun lies the path of gods. 90

## \|\| 2.8.91 \|\|

*tatra te vāsinaḥ siddhāvimalā brahmacāriṇaḥ \|*  
*santatiṃ te jugupsaṃti tasmānmṛtyurjitaś ca taiḥ \|\| 91 \|\|*

There reside the chaste and stainless siddhas, who have no thought of progeny, and have therefore conquered death. 91

## \|\| 2.8.92 \|\|

*aṣṭāśītisahasrāṇi munīnāmūrdhvareta sām \|*  
*udakpanthanamaryamṇaḥ sthitā hyābhūtasaṃplavam \|\| 92 \|\|*

Eighty-eight thousand ascetics, retaining their semen, reside on the northern path of the sun until the world is destroyed. 92

## \|\| 2.8.93 \|\|

*te seprayogāllobhasya maithunasya ca varjanāt \|*  
*icchādveṣāpravṛttyā ca bhūtārambhavi varjanāt \|\| 93 \|\|*

Because they’re free of greed and have renounced all sexual union, and because they abjure the creation of life, which is characterised by love and hate, 93

## \|\| 2.8.94 \|\|

*punaś ca kāmāsaṃyogācchabdāderdeṣadarśanāt \|*  
*ityebhiḥ kāraṇaiḥ śuddhāstemṛtatvaṃ hi bhejire \|\| 94 \|\|*

And further, because they’re free from desire and misapprehension of sound and other sensations—for all these reasons, they’re purified and immortal. 94

## \|\| 2.8.95 \|\|

*ābhūtasaṃplavaṃ sthānamamṛtatvaṃ vibhāvyate \|*  
*trailokyasthitikāloyamapunarmāra ucyate \|\| 95 \|\|*

This deathless state persists until the destruction of the world, and they’re said to be free from death while the threefold worlds endure. 95

## \|\| 2.8.96 \|\|

*brahmahatyāśvamedhābhyāṃ pāpapuṇyakṛto vidhiḥ \|*  
*ābhūtasaṃplavāntantu phalamuktaṃ tayordvija \|\| 96 \|\|*

The results of wicked acts like brahminicide, brahmin, and of auspicious ones like the Aśvamedha sacrifice, will endure until universal dissolution. 96

## \|\| 2.8.97 \|\|

*yāvanmātre pradeśe tu maitreyāvasthito dhruvaḥ \|*  
*kṣayamāyāti tāvattu bhūmerābhūtasaṃplavāt \|\| 97 \|\|*

At such a time, Maitreya, all that lies between the Pole Star and the earth will be destroyed. 97

## \|\| 2.8.98 \|\|

*ūrdhvottaramṛṣibhyastu dhruvo yatra vyavasthitaḥ \|*  
*etadviṣṇupadaṃ divyaṃ tṛtīyaṃ vyomni bhāsuram \|\| 98 \|\|*

North of the Great Bear and extending as far as the Pole Star is the third division of the sky, Viṣṇu’s brilliant, heavenly realm. 98

## \|\| 2.8.99 \|\|

*nirdhūtadoṣapaṅkānāṃ yatīnāṃ saṃyatātmanām \|*  
*sthānaṃ tatparamaṃ vipra puṇyapāpaparikṣaye \|\| 99 \|\|*

This is the highest state for self-controlled ascetics who’ve washed away the stains of sin, brahmin, and for whom vice and virtue are no more. 99

## \|\| 2.8.100 \|\|

*apuṇyapuṇyoparame kṣīṇāśeṣāptihetavaḥ \|*  
*yatra gatvā na śocanti tadviṣṇoḥ parapaṃ padam \|\| 100 \|\|*

When ideas of ‘good’ and ‘evil’ cease and the cause of further birth is gone—when they attain all this, they grieve no more; that’s Viṣṇu’s highest state. 100

## \|\| 2.8.101 \|\|

*dharmadhruvādyāstiṣṭhanti yatra te lokasākṣiṇaḥ \|*  
*tatsārṣṭyotpannayogardhistadviṣṇoḥ paramaṃpadam \|\| 101 \|\|*

Where Dharma, Dhruva and other witnesses of the world abide, blazing in their union with the majesty that imbues the god—that’s Viṣṇu’s highest state. 101

## \|\| 2.8.102 \|\|

*yatrotametatprotaṃ ca yadbhūtaṃ sacarācaram \|*  
*bhavyaṃ ya viśvaṃ maitreya tadviṣṇoḥ paramaṃ padam \|\| 102 \|\|*

The warp and weft of all creation, of all moving and unmoving things and of all that will arise in future, Maitreya—that’s Viṣṇu’s highest state. 102

## \|\| 2.8.103 \|\|

*divīva cakṣurātataṃyogināṃ tanmayātmanām \|*  
*vivekajñānadṛṣṭaṃ ca tadviṣṇoḥ paramaṃ padam \|\| 103 \|\|*

That which the wise regard as the great heavenly eye and that which appear as knowledge and discernment—that’s Viṣṇu’s highest state. 103

## \|\| 2.8.104 \|\|

*yasminpratiṣṭhito bhāsvānmeḍhībhūtaḥ svayaṃ dhruvaḥ \|*  
*dhruve ca sarvajyotīṃṣi jyotiṣvambhomuco dvija \|\| 104 \|\|*

Brilliant Dhruva himself, serving as the axis of the heavens, is located there. All the heavenly luminaries depend on him, brahmin, as do water-yielding clouds. 104

## \|\| 2.8.105 \|\|

*megheṣu saṃgatā vṛṣṭirvṛṣṭeḥ sṛṣṭeś ca poṣaṇam \|*  
*āpyāyanaṃ ca sarveṣāṃ devādīnāṃ mahāmune \|\| 105 \|\|*

Rain is born in them, and all creation, including deities and the other beings, great sage, are nourished and refreshed by rain. 105

## \|\| 2.8.106 \|\|

*tataścājyāhutidvārā poṣitāste havirbhujaḥ \|*  
*vṛṣṭeḥ kāraṇatāṃ yānti bhūtānāṃ sthitaye punaḥ \|\| 106 \|\|*

The gods who consume the offerings are sustained by oblations of butter, and they send rain for the further sustenance of the world. 106

## \|\| 2.8.107 \|\|

*evametatpadaṃ viṣṇostṛtīyamamalātmakam \|*  
*ādhārabhūtaṃ lokānāṃ trayāṇāṃ vṛṣṭikāraṇam \|\| 107 \|\|*

Such is the third portion of the sky, the pure abode of Viṣṇu, foundation of the threefold worlds and source of rain. 107

## \|\| 2.8.108 \|\|

*tataḥ prabhavati brahman sarvapāpaharā sarit \|*  
*gaṅgā devāṅganāṅgānāmanulepanapiñjarā \|\| 108 \|\|*

Hence arises the river Gaṅgā, brahmin, which removes all sins and is itself suffused with lotions washed from the limbs of the deities’ wives. 108

## \|\| 2.8.109 \|\|

*vāmapādāmbujāṅguṣṭha-nakhastrotovinirgatām \|*  
*viṣṇorbibhārti yāṃ bhaktyā śirasāharniśaṃ dhruvaḥ \|\| 109 \|\|*

Her torrents, which flow from the nail of the big toe of Viṣṇu’s lotus-like left foot, are devoutly borne by Dhruva on his head by day and night. 109

## \|\| 2.8.110 \|\|

*tataḥ saptarṣayo yasyāḥ prāṇāyāmaparā yaṇāḥ \|*  
*tiṣṭhanti vīcimālābhiruhyamānajaṭājale \|\| 110 \|\|*

Downstream, the Seven Sages, intent on breath control, their matted locks swept along by her wreath-like waves, stand amid her waters. 110

## \|\| 2.8.111 \|\|

*vāryoghaiḥ saṃtatair yasyāḥ plāvitaṃ śaśimaṇḍalam \|*  
*bhūyodhikatarāṃ kāntiṃ vahatyetaduha kṣaye \|\| 111 \|\|*

The disk of the hare-marked moon, washed by her spreading currents, acquires still greater loveliness as her waters remove its stain. 111

## \|\| 2.8.112 \|\|

*merupṛṣṭe patatyuccair niṣkrāntā śaśimaṇḍalāt \|*  
*jagataḥ pāvanārthāya prayāti ca caturdiśam \|\| 112 \|\|*

Descending from the moon, she cascades upon Mount Meru’s summit, and, to purify the world, flows forth in four directions. 112

## \|\| 2.8.113 \|\|

*sītī cālakanandā ca cakṣurbhadrā ca saṃsthitā \|*  
*ekaiva yā caturbhedā digbhedagatilakṣaṇā \|\| 113 \|\|*

In the forms of Sītā, Alakanandā, Cakṣu and Bhadrā, she’s a fourfold stream in one, according to the path she takes. 113

## \|\| 2.8.114 \|\|

*bhedaṃ cālakanandākhyaṃ yasyāḥ śarvopi dakṣiṇām \|*  
*dadhāra śirasā prītya varṣāṇāmadhikaṃ śatam \|\| 114 \|\|*

Śiva kindly bore the southern branch of Alakanandā on his head for more than a hundred years. 114

## \|\| 2.8.115 \|\|

*śambhorjaṭākalāpācca viniṣkrāntāśthiśarkarāḥ \|*  
*plāvayitvā divaṃ ninye yā pāpānsagarātmajān \|\| 115 \|\|*

Emerging from the deity’s mass of matted locks, she bathed the shattered bones of Sagara’s wicked sons and raised them up to heaven. 115

## \|\| 2.8.116 \|\|

*strātasya salile yasyāḥ sadyaḥ pāpaṃ praṇaśyati \|*  
*apūrvapuṇyaprāptiś ca sadyo maitreya jāyate \|\| 116 \|\|*

The sins of those who bathe in her are instantly expunged, and they taste good fortune as they never have before. 116

## \|\| 2.8.117 \|\|

*dattāḥ pitṛbhyo yatrāpastanayaiḥ śraddhayānvitaiḥ \|*  
*samāśataṃ prayacchanti tṛptiṃ maitreya durlabhām \|\| 117 \|\|*

Ancestors, offered her waters by faithful sons, are nourished for three years, Maitreya—a feat of no mean achievement. 117

## \|\| 2.8.118 \|\|

*yasyāmiṣṭvā mahāyajñair yajñeśaṃ puruṣottamam \|*  
*dvijabhūpāḥ parāṃ siddhimavāpurdivi ceha ca \|\| 118 \|\|*

Worshipping Viṣṇu, lord of sacrifice and highest spirit, with great rituals on her banks, kings and brahmins reap supreme rewards in this world and the next. 118

## \|\| 2.8.119 \|\|

*snānādvidhūtapāpāś ca yajjalair yatayas tathā \|*  
*keśabāsaktamanasaḥ prāptā nirvāṇamuttamam \|\| 119 \|\|*

Cleansed of sin by bathing in her waters, ascetics and those whose minds are set on Keśava reach ultimate extinction. 119

## \|\| 2.8.120 \|\|

*śrutābhilaṣitā dṛṣṭā spṛṣṭā pītāvagāhitā \|*  
*yā pāvayati bhūtāni kīrtitā ca divedine \|\| 120 \|\|*

When heard, desired, seen or touched, imbibed or bathed in, or praised each day, she purifies all beings. 120

## \|\| 2.8.121 \|\|

*gagāgaṅgeti yair nāmayojanānā śataṣvapi \|*  
*sthitair uccāritaṃ hanti pāpaṃ janmatrayārjitam \|\| 121 \|\|*

Those who summon her by name—‘Gaṅgā! Gaṅgā!’—even at a distance of a hundred leagues, are cleansed of all the deeds they’ve done in three previous lives. 121

## \|\| 2.8.122 \|\|

*yataḥ sā pāvanāyālaṃ trayāṇāṃ jagatāmapi \|*  
*samudbhūtā paraṃ tattu tṛtīyaṃ bhagavatpadam \|\| 122 \|\|*

The place whence Gaṅgā flows to purify the worlds is the third division of the sky, the supreme abode of Viṣṇu. 122

## Chapter 9 - The source of rain

## \|\| 2.9.1 \|\|

*śrīpāraśara uvāca*  
*tārāmayaṃ bhagavataḥ śiśumārākṛti prabhoḥ \|*  
*divi rūpaṃ harer yattu tasya pucche sthito dhruvaḥ \|\| 1 \|\|*

Lord Hari takes the astral form of a dolphin, Śiśumāra, in the heavens, with Dhruva standing on his tail. 1

## \|\| 2.9.2 \|\|

*saiṣa bhraman bhrāmayati candrādityādikān grahān \|*  
*bhramantamanu taṃ yānti nakṣatrāṇi ca cakravat \|\| 2 \|\|*

Dhruva, turning, sets the moon, the sun and planets in motion. The constellations follow them, spinning like a wheel. 2

## \|\| 2.9.3 \|\|

*sūryācandramasau tārā nakṣatrāmi grahaiḥ saha \|*  
*vātānīkamayair bandhair dhruve baddhāni tāni vai \|\| 3 \|\|*

The sun, moon, stars, constellations and planets are all tied to Dhruva with bonds of wind. 3

## \|\| 2.9.4 \|\|

*śiśumārākṛti proktaṃ yadrūpaṃ jyotiṣāṃ divi \|*  
*nārāyaṇo 'yanaṃ dhāmnāṃ tasyādhāraḥ svayaṃ hṛdi \|\| 4 \|\|*

Nārāyaṇa, refuge of the worlds, who exists in the heavens in astral form, is Śiśumāra’s foundation and is himself located at its heart. 4

## \|\| 2.9.5 \|\|

*uttānapādaputrastu tamārādhya jagatpatim \|*  
*sa tārā śiśumārasya dhruvaḥ pucche vyavasthitaḥ \|\| 5 \|\|*

Dhruva, son of Uttānapāda, worshipping the universal lord, won a place on Śiśumāra’s tail. 5

## \|\| 2.9.6 \|\|

*ādhāraḥ śiśumārasya sarvādhyakṣo janārdanaḥ \|*  
*dhruvasya śiśumārastu dhruve bhānurvyavasthitaḥ \|\| 6 \|\|*

Janārdana, witness of the world, is Śiśumāra’s support. Śiśumāra supports Dhruva, and Dhruva supports the sun. 6

## \|\| 2.9.7 \|\|

*tadādhāraṃ jagaccedaṃ sadevāsuramānuṣam \|\| 7 \|\|*

The sun supports the universe, including gods and demigods and mortals. 7

## \|\| 2.9.8 \|\|

*yena vipravidhānena tanmamaikamanāḥ śṛṇu \|*  
*vivasvānaṣṭabhir māsair ādāyāpo rasātmikāḥ \|*  
*varṣatyambutataścānnamannādapyakhilaṃ jagat \|\| 8 \|\|*

Listen carefully how this happens, brahmin. Eight months each year, the sun draws up water, the essence of existence. Falling showers then nourish crops on which the world relies. 8

## \|\| 2.9.9 \|\|

*vivasvānaṃśubhis tīkṣṇair ādāya jagato jatam \|*  
*somaṃ puṣmātyathenduś ca vāyunāḍīmayair divi \|*  
*nālair vikṣipate 'bhreṣu dhūmāgnyanilamūrtiṣu \|\| 9 \|\|*

The sun takes water from the earth with drying rays and releases it to the moon. The moon directs this water through aerial channels to the clouds composed of smoke and fire and wind. 9

## \|\| 2.9.10 \|\|

*na bhraśyanti yatastebhyo jalānyabhrāṇi tānyataḥ \|*  
*abhrasthāḥ prapatantyāpo vāyunā samudīritāḥ \|*  
*saṃskāraṃ kāṃlajanitaṃ maitreyāsādya nirmalāḥ \|\| 10 \|\|*

Because the rains don’t fall (bhraśyanti) at once, clouds are known as abhra. Water held in clouds descends when stirred by wind, unsullied and purified by passing time, Maitreya. 10

## \|\| 2.9.11 \|\|

*saritsamudrabhomāstu tathāpaḥ prāṇisambhavāḥ \|*  
*catuṣprakārā bhagavānādatte savitā mune \|\| 11 \|\|*

The lordly sun draws water from four sources, sage: from rivers, oceans, earth and living things. 11

## \|\| 2.9.12 \|\|

*ākāśagaṅgāsalilaṃ tathādāyagabhastimān \|*  
*anabhragatamevorvyāṃ sadyaḥ kṣipati raśmibhiḥ \|\| 12 \|\|*

Taking water from the Gaṅgā flowing through the sky, the sun bears it immediately to earth by means of rays, without the need for clouds. 12

## \|\| 2.9.13 \|\|

*tasya saṃsparśanirdhūtapāpapaṅko dvijottama \|*  
*na yāti narakaṃ martyo divyaṃ snānaṃ hi tatsmṛtam \|\| 13 \|\|*

A mortal touched by water of this kind is cleansed of the stain of his misdeeds, best of brahmins, and doesn’t go to hell, as it’s thought to be divine ablution. 13

## \|\| 2.9.14 \|\|

*dṛṣṭasūryaṃ hi yadvāri patatyabhrair vinā divaḥ \|*  
*ākāśagaṅgāsalilaṃ tadgobhiḥ kṣipyate raveḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

Rain that falls while the sun appears in a cloudless sky is the water of aerial Gaṅgā, and is borne to earth on sunbeams. 14

## \|\| 2.9.15 \|\|

*kṛttikādiṣu ṛkṣeṣu viṣameṣu ca yaddivaḥ \|*  
*dṛṣṭārkapatitaṃ jñeyaṃ tadgāṅgaṃ diggajojjhitam \|\| 15 \|\|*

That which falls when the sun is seen in Kṛttikā or other odd-numbered lunar mansions is also from the Gaṅgā, but is scattered by the elephants that guard the four directions. 15

## \|\| 2.9.16 \|\|

*yugmarkṣeṣu ca yattoyaṃ patatyarkojjhitaṃ divaḥ \|*  
*tatsūryaraśmibhiḥ sarvaṃ samādāya nirasyate \|\| 16 \|\|*

That which falls when the sun is in the even-numbered mansions is dispersed by the sun itself, borne at once to earth by rays. 16

## \|\| 2.9.17 \|\|

*ubhayaṃ puṇyamatyarthaṃ nṛṇāṃ pāpabhayāpaham \|*  
*ākāśagaṅgāsalilaṃ divyaṃ snānaṃ mahāmune \|\| 17 \|\|*

In all these cases, rain is highly auspicious and cleanses people of their sins, brahmin, as the waters of airborne Gaṅgā provide divine ablution, sage. 17

## \|\| 2.9.18 \|\|

*yattu maighaiḥ samutsṛṣṭaṃ vāri tatprāṇināṃ dvija \|*  
*puṣṇātyauṣadhayaḥ sarvā jīvanāyāmṛtaṃ hi tat \|\| 18 \|\|*

Rain falling from the clouds nourishes every kind of plant, brahmin, and is nectar for living beings. 18

## \|\| 2.9.19 \|\|

*tena vṛddhiṃ parāṃ nītaḥ sakalaścauṣadhīgaṇaḥ \|*  
*sādhakaḥ phalapākantaḥ prajānāṃ dvija jāyate \|\| 19 \|\|*

Rain leads plants to full maturity, brahmin, thanks to which, ripe crops appear for humankind. 19

## \|\| 2.9.20 \|\|

*tena yajñānyathāproktānmānavāḥ śāstracakṣuṣaḥ \|*  
*kurvantyaharahastaiś ca devānāpyāyayantite \|\| 20 \|\|*

Relying on these, men who have regard for scripture perform the proper rites each day, and nourish the gods thereby. 20

## \|\| 2.9.21 \|\|

*evaṃ yajñāś ca vedāś ca varṇāś ca dvijapūrvakāḥ \|*  
*sarve devanikāyāś ca sarve bhūtagaṇāś ca ye \|\| 21 \|\|*

In this way, the sacrifices, the Vedas, the communities with brahmins as the first, all the assembled deities and herds of beasts all depend on rain. 21

## \|\| 2.9.22 \|\|

*vṛṣṭyā dhṛtamidaṃ sarvamannaṃ niṣpādyate yayā \|*  
*sāpi niṣpādyate vṛṣṭiḥ savitrā munisattama \|\| 22 \|\|*

Rain brings food, best of brahmins, and the sun gives rise to rain. 22

## \|\| 2.9.23 \|\|

*ādhārabhūtaḥ saviturdhruvo munivarottama \|*  
*dhruvasya śiśupāro 'sau so 'pi nārāyaṇātmakaḥ \|\| 23 \|\|*

Dhruva is the sun’s support, best of brahmins, as Śiśumāra is of Dhruva, while Śiśumāra depends on Nārāyaṇa. 23

## \|\| 2.9.24 \|\|

*hṛdi nārāyaṇastasya śiśumārasyaṃ saṃsthitaḥ \|*  
*bibhartā sarvabhūtānāmādibhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

At Śiśumāra’s heart is Nārāyaṇa, support of every being, primal and eternal. 24

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*tryaśītimaṇḍalaśataṃ kāṣṭhayorantaraṃ dvayoḥ \|*  
*ārohaṇāvarohābhyāṃ bhānorabdena yā gatiḥ \|\| 1 \|\|*

The sun proceeds through one hundred and eighty degrees on its passage between its ascending and descending nodes. 1

Dhātṛ, Kṛtasthalā, Pulastya, Vāsuki, the yakṣa Rathakṛt and Heti, with Tumburu as the seventh— 3

These seven always ride on the sun’s carriage during Caitra, also known as Madhu, Maitreya, and preside over this month. 4

Aryaman, Pulaha, Rathaujas, Puñjikasthalā, Praheti, Kacchanīra and Nārada ride the sun’s carriage during Mādhava. 5

## \|\| 2.10.6 \|\|

Now hear about the month of Śuci: 6

## \|\| 2.10.7 \|\|

Mitra, Atri, Takṣaka, the rākṣasa Pauruṣeya, Menakā, Hāhā and Rathasvana ride the carriage at that time, Maitreya. 7

*varuṇo vasiṣṭho rambhā ca sahajanyā huhūrathaḥ \|*  
*rathacitras tathā śukre vasantyāṣāḍhasaṃjñake \|\| 8 \|\|*

In the month of Śukra, also known as Āṣāḍha, Varuṇa, Vasiṣṭha, Rambhā, Sahajanyā, Huhū, Ratha and Rathacitra ride it. 8

*pramlocā ca na bhasyete saptiścārke vasanti vai \|\| 9 \|\|*

Indra, Viśvāvasu, Srotas, Elāpatra, Aṅgiras, Pramlocā and Sarpa ride the sun’s carriage in the month of Nabhas. 9

## \|\| 2.10.10 \|\|

*vivasvānugrasenaś ca bhṛgurāpūraṇas tathā \|*  
*anumlocā śaṅkhapālo vyāghro bhādrapade tathā \|\| 10 \|\|*

During Bhādrapada, it’s the turn of Vivasvat, Ugrasena, Bhṛgu, Āpūraṇa, Umlocā, Śaṅkhapāla and Vyāghra. 10

## \|\| 2.10.11 \|\|

*pūṣā ca surucirvāto gautamotha dhanañjayaḥ \|*  
*suṣeṇo 'nyo ghṛtācī ca vasantyāśvayuje ravau \|\| 11 \|\|*

In the month of Āśvin, Pūṣan, Suruci, Vāta, Gautama, Dhanaṃjaya, Suṣeṇa and Ghṛtācī ride it. 11

## \|\| 2.10.12 \|\|

*viśvāvasurbharadvājaḥ parjanyair āvatau tathā \|*  
*viśvācī senajiccāpi kārttike ca vasanti vai \|\| 12 \|\|*

Viśvāvasu, Bharadvāja, Parjanya, Airāvata, Viśvācī, Senajit and Cāpi preside over the month of Kārttika. 12

## \|\| 2.10.13 \|\|

*aṃśaḥ kāśyapatārkṣyāstu mahāpadmastathorvaśī \|*  
*citrasenas tathā vidyunmārgaśīrṣedhikāriṇaḥ \|\| 13 \|\|*

Aṃśu, Kaśyapa, Tārkṣya, Mahāpadma, Urvaśī, Citrasena and Vidyut preside over Mārgaśīrṣa. 13

## \|\| 2.10.14 \|\|

*kraturbhagastatorṇāyuḥ sphūrjaḥ kārkoṭakas tathā \|*  
*ariṣṭanemiścaivānyā pūrvācittirvarāpsarāḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

Kratu, Bhaga, Urṇāyu, Sphūrja, Karkoṭaka, Ariṣṭanemi and the excellent apsaras Pūrvacitti— 14

## \|\| 2.10.15 \|\|

*pauṣamāse vasantyete saptabhāskaramaṇāle \|*  
*lokaprakāśanārthāya vigravaryādhikāriṇaḥ \|\| 15 \|\|*

These seven ride the solar disk in the month of Pauṣa to ensure the world is properly lit, and they are its outstanding overseers. 15

## \|\| 2.10.16 \|\|

*tvaṣṭātha jamadagniś ca kambalobha tilottamā \|*  
*brahmopetotha ṛtajiddhṛtarāṣṭrotha saptamaḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

Tvaṣṭṛ, Jamadagni, Kambala, Tilottamā, Brahmāpeta and Ṛtajit, with Dhṛtarāṣṭra as the seventh— 16

## \|\| 2.10.17 \|\|

*māghamāse vasanttyete sapta maitreya bhāskare \|*  
*śrūyatāṃ cāpare sūrye phālgunenivasanti yo \|\| 17 \|\|*

In the month of Māgha, these seven ride the sun, Maitreya. Now you’ll hear about those who ride its carriage in the month of Phālguna: 17

## \|\| 2.10.18 \|\|

*viṣṇuraśvataro rambhā sūryavarcāś ca satyajit \|*  
*viśvāmitras tathā rakṣo yajñopeto mahāmune \|\| 18 \|\|*

Viṣṇu, Aśvatara, Rambhā, Sūryavarcas, Satyajit, Viśvāmitra and the rākṣasa Yañjopeta, great sage. 18

## \|\| 2.10.19 \|\|

*māseṣveteṣu maitreya vasantyete tu saptakāḥ \|*  
*saviturmaṇḍale brahmanviṣṇusaktyupabṛṃhitāḥ \|\| 19 \|\|*

During these months, Maitreya, these groups of seven beings occupy the solar disk, brahmin, strengthened by the power of Viṣṇu. 19

## \|\| 2.10.20 \|\|

*stuvanti munayaḥ sūryaṃ gandharvair gīyate puraḥ \|*  
*nṛtyantyapsaraso yānti sūryasyānuniśācarāḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

The sages praise the sun, gandharvas sing before it, apsarases dance while rākṣasas attend it. 20

## \|\| 2.10.21 \|\|

The nāgas guide the carriage and yakṣas hold the reins. 21

## \|\| 2.10.22 \|\|

*vālakhityāstathaivainaṃ parivārya samāsate \|\| 22 \|\|*

The Vālakhilya sages surround it on all sides. 22

## \|\| 2.10.23 \|\|

Each cohort residing on the solar disk, great sage, brings heat and cold and showers of rain in season. 23

## Chapter 11 - The sun’s energy

## \|\| 2.11.1 \|\|

*maitreya uvāca*  
*yadetadbhagavānāha gaṇaḥ saptavidho raveḥ \|*  
*maṇḍale himatāpādeḥ kāraṇaṃ tanmayā śrutam \|\| 1 \|\|*

Master, I’ve listened to your description of the groups of seven beings who occupy the solar disk, bringing heat and cold and so on. 1

## \|\| 2.11.2 \|\|

*vyāpāraścāpikathito gandharvoragarakṣasām \|*  
*ṛṣīṇāṃ vālakhilyānāṃ tathaivāpsarasāṃ guro \|\| 2 \|\|*

You’ve spoken of the role of gandharvas, nāgas, rākṣasas, sages, Vālakhilyas and apsarases, guru, 2

## \|\| 2.11.3 \|\|

*yakṣāṇāṃ ca rathe bhānorviṣṇuśaktidhṛtātmanām \|*  
*kiṃ cādityasya yatkarma tannātroktaṃ tvayā mune \|\| 3 \|\|*

As well as yakṣas on the carriage of the sun, all of whom are held by Viṣṇu’s power. But you’ve said nothing, sage, of the function of the sun itself. 3

## \|\| 2.11.4 \|\|

*yadi saptagaṇo vāri himamuṣṇaṃ ca varṣati \|*  
*tatkimatra raveryena vṛṣṭiḥ sūryāditīryate \|\| 4 \|\|*

If these seven beings cause heat and cold and rain, then what role has the sun in this regard? I thought you said it sends down showers. 4

## \|\| 2.11.5 \|\|

*vivasvānudito madhye yātyastamiti kiṃ janaḥ \|*  
*bravītyetatsamaṃ karma yadi saptagaṇasya tat \|\| 5 \|\|*

Why do folk contend the sun first rises, passes overhead, then sets, if these are but the work of seven beings? 5

## \|\| 2.11.6 \|\|

*parāśara uvāca*  
*maitreyaśrūyatāmetadyadbhavānparipṛccati \|*  
*yathā saptagaṇopyekaḥ prādhānyenādhiko raviḥ \|\| 6 \|\|*

Listen, Maitreya, and I’ll answer your question as to how the sun alone is superior to the seven. 6

## \|\| 2.11.7 \|\|

*sarvaśaktiḥ parā viṣṇo ṛgyajuḥsāmasaṃjñitā \|*  
*saiṣātrayī tapatyaṃho jagataś ca hinasti yā \|\| 7 \|\|*

Viṣṇu’s threefold power, known as the Ṛg, Yajur and Sāma Vedas, warms the world and dispels its sins. 7

## \|\| 2.11.8 \|\|

*saiṣa viṣṇuḥ sthitaḥ sthityāṃ jagataḥ pālanodyataḥ \|*  
*ṛgyajuḥ sāmabhūtontaḥ saviturdvijatiṣṭhati \|\| 8 \|\|*

This power is Viṣṇu and is actively engaged in the upkeep of the world. In the form of the threefold Veda, brahmin, it dwells within the sun. 8

## \|\| 2.11.9 \|\|

*māsimāsi raviryoyastatra tatra hi sā parā \|*  
*trayīmayī viṣṇuśaktiravasthānaṃ karoti vai \|\| 9 \|\|*

Month by month, wherever the sun abides, Viṣṇu’s peerless threefold energy determines its condition. 9

## \|\| 2.11.10 \|\|

*ṛcastuvantiḥ pūrvāhne madhyāhnetha yajūṃṣi vai \|*  
*bṛhadrathantarādīni sāmānyahnaḥ kṣaye ravim \|\| 10 \|\|*

Ṛg Vedic verses light the world each morning, the Yajur does at noon and the Sāma Veda, including the Bṛhadrathantara, illuminates the world at sunset. 10

## \|\| 2.11.11 \|\|

*aṅgameṣā trayī viṣṇo ṛgyajuḥ sāmasaṃjñitā \|*  
*viṣṇuśaktiravasthānaṃ sadāditye karoti sā \|\| 11 \|\|*

These three Vedas are all aspects of the lord. His energy always dwells within the sun and determines its condition. 11

## \|\| 2.11.12 \|\|

*na kevalaṃ raveḥ śaktirvaiṣṇavī sā trayīmayī \|*  
*brahmātha puruṣo rudrastrayametattrayīmayam \|\| 12 \|\|*

This threefold energy of the Divine is not confined to the sun alone, as Brahmā, the highest spirit, and Rudra also share its nature. 12

## \|\| 2.11.13 \|\|

*sargādau ṛṅmayo brahmā sthitau viṣṇuryajurmayaḥ \|*  
*rudraḥ sāmamayontāya tasmāttasyāśucirthvaniḥ \|\| 13 \|\|*

At the onset of creation, this energy is Brahmā in the form of the Ṛg Veda. While the world endures, it’s Viṣṇu as the Yajur and, at its dissolution, it’s Rudra as the Sāma Veda. That’s why the recitation of the latter is considered inauspicious. 13

## \|\| 2.11.14 \|\|

*evaṃ sā sāttvikī śaktirvaiṣṇavī yā trayīmayī \|*  
*ātmasaptagaṇasthaṃ taṃ bhāsvantamadhitiṣṭhati \|\| 14 \|\|*

Thus, Viṣṇu’s pure and threefold power invests the sun, even while the seven beings occupy it. 14

## \|\| 2.11.15 \|\|

*tayā cādhiṣṭhitaḥ so 'pi jājvalīti svaraśmibhiḥ \|*  
*tamaḥ samastajagatāṃ nāśaṃnayati cākhilam \|\| 15 \|\|*

Filled with power, the sun shines brightly, and with its rays it banishes the darkness from all the worlds. 15

## \|\| 2.11.16 \|\|

*stuvanti cainaṃ munayo gandharvair gīyate puraḥ \|*  
*nṛtyantyo 'psaraso yānti tasya cānu niśācarāḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

The sages praise the sun, the gandharvas sing before it and apsarases dance while rākṣasas attend it. 16

## \|\| 2.11.17 \|\|

*vahanti pannagā yakṣaiḥ kriyate 'bhīṣusaṃgrahaḥ \|*  
*vālakhilyāstathaivainaṃ parivārya samāsate \|\| 17 \|\|*

The nāgas guide its carriage, yakṣas hold the reins and Vālakhilya seers surround it on all sides. 17

## \|\| 2.11.18 \|\|

*nodetā nāstametā ca kadācicchaktirūpadhṛk \|*  
*viṣṇurviṣṇoḥ pṛthak tasya gaṇāḥsaptavidho 'pyayam \|\| 18 \|\|*

The sun itself neither sets nor rises, but is ever filled with Viṣṇu’s power. The seven beings are all Viṣṇu, but are at once distinct from him. 18

## \|\| 2.11.19 \|\|

*stambhasthadarpaṇasyeva yo 'yamāsannatāṃ gataḥ \|*  
*chāyādarśanasaṃyogaṃ sa taṃ prāpnotyathātmanaḥ \|\| 19 \|\|*

Just like a man who nears a mirror perceives his own reflection in it, 19

## \|\| 2.11.20 \|\|

*evaṃ sā vaiṣṇavī śaktirnaivāpaiti tato dvija \|*  
*māsānumāsaṃ bhāsvantamadhyāste tatra saṃsthitam \|\| 20 \|\|*

In that same way, the power of Viṣṇu never leaves the sun, but month by month abides there, brahmin. 20

## \|\| 2.11.21 \|\|

*pitṛdevamanuṣyādīnsa sadāpyāyayanprabhuḥ \|*  
*parivartatyahorātrakāraṇaṃ savitā dvaja \|\| 21 \|\|*

The lordly sun brings night and day and, ever moving, brahmin, wheels above the ancestors and gods and men. 21

## \|\| 2.11.22 \|\|

*sūryaraśmisuṣumṇāyastarpitastena candramāḥ \|*  
*kṛṣṇapakṣemaraiḥ śaśvatpīyate vai sudhāmayaḥ \|\| 22 \|\|*

The solar ray called Suṣumṇa nourishes the moon, but when it wanes, the deities imbibe its nectarine disk. 22

## \|\| 2.11.23 \|\|

*pītaṃ taṃ dvikalaṃ somaṃ kṛṣṇapakṣakṣaye dvija \|*  
*pibanti patarasteṣāṃ bhāskarāttarpamaṃ tathā \|\| 23 \|\|*

Finally, the ancestors drink up the last two slivers of the moon, and thus both gods and ancestors are nurtured by the sun. 23

## \|\| 2.11.24 \|\|

*ādatte raśmibhir yaṃtu kṣitisaṃsthaṃ rasaṃ raviḥ \|*  
*tamutsṛjati bhūtānāṃ puṣṭyarthaṃ sasyavṛddhaye \|\| 24 \|\|*

With its rays, the sun draws moisture from the earth, which falls as rain to prosper beings and nourish crops. 24

## \|\| 2.11.25 \|\|

*tena prīṇātyaśeṣāṇi bhūtāni bhagavānraviḥ \|*  
*pitṛdevamanuṣyādīnevamāpyāyayatyasau \|\| 25 \|\|*

Thus, the lordly sun delights all beings and gives new strength to ancestors, gods and humankind. 25

## \|\| 2.11.26 \|\|

*pakṣatṛptiṃ tu devānāṃ pitṝṇāṃ caiva māsikīm \|*  
*śaśvattṛptiṃ ca martyānāṃ maitreyārkaḥ prayacchati \|\| 26 \|\|*

Satisfying the gods each fortnight and the ancestors each month, the sun provides perpetual nourishment for mortals, Maitreya. 26

## Chapter 12 - The moon and planets

## \|\| 2.12.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*rathastricakraḥ somasya kundābhāstasya vājinaḥ \|*  
*vāmadakṣiṇato yuktā daśatena caratyasau \|\| 1 \|\|*

Parāśara:

The three-wheeled carriage of the moon is drawn by horses, ten in number, white as jasmine, harnessed left and right. 1

## \|\| 2.12.2 \|\|

*vīthyāśrayāṇi ṛkṣāṇi dhruvādhāreṇa veginā \|*  
*hrāsavṛddhikramastasya raśmīnāṃ savituryathā \|\| 2 \|\|*

With that swift carriage, it traverses lunar mansions on the way, ruled by Dhruva, when the reins are loosed or drawn back, just like the sun. 2

## \|\| 2.12.3 \|\|

*arkasyeva hi tasyāśvāḥ sakṛdyuktā vahanti te \|*  
*kalpamekaṃ muniśreṣṭha vārīgarbhasamudbhavāḥ \|\| 3 \|\|*

As with the horses of the sun, the moon’s own steeds, born of water, are harnessed together and draw its chariot for an aeon, best of sages. 3

## \|\| 2.12.4 \|\|

*kṣīṇaṃ pītaṃ suraiḥ somamāpyāyayati dīptimān \|*  
*maitreyaikakalaṃ santaṃ raśminaikena bhāskaraḥ \|\| 4 \|\|*

When the moon, drunk up by gods, has been reduced to just one sliver, the blazing sun replenishes it with a ray, Maitreya. 4

## \|\| 2.12.5 \|\|

*krameṇa yena pīto 'sau devais tena niśākaram \|*  
*āpyāyayatyanudinaṃ bhāskaro vāritaskaraḥ \|\| 5 \|\|*

The deities reduce the moon, who makes the night, by stages as it wanes, but the water-stealing sun restores it day by day. 5

## \|\| 2.12.6 \|\|

*saṃbhṛtaṃ cārdhamāsena tatsomasthaṃ sudhāmṛtam \|*  
*pibandi devā maitreya sudhāhārā yato 'marāḥ \|\| 6 \|\|*

The deities drink nectar, which gathers while it waxes, Maitreya, and with this nectar as their sustenance, they, too, become immortal. 6

## \|\| 2.12.7 \|\|

*trayāstriṃśatsahasrāṇi trayastriṃśacchatāni ca \|*  
*trayastriṃśattathā devāḥ pibanti kṣaṇadākaram \|\| 7 \|\|*

All thirty-six thousand, three hundred and thirty-three gods drink from the night-making moon. 7

## \|\| 2.12.8 \|\|

*kalādvayāviśiṣṭastu praviṣṭaḥ sūryamaṇḍalam \|*  
*amākhyāraśmau vasati amāvāsyā tataḥ smṛtā \|\| 8 \|\|*

When two slivers alone remain, the moon draws near the sun and abides (vasati) in a ray of light called Amā, hence this phase is known as Amāvāsyā. 8

## \|\| 2.12.9 \|\|

*apsu tasminnahorātre pūrvam viśati candramāḥ \|*  
*tato vīrutsu vasati prayātyarkaṃ tataḥ kramāt \|\| 9 \|\|*

During that day and night, the moon first enters the waters and, after dwelling among the plants, draws closer to the sun. 9

## \|\| 2.12.10 \|\|

*chinatti virudho yastu vīrutsaṃsthe niśākare \|*  
*patraṃ vā pātayatyekaṃ brahmahatyāṃ sa vindati \|\| 10 \|\|*

One who cuts a plant while the moon is in that state, or causes a single leaf to fall, is guilty of brahminicide. 10

## \|\| 2.12.11 \|\|

*somaṃ pañcadaśe bhāge kiñcicchiṣṭe kalātmake \|*  
*apārāhne pitṛgaṇājaghanyaṃ paryupāsate \|\| 11 \|\|*

When just one-fifteenth of the moon remains, a host of ancestors attend it in the afternoon. 11

## \|\| 2.12.12 \|\|

*pibanti dvikalākāraṃ śiṣṭā tasya kalā tu yā \|*  
*sudhāmṛtamayī puṇyā tāmindoḥ pitaro mune \|\| 12 \|\|*

And from this moon of two mere slivers, sage, they drink the auspicious nectar of immortality until just one remains. 12

## \|\| 2.12.13 \|\|

*niḥsṛtantadamāvāsyāṃ gabhastibhyaḥ sudhāmṛtam \|*  
*māsaṃ tṛptimavāpyāgryāṃ pitaraḥ santi nirvṛtāḥ \|*  
*saumyā barhiṣadaścaiva agniṣvāttāś ca te tridhā \|\| 13 \|\|*

Having drunk the finest nectar shed by the moon’s own rays at the phase of Amāvāsyā, the ancestors—these Saumyas, Barhiṣads and Agniṣvāttas—are sustained all month and are satisfied. 13

## \|\| 2.12.14 \|\|

*evaṃ devān site pakṣe kṛṣṇapakṣe tathā pitṝn \|*  
*vīrudhaścāmṛtamayaiḥ śītair apparamāṇubhiḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

Thus, the waxing moon sustains the gods and, when it wanes, it succours ancestors and plants with cooling drops of nectar. 14

## \|\| 2.12.15 \|\|

*vīrudhauṣadhiniṣpattyā manuṣyapaśukīṭakān \|*  
*āpyāyayati śītāṃśuḥ prākāśyāhlādanena tu \|\| 15 \|\|*

By bringing forth both plants and herbs, the cool-rayed moon quickens man and beast and creeping things, refreshing them with light. 15

## \|\| 2.12.16 \|\|

*vāyvagnidravyasaṃbhūto rathaścandrasutasya ca \|*  
*piśaṅgais turagair yuktaḥ so 'ṣṭābhir vāyuvegibhiḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

The chariot of Mercury, the moon’s own son, composed of wind and fire, is drawn by eight bay horses, swift as wind itself. 16

## \|\| 2.12.17 \|\|

*savarūthaḥ sānukarṣo yukto bhūsaṃbhavair hayaiḥ \|*  
*sopāsaṃgapatākastu śukrasyāpi ratho mahān \|\| 17 \|\|*

The great chariot of pure Venus, with fender, floor, bench and banner, is drawn by horses born of earth. 17

## \|\| 2.12.18 \|\|

*aṣṭāśvaḥ kāñcanaḥ śrīmānbhaumasyāpi ratho mahān \|*  
*padmarāgāruṇair aśvaiḥ saṃyukto vahnisambhavaiḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

Eight golden horses, ruby-red and fire-born, draw the chariot of Mars, glorious and huge. 18

## \|\| 2.12.19 \|\|

*aṣṭābhiḥ pāṇḍurair yukto vājibhiḥ kāñcanorathaḥ \|*  
*tasmiṃstiṣṭhati varṣānte rāśaurāśau vṛhaspatiḥ \|\| 19 \|\|*

Jupiter rides throughout the year from one star sign to another in a chariot made of gold, drawn by eight steeds, swift and white. 19

## \|\| 2.12.20 \|\|

*ākāśasambhavair aśvaiḥ śabalaiḥ syandanaṃ yutam \|*  
*tamāruhya śanair yāti mandagāmīśanaiścaraḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

Sluggish Saturn moves at leisure on a chariot yoked to dappled horses born of air. 20

## \|\| 2.12.21 \|\|

*svarbhānosturagā hyaṣṭau bhṛṅgābhā dhūsaraṃ ratham \|*  
*sakṛdyuktāstu maitreya vahantyavirataṃ sadā \|\| 21 \|\|*

Eight steeds, black as bees and hitched together, draw the dusky carriage of Svarbhānu, demon of eclipses; they never rest, Maitreya. 21

## \|\| 2.12.22 \|\|

*ādityānniḥsṛto rāhuḥ somaṃ gacchati parvasu \|*  
*ādityameti somācca punaḥ saureṣu parvasu \|\| 22 \|\|*

At times, this demon, also known as Rāhu, leaves the sun to hide the moon, then from the moon he blocks the sun again to bring eclipses. 22

## \|\| 2.12.23 \|\|

*tathā keturathasyāśvā apyaṣṭau vātaraṃhasaḥ \|*  
*palāladhūmavarṇābhā lākṣārasanibhāruṇāḥ \|\| 23 \|\|*

Likewise, the carriage of the shadow-planet Ketu is drawn by horses, eight in number, swift as wind and dark as the smoke of burning straw, or red as cochineal. 23

## \|\| 2.12.24 \|\|

*ete mayā grahāṇāṃ vai tavākhyātā rathā nava \|*  
*sarve dhruve mahābhāga prabaddhā vāyuraśmibhiḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

I’ve described to you the chariots of nine planets, lucky sage, all bound to Dhruva by bonds of air. 24

## \|\| 2.12.25 \|\|

*graharkṣatārādhiṣṇyāni dhruve baddhānyaśeṣataḥ \|*  
*bhramantyucitacāreṇa maitreyānilaraśmibhiḥ \|\| 25 \|\|*

The planets, mansions and constellations, all tied to Dhruva, circle in their proper orbits, restricted by these ties, Maitreya. 25

## \|\| 2.12.26 \|\|

*yāvāntyaścaiva tārāstā tāvanto vātaraśmayaḥ \|*  
*sarve dhruve nibaddhāste bhramanto bhrāmayanti tam \|\| 26 \|\|*

For every star, there is a bond and, as they move, the Pole Star rotates with them. 26

## \|\| 2.12.27 \|\|

*tailapīḍā yathā cakraṃ bhramanto bhrāmayanti vai \|*  
*tathā bhramanti jyotīṃṣi vātaviddhāni sarvaśaḥ \|\| 27 \|\|*

Just as workers pressing oil turn the wheel by pacing round it, similarly, the stars revolve, all bound with aerial bonds. 27

## \|\| 2.12.28 \|\|

*alātacakravadyānti vātacakreritāni tu \|*  
*yasmajyotīṃṣi vahati pravahastena sa smṛtaḥ \|\| 28 \|\|*

The stars turn like a disk of fire driven by a wheel of wind. Because it carries (vahati) the stars, this force is called Pravaha. 28

## \|\| 2.12.29 \|\|

*śiśumārastu yaḥ proktaḥ sa dhruvo yatra tiṣṭhati \|*  
*sanniveśaṃ ca tasyāpi śṛṇuṣva munisattama \|\| 29 \|\|*

The constellation where Dhruva stands is Śiśumāra, as I mentioned. Now I’ll tell you, best of sages, about its stars as well. 29

## \|\| 2.12.30 \|\|

*yadahnā kurute pāpaṃ taṃ dṛṣṭvā niśi mucyate \|*  
*yāvantyaścaiva tārāstāḥ śiśumārāśritā divi \|*  
*tāvantyeva tu varṣāṇi jīvatyabhyadhikānica \|\| 30 \|\|*

If you see it in the evening, you’re freed from misdeeds done by day. As many stars are seen in Śiśumāra in the heavens, that’s how many years you’ll live, or maybe more. 30

## \|\| 2.12.31 \|\|

*uttānapādastasyādho vijñeyo hyuttaro hanuḥ \|*  
*yajño 'dharaś ca vijñeyo dharmo mūrdhānamāśritaḥ \|\| 31 \|\|*

Uttānapāda is thought to be its upper jaw, the lower one is Yajña, while Dharma is standing at its head. 31

## \|\| 2.12.32 \|\|

*hṛdi nārāyaṇaścāste aśvinau pūrvapādayoḥ \|*  
*varuṇaścāryamā caiva paścime tasya sakthinī \|\| 32 \|\|*

At its heart sits Nārāyaṇa, the Aśvins are its two front feet, with Varuṇa and Āryaman behind them. 32

## \|\| 2.12.33 \|\|

*śiśraḥ saṃvatsarastasya mitro 'pānaṃ samāśritaḥ \|\| 33 \|\|*

Saṃvatsara is at Śiśumāra’s penis, with Mitra at its anus. 33

## \|\| 2.12.34 \|\|

*pucchegniś ca mahendraś ca kasyapotha tato dhruvaḥ \|*  
*tārakā śiśumārasya nāstameti catuṣṭayam \|\| 34 \|\|*

At its tail are Agni, Mahendra, Kaśyapa and Dhruva—Śiśumāra’s quartet of stars, which never set. 34

## \|\| 2.12.35 \|\|

*ityeṣa sanniveśo 'yaṃ pṛthivyā jyotiṣāṃ tathā \|*  
*dvīpānāmudadhīnāṃ ca parvatānāṃ ca kīrtitaḥ \|\| 35 \|\|*

I’ve described the nature of the earth, the planets, continents, seas and mountains, 35

## \|\| 2.12.36 \|\|

*varṣāṇāṃ ca nadīnāṃ ca ye ca teṣu vasanti vai \|*  
*teṣāṃ svarūpamākhyātaṃ saṃkṣepaḥ śruyatāṃ punaḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

And lands and rivers, as well as those who dwell there. Now listen while I summarise all this once more. 36

## \|\| 2.12.37 \|\|

*yadambu vaiṣṇavaḥ kāyastato vipra vasundharā \|*  
*padmākārā samudbhūtā parvatābdhyādisaṃyutā \|\| 37 \|\|*

From the waters, Viṣṇu’s body, arose the world in lotus form, brahmin, replete with seas and ranges. 37

## \|\| 2.12.38 \|\|

*jyotīṣi viṣṇurbhuvanāni viṣṇurvanāni viṣṇurgirayo diśaś ca \|*  
*nadyaḥ samudrāś ca sa eva sarvaṃ yadasti yannāsti ca vipravarya \|\| 38 \|\|*

The stars are Viṣṇu, as are the worlds, the forests, lands and mountains, as are streams and oceans. He is all that is and all that isn’t, best of brahmins. 38

## \|\| 2.12.39 \|\|

*jñānasvarūpo bhagavānyatosā vaśeṣamūrtirna tu vastubhūtaḥ \|*  
*tato hi śailabdhidharādibhedāñjānīhi vijñānavijṛmbhitāni \|\| 39 \|\|*

Because the lord is knowledge, and is all forms but has no substance, understand that distinctive forms of mountains, oceans, earth and so on give rise to a misconception. 39

## \|\| 2.12.40 \|\|

*yadā tu śuddhaṃ nijarūpi sarvaṃ karmakṣaye jñānamapāstadoṣam \|*  
*tadā hi saṃkalpataroḥ phalāni bhavanti no vastuṣu vastubhedāḥ \|\| 40 \|\|*

When knowledge is pure, natural, complete, independent of action and free from faults, then the distinction between manifold substances, which are the fruits of the tree of longing, is no more. 40

## \|\| 2.12.41 \|\|

*vastvasti kiṃ kutrācidādimadhyaparyantahīnaṃ satataikarūpam \|*  
*yaccānyathātvaṃ dvija yāti bhūyo na tattathā tatra kuto hi tattvam \|\| 41 \|\|*

What is substance? Where is an entity without beginning, middle or end, and eternally uniform? Where is the reality of that which proceeds again to a state of otherness and doesn’t stay the same, brahmin? 41

## \|\| 2.12.42 \|\|

*mahī ghaṭatvaṃ ghaṭataḥ kapālikā kapālikācūrṇarajastato 'ṇuḥ \|*  
*janaiḥ svakarmastimitātmaniścayair ālakṣyate brūhi kimatra vastu \|\| 42 \|\|*

Clay is shaped into a pot; the pot is broken into pieces; the pieces ground to dust; the dust reduced to atoms. This is clearly seen by folk whose notions are informed by personal experience. But tell me, what is substance? 42

## \|\| 2.12.43 \|\|

*tasmān na vijñānam ṛte 'sti kiṃcit kvacit kadācid dvija vastujātam \|*  
*vijñānam ekaṃ nijakarmabhedavibhinnacitter bahudhābhyupetam \|\| 43 \|\|*

Therefore, except for truest knowledge, there is never anything anywhere, brahmin, that arises from such substances. This knowledge is uniform but appears manifold to those who perceive it as differentiated, as the result of the varied nature of their own actions. 43

## \|\| 2.12.44 \|\|

*jñānaṃ viśuddhaṃ vimalaṃ viśo kamaśeṣalobhādinirastasaṅgam \|*  
*ekaṃ sadaikaṃ paramaḥ sa vāsudevo na yato 'nyadasti \|\| 44 \|\|*

Knowledge is pure, unblemished, painless, free from the influence of greed and other faults. It’s uniform, ever one, supreme, the highest lord. It’s Vāsudeva, other than whom no thing exists. 44

## \|\| 2.12.45 \|\|

*sadbhāva evaṃ bhavato mayokto jñānaṃ yathā satyamastayatyamanyat \|*  
*etattu yatsaṃvyavahārabhūtaṃ tatrāpi coktaṃ bhuvanāśritaṃ te \|\| 45 \|\|*

I’ve explained this verity to you: this knowledge is truth, all else is false and I’ve spoken of the mundane aspects of the world. 45

## \|\| 2.12.46 \|\|

*yajñaḥ paśurvahniraśeṣaṛtviksomaḥ surāḥ svargamayaś ca kāmaḥ \|*  
*ityādikarmāśritamārgadṛṣṭaṃ bhūrādibhogāś ca phalāni teṣām \|\| 46 \|\|*

I’ve shown the sacrifice to you, the beasts, the fire, the ministrants, the soma, sacrificial liquor and the wish for heaven—all these and other aspects related to the rites—and all the worlds and pleasures that arise therefrom. 46

## \|\| 2.12.47 \|\|

*yaccaitadbhuvanagataṃ mayā tavoktaṃ sarvatra vrajati hi tatra karmavaśyaḥ \|*  
*jñātvaivaṃ dhruvamacalaṃ sadaikarūpaṃ tatkuryādviśati hi yena vāsudevam \|\| 47 \|\|*

Driven by one’s actions, a being moves alone among the worlds that I’ve described to you. Knowing Vāsudeva to be firm, unmoving and unchanging, one may still do his duty and, doing so, may enter the divine. 47

## Chapter 13 - ‘Foolish’ Bharata

## \|\| 2.13.1 \|\|

*maitreya uvāca*  
*bhagavansamyagākhyātaṃ yatpṛṣṭosi mayā kila \|*  
*bhūsamudrādisaritāṃ saṃsthānaṃ grahasaṃ sthitiḥ \|\| 1 \|\|*

Master, you’ve given me the perfect answer to my question on the nature of the earth, the oceans, rivers and the planets, 1

## \|\| 2.13.2 \|\|

*viṣṇvādhāraṃ yathā caitattrailokyaṃ samavasthitam \|*  
*paramārthastu te prokto yathājñānaṃ pradhānataḥ \|\| 2 \|\|*

And the system of the threefold worlds, with Viṣṇu as their foundation. You’ve described to me the highest goal of life and knowledge, because of their significance. 2

## \|\| 2.13.3 \|\|

*yattvetadbhagavānāha bharatasya mahīpateḥ \|*  
*śrotumicchami caritaṃ tanmamākhyātumarhasi \|\| 3 \|\|*

As you said you’d speak about the deeds of Bharata the king, please do so now. 3

## \|\| 2.13.4 \|\|

*bharataḥ sa mahīpālaḥ sālagrāme 'vasatkila \|*  
*yogayuktaḥ samādhāya vāsudeve sadā manaḥ \|\| 4 \|\|*

King Bharata dwelled at Sālagrāma, intent on yoga, his heart ever set on Vāsudeva. 4

## \|\| 2.13.5 \|\|

*puṇyedeśaprabhāvena dhyāyataś ca sadā harim \|*  
*kathaṃ tu nābhavanmuktiryadabhūtsa dvijaḥ punaḥ \|\| 5 \|\|*

With the power of that sacred place and his unbroken thoughts of Hari, why was he not liberated, but reborn a brahmin? 5

## \|\| 2.13.6 \|\|

*vipratve ca kṛtaṃ tena yadbhūyaḥ sumahātmanā \|*  
*bharatena muniśreṣṭha tatsarvaṃ vaktumarhasi \|\| 6 \|\|*

Please tell me what that illustrious being achieved when, driven by his previous karma, he was born again in that community. 6

## \|\| 2.13.7 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*sālagrāme mahābhāgo bhagavannyastamānasaḥ \|*  
*sa uvāsa ciraṃ kālaṃ maitreya pṛtivīpatiḥ \|\| 7 \|\|*

In Sālagrāma, that pious king reposed for many long years, Maitreya, his heart fixed on the lord. 7

## \|\| 2.13.8 \|\|

*ahiṃsādiṣvaśeṣeṣu guṇeṣu guṇināṃ varaḥ \|*  
*avāpa paramāṃ kāṣṭhāṃ manasaścāpi saṃyame \|\| 8 \|\|*

He was the best of worthy men. While shunning harm, he clung to virtue, having reached perfection in the mastery of his mind. 8

## \|\| 2.13.9 \|\|

*yajñeśācyuta govinda mādhavānanta keśava \|*  
*kṛṣṇa viṣṇo hṛṣīkeśa vāsudeva namostu te \|\| 9 \|\|*

The names ‘Viṣṇu’, ‘Yajñeśa’, ‘Acyuta’, ‘Govinda’, ‘Mādhava’, ‘Ananta’, ‘Keśava’, ‘Kṛṣṇa’ and ‘Hṛṣīkeśa’ were the only words he ever uttered. 9

## \|\| 2.13.10 \|\|

*iti rājāha bharato harernāmāni kevalam \|*  
*nānyajjagāda maitreya kiñcitsvaprāntarepi ca \|*  
*etatpadantadarthaṃ ca vinā nānyadacintayat \|\| 10 \|\|*

He spoke no others, Maitreya, even in his dreams, and thought of nothing but these names and their significance. 10

## \|\| 2.13.11 \|\|

*samitpuṣpakuśādānaṃ cakre devakriyākṛte \|*  
*nānyāni cakre karmāṇi niḥsaṅgo yogatāpasaḥ \|\| 11 \|\|*

Accepting gifts of kindling, flowers and kuśa-grass so he could worship the divine, he shunned all other action, unattached, intent on his austerities and yoga. 11

## \|\| 2.13.12 \|\|

*jagāma sobhiṣekārthamekadā tu mahānadīm \|*  
*sasnau tatra tadā cakre snānasyānantarakriyāḥ \|\| 12 \|\|*

One day, he reached a mighty river to purify himself and, having bathed, undertook the rites that follow such ablutions. 12

## \|\| 2.13.13 \|\|

*athājagāma tattīraṃ jalaṃ pātuṃ pipāsitā \|*  
*āsannaprasavā brahmannekaiva hariṇī vanāt \|\| 13 \|\|*

And to that sacred bathing place there came from the woods a pregnant doe, thirsting for some water, brahmin. 13

## \|\| 2.13.14 \|\|

*tataḥ samabhavattatra pītaprāye jale tathā \|*  
*siṃhasya nādaḥ sumahānsarvaprāṇibhayaṅkaraḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

But while she drank, she heard the roaring of a lion, which frightens every living thing. 14

## \|\| 2.13.15 \|\|

*tataḥ sā sahasā trāsādāplutā nimnagātaṭam \|*  
*atyuccārohaṇenāsyā nadyāṃ garbhaḥ papāta ha \|\| 15 \|\|*

The startled doe leapt from the water to the shore, but the violence of her movement forced the infant from her womb, and it fell into the river. 15

## \|\| 2.13.16 \|\|

*tamūhyamānaṃ vegena vīcimālāpariplutam \|*  
*jagrāha sa nṛpo garbhātpatitaṃ mṛgapotakam \|\| 16 \|\|*

Carried by the current and sinking beneath the waves, the miscarried fawn was fetched up by the king. 16

## \|\| 2.13.17 \|\|

*garbhapracyutidoṣeṇa prottuṅgākramaṇena ca \|*  
*maitreya sāpi hariṇī papāta ca mamāra ca \|\| 17 \|\|*

With the pain of her miscarriage and the violence of her leap, Maitreya, the doe fell down and died. 17

## \|\| 2.13.18 \|\|

*hariṇīṃ tāṃ vilokyātha vipannāṃ tṛpatāpasaḥ \|*  
*mṛgapotaṃ samādāya nijamāśramamāgataḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

Seeing that its dam had perished, the ascetic king took up the fawn and bore it to his ashram. 18

## \|\| 2.13.19 \|\|

*cakārānudinaṃ cāsau mṛgapotasya vai nṛpaḥ \|*  
*poṣaṇaṃ puṣyamāṇaś ca sa tena vavṛdhe mune \|\| 19 \|\|*

Every day he fed that fawn, sage, and, nurtured by the king, it grew and grew. 19

## \|\| 2.13.20 \|\|

*cacārāśramaparyante tṛṇāni gahaneṣu saḥ \|*  
*dvaraṃ gatvā ca śārdulatrāsādabhyāyayau punaḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

The young deer grazed on grass in thickets near the ashram, but even when it strayed afar, the fear of tigers drove it back. 20

## \|\| 2.13.21 \|\|

*prātargatvātidūraṃ ca sāyamāyātyathāśramam \|*  
*punaś ca bharatasyabhūdāśramasyoṭajājire \|\| 21 \|\|*

Having wandered out at dawn and returning in the dusk, it could be found as usual in the thatched byre of Bharata’s retreat. 21

## \|\| 2.13.22 \|\|

*tasya tasminmṛge dūrasamīpaparivartini \|*  
*āsīccetaḥ samāsaktaṃ na yayāvanyato dvija \|\| 22 \|\|*

The king was always worried when the deer strayed near and far, brahmin, and thought of nothing else. 22

## \|\| 2.13.23 \|\|

*vimuktarājyatanayaḥ projjhitāśeṣabāndhavaḥ \|*  
*mamatvaṃ sa cakāroccais tasminhariṇabālake \|\| 23 \|\|*

Free from his kingdom and his sons and free from all his kinsfolk, he now became possessive of that fawn in high degree. 23

## \|\| 2.13.24 \|\|

*kiṃ vṛkair bhakṣito vyaghraiḥ kiṃ siṃhena nipātitaḥ \|*  
*cirāyamāṇe niṣkrānte tasyāsīditi mānasam \|\| 24 \|\|*

‘Perhaps it’s fallen prey to wolves or tigers or been taken by a lion’—such were his worries when the deer was gone for long. 24

## \|\| 2.13.25 \|\|

*eṣā vasumatī tasya khurāgrakṣatakarburā \|\| 25 \|\|*

‘This fawn, dotting the earth with hoofprints, 25

## \|\| 2.13.26 \|\|

*prītaye mama jātosau kva mamaiṇakabālakaḥ \|*  
*viṣāmāgroṇa madbāhuṃ kaṇḍūyanaparo hi saḥ \|*  
*kṣemeṇābhyāgatoraṇyādapi māṃ sukhayiṣyati \|\| 26 \|\|*

Has brought me joy since the day that he arrived. But where is he now? He loves to scratch my arm with his antler tip. I’ll be so happy when he’s safely home from roaming in the forest. 26

## \|\| 2.13.27 \|\|

*ete lūnaśikhāstasya daśanair acirodgataiḥ \|*  
*kuśāḥ kāśā virājante baṭavaḥ sāmāgā iva \|\| 27 \|\|*

With his young teeth, he’s nibbled off the tips of kuśa-grass so they resemble brahmin boys who chant the Sāma Veda.’ 27

## \|\| 2.13.28 \|\|

*itthaṃ ciragate tasminsa cakre mānasaṃ muniḥ \|*  
*prītiprasannavadanaḥ pārśvasthe cābhavanmṛge \|\| 28 \|\|*

Such worries filled the sage’s mind when the fawn was overdue, but his face would shine with pleasure whenever it was near. 28

## \|\| 2.13.29 \|\|

*samādhibhaṅgastasyāsīttanmayatvādṛtātmanaḥ \|*  
*santyaktarājyabhogardhisvajanasyāpi bhūpateḥ \|\| 29 \|\|*

The king, who shunned his kingdom, pleasures, wealth and kin, now lost his peace of mind as his heart was filled with worry. 29

## \|\| 2.13.30 \|\|

*capalaṃ capale tasmindūragaṃ dūragāmini \|*  
*mṛgapote 'bhavaccittaṃ stairyavattasya bhūpateḥ \|\| 30 \|\|*

That mind, once firm, began to wander and grew distant when the fawn strayed far from home. 30

## \|\| 2.13.31 \|\|

*kālena gacchatā so 'tha kālaṃ cakre mahīpatiḥ \|*  
*piteva sāstraṃ putreṇa mṛgapotena vīkṣitaḥ \|\| 31 \|\|*

In time, the sovereign’s death drew near, and that young deer watched over him, just like a tearful son who sees his sire’s demise. 31

## \|\| 2.13.32 \|\|

*mṛgameva tadādrākṣīttyajanprāṇānasāvapi \|*  
*tanmayatvena maitreya nānyatkiñcidacintayat \|\| 32 \|\|*

As he drew his final breath, the king beheld the deer and, being still obsessed with it, he thought of nothing else. 32

## \|\| 2.13.33 \|\|

*tataś ca tatkālakṛtāṃ bhāvanāṃ prāpya tādṛśīm \|*  
*jambūmārge mahāraṇye jāto jātismaromṛgaḥ \|\| 33 \|\|*

In that state of mind when the time of death arrived, the king was born a deer in the expansive forest of Jambūmārga, but he could still recall his former life. 33

## \|\| 2.13.34 \|\|

*jātismaratvadudvignaḥ saṃsārasya dvijottama \|*  
*vihāya mātaraṃ bhūyaḥ sālagrāmamupāyayo \|\| 34 \|\|*

Because of this ability and troubled by saṃsāra, best of brahmins, the fawn now left his mother and returned to Sālagrāma. 34

## \|\| 2.13.35 \|\|

*śuṣkais tṛṇais tathā parṇaiḥ sa kurvann ātmapoṣaṇam \|*  
*mṛgatvahetubhūtasya karmaṇo niṣkṛtiṃ yayau \|\| 35 \|\|*

Living on dry grass and leaves, he atoned for deeds that caused his birth in the body of a deer. 35

## \|\| 2.13.36 \|\|

*tatra cotsṛṣṭadeho 'sau jajñe jātismaro dvijaḥ \|*  
*sadācāravatāṃśuddhe yogināṃ pravare kule \|\| 36 \|\|*

He perished there but was born a brahmin in a pure and lofty family of virtuous ascetics, yet still recalled his previous existence. 36

## \|\| 2.13.37 \|\|

*sarvavijñānasaṃpannaḥ sarvaśāstrarthatattvavit \|*  
*apaśyatya ca maitreya ātmānaṃ prakṛteḥ param \|\| 37 \|\|*

Having mastered every branch of learning and knowing the true purport of all the sacred texts, he saw that ātman, the Self, Maitreya, lay beyond prakṛti, matter. 37

## \|\| 2.13.38 \|\|

*ātmano 'dhigatajñāno devādīni mahāmune \|*  
*sarvabhūtānyabhedena sa dadarśa tadātmanaḥ \|\| 38 \|\|*

Knowing well the nature of the Self, that wise man viewed all creatures, deities and the rest as essentially the same, great sage. 38

## \|\| 2.13.39 \|\|

*na papāṭha guruproktaṃ kṛtopanayanaḥ śrutim \|*  
*na dadarśa ca karmāṇi śāstrāṇi jagṛhe na ca \|\| 39 \|\|*

But once invested with the sacred thread, he took no part in Vedic recitation as instructed by his elders, nor witnessed any sacrifice, nor studied any text. 39

## \|\| 2.13.40 \|\|

*uktopi bahuśaḥ kiñcijjaḍavākyamabhāṣata \|*  
*tadapyasaṃskāraguṇaṃ grāmyavākyoktisaṃśritam \|\| 40 \|\|*

Whenever he was spoken to, he’d answer with some foolish quip and, like some country bumpkin, his speech was un-Sanskritic. 40

## \|\| 2.13.41 \|\|

*apadhvastavapuḥ so 'pi malināmbaragdvijaḥ \|*  
*klinnadantāntaraḥ sarvaiḥ paribhūtaḥ sa nāgaraiḥ \|\| 41 \|\|*

This brahmin failed to groom himself, his clothes were vile, saliva dribbled down his chin and all the townsfolk shunned him. 41

## \|\| 2.13.42 \|\|

*saṃmānanāparāṃ hāniṃ yogardhaḥ kurute yataḥ \|*  
*janenāvamato yogī yogasiddhaṃ ca vindati \|\| 42 \|\|*

‘Praise does great harm to those of self-restraint whose wealth is yoga. Such ascetics, despised by folk, should seek success through practice. 42

## \|\| 2.13.43 \|\|

*tasmāccareta vai yogī satāṃ dharmamadūṣayan \|*  
*janā yathā vamanyerangaccheyurnaiva saṅgatim \|\| 43 \|\|*

Ascetics must therefore train without offending the virtue of the wise. Whenever folk abuse them, let them walk away.’ 43

## \|\| 2.13.44 \|\|

*hiraṇyagarbhavacanaṃ vicintyetthaṃ mahāmatiḥ \|*  
*ātmānaṃ darśayāmāsa jaḍonmattākṛtiṃ jane \|\| 44 \|\|*

Recalling these words of Brahmā, born of the golden egg, that prudent being appeared to others to be a foolish madman. 44

## \|\| 2.13.45 \|\|

*bhukte kulmāṣavāṭyādiśākaṃ vanyaṃ phalaṃ kaṇān \|*  
*yadyadāpnoti subahu tadatte kālasaṃyamam \|\| 45 \|\|*

He ate half-ripe barley, raw beans and other greens, as well as forest seeds and berries—whatever came to his fair hand at any time. 45

## \|\| 2.13.46 \|\|

*pitaryuparate so 'tha bhrātṛbhrātṛvyabāndhavaiḥ \|*  
*kāritaḥ kṣetrakarmādikadannāhārapoṣitaḥ \|\| 46 \|\|*

When his father passed away, his brothers, cousins and other kin sent him to the fields to work, with little food to succour him. 46

## \|\| 2.13.47 \|\|

*samākṣapīnāvayavo jaḍakārī ca karmaṇi \|*  
*sarvalokopakaraṇaṃ babhūvāhāracetanaḥ \|\| 47 \|\|*

Though he seemed a simpleton, his limbs proved tough while labouring, and so he helped out other folk, receiving food as wages. 47

## \|\| 2.13.48 \|\|

*taṃ tādṛśamasaṃskāraṃ viprākṛtiviceṣṭitam \|*  
*kṣattāsauvīrarājasya kālyai paśumakalpayat \|\| 48 \|\|*

Now, the chamberlain of the king of Sauvīra thought this man a fool who just effected the appearance of a brahmin, but who could provide free labour. 48

## \|\| 2.13.49 \|\|

*rātrau taṃ samalaṅkṛtya vaiśasasya vidhānataḥ \|*  
*adhiṣṭhitaṃ mahākālī jñātvā yogeśvaraṃ tathā \|\| 49 \|\|*

The king, riding in his palanquin, wished to visit Kapila’s retreat, that best of sages, on the banks of the Ikṣumatī River, brahmin. 49

## \|\| 2.13.50 \|\|

*tataḥ khaṅgaṃ samādāya niśitaṃ niśi sā tathā \|*  
*kṣattāraṃ krūrakarmāṇamacchinatkaṇṭhamūlataḥ \|*  
*svapārṣadayutā devī papau rudhiramulbaṇam \|\| 50 \|\|*

He sought to ask that learned sage, who understood the dharma of liberation, about the best course for humankind in this world of suffering. 50

## \|\| 2.13.51 \|\|

*tataḥsauvīrarājasya prayātasya mahātmanaḥ \|*  
*viṣṭakartātha manyeta viṣṭiyogyoyamityapi \|\| 51 \|\|*

The brahmin, along with others who, at the chamberlain’s command, had been pressed into service, now bore the royal carriage. 51

## \|\| 2.13.52 \|\|

*taṃ tādṛśaṃ mahātmānaṃ bhasmacchannamivānalam \|*  
*kṣattā sauvīrarājasya viṣṭiyogyamamanyata \|\| 52 \|\|*

Forced to work, the brahmin, who alone had grasped all understanding, recalled his previous existence and bore the palanquin, desiring to atone for past misdeeds. 52

## \|\| 2.13.53 \|\|

*sa rājā śibikārūḍho gantuṃ kṛtamatirdvija \|*  
*babhūvekṣumatītīre kapilarṣer varāśramam \|\| 53 \|\|*

His eyes fixed solely on the pole, the wisest of the wise just stumbled on, while all the others walked in step. 53

## \|\| 2.13.54 \|\|

*śreyaḥ kimatra saṃsāre duḥkhaprāye nṛṇāmiti \|*  
*praṣṭuṃ taṃ mokṣadharmajñaṃ kapilākhyaṃ mahāmunim \|\| 54 \|\|*

Seeing that the palanquin was moving so unevenly, the king called out, ‘What’s going on? You bearers should all walk in step!’ 54

## \|\| 2.13.55 \|\|

*uvāha śibikāṃ tasya kṣatturvacanacoditaḥ \|*  
*nṛṇāṃ viṣṭigṛhītānāmanyeṣāṃ so 'pi madhyagaḥ \|\| 55 \|\|*

But noting no improvement, the king then laughed and cried aloud, ‘What now? You’re still all out of kilter!’ 55

## \|\| 2.13.56 \|\|

*gṛhīto viṣṭinā vipraḥ sarvajñānaikabhājanam \|*  
*jātismarosau papāsya kṣayakāma uvāha tām \|\| 56 \|\|*

Hearing the sovereign’s new complaint, the carriers replied, ‘There’s a fellow here who’s much too slow.’ 56

## \|\| 2.13.57 \|\|

*yayau jaḍamatiḥ sotha mṛgamātrāvalokanam \|*  
*kurvanmatimatāṃ śraṣṭhastadanye tvaritaṃ yayuḥ \|\| 57 \|\|*

The king:

How can you be tired? You haven’t borne my palanquin that far. Can’t you take the weight? You look strong enough to me. 57

## \|\| 2.13.58 \|\|

*vilokya nṛpatiḥ sotha viṣamāṃ śibikāgatim \|*  
*kimetadityāha samaṃ gamyatāṃ śibikāvahāḥ \|\| 58 \|\|*

The brahmin:

It isn’t me who’s weak, nor me who bears your palanquin. It isn’t me who is fatigued, nor me who takes its weight, your majesty. 58

## \|\| 2.13.59 \|\|

*punastathaiva śibikaṃ vilokya viṣamāṃ hi saḥ \|*  
*nṛpaḥ kimetadityāha bhavadbhir gamyatenyathā \|\| 59 \|\|*

The king:

I see myself that you are strong, and even now you bear my carriage. Everyone is subject to fatigue when shouldering a burden. 59

## \|\| 2.13.60 \|\|

*bhūpatervadatastasya śrutvetthaṃbahuśo vacaḥ \|*  
*śibikāvāhakāḥ projurayaṃ yātītyasatvaram \|\| 60 \|\|*

The brahmin:

Please state exactly what you see in me, your majesty, as only then is it possible to speak of strong and weak. 60

## \|\| 2.13.61 \|\|

*rājovāca*  
*kiṃ śrintosyalpamadhvānaṃ tvayoḍhā śibikā mama \|*  
*kimāyā sasaho na tvaṃ pīvānasi nirīkṣyase \|\| 61 \|\|*

You just said I’m carrying your palanquin, but that is not the case. Please listen while I explain. 61

## \|\| 2.13.62 \|\|

*brāhmaṇa uvāca*  
*nāhaṃ pīvā na caivoḍhā śibikā bhavato mayā \|*  
*na śrāntosmi na cāyāso soḍhavyosti mahīpate \|\| 62 \|\|*

My feet are on the ground, and they support my shins. My shins uphold my thighs, and they in turn hold up my belly. 62

## \|\| 2.13.63 \|\|

*rājovāca*  
*pratyakṣaṃ dṛśyase pīvānadyāpi śibikā tvayi \|*  
*śramaś ca bhārodvahane bhavatyeva hi dehinām \|\| 63 \|\|*

My belly supports my chest, my arms and shoulders. As my shoulders bear this palanquin, how can you say the load is mine alone? 63

## \|\| 2.13.64 \|\|

*brahmaṇa uvāca*  
*pratyakṣaṃ bhavatā bhūpa yaddṛṣṭaṃ mama tadvada \|*  
*balavānabalaśceti vācyaṃ paścādviśeṣaṇam \|\| 64 \|\|*

The body sitting on the chair is defined as ‘you’, but it’s not right to say that you are there and I am here. 64

## \|\| 2.13.65 \|\|

*tvayoḍhā śibikā ceti tvayyadyāpi ca saṃsthitā \|*  
*mithyaitadatra tu bhavāñchṛṇotu vacanaṃ mama \|\| 65 \|\|*

Both you and I and all the others are composed of elements, your majesty, and this mass of elements becomes a body, determined by the guṇas, the stream of qualities. 65

## \|\| 2.13.66 \|\|

*bhūmau pādayugaṃ tvāste jaṅghe pādadvaye sthite \|*  
*ūrvorjaṅghādvayāvasthau tadādhāraṃ tathodaram \|\| 66 \|\|*

These qualities—namely, purity and the others—are subject to one’s karma, sire. And karma, accumulated in ignorance, affects all beings without exception. 66

## \|\| 2.13.67 \|\|

*vakṣasthalaṃ tathā bāhū skandhau codarasaṃsthitau \|*  
*skandhāśriteyaṃ śibikā mama bhārotra kiṃ kṛtaḥ \|\| 67 \|\|*

The Self—the ātman—is pure and indestructible, tranquil, lacking qualities and transcends materiality. It does not wax or wane but is the same in every being. 67

## \|\| 2.13.68 \|\|

*śibikāyāṃ sthitaṃ cedaṃ vapustvadupalakṣitam \|*  
*tatra tvamahamapyatra projyate cedamanyathā \|\| 68 \|\|*

Since it’s beyond decay and growth, your majesty, on what grounds say you that I am strong? 68

## \|\| 2.13.69 \|\|

*ahaṃ tvaṃ ca tathānye ca bhūtair uhyāma parthiva \|*  
*guṇapravāhapatito bhūtavargaupi yātyayam \|\| 69 \|\|*

This palanquin is borne on shoulders that depend on earth and feet, on shins and thighs, on hips and bellies and the rest, and so this weight is shared by all, including you. 69

## \|\| 2.13.70 \|\|

*karmavaśyā guṇaścaite sattvādyāḥ pṛthivīpate \|*  
*avidyāsaṃcitaṃ karma taccāseṣeṣu jantuṣu \|\| 70 \|\|*

Similarly, sire, it’s not just other entities that bear your carriage, as mountains, trees and houses all rest upon the earth as well. 70

## \|\| 2.13.71 \|\|

*ātmā śuddhokṣaraḥ śānto nirguṇaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ \|*  
*pravṛddhyapacayau nāsya ekasyākhilajantuṣu \|\| 71 \|\|*

If people’s natures were unalike or differed for given reasons, your majesty, then I’d agree this load was borne by one or other person. 71

## \|\| 2.13.72 \|\|

*yadā nopacayastasya na caivāpacayo nṛpa \|*  
*tadā pīvānasītītthaṃ kayā yuktyā tvayeritam \|\| 72 \|\|*

But the stuff that forms this chair comprises you and me and other things and is composed of elements that are moulded by a sense of self. 72

## \|\| 2.13.73 \|\|

*bhūpādajaṅghākaṭyūrujaṭharādiṣu saṃsthite \|*  
*śibikeyaṃ yathā skandhe tathā bhāraḥ samastvayā \|\| 73 \|\|*

Parāśara:

Having spoken, the brahmin bore the palanquin in silence, but the king climbed down and hastened to touch his feet. 73

## \|\| 2.13.74 \|\|

*tathānyair jantubhir bhūpa śibikoḍhā na kevalam \|*  
*śailadrumagṛhotthopi pṛthivī saṃbhavopi vā \|\| 74 \|\|*

The king:

I salute you, brahmin! Forget about the palanquin. I have a favour to ask of you. Pray tell me who you really are, appearing here in such disreputable form? 74

## \|\| 2.13.75 \|\|

*yadā puṃsaḥ sṛthagbhāvaḥ prākṛtaiḥ kāraṇernṛpa \|*  
*soḍhavyastu tadāyāsaḥ kathaṃ vā nṛpate mayā \|\| 75 \|\|*

Why are you like this and why have you come? Tell me all, wise man. I wish to hear the reason. 75

## \|\| 2.13.76 \|\|

*yaddravyā śibikā ceyaṃ taddravyo bhūtasaṃgrahaḥ \|*  
*bhavato me 'kilasyāsya mamatvenopabṛṃhitaḥ \|\| 76 \|\|*

The brahmin:

Listen well, your majesty, but I cannot tell you who I am. The act of going anywhere is for the sake of some experience. 76

## \|\| 2.13.77 \|\|

*śrīpāraśara uvāca*  
*evamuktvābhavanmaunī sa vahañchibikāṃ dvija \|*  
*so 'pi rājāvatīryovyāṃ tatpādau jagṛhe tvaran \|\| 77 \|\|*

Pain and pleasure are the source of everybody and all else. A living thing assumes a body to undergo the karmic fruit of vice and virtue. 77

## \|\| 2.13.78 \|\|

*rājovāca*  
*bhobhovisṛjya śibikāṃ prasādaṃ kuru me dvijaḥ \|*  
*kathyatāṃ ko bhavānatra jālmarūpadharaḥ sthitaḥ \|\| 78 \|\|*

Since vice and virtue are the cause of everything for every being, sire, why ask about the reason that I’m here? 78

## \|\| 2.13.79 \|\|

*yo bhavānyannimittaṃ vā yadāgamanakāraṇam \|*  
*tatsarvaṃ kathyatāṃ vidvanmahyaṃ śuśrūṣave tvayā \|\| 79 \|\|*

The king:

Vice and virtue are indeed the cause of all events, and beings appear in place to suffer their effects. 79

## \|\| 2.13.80 \|\|

*brahmaṇa uvāca*  
*śrūyatāṃ sohamityetadvaktuṃ bhūpa na śakyate \|*  
*upabhoganimittaṃ ca sarvatrāgamanakriyā \|\| 80 \|\|*

But this is what I’d like to hear: Why claim you cannot tell me who you are? 80

## \|\| 2.13.81 \|\|

*sukhaduḥkhopabhogau tu tau dehādyupapādakau \|*  
*dharmādharmodbhavau bhoktuṃ janturdehādimṛcchati \|\| 81 \|\|*

How can it be impossible for one to describe himself? The word ‘I’ can’t harm you, brahmin. 81

## \|\| 2.13.82 \|\|

*sarvasyaiva hi bhūpāla jantoḥ sarvatra kāraṇam \|*  
*dharmādharmau yataḥ kasmātkāraṇaṃ pṛcchate tvayā \|\| 82 \|\|*

The brahmin:

It’s true the word can do no harm, but the very term betrays the fault of seeing a ‘self’ in something that is without it. 82

## \|\| 2.13.83 \|\|

*rājovāca*  
*dharmādharmau na sandehaḥsarvakāryeṣu kāraṇam \|*  
*upabhoganimittaṃ ca dehāddehāntarāgamaḥ \|\| 83 \|\|*

The tongue, the teeth, the lips and palate utter this word ‘I’, your majesty. Yet none of these is ‘I’, as they are just the means by which the sound is made. 83

## \|\| 2.13.84 \|\|

*yastvetadbhavatā proktaṃ sohamityetadātmanaḥ \|*  
*vaktuṃ na sakyate śrotuṃ tanmamecchā pravartate \|\| 84 \|\|*

If speech itself just forms this word by means of these same organs, it’s wrong to claim that speech is ‘I’. 84

## \|\| 2.13.85 \|\|

*yosti sohamiti brahmankathaṃ vaktuṃ na śakyate \|*  
*ātmanyeṣa na doṣāya śabdohamiti yo dvija \|\| 85 \|\|*

Since a person’s body is made of many parts—the head, the hands and all the rest—to which of these does ‘I’ apply, your majesty? 85

## \|\| 2.13.86 \|\|

*brāhmaṇa uvāca*  
*śabdohamiti doṣāya nātmanyeṣa tathaiva tat \|*  
*anātmanyātmavijñānaṃ śabdo vā bhrāntilakṣaṇaḥ \|\| 86 \|\|*

If another man is different from me, best of kings, then it may be said that ‘I am this’ and ‘He’s the other’. 86

## \|\| 2.13.87 \|\|

*jihvā bravītyahamiti dantoṣṭhau tāluke nṛpa \|*  
*etenāhaṃ yataḥ sarve vāṅniṣpādanahetavaḥ \|\| 87 \|\|*

But when a single primal spirit—puṃs—pervades all bodies, it’s vain to ask the questions, ‘Who are you?’ and ‘Who am I?’ 87

## \|\| 2.13.88 \|\|

*kiṃ hetubhir vadatyeṣā vāgevāhamiti svayam \|*  
*ataḥ pīvānasītyetad vaktum itthaṃ na yujyate \|\| 88 \|\|*

You’re the king and this the palanquin. We’re the bearers who attend you and this your realm—but none of this is really true, your majesty. 88

## \|\| 2.13.89 \|\|

*piṇḍaḥ pṛthagyataḥ puṃsaḥ śiraḥ pāṇyādilakṣaṇaḥ \|*  
*tatohamiti kutraitāṃ saṃjñāṃ rājankaromyaham \|\| 89 \|\|*

The palanquin on which you ride was made of wood from certain trees. To which does ‘tree’ apply, your majesty, or is it ‘wood’? 89

## \|\| 2.13.90 \|\|

*yadyanyosti paraḥ kopi mattaḥ pārthivasattama \|*  
*tadaiṣohamayaṃ cānyo vaktumevamapīṣyate \|\| 90 \|\|*

When you’re seated on the palanquin, none would say the king is riding on a tree or on a piece of timber. 90

## \|\| 2.13.91 \|\|

*yadā samastadeheṣu pumāneko vyavasthitaḥ \|*  
*tadā hi ko bhavānsohamityetadviphalaṃ vacaḥ \|\| 91 \|\|*

The palanquin is an assembly of wood, created in the process of manufacture. Can you see a distinction between the wood and the palanquin, best of kings? 91

## \|\| 2.13.92 \|\|

*tvaṃ rājā śibikā ceyamime vāhāḥ puraḥ sarāḥ \|*  
*ayaṃ ca bhavato loko na sadetannṛpocyate \|\| 92 \|\|*

Similarly, consider now the separate existence of an umbrella’s ribs. Whence does the umbrella arise? With this in mind, the same idea applies to you and me. 92

## \|\| 2.13.93 \|\|

*vṛkṣāddāru tataśceyaṃ śibikā tvadadhiṣṭhitā \|*  
*kiṃ vṛkṣasaṃjñā vāsyāḥ syāddārusaṃjñātha vā nṛpa \|\| 93 \|\|*

Man, woman, cow, horse, elephant, bird and tree—such names are commonly assigned to things, which, we must understand, arise because of karma. 93

## \|\| 2.13.94 \|\|

*vṛkṣārūḍho mahārājo nāyaṃ vadati te janaḥ \|*  
*na ca dāruṇi sarvastvāṃ bravītiśibikāgatam \|\| 94 \|\|*

The primal spirit is neither god, nor man, nor beast, nor tree. These differences are merely in the shape of forms, your majesty, caused by prior actions. 94

## \|\| 2.13.95 \|\|

*śibikādārusaṃghāto racanāsthitisaṃsthitaḥ \|*  
*anviṣyatāṃ nṛpaśreṣṭha tadbhede śibikā tvayā \|\| 95 \|\|*

That entity which folk call ‘king’, that which is the ‘soldier of the king’ and similarly all else, sire, lack their own reality, yet arise in our conceptions. 95

## \|\| 2.13.96 \|\|

*evaṃ chatraśalākānāṃ pṛthagbhāve vimṛśyatām \|*  
*kva yātaṃ chatramityetannyāyastvayi tathā mayi \|\| 96 \|\|*

What entity, subject to varied changes, isn’t known by other names in time, your majesty? 96

## \|\| 2.13.97 \|\|

*pumāṃstrīgaurajo vājī kuñjaro vihagastaruḥ \|*  
*deheṣulokasaṃjñeyaṃ vijñeyā karmahetuṣu \|\| 97 \|\|*

You’re the king of all your realm, your father’s son, your rival’s foe, the husband of your wife and father of your son, sire. Who should I say you truly are? 97

## \|\| 2.13.98 \|\|

*pumānna devo na naro na paśurna ca pādapaḥ \|*  
*śarīrākṛtibhedāstu bhūpaite karmayonayaḥ \|\| 98 \|\|*

Are you this head, or does this head belong to you? What about your belly? Are you your feet and so on, sire, or are they yours? 98

## \|\| 2.13.99 \|\|

*vastu rājeti yalloke yacca rājabhaṭātmakam \|*  
*tathānyacca nṛpetthaṃ tanna satsaṃkalpanāmayam \|\| 99 \|\|*

You seem to be apart from all these component limbs. Now that you are better informed, sire, please contemplate the question ‘Who am I?’ 99

## \|\| 2.13.100 \|\|

*yattu kālāntereṇāpi nānyāṃ saṃjñāmupaiti vai \|*  
*pariṇāmādisaṃbhūtāṃ tadvastu nṛpa tacca kim \|\| 100 \|\|*

Seeing the truth has been established in this way, how can I speak of ‘I’, sire, which is the mere result of a process of differentiation? 100

## Chapter 14 - Bharata teaches the highest goal

## \|\| 2.14.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*niśamya tasyeti vacaḥ paramārthasamanvitam \|*  
*praśrayāvanato bhūtvā tamāha nṛpatirdvijam \|\| 1 \|\|*

Hearing the brahmin’s words imbued with such significance, the king bowed down with deep respect and spoke to him as follows. 1

## \|\| 2.14.2 \|\|

*rājovāca*  
*bhagavanyattvayā proktaṃ paramārthamayaṃ vacaḥ \|*  
*śrute tasminbhramantīva manaso mama vṛttayaḥ \|\| 2 \|\|*

The king:

The words you’ve uttered, sir, contain the highest truth, but hearing them, the patterns of my mind are troubled. 2

## \|\| 2.14.3 \|\|

*etadvivekavijñānaṃ yadaśeṣeṣu jantuṣu \|*  
*bhavatā darśitaṃ vipratatparaṃ prakṛtermahat \|\| 3 \|\|*

This understanding of discrimination, present in all beings, as you’ve shown, brahmin, is the greatest principle that transcends the mundane world. 3

## \|\| 2.14.4 \|\|

*nāhaṃ vahāmi śibikāṃ śivikā na mayi sthitā \|*  
*śarīramanyadasmattau yoneyaṃ śibikā dhṛtā \|\| 4 \|\|*

‘I don’t carry the palanquin.’ ‘The palanquin doesn’t rest on me.’ ‘The body that supports it is different from me.’ 4

## \|\| 2.14.5 \|\|

*guṇapravṛttyā bhūtānāṃ pravṛttiḥ karmacoditā \|*  
*pravartante guṇā hyete kiṃ mameti tvayoditam \|\| 5 \|\|*

‘The actions of all beings are determined by their karma as driven by the guṇas.’ ‘The guṇas are preeminent.’ What do such statements mean to me? 5

## \|\| 2.14.6 \|\|

*etasminparamārthajña mama śrotrapathaṃ gate \|*  
*mano vihvalatāmeti paramārthārthitāṃ gatam \|\| 6 \|\|*

You understand the highest truth, but when I heard what you have said, my mind, though yearning for such knowledge, is perplexed. 6

## \|\| 2.14.7 \|\|

*pūrvameva mahābhāgaṃ kapilarṣimahaṃ dvija \|*  
*praṣṭumabhyudyato gatvā śreyaḥ kiṃ tvatra śaṃsa me \|\| 7 \|\|*

I’ve just set out to meet that sage of greatest fortune, Kapila, fully prepared to ask him what is best. 7

## \|\| 2.14.8 \|\|

*tadantare ca bhavatā yadeta dvākyamīritam \|*  
*tenaiva paramārthārthaṃ tvayi cetaḥ pradhāvati \|\| 8 \|\|*

But in the meantime, because of words that you have spoken, my mind, which hankers after highest truth, is drawn to you. 8

## \|\| 2.14.9 \|\|

*kapilarṣirbāgavataḥ sarvabhūtasya vai dvija \|*  
*viṣṇoraṃśo jaganmohanāśāyorvīmupāgataḥ \|\| 9 \|\|*

The sage Kapila is an aspect of Lord Viṣṇu, the universal being, manifesting in the world to free it from delusion. 9

## \|\| 2.14.10 \|\|

*sa eva bhagavānnūnamasmākaṃ hitakāmyayā \|*  
*pratyakṣatāmatra gato yathaitadbhāvatocyate \|\| 10 \|\|*

Surely, it’s the lord himself who came here as you spoke, from his desire for our wellbeing. 10

## \|\| 2.14.11 \|\|

*tanmahyaṃ praṇatāya tvaṃ yacchreyaḥ paramaṃ dvijaḥ \|*  
*tadvadākhila vijñānajalavīcyudadhirbhavān \|\| 11 \|\|*

As I bow before you, brahmin, teach me about the highest good, for you’re an ocean filled with waves of wisdom. 11

## \|\| 2.14.12 \|\|

*brāhmaṇa uvāca*  
*bhūpa pṛcchasi kiṃ śreyaḥ paramārthaṃ nu pṛcchasi \|*  
*śreyāṃsyaparamārthāni aśeṣāṇi ca bhūpate \|\| 12 \|\|*

The brahmin:

Your majesty, you ask what’s best, but not about the highest goal. Something may be one, great king, but may not be the other. 12

## \|\| 2.14.13 \|\|

*devatārādhanaṃ kṛtvā dhanasampadamicchati \|*  
*putrānicchati rājyaṃ ca śreyastasyaiva tannṛpa \|\| 13 \|\|*

Having worshipped all the gods, a man may wish for wealth, prosperity, sons or sovereignty. Fulfilling that desire then counts as ‘best’ for him. 13

## \|\| 2.14.14 \|\|

*karma yajñātmakaṃ śreyaḥ phalaṃ tatprāptilakṣaṇam \|*  
*śreyaḥ pradhānaṃ ca phale tadevānabhisaṃhite \|\| 14 \|\|*

Some think that acts of sacrifice are best, because they lead to heavenly realms. But ‘best’ is often found when no specific goal is wanted. 14

## \|\| 2.14.15 \|\|

*ātmā dhyeyaḥ sadā bhupa yogayuktais tathā param \|*  
*śreyastasyaiva saṃyogaḥ śreyo yaḥ paramātmanaḥ \|\| 15 \|\|*

Those disciplined in yoga, sire, should always contemplate the Self, as that is best for them. This union is best for one who holds as ultimate the highest spirit. 15

## \|\| 2.14.16 \|\|

*śreyāṃsyevamanekāni śataśoya sahasrasaḥ \|*  
*santyatra paramārthastu na tvete śrūyatāṃ ca me \|\| 16 \|\|*

Things by hundreds and by thousands may be considered ‘best’, but they are not the highest goal in life, so listen to me now: 16

## \|\| 2.14.17 \|\|

*dharmāya tyajyate kintu paramārtho dhanaṃ yadi \|*  
*vyayaś ca kriyate kasmāt kāmaprāptyupalakṣaṇaḥ \|\| 17 \|\|*

How can wealth be the highest goal if sacrificed for virtue? Why spend money just to satisfy desire? 17

## \|\| 2.14.18 \|\|

*putraścetparamārthaḥ syātso 'py anyasya nareśvara \|*  
*paramārthabhūtaḥ sonyasya paramārtho hi tatpitā \|\| 18 \|\|*

While a son is said to be the highest goal, the term may be applied to something else, your majesty. A son may be another person’s highest goal, as his father was once his father’s deepest wish. 18

## \|\| 2.14.19 \|\|

*evaṃ na paramārthosti jagatyasmiñcarācare \|*  
*paramārtho hi kāryāṇi kāraṇānāmaśeṣataḥ \|\| 19 \|\|*

Thus, in this world of moving and unmoving things, there’s no single highest goal, as all these goals have causes. 19

## \|\| 2.14.20 \|\|

*rājyādiprāptiratroktā paramārthatayā yadi \|*  
*paramārthā bhavantyatra na bhavanti ca vai tataḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

If winning a kingdom was said to be the highest, then such goals would exist at times and at others cease to be. 20

## \|\| 2.14.21 \|\|

*ṛgyajuḥsāmaniṣpādyaṃ yajñakarma mataṃ tava \|*  
*paramārthabhūtaṃ tatrāpi śruyatāṃ gadato mama \|\| 21 \|\|*

If you think the highest goal is sacrifice effected by the Ṛg, Yajur and Sāma Vedas, then listen to what I say. 21

## \|\| 2.14.22 \|\|

*yattu niṣpādyate kāryaṃ mṛdā kāraṇabhūtayā \|*  
*tatkāraṇānugamanāj jñāyate nṛpa mṛṇmayam \|\| 22 \|\|*

If a thing is formed from clay as its raw material, the finished product, following the nature of that substance, is also made of clay, your majesty. 22

## \|\| 2.14.23 \|\|

*evaṃ vināśibhir dravyaiḥ samidājyakuśādibhiḥ \|*  
*niṣpādyate kriyā yā tu sā bhavitrī vināśinī \|\| 23 \|\|*

Similarly, a rite performed with perishable materials, like kindling, butter and kuśa-grass, will only yield impermanent results. 23

## \|\| 2.14.24 \|\|

*anāśo paramārthaś ca prājñaurabhyupagamyate \|*  
*tattu nāśi na sandeho nāśidravyopapāditam \|\| 24 \|\|*

The wise understand that the highest goal is permanent, but this would certainly not be true if it depended on impermanent materials. 24

## \|\| 2.14.25 \|\|

*tadevāphaladaṃ karma paramārtho matastava \|*  
*muktisādhanabhūtatvātparamārtho na sādhanam \|\| 25 \|\|*

You may think the highest goal is ritual action, from which no reward is wanted, as it leads to liberation, but the highest goal is no mere means of achieving things. 25

## \|\| 2.14.26 \|\|

*dhyānaṃ caivātmano bhūpu paramārthārthaśabditam \|*  
*bhedakāri parebhyastu paramārtho na bhedavān \|\| 26 \|\|*

Meditation on the Self is said to be the meaning of the highest goal, your majesty, but this divides the Self from others. The highest goal yields no such divisions. 26

## \|\| 2.14.27 \|\|

*paramātmātmanoryogaḥ paramārtha itīṣyate \|*  
*mithyaitadanyadravyaṃ hi naiti taddravyatāṃ yataḥ \|\| 27 \|\|*

The highest goal is said to be the union of the spirit and oneself, but this is also false because one kind of substance cannot become another. 27

## \|\| 2.14.28 \|\|

*tasmācchreyāṃsyaśeṣāṇi nṛvaitāni na saṃśayaḥ \|*  
*paramārthastu bhūpāla saṃkṣepācchrūyatāṃ mama \|\| 28 \|\|*

All these are said to be ‘the best’, sire, but now I’ll briefly tell you, a guardian of the world, what truly is the highest goal. 28

## \|\| 2.14.29 \|\|

*eko vyāpī samaḥ śuddho nirguṇaḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ \|*  
*janmavṛddhyādirahita ātmā sarvagatovyayaḥ \|\| 29 \|\|*

It is the Self: unitary, pervading, constant, pure, unqualified, immaterial, free from birth and ageing and the rest, universal and unchanging, 29

## \|\| 2.14.30 \|\|

*parajñānamayo sadbhir nāmajātyādibhir vibhuḥ \|*  
*na yogavānna yuktobhūnnaiva pārthiva yokṣyate \|\| 30 \|\|*

Comprising highest wisdom and unyoked to falsehood, name or lineage in the past, the present or the future, your majesty. 30

## \|\| 2.14.31 \|\|

*tasyātmaparadeheṣu satopyekamayaṃ hi yat \|*  
*vijñānaṃ paramārtho 'sau dvaitino 'tathyarśinaḥ \|\| 31 \|\|*

The form of knowledge that exists as one in each and every body is the highest goal; the dualists’ view is incorrect. 31

## \|\| 2.14.32 \|\|

*veṇurandhraprabhedena bhedaḥ ṣaṅjādisaṃjñitaḥ \|*  
*abhedavyāpinovāyostathāsya paramātmanaḥ \|\| 32 \|\|*

Just as a single current of air is divided into notes on passing through the body of a flute, 32

## \|\| 2.14.33 \|\|

*ekasvarūpabhedaś ca brāhmakarmāvṛtiprajaḥ \|*  
*devādibhede 'padhvasteṃ nāstyevāvaraṇe hi saḥ \|\| 33 \|\|*

The unity of this great spirit is divided into varied forms under the sway of external factors. When the difference between the deities and the rest is removed, no distinction between high and low remains. 33

## Chapter 15 - Ṛbhu’s discourse on the highest truth

## \|\| 2.15.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*ityukte mauninaṃ bhūyaścintayānaṃ mahīpatim \|*  
*pratyuvācātha viprosāvadvaitāntargatāṃ kathām \|\| 1 \|\|*

After he had spoken, the brahmin then imparted to the silent king, still deep in thought, this account of nonduality. 1

## \|\| 2.15.2 \|\|

*brāhmaṇa uvāca*  
*śrūyatāṃ nṛpaśārdūla yadgītamṛbhuṇā purā \|*  
*avabodhaṃ janayatā nidāghasya mahātmanaḥ \|\| 2 \|\|*

The brahmin:

Listen, tiger of a king, to what was sung in former times by Ṛbhu, who led the brahmin Nidāgha to enlightenment. 2

## \|\| 2.15.3 \|\|

*ṛbhunāmābhavatputro brahmaṇaḥ parameṣṭhinaḥ \|*  
*vijñātatattvasadbhāvo nisargādeva bhūpate \|\| 3 \|\|*

Ṛbhu by name, Lord Brahmā’s son, understood the nature of reality, sire, on account of his noble origin. 3

## \|\| 2.15.4 \|\|

*tasya śiṣyo nidāghobhūtpulastyatanayaḥ purā \|*  
*prādādaśeṣavijñānaṃ sa tasmai parayā mudā \|\| 4 \|\|*

In former times, Ṛbhu had a disciple, Pulastya’s scion Nidāgha, to whom he taught all knowledge with greatest joy. 4

## \|\| 2.15.5 \|\|

*apāptajñānatantrasya na tasyādvaitavāsanā \|*  
*sa ṛbhustarkayāmāsa nidāghasya nareśvara \|\| 5 \|\|*

When Nidāgha grasped reality, sire, Ṛbhu thought that he was free from false notions of dichotomy. 5

## \|\| 2.15.6 \|\|

*devikāyāstaṭe vīranagaraṃ nāma vai puram \|*  
*samṛddamatiramyaṃ ca pulastyena viveśitma \|\| 6 \|\|*

Pulastya lived in the rich and pleasant city known as Vīranagara on the banks of the Devikā River. 6

## \|\| 2.15.7 \|\|

*ramyopavanaparyante sa tasminpārthivottama \|*  
*nidāgho nāma yogajña ṛbhuśiṣyo 'vasat purā \|\| 7 \|\|*

Ṛbhu’s disciple Nidāgha, now a yoga expert, sire, resided in a grove outside the city long ago. 7

## \|\| 2.15.8 \|\|

*divye varṣasahasre tu samatītesya tatpuram \|*  
*jagāma sa ṛbhuḥ śiṣyaṃ nidāghamavalokakaḥ \|\| 8 \|\|*

One thousand divine years later, Ṛbhu approached that city to visit him. 8

## \|\| 2.15.9 \|\|

*sa tasya vaiśvadevānte dvārālokanagocare \|*  
*sthitastena gṛhītārghyo nijaveśma praveśitaḥ \|\| 9 \|\|*

After the midday rituals, Ṛbhu came to Nidāgha’s door unrecognised, where his disciple welcomed him hospitably and showed him in. 9

## \|\| 2.15.10 \|\|

*prakṣālitāṅghripāṇiṃ ca kṛtā sanaparigrahma \|*  
*uvāca sa dvijāśreṣṭho bhujyatāmiti sādaram \|\| 10 \|\|*

Ṛbhu washed his hands and feet and accepted a place to sit. That best of brahmins Nidāgha then invited his guest to dine. 10

## \|\| 2.15.11 \|\|

*ṛbhuruvāca*  
*bho vipravarya bhoktavyaṃ yadannaṃ bhavato guhe \|*  
*tatkathyatāṃ kadanneṣu na prītiḥ satataṃ mama \|\| 11 \|\|*

Ṛbhu:

Tell me, best of brahmins, what food you have at home, because I find no pleasure in inferior cuisine. 11

## \|\| 2.15.12 \|\|

*nidāgha uvāca*  
*saktuyāvakavāṭyānāmapūpānāṃ ca me gṛhe \|*  
*yadrocate dvijaśreṣṭha tattvaṃ bhukṣva yathecchayā \|\| 12 \|\|*

Nidāgha:

We have cakes of saktu, yāvaka and vāṭya barley in the house. You’re welcome to have whichever of these appeals to you, best of brahmins, as you wish. 12

## \|\| 2.15.13 \|\|

*ṛbhuruvāca*  
*kadannāni dvijautāni mṛṣṭamannaṃ prayaccha me \|*  
*saṃyāvapāyasādīni drapsaphāṇitavanti ca \|\| 13 \|\|*

Ṛbhu:

These are all inferior foods, brahmin. Bring me something sweet, like saṃyāva cakes, rice pudding or dishes made with curds and treacle. 13

## \|\| 2.15.14 \|\|

*nidāgha uvāca*  
*hehe śālini madgehe yatkiñcidatiśobhanam \|*  
*bhakṣyopasādhanaṃ mṛṣṭaṃ tenāsyānnaṃ prasādhaya \|\| 14 \|\|*

Nidāgha:

Hey, wife! Use the best we have at home to make something sweet to please our visitor’s palate. 14

## \|\| 2.15.15 \|\|

*brāhmaṇa uvāca*  
*ityuktā tena sā patnīmṛṣṭamannaṃ dvijasya yat \|*  
*prasāditavatī tadvai bharturvacanagauravāt \|\| 15 \|\|*

The brahmin:

Thus addressed by Nidāgha, his wife, in deference to her husband’s words, made some sweets for Ṛbhu. 15

## \|\| 2.15.16 \|\|

*taṃ bhuktavantamicchāto mṛṣṭamannaṃ mahāmunim \|*  
*nidādhaḥ prāha bhūpāla praśrayāvanataḥ sthitaḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

Nidāgha, bowing politely, then addressed the sage, your majesty, while the latter ate the sweets that he’d requested. 16

## \|\| 2.15.17 \|\|

*nidāgha uvāca*  
*apite paramā tṛptirutpannā tuṣṭireva ca \|*  
*api te mānasaṃsvasthamāhāreṇa kṛtaṃ dvija \|\| 17 \|\|*

Nidāgha:

Are you really satisfied? Do you like the food? Is your mind well-pleased with it, brahmin? 17

## \|\| 2.15.18 \|\|

*kvanivāso bhavānvipra kva ca gantuṃsamudyataḥ \|*  
*āgamyate ca bhavatā yatastacca dvijocyatām \|\| 18 \|\|*

Where are you residing, and where do you plan to travel? Tell me the reason for your visit, brahmin. 18

## \|\| 2.15.19 \|\|

*ṛmuruvāca*  
*kṣudyasya tasya bhuktenne tṛptirbrāhmaṇa jāyate \|*  
*na me kṣunnābhavattṛptiḥ kasmānmāṃ paripṛcchasi \|\| 19 \|\|*

Ṛbhu:

A hungry person knows satisfaction once he’s eaten, brahmin. As I’ve finished, why ask if I am satisfied? 19

## \|\| 2.15.20 \|\|

*vahninā pārthive dhātau kṣapite kṣutsamudbhavaḥ \|*  
*bhavatyambhasi ca kṣīṇe nṛṇāṃ tṛḍapi jāyate \|\| 20 \|\|*

Hunger arises when the element of earth has been digested. When the body loses moisture, folk get thirsty. 20

## \|\| 2.15.21 \|\|

*kṣuttuṣṇe dehadharmākhye na mamaite yato dvija \|*  
*tataḥ kṣutsambhavābhāvāttṛptirastyeva me sadā \|\| 21 \|\|*

Because I’m not subject to thirst or hunger, even though they’re said to be the natural condition of the body, brahmin, and because I’m free from the cause of hunger, I’m always satisfied. 21

## \|\| 2.15.22 \|\|

*manasaḥ svasthatā tuṣṭiścittadharmāvimau dvija \|*  
*cetaso yasya tatpṛccha pumān ebhir na yujyate \|\| 22 \|\|*

Mental wellbeing and contentment are both natural states of mind, brahmin, but the spirit—puṃs—is untouched by these. Ask your questions of one whose mind is prone to them. 22

## \|\| 2.15.23 \|\|

*kva nivāsastavetyuktaṃ kva gantāsi ca yattvāyā \|*  
*kutāścāgamyate tatra tritayepi nibodha meḥ \|\| 23 \|\|*

You asked me where I live, where I’m going and why I’ve come. Listen to my answers to these three questions. 23

## \|\| 2.15.24 \|\|

*pumānsarvagato vyāpī ākāśavadayaṃ yataḥ \|*  
*kutaḥ kutra kva gantāsītye tadapyarthavatkatham \|\| 24 \|\|*

Since this spirit is everywhere and pervasive like the sky, how can questions such as ‘Why?’ and ‘Whence?’ and ‘Whither?’ be meaningful? 24

## \|\| 2.15.25 \|\|

*so 'haṃ gantā na cāgantā naikadeśaniketanaḥ \|*  
*tvaṃ cānye ca na ca tvaṃ ca nānye naivāhamapyaham \|\| 25 \|\|*

I neither come nor go, nor do I reside in a particular place. You’re not you, others are not themselves and I’m not me. 25

## \|\| 2.15.26 \|\|

*mṛṣṭaṃ na mṛṣṭamapyeṣā jijñāsā me kṛtā tava \|*  
*kiṃ vakṣyasīti tatrāpi śrūyatāṃ dvijasattama \|\| 26 \|\|*

If you want to know why I chose between sweet and unsweet food, then listen to my explanation, best of brahmins. 26

## \|\| 2.15.27 \|\|

*kimasvādvatha vā mṛṣṭaṃ bhuñjatosti dvijottama \|*  
*mṛṣṭameva yathā mṛṣṭaṃ tadevodvegakārakam \|\| 27 \|\|*

What is sweet or bland to one who eats the food, best of brahmins? Sweet food loses its appeal when you don’t desire it. 27

## \|\| 2.15.28 \|\|

*amṛṣṭaṃ jāyate mṛṣṭaṃ mṛṣṭādudvijate janaḥ \|*  
*ādimadhyāvasāneṣu kimannaṃ rucikārakam \|\| 28 \|\|*

Tasteless food is still delicious to a man who’s hungry, but what food tastes as good at first, halfway through and at the end? 28

## \|\| 2.15.29 \|\|

*mṛṇmayaṃ hi gṛhaṃ yadvan mṛdā liptaṃ sthiraṃ bhavet \|*  
*parthivoyaṃ tathā dehaḥ pārthivaiḥ paramāṇubhiḥ \|\| 29 \|\|*

Just as houses made from mud are strengthened when plastered with the same material, similarly, this body, composed of earthy elements, is strengthened with particles of the same. 29

## \|\| 2.15.30 \|\|

*yavagodhūmamudgādi ghṛtaṃ tailaṃ payo dadhi \|*  
*guḍaṃphalādīni tathā pārthivāḥ paramāṇavaḥ \|\| 30 \|\|*

Barley, wheat, lentils, butter, oil, milk, yoghurt, sugar, fruit and the others all consist of atoms. 30

## \|\| 2.15.31 \|\|

*tadetadbhavatā jñātvā mṛṣṭāmṛṣṭavicāri yat \|*  
*tanmanaḥsamatālambi kāryaṃ sāmyaṃ hi muktaye \|\| 31 \|\|*

When you’ve therefore understood this false distinction between bland and tasty, then your mind will be impartial, and impartiality leads to liberation. 31

## \|\| 2.15.32 \|\|

*brāhmaṇa uvāca*  
*ityākarṇya vacastasya paramārthaśritaṃ nṛpa \|*  
*praṇipatya mahābhāgo nidāgho vākyamabravīt \|\| 32 \|\|*

The brahmin:

Hearing these words of great import, your majesty, fortunate Nidāgha threw himself to the floor and said, 32

## \|\| 2.15.33 \|\|

*prasīda maddhitārthāya kathyatāṃ yattvamāgataḥ \|*  
*naṣṭo mohastavākarṇya vacāṃsyetāni me dvīja \|\| 33 \|\|*

‘Please have mercy! Tell me who you really are, now that you’ve come to help me, brahmin. Hearing what you’ve said, my confusion is dispelled.’ 33

## \|\| 2.15.34 \|\|

*ṛbhuruvāca*  
*ṛmurasmi tavācāryaḥ prajñādānāya te dvija \|*  
*ihāga tohaṃ yāsyāmi paramārthastavoditaḥ \|\| 34 \|\|*

Ṛbhu:

I’m your teacher, Ṛbhu, and I came to teach you, brahmin, but now I’ve imparted the highest truth, I’ll set out again. 34

## \|\| 2.15.35 \|\|

*evamekamidaṃ viddhi na bhedi sakalaṃ jagat \|*  
*vāsudevābhidheyasya svarūpaṃ paramātmanaḥ \|\| 35 \|\|*

Understand that all this world is one and undivided and is just a form of the highest spirit known as Vāsudeva. 35

## \|\| 2.15.36 \|\|

*brāhmaṇa uvāca*  
*tathatyuktvā nidāghena praṇipātapuraḥsaram \|*  
*pūjataḥ parayā bhaktyā icchātaḥ prayayāv ṛbhuḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

The brahmin:

After Ṛbhu had spoken thus, Nidāgha bowed with reverence and worshipped him with deepest faith. Then the sage departed and wandered of his own accord. 36

## Chapter 16 - Ṛbhu’s discourse continues

## \|\| 2.16.1 \|\|

*brāhmaṇa uvāca*  
*ṛbhurvarṣasahasre tu samatīte nareśvara \|*  
*nidāghajñānadānāya tadeva nagaraṃ yayau \|\| 1 \|\|*

The brahmin:

When another thousand years had passed, your majesty, Ṛbhu returned to that same place to teach Nidāgha again. 1

## \|\| 2.16.2 \|\|

*nagarasya bahiḥ sotha nidāghaṃ dadṛśe muniḥ \|*  
*mahībalaparīvāre puraṃ viśati pārthive \|\| 2 \|\|*

The sage beheld Nidāgha outside the city as the king was entering with a mighty army. 2

## \|\| 2.16.3 \|\|

*dūre sthitaṃ mahābhāgaṃ najasaṃmardavarjakam \|*  
*kṣutkṣāmakaṇṭhamāyāntamaraṇyātsasamitkuśam \|\| 3 \|\|*

Fortunate Nidāgha, returning from the forest with firewood and kuśa-grass, stood apart from the pressing crowd, wracked by pangs of hunger. 3

## \|\| 2.16.4 \|\|

*dṛṣṭvā nidāghaṃ sa ṛbhurupagamyābhivādya ca \|*  
*uvāca kasmādekānte sthīyate bhavatā dvija \|\| 4 \|\|*

Seeing Nidāgha, Ṛbhu approached unrecognised and greeted him: ‘Brahmin, why are you standing here alone?’ 4

## \|\| 2.16.5 \|\|

*nidāgha uvāca*  
*bho vipra janasaṃmarde mahāneṣa nareśvaraḥ \|*  
*pravivikṣuḥ puraṃ ramyaṃ tenātra sthīyate mayā \|\| 5 \|\|*

Nidāgha:

Greetings, brahmin! This crowd is here to see the king return to our fair city. That’s why I’m here as well. 5

## \|\| 2.16.6 \|\|

*ṛbhuruvāca*  
*narādhipotra katamaḥ katamaścetarojanaḥ \|*  
*kathyatāṃ me dvija śreṣṭha tvamabhijño mato mama \|\| 6 \|\|*

Ṛbhu:

Which is the king and which are his attendants? Tell me, best of brahmins, because I think you must be wise. 6

## \|\| 2.16.7 \|\|

*nidāgha uvāca*  
*yoyaṃ gajendramunmattamadriśṛṅgasamucchritam \|*  
*adhirūḍho naredro 'yaṃ parilo kastathetaraḥ \|\| 7 \|\|*

Nidāgha:

The king is mounted on that sacred raging elephant, towering like a mountain peak, and the others are his attendants. 7

## \|\| 2.16.8 \|\|

*ṛbhuruvāca*  
*etau hi gajarājānau yugapaddarśitau mama \|*  
*bhavatā na viśeṣeṇa pṛthakcihnopalakṣaṇau \|\| 8 \|\|*

Ṛbhu:

You’ve shown me the elephant and the king together but haven’t said which features may distinguish them. 8

## \|\| 2.16.9 \|\|

*tatkathyatāṃ mahābhāga viśeṣo bhavatānayoḥ \|*  
*jñātumicchāmyahaṃ kotra gajaḥ ko vā narādhipaḥ \|\| 9 \|\|*

Therefore, fortunate brahmin, describe for me the difference between these two, because I wish to know which one is which. 9

## \|\| 2.16.10 \|\|

*nidāgha uvāca*  
*gajo yoyamatho brahmannuparyasyaiṣa bhūpatiḥ \|*  
*vāhyavā hakasambandhaṃ ko na jānāni vai dvija \|\| 10 \|\|*

Nidāgha:

The elephant is the one below, brahmin, and the one on top is king. One is being carried by the other. Who doesn’t understand this, brahmin? 10

## \|\| 2.16.11 \|\|

*ṛbhuruvāca*  
*jānāmyahaṃ yathā brahyaṃs tathā māmavabodhaya \|*  
*adhaḥśabdanigadyaṃ hi kiṃ cordhvamabhidhiyate \|\| 11 \|\|*

Ṛbhu:

Kindly tell me, brahmin, so I can comprehend: what’s meant by the word ‘below’, and what’s the meaning of ‘on top’? 11

## \|\| 2.16.12 \|\|

*brāhmaṇa uvāca*  
*ityuktaḥ sahasāruhya nidāghaḥ prāha tamṛbhum \|*  
*śrūyatāṃ kathayāmyeṣa yanmāṃ tvaṃ paripṛcchasi \|\| 12 \|\|*

The brahmin:

Hearing this, Nidāgha suddenly leapt on Ṛbhu’s back and cried, ‘Listen and I’ll tell you. 12

## \|\| 2.16.13 \|\|

*uparyahaṃ yathā rājā tvamadhaḥ kuñjaro yathā \|*  
*avabodhāya te brahmandṛṣṭānto darśito mayā \|\| 13 \|\|*

I’m on top of you, just like the king, and, like the elephant, you’re below. I’m giving you this demonstration, brahmin, to help you understand.’ 13

## \|\| 2.16.14 \|\|

*ṛbhuruvāca*  
*tvaṃ rājena dvijaśreṣṭha sthitohaṃ gajavadyadi \|*  
*tadetattvaṃ samācakṣva katamastvamahaṃ tathā \|\| 14 \|\|*

Ṛbhu:

If you are like the king and I am like the elephant, kindly tell me, best of brahmins, which of us am I and which is you? 14

## \|\| 2.16.15 \|\|

*brāhmama uvāca*  
*ityuktaḥ satvaraṃ tasya pragṛhya caramābubhau \|*  
*nidāghastvāha bhagavānācāryastvamṛbhurdhruvam \|\| 15 \|\|*

The brahmin:

Hearing this, Nidāgha reverently clasped the sage’s feet and said, ‘You must be Ṛbhu, my holy teacher! 15

## \|\| 2.16.16 \|\|

*nānyasyādvaitasaṃskārasaṃskṛtaṃ mānasaṃ tathā \|*  
*yathācāryasya tena tvāṃ manye prāptamahaṃ gurum \|\| 16 \|\|*

None other than my master has a mind so perfectly impressed by the unity of all things. That’s why I guess that you’re my guru, finally returned.’ 16

## \|\| 2.16.17 \|\|

*ṛburuvāca*  
*tavopadeśadānāya pūrvaśuśruṣaṇādṛtaḥ \|*  
*gurusnehādṛbhurnāma nidāgha samupāgataḥ \|\| 17 \|\|*

Ṛbhu:

I am indeed your guru, Nidāgha, and Ṛbhu is my name. Having received your homage in former times, I’ve returned to teach you further. 17

## \|\| 2.16.18 \|\|

*tadetadupadiṣṭaṃ te saṃkṣepeṇa mahāmate \|*  
*paramārthasārabhūtaṃ yad advaitam aśeṣataḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

I therefore give you this instruction, wise brahmin, which in brief consists of the essence of the highest truth: all this is one. 18

## \|\| 2.16.19 \|\|

*brāhma uvāca*  
*evamuktvā yayau vidvānnidāghaṃ sa ṛbhurguruḥ \|*  
*nidāghopyupadeśena tenādvaitaparobhavat \|\| 19 \|\|*

The brahmin:

After that wise guru Ṛbhu had spoken thus to Nidāgha, he set out and, following his instruction, Nidāgha also came to realise the unity of everything. 19

## \|\| 2.16.20 \|\|

*sarvabhūtānyabhedena dadṛśe sa tadātmanaḥ \|*  
*yathā brahmaparo muktimavāpa paramāṃ dvijaḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

He saw no distinction between himself and other creatures, brahmin, and so that twice-born sage reached final liberation. 20

## \|\| 2.16.21 \|\|

*tathā tvamapi dharmajña tulyotmarīpubāndhavaḥ \|*  
*bhava sargagataṃ jānannatmānamavanīpate \|\| 21 \|\|*

Similarly, you, understanding virtue, should look upon yourself, your friends and foe as one, your majesty, while realising that the Self—the ātman—is all-pervading. 21

## \|\| 2.16.22 \|\|

*sitanīlā dibhedena yathaikaṃ dṛśyate nabhaḥ \|*  
*bhrāntadṛṣṭibhir ātmāpi tathaikaḥ sanpṛthakpṛthak \|\| 22 \|\|*

Just as the sky looks white or blue or another colour, the single Self appears divided to those whose perception is imperfect. 22

## \|\| 2.16.23 \|\|

*ekaḥ samastaṃ yadihāsti kiñcittadacyuto nāsti paraṃ tatonyat \|*  
*so 'haṃ sa ca tvaṃ ca sarvametadātmasvarūpaṃ tyaja bhedamoham \|\| 23 \|\|*

That single entity, all that’s in this world, is unfailing Viṣṇu. Aside from him, there’s nothing. He is me, and he is you, and he is all of this. Abandon the delusion that the Self is still divided! 23

## \|\| 2.16.24 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*itīritastena sa rājavarstatyāja bhedaṃ paramārthadṛṣṭiḥ \|*  
*sa cāpi jātismaraṇāptabodhastatraiva janmanyapavargamāpa \|\| 24 \|\|*

Thus addressed by the brahmin, that excellent king, perceiving the highest truth, now cast off all distinctions and the brahmin who recalled his former lives won freedom from rebirth. 24

## \|\| 2.16.25 \|\|

*iti bharatanarendrasāravṛttaṃ kathayati yaś ca śṛṇoti bhaktiyuktaḥ \|*  
*sa vimalamatireti nātma mohaṃ bhavati ca saṃsaraṇeṇu muktiyogyaḥ \|\| 25 \|\|*

A pure-minded individual who tells or hears this pithy tale of Bharata and the king while filled with true devotion will not mistake the nature of the Self, and will be fit for freedom from saṃsāra. 25
