# Visnu Purana 6

## Book Six: Dissolution

## Chapter 1 - The decline of virtue in the age of Kali

## \|\| 6.1.1 \|\|

*atha śrīviṣṇumahāpurāṇe viṣṇucittyātmaprakāśākhyaśrīdharīya vyākhyādvayopete ṣaṣṭhāṃśaḥ prārabhyate \|*  
*śrīmate rāmānujāya namaḥ*  
*maitreya uvāca*  
*vyākhyātā bhavatā sargavaṃśamanvantarasthitiḥ \|*  
*vaṃśānucaritaṃ caiva vistareṇa mahāmune \|\| 1 \|\|*

You’ve described in detail the creation of the world, great sage, the lineages of patriarchs, the duration of the Manvantaras and the deeds of the dynasties. 1

## \|\| 6.1.2 \|\|

*śrotumicchāmyahaṃ tvatto yathāvadupasaṃhṛtim \|*  
*mahāpralayasaṃjñāṃ ca kalpānte ca mahāmune \|\| 2 \|\|*

Now I wish to hear about the destruction of the world, known as the ‘great dissolution’, and annihilation at the end of every era, foremost sage. 2

## \|\| 6.1.3 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*maitreya śrūyatāṃ matto yathāvadupasaṃhṛtiḥ \|*  
*kalpānte prākṛte caiva pralayo jāyate yathā \|\| 3 \|\|*

I’ll now describe precisely how dissolution happens at the era’s end, Maitreya, and explain how primal nature is absorbed. 3

## \|\| 6.1.4 \|\|

*ahorātraṃ pitṝṇāṃ tu māso 'bdastridivaukasām \|*  
*caturyugamahasre tu brahmaṇo vai dvijottama \|\| 4 \|\|*

One day and night for ancestors are a month for mortals. One year for mortals is a day and night for gods. Two sets of a thousand cycles of four ages are a day and night for Brahmā, best of brahmins. 4

## \|\| 6.1.5 \|\|

*kṛtaṃ tretā dvāparaṃ ca kaliśceti caturyugam \|*  
*divyair varṣasahasrais tu taddvādaśabhir ucyate \|\| 5 \|\|*

Kṛta, Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali are the ages, and each fourfold cycle takes twelve thousand years of gods. 5

## \|\| 6.1.6 \|\|

*caturyugāṇy aśeṣāṇi sadṛśāni svarūpataḥ \|*  
*ādyaṃ kṛtayugaṃ muktvā maitreyāntyaṃ tathā kalim \|\| 6 \|\|*

Every cycle is the same, Maitreya, as they start with Kṛta and end with Kali. 6

## \|\| 6.1.7 \|\|

*ādye kṛtayuge sargo brahmaṇā kriyate yathā \|*  
*kriyate copasaṃhārastathāṃ'te ca kalau yuge \|\| 7 \|\|*

In the beginning, Brahmā creates the world in the Kṛta age, and he brings about its dissolution in the age of Kali. 7

## \|\| 6.1.8 \|\|

*maitreya uvāca*  
*kaleḥsvarūpaṃ bhagavanvistarādvaktumarhasi \|*  
*dharmaś catuṣpād bhagavān yasmin viplavam ṛcchati \|\| 8 \|\|*

Maitreya:

Please describe in detail the nature of this age of Kali, master, during which fourfold virtue is corrupted. 8

## \|\| 6.1.9 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*kaleḥsvarūpaṃ maitreya yadbhavāñchrotu micchati \|*  
*tannibodha samāsena vartate yanmahāmune \|\| 9 \|\|*

Parāśara:

You want to ask about the nature of the Kali age, Maitreya, so listen, sage, because that age is now upon us. 9

## \|\| 6.1.10 \|\|

*varṇāśramācāravatī pravṛttirna kalau nṛṇām \|*  
*na sāmaṛgyajurdharmaviniṣpādanahaitukī \|\| 10 \|\|*

At this time, folk no longer follow the provisions for their orders or stage of life, nor do they perform the rites determined by the Sāma, Ṛg and Yajur Vedas. 10

## \|\| 6.1.11 \|\|

*vivāhā na kalau dharmyā na śiṣyagurusaṃsthitiḥ \|*  
*na dāṃpatyakramo naiva vahnidevātmakaḥ kramaḥ \|\| 11 \|\|*

Marriage in the Kali age is no longer moral, nor do students live in their gurus’ homes. The customs governing husbands and their wives will be ignored, as will rituals for the sacred fires and deities. 11

## \|\| 6.1.12 \|\|

*yatra kutra kule jāto balī sarveśvaraḥ kalau \|*  
*sarvebhya eva varṇebhyo yogyaḥ kanyāvarodhane \|\| 12 \|\|*

In this age, a powerful lord, irrespective of his natal clan, will feel entitled to procure a woman of any order. 12

## \|\| 6.1.13 \|\|

*yena kena ca yogena dvijātirdīkṣitaḥ kalau \|*  
*yaiva saiva ca maitreya prāyaścittaṃ kalau kriyā \|\| 13 \|\|*

In the age of Kali, a twice-born man may be initiated into any spiritual tradition, but, irrespective of his choice, Maitreya, it will merely be atonement. 13

## \|\| 6.1.14 \|\|

*sarvameva kalau śāstraṃ yasya yadvajanaṃ dvija \|*  
*devatā ca kalau sarvā sarvaḥsarvasya cāśramaḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

Anything said to be a scripture will be thus regarded in the Kali age, brahmin; anything may be worshipped as a god and anyone may follow each stage of life. 14

## \|\| 6.1.15 \|\|

*upavāsastathā'yāsau vittotsargastapaḥ kalau \|*  
*dharmo yathābhirucitair anuṣṭhanair anuṣṭhitaḥ \|\| 15 \|\|*

Fasting, austerities and donations made by those who feel the need will amount to virtue at this time. 15

## \|\| 6.1.16 \|\|

*vittena bhavitā puṃsāṃ svalpenāḍhyamadaḥ kalau \|*  
*strīṇāṃ rūpamadaścaivaṃ keśair eva bhaviṣyati \|\| 16 \|\|*

Pride in small amounts of wealth will count as riches in the age of Kali, and women will be proud of their appearance, merely thinking of their hair. 16

## \|\| 6.1.17 \|\|

*muvarṇamaṇiratnādau vastre copakṣayaṃ gate \|*  
*kalau striyo bhaviṣyanti tadā keśair alaṅkṛtāḥ \|\| 17 \|\|*

In the Kali age, when garments have no gold or gems, jewels or other ornaments, women’s only source of beauty will be coiffure. 17

## \|\| 6.1.18 \|\|

*parityakṣyanti bhartāraṃ vittahīnaṃ tathā striyaḥ \|*  
*bhartā bhaviṣyati kalau vittavāneva yoṣitām \|\| 18 \|\|*

Wives will abandon husbands when they lose their wealth, but will marry any man with money in this age. 18

## \|\| 6.1.19 \|\|

*yo vai dadāti bahulaṃ svaṃ sa svāmī sadā nṛṇām \|*  
*svāmitvahetuḥsaṃbandho na cābhijanatā tathā \|\| 19 \|\|*

One who pays the biggest bribes gains mastery over men, and positions of nobility will derive from one’s connections, not one’s birth. 19

## \|\| 6.1.20 \|\|

*gṛhāntā dravysaṃghātā dravyāntā ca tathā matiḥ \|*  
*arthāścātmopabhogyāntā bhaviṣyanti kalau yuge \|\| 20 \|\|*

One’s concerns will be limited to one’s home, one’s wealth and chattels, and money will be squandered on amusements in the age of Kali. 20

## \|\| 6.1.21 \|\|

*striyaḥ kalau bhaviṣyanti svairiṇyo lalitaspṛhāḥ \|*  
*anyāyādāptavitteṣu puruṣāḥ spṛhayālabaḥ \|\| 21 \|\|*

Women will be free and yoked to pleasure, while men will hanker after ill-gotten gains. 21

## \|\| 6.1.22 \|\|

*abhyarthitāpi suhṛdā svārthameva nirīkṣyate \|*  
*paṇārdhārdhāddhamātre 'pi kariṣyanti tathā spṛhām \|\| 22 \|\|*

Even when requested by a friend, a person will not give up one-eighth part of a farthing, brahmin. 22

## \|\| 6.1.23 \|\|

*samānapauruṣaṃ ceto bhuvi vipreṣu vai kalau \|*  
*kṣīrapradānasaṃbandhi bhāvi goṣu ca gauravam \|\| 23 \|\|*

Commoners regard themselves as equal to the brahmins in this age, and cows are only valued for their milk. 23

## \|\| 6.1.24 \|\|

*anāvṛṣṭibhayaprāyāḥ prajāḥ kṣudbhayakātarāḥ \|*  
*bhaviṣyanti tadā sarve gaganāsaktadṛṣṭayaḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

All will live in fear of drought—the cause of famine—always looking at the sky. 24

## \|\| 6.1.25 \|\|

*kandamūlaphalāhārāstāpasā iva mānavāḥ \|*  
*ātmānaṃ ghātayiṣyanti hyanāvṛṣṭyādiduḥkhitāḥ \|\| 25 \|\|*

Wracked by drought and other troubles, they will starve themselves to death, eating roots and leaves and berries like ascetics. 25

## \|\| 6.1.26 \|\|

*durbhikṣameva satataṃ tathā kleśamanīśvarāḥ \|*  
*prāpsyanti vyāhatasukhapramodā mānavāḥ kalau \|\| 26 \|\|*

Always hungry, troubled and unprotected, they’ll find no ease or pleasure in the age of Kali. 26

## \|\| 6.1.27 \|\|

*asnānabhojino nāgnidevatātithipūjaṃnam \|*  
*kariṣyanti kalau prāpte na ca piṇḍodakakriyām \|\| 27 \|\|*

They’ll eat before they wash; sacred fires and gods and guests will not be tended; nor will they offer water to the ancestors when this age arrives. 27

## \|\| 6.1.28 \|\|

*lolupā hrasvadehāś ca bahvannādanatatparāḥ \|*  
*bahuprajālpabhāgyāś ca bhaviṣyanti kalau striyaḥ \|\| 28 \|\|*

Women will be greedy, short and gluttonous, having few resources but many mouths to feed. 28

## \|\| 6.1.29 \|\|

*ubhābhyāmapi pāṇibhyāṃ śiraḥkaṇḍūyanaṃ striyaḥ \|*  
*kurvantyo gurubhartṝṇām ājñāṃ bhartsyantyanādarāḥ \|\| 29 \|\|*

Scratching their heads with both their hands, surly women will ignore the orders of their elders and their husbands. 29

## \|\| 6.1.30 \|\|

*svapoṣaṇaparāḥ kṣudrā dehasaṃskāravarjitāḥ \|*  
*paruṣānṛtabhāṣiṇyo bhaviṣyanti kalau striyaḥ \|\| 30 \|\|*

They’ll be selfish, sullen, slovenly, abusive and untruthful in the Kali age. 30

## \|\| 6.1.31 \|\|

*duḥśīlā duṣṭaśīleṣu kurvantyaḥsatataṃ spṛhām \|*  
*asadvṛttā bhaviṣyanti puruṣeṣu kulāṅganāḥ \|\| 31 \|\|*

Even well-born women, drawn to wicked men, will be vulgar and depraved. 31

## \|\| 6.1.32 \|\|

*vedādānaṃ kariṣyanti baṭavaścākṛtavratāḥ \|*  
*gṛhasthāś ca na hoṣyanti na dāsyantyucitānyapi \|\| 32 \|\|*

Boys and the uninitiated will receive instruction in the Vedas, and householders will neither sacrifice nor make suitable donations. 32

## \|\| 6.1.33 \|\|

*vānaprasthā bhaviṣyanti grāmyāhāraparigrahāḥ \|*  
*bhikṣavaścāpi mitrādisnehasaṃbandhayantraṇāḥ \|\| 33 \|\|*

Ascetics in the forests will take the food of villagers, and mendicants will be bound by bonds of sentiment to family and friends. 33

## \|\| 6.1.34 \|\|

*arakṣitāro hartāraḥ śulkavyājena pārthivāḥ \|*  
*hāriṇo janavittānāṃ saṃprāpte tu kalau yuge \|\| 34 \|\|*

Kings will not protect their subjects, but, under the pretext of levying taxes, will plunder them like bandits when the age of Kali comes. 34

## \|\| 6.1.35 \|\|

*yoyo 'śvarathanāgāḍhyaḥsa sa rājā bhaviṣyati \|*  
*yaś ca yaścābalaḥ sarvaḥ sa sa bhṛtyaḥ kalau yuge \|\| 35 \|\|*

Anyone rich in horses, chariots and elephants will be a monarch, and the weak will be their subjects at this time. 35

## \|\| 6.1.36 \|\|

*vaiśyāḥ kṛṣivaṇijyādi saṃtyajya nijakarma yat \|*  
*śūdravṛttyā pravartsyanti kārukarmopajīvinaḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

Vaiśyas will leave their natural occupations in trade and farming and will take on roles of śūdras and live as artisans. 36

## \|\| 6.1.37 \|\|

*bhaikṣavrataparāḥ śūdrā pravrajyāliṅgino 'dhamāḥ \|*  
*pāṣaṇḍasaṃśrayāṃ vṛttimāśrayiṣyanti satkṛtāḥ \|\| 37 \|\|*

The lowliest śūdras will take the vows of mendicants and, bearing outward signs of wandering monkhood, will lead a life of heresy, being unordained. 37

## \|\| 6.1.38 \|\|

*durbhikṣakarapīḍābhir atīvopadrutā janāḥ \|*  
*godhūmānnayavānnāḍhyāndeśānyāsyanti duḥkhitāḥ \|\| 38 \|\|*

Miserable people, gravely oppressed by the weight of tax and famine, will flee to lands still rich in wheat and barley. 38

## \|\| 6.1.39 \|\|

*vedamārge pralīne ca pāṣaṇḍāḍhye tato jane \|*  
*adharmavṛddhyā lokānāmalpamāyurbhaviṣyati \|\| 39 \|\|*

When the Vedic path has been abandoned and folk devote themselves to heresy, with the spread of vice, the people’s lifespan will decrease. 39

## \|\| 6.1.40 \|\|

*aśāstravihitaṃ ghoraṃ tapyamāneṣu vai tapaḥ \|*  
*nareṣu nṛpadoṣeṇa bālye mṛtyurbhaviṣyati \|\| 40 \|\|*

Folk will practise awesome penance not sanctioned by the scriptures, and infants will perish on account of the sovereign’s sins. 40

## \|\| 6.1.41 \|\|

*bhavitā yoṣitāṃ sūtiḥ pañcaṣaṭmaptavārṣikī \|*  
*navāṣṭadaśavarṣāṇāṃ manuṣyāṇāṃ tathā kalau \|\| 41 \|\|*

Girls will bear children at the age of five or six or seven with boys aged eight or nine or ten in the Kali age. 41

## \|\| 6.1.42 \|\|

*palitodbhavaś ca bhavitā tathā dvādaśavārṣikaḥ \|*  
*nātijīvati vai kaścitkalau varṣāṇi viṃśatiḥ \|\| 42 \|\|*

The hair of youths will turn grey at twelve, and none will live past twenty. 42

## \|\| 6.1.43 \|\|

*alpaprajñā vṛthāliṅgā duṣṭāntaḥkaraṇāḥ kalau \|*  
*yatastato vinaṅkṣyanti kālenālpena mānavāḥ \|\| 43 \|\|*

Since men are foolish, false and mean, they’ll all die young in the age of Kali. 43

## \|\| 6.1.44 \|\|

*yadāyādā hi maitreya hānirdharmasya lakṣyate \|*  
*tadātadā kalervṛddhiranumeyā vicakṣaṇaiḥ \|\| 44 \|\|*

Whenever a rise in heresy is noted, Maitreya, the wise infer the spread of Kali. 44

## \|\| 6.1.45 \|\|

*yadāyadā hi pāṃṣaḍavṛddhirmaitreya lakṣyate \|*  
*tadātadā kalervṛddhiranumeyā mahātmabhiḥ \|\| 45 \|\|*

Whenever pious men on the Vedic path grow fewer, the wise perceive the growth of Kali. 45

## \|\| 6.1.46 \|\|

*yadāyadā satāṃ hīnirvedamārgānusāriṇām \|*  
*tadātadā kalervṛddhiranumeyā vicakṣaṇaiḥ \|\| 46 \|\|*

When the endeavours of men who practise virtue vanish, then the rise of the age of Kali is inferred by the learned. 46

## \|\| 6.1.47 \|\|

*prāraṃbhāścāvasīdanti yadā dharmabhṛtāṃ nṛṇām \|*  
*tadānumeyaṃ prādhānyaṃ kaler maitreya paṇḍitaiḥ \|\| 47 \|\|*

When the lord of sacrifice, foremost being, is no longer honoured by men with offerings, the influence of this age can be discerned. 47

## \|\| 6.1.48 \|\|

*yadāyāda na yajñānāmīśvaraḥ puruṣottamaḥ \|*  
*ijyate puruṣair yajñais tadā jñeyaṃ kaler balam \|\| 48 \|\|*

When people find no joy in Vedic recitation but delight in heresy, best of brahmins, the wise infer the spread of Kali. 48

## \|\| 6.1.49 \|\|

*na prītirvedavādeṣu pāṣaṇḍeṣu yadā ratiḥ \|*  
*kalervṛddhistadā prājñair anumeyā vicakṣaṇaiḥ \|\| 49 \|\|*

During this period, Maitreya, people are seduced by heresies and no longer worship Viṣṇu, the universal lord, the master and creator of the world. 49

## \|\| 6.1.50 \|\|

*kalau jagatpatiṃ viṣṇuṃsarvasraṣṭāramīśvaram \|*  
*nārcayiṣyanti maitreya pāṣaṇḍopahatā janāḥ \|\| 50 \|\|*

Brahmin, those seduced by heresies exclaim, ‘What do we care for gods or brahmins, the Vedas or ritual baths!’ 50

## \|\| 6.1.51 \|\|

*kiṃ devaiḥ kiṃ dvijair vedaiḥ kiṃ śauce nāṃbujanmanā \|*  
*ityevaṃ vipravakṣyanti pāṣaṇḍopahatā janāḥ \|\| 51 \|\|*

Parjanya will release few showers, brahmin, the crops will yield but little grain and fruit will have no substance when the Kali age begins. 51

## \|\| 6.1.52 \|\|

*svalpāṃbuvṛṣṭiḥ parjanyaḥ sasyaṃ svalpaphalaṃ tathā \|*  
*phalaṃ tathālpasāraṃ ca vipra prāpte kalau yuge \|\| 52 \|\|*

Most clothes will be made of flax, most trees will be śamīs and the people mostly śūdras. 52

## \|\| 6.1.53 \|\|

*śāṇīprāyāṇi vastrāṇi śamīprāyā mahīruhāḥ \|*  
*śūdraprāyās tathā varṇā bhaviṣyanti kalau yuge \|\| 53 \|\|*

Most grain will be millet, most milk will come from goats when the age of Kali comes and massage oil will be made from uśīra-grass. 53

## \|\| 6.1.54 \|\|

*aṇuprāyāṇi dhānyāni cājyaprāyaṃ tathā payaḥ \|*  
*bhaviṣyati kalau prāpte hyauśīraṃ cānulepanam \|\| 54 \|\|*

One’s in-laws will be honoured in preference to one’s parents in the age of Kali, best of sages. One will befriend the brothers of one’s wife, her kin and men with pretty wives. 54

## \|\| 6.1.55 \|\|

*śvaśrūśvaśurabhūyiṣṭhā guravaś ca nṛṇāṃ kalau \|*  
*śyālādya hāribhāryāś ca sṛhṛdo munisattama \|\| 55 \|\|*

‘What’s a mother and what’s a father, when one’s role is shown by actions?’ Such will be the view of those who regard their parents-in-law as their own. 55

## \|\| 6.1.56 \|\|

*kasya mātā pitā kasya yathā karmānugaḥ pumān \|*  
*iti codāhariṣyanti śvaśurānugatānarāḥ \|\| 56 \|\|*

Constantly beset by sins of body, voice and mind, foolish folk will err each day. 56

## \|\| 6.1.57 \|\|

*vāṅmanaḥkāyagair doṣair abhibhūtāḥ punaḥ punaḥ \|*  
*narāḥ pāpānyanudinaṃ kariṣyantyalpamedhasaḥ \|\| 57 \|\|*

All that causes pain to false, impure and shameless men will flourish in the age of Kali. 57

## \|\| 6.1.58 \|\|

*niḥsattvānāmaśaucānāṃ nihrīkāṇāṃ tathā nṛṇām \|*  
*yadyadduḥkhāya tatsarvaṃ kalikāle bhaviṣyati \|\| 58 \|\|*

When Vedic recitation ceases and the cries of vaṣaṭ, svadhā and svāhā are heard no more, the world will be as if alone. 58

## \|\| 6.1.59 \|\|

*niḥsvādhyāyavaṣaṭkāre svadhāsvāhāvivarjite \|*  
*tadā praviralo dharmaḥ kvacilloke nivatsyati \|\| 59 \|\|*

In that world, one may, with little effort, earn a peerless store of merit, which, in the Kṛta age, would require much more austerity. 59

## Chapter 2 - Vyāsa reveals the single virtue of the Kali age

## \|\| 6.2.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*vyāsaścāha mahābuddhiryadatraiva hi vastuni \|*  
*tacchrūyatāṃ mahābhāgā gadato mama tattvataḥ \|\| 1 \|\|*

Hear me, fortunate sage, while I truthfully relate wise Vyāsa’s words in this regard. 1

## \|\| 6.2.2 \|\|

*kasminkāle 'lpako dharmo dadāni sumahatphalam \|*  
*munīnāṃ puṇyavādo 'bhūtkaiś cāsau kriyate sukham \|\| 2 \|\|*

There was once a debate among the seers about the age in which the smallest virtue gave the most reward, and by whom this could be achieved most easily. 2

## \|\| 6.2.3 \|\|

*saṃdehanirṇayārthāya vedavyāsaṃ mahāmunim \|*  
*yayuste saṃśayaṃ praṣṭuṃ maitreya munipuṅgavāḥ \|\| 3 \|\|*

To put forward their uncertainties and dispel their doubts, the leading sages called on Vyāsa, the greatest seer and arranger of the Vedas. 3

## \|\| 6.2.4 \|\|

*dṛdṛśuste muniṃ tatra jāhnavīsalile dvija \|*  
*vedavyāsaṃ mahābhāgamardhasnātaṃ sutaṃ mama \|\| 4 \|\|*

They saw my son, the blessed sage Veda-vyāsa, in the waters of the Gaṅgā, brahmin, performing his ablutions. 4

## \|\| 6.2.5 \|\|

*snānāvasānaṃ te tasya pratīkṣanto maharṣayaḥ \|*  
*tasthus tīre mahānadyās taruṣaṇḍam upāśritāḥ \|\| 5 \|\|*

Those great seers waited on the bank of that mighty river in a shady grove while Vyāsa finished washing. 5

## \|\| 6.2.6 \|\|

*magno 'tha jāhnavītoyādutthāyāha suto mama \|*  
*śudraḥsādhuḥ kaliḥsādhurityevaṃ śṛṇvatāṃ vacaḥ \|*  
*teṣāṃ munīnāṃ bhūyaś ca mamajja sa nadījale \|\| 6 \|\|*

Rising from the waters of the river where he’d been bathing, my son Vyāsa cried, ‘This age of Kali is the best! The very best!’ Those sages heard his cry as he dived back underwater. 6

## \|\| 6.2.7 \|\|

*sādhusādhviti cotthāya śūdra dhanyosi cābravīt \|\| 7 \|\|*

When he emerged a second time, he shouted, ‘Śūdra, you’re the best. The very best! How fortunate you are!’ 7

## \|\| 6.2.8 \|\|

*nimagnaś ca samutthāya punaḥ prāha mahāmuniḥ \|*  
*yoṣitaḥ sādhu dhanyāstāstābyo dhanyatarostikaḥ \|\| 8 \|\|*

Once more the sage immersed himself and, coming up again, he cried, ‘Women are the best and luckiest. Who’s luckier than them?’ 8

## \|\| 6.2.9 \|\|

*tataḥ snātvā yathānyāyamācāntaṃ ca kṛtakriyam \|*  
*upatasthurmahābhāgaṃ munayaste sutaṃ mama \|\| 9 \|\|*

When my son had finished washing, duly rinsed his mouth and completed all the rites, those holy seers approached him with respect. 9

## \|\| 6.2.10 \|\|

*kṛtasaṃvandanāṃścāha kṛtāsanaparigrahān \|*  
*kimartha māgatā yūyamiti satyavatīsutaḥ \|\| 10 \|\|*

They offered salutations and found themselves a seat. Vyāsa, son of Satyavatī, then asked them why they’d come. 10

## \|\| 6.2.11 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*tamūcuḥsaṃśayaṃ praṣṭaṃ bhavantaṃ vayamāgatāḥ \|*  
*alaṃ tenāstu tāvannaḥ kathyatāmaparaṃ tvayā \|\| 11 \|\|*

‘We came to ask about some doubts,’ they said, ‘but let’s forget all that. Now we want you to explain another matter. 11

## \|\| 6.2.12 \|\|

*kaliḥsādhviti yatproktaṃ śūdraḥ sādhviti yoṣitaḥ \|*  
*yadāha bhagavāntsādhu dhanyāśceti punaḥ punāḥ \|\| 12 \|\|*

You just said the Kali age, a śūdra and a woman are the best, and stated that they’re fortunate. 12

## \|\| 6.2.13 \|\|

*tat sarvaṃ śrotum icchāmo na ced guhyaṃ mahāmune \|*  
*tatkathyatāṃ tato hṛtsthaṃ pṛcchāmastvāṃ prayojanam \|\| 13 \|\|*

We want to hear about this, sage, as long as it’s not secret. Then we’ll ask about the other thing that’s on our mind.’ 13

## \|\| 6.2.14 \|\|

*śrīparāśa uvāca*  
*ityukto munibhir vyāsaḥ prahasyedamathābravīt \|*  
*śrūyatāṃ bho muniśreṣṭhā yaduktaṃ sādhusādhviti \|\| 14 \|\|*

Thus questioned by the sages, Vyāsa gave a laugh and said, ‘Listen, best of seers, to why I said that they’re the best. 14

## \|\| 6.2.15 \|\|

*vyāsa uvāca*  
*yatkṛte daśabhir varṣais tretāyāṃ hāyanena tat \|*  
*dvāpare tacca māsena hyahorātreṇa tatkalau \|\| 15 \|\|*

Merit that takes ten years to earn in the Kṛta age takes but a single year in the Tretā, a month in the Dvāpara and just a day in this age of Kali. 15

## \|\| 6.2.16 \|\|

*tapaso brahmacaryasya japādeś ca phalaṃ dvijāḥ \|*  
*prāpnoti puraṣastena kaliḥsādhviti bhāṣitam \|\| 16 \|\|*

The same applies to the rewards of austerity, the student’s life of chastity, the recitation of mantras and so on, brahmins. That’s why I said the Kali age is best. 16

## \|\| 6.2.17 \|\|

*dhyāyankṛte yajanyajñais tretāyāṃ dvāparer'cayan \|*  
*yadāpnoti tadāpnoti kalau saṃkīrtya keśavam \|\| 17 \|\|*

The rewards for meditating in the Kṛta, sacrificing in the Tretā and worshipping in the Dvāpara may all be won by merely adoring Keśava in the age of Kali. 17

## \|\| 6.2.18 \|\|

*dharmotkarṣamatīvātra prāpnoti puruṣaḥ kalau \|*  
*alpāyāsena dharmajñāstena tuṣṭosmyahaṃ kaleḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

A person may experience a great increase in his merit with little effort in this age. That’s why I delight in it, you sages who know virtue. 18

## \|\| 6.2.19 \|\|

*vratacaryāparair grāhyā vedāḥ pūrvaṃ dvijātibhiḥ \|*  
*tataḥsa dharmasaṃprāptair yaṣṭavyaṃ vidhivad dhanaiḥ \|\| 19 \|\|*

The Vedas are adopted by the twice-born orders who’ve taken vows. They then perform the sacrifice with wealth acquired through communal duty as ordained by custom. 19

## \|\| 6.2.20 \|\|

*vṛthā kathā vṛthā bhojyaṃ vṛthejyā ca dvijanmanām \|*  
*patanāya tato bhāvyaṃ tais tu saṃyamibhiḥ sadā \|\| 20 \|\|*

Recitation, sacrificial feasts and offerings don’t benefit those communities, and are always undertaken by self-restrained practitioners to their detriment. 20

## \|\| 6.2.21 \|\|

*asamyakkaraṇe doṣasteṣāṃ sarveṣu vastuṣu \|*  
*bhojyapeyādikaṃ caiṣāṃ necchāprāptikaraṃ dvijāḥ \|\| 21 \|\|*

If these rites are not performed correctly, the doers incur iniquity from them all. Merely eating and drinking, brahmins, will not confer the outcome they desire. 21

## \|\| 6.2.22 \|\|

*pāratantryaṃ samasteṣu teṣāṃ kāryeṣu vai yataḥ \|*  
*jayanti te nijāṃllokānkleśena mahatādvijāḥ \|\| 22 \|\|*

In all their undertakings, they’re subject to the will of others, brahmins, and attain the realms ordained for them with difficulty. 22

## \|\| 6.2.23 \|\|

*dvijaśuśrūṣayaivaiṣa pākayajñādhikāravān \|*  
*nijāñjayati vai lokāñchūdro dhanyatarastataḥ \|\| 23 \|\|*

By serving the twice-born orders, a śūdra who oversees the offerings of food attains the realms ordained for him and is therefore luckier. 23

## \|\| 6.2.24 \|\|

*bhakṣyābhakṣyeṣu nāsyāsti peyāpeyeṣu vai yataḥ \|*  
*niyamo muniśārdūlāstenāsau sādhvitīritaḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

Since there’s no restriction for such a person on what he may or may not eat or drink, you tigers among sages, I say that he’s the best. 24

## \|\| 6.2.25 \|\|

*svadharmasyāvirodhena narair labdhaṃ dhanaṃ sadā \|*  
*pratipādanīyaṃ pātreṣu yaṣṭavyaṃ ca yathāvidhi \|\| 25 \|\|*

The wealth that’s won by men through constant obedience to communal duty should be placed in worthy hands or spent on sacrifice, as prescribed by custom. 25

## \|\| 6.2.26 \|\|

*tasyārjane mahākleśaḥ pālane ca dvijottamāḥ \|*  
*tathāsadviniyogena vijñātaṃ gahanaṃ nṛṇām \|\| 26 \|\|*

Great pains are undergone in earning and retaining wealth, you best of brahmins, and similarly, men know pain from its improper use. 26

## \|\| 6.2.27 \|\|

*evamanyais tathā kleśaiḥ puruṣā dvijasattamāḥ \|*  
*nijāñjayanti vai lokānprājāpatyādikānkramāt \|\| 27 \|\|*

Enduring these and other challenges, men attain their proper realms, best of brahmins, such as that of Prajāpati and others, in order of their merit. 27

## \|\| 6.2.28 \|\|

*yoṣicchuśrūṣaṇādbhartuḥ karmaṇā manasā girā \|*  
*taddhitā śubhamāpnoti tatsālokyaṃ yato dvijāḥ \|\| 28 \|\|*

A woman may attain the same realm as her husband, brahmins, merely by attending him with body, voice and mind. 28

## \|\| 6.2.29 \|\|

*nātikleśena mahatā tāneva puruṣo yathā \|*  
*tṛtīyaṃ vyāhṛtaṃ tena mayā sādhviti yoṣitaḥ \|\| 29 \|\|*

She’s unlike the man who only wins such realms through great travails. That’s the reason for my third remark, that women are the best. 29

## \|\| 6.2.30 \|\|

*etadvaḥ kathitaṃ viprāyannimittamihāgataḥ \|*  
*tatpṛcchataḥ yathākāmaṃ sava bakṣyāmi vaḥ sphuṭam \|\| 30 \|\|*

Now that I’ve explained all this to you, brahmins, feel free to ask the question that brought you here, and I’ll duly answer.’ 30

## \|\| 6.2.31 \|\|

*ṛṣayaste tataḥ procuryatpraṣṭavyaṃ mahāmune \|*  
*asminneva ca tatpraśne yathāvatkathitaṃ tvayā \|\| 31 \|\|*

Parāśara:

‘The matter that we came to ask about,’ the sages said, ‘has been addressed most adequately in your response to our second question, sage.’ 31

## \|\| 6.2.32 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*tataḥ prahasya tānāha kṛṣṇadvaipāyanomuniḥ \|*  
*vismayotphullanayanāṃstāpasāṃstānupāgatān \|\| 32 \|\|*

Vyāsa Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana the seer then laughed and addressed the gathered ascetics, whose eyes were wide with astonishment. 32

## \|\| 6.2.33 \|\|

*mayaiṣa bhavatāṃ praśro jñāto divyena cakṣuṣā \|*  
*tatohaṃ vaḥ prasaṃgena sādhusādhviti bhāṣitam \|\| 33 \|\|*

‘With divine insight, I perceived your question. That’s why I said, “The best, the very best!” to you in that regard. 33

## \|\| 6.2.34 \|\|

*svalpena hi pratyanenadharmaḥsidhyati vai kalau \|*  
*narair ātmaguṇāṃbhobhiḥ kṣalitākhilakilbiṣaiḥ \|\| 34 \|\|*

Merit is achieved in the Kali age with little effort by those whose faults are washed away by the water of their own virtues: 34

## \|\| 6.2.35 \|\|

*śudraiś ca dvijaśuśrūṣātatparair dvijasattamāḥ \|*  
*tathā strībhir anāyā sātpatiśuśrūṣayaiva hi \|\| 35 \|\|*

By śūdras intent on serving the twice-born orders, best of sages, and similarly, by women with the simple task of waiting on their husbands. 35

## \|\| 6.2.36 \|\|

*tatastritayamapyetanmama dhanyataraṃ matam \|*  
*dharmasaṃpādane kleśo dvijātīnāṃ kṛtādiṣu \|\| 36 \|\|*

Hence, I regard these three as fortunate, as it’s fraught for twice-born folk to win such merit in the Kṛta age and others. 36

## \|\| 6.2.37 \|\|

*bhavadbhir yad abhipretaṃ tadetatkathitaṃ mayā \|*  
*apṛṣṭenāpi dharmajñāḥ kimanyatkriyatāṃ dvijāḥ \|\| 37 \|\|*

I’ve told you all you wished to hear, even before you brahmins who know virtue asked. What further may I do for you?’ 37

## \|\| 6.2.38 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*tataḥsaṃpūjya te vyāsaṃ praśaśaṃsuḥ punaḥ punaḥ \|*  
*yathāgatandvijā jagmurvyāsoktikṛtaniścayāḥ \|\| 38 \|\|*

Parāśara:

The sages worshipped Vyāsa and praised him repeatedly, then departed as they’d come, their doubts dispelled by him. 38

## \|\| 6.2.39 \|\|

*bhavato 'pi mahābhāga rahasyaṃ kathitaṃ mayā \|*  
*atyantaduṣṭasya kalerayameko mahānmuṇaḥ \|\| 39 \|\|*

I’ve revealed to you this secret, lucky sage, the sole great virtue of the terrifying age of Kali. 39

## \|\| 6.2.40 \|\|

*yaccāhaṃ bhavatā pṛṣṭo jagatāmupasaṃhṛtim \|*  
*prākṛtāmantarālāṃ ca tāmapyeṣa vadāmi te \|\| 40 \|\|*

I’ll now describe the destruction of the world you asked about, and the periodic breakdown of the elements. 40

## Chapter 3 - The causal dissolution at the end of every aeon

## \|\| 6.3.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*sarveṣāmeva bhūtānāṃ trividhaḥ pratisaṃcaraḥ \|*  
*naimittikaḥ prākṛtikaḥ tathaivātyantiko layaḥ \|\| 1 \|\|*

It’s thought that dissolution of creation is threefold: causal, elemental and final. 1

## \|\| 6.3.2 \|\|

*brāhmo naimittikasteṣāṃ kalpānte pratisaṃcaraḥ \|*  
*ātyantikastu mokṣākhyaḥ prākṛto dviparārdhakaḥ \|\| 2 \|\|*

Causal or Brāhma dissolution occurs at the end of every aeon. Elemental dissolution occurs after two parārdhas, and final dissolution is liberation from existence. 2

## \|\| 6.3.3 \|\|

*maitreya uvāca*  
*parārdhasaṃkhyāṃ bhagavanmamācakṣva yayā tu saḥ \|*  
*dviguṇīkṛtayā jñeyaḥ prākṛtaḥ pratisaṃcaraḥ \|\| 3 \|\|*

Describe for me the duration of a parārdha, master, after two of which, elemental dissolution then occurs. 3

## \|\| 6.3.4 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*sthānātsthānaṃ daśaguṇamekasmādraṇyate dvija \|*  
*tatoṣṭādaśame bhāge parārdhamabhidhīyate \|\| 4 \|\|*

Parāśara:

Counting from one place to the next in the decimal system, brahmin, a parārdha is said to occupy the eighteenth place. 4

## \|\| 6.3.5 \|\|

*parārdhadviguṇaṃ yattu prākṛtaḥsa layo dvija \|*  
*tadāvyaktekhilaṃ vyaktaṃ svahetau layameti vai \|\| 5 \|\|*

The dissolution that occurs after two parārdhas is elemental, brahmin. At that time, the Seen is absorbed into the Unseen, which is its ultimate cause. 5

## \|\| 6.3.6 \|\|

*nimeṣo mānuṣo yo 'sau mātrāmātrāpramāṇataḥ \|*  
*taiḥ pañcadaśabhiḥ kāṣṭhā triṃśatkāṣṭha kalā smṛtā \|\| 6 \|\|*

A nimeṣa—a blinking of the eye—is the length of a short syllable in the Sanskrit language. Fifteen nimeṣas make one kāṣṭhā, and thirty kāṣṭhās make a kalā. 6

## \|\| 6.3.7 \|\|

*nāḍikā tu pramāṇena sā kalā daśa pañca ca \|\| 7 \|\|*

A nāḍikā lasts for fifteen kalās. 7

## \|\| 6.3.8 \|\|

*unmānenāṃbhasaḥsā tu palānyardhatrayodaśa \|*  
*māgadhena tu mānena jalaprasthastu sa smṛtaḥ \|*  
*hemamāṣaiḥ kṛtacchidracaturbhiścāturaṅgulaiḥ \|\| 8 \|\|*

Measured with a water clock, a nāḍikā is also the time taken to fill a vessel made of twelve and a half palas of copper, having the capacity of one prastha of water (following the Magadhan system of measurement), with a hole in the bottom into which is inserted a tube made of four māṣas of gold, as long as four fingers are wide. 8

## \|\| 6.3.9 \|\|

*nāḍikābhyāmatha dvābhyāṃ muhūrto dvijasattama \|*  
*ahorātraṃ muhurtāstu triṃśanmāso dinais tathā \|\| 9 \|\|*

Two nāḍikās make one muhūrta, best of brahmins. There are thirty muhūrtas in one day and night, and thirty days in a month. 9

## \|\| 6.3.10 \|\|

*māsair dvādaśabhir varṣamahorātraṃ tutaddivi \|*  
*tribhir varṣaśatair varṣaṃ ṣaṣṭyā caivāsuradviṣām \|\| 10 \|\|*

There are twelve months in a year, which is one day and night for gods. Three hundred and sixty mortal years are one year for gods, the rivals of the demigods. 10

## \|\| 6.3.11 \|\|

*tais tu dvādaśasāhastraiś caturyugamudāhṛtam \|*  
*caturyugasahasraṃ tu kathyate brahmaṇo dinam \|\| 11 \|\|*

One cycle of four ages takes twelve thousand divine years, and a thousand such cycles are said to be a day for Brahmā. 11

## \|\| 6.3.12 \|\|

*sa kalpastatra manavaścaturdaśa mahāmune \|*  
*tadante caiva maitreya brāhmo nemittiko layaḥ \|\| 12 \|\|*

One such day is one aeon or kalpa, during which fourteen Manus preside, great sage. At the end of each aeon, the causal or Brāhma dissolution of the world takes place, Maitreya. 12

## \|\| 6.3.13 \|\|

*tasya svarūpamatyugraṃ maitreya gadato mama \|*  
*śṛṇuṣva prākṛtaṃ bhūyastava vakṣyāmyahaṃ layam \|\| 13 \|\|*

The nature of elemental dissolution is terrifying, Maitreya; now listen while I describe it for you. 13

## \|\| 6.3.14 \|\|

*caturyugasahasrānte kṣīṇaprāye mahītale \|*  
*anāvṛṣṭiratīvogrā jāyate śatavārṣikī \|\| 14 \|\|*

At the end of a thousand cycles, when the earth is generally exhausted, there’ll be a dreadful drought that lasts a hundred years. 14

## \|\| 6.3.15 \|\|

*tato yānyalpasārāṇi tāni sattvānyaśeṣataḥ \|*  
*kṣayaṃ yānti muniśreṣṭha pārthivānyanupījanāt \|\| 15 \|\|*

As a result, all living things on earth, lacking nourishment, proceed to their destruction, best of sages, driven by their suffering. 15

## \|\| 6.3.16 \|\|

*tataḥ sabhagavānviṣṇu rudrarūpadharo 'vyayaḥ \|*  
*kṣayāya yatate kartumātmasthāḥsakalāḥ prajāḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

Next, Kṛṣṇa, the eternal lord, appearing in the form of Rudra, will strive for the dissolution of all beings that abide within himself. 16

## \|\| 6.3.17 \|\|

*tataḥ sa bhagavān viṣṇur bhānoḥ saptasu raśmiṣu \|*  
*sthitaḥ pibatyaśeṣāṇi jalāni munisaptama \|\| 17 \|\|*

Lord Viṣṇu, occupying seven solar rays, then imbibes the waters, best of sages. 17

## \|\| 6.3.18 \|\|

*pītvāṃbhāṃsi samastāni prāṇibhūmigatānyapi \|*  
*śoṣaṃ nayati maitreya samastaṃ pṛthivītalam \|\| 18 \|\|*

By absorbing all the moisture from the earth and living things, Maitreya, he desiccates the world. 18

## \|\| 6.3.19 \|\|

*samudrānsāritaḥ śailanadīprasravaṇāni ca \|*  
*pātāleṣu ca yattoyaṃ tatsarvaṃ nayati kṣayam \|\| 19 \|\|*

He’ll destroy the oceans and the rivers, the mountains and their streams and the water found in Pātāla and other lower realms. 19

## \|\| 6.3.20 \|\|

*tatastasyānubhāvena toyāhāropabṛṃhitāḥ \|*  
*ta eva raśmayaḥsapta jāyante sapta bhāskarāḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

Next, with his power, those seven rays, strengthened by the added water, give rise to seven suns. 20

## \|\| 6.3.21 \|\|

*adhaścordhvaṃ ca te dīptā stataḥsapta divākarāḥ \|*  
*dahantyaśaṣaṃ trailokyaṃ sapātālatalaṃ dvija \|\| 21 \|\|*

Blazing up and down, brahmin, those suns will burn the threefold worlds and all the lower realms. 21

## \|\| 6.3.22 \|\|

*dahyamānaṃ tu tair dīptais trailokyaṃ dvija bhāskaraiḥ \|*  
*sādrinadyarṇavābhogaṃ nisnehamabhijāyate \|\| 22 \|\|*

The threefold worlds, with mountains, seas and rivers, brahmin, being scorched by those blazing suns, will lose their moisture. 22

## \|\| 6.3.23 \|\|

*tato nirdagdhavṛkṣāṃbutrailokyamākhitaṃ dvija \|*  
*bhavatyeṣā ca vasudhā kūrmapṛṣṭhopamākṛtiḥ \|\| 23 \|\|*

When all the world is robbed of plants and water, brahmin, it resembles a tortoise shell. 23

## \|\| 6.3.24 \|\|

*tataḥ kālāgnirudro 'sau bhūtvā sarvaharo 'nalaḥ \|*  
*śeṣāhiśvāsasaṃbhūtaḥ pātālāni dahatyadhaḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

All-consuming Hari, appearing in the form of Rudra and the conflagration at the end of time, then becomes the serpent Śeṣa’s scorching breath and burns the lower realms of Pātāla. 24

## \|\| 6.3.25 \|\|

*pātālāni samastāni sa dagdhvā jvalano mahān \|*  
*bhūmimabhyetya sakalaṃ babhasti vasudhātalam \|\| 25 \|\|*

When that blaze has burned those realms, it spreads up here and destroys all things upon the surface of the earth. 25

## \|\| 6.3.26 \|\|

*bhuvarlokaṃ tataḥsarvaṃ svarlokaṃ ca sudāruṇaḥ \|*  
*jvālāmālāmahāvartastatraiva parivartate \|\| 26 \|\|*

A great, cruel whirling wreath of flames consumes the world, the atmospheric realms and heavens. 26

## \|\| 6.3.27 \|\|

*aṃbarīṣamivābhāti trailokyamakhilaṃ tadā \|*  
*jvālāvartaparivāramupakṣīṇacarācaram \|\| 27 \|\|*

All threefold worlds then glow like a griddle, ringed by swirling flames, deprived of lifeforms, mobile and immobile. 27

## \|\| 6.3.28 \|\|

*tatastāpaparītāstu lokadvayanivāsinaḥ \|*  
*kṛtādhikārāgacchanti maharlokaṃ mahāmune \|\| 28 \|\|*

Beset by heat, the residents of the upper realms, great sage, who are privileged to do so, then retreat to Maharloka. 28

## \|\| 6.3.29 \|\|

*tasmādapi mahātāpataptā lokāttataḥ param \|*  
*gañcchanti janalokaṃ te daśāvṛttyā paraiṣiṇaḥ \|\| 29 \|\|*

But when that realm becomes too hot for them, its denizens, fleeing for their lives and seeking somewhere higher, withdraw to Janaloka. 29

## \|\| 6.3.30 \|\|

*tato dagdhvā jagatsarvaṃ rudrarūpījanārdanaḥ \|*  
*mukhaniśvāsajānmeghānkaroti munisattama \|\| 30 \|\|*

When Janārdana in Rudra’s form has burned the world, best of sages, the breath emerging from his mouth gives rise to clouds. 30

## \|\| 6.3.31 \|\|

*tato gajakulaprakhyāstaḍitvanto 'tinādinaḥ \|*  
*uttiṣṭhanti tathā vyomnighorāḥsaṃvartakā ghanāḥ \|\| 31 \|\|*

These terrifying clouds, called Saṃvartakas, resembling herds of elephants, shedding bolts of lightning and roaring thunderously, arise above. 31

## \|\| 6.3.32 \|\|

*kecinnīlotpalaśyāmāḥ kecitkumudasannibhāḥ \|*  
*dhūmravarṇā ghanāḥ kecitkecitpītāḥ payo dharāḥ \|\| 32 \|\|*

Some are blue like utpala lilies, some glow white like kumuda flowers, some are grey as smoke and others yellow. 32

## \|\| 6.3.33 \|\|

*kecidrāsabhavarṇābhā lākṣārasanibhās tathā \|*  
*kecidvaiḍūryasaṃkāśā indranīlanibhāḥ kvacit \|\| 33 \|\|*

Some are dun like donkeys, some are red as lodh-tree sap, some are blue as lazuli, others are green as emerald. 33

## \|\| 6.3.34 \|\|

*śaṅkhakundanibhāś cānye jātyaṃ jananibhāḥ pare \|*  
*indragopanibhāḥ kecit tataḥ śakhinibhās tathā \|\| 34 \|\|*

Some are white as conch or jasmine flowers, some are black as kohl, some glow like fireflies and others are as red as arsenic. 34

## \|\| 6.3.35 \|\|

*manaḥśilābhāḥ kecid vai haritālanibhāḥ pare \|*  
*cāṣapatranibhāḥ keciduttiṣṭhante mahāghanāḥ \|\| 35 \|\|*

Some arise like mounds the hue of blue-jay feathers, some resemble splendid cities, others tower like mountains. 35

## \|\| 6.3.36 \|\|

*kecitpuravarākārāḥ kecitparvatasannibhāḥ \|*  
*kūṭāgāranibhāścānye kecitsthalanibā ghanāḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

Some rise up like roof-top dwellings, while others appear flat. Huge and rumbling, they fill the sky. 36

## \|\| 6.3.37 \|\|

*mahārāvā mahākāyāḥ pūrayanti nabhaḥsthalam \|*  
*varṣantaste mahāsārāstamagnimatibhairavam \|*  
*śamayantyakhilaṃ vipra trailokyāntaradhiṣṭhitam \|\| 37 \|\|*

Shedding mighty torrents, brahmin, they quell the dreadful fires that swept across the threefold worlds. 37

## \|\| 6.3.38 \|\|

*naṣṭe cāgnau ca satataṃ varṣamāṇā hyaharniśam \|*  
*plāvayanti jagat sarvam aṃbhobir munisattama \|\| 38 \|\|*

They douse the flames, then pour down rain for a hundred years or more, best of sages, inundating this whole world. 38

## \|\| 6.3.39 \|\|

*dhārābhir atimātrābhiḥ plāvayitvākhilāṃ bhuvam \|*  
*bhuvarlokaṃ tathevordhū plāvayanti hi te dvija \|\| 39 \|\|*

When all the earth is filled with raindrops as big as dice, the waters flood the atmospheric realm and then the heavens, brahmin. 39

## \|\| 6.3.40 \|\|

*andhakārīkṛte loke naṣṭe sthāvarajaṅgame \|*  
*varṣati te mahāmeghā varṣāṇāmadhikaṃ śatam \|\| 40 \|\|*

And when this whole dark universe with all things moving and unmoving has disappeared, those great clouds rain for yet another century. 40

## Chapter 4 - The elemental dissolution

## \|\| 6.4.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*saptarśisthānāmākramya sthiteṃbhasi mahāmune \|*  
*ekārṇavaṃ bhavatyetattrailokyamakhilaṃ tataḥ \|\| 1 \|\|*

When they reach the dwelling of the Seven Sages, the waters cease to rise, great seer, and all this threefold universe becomes a single ocean. 1

## \|\| 6.4.2 \|\|

*mukhaniśvāsajo viṣṇorvāyustāñjaladāṃstataḥ \|*  
*nāśayanvāti maitreya varṣāṇāmaparaṃ śatam \|\| 2 \|\|*

Wind born as breath from Viṣṇu’s mouth dispels the clouds, Maitreya, and blows for yet another hundred years. 2

## \|\| 6.4.3 \|\|

*sarvabhūtamayo 'cintyo bhagavānbhūtabhāvanaḥ \|*  
*anādirādirviśvasya pītvā vāyumaśeṣataḥ \|\| 3 \|\|*

The lord, who is all things, beyond all thought, creation’s source, beginningless yet the origin of all, then absorbs the wind in its entirety. 3

## \|\| 6.4.4 \|\|

*ekārṇave tatastasmiñcheṣaśayyāgataḥ prabhuḥ \|*  
*brahmarūpadharaḥ śete bhagavānādikṛddhariḥ \|\| 4 \|\|*

In the middle of that ocean, Lord Viṣṇu rests on Śeṣa as on a couch, then Hari, source of all, in Brahmā’s form, reposes. 4

## \|\| 6.4.5 \|\|

*janalokagataiḥ siddhaiḥ samakādyair abhiṣṭutaḥ \|*  
*brahmalokagataiś caiva cintyamāno mumukṣubhiḥ \|\| 5 \|\|*

Adored by Sanaka and other siddhas who withdrew to Janaloka, and contemplated by those who rose to Brahmaloka seeking liberation, 5

## \|\| 6.4.6 \|\|

*ātmamāyāmayīṃ divyāṃ yoganidrāṃ samāsthitaḥ \|*  
*ātmānaṃ vāsudevākhyaṃ cintayanmadhusūdanaḥ \|\| 6 \|\|*

Firm in heavenly yoganidrā, which emanates from his own illusion, the highest lord contemplates himself in the form called Vāsudeva. 6

## \|\| 6.4.7 \|\|

*eṣa naimittiko nāma maitreyaḥ pratisaṃcaraḥ \|*  
*nimittaṃ tatra yacchete brahmarūpadharo hariḥ \|\| 7 \|\|*

This is known as causal dissolution, Maitreya, because it’s caused by Hari reposing in the form of Brahmā. 7

## \|\| 6.4.8 \|\|

*yadā jāgarti sarvātmā sa tadā ceṣṭate jagat \|*  
*nimīlatyetadakhilaṃ māyāśayyāṃ gate 'cyute \|\| 8 \|\|*

When that universal being awakes, the world begins to stir and, when the everlasting god reclines upon his own bed of illusion, the whole world shuts its eyes. 8

## \|\| 6.4.9 \|\|

*padmayonerdinaṃ yattu caturyugasahasravat \|*  
*ekārṇavīkṛte loke tāvatī rātririṣyate \|\| 9 \|\|*

Just as one day for lotus-born Brahmā is a thousand cycles of ages, the night, when the world is submerged beneath the universal ocean, is of the same duration. 9

## \|\| 6.4.10 \|\|

*tataḥ prabuddho rātryante punaḥsṛṣṭiṃ karotyajaḥ \|*  
*brahmasvarūpadhṛgviṣṇuryathā te kathitaṃ purā \|\| 10 \|\|*

When he wakes at the end of the night, Viṣṇu, the unborn being in the form of Brahmā, creates the world again, as I explained to you before. 10

## \|\| 6.4.11 \|\|

*ityeṣa kalpasaṃhāro 'vāntarapralayo dvija \|*  
*naimittikaste kathitaḥ prākṛtaḥ śṛṇvataḥ param \|\| 11 \|\|*

I’ve thus described the causal dissolution at the end of every aeon, brahmin. Now listen while I describe the elemental dissolution. 11

## \|\| 6.4.12 \|\|

*anāvṛṣṭyādisaṃparkāt kṛte saṃkṣālane mune \|*  
*samasteṣveva lokeṣu pātāleṣvakhileṣu ca \|\| 12 \|\|*

After the onslaught of drought and fire, sage, when everything in all the worlds and the lower realms of Pātāla is under water, 12

## \|\| 6.4.13 \|\|

*mahadādervikārasya viśeṣāntasya saṃkṣaye \|*  
*kṛṣṇecchākārite tasminpravṛtte pratisaṃcare \|\| 13 \|\|*

The elemental dissolution starts, in which the permutations of mahat and all the other basic elements are destroyed in accordance with the will of Kṛṣṇa. 13

## \|\| 6.4.14 \|\|

*āpo grasaṃti vai pūrvaṃ bhūmergandhātmakaṃ guṇam \|*  
*āttagandhā tato bhūmiḥ pralayatvāya kalpate \|\| 14 \|\|*

The waters swallow up the quality previously possessed by earth, the phenomenon of scent, and, when the element of earth is robbed of scent, it is destroyed. 14

## \|\| 6.4.15 \|\|

*praṇaṣṭe gandhatanmātre bhavatyurvī jalātmikā \|*  
*āpastadā pravṛddhāstu vegavatyo mahāsvanāḥ \|\| 15 \|\|*

The earth without its fragrance is united with the waters. The rushing, roaring waters then increase. 15

## \|\| 6.4.16 \|\|

*sarvamāpūrayantīdaṃ tiṣṭhanti vicaranti ca \|*  
*salilenormimālena ālokāntātsamantataḥ \|\| 16 \|\|*

Filling all the world, the billowing floods spread fitfully across each realm on every side. 16

## \|\| 6.4.17 \|\|

*apāmapi guṇo yastu jyotiṣā pīyate tu saḥ \|*  
*naśyantyāpastatastāś ca rasatanmātrasaṃkṣayāt \|\| 17 \|\|*

The quality of taste imbuing water is then absorbed by the element of fire and, when that quality is lost, water is no more. 17

## \|\| 6.4.18 \|\|

*tataścāpo hṛtarasājyotiṣaṃ prāpnuvanti vai \|*  
*agnāvasthe tu salile tejasā sarvato vṛte \|\| 18 \|\|*

When the waters lose their taste, they become one with fire and, when this happens, fire overtakes the world. 18

## \|\| 6.4.19 \|\|

*sa cāgniḥ sarvato vyāpya cādatte tajjalaṃ tathā \|*  
*sarvamāpūryatercirbhis tadā jagadidaṃ śanaiḥ \|\| 19 \|\|*

When fire spreads in all directions, it consumes the waters, and this whole world is slowly filled with flames. 19

## \|\| 6.4.20 \|\|

*arcirbhiḥ saṃvṛte tasmiṃstiryagūrdhvamadhastadā \|*  
*jyotiṣo 'pi paraṃ rūpaṃ vāyuratti prabhākaram \|\| 20 \|\|*

When the universe above, below and on all sides is burned by fire, the element of air then swallows form, the ultimate quality of fire. 20

## \|\| 6.4.21 \|\|

*pralīne ca tatastasminvāyubhūte 'khilātmani \|*  
*pranaṣṭe rūpatanmātre hṛtarūpo vibhāvasuḥ \|\| 21 \|\|*

When fire has been entirely reduced to air and the element of form destroyed, then light is deprived of form. 21

## \|\| 6.4.22 \|\|

*praśāmyati tadājyotirvāyurdedhūyate mahān \|*  
*nirā loke tathā loke vāyvavasthe ca tejasi \|\| 22 \|\|*

Light grows dim and the element of air begins to tremble when the world is dark, and fire and air are one. 22

## \|\| 6.4.23 \|\|

*tatastu mūlamāsādya vāyuḥsaṃbhavamātmanaḥ \|*  
*ūrdhvaṃ cādhaś ca tiryakca dodhavīti diśo daśa \|\| 23 \|\|*

Air, having reached the root that is its origin, shakes violently in all directions: above, below and on each side. 23

## \|\| 6.4.24 \|\|

*vāyorapi guṇaṃ sparśamākāśo grasate tataḥ \|*  
*praśāmyati tato vāyuḥ khaṃ tu tiṣṭhatyanāvṛtam \|\| 24 \|\|*

Space then swallows touch, the property of air. Air grows still, and limitless space alone abides. 24

## \|\| 6.4.25 \|\|

*arūparasamasparśamagandhaṃ na ca mūrtimat \|*  
*sarvamāpūrayaccaiva sumahāttatprakāśate \|\| 25 \|\|*

Lacking form, taste, touch, scent and shape, space appears in pure immensity and fills the universe. 25

## \|\| 6.4.26 \|\|

*sanskrit not found \|\| 26 \|\|*

Vacant and ubiquitous, it’s characterised by the quality of sound. Consisting of sound alone, space is all-pervading. 26

## \|\| 6.4.27 \|\|

*bhūtendriyeṣu yugapadbhūtādau saṃsthiteṣu vai \|*  
*abimānātmako hyeṣa bhūtādistāmasaḥsmṛtaḥ \|\| 27 \|\|*

When the elements with sound and other qualities have been absorbed together, they’re characterised by pride and dullness, it is thought. 27

## \|\| 6.4.28 \|\|

*bhītādiṃ grasate cāpi mahānve buddhilakṣaṇaḥ \|\| 28 \|\|*

The elements and all the rest are swallowed by mahat, the element of Greatness, which is characterised by intellect. 28

## \|\| 6.4.29 \|\|

*urvī mahaṃś ca jagataḥ pratintarbāhyatas tathā \|\| 29 \|\|*

The earth and mahat form the inner and outer limits of the universe. 29

## \|\| 6.4.30 \|\|

*evaṃ sapta mahābuddhe kramātprakṛtayaḥsmṛtāḥ \|*  
*pratyāhāre tu tāḥsarvāḥ praviśanti parasparam \|\| 30 \|\|*

Thus, the seven natural elements that arise from mahābuddhi, ‘great intellect’, all re-enter one another at the time of elemental dissolution. 30

## \|\| 6.4.31 \|\|

*yenedamāvṛtaṃ sarvamaṇḍamapsu pralīyate \|*  
*saptadvīpasamudrāntaṃ saptalokaṃ saparvatam \|\| 31 \|\|*

The cosmic egg is dissolved in the waters that surround it, along with the seven continents and oceans, the realms and mountain ranges. 31

## \|\| 6.4.32 \|\|

*udakāvaraṇaṃ yattu jyotiṣā pīyate tu tam \|*  
*jyotirvāyau layaṃ yāti yātyākāśe samīramaḥ \|\| 32 \|\|*

This sheath of water is then absorbed by fire; fire by air, and air by space. 32

## \|\| 6.4.33 \|\|

*ākāśaṃ caiva bhūtādirgrasate taṃ tathā mahān \|*  
*mahāntamebhiḥ sahitaṃ prakṛtirgrasate dvija \|\| 33 \|\|*

Mahat and other elements swallow space and the elemental state, prakṛti, absorbs mahat, along with all the elements, brahmin. 33

## \|\| 6.4.34 \|\|

*gumasāmyamanudriktamanyūnaṃ ca mahāmune \|*  
*procyate prakatirhetuḥ pradhānaṅkāraṇaṃ param \|\| 34 \|\|*

When the qualities are in balance, neither too little nor too much, sage, it’s called the elemental state, prakṛti, the origin, primal Nature, the highest cause. 34

## \|\| 6.4.35 \|\|

*ityeṣā prakṛtiḥsarvā vyaktāvyaktasvarūpiṇī \|*  
*vyaktasvarūpamavyakte tasmānmaitreya līyate \|\| 35 \|\|*

Thus, this entire elemental state is both Seen and Unseen, and the first is absorbed into the second, Maitreya. 35

## \|\| 6.4.36 \|\|

*ekaḥ śuddho 'kṣaro nityaḥsarvavyāpī tathā pumān \|*  
*so 'py aṃśaḥsarvabhūtasya maitreya paramātmanaḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

The singular, pure, immutable, eternal, ubiquitous primal Spirit, puṃs, is also an aspect of the universal highest Self, Maitreya. 36

## \|\| 6.4.37 \|\|

*na saṃti yatra sarveśe nāmajātyādikalpanāḥ \|*  
*sattāmātratmake jñeye jñānātmanyātmanaḥ pare \|\| 37 \|\|*

Concepts such as name and station don’t apply to the universal lord, who should be understood as pure existence and who consists in knowledge and transcends the Self. 37

## \|\| 6.4.38 \|\|

*tadbrahma paramaṃ dhāma paramātmā sa ceśvaraḥ \|*  
*sa viṣṇuḥsarvamevedaṃ yato nāvartate yatiḥ \|\| 38 \|\|*

This is brahman. This is the Absolute. This is the abode, the highest Self, the lord. This whole world is Viṣṇu, from whom the perfect sage does not return. 38

## \|\| 6.4.39 \|\|

*prakṛtiryā mayākhyātā vyaktāvyaktasvarūpiṇī \|*  
*puruṣaścāpyubhāvetau liyate paramātmani \|\| 39 \|\|*

Primal Nature, prakṛti, which I’ve described, manifest and unmanifest in form, and primal Spirit, puruṣa, are absorbed by the highest Self. 39

## \|\| 6.4.40 \|\|

*paramātmā ca sarveṣāmādhāraḥ parameśvaraḥ \|*  
*viṣṇunāmā sa vedeṣu vedānteṣu ca gīyate \|\| 40 \|\|*

This highest Self, foundation of the universe and highest lord, is hymned by name as Viṣṇu in the Vedas and their supplements. 40

## \|\| 6.4.41 \|\|

*pravṛttaṃ ca nivṛttaṃ ca dvividhaṅkarma vaidikam \|*  
*tābhyāmubhābhyāṃ puruṣaiḥ sarvamūrtiḥsa ijyate \|\| 41 \|\|*

Sacrifice and renunciation are two forms of pious acts prescribed in Vedic texts. Through these two paths, men worship Viṣṇu in universal form. 41

## \|\| 6.4.42 \|\|

*ṛgyajuḥsāmabhir mārgaiḥ pravṛtterijyate hyasau \|*  
*yajñeśvaro yajñapumānpuruṣaiḥ puruṣottamaḥ \|\| 42 \|\|*

Following the paths of the Ṛg, Yajur and Sāma Vedas, the lord and spirit of the sacrifice, the highest spirit, is adored by men with sacrifices. 42

## \|\| 6.4.43 \|\|

*jñānātmā jñānayogena jñānamūrtiḥ sa cejyate \|*  
*nivṛtte yogibhir mārge viṣṇurmuktiphalapradaḥ \|\| 43 \|\|*

Viṣṇu, who confers rewards of liberation and is at the heart of knowledge, is worshipped in the form of knowledge through the yoga of knowledge by ascetics on the path of renunciation. 43

## \|\| 6.4.44 \|\|

*hrasvadīrghaplutair yattu kiñcidvastvabhidhīyate \|*  
*yacca vācāmaviṣayaṃ tatsarvaṃ viṣṇuravyayaḥ \|\| 44 \|\|*

All that’s named with short, long or extended syllables, and all that’s beyond the reach of words, is eternal Viṣṇu. 44

## \|\| 6.4.45 \|\|

*vyaktaḥsa eva cāvyaktaḥsa eva puruṣottamaḥ \|*  
*paramātmā ca viśvātmā viśvarūpadharohariḥ \|\| 45 \|\|*

He is Seen, Unseen, eternal Spirit, highest Self, universal Self and Hari of universal form. 45

## \|\| 6.4.46 \|\|

*vyaktāvyaktātmikā tasminprakṛtiḥsaṃpralīyate \|*  
*puruṣaścāpi maitreya vyāpinyavyāhatātmani \|\| 46 \|\|*

Primal Nature, consisting of the Seen and Unseen, and primal Spirit, Maitreya, are absorbed into that all-pervasive, unimpeded state. 46

## \|\| 6.4.47 \|\|

*dviparārdhātmakaḥ kālaḥ kathito yo mayā tava \|*  
*tadahastasya maitreya viṣṇorīśasya kathyate \|\| 47 \|\|*

The period of two parārdhas that I described to you, Maitreya, is said to be a single day for Viṣṇu. 47

## \|\| 6.4.48 \|\|

*vyakte ca prakṛtau līne prakṛtyāṃ puruṣe tathā \|*  
*tatra sthite niśā cāsya tatpramāṇā mahāmune \|\| 48 \|\|*

When the Seen is absorbed into primal Nature, and Nature is absorbed in Spirit, and Spirit is absorbed in that supreme being, then there is another period, a night, which is of the same duration as one of Viṣṇu’s days, great sage. 48

## \|\| 6.4.49 \|\|

*naivāhastasya na niśā nityasya paramātmanaḥ \|*  
*upacārastathāpyeṣa tasyeśasya dvijocyate \|\| 49 \|\|*

There is, in fact, no day or night for the everlasting highest being, brahmin. It’s just a way of speaking of the lord. 49

## \|\| 6.4.50 \|\|

*ityeṣa tava maitreya kathitaḥ prākṛto layaḥ \|*  
*ātyantikamatho brahmannibodha pratisaṃcaram \|\| 50 \|\|*

Thus, I’ve described the dissolution of the elements for you, Maitreya. Now you’ll hear about the final dissolution, brahmin. 50

## Chapter 5 - The final dissolution and the nature of suffering

## \|\| 6.5.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*ādhyātmikādi maitreya jñātvā tāpatrayaṃ budhaḥ \|*  
*utpannajñānavairāgyaṃ prāpnotyātyantikaṃ layam \|\| 1 \|\|*

The wise person who understands three kinds of worldly suffering, including pain arising from within, Maitreya, and in whom spiritual knowledge and dispassion have arisen, will attain the final dissolution. 1

## \|\| 6.5.2 \|\|

*ādhyātmikopi dvividhaḥ śarīro mānasas tathā \|*  
*śarīro bahubhir bhedair bhidyate śruyatāṃ ca saḥ \|\| 2 \|\|*

Pain arising internally is of two kinds, affecting both the body and the mind. Bodily afflictions are divided into many classes. Listen to these: 2

## \|\| 6.5.3 \|\|

*śirorogapratiśyāyajvaraśūlabhagandaraiḥ \|*  
*gulmārśaḥ śvayayuśvāsacchardyādibhir anekadhā \|\| 3 \|\|*

Headaches, colds, fever, gout, fistula, spleen, haemorrhoids, asthma, tumours, vomiting and many others. 3

## \|\| 6.5.4 \|\|

*tathākṣirogātīsārakuṣṭhāṅgāmayasaṃjñitaiḥ \|*  
*bhidyate dehajastāpo mānasaṃ śrotumarhasi \|\| 4 \|\|*

There are also diseases of the eye, dysentery and leprosy. This is how bodily afflictions are classified. Now hear about afflictions of the mind. 4

## \|\| 6.5.5 \|\|

*kāmakrodhabhayadveṣalobhamohaviṣādajaḥ \|*  
*śokāsūyāvamānerṣyāmātsaryādimayas tathā \|\| 5 \|\|*

Desire, anger, fear, hatred, greed, delusion, depression, grief, envy, contempt, jealousy, selfishness and other emotions— 5

## \|\| 6.5.6 \|\|

*mānaso 'pi dvijaśreṣṭha tāpo bhavati naikadhā \|*  
*ityevamādibhir bhedais tāpo hyādhyātmikaḥ smṛtaḥ \|\| 6 \|\|*

There are many such mental afflictions, best of brahmins. These are the classes of suffering arising from within. 6

## \|\| 6.5.7 \|\|

*mṛgapakṣimanuṣyādyaiḥ piśācoragarākṣasaiḥ \|*  
*sarīsṛpādyaiś ca nṛṇāṃ jāyate cādhibhautikaḥ \|\| 7 \|\|*

Wild animals, birds and other people, flesh-eating piśāca demons, serpents and rākṣasas, as well as snakes and insects—these living things may bring harm to people. 7

## \|\| 6.5.8 \|\|

*śītavā toṣṇavārṣābubaidyutādisamudbhavaḥ \|*  
*tāpo dvijavaraśreṣṭhaiḥ kathyate cādhidaivikaḥ \|\| 8 \|\|*

I shall also mention suffering inflicted by natural forces, including cold, heat, wind, rain, floods and lightning, most excellent of brahmins. 8

## \|\| 6.5.9 \|\|

*garbhajanmajarājñānamṛtyunārakajaṃ tathā \|*  
*duḥkhaṃ sahasraśo bhedair bhidyate munisattama \|\| 9 \|\|*

The sufferings arising in pregnancy, birth, old age, ignorance, death and hell are divided into myriad classes, best of sages. 9

## \|\| 6.5.10 \|\|

*sukumāratanurgarbhe janturbahumalāvṛte \|*  
*ulvasaṃveṣṭito bhugnapṛṣṭhagrīvāsthisaṃhatiḥ \|\| 10 \|\|*

The tender-bodied embryo in the womb is surrounded by filth, wrapped in a caul, with its back, neck and bones hunched over. 10

## \|\| 6.5.11 \|\|

*atyamlakaṭutīkṣmoṣṇalavaṇair mātṛbhojanaiḥ \|*  
*atyantatāpair atyarthaṃ vardhamānātivedanaḥ \|\| 11 \|\|*

The suffering of the growing foetus is heightened by discomfort caused by acidic, pungent, sharp, hot and salty foods eaten by its mother. 11

## \|\| 6.5.12 \|\|

*prasāraṇākuñcanādau nāṅgānāṃ prabhurātmanaḥ \|*  
*śakṛnmūtramahāpaṅkaśāyī sarvatra pīḍitaḥ \|\| 12 \|\|*

Unable to stretch or bend its limbs and lying in a mire of excrement and urine, it’s afflicted on all sides. 12

## \|\| 6.5.13 \|\|

*nirucchvāsaḥ sacaitanyaḥ smarañ janmaśatāny atha \|*  
*āste garbhe 'tiduḥkhena nijakarmanibandhanaḥ \|\| 13 \|\|*

It cannot breathe but is conscious and, recalling earlier births by the hundred, it waits in the womb amid great suffering, bound by deeds performed in previous lives. 13

## \|\| 6.5.14 \|\|

*jāyamānaḥ purīṣāsṛṅmūtraśukrāvilānanaḥ \|*  
*prājāpatyena vātena pīḍyamānāsthibandhanaḥ \|\| 14 \|\|*

As it’s being born, its face is smeared with faeces, urine, blood and semen, and its bones and sinews wracked by labour pains. 14

## \|\| 6.5.15 \|\|

*adhomukho vai kriyate prabalaiḥ sūtimārutaiḥ \|*  
*kleśānniṣkrāntimāpnoti jaṭharānmāturāturaḥ \|\| 15 \|\|*

Upside down, it’s squeezed from the womb by strong and painful contractions and is beset by woe. 15

## \|\| 6.5.16 \|\|

*murchāmavāpya mahatīṃ saṃspṛṣṭo bāhyavāyunā \|*  
*vijñānabhraṃśamāpnoti jātaś ca munisattama \|\| 16 \|\|*

The infant is deprived of earlier memories when it breathes the air outside, best of brahmins, and is born in ignorance. 16

## \|\| 6.5.17 \|\|

*kaṇṭakair iva tunnāṅgaḥ krakacair iva dāritaḥ \|*  
*pūtivraṇānnipatito dharaṇyāṃ krimiko yathā \|\| 17 \|\|*

As if its limbs are pricked by thorns or hacked with saws, it falls to the ground like a maggot from a foetid wound. 17

## \|\| 6.5.18 \|\|

*kaṇḍūyane 'pi cāśaktaḥ parivartepyanīśvaraḥ \|*  
*snānapānādikāhāram apy āpnoti parecchayā \|\| 18 \|\|*

Unable even to scratch itself, the infant lies in a helpless state, dependent on the will of others for washing, food and drink. 18

## \|\| 6.5.19 \|\|

*aśuciprastare suptaḥ kīṭadraṃśādibhis tathā \|*  
*bhakṣyamāṇo 'pi naivaiṣāṃ samartho vinivāraṇe \|\| 19 \|\|*

Lying on a dirty rug, the babe is bitten by mosquitoes, bugs and other pests, and cannot fend them off. 19

## \|\| 6.5.20 \|\|

*janmaduḥkhānyanekāni janmanonantariṇī ca \|*  
*bālabhāve yadā pnoti hyādhibhautādikāni ca \|\| 20 \|\|*

Many are the earthly pains attending birth, and many are those which follow them in childhood. 20

## \|\| 6.5.21 \|\|

*ajñānatamasācchanno mūḍhāntaḥkaraṇo naraḥ \|*  
*na jānāti kutaḥ kohaṃ kvāhaṃ gantā kimātmanaḥ \|\| 21 \|\|*

Shrouded in ignorance and dullness, and filled with delusion, the individual knows not whence he came, who he is, whither he will go or his own nature. 21

## \|\| 6.5.22 \|\|

*kenabandhena baddhohaṃ kāraṇaṃ kimakāraṇam \|*  
*kiṃ kāryaṃ kimakāryaṃ vā kiṃ vācyaṃ kiṃ ca nocyate \|\| 22 \|\|*

He knows not the bonds that bind him, what’s a cause and what is not, what’s to be done and what is not, what’s to be said and what is not, 22

## \|\| 6.5.23 \|\|

*ko dharmaḥ kaś ca vādharmaḥ kasminvarte 'tha vā katham \|*  
*kiṃ kartavyamakartavyaṃ kiṃ vā kiṅguṇadoṣavat \|\| 23 \|\|*

What is virtue and what is not, where virtue exists and why, what is duty and what is not and what is good and what is not. 23

## \|\| 6.5.24 \|\|

*evaṃ paśusamair muḍhair ajñānaprabhavaṃ mahat \|*  
*avāpyate narair duḥkhaṃ śiśrodaraparāyaṇaiḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

Thus, deluded individuals, obsessed with their own penis and their stomach, are no better than the animals and endure great suffering born of ignorance. 24

## \|\| 6.5.25 \|\|

*ajñānaṃ tāmaso bhāvaḥ kāryāraṃbhapravṛttayaḥ \|*  
*ajñānināṃ pravartante karmalopāstato dvija \|\| 25 \|\|*

Ignorance is a state of dullness, brahmin, because of which unknowing beings, who should engage in sacrifice, neglect such acts. 25

## \|\| 6.5.26 \|\|

*narakaṃ karmaṇāṃ lopātphalamāhurmanīṣiṇaḥ \|*  
*tasmādajñānināṃ duḥkhamiha cāmutra cottamam \|\| 26 \|\|*

Great sages have declared that hell is the consequence of neglected worship. As a result, the ignorant suffer greatly in this life and the next. 26

## \|\| 6.5.27 \|\|

*jarājarjaradehaś ca śithilāvayavaḥ pumān \|*  
*vicalacchīrṇadaśano valisnāyuśirāvṛtaḥ \|\| 27 \|\|*

When a man’s body is old and frail, his limbs grow weak, his teeth are loose or missing and sinews, veins and wrinkles cover him, 27

## \|\| 6.5.28 \|\|

*dūrapranaṣṭanayano vyomāntargatatārakaḥ \|*  
*nāsāvivalaniryātalomapuñjaścaladvapuḥ \|\| 28 \|\|*

He can’t see far but stares blankly, hairs sprout from his nostrils and he’s wracked by tremors. 28

## \|\| 6.5.29 \|\|

*prakaṭībhūtasarvāsthirnatapṛṣṭhāsthisaṃhatiḥ \|*  
*utsannajaṭharāgnitvād alpāhāro 'placeṣṭitaḥ \|\| 29 \|\|*

His bones stick out, his back is bent, his joints are swollen, he eats little as digestion fails and he hardly moves at all. 29

## \|\| 6.5.30 \|\|

*kṛcchrāccaṅkramaṇotthānaśayanāsanaceṣṭitaḥ \|*  
*mandībhavacchrotranetraḥsravallālāvilānanaḥ \|\| 30 \|\|*

It’s hard for him to walk or rise, lie or sit or move; his eyes and ears grow weak; he dribbles down his chin. 30

## \|\| 6.5.31 \|\|

*anāyattaiḥ samastaiś ca karaṇermaraṇonmukhaḥ \|*  
*tatkṣaṇepyanubhūtānāmasmarta'khilavastunām \|\| 31 \|\|*

All his senses fail as he approaches death, and he forgets events as soon as they have happened. 31

## \|\| 6.5.32 \|\|

*sakṛduccārite vākye samudbhūta mahāśramaḥ \|*  
*śvāsakāśasamudbhūtamahāyāsaprajāgaraḥ \|\| 32 \|\|*

When he has to repeat himself, he’s overcome with weariness, but the dreadful strain of drawing breath and coughing robs him of his sleep. 32

## \|\| 6.5.33 \|\|

*anyenotthāpyate 'nyena tathā saṃveśyate jarī \|*  
*bhṛtyātmaputradārāṇāmavāmānāspadī kṛtaḥ \|\| 33 \|\|*

When a man is old, others must help him up, and others dress him, but his servants, sons and wife all mock him. 33

## \|\| 6.5.34 \|\|

*prakṣīṇākhilaśaucaś ca vihārāhārasaspṛhaḥ \|*  
*hāsyaḥ parijanasyāpi nirviṇṇāśeṣabāndhavaḥ \|\| 34 \|\|*

He cannot clean himself, yet still craves food and other pleasures. His attendants laugh at him and all his kin ignore him. 34

## \|\| 6.5.35 \|\|

*anubhūtamivānyasmiñjanmanyātmaviceṣṭitam \|*  
*saṃsmaranyauvane dīrghaṃ niśvasatyabhitāpitaḥ \|\| 35 \|\|*

Recalling exploits of his younger days as if they happened in another lifetime, he sighs deeply and is troubled. 35

## \|\| 6.5.36 \|\|

*evamādīni duḥkhāni jarāyāmanubūya vai \|*  
*maraṇe yāni duḥkhāni prāpnoti śṛṇutānyapi \|\| 36 \|\|*

Having endured the woes of dotage, now hear about the torments met in death. 36

## \|\| 6.5.37 \|\|

*ślathadgrīvāṅghrihaste 'tha vyāpto vepathunā bhṛśam \|*  
*muhurglāniḥ paravaśo muhurjñānalavānvitaḥ \|\| 37 \|\|*

His neck, fingers and hands hang loose and his body begins to twitch. One minute, he lies limp and tired, the next, he seems to wake a little. 37

## \|\| 6.5.38 \|\|

*hiraṇyadhānyatanayabhāryābhṛtyagṛhādiṣu \|*  
*ete kathaṃ bhaviṣyantītyatīva mamatākulaḥ \|\| 38 \|\|*

Remembering the things he owns, he frets about his gold, stores, sons, wives, servants, houses and all the rest. 38

## \|\| 6.5.39 \|\|

*marmabhidbhir bhahārogaiḥ krakacair iva dāruṇaiḥ \|*  
*śarair ivāntakasyograiś chadyamānāsubandhanaḥ \|\| 39 \|\|*

His bones and joints are wracked by stabbing pains of Death’s sharp arrows, which rip his vital organs like cruel saws. 39

## \|\| 6.5.40 \|\|

*parivartitatārākṣo hastapādaṃ muhuḥ kṣipan \|*  
*saṃśuṣyamāṇatālvoṣṭhapuṭo ghuraghurāyate \|\| 40 \|\|*

His eyes roll back, his hands and feet convulse, his palate, lips and tongue are dry, then the rattle of death is heard. 40

## \|\| 6.5.41 \|\|

*niruddhakaṇṭho doṣaughair udānaśvasāpīḍitaḥ \|*  
*tāpena mahatā vyāptastṛṣā cārtas tathā kṣudhā \|\| 41 \|\|*

His throat is blocked by floods of morbid humours and breath is laboured. Afflicted by great suffering, thirst and hunger torment him. 41

## \|\| 6.5.42 \|\|

*kleśādutkrāntimāpnoti yamakiṅkarapīḍitaḥ \|*  
*tataś ca yātanādehaṃ kleśena pratipadyate \|\| 42 \|\|*

Overwhelmed by pain as Yama’s minions goad him, he dies, only then to receive another body to be tortured in the realms of hell. 42

## \|\| 6.5.43 \|\|

*etānyanyāni cogrāṇi duḥkhāni maraṇe nṛṇām \|*  
*śṛṇuṣva narake yāni prāpyante puruṣair mṛtaiḥ \|\| 43 \|\|*

The living endure these and other frightful pains at the time of death. Now listen to the torments that the dead will meet there. 43

## \|\| 6.5.44 \|\|

*yāmyakiṅkarapāśādigrahaṇaṃ daṇḍatāḍanam \|*  
*yamasya darśanaṃ cogramugramārgavilokanam \|\| 44 \|\|*

Caught in the noose of Yama’s servants and struck with rods, they must bear the fearful sight of the Lord of Death as they face the terrible road to hell. 44

## \|\| 6.5.45 \|\|

*karaṃbhavālukāvahniyantraśastrāsibhīṣaṇe \|*  
*pratyekaṃ narakeyāś ca yātanā dvija duḥsahāḥ \|\| 45 \|\|*

In that realm of frightful burning sands, fires, machines, weapons and the rest are found each kind of hellish torment, brahmin, so difficult to endure. 45

## \|\| 6.5.46 \|\|

*krakacaiḥ pāṭyamānānāṃ mūṣāyāṃ cāpi dahyatām \|*  
*kuṭhāraiḥ kṛtyamānānāṃ bhūmau cāpi nikhanyatām \|\| 46 \|\|*

Hacked with saws, cooked in pots, chopped with axes, buried in the ground, 46

## \|\| 6.5.47 \|\|

*śūleṣvāropyamāṇānaṃ vyāghravaktre praveśyatām \|*  
*gṛdhraiḥ saṃbhakṣyamāṇānāṃ dvīpibhiścopabhūjyatām \|\| 47 \|\|*

Impaled on stakes, eaten by tigers, torn by vultures, devoured by leopards, 47

## \|\| 6.5.48 \|\|

*kvāthyatāṃ tailamadhye ca klidyatāṃ kṣārakardame \|*  
*uccānnipātyamānānāṃ kṣipyatāṃ kṣepayantrakaiḥ \|\| 48 \|\|*

Boiled in oil, drowned in slime, dropped from heights, thrown from catapults— 48

## \|\| 6.5.49 \|\|

*narake yāni duḥkhāni pāpahetūdbhavāni vai \|*  
*prāpyante nārakair vipra teṣāṃ saṃkhyā na vidyate \|\| 49 \|\|*

The torments they endure in hell, arising from their own misdeeds and administered by the denizens of that realm, brahmin, are innumerable. 49

## \|\| 6.5.50 \|\|

*na kevalaṃ dvijaśreṣṭha narake duḥkhapaddhatiḥ \|*  
*svagaṃpi pātabhītasta kṣayiṣṇornāsti nirvṛtiḥ \|\| 50 \|\|*

But hell is not the only place of pain, best of brahmins. In heaven, too, a being is never free from the fear of falling to a lower realm. 50

## \|\| 6.5.51 \|\|

*punaś ca garbhe bhavati jāyate ca punaḥ punaḥ \|*  
*garbhe vilīyate bhūyo jāyamāno 'stameti vai \|\| 51 \|\|*

There he will be conceived, only to be born once more. Leaving the womb and entering the world again, he’s destined for another death. 51

## \|\| 6.5.52 \|\|

*jātamātraś ca mriyate bālabhāve 'tha yauvane \|*  
*madhyamaṃ vā vayaḥ prāpya vārdhake vātha vā mṛtiḥ \|\| 52 \|\|*

He may die an infant or in childhood, as a youth or adult or in his dotage, but death is certain. 52

## \|\| 6.5.53 \|\|

*yāvajjīvati tāvacca duḥkhair nānāvidhaiḥ plutaḥ \|*  
*taṃ tu kāraṇapakṣmaughair āste kārpāsabījavat \|\| 53 \|\|*

While he lives, he’s beset by varied troubles, like a cotton seed amid a mass of fibres before they’re spun as thread. 53

## \|\| 6.5.54 \|\|

*dravyanāśe tathotpattau pālane ca sadā nṛṇām \|*  
*bhavantyanekaduḥkhāni tathaiveṣṭavipattiṣu \|\| 54 \|\|*

Winning, keeping and losing wealth all bring woe to men, as do the pains of those we love. 54

## \|\| 6.5.55 \|\|

*yadyatprītikaraṃ puṃsāṃ vastu maitreya jāyate \|*  
*tadeva duḥkhavṛkṣasya bījatvamupagacchati \|\| 55 \|\|*

Each thing that brings us joy, Maitreya, becomes the seed of a tree of pain. 55

## \|\| 6.5.56 \|\|

*kalatraputramitrārthagṛhakṣetradhanādikaiḥ \|*  
*kriyate na tathā bhūri sukhaṃ puṃsāṃ yathā sukham \|\| 56 \|\|*

Wives, sons, friends, possessions, houses, fields, wealth and all the rest cause more trouble than the joy they yield. 56

## \|\| 6.5.57 \|\|

*iti saṃsāraduḥkhārkatāpatāpitacetasām \|*  
*vimuktipādapacchāyāmṛte kutra sukhaṃ nṛṇām \|\| 57 \|\|*

Where may those whose hearts are thus tormented by scorching rays of worldly pain seek shelter, other than the shade of the tree of liberation? 57

## \|\| 6.5.58 \|\|

*tadasya trividhasta'pi duḥkhajātasya vai mama \|*  
*garbhajanmajarādyeṣu sthāneṣu prabhaviṣyataḥ \|\| 58 \|\|*

The wise therefore hold that the antidote for this threefold suffering arising in the womb—birth, old age and the other states of life— 58

## \|\| 6.5.59 \|\|

*nirastātiśayāhlādasukhabhāvaikalakṣaṇā \|*  
*bheṣajaṃ bhagavatprāptirekāntātyantikī matā \|\| 59 \|\|*

Is complete and final union with the lord, which alone is marked by a state of bliss and eclipses every other joy. 59

## \|\| 6.5.60 \|\|

*tasmāt tatprāptaye yatnaḥ kartavyaḥ paṇḍitair naraiḥ \|*  
*tatprāptiheturjñānaṃ ca karma coktaṃ mahāmune \|\| 60 \|\|*

That’s why the wise should strive for him. Knowledge and sacrifice are said to be the means for doing so, great sage. 60

## \|\| 6.5.61 \|\|

*āgamotthaṃ vivekācca dvidhā jñānaṃ tad ucyate \|*  
*śabdabrahmāgamamayaṃ paraṃ brahma vivekajam \|\| 61 \|\|*

They say that knowledge is twofold, arising from scripture and from reflection. Scripture consists of the Absolute in the form of the Word, but the highest form of the Absolute is born of meditation. 61

## \|\| 6.5.62 \|\|

*andhaṃ tama ivājñānaṃ dīpavaccendriyodbhavam \|*  
*yathā sūryas tathā jñānaṃ yadviprarṣe vivekajam \|\| 62 \|\|*

Just as the sun dispels the deepest darkness, wise brahmin, so, too, does knowledge gained through the senses and born of meditation shine like a lamp on ignorance. 62

## \|\| 6.5.63 \|\|

*manurapyāha vedārthaṃ smṛtvā yanmunisattama \|*  
*tadetacchrūyatāmatra saṃbandhe gadato mama \|\| 63 \|\|*

Manu himself relayed the import of the Vedas as he recalled it, best of sages. Now listen as I repeat what he said in this regard. 63

## \|\| 6.5.64 \|\|

*dve brahmaṇī veditavye śabdabrahma paraṃ ca yat \|*  
*śabdabrahmaṇi niṣṇātaḥ paraṃ brahmādhigacchati \|\| 64 \|\|*

Two forms of the Absolute are to be recognised: the Word and the Supreme. One well versed in the former may reach the highest state of the latter. 64

## \|\| 6.5.65 \|\|

*dve vai vidye veditavye iti cātharvaṇī śrutiḥ \|*  
*parayā tvakṣaraprāptirṛgvedādimayāparā \|\| 65 \|\|*

The Atharva Veda also describes two forms of knowledge. By means of the higher, one attains the imperishable state, while the other consists of the Ṛg and other Vedas. 65

## \|\| 6.5.66 \|\|

*yat tad avyaktam ajaram acintyam ajam avyayam \|*  
*anirdeśyamarūpaṃ ca pāṇipādādyasaṃyutam \|\| 66 \|\|*

That which is unmanifest, unageing, inconceivable, unborn, undecaying, indescribable, formless, lacking hands and feet and other features 66

## \|\| 6.5.67 \|\|

*vibhu sarvagataṃ nityaṃ bhūtayonirakāraṇam \|*  
*vyāpya vyāptaṃ yataḥ sarvaṃ yad vai paśyanti sūrayaḥ \|\| 67 \|\|*

Is the universal, eternal lord, the uncaused source of all creation, the all-pervading but unpervaded origin of everything—it’s this the sages see. 67

## \|\| 6.5.68 \|\|

*tadbrahma tatparaṃ dhāma tad dhyeyaṃ mokṣakāṃkṣibhiḥ \|*  
*śrutivākyoditaṃ sūkṣmaṃ tadviṣṇoḥ paramaṃ padam \|\| 68 \|\|*

This is the Absolute, Supreme, the abode to be contemplated by those who yearn for liberation. Subtle, but alluded to by words of sacred scripture, it’s the highest state of Viṣṇu. 68

## \|\| 6.5.69 \|\|

*tadeva bhagavadvācyaṃ svarūpaṃ paramātmanaḥ \|*  
*vācako bhagavacchabdastasyādyasvākṣayātmanaḥ \|\| 69 \|\|*

The word ‘blessed’—bhagavant—is also a form of the Supreme, and is the means of expressing the primal, eternal being. 69

## \|\| 6.5.70 \|\|

*evaṃ nigaditārthasta tattattvaṃ tasya tattvataḥ \|*  
*jñāyate yena tajjñānaṃ paramanyattrayīmayam \|\| 70 \|\|*

One who truly grasps the import of this expression will comprehend the other form of knowledge embodied in the Vedas. 70

## \|\| 6.5.71 \|\|

*aśabdagocarasyāpi tasya vai brahmaṇo dvija \|*  
*pūjāyāṃ bhagavacchabdaḥ kriyate hyupacārataḥ \|\| 71 \|\|*

The term bhagavat is used to worship that form of the Absolute, brahmin, which lies beyond the reach of words. 71

## \|\| 6.5.72 \|\|

*śuddhe mahāvibhūtvākhye pare brahmaṇi śabdite \|*  
*maitreya bhagavacchabdaḥsarvakāraṇakāraṇe \|\| 72 \|\|*

Therefore, Maitreya, it’s used to express the pure, supreme Absolute, known as the almighty, the cause of the cause of everything. 72

## \|\| 6.5.73 \|\|

*saṃbharteti tathā bhartā bhakārorthadvayānvitaḥ \|*  
*netā gamayatā sraṣṭā gakārārthas tathā mune \|\| 73 \|\|*

The syllable bha implies the two meanings of maintainer (saṃbhartṛ) and protector (bhartṛ), sage, while the syllable ga signifies the leader, driver (gamayitṛ) and creator. 73

## \|\| 6.5.74 \|\|

*aiśvaryasya samagrasya vīryasya yaśasaḥ śriyaḥ \|*  
*jñānavairāgyayoścaiva ṣaṇṇāṃ bhaga itīraṇā \|\| 74 \|\|*

The syllables bha and ga express six qualities: supremacy over all, power, glory, majesty, knowledge and asceticism. 74

## \|\| 6.5.75 \|\|

*vasaṃti tatra bhūtāni bhūtātmanyakhilātmani \|*  
*sa ca bhūteṣvaśeṣeṣu vakārārthastato 'vyayaḥ \|\| 75 \|\|*

Va implies all creatures dwell (vasanti) in the universal being at the heart of all creation, and that the everlasting deity dwells in all. 75

## \|\| 6.5.76 \|\|

*evameṣa mahāñchabdo maitreya bhagavāniti \|*  
*paramabrahmabhūtasya vāsudevasya nānyagaḥ \|\| 76 \|\|*

Thus, this great word bhagavat applies to the highest Absolute, Vāsudeva and nothing else, you peerless sage. 76

## \|\| 6.5.77 \|\|

*tatra pūjyapadārthoktiparibhāṣāsamanvitaḥ \|*  
*śabdo 'yaṃ nopacāreṇa tvanyatra hy upacārataḥ \|\| 77 \|\|*

In this context, the term implies a state of reverence. It has a special expressive function and is not figurative, but in other contexts, it may be so. 77

## \|\| 6.5.78 \|\|

*utpattiṃ pralayaṃ caiva bhūtānāmāgatiṃ gatim \|*  
*vetti vidyāmāvidyāṃ ca sa vācyo bhagavāniti \|\| 78 \|\|*

One who understands the beginning of creation and its end, the right way and its opposite and knowledge and its absence may be called a bhagavat, a blessed one. 78

## \|\| 6.5.79 \|\|

*jñānaśaktibalaiśvayavīryatejāṃsyaśeṣataḥ \|*  
*bhagavacchabdavācyāti vinā heyair guṇādibhiḥ \|\| 79 \|\|*

In the former instance, the term denotes wisdom, energy, strength, supremacy, power and brilliance without remainder, and the absence of any negative traits. 79

## \|\| 6.5.80 \|\|

*sarvāṇi tatra bhūtāni vasāṃti paramātmani \|*  
*bhūteṣu ca sa sarvātmā vāsudevastataḥ smṛtaḥ \|\| 80 \|\|*

As all beings dwell in the highest being and the universal being dwells in them, he is known as Vāsudeva. 80

## \|\| 6.5.81 \|\|

*khāṇḍikyajanakāyāha pṛṣṭaḥ keśidhvajaḥ purā \|*  
*nābhavyākhyāmanantasya vāsudevasya tattvataḥ \|\| 81 \|\|*

This is how in former times Keśidhvaja, when asked about eternal Vāsudeva’s name, accurately explained it to Khāṇḍikya Janaka: 81

## \|\| 6.5.82 \|\|

*bhūteṣu vasate soṃ'tarvasaṃtyatra ca tāni yat \|*  
*dhātā vidhātā jagatāṃ vāsudevastataḥ prabhuḥ \|\| 82 \|\|*

Because he abides in beings and they abide in him, the creator and disposer of the worlds is Lord Vāsudeva. 82

## \|\| 6.5.83 \|\|

*sa sarvabhūtaprakṛtiṃ vikārānguṇādidośāṃś ca mune vyatītaḥ \|*  
*atītasarvāvaraṇokhilātmā tenāstṛtaṃ yadbhuvanāntarāle \|\| 83 \|\|*

He transcends the nature of all creation, sage, its transformations and all its qualities, good and bad. He’s beyond all limits and is at the heart of everything. All space in the world is filled by him. 83

## \|\| 6.5.84 \|\|

*samastakalyāṇaguṇātmako 'sau svaśaktileśāvṛtabhūtavargaḥ \|*  
*icchāgṛhītābhimatorudehaḥsaṃsādhitāśeṣajagadvito yaḥ \|\| 84 \|\|*

He is at the heart of all blessings and goodness, and accomplishes the creation of all beings with a mere fraction of his ability. He assumes an expansive form at will, and showers benefits on all the world. 84

## \|\| 6.5.85 \|\|

*tejobalaiśvaryamahāvabodha suvīryaśaktyādiguṇaikarāśiḥ \|*  
*paraḥ parāṇāṃ sakalā na yatra kleśādayaḥsaṃti parāvareśe \|\| 85 \|\|*

He is the sole store of energy, power, supremacy, perception, might, strength and other qualities. Higher than the highest, no faults exist in the all-inclusive lord. 85

## \|\| 6.5.86 \|\|

*sa īśvaro vyaṣṭisamaṣṭirūpo vyaktasvarūpaḥ \|*  
*sarveśvaraḥsarvadṛk sarvavicca samastaśaktiḥ parameśvarākhyaḥ \|\| 86 \|\|*

He is sovereign, one but many, unmanifest yet visible, lord of all, all-seeing, all-knowing, all-powerful and is called the highest master. 86

## \|\| 6.5.87 \|\|

*saṃjñāyate yena tadastadoṣaṃ śuddhaṃ paraṃ nirmalamekarūpam \|*  
*saṃdṛśyate vāpyavagamyate vā tajjñānamajñānamatonyaduktam \|\| 87 \|\|*

That by which he’s known, beheld or understood is faultless, pure, supreme, unsullied, uniform spiritual knowledge. All else is ignorance. 87

## Chapter 6 - Khāṇḍikya and his cousin Keśidhvaja

## \|\| 6.6.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*svādhyāyasaṃyamābhyāṃ sa dṛśyate puruṣottamaḥ \|*  
*tatprāptikāraṇaṃ brahma tad etad iti paṭhyate \|\| 1 \|\|*

The highest spirit may be perceived through Vedic recitation and through yoga. That’s why the Absolute, the means of attaining that state, is described by the formula ‘It is this—tad etat’. 1

## \|\| 6.6.2 \|\|

*svādhyāyādhyogamāsīta yogātsvādhyāyamāvaset \|*  
*svādhyāyayogasaṃpattyā paramātmā prakāśate \|\| 2 \|\|*

After recitation, one should practise yoga, and, after yoga, recitation. Through excellence in both, the highest spirit manifests. 2

## \|\| 6.6.3 \|\|

*tadīkṣaṇāya svādhyāyaścakṣuryogas tathā param \|*  
*na māṃ sa cakṣuṣā draṣṭuṃ brahmabhūtaḥsa śakyate \|\| 3 \|\|*

Vedic recitation is one eye with which to see the lord, and yoga is the other, as the Absolute lies beyond the bodily eye. 3

## \|\| 6.6.4 \|\|

*maitreya uvāca*  
*bhagavaṃstamahaṃ yogaṃ jñātumicchāmi taṃ vada \|*  
*jñāte yatrākhilādhāraṃ paśyeyaṃ parameśvaram \|\| 4 \|\|*

Speak on yoga, master, as I wish to understand it. When I comprehend that, I may be able to perceive the highest lord, foundation of the universe. 4

## \|\| 6.6.5 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*yathā keśidhvajaḥ prāha khāṇḍikyāya mahātmane \|*  
*najakāya purā yoga tamahaṃ kathayāmi te \|\| 5 \|\|*

Parāśara:

I’ll describe yoga to you, great sage, just as Keśidhvaja explained it to Khāṇḍikya Janaka in former times. 5

## \|\| 6.6.6 \|\|

*maitreya uvāca*  
*khāṇḍikya ko 'bhavadbrahmanko vā keśidhvajaḥ kṛtī \|*  
*kathaṃ tayoś ca saṃvādo yogasaṃbandhakāraṇāt \|\| 6 \|\|*

Maitreya:

Who was Khāṇḍikya, brahmin, and who was Keśidhvaja? How did their talk of yoga come about? 6

## \|\| 6.6.7 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*dharmadhvajo vai janaka stasya putro 'mitadhvajaḥ \|*  
*kṛtadhvajaś ca nāmnāsītsadādhyātmaratirnṛpaḥ \|\| 7 \|\|*

Parāśara:

Janaka Dharmadhvaja had one son, Amitadhvaja, and another named Kṛtadhvaja, a king who constantly found joy in the highest spirit. 7

## \|\| 6.6.8 \|\|

*kṛtadhvajasya putro 'bhūtkhyātaḥ keśidhvajo nṛpaḥ \|*  
*putromitadhvajasyāpi khāṇḍikyajanako 'bhavat \|\| 8 \|\|*

Kṛtadhvaja had a son named Keśidhvaja, brahmin. Amitadhvaja’s son was Khāṇḍikya Janaka. 8

## \|\| 6.6.9 \|\|

*karmamārgeṇa khāṇḍikyaḥ pṛtivyāmabhavatpatiḥ \|*  
*keśidhvajo 'pyatīvāsīdātmavidyāviśāradaḥ \|\| 9 \|\|*

Khāṇḍikya excelled on the path of ritual action and was skilled in worldly matters, but Keśidhvaja was an expert in spiritual knowledge. 9

## \|\| 6.6.10 \|\|

*tāvubhāvapi caivāstāṃ vijigīṣū parasparam \|*  
*keśidhyajena khāṇḍikyaḥsvarājyādavaropitaḥ \|\| 10 \|\|*

These two, however, both wished to usurp the other, and Keśidhvaja deprived Khāṇḍikya of his kingdom. 10

## \|\| 6.6.11 \|\|

*purodhasā mantribhiś ca samaveto 'lpasādhanaḥ \|*  
*rājyānnirākṛtaḥso 'tha durgāraṇyacaro 'bhavat \|\| 11 \|\|*

Driven from his realm, Khāṇḍikya wandered in the jungle, where travel is not easy, accompanied by his priest and ministers but taking little else. 11

## \|\| 6.6.12 \|\|

*iyāha so 'pi subahūnyajñāñjñānavyapāśrayaḥ \|*  
*brahmavidyāmadhiṣṭhāya tarttu mṛtyumavidyayā \|\| 12 \|\|*

He still conducted sacrifices, but, lacking true spiritual insight, he hoped in his ignorance to gain knowledge of the Absolute and escape from death thereby. 12

## \|\| 6.6.13 \|\|

*ekadā varttamānasya yāgo yogavidāṃ vara \|*  
*gharmadhenuṃ jaghānograḥ śārdulo vijane vane \|\| 13 \|\|*

One day, Keśidhvaja was engaged in yoga when a fearsome tiger killed his dairy-cow in a trackless forest, you yoga expert. 13

## \|\| 6.6.14 \|\|

*tato rājā hatāṃ śrutvā dhanuṃ vyāghreṇa cartvijaḥ \|*  
*prāyaścitaṃ sa papraccha kimatreti vidhīyatām \|\| 14 \|\|*

Hearing that his cow was dead, the king then asked his priests about atonement. 14

## \|\| 6.6.15 \|\|

*tepyūcurna vayaṃ vidma kaśeruḥ pṛcchatāmiti \|*  
*kaśerurapi tenoktastathaiva prāha bhārgavam \|\| 15 \|\|*

‘We don’t know,’ they said, ‘but you should question Kaśeru.’ The king then asked that sage, who gave the scion of Bhṛgu the same reply: 15

## \|\| 6.6.16 \|\|

*śunakaṃ pṛccha rājaindra nāhaṃ vedmi sa vetsyati \|*  
*sa gatvā kamapṛcchacca so 'py āha śṛṇu yanmune \|\| 16 \|\|*

‘I don’t know, your majesty, but ask Śunaka. He’s an expert in such things.’ The king then went to Śunaka and asked him, sage, and Śunaka replied, ‘Listen: 16

## \|\| 6.6.17 \|\|

*na keśarurna caivāhaṃ na caikaḥsāṃprataṃ bhuvi \|*  
*vettyeka eva tvacchatruḥ khāṇḍikyo yo jitastvayā \|\| 17 \|\|*

Neither Kaśeru, nor I, nor any other man on earth at present knows the answer. The only one who truly knows is your rival Khāṇḍikya whom you’ve vanquished.’ 17

## \|\| 6.6.18 \|\|

*sa cāha taṃ vrajāmyeṣa praṣṭumātmarīpuṃ mune \|*  
*prāpta eva mahāyajño yadi māṃ sa haniṣyati \|\| 18 \|\|*

The king replied to Śunaka, ‘I’ll go and ask my enemy, sage, and even if he kills me, I’ll obtain the reward of sacrifice. 18

## \|\| 6.6.19 \|\|

*prāyaścittamaśeṣeṇa sa cetpṛṣṭo vadiṣyati \|*  
*tataścāvikalo yogo muniśreṣṭha bhaviṣyati \|\| 19 \|\|*

On the other hand, if I ask him and he tells me how to make amends, then my yoga practice will be unimpaired, best of sages.’ 19

## \|\| 6.6.20 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*ityuktvā rathamāruhya kṛṣṇājinadharo nṛpaḥ \|*  
*vanaṃ jagāma yatrāste sa khāṇḍikyo mahāmatiḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

Parāśara:

So saying, the king, dressed like an ascetic in a black antelope hide, mounted his chariot and entered the forest where wise Khāṇḍikya camped. 20

## \|\| 6.6.21 \|\|

*tamāpatantamālokya khāṇḍikyo ripumātmanaḥ \|*  
*provāca krodhatāmrākṣaḥsamāropitakārmukaḥ \|\| 21 \|\|*

When Khāṇḍikya saw his enemy approaching, his eyes grew red with fury and, nocking an arrow to his bow, he said, 21

## \|\| 6.6.22 \|\|

*khāṇḍikya uvāca*  
*kṛṣṇājinaṃ tvaṃ kavacamābadhyāsmānhaniṣyasi \|*  
*kṛṣṇājinadhare vetsi na mayi prahariṣyati \|\| 22 \|\|*

Khāṇḍikya:

You attack me with a deer skin as protection, and you think I won’t fight back because I’m wearing the same thing! 22

## \|\| 6.6.23 \|\|

*mṛgāṇāṃ vada pṛṣṭheṣu mūḍha kṛṣṇājinaṃ na kim \|*  
*yeṣāṃ mayā tvāya cogrāḥ prahitāḥ śitasāyakāḥ \|\| 23 \|\|*

But tell me, fool, isn’t this the hide from the back of an antelope that you and I both killed with our deadly sharpened arrows? 23

## \|\| 6.6.24 \|\|

*sa tvāmahaṃ haniṣyāmi na me jīvanvimokṣyase \|*  
*ātatāyyasi durbuddhe mama rājyaharo ripuḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

I’m going to kill you now. You’ll not escape alive. You, the foolish thief who stole my realm, are now my enemy! 24

## \|\| 6.6.25 \|\|

*keśidhvaja uvāca*  
*khāṇḍikya saṃśayaṃ praṣṭuṃ bhavantamahāmāgataḥ \|*  
*na tvāṃ hantuṃ vicāryaitatkopaṃ bāṇaṃ vimuñca vā \|\| 25 \|\|*

Keśidhvaja:

I’ve come to ask a question, Khāṇḍikya, not to fight you, so set aside your anger. Put down that arrow. 25

## \|\| 6.6.26 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*tataḥsa mantribhiḥ sārdhamekānte sapurohitaḥ \|*  
*mantrayāmāsa khāṇḍikyaḥsarvair eva mahāmatiḥ \|\| 26 \|\|*

Parāśara:

Wise Khāṇḍikya then consulted all his ministers and priests in private. 26

## \|\| 6.6.27 \|\|

*tamūcurmatriṇo vadhyo ripureṣa vaśaṃ gataḥ \|*  
*hate 'smin pṛthivī sarvā tava vaśyā bhaviṣyati \|\| 27 \|\|*

‘Now your rival’s in your power you should kill him,’ his advisors said. ‘If you do so this whole world is yours.’ 27

## \|\| 6.6.28 \|\|

*khāṇḍikyaścāha tānsarvānevametanna saṃśayaḥ \|*  
*hatesminpṛtivī sarvā mama vaśyā bhaviṣyati \|\| 28 \|\|*

‘It’s surely true,’ Khāṇḍikya said, ‘that once he’s dead the world is mine. 28

## \|\| 6.6.29 \|\|

*paralokajayastasya pṛthivī sakalā mama \|*  
*na hanmi cellokajayo mama tasya vasuṃdharā \|\| 29 \|\|*

But victory in the next world will be his, even if this world belongs to me. If I forbear to kill him, I’ll win in the afterlife, and he can have this mundane realm. 29

## \|\| 6.6.30 \|\|

*nāhaṃ manye lokajayādadhikā syādvasuṃdharā \|*  
*paralokajayo 'nantaḥsvalpakālo mahījayaḥ \|\| 30 \|\|*

Victory in the next world is eternal, but victory here is fleeting. I’ll therefore spare the man and listen to his question.’ 30

## \|\| 6.6.31 \|\|

*tasmānnainaṃ haniṣyāmi yatpṛcchati vadāmi tat \|\| 31 \|\|*

Parāśara:

Khāṇḍikya Janaka approached his adversary and said, ‘Ask me all you wish to say, and I’ll reply.’ 31

## \|\| 6.6.32 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*tatastamabyupetyāha khāṇḍikyajanako ripum \|*  
*praṣṭavyaṃ yattvāya sarvaṃ tatpṛcchasva vadāmyaham \|\| 32 \|\|*

Keśidhvaja told him that his cow had died, brahmin, and asked how he should make amends. 32

## \|\| 6.6.33 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*tataḥ sarvaṃ yathāvṛttaṃ gharmadhenuvadhaṃ dvija \|*  
*kathāṃyatvā sa papraccha prāyaśtittaṃ hi tadgatam \|\| 33 \|\|*

Khāṇḍikya then described to Keśidhvaja the atonement to be performed in full, brahmin, according to the law. 33

## \|\| 6.6.34 \|\|

*sa cācaṣṭa yathānyāyaṃ dvija keśidhvajāya tat \|*  
*prāyaścittamaśeṣeṇa yaddhai tatra vidhīyate \|\| 34 \|\|*

Now that great Khāṇḍikya understood Keśidhvaja’s intention, he granted him permission to proceed to the sacrificial ground, where he duly undertook the rites. 34

## \|\| 6.6.35 \|\|

*viditārthaḥsa tenaiva hyanujñāto mahātmanā \|*  
*yāgabhūmim upāgamya cakre sarvāḥ kriyāḥ kramāt \|\| 35 \|\|*

When King Keśidhvaja had carried out the rituals in the proper order and according to the law, and performed the requisite purification afterwards, he’d done his duty, but then he thought, 35

## \|\| 6.6.36 \|\|

*krameṇa vidhivadyāgaṃ nītvā so 'vabhṛthāplutaḥ \|*  
*kṛtakṛtyastato bhūtvā cintayāmāsa pārthivaḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

‘I’ve worshipped all the priests and honoured the assembled officiants. Similarly, I’ve satisfied the desires of all who wanted something. 36

## \|\| 6.6.37 \|\|

*pūjitāś ca dvijāḥsarve sadasyā mānitā mayā \|*  
*tathaivārthijano 'py arthair yo 'rjito 'bhimatair mayā \|\| 37 \|\|*

I’ve behaved appropriately towards everybody here, so why’s my heart not satisfied with this sacrifice?’ 37

## \|\| 6.6.38 \|\|

*yathārhamasya lokasya mayā sarvaṃ viceṣṭitam \|*  
*aniṣpannakriyaṃ cetastathāpi mama kiṃ yathā \|\| 38 \|\|*

Reflecting thus, he realised that he hadn’t paid Khāṇḍikya his sacrificial fee, the guru-dakṣiṇā. 38

## \|\| 6.6.39 \|\|

*itthaṃ saṃcintayannaiva sasmāra sa mahīpatiḥ \|*  
*khāṇḍikyāya na datteti mayā vai gurudakṣiṇā \|\| 39 \|\|*

Mounted on his chariot, the king returned to the trackless wood, Maitreya, where Khāṇḍikya resided. 39

## \|\| 6.6.40 \|\|

*sa jagāma tadā bhūyo rathamāruhya pārthivaḥ \|*  
*maitreya durgagahanaṃ khāṇḍikyo yatra saṃsthitaḥ \|\| 40 \|\|*

When Khāṇḍikya saw the king returning, he raised his bow and stood intent on killing him, but Keśidhvaja spoke again: 40

## \|\| 6.6.41 \|\|

*khāṇḍikyo 'pi punardṛṣṭvā tamāyāntaṃ dhṛtāyudham \|*  
*tasthau hantuṃ kṛtamatistamāha sa punarnṛpaḥ \|\| 41 \|\|*

‘I haven’t come to fight, Khāṇḍikya, so don’t be angry. Understand I’m here to make the payment due to you as guru. 41

## \|\| 6.6.42 \|\|

*bho nāhaṃ te 'parādhāya prāptaḥ khāṇḍikya mā kruddhāḥ \|*  
*gurornikrayadānāya māmavehi tvamāgatam \|\| 42 \|\|*

I’ve completed all the rituals as instructed, and I wish to compensate you, so name your fee.’ 42

## \|\| 6.6.43 \|\|

*niṣpādito mayā yāgaḥ samyaktvadupadeśataḥ \|*  
*so 'haṃ te dātumicchāmi vṛṇīṣva gurudakṣiṇām \|\| 43 \|\|*

Parāśara:

King Khāṇḍikya consulted with his ministers once more, saying, ‘He wants to pay a fee, so how much should I ask for?’ 43

## \|\| 6.6.44 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*bhūyasya mantribhiḥ sārdhaṃ mantrayāmāsa pārthivaḥ \|*  
*guruniṣkrayakāmo 'yaṃ kiṃ mayā prārthyatāmiti \|\| 44 \|\|*

‘You should ask for your whole realm back again,’ the ministers replied. ‘Skilful men win sovereignty without deploying arms.’ 44

## \|\| 6.6.45 \|\|

*tamūcurmatriṇo rājyamaśaṃṣaṃ prārthyatāmayam \|*  
*śatribhiḥ prārthyate rājyamanā yāsitasainikaiḥ \|\| 45 \|\|*

Wise King Khāṇḍikya laughed at them and said, ‘Why would such a man as me request a fleeting earthly kingdom? 45

## \|\| 6.6.46 \|\|

*prahasya tānāha nṛpaḥsa khāṇḍikyo mahāmatiḥ \|*  
*svalpakālaṃ mahīpālyaṃ mādṛśaiḥ prārthyate katham \|\| 46 \|\|*

You provide advice for handling mundane matters but know nothing of the highest truth or how it may be reached.’ 46

## \|\| 6.6.47 \|\|

*evametadbhavanto 'tra hyarthasādhanamantriṇaḥ \|*  
*paramārthaḥ kathaṃ ko 'tra yūyaṃ nātra vicakṣaṇāḥ \|\| 47 \|\|*

Parāśara:

Having spoken thus, Khāṇḍikya went to King Keśidhvaja, asking, ‘Do you truly wish to pay the guru-dakṣiṇā?’ 47

## \|\| 6.6.48 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*ityuktvā samupetyainaṃ sa tu keśidhvajaṃ nṛpaḥ \|*  
*uvāca kimavaśyaṃ tvaṃ dadāsi gurudakṣiṇām \|\| 48 \|\|*

‘Indeed, I do,’ was his reply. Khāṇḍikya then said, ‘You’re well-versed in spiritual knowledge and highest truth. 48

## \|\| 6.6.49 \|\|

*bāḍhamityeva tenoktaḥ khāṇḍikyastamathābravīt \|*  
*bhavānadyātma vijñānaparamārthavicakṣaṇaḥ \|\| 49 \|\|*

If you wish to pay my fee, then say what acts are able to dispel my misery?’ 49

## Chapter 7 - Keśidhvaja instructs Khāṇḍikya in yoga

## \|\| 6.7.1 \|\|

*keśidhvaja uvāca*  
*na prārthitaṃ tvayā kasmādasmadrājyamakaṇṭakam \|*  
*rājyalābhādvinā nānyatkṣatriyāṇā matipriyam \|\| 1 \|\|*

Why didn’t you demand my kingdom, free from impositions? Nothing is dearer to kṣatriyas than winning sovereignty. 1

## \|\| 6.7.2 \|\|

*khāṇḍikya uvāca*  
*keśidhvaja nibodha tvaṃ mayā na prārthitaṃ yataḥ \|*  
*rājyametadaśeṣaṃ te yatra gṛdhnantyapaṇḍitāḥ \|\| 2 \|\|*

Understand, Keśidhvaja, why I didn’t request your realm, which an unwise person might desire. 2

## \|\| 6.7.3 \|\|

*kṣatriyāṇāmayaṃ dharmo yatprajāparipālanam \|*  
*vadhaś ca dharmayuddhena svarājyaparipanthinām \|\| 3 \|\|*

It’s the duty of all kṣatriyas to defend their subjects and to slay in righteous battles those who challenge their authority. 3

## \|\| 6.7.4 \|\|

*tatrāśaktasya me doṣo naivāstyapahṛte tvayā \|*  
*bandhāyaiva bhavatyeṣā hyavidyāpyakramojjhitā \|\| 4 \|\|*

It wasn’t wrong of you to seize the kingdom from me, when I was unable to defend it. I’ve now set aside such ignorance that merely tied me down. 4

## \|\| 6.7.5 \|\|

*alpopabhogalipsārtham iyaṃ rājyaspṛhā mama \|*  
*anyeṣāṃ doṣaja saiva dharmaṃ vai nānurudhyate \|\| 5 \|\|*

My desire for sovereignty was the product of my birth, my lust and greed, not the fault of another man, and was a bar to virtue. 5

## \|\| 6.7.6 \|\|

*na yācñā kṣatrabandhūnāṃ dharmayaitatsatāṃ matam \|*  
*ato na yācitaṃ rājyamavidyāntargataṃ tava \|\| 6 \|\|*

Wise men think it’s wrong for kṣatriyas to ask for gifts. That’s why I didn’t foolishly request the realm. 6

## \|\| 6.7.7 \|\|

*rājye gṛdhnantyavidvāṃso mamatvāhṛtacetasaḥ \|*  
*ahaṃmānamahāpānamadamattā na mādṛśāḥ \|\| 7 \|\|*

Unwise men whose hearts are carried off by a sense of self, drunk with pride, crave sovereignty—not men like me! 7

## \|\| 6.7.8 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*prahṛṣṭaḥsādhviti tataḥ prāha keśidhvajo nṛpaḥ \|*  
*khāṇḍikyajanakaṃ prītyā śrūyatāṃ vacanaṃ mama \|\| 8 \|\|*

King Keśidhvaja was delighted. ‘Excellent!’ he cried and said to Khāṇḍikya Janaka with affection: ‘Listen to what I say. 8

## \|\| 6.7.9 \|\|

*keśidhvaja uvāca*  
*ahaṃ hyavidyayā kṛtyuṃ tartukāmaḥ karomi vai \|*  
*rājyaṃ yāgāṃś ca vividhānyogaiḥ puṇyakṣayaṃ tathā \|\| 9 \|\|*

On account of ignorance, I wanted to escape from death, so I ruled the kingdom and undertook many sacrifices, but I squandered merit in enjoyments. 9

## \|\| 6.7.10 \|\|

*tadidaṃ te mano diṣṭyā vivekaiśvaryatāṃ gatam \|*  
*tacchrūyatāmavidyāyāḥsvarūpaṃ kulanandana \|\| 10 \|\|*

You’re lucky that your heart was drawn to the power of contemplation. Now listen, pride of our family, to the nature of ignorance. 10

## \|\| 6.7.11 \|\|

*anātmanyātmabuddhiryā cāsve svamiti yā matiḥ \|*  
*saṃsāratarusaṃbhūtibījametaddvidhā sthitam \|\| 11 \|\|*

The error that the self consists in something that’s not the self, and what is not one’s own is indeed one’s own, is the twofold seed from which grows the tree of ignorance. 11

## \|\| 6.7.12 \|\|

*pañcabhūtātmake dehe dehī mohatamovṛtaḥ \|*  
*ahaṃ mamaitadityuccaiḥ kurute kumatirmatim \|\| 12 \|\|*

The ignorant embodied being, who occupies a body composed of fivefold elements, bewildered by the darkness of delusion, stridently proclaims this view: “I am this body.” 12

## \|\| 6.7.13 \|\|

*ākāśavāyvagnijalapṛthivībhyaḥ pṛthak sthite \|*  
*ātmanyātmamayaṃ bhāvaṃ kaḥ karoti kalevare \|\| 13 \|\|*

The self is different from space, air, fire, water and earth, so who’d maintain that it exists within the body? 13

## \|\| 6.7.14 \|\|

*kalevaropabhogyaṃhi gṛhakṣetrādikaṃ ca kaḥ \|*  
*adehe hyātmani prājñe mamedamiti manyate \|\| 14 \|\|*

As the self is not an aspect of the body, what wise person thinks he owns his house, his fields and other things, when these can only be enjoyed through bodily means? 14

## \|\| 6.7.15 \|\|

*itthaṃ ca puttrapauttreṣu taddehotpāditeṣu kaḥ \|*  
*karoti paṇḍitaḥsvāmyamanātmani kalevare \|\| 15 \|\|*

Similarly, as the self is not an aspect of the body, what thoughtful man regards his sons and grandsons arising from his body as his own? 15

## \|\| 6.7.16 \|\|

*sarvaṃ dehopabhogāya kurute karma mānavaḥ \|*  
*dehaścānyo yadā puṃsastadā bandhāya tatparam \|\| 16 \|\|*

Every act a person undertakes is to satisfy the body, but if the spirit and the body are distinct, then everything that he regards as vital will further bind him. 16

## \|\| 6.7.17 \|\|

*mṛṇmayaṃ hi yathā gehaṃ lipyate vai mṛdaṃbhasā \|*  
*pārthivo 'yaṃ tathā deho mṛdaṃbvālepanasthitaḥ \|\| 17 \|\|*

Just as a house that’s built of clay is plastered with a mix of clay and water, in that same way, the body, which consists of earth, is kept alive with earth and water. 17

## \|\| 6.7.18 \|\|

*pañjabhūtatmakair bhogoḥ pañcabhūtātmakaṃ vapuḥ \|*  
*āpyāyate yadi tataḥ puṃso bhogo 'tra kiṃ kṛtaḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

If a person’s body, consisting of five elements, is sustained by food of these same elements, then what has that person to be proud of? 18

## \|\| 6.7.19 \|\|

*anekajanmasāhasrīṃ saṃsārapadavīṃ vrajan \|*  
*mohaśramaṃ prayāto 'sau vāsanaremukuṇṭhitaḥ \|\| 19 \|\|*

Trudging on the worldly path of a thousand births, he’s wearied by delusion and cloaked in the dust of yearning. 19

## \|\| 6.7.20 \|\|

*prakṣālyate yadā sosya reṇujñānoṣṇavāriṇā \|*  
*tadā saṃsārapānthasya yāti mohaśramaḥ śamam \|\| 20 \|\|*

Washing off that dust in the warming bath of knowledge, he sheds the weariness of delusion gathered on saṃsāra’s roads. 20

## \|\| 6.7.21 \|\|

*mohaśrame śamaṃ yāte svasthāntaḥkaraṇaḥ pumān \|*  
*ananyātiśayābādhaṃ paraṃ nirvāṇamṛcchati \|\| 21 \|\|*

When fatigue is at an end, his heart grows light and he attains supreme nirvāṇa, matchless and transcending pain. 21

## \|\| 6.7.22 \|\|

*nirvāṇamaya evāyam ātmā jñānamayo 'malaḥ \|*  
*duḥkhājñānamayā dharmāḥ prakṛtes te tu nātmanaḥ \|\| 22 \|\|*

The self entails the pure state of nirvāṇa and embodies wisdom. The blemishes of suffering and ignorance mark the natural world, but not the self. 22

## \|\| 6.7.23 \|\|

*jalasya nāgnisaṃsargasthālīsaṃgāttathāpi hi \|*  
*śabdodrekādikāndharmāṃstatkaroti yathā nṛpa \|\| 23 \|\|*

Water in a pot takes heat from the fire beneath it, without contact with the flames, even as it boils and bubbles, sage. 23

## \|\| 6.7.24 \|\|

*tathātmā prakṛteḥsaṃgādahaṃmānādidūṣitaḥ \|*  
*bhajate prākṛtān dharmān anyas tebhyo hi so 'vyayaḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

Similarly, the self, interacting with the natural world and corrupted by egoism and pride, manifests properties of that world, even though it’s separate and unchanging. 24

## \|\| 6.7.25 \|\|

*tadetatkathitaṃ bījamavidyāyā mayā tava \|*  
*kleśānāṃ ca kṣayakaraṃ yogādanyanna vidyate \|\| 25 \|\|*

That’s why I say that it is the seed of ignorance and, apart from yoga, there is no antidote for suffering. 25

## \|\| 6.7.26 \|\|

*khāṇḍikya uvāca*  
*taṃ bravīhi mahābhāga yogaṃ yogaviduttama \|*  
*vijñātayogaśāstrārthastvamasyāṃ nimisaṃtatau \|\| 26 \|\|*

Khāṇḍikya:

Then describe that yoga, fortunate king. In the lineage of Nimi, you’re thought to be an expert on the subject, as you grasp the sense of all the relevant treatises. 26

## \|\| 6.7.27 \|\|

*keśidhvaja uvāca*  
*yogasvarūpaṃ khāṇḍikya śrūyatāṃ gadato mama \|*  
*yatra sthito na cyavate prāpya brahmalayaṃ muniḥ \|\| 27 \|\|*

Keśidhvaja:

Listen, Khāṇḍikya, and I’ll explain the reality of yoga. A sage established in this practice, having reached the state of the Absolute, will never fall from it again. 27

## \|\| 6.7.28 \|\|

*mana eva manuṣyāṇāṃ kāraṇaṃ bandhamokṣayoḥ \|*  
*bandhāya viṣayāsaṃgi muktyai nirviṣayaṃ manaḥ \|\| 28 \|\|*

A man’s mind is the cause of both his bondage and his liberation. Attachment to the objects of the senses causes bondage, while freedom from attachment leads to liberation. 28

## \|\| 6.7.29 \|\|

*viṣayebhyaḥ samāhṛtya vijñānātmāmano muniḥ \|*  
*cintayenmuktaye tena brahmabhūtaṃ pareśvaram \|\| 29 \|\|*

Having withdrawn the mind from the objects of the senses, the knowledgeable sage should contemplate the highest lord, the Absolute, to accomplish liberation. 29

## \|\| 6.7.30 \|\|

*ātmabhāvaṃ nayatyenaṃ tadbahya dhyāyinaṃ munim \|*  
*vikāryamātmanaḥ śaktyā lohamākarṣako yathā \|\| 30 \|\|*

The Absolute attracts the being who shares its nature and contemplates that state, sage, just as a magnet, made of iron, has the power to attract the same material. 30

## \|\| 6.7.31 \|\|

*ātmaprayatnasāpekṣā viśiṣṭā yā manogatiḥ \|*  
*tasya brahmaṇi saṃyogo yoga ityabhidhīyate \|\| 31 \|\|*

The particular mental exercise, undertaken through one’s own endeavour, and during which the union with the Absolute is felt, is known as ‘yoga’. 31

## \|\| 6.7.32 \|\|

*evamatyantavaiśiṣṭyayuktadharmopalakṣaṇaḥ \|*  
*yasya yogaḥsa vai yogī mumukṣurabhidhīyate \|\| 32 \|\|*

A practitioner whose yoga is marked by performance of the highest excellence in this regard is said to occupy the cusp of liberation. 32

## \|\| 6.7.33 \|\|

*yogayuk prathamaṃ yogī yuñjāno hyabhidhīyate \|*  
*viniṣpannasamādhistu paraṃ brahmopalabdhimān \|\| 33 \|\|*

A practitioner who begins to master himself in this way is said to be disciplined through yoga, and one who has attained the supreme Absolute is said to have accomplished samādhi, or perfect union. 33

## \|\| 6.7.34 \|\|

*yadyantarāyadoṣeṇa dūṣyate cāsya mānasam \|*  
*janmāntarair abhyasato muktiḥ purvasya jāyate \|\| 34 \|\|*

If the heart of the former has been sullied by faults, then he will experience liberation after practising yoga during further lifetimes. 34

## \|\| 6.7.35 \|\|

*viniṣpannasamādhistu muktiṃ tatraiva janmani \|*  
*prāpnoti yogī yogāgnidagdhakarmacayo 'cirāt \|\| 35 \|\|*

A practitioner who has accomplished samādhi will achieve liberation in his own lifetime, as his accumulated karma is soon consumed in the fire of yoga. 35

## \|\| 6.7.36 \|\|

*brahmacaryamahiṃsāṃ ca satyāsteyāparigrahān \|*  
*seveta yogī niṣkāmo yogyatāṃ svamano nayan \|\| 36 \|\|*

The practitioner should embrace chastity, nonviolence, truth, honesty and poverty, while remaining free from desire and directing his mind towards its proper object. 36

## \|\| 6.7.37 \|\|

*svādhyāyaśaucasaṃtoṣatapāṃsi niyatātmavān \|*  
*kurvīta brahmaṇi tathā parasminpravaṇaṃmanaḥ \|\| 37 \|\|*

The self-restrained individual should undertake study, purity, contentment and austerity, while bending his mind towards the highest Absolute. 37

## \|\| 6.7.38 \|\|

*ete yamāḥsaniyamāḥ pañca pañca ca kīrtitāḥ \|*  
*viśiṣṭaphaladāḥ kāmyā niṣkāmānāṃ vimuktidāḥ \|\| 38 \|\|*

These five, known as the major and minor observances, respectively, offer great rewards to those who want them, but result in liberation for those who are free from yearning. 38

## \|\| 6.7.39 \|\|

*ekaṃ bhadrāsanādīnāṃ samāsthāya guṇair yutaḥ \|*  
*yamākhyair niyamākhyaiś ca yuñjīta niyato yatiḥ \|\| 39 \|\|*

Imbued with these qualities, and adopting a posture such as bhadrāsana, the self-restrained practitioner should discipline himself by means of the ten previously mentioned observances. 39

## \|\| 6.7.40 \|\|

*prāṇākhyamanilaṃ vaśyamabhyāsātkurute tu yat \|*  
*prāṇāyāmaḥsavijñeyaḥsabījo 'bīja eva ca \|\| 40 \|\|*

After repeated practice, he supresses the flow of air called prāṇa with breath control or prāṇāyāma, either with a seed or without one. 40

## \|\| 6.7.41 \|\|

*paraspareṇābhibhavaṃ prāṇāpānau yathānilau \|*  
*kurutaḥsadvidhānena tṛtīyaḥsaṃyamāttayoḥ \|\| 41 \|\|*

By means of the twofold practice of alternately controlling the airflows of prāṇa and apāna—inhalation and exhalation—a third form of control arises from the suppression of both of these. 41

## \|\| 6.7.42 \|\|

*tasya cālaṃbanavataḥ sthūlarūpaṃ dvijottama \|*  
*ālaṃbanamanantasya yogino 'bhyasataḥ smṛtam \|\| 42 \|\|*

The foundation for most individuals is gross form, best of brahmins, but for one who practises yoga, the foundation is the infinite deity. 42

## \|\| 6.7.43 \|\|

*śabdādiṣvanuraktāni nigṛhyākṣāṇi yogavit \|*  
*kuryāccittānukārīṇi pratyāhāraparāyaṇaḥ \|\| 43 \|\|*

Having withdrawn the sense organs from sensations such as sound to which they are attracted, one who knows yoga should focus on mental activities, intent on curbing senses. 43

## \|\| 6.7.44 \|\|

*vaśyatā paramā tena jāyateti calātmanām \|*  
*indriyāṇāmavaśyais tair na yogī yogasādhakaḥ \|\| 44 \|\|*

By this practice, a state of supreme control arises even for those whose minds are fickle, but if the senses are untamed, the practitioner will remain unable to reach union. 44

## \|\| 6.7.45 \|\|

*prāṇayāmena pavane pratyāhāreṇa cendriye \|*  
*vaśīkṛte tataḥ kuryātsthitaṃ cetaḥ śubhāśraye \|\| 45 \|\|*

With breath controlled through prāṇāyāma, and senses curbed by the practice of withdrawal, one may set one’s steadied mind on the perfect refuge. 45

## \|\| 6.7.46 \|\|

*khāḍikya uvāca*  
*kathyatāṃ me mahābhāgā cetaso yaḥ śubhāśrayaḥ \|*  
*yadādhāramaśeṣaṃ taddhanti doṣamalodbhavam \|\| 46 \|\|*

Khāṇḍikya:

Tell me, fortunate king, what’s the perfect refuge for the mind, on which basis every source of blemish is destroyed? 46

## \|\| 6.7.47 \|\|

*keśidhvāja uvāca*  
*āśrayaścetaso brahma dvidhā taccasvabhāvataḥ \|*  
*bhūpa mūrtamamūrtaṃ ca paraṃ cāparameva ca \|\| 47 \|\|*

Keśidhvaja:

The refuge of the mind is the Absolute, and it’s twofold, sire: formed and formless, or primary and secondary. 47

## \|\| 6.7.48 \|\|

*trividhā bhāvanā bhūpa viśvametannibodhatām \|*  
*brahmākhyā karmasaṃjñā ca tathā caivobhayātmikā \|\| 48 \|\|*

Realisation of this refuge is threefold, your majesty. You must understand all this: that which is called the Absolute, that which is known as action and that which consists of both. 48

## \|\| 6.7.49 \|\|

*karmabhāvātmikā hyekābrahmabhāvātmikā parā \|*  
*ubhayātmikā tathaivānyā trividhā bhāvabhāvanā \|\| 49 \|\|*

Realisation in the form of action is one; that marked by the Absolute is another; and that marked by both is the third. Hence, realisation is threefold. 49

## \|\| 6.7.50 \|\|

*sanandanādayo ye tu brahmabhāvanayā yutāḥ \|*  
*karmabhāvanayā cānye devādyāḥ sthāvarāvarāḥ \|\| 50 \|\|*

Sanandana and other sages reached realisation of the refuge marked by the Absolute. The deities and other beings, both mobile and immobile, achieved realisation marked by action. 50

## \|\| 6.7.51 \|\|

*hiraṇyagarbhādiṣu ca brahmakarmātmikā dvidhā \|*  
*bodhādhikārayukteṣu vidyate bhāvabhāvanā \|\| 51 \|\|*

For Brahmā, born of the golden egg, and other deities, knowing their responsibilities, realisation is twofold and is marked by both Absolute and action. 51

## \|\| 6.7.52 \|\|

*akṣīṇeṣu samasteṣu viśeṣajñānakarmasu \|*  
*viśvametatparaṃ cānyadbhedabhinnadṛśāṃ nṛṇām \|\| 52 \|\|*

Until all knowledge and action directed towards differentiation have ceased, for those who see distinctions, all the world is one thing and spirit is another, your majesty. 52

## \|\| 6.7.53 \|\|

*pratyastamitabhedaṃ yatsattāmātramagocaram \|*  
*vacasāmātmasaṃvedyaṃ tajjñānaṃ brahmasaṃjñitam \|\| 53 \|\|*

Realisation that is free from differentiation is pure existence, is beyond the reach of words and is to be comprehended as the Self; this is called the Absolute. 53

## \|\| 6.7.54 \|\|

*tacca viṣṇoḥ paraṃ rūpamarūpākhyamanuttamam \|*  
*viśvasvarūpavairūpyalakṣaṇaṃ paramātmanaḥ \|\| 54 \|\|*

This is the supreme, unborn, unchanging form of formless Viṣṇu, the highest spirit, marked by ubiquity and diversity. 54

## \|\| 6.7.55 \|\|

*na tadyogayujā śakyaṃ nṛpa cintayituṃyataḥ \|*  
*tataḥsthūlaṃ hare rūpaṃ cintayedviśvagocaram \|\| 55 \|\|*

Since the practitioner cannot contemplate that form, your majesty, one should meditate on the physical form of Hari that lies within the reach of all. 55

## \|\| 6.7.56 \|\|

*hiraṇyagarbho bhagavānvāsudevaḥ prajāpatiḥ \|*  
*maruto vasavo rudrā bhāskarāstārakā grahāḥ \|\| 56 \|\|*

Lord Brahmā, Indra, chief of Vasus and protector of his subjects, the Maruts, Vasus, Rudras, the suns, the stars and planets, 56

## \|\| 6.7.57 \|\|

*gandharvayakṣadaityādyaḥsakalā devayonayaḥ \|*  
*manuṣyāḥ paśavaḥ śailāḥsamudrāḥsarito drumāḥ \|\| 57 \|\|*

Gandharvas, yakṣas, Daityas and all other creatures of divine origin, humans, animals, mountains, oceans, trees and rivers, 57

## \|\| 6.7.58 \|\|

*bhūpa bhūtānyaśeṣāṇi bhūtānāṃ ye ca hetavaḥ \|*  
*pradhānādiviśeṣāntaṃ cetanācetanātmakam \|\| 58 \|\|*

All beings, your majesty, and those which are the cause of beings, beginning with primal Matter and ending with differentiated entities, sentient and insentient, 58

## \|\| 6.7.59 \|\|

*ekapādaṃ dvipādaṃ ca bahupādamapādakam \|*  
*mūrtametaddhare rūpaṃ bhāvanātritayātmakam \|\| 59 \|\|*

One-footed, two-footed, many-footed and footless—all these are manifest forms of Hari to be apprehended by three kinds of realisation. 59

## \|\| 6.7.60 \|\|

*etatsarvamidaṃ viśvaṃ jagadetaccarācaram \|*  
*parabrahmasvarūpasya viṣṇoḥ śaktisamanvitam \|\| 60 \|\|*

All this, the entire world of mobile and immobile things, is filled with Viṣṇu’s energy in the form of the highest Absolute. 60

## \|\| 6.7.61 \|\|

*viṣṇuśaktiḥ parā proktā kṣetrajñākhyā tathāparā \|*  
*avidyākarmasaṃjñānyā tṛtīyā śaktiriṣyate \|\| 61 \|\|*

This energy of Viṣṇu is said to be primary. That energy which is called consciousness, kṣetrajña, is secondary. That which is known as ignorance or action is called energy of the third kind. 61

## \|\| 6.7.62 \|\|

*yayā kṣetrajñaśaktiḥsā veṣṭitā nṛpasarvagā \|*  
*saṃsāratāpānakhilānavāprotyatisaṃtatān \|\| 62 \|\|*

On account of the all-embracing and omnipresent energy of consciousness, your majesty, one undergoes all kinds of worldly sufferings in succession. 62

## \|\| 6.7.63 \|\|

*tayā tirohitatvācca śaktiḥ kṣetrajñasaṃjñitā \|*  
*sarvabhūteṣu bhūpāla tāratamyenalakṣyate \|\| 63 \|\|*

And because it’s obscured by that third force, the energy known as consciousness is observed to a greater or lesser degree in creatures, sire. 63

## \|\| 6.7.64 \|\|

*aprāṇavatsusvalpā sā sthāvareṣu tato 'dhikā \|*  
*sarīsṛpeṣu tebhyopi hyatiśaktyā patattriṣu \|\| 64 \|\|*

In unliving things, it’s very weak. In living things that do not move, it’s a little stronger. In those that creep or slither it’s stronger still, and stronger yet again in those with wings. 64

## \|\| 6.7.65 \|\|

*pātattriṃbhyo mṛgāstebhyastacchaktyā paśavo 'dhikāḥ \|*  
*paśubhyo manujāścātiśaktyā puṃsaḥ prabhāvitāḥ \|\| 65 \|\|*

It’s stronger in wild animals than in birds, and stronger again in domesticated beasts. It’s stronger yet in humans than in animals. That’s why we have mastery over them. 65

## \|\| 6.7.66 \|\|

*tebhyopi nāgagandharvayakṣādyā devatā nṛpa \|\| 66 \|\|*

It’s stronger in nāgas, gandharvas, yakṣas and other divine beings than in humans, sire. 66

## \|\| 6.7.67 \|\|

*śakraḥsamastadevebhyastataścātiprajāpatiḥ \|*  
*hiraṇyagarbho 'pi tataḥ puṃsaḥ śaktyupalakṣitaḥ \|\| 67 \|\|*

In mighty Indra, protector of his subjects, it’s stronger than in all the other deities. Brahmā is also set apart by a greater share of energy than mortal men. 67

## \|\| 6.7.68 \|\|

*etānyaśeṣarūpāṇi tasya rūpāṇi pārthiva \|*  
*yatastacchaktiyogena yuktāni nabhasā yathā \|\| 68 \|\|*

All these are forms of Viṣṇu, sire, since they’re pervaded by his energy, as by ether. 68

## \|\| 6.7.69 \|\|

*dvitīyaṃ viṣṇusaṃjñasya yogidhyeyaṃ mahāmate \|*  
*amūrtaṃ brahmaṇo rūpaṃ yat sad ityu cyate budhaiḥ \|\| 69 \|\|*

The second state of the being known as Viṣṇu on which practitioners of yoga meditate, great sage, is the shapeless form of the Absolute, called by the wise ‘the existent’. 69

## \|\| 6.7.70 \|\|

*samastāḥ śaktayaścaitā nṛpa yatra pratiṣṭhitāḥ \|*  
*tadviśvarūpavairūpyaṃ rūpam anyad dharer mahat \|\| 70 \|\|*

All those energies are established in it, sire; it’s a form of the universal being and is the other great form of Hari. 70

## \|\| 6.7.71 \|\|

*samastaśaktirūpāṇi tatkaroti janeśvara \|*  
*devatiryaṅmanuṣyādiceṣṭāvanti svalīlayā \|\| 71 \|\|*

It brings forth, as if in sport, deities, animals, humans and other living things, all of which are forms of his energy, your majesty. 71

## \|\| 6.7.72 \|\|

*jagatāmupakārāya na sā karmanimittajā \|*  
*ceṣṭā tasyāprameyasya vyāpinyavyāhatātmikā \|\| 72 \|\|*

This all-pervading, uninterrupted activity of the immeasurable deity is for the benefit of the worlds and does not arise for the sake of action. 72

## \|\| 6.7.73 \|\|

*tadrūpaṃ viśvarūpasya tasya yogayujā nṛpa \|*  
*cintyamātmaviśuddhyarthaṃ sarvakilbiṣanāśanam \|\| 73 \|\|*

One disciplined through yoga should contemplate that form of the universal being, sire, to purify himself, as it expunges all iniquity. 73

## \|\| 6.7.74 \|\|

*yathāgniruddhataśikhaḥ kakṣaṃ dahati sānilaḥ \|*  
*tathā cittasthito viṣṇuryogināṃ sarvakilbiṣam \|\| 74 \|\|*

Just as fire with leaping flames burns a dried-out thicket when fanned by wind, so does Viṣṇu abiding in ascetics’ hearts destroy their sins. 74

## \|\| 6.7.75 \|\|

*tasmātsamastaśaktīnāmādhāre tatra cetasaḥ \|*  
*kurvīta saṃsthitiṃ sā tu vijñeyā śuddhadhāraṇā \|\| 75 \|\|*

One should therefore fix one’s mind on the basis of all forms of energy, a technique known as pure dhāraṇā, or ‘holding’. 75

## \|\| 6.7.76 \|\|

*śubhāśrayasya cittasya sarvagasyācalātmanaḥ \|*  
*tribhāvabhāghanātīto muktaye yogino nṛpa \|\| 76 \|\|*

It’s the perfect place for one’s own mind and for the universal being. It lies beyond the three modes of realisation and is attained by ascetics for their liberation, sire. 76

## \|\| 6.7.77 \|\|

*anye tu puruṣavyāghra cetaso ye vyāpāśrayāḥ \|*  
*aśuddhāste samastāstu devādyāḥ karmayonayaḥ \|\| 77 \|\|*

The minds of others find no refuge, tiger of a man. The deities and all other imperfect beings come into existence as the result of actions. 77

## \|\| 6.7.78 \|\|

*mūrtaṃ bhagavato rūpaṃ sarvāpāśrayanispṛham \|*  
*eṣā vai dhāraṇa proktā yaccittaṃ tatra dhāryate \|\| 78 \|\|*

The manifest form of the lord has no desire for any other refuge. This meditation is called ‘holding’ because the mind retains this very image. 78

## \|\| 6.7.79 \|\|

*yacca mūrtaṃ hare rūpaṃ yādṛkcintyaṃ narādhipa \|*  
*tacchrūyatāmanādhārā dhāramā nopapadyate \|\| 79 \|\|*

Now you’ll hear about the form of Hari to contemplate, your majesty. In the absence of such a basis, holding is impossible: 79

## \|\| 6.7.80 \|\|

*prasannavadanaṃ cārupadmapatropamekṣaṇam \|*  
*sukapolaṃ suvistīrṇalalāṭaphalakojjvalam \|\| 80 \|\|*

A lovely, pleasing face, eyes like lotus petals, smooth cheeks, a broad and brilliant forehead, 80

## \|\| 6.7.81 \|\|

*samakarṇāntavinyastacārukuṇḍalabhūṣaṇam \|*  
*kaṃbugrīvaṃ suvīstīrṇaśrīvatsāṃkitavakṣasam \|\| 81 \|\|*

His well-matched ears with finest earrings hanging from their lobes, a neck as white as conch-shell, while his broad chest bears Śrīvatsa. 81

## \|\| 6.7.82 \|\|

*valitribhaṅginā magnanābhinā hyudareṇa ca \|*  
*pralaṃbāṣṭabhujaṃ viṣṇumathavāpi caturbhujam \|\| 82 \|\|*

His belly with its deep navel is crossed by three graceful folds. His long arms are either four or eight in number. 82

## \|\| 6.7.83 \|\|

*samasthitorujaṅghaṃ ca susthitāṅghrivarāṃbujam \|*  
*cintayedbrahmabhūtaṃ taṃ pītanirmalavāsasam \|\| 83 \|\|*

His strong legs are well-formed, and his lotus-like feet and hands are shapely. One should meditate on this form of Viṣṇu, the Absolute being, clad in garments of pure yellow. 83

## \|\| 6.7.84 \|\|

*kirīṭahārakeyūrakaṭakādivibhūṣitam \|\| 84 \|\|*

Adorned with diadem, lovely armlets, bracelets and other jewellery, 84

## \|\| 6.7.85 \|\|

*śārṅgaśaṅkhagadākhaḍgacakrākṣavalayānvitam \|*  
*varadābhayahastaṃ ca mudrikāratnabhūṣitam \|\| 85 \|\|*

Holding bow and conch, mace and sword, prayer beads and discus—the ascetic, who himself consists of Viṣṇu, should contemplate this form with a focused mind. 85

## \|\| 6.7.86 \|\|

*cintayettanmayo yogī samādhāyātmamānasam \|*  
*tāvadyāvadṛḍhībhūtā tatraiva nṛpa dhāraṇā \|\| 86 \|\|*

As long as he maintains the practice of dhāraṇā, your majesty, while walking, standing or doing anything he likes, if this image never leaves his heart, he may regard the practice as perfected. 86

## \|\| 6.7.87 \|\|

*vrajatastiṣṭhatonyadvā svecchayā karma kurvataḥ \|*  
*nāpayāti yadā cittatsiddhāṃ manyeta tāṃ tadā \|\| 87 \|\|*

Next, the wise person may meditate on the lord in tranquil form, without the conch or sword, bow or discus or other items, holding only prayer beads. 87

## \|\| 6.7.88 \|\|

*tataḥ śaṅkhagadācakraśarṅgīdirahitaṃ budhaḥ \|*  
*cintayedbhagavadrūpaṃ praśāntaṃ sākṣasūtrakam \|\| 88 \|\|*

When this state of dhāraṇā is stable, he should visualise the image without the diadem, armlets and other ornaments. 88

## \|\| 6.7.89 \|\|

*sā yadā dhāraṇā tadvadavasthānavatī tataḥ \|*  
*kiriṭakeyūramukhair bhūṣaṇai rahitaṃ smaret \|\| 89 \|\|*

Then the wise man should set his mind on the deity with a single limb. Next he should meditate on the body to which that limb belongs. 89

## \|\| 6.7.90 \|\|

*tadekāvayavaṃ devaṃ cetasā hi punarbudhaḥ \|*  
*kuryāttato 'vayavini praṇidhānaparo bhavet \|\| 90 \|\|*

A single mental image of Viṣṇu’s form, stable and unbroken, is known as dhyāna, or ‘meditation’, and is reached in six initial stages, sire. 90

## \|\| 6.7.91 \|\|

*tadrūpapratyayā caikā saṃtatiścānyaniḥspṛhā \|*  
*taddhyānaṃ prathamair aṅgaiḥ ṣaḍbhir niṣpādyate nṛpa \|\| 91 \|\|*

When this heavenly form is borne in mind, free from other thoughts, as a result of meditation, that state is known as samādhi. 91

## \|\| 6.7.92 \|\|

*tasyaiva kalpanāhīnaṃ svarūpagrahaṇaṃ hiyat \|*  
*manasā dhyānaniṣpādyaṃ samādhiḥso 'bhidhīyate \|\| 92 \|\|*

If the highest Absolute is to be achieved, your majesty, then knowledge will enable it. Similarly, the Self is reached when all other forms of realisation are exhausted. 92

## \|\| 6.7.93 \|\|

*vijñānaṃ prāpakaṃ prāpye pare brahmaṇi pārthiva \|*  
*prāpaṇīyastathaivātmā prakṣīṇāśeṣabhāvanaḥ \|\| 93 \|\|*

Consciousness has the means, while knowledge is the means by which one reaches liberation. When liberation has been reached, the role of knowledge has been fulfilled and is no more. 93

## \|\| 6.7.94 \|\|

*kṣetrajñaḥ karaṇī jñānaṃ karaṇaṃ tasya tena tat \|*  
*niṣpādya muktikāryaṃ vai kṛtakṛtyaṃ nivartate \|\| 94 \|\|*

When the individual self reaches the realisation of its true nature, then it becomes one with the supreme Absolute, no longer divided from it. The separation of the two is caused by ignorance. 94

## \|\| 6.7.95 \|\|

*tadbhāvabhāvamāpannastato 'sau paramātmanā \|*  
*bhavatyabhedī bhedaś ca tasyājñānakṛto bhavet \|\| 95 \|\|*

When ignorance that gives rise to difference is finally transcended, who would distinguish between the Absolute and the self, when such distinction does not exist? 95

## \|\| 6.7.96 \|\|

*vibhedajanake jñāne nāśamātyantikaṃ gate \|*  
*ātmano brahmaṇo bhedama saṃtaṃ kaḥ karīṣyati \|\| 96 \|\|*

I’ve described yoga to you, Khāṇḍikya, as requested, part in brief and part in full. What else may I do for you? 96

## \|\| 6.7.97 \|\|

*ityuktaste mayā yogaḥ khāṇḍikya paripṛcchataḥ \|*  
*saṃkṣepavistarābhyāṃ tu kimanyatkriyatāṃ tava \|\| 97 \|\|*

Khāṇḍikya:

Now that the reality of yoga has been explained, you’ve done everything for me. All impurities in my mind have been removed through your instruction. 97

## \|\| 6.7.98 \|\|

*khāṇḍikya uvāca*  
*kathite yogasadbhāve sarvameva kṛtaṃ mama \|*  
*tavopadeśenāśeṣo naṣṭaścittamalo yataḥ \|\| 98 \|\|*

The word ‘mine’, which I’ve used so often, is a lie. Those who know what must be known can’t say otherwise, your majesty. 98

## \|\| 6.7.99 \|\|

*mameti yanmayā coktamasadetanna cānyathā \|*  
*naredra gadituṃ śakyamapi vijñeyavedibhiḥ \|\| 99 \|\|*

‘I’ and ‘mine’ are forms of ignorance that dictate daily conduct. The highest goal can’t be described, as it’s beyond the scope of words. 99

## \|\| 6.7.100 \|\|

*ahaṃ mametyavidyeyaṃ vyavahārastathānayoḥ \|*  
*paramārthastvasaṃlāpo gocare vacasāṃ na yaḥ \|\| 100 \|\|*

Go now, for all you’ve done is for my benefit, Keśidhvaja, as you’ve described eternal yoga, which leads to liberation. 100

## \|\| 6.7.101 \|\|

*tadgaccha śreyase sarvaṃ mamaitadbhavatā kṛtam \|*  
*yadvimuktiprado yogaḥ proktaḥ keśidhvajāvyayaḥ \|\| 101 \|\|*

Parāśara:

King Keśidhvaja received due homage from Khāṇḍikya, brahmin, then returned to his own capital. 101

## \|\| 6.7.102 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*yathārhaṃ pūjayā tena khāṇḍikyena sa pūjitaḥ \|*  
*ājagāma puraṃ brahmaṃstataḥ keśidhvajo nṛpaḥ \|\| 102 \|\|*

Khāṇḍikya gave his kingdom to his son and retreated to the forest with his heart set on Govinda to perfect his yoga practice. 102

## \|\| 6.7.103 \|\|

*khāṇḍikyo 'pi sutaṃ kṛtvā rājānaṃ yogasiddaye \|*  
*vanaṃ jagāma govinde viniveśitamānasaḥ \|\| 103 \|\|*

There, delighting in this single quest and purified by virtues such as self-restraint, the king achieved absorption in the purest Absolute in the form that’s known as Viṣṇu. 103

## \|\| 6.7.104 \|\|

*tatraikāntamatirbhūtvā yamādiguṇasaṃyutaḥ \|*  
*viṣṇvākhye nirmale brahmaṇy avāpa nṛpatir layam \|\| 104 \|\|*

Keśidhvaja turned his back on his responsibilities and indulged his senses, but still performed the rituals without attachment to the outcome to accomplish liberation. 104

## \|\| 6.7.105 \|\|

*keśidhvajo vimuktyarthaṃ svakarmakṣapaṇonmukhaḥ \|*  
*bubhuje viṣayānkarma cakre cānabhisaṃhitam \|\| 105 \|\|*

In spite of worldly pleasures, he was cleansed of all his sins and, being purified, brahmin, achieved that perfection which results in the destruction of all suffering. 105

## Chapter 8 - The benefits of listening to the Viṣṇu Purāṇa

## \|\| 6.8.1 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*ityeṣa kathitaḥsamyak tṛtīyaḥ pratisaṃcaraḥ \|*  
*ātyantiko vimuktiryā layo brahmaṇi śāśvate \|\| 1 \|\|*

I’ve thus described in full the third and final kind of dissolution: liberation as absorption in the everlasting Absolute. 1

## \|\| 6.8.2 \|\|

*sargaś ca pratisargaś ca vaṃśamanvantarāṇi ca \|*  
*vaṃśānucaritaṃ caiva bhavato gaditaṃ mayā \|\| 2 \|\|*

I’ve told you all about the first and subsequent creations, the lineages of the patriarchs, the periods of the Manus and the deeds of the royal dynasties. 2

## \|\| 6.8.3 \|\|

*purāmaṃ baiṣṇavaṃ caitatsarvakilbiṣanāśanam \|*  
*viśiṣṭaṃ sarvaśāstrebhyaḥ puruṣārthopapādakam \|\| 3 \|\|*

This imperishable Vaiṣṇava purāṇa, which sweeps away all sins, is the first among all sacred texts as a path to reach the goals of human life. 3

## \|\| 6.8.4 \|\|

*tubhyaṃ yathāvanmaitreya proktaṃ śuśruṣave 'vyayam \|*  
*yadanyadapi vaktavyaṃ tṛtpṛcchādya vadāmi te \|\| 4 \|\|*

I’ve duly related this everlasting scripture to you, Maitreya, as you wished to hear it. What else should I speak about? Ask me now, and I’ll respond. 4

## \|\| 6.8.5 \|\|

*maitreya uvāca*  
*bhagavankathitaṃ sarvaṃ yatpṛṣṭosi mayā mune \|*  
*śrutaṃ caitanmayā bhaktyā nānyatpraṣṭavyamasti me \|\| 5 \|\|*

Maitreya:

Master, you’ve told me all I asked about. I’ve listened with devotion, sage, and have no further questions. 5

## \|\| 6.8.6 \|\|

*vicchinnāḥsarvasaṃdehā vaimalyaṃ manasaḥ kṛtam \|*  
*tvatprasādānmayā jñātā utpattisthitisaṃkṣayāḥ \|\| 6 \|\|*

All my doubts have been dispelled and now my mind is clear. Through your kindness, I understand the origin, state and conclusion of the world. 6

## \|\| 6.8.7 \|\|

*jñātaś ca trividho rāśiḥ śaktiś ca trividhā guro \|*  
*vijñātā sā ca kārtsnyena trividhā bhāvabhāvanā \|\| 7 \|\|*

I understand Viṣṇu’s fourfold nature and his threefold energy, guru, and I comprehend completely the threefold nature of realisation. 7

## \|\| 6.8.8 \|\|

*tvatprasādānmayā jñātaṃ jñeyamanyair alaṃ dvija \|*  
*yadetadakhilaṃ viṣṇorjaganna vyatiricyate \|\| 8 \|\|*

I understand all this as the product of your kindness. What use is further knowledge, brahmin, when the universe and Viṣṇu are but one? 8

## \|\| 6.8.9 \|\|

*kṛtārthohamasaṃdehastvatprasādānmahāmune \|*  
*varṇadharmādayo dharmā viditā yadaśeṣataḥ \|\| 9 \|\|*

I’ve reached my goal and have no further doubts, thanks to your mercy, sage, as now I fully understand the duties of all four orders and other practices. 9

## \|\| 6.8.10 \|\|

*pravṛttaṃ ca nivṛttaṃ ca jñānaṃ karmamayākhilam \|*  
*prasīda viprapravara nānyatpraṣṭavyamasti me \|\| 10 \|\|*

I understand completely the sacrificial and meditative modes of pious life. Be merciful, best of brahmins. I have nothing more to ask. 10

## \|\| 6.8.11 \|\|

*yadasya kathanāyāsair yo 'rjito 'si mayā guro \|*  
*tatkṣamyatāṃ viśeṣosti na satāṃ putraśiṣyayoḥ \|\| 11 \|\|*

Forgive me for troubling you to relate all this, guru, but the wise make no distinction between a student and a son. 11

## \|\| 6.8.12 \|\|

*śrīparāśara uvāca*  
*etatte yanmayākhyātaṃ purāṇaṃ vedasaṃmatam \|*  
*śrute 'sminsarvadoṣotthaḥ pāparāśiḥ praṇaśyati \|\| 12 \|\|*

Parāśara:

This purāṇa which I’ve relayed to you is the equal of the Vedas. By hearing it, the sins amassed from faults of every kind will be dispelled. 12

## \|\| 6.8.13 \|\|

*sargaś ca pratisargaś ca vaṃśamanvantarāmi ca \|*  
*vaṃśānucaritaṃ kṛtsnaṃ mayātra tava kīrtitam \|\| 13 \|\|*

I’ve told you all about the first and subsequent creations of the world, the lineages, Manvantaras and dynastic histories. 13

## \|\| 6.8.14 \|\|

*atra devās tathā daityā gandharvoragarākṣasāḥ \|*  
*yakṣavidyādharāḥsiddhāḥ kathyante 'psarasas tathā \|\| 14 \|\|*

I’ve spoken of the deities, Daityas, gandharvas, nāgas, rākṣasas, yakṣas, vidyādharas, siddhas and apsarases. 14

## \|\| 6.8.15 \|\|

*munayo bhāvitātmānaḥ kathyante tapasānvitāḥ \|*  
*cāturvaṇyaṃ tathā puṃsā viśiṣṭacaritānica \|\| 15 \|\|*

I’ve described the purest sages imbued with ascetic energy, the fourfold social system, men of uncommon feats, 15

## \|\| 6.8.16 \|\|

*puṇyāḥ pradeśā medinyāḥ puṇyā nadyo 'tha sāgarāḥ \|*  
*parvatāś ca mahāpuṇyāścaritāni ca dhīmatām \|\| 16 \|\|*

Hallowed places on this fertile earth, holy seas and rivers, sacred mountains and the exploits of the wise, 16

## \|\| 6.8.17 \|\|

*varṇadharmādayo dharmāvedaśāstrāṇi kṛtsnaśaḥ \|*  
*yeṣāṃ saṃsmaramātsadyaḥsarvapāpaiḥ pramucyate \|\| 17 \|\|*

Traditions such as those that govern the four communities and all about the schools of Vedic learning. Hearing this, one is freed at once from all misdeeds. 17

## \|\| 6.8.18 \|\|

*utpattisthitināśānāṃ heturyo jagato 'vyayaḥ \|*  
*sa sarvabhūtaḥsarvātmā kathyate bhagavānhariḥ \|\| 18 \|\|*

I’ve described Lord Hari, the universal everlasting cause of creation, preservation and destruction of the world, who abides at the heart of everything. 18

## \|\| 6.8.19 \|\|

*avaśenāpi yannāmni kīrtite sarvapātakaiḥ \|*  
*pumānvimanacye sadyaḥsiṃhatrastair mṛgair iva \|\| 19 \|\|*

By calling his name, even unintentionally, all one’s sins are scattered in an instant, like frightened deer fleeing from a lion. 19

## \|\| 6.8.20 \|\|

*yannāmakīrtanaṃ bhaktyā vilāyanamanuttamam \|*  
*maitreyāseṣapāpānāṃ dhātūnāmiva pāvakaḥ \|\| 20 \|\|*

When a person speaks his name with faith, he is completely purified, Maitreya, just as fire refines the ores of minerals. 20

## \|\| 6.8.21 \|\|

*kalikalmaṣamatyugraṃ narakārttipradaṃ nṛṇām \|*  
*prayāti vilayaṃ sadyaḥsakṛdyatra ca saṃsmṛte \|\| 21 \|\|*

The moment Viṣṇu is called to mind, the awful faults of the Kali age, which lead folk to infernal grief, are ended. 21

## \|\| 6.8.22 \|\|

*hiraṇyagarbhadevendrarudrādityāśvivāyubhiḥ \|*  
*pāvakair vasubhiḥ sādhyair viśvedevādibhiḥ suraiḥ \|\| 22 \|\|*

Brahmā, born of the golden egg, Indra king of gods, Rudra, Āditya, the Aśvins, Vāyu, the Fires, the Vasus, the Sādhyas, the Viśvedevas and other gods, 22

## \|\| 6.8.23 \|\|

*yakṣarakṣoragaiḥ siddhair daityagandharvadānavaiḥ \|*  
*apsarobhis tathā tārānakṣatraiḥ sakalair grahaiḥ \|\| 23 \|\|*

The yakṣas, rākṣasas, nāgas, siddhas, Daityas, gandharvas, Dānavas, apsarases, stars, constellations and all the planets, 23

## \|\| 6.8.24 \|\|

*saptarṣibhis tathā dhiṣṇyair dhiṣṇyādhipatibhis tathā \|*  
*brahmaṇādyair manuṣyaiś ca tathaiva paśubhir mṛgaiḥ \|\| 24 \|\|*

The Seven Sages, the quarters of the sky and their protectors, brahmins and other mortals, animals of farm and forest, 24

## \|\| 6.8.25 \|\|

*sarīsṛpair vihaṅgaiś ca palāśādyair mahīruhaiḥ \|*  
*vanāgnisāgarasaritpātālaiḥ sasurāgnibhiḥ \|\| 25 \|\|*

Those that creep and crawl, those that course the sky, palāśa trees and other plants that grow upon the earth, forests, mountains, oceans, rivers, the lower realms and all that underlies them, 25

## \|\| 6.8.26 \|\|

*śabdādibhiścasahitaṃ brahmāṇḍamakhilaṃ dvija \|*  
*merorivāṇuryasyaitadyanmayaṃ ca dvijottama \|\| 26 \|\|*

Together with sound and other objects of the senses, and the universe entirely in the form of the cosmic egg, brahmin—all this consists of Viṣṇu, yet is no more to him than an atom is to Meru, best of brahmins. 26

## \|\| 6.8.27 \|\|

*sa sarvaḥ sarvavitsarvasvarūpo rūpavarjitaḥ \|*  
*bhagavānkīrtito viṣṇuratra pāpapraṇāśanaḥ \|\| 27 \|\|*

He is all and is all-knowing. He takes all forms, and yet is formless. He is the lord who is praised as Viṣṇu, destroyer of all worldly sin. 27

## \|\| 6.8.28 \|\|

*yadaśvamedhāvabhṛthe snātaḥ prāpnoti vai phalam \|*  
*mānavastadavāpnoti śrutvaitanmunisattama \|\| 28 \|\|*

The entire reward that’s won by a man who has finished his ablutions at the end of an Aśvamedha, best of sages, may be gained by hearing this account. 28

## \|\| 6.8.29 \|\|

*prayāge puṣkarecaiva kurukṣetre tathār'ṇave \|*  
*kṛtopavāsaḥ prāpnoti tadasya śravaṇānnaraḥ \|\| 29 \|\|*

The reward that’s won by a man who has fasted at Prayāga, Puṣkara, Kurukṣetra or Arbuda may be gained by hearing this. 29

## \|\| 6.8.30 \|\|

*yadagnihotre suhute varṣeṇāpnoti mānavaḥ \|*  
*mahāpuṇyaphalaṃ vipra tadasya śravaṇātsakṛt \|\| 30 \|\|*

The highly meritorious reward achieved from a well-conducted year-long Agnihotra sacrifice, brahmin, may be gained at once by hearing this purāṇa. 30

## \|\| 6.8.31 \|\|

*yajjyeṣṭhasukladvādaśyāṃ snātvā vai yamunājale \|*  
*mathurāyāṃ hariṃ dṛṣṭvā prāpnoti puruṣaḥ phalam \|\| 31 \|\|*

After bathing in the waters of the Yamunā at Mathurā on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight in the month of Jyeṣṭha, and having beheld Hari, one attains the highest state. 31

## \|\| 6.8.32 \|\|

*tadāpnotyakhilaṃ samyagadhyāyaṃ yaḥ śṛṇoti vai \|*  
*purāṇasyāsya viprarṣa keśavārpitamānasaḥ \|\| 32 \|\|*

But that same reward is gained when one recites this narrative, sagacious brahmin, with perfect concentration and one’s mind on Keśava. 32

## \|\| 6.8.33 \|\|

*yamunāsalilasnātaḥ puruṣo munisattama \|*  
*jyeṣṭhāmūle site pakṣe dvādaśyāṃ samupoṣitaḥ \|\| 33 \|\|*

One who bathes in the Yamunā, best of sages, on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight in Jyeṣṭhāmūla, after fasting, 33

## \|\| 6.8.34 \|\|

*samabhyarcyācyutaṃ samyaṅ mathurāyāṃ samāditaḥ \|*  
*aśvamedhasya yajñasya prāpnotyavikalaṃ phalam \|\| 34 \|\|*

And who has worshipped Acyuta correctly at Mathurā with a focused mind, achieves the full reward of an Aśvamedha sacrifice. 34

## \|\| 6.8.35 \|\|

*ālokyardhimathānyeṣāmunnītānāṃ svavaṃśajaiḥ \|*  
*etatkilocurapyeṣāṃ pitaraḥ sapitāmahāḥ \|\| 35 \|\|*

Seeing the felicity enjoyed by ancestral spirits who’d been redeemed by their descendants, other spirits uttered this complaint: 35

## \|\| 6.8.36 \|\|

*kaccidasmatkule jātaḥ kālindīsalilāplutaḥ \|*  
*artayiṣyati govindaṃ mathurāyāmupoṣitaḥ \|\| 36 \|\|*

‘If only someone in our family would bathe in the waters of the Yamunā and praise Govinda at Mathurā after fasting, 36

## \|\| 6.8.37 \|\|

*jyoṣṭhāmūle site pakṣe kenaivaṃ vayamapyuta \|*  
*parāmṛddhimavāpsyāmastāritāḥsvakulodbhavaiḥ \|\| 37 \|\|*

In the bright fortnight of Jyeṣṭhāmūla, we, too, might enjoy such felicity and be redeemed by our descendants.’ 37

## \|\| 6.8.38 \|\|

*jyeṣṭhāmūle site pakṣe samabhyarcya janārdanam \|*  
*dhanyānāṃ kulajaḥ piṇḍānyamunāyāṃ pradāsyati \|\| 38 \|\|*

After worshipping Janārdana in the bright fortnight of Jyeṣṭhāmula, one born in an upright family may make food offerings of piṇḍa to his ancestors by the Yamunā. 38

## \|\| 6.8.39 \|\|

*tasminkāle samabhyarcya tatra kṛṣṇaṃ samāhitaḥ \|*  
*dattvā piṇḍaṃ pitṛbhyaś ca yamunāsalilāplutaḥ \|\| 39 \|\|*

After praising Kṛṣṇa with devotion at that time and place, and offering piṇḍa to his ancestors and bathing in the waters of the Yamunā, 39

## \|\| 6.8.40 \|\|

*yadāpnoti naraḥ puṇyaṃ tārayansvapitāmahān \|*  
*śrutvādhyāyaṃ tadāpnoti purāṇasyāsya śaktitaḥ \|\| 40 \|\|*

The auspicious reward he receives while redeeming his own forefathers is the same as a devotee receives from hearing just one chapter of this purāṇa. 40

## \|\| 6.8.41 \|\|

*etatsaṃsārabhīrūṇāṃ paritrāṇamanuttamam \|*  
*śrāvyāṇāṃ paramaṃ śrāvyaṃ pavitrāṇāmanuttamam \|\| 41 \|\|*

This is the peerless means of salvation for those who fear saṃsāra. It dispels all nightmares and sets folk free from all defilements. 41

## \|\| 6.8.42 \|\|

*duḥsvapnanāśanaṃ nṝṇāṃ sarvaduṣṭanibarhaṇam \|*  
*maṅgalaṃ maṅgalānāṃ ca putrasaṃpatpradāyakam \|\| 42 \|\|*

This sage discourse was related in former times by lotus-born Brahmā to Ṛbhu. He relayed it to Priyavrata, and Priyavrata to Bhāguri. 42

## \|\| 6.8.43 \|\|

*idamārṣaṃ purā prāha ṛbhave kamalodbhavaḥ \|*  
*ṛbhuḥ priyavratāyāha sa ca bhāguraye 'bavīt \|\| 43 \|\|*

Bhāguri passed it to Stambhamitra, and he related it to Dadhīca. Dadhīca passed it to Sārasvata, and Bhṛgu heard it from him. 43

## \|\| 6.8.44 \|\|

*bhāguristaṃbhamitrāya dadhīcāya sa coktavān \|*  
*sārasvatāya tenoktaṃ bhṛguḥsārasvatena ca \|\| 44 \|\|*

Bhṛgu passed it to Purukutsa, and he repeated it to the river Narmadā. Narmadā passed it to the nāgas Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Āpūraṇa. 44

## \|\| 6.8.45 \|\|

*bhṛguṇā purukutsāya narmadāyai sa coktavān \|*  
*narmadā dhṛtarāṣṭrāyanāgāyāpūraṇāya ca \|\| 45 \|\|*

They related it to the nāga king Vāsuki, brahmin, and he passed it to Vatsa, who repeated it to Aśvatara. 45

## \|\| 6.8.46 \|\|

*tābhyāṃ ca nāgarājāya proktaṃ vāsukaye dvija \|*  
*vāsukiḥ prāha vatsāya vatsaścāśvatarāya vai \|\| 46 \|\|*

He passed it on to Kambala, from whom it came to Elāpatra. 46

## \|\| 6.8.47 \|\|

*kaṃbalāya ca tenoktamelāputrāya tena vai \|\| 47 \|\|*

When the sage Vedaśiras visited the lower realm of Pātāla, he heard this purāṇa and related it to Pramati. 47

## \|\| 6.8.48 \|\|

*pātālaṃ samanuprāptaḥ tato vedaśirā muniḥ \|*  
*prāptavānetadakhilaṃ sa ca pramataye dadau \|\| 48 \|\|*

Pramati passed it on to wise Jātūkarṇa, and Jātūkarṇa repeated it for other beings of worthy conduct. 48

## \|\| 6.8.49 \|\|

*dattaṃ pramatinā caitajjātukarṇāya dhīmate \|*  
*jātukarṇena caivoktamanyeṣāṃ puṇyakarmaṇām \|\| 49 \|\|*

With the boon Pulastya granted me, I acquired this sacred text and now I’ve given it to you, Maitreya. 49

## \|\| 6.8.50 \|\|

*pulastyavaradānena mamāpyetatsmṛtiṃ gatam \|*  
*mayāpi tūbhyaṃ maitreya yathāvatkathitaṃ tbidam \|\| 50 \|\|*

At the end of the Kali age, you’ll narrate it to Śinīka. 50

## \|\| 6.8.51 \|\|

*tvamapyetacchinīkāya kalerante vadiṣyasi \|\| 51 \|\|*

One who hears this—the closely guarded means of removing the stains of Kali—brahmin, will be freed from all misdeeds. 51

## \|\| 6.8.52 \|\|

*ityetatparamaṃ guhyaṃ kalikalmaṣanāśanam \|*  
*yaḥ śṛṇoti naro bhaktyā sarvapāpaiḥ pramucyate \|\| 52 \|\|*

One who hears it daily thereby praises all the gods with more effect than may ancestors, yakṣas and other mortals. 52

## \|\| 6.8.53 \|\|

*samastatīrthasnānāni samastāmarasaṃstutiḥ \|*  
*kṛtā tena bhavedetadyaḥ śṛṇotidinedite \|\| 53 \|\|*

The hard-won merit gained by giving a tawny milk cow to a brahmin is certainly achieved by hearing just ten chapters of this discourse. 53

## \|\| 6.8.54 \|\|

*kapilādānajanitaṃ pumyamatyantadurlabham \|*  
*śrutvaitasya daśādhyāyānavāpnoti na saṃśayaḥ \|\| 54 \|\|*

One who hears this whole purāṇa with his mind on Acyuta, the universal, all-embracing foundation of all the worlds, the refuge of the Self, who is both knowledge and its object, who has neither start nor finish, the benefactor of all immortals—such a one will surely win the whole reward of an Aśvamedha sacrifice. 54

## \|\| 6.8.55 \|\|

*yastvetatsakalaṃ śṛṇotipuruṣaḥ kṛtvā manasyacyutaṃ sarvaṃ sarvamayaṃ samastajagatāmādhāramātmāśrayam \|*  
*jñānajñeyamanādimantarahitaṃ sarvāmarāṇāṃ hitaṃ sa prāpnoti na saṃśayostyavikalaṃ yadvājimedhe phalam \|\| 55 \|\|*

The lord, master of moving and unmoving things, who consists of knowledge of the Absolute, infallible Acyuta, overseer of creation, preservation and destruction of all the world, is present at the beginning, end and middle of this purāṇa. Those who hear it, read it or hold it in their hearts with faith will win the highest state of purity existing nowhere else in all the worlds: Hari, the singular state of perfection. 55

## \|\| 6.8.56 \|\|

*yatrādau bhagavāṃścarācaragururmadhye tathānte ca sa brahmajñānamayo 'cyuto 'khilajaganmadhyāntasargaprabhuḥ \|*  
*tatasarvaṃ puruṣaḥ pavitramamalaṃ śṛṇvanpaṭhanvācayanprāpnotyasti na tatphalaṃ tribhuvaneṣvekāntasiddhirhariḥ \|\| 56 \|\|*

Those who set their minds on Hari never go to hell, but heaven also makes it hard to contemplate him. When Hari fills a person’s heart and mind, even Brahmā’s realm will count for little. Since you are the eternal being abiding in people’s stainless minds, the bestower of liberation, then is it any wonder that misdeeds are dispelled when you, Acyuta, are praised? 56

## \|\| 6.8.57 \|\|

*yasminnyastamanirna yāti narakaṃ svargo 'pi yaccintane vighno yatra niveśitātmamanaso brāhmo 'pi loko 'lpakaḥ \|*  
*muktiṃ cetasi yaḥsthito maladhiyāṃ puṃsāṃ dadātyavyayaḥ kiṃ citraṃ yadaghaṃ prayāti vilayaṃ tatrācyute kīrtite \|\| 57 \|\|*

Those who understand and offer sacrifices worship Hari as the everlasting lord of sacrifice with the offerings that they make. The knowledgeable contemplate him as consisting of the Absolute, the highest and higher than the highest. Having attained his state, one is no longer subject to birth or death, gain or loss. He is not existent or nonexistent—what more can be said about Hari? 57

## \|\| 6.8.58 \|\|

*yajñair yajñavido yajanti satataṃ yajñeśvaraṃ karmiṇo yaṃ vai brahmamayaṃ parāvaramayaṃ dhyāyanti ca jñāninaḥ \|*  
*yaṃ saṃcitya na jāyate na mriyate no vardhate hīyate naivāsanna ca sadbhavatyati tataḥ kiṃ vā hareḥ śrūyatām \|\| 58 \|\|*

In the form of the ancestors, the lord enjoys correctly made oblations. In the form of a deity, he has neither start nor finish and is invoked with cries of svāhā and svadhā. In the form of the Absolute, all power abides in him. The measures by which other things are quantified cannot be applied to him. When Hari enters through your ear, he destroys all sins. 58

## \|\| 6.8.59 \|\|

*kavyaṃ yaḥ putṛrūpadhṛgvidhihutaṃ havyaṃ ca bhuktaṃ vibhurdevatve bhagavānanādinidhanaḥsāvahāsvadhāsaṃjñite \|*  
*yasminbrahmaṇi sarvaśaktinilaye mānāni no mānināṃ viṣṭhāyai prabhavanti hanti kaluṣaṃ śrotraṃ sa yāto hariḥ \|\| 59 \|\|*

I bow to the highest spirit, praiseworthy lord, who has neither start nor finish, who is not subject to decay or gain or loss, whose substance is untouched by change. 59

## \|\| 6.8.60 \|\|

*nāntosti yasya na ca yasya samudbhavo 'sti vṛddhirna yasya pariṇāmavivarjitasya \|*  
*nāpakṣayaṃ ca samupaityavikāri vastu yastaṃ natosmi puruṣotmamīśamīḍyam \|\| 60 \|\|*

I bow to the everlasting, changeless spirit who assumes all qualities, both one and many, pure but seeming not so, as he appears in different forms, imbued with knowledge and the source of every being’s power. 60

## \|\| 6.8.61 \|\|

*tasyaiva yonu gumabhugbahudhaika eva suddhopyaśuddha iva bhāti hi mūrtibhedaiḥ \|*  
*jñānānvitaḥsakalasattvavibhūtikartā tasmai namostu puruṣāya sadāvyayāya \|\| 61 \|\|*

I praise him, the single essence of all knowledge, sacrifice and ascetic practice, who skilfully provides enjoyments for individuals, who is at the heart of all three qualities, who is the changeless cause of the cause of existence, the source of every form, who never ages. 61

## \|\| 6.8.62 \|\|

*jñānapravṛttiniyamaikyamayāya puṃso bhogapradānapaṭave triguṇātmakāya \|*  
*avyākṛtāya bhavabāvanakāraṇāya vande svarūpabhavanāya sadājarāya \|\| 62 \|\|*

I always bow to him, who arranges space and air, fire and earth and water, who gifts the sound and other sensations experienced by the senses, who benefits humanity with all his actions and who is manifest, pure and subtle. 62

## \|\| 6.8.63 \|\|

*vyomānilāgnijalabhūracanāmayāya śabdādibhogyaviṣayopanayakṣamāya \|*  
*puṃsaḥsamastakaraṇair upakārakāya vyaktāya sūkṣmabṛhadātmavate natosmi \|\| 63 \|\|*

Thus may Hari, the eternal unborn lord, whose form is manifold and embodies both Spirit and Nature, bestow on all humanity supreme beatitude beyond the reach of birth, decay and other woes. 63
