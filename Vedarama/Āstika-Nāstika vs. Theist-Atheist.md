# Āstika-Nāstika vs. Theist-Atheist

## Āstika-Nāstika vs. Theist-Atheist

JANUARY 29, 2024

Reading Time: 8 minutes

There is no equivalent of the term Atheist in the Vedic tradition. But there is a word—Nāstika—that describes an intellectual position little different from Atheism. Nāstika comprises two roots—na (which means not) and asti (which means it is). Together, they mean “it is not”. What is Nāstika not? The answer is the Vedic tradition. Veda means the truth. Hence, anyone who denies the truth is a Nāstika.

The Āstika position recognizes a Supreme Truth which is also the Supreme Person, with three aspects called sat-chit-ānanda. This Supreme Truth or the Supreme Person is an axiom for the Vedic tradition. This axiom can be described quite simply—(a) chit means that there is truth, (b) sat means that we can know the truth, and (c) ānanda means that knowing the truth will make us happy. These axioms are necessary for anyone interested in the truth because if there were no truth, or if it were not knowable, or if there was no benefit from knowing the truth then we would not endeavor to know the truth.

The term Supreme Truth entails a ladder of truths. The topmost rung of this ladder is the complete truth while the lower rungs of the ladder are partial truths. The Supreme Truth is also the Supreme Person, which means that the Supreme Truth has the power to generate partial and inferior truths by dividing itself. That division would occur if the result was greater happiness. Thus, chit of the Supreme Person is the complete truth, His sat is the process of dividing the complete truth into partial truths, and His ānanda is the reason that the complete and supreme truth divides into partial and inferior truths.

The division of the Supreme Truth creates partial truth just like the division of a higher concept produces lower concepts which remain its parts. For instance, if the concept of color is divided into red, green, and blue, they will be called the parts of color and yet they will not be separable from color. Rather, color will be immanent in each shade, alongside a color transcendent to each of the shades. The division of the whole into parts explains, elaborates, and enlarges the understanding of the whole. For example, to explain, elaborate, and enlarge the understanding of color, we can divide it into red, green, and blue.

The personalization of the truth is necessary for a generative conception of truth. An impersonal truth is static. The truth can be eternal in both cases. If the Supreme Truth was not the Supreme Person (e.g., if truth was a static idea) then it would never expand into partial truths (such as us) and the question of the existence of the truth, knowing that truth, in order to become happy, would also not arise.

By personalizing the Supreme Truth into the Supreme Person, denial of the Supreme Truth becomes denial of the Supreme Person. By equating the Supreme Person and Truth, the Vedic tradition transforms religion into science. Science is the quest for the Supreme Truth. Religion is the quest for the Supreme Person. When the Supreme Person is the Supreme Truth, then science and religion are identical.

Now, the Nāstika position can be described very simply—(a) there is no truth, or (b) the truth cannot be known, or (c) there is no value in knowing the truth. It can also be the separation of Supreme Truth and Supreme Person which makes the complete truth incapable of producing partial truths, such that the questions of the existence of the truth, knowing the truth, and enjoying the truth also don’t arise.

The Nāstika position is quite different from the Atheist position because most Atheists believe that there is truth, the truth can be known, and there is value in knowing the truth. They are Nāstika because of separating the Supreme Truth from the Supreme Person, which means that they hold an impersonal conception of truth. The impersonal conception of truth also becomes a static conception of truth.

For instance, an Atheist scientist—who thinks of reality mathematically—holds an impersonal view of truth as a mathematical formula, not a person. A mathematical formula is not self-generative. Rather, it needs a ginormous computer to calculate. That computer must run according to some laws, which also must be computed by a bigger computer, and so on, ad infinitum. An infinite cascade of computers is required for any mathematical law to produce anything, because no computer is self-computing. Thus, an impersonal conception of truth makes the truth static. Something other than that truth (e.g., a computer that computes a mathematical formula) is required to make that static truth do something. Thereby, the static truth cannot be the Supreme Truth as it relies on something other than itself.

This problem doesn’t arise when we equate the Supreme Truth to the Supreme Person because a person is self-computing. Now, the truth doesn’t need anything other than itself to generate because it is self-sufficient. Mathematical truths are not self-sufficient. Therefore, if someone separates the truth from a person, then they make the theory incapable of generating. The mathematical conception of truth was created at the dawn of modern science by postulating that God is a mathematical thinker and He created the world according to mathematical laws. But it did not explain how mathematical laws are computed. Did God create a mathematical universe alongside a ginormous computer, which requires another computer to compute its own working leading to an infinite cascade of computers? Or, is God the ginormous computer constantly computing the laws for every material particle in the universe?

The impersonal mathematical conception of the truth either leads to an infinite cascade of computers or it requires a God computing mathematical equations without Himself being computed by a computer. In the former case, reality cannot exist and in the latter case the reality must be rooted in a Supreme Person. Therefore, if someone accepts that there is truth, it can be known, and it is worthy of being known, but claims that the Supreme Truth is not the Supreme Person, then his position is incoherent.

And yet, the depersonalization of truth is the standard view regarding the truth at present. In this view, truth is an idea about a thing, but that thing is not truth. We can put this in a philosophical language—truth is epistemological and not ontological at present. In short, truth is not a thing but an idea about that thing. The separation between truths and things begs the question of how these things work, including our minds that try to know the truth of those things. In the personalized version of truth, truth is a person, hence, truth is epistemological and ontological. The Supreme Person is the Supreme Truth.

Under the Nāstika position (a) there is no reality to be known, or (b) even if there is a reality it cannot be known, or (c) there is no value in trying to know reality. This is quite different from the Atheist position in which there is reality, it can be known, and it is worthy of being known, but it is not the Supreme Person. The Nāstika is a nihilist but an Atheist is an impersonalist. Nihilism is logically self-consistent although nobody can be happy being a nihilist. Atheism is logically self-inconsistent because it cannot explain how one static truth produces infinite dynamic variety. The Atheist position is held without recognizing its logical incoherence. The Nāstika position is held uncritically without recognizing that it is nihilistic.

Factually, all Atheists are not completely Nāstika as they may believe that truth exists, it can be known, and there is value in knowing the truth. But all Nāstika are Atheists because for them there is no truth, or it cannot be known, or there is no value in knowing the truth. Charvaka materialists were Nāstika because they claimed that even if there is reality it cannot be known because reality is material and knowledge is conceptual and we can never precisely map the material to the conceptual. Buddhists were similarly Nāstika because they asserted that there is no truth, hence it cannot be known. The ideal situation for Buddhists is to end the attempt to know a non-existent truth and enter deep sleep.

Atheism is a Western phenomenon in which someone who believes in the existence of reality, knowing that reality, and valuing that knowledge, insists that the Supreme Person is not reality. Likewise, Theism is a Western phenomenon in which someone who believes in the existence of reality, knowing that reality, and valuing that knowledge, insists that the Supreme Person is reality, but the world is working due to mathematical laws independent of the Supreme Person and a self-computing computer.

We cannot call a Charvaka materialist an Atheist since he states something stronger—that there is a reality but it cannot be known. We cannot call a Buddhist an Atheist since he states something stronger—that there is no reality hence the question of knowing that reality or valuing that knowledge doesn’t arise. Similarly, Āstika is not Theism because truth and person are identical for the Āstika but not for Theism. The Āstika position is also not Atheism because the Supreme Truth and the Supreme Person are identical.

Thus, Āstika does not mean Theism or vice versa. Similarly, Nāstika doesn’t mean Atheism or vice versa. But since most people don’t understand Āstika-Nāstika, we use Theist-Atheist to say something rather than nothing. That translation should be taken with a pinch of salt as an exigent compromise.

Finally, it must be noted that Advaita Impersonalism is both Āstika and Atheist. There is a reality to be known, it can be known, and knowing it is valuable. That reality is also the Supreme Truth. But since that reality is the self, hence, knowing the self is knowing the Supreme Truth. Anything other than the self is not truth. Thus, knowing the Supreme Person—something other than the self—is a falsehood. Now we must conclude that when we see the self, we are seeing the Supreme Person. Since there is no other Supreme Person, hence, the position is Atheist. But since the truth is knowable and valuable hence it is Āstika. This is another reason to not equate Āstika with Theism since an Āstika can be an Atheist.

The Atheist Āstika suffers from the reverse problem than the Atheist Nāstika. Recall that a mathematical conception of truth cannot produce anything as mathematics is not self-computing. The reverse problem is that if the power of self-computation exists in the self, and the self is the complete truth, then this self must be able to wind up their personal world at will (or create an alternative world at will). Specifically, nobody should be suffering and everyone should be happy if the self is the Supreme Person. Since people are indeed suffering, therefore, the Atheist Āstika position is disconfirmed by that suffering.

The Atheist Āstika position is sometimes called Idealism. An Idealist is an anti-Realist. We establish Realism by the fact of our suffering: (a) nobody wants to suffer, nobody will create their own suffering, and everybody will try to avoid their suffering, and (b) still we are suffering, which means that suffering is forced upon us by something other than the self, which we must call Reality different from the self. Idealism can be true only if there is no suffering, in which case, there is no need for philosophizing. On the other hand, we are philosophizing because we are suffering and want to get out of it. Therefore, the conclusion of philosophy cannot deny the very foundation on which it stands—namely, suffering.

In a materialistic Atheist Āstika position, a mathematical truth is not self-computing. In a spiritualistic Atheist Āstika position, the self creates its own suffering and then tries to get out of it. Thus, all Atheist Āstika positions are self-contradictory. The sustainable Āstika position is in which the Supreme Person is distinct from the self. It is not identical to Theism. An Āstika is a Theist but a Theist is not Āstika, because Supreme Truth and Supreme Person are identical for the Āstika but different for the Theist.

This understanding is quite useful for a conversation with both Theists and Atheists. To the Theist, we can say that you accept a Supreme Person, but we additionally say that this Supreme Person is the Supreme Truth. To the Atheist, we can say that you accept a Supreme Truth, but we additionally say that this Supreme Truth is the Supreme Person. There are legitimate arguments for both of these positions.
