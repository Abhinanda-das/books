# Śrī Kṛṣṇa Karṇāmṛta: Sāraṅga-raṅga-dā Ṭīkā

### Text 1

*cintāmaṇir jayati somagirir gurur me*  
*śikṣā-guruś ca bhagavān śikhi-piñcha-mauliḥ \|*  
*yat-pāda-kalpa-taru-pallava-śekhareṣu*  
*līlā-svayaṁvara-rasaṁ labhate jaya-śrīḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer my respects to my** ***guru*** **Somagiri and my** ***śikṣā-guru,*** **Bhagavān with a peacock feather in his crown. Just by the beauty of the toes of his two feet, which are buds of a desire tree, the goddess of victory attains joy, and by deep attraction to the toe nails of his desire tree feet, Rādhā attains unrestrained** ***rasa.***

**Комментарий:**

I offer respects to the bewilderer of Cupid, the infallible Lord, who is eager for the *rāsa* dance, and who becomes joyful by *rasa* made fortunate by Rādhā. The intelligence of those who meditate on him with devotion, and even the indications of their words arising from that devotion, are most difficult to comprehend even for the best of sages.

I take shelter of Lord Caitanya, whose mercy in the form of a river of nectar, flooding the universe down to the lowest realms, continually shines. The words of Bilvamaṅgala which relish the wealth of Kṛṣṇa’s sweetness, pastimes and beauty, and which arise by his *bhāva* cannot be understood by anyone. One person, though a fool, maddened by the honey from the lotus feet of Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī, explains the work called Kṛ*ṣṇa-karṇāmṛta* according to his understanding. Freely displaying my obstinacy, I will describe with strong inclination the confidential meaning of the work, since the external meaning is clear.By the pool of *Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta* may the affectionate devotees nourish my mouth, which is like a cow yearning for Gokula but exhausted from wandering in the desert

May this commentary called “Giver of Joy to the Swans” be purified by the devotees who are like swans greedy for the *rasa* of songs sung by persons endowed the mood of real devotees.

There was a *brāhmaṇa* named Bilvamaṅgala, a wise king among poets, living on the western bank of the Kṛṣṇa-veṇvā River\[1\]in South India. Spending his time on his seat of *kuśa* grass facing east, he became attracted to a prostitute named Cintāmaṇi, the best among singers, who shamed the best musical skills. She lived on the east bank of the river. As his love was stimulated by the clouds thundering in the darkness of the rain, like a blind man, he left his house without considering the obstacles on his path, crossed the river by holding onto a floating corpse with his hands, and arrived at the door of her house. Not hearing its repeated hissing, grasping the tail of a black snake which had half-entered a hole in the wall, he scaled the wall. Falling into a channel of water, he fainted.

She and her friends saw him because of flashing lightning. “How unfortunate” she said and, bringing him inside, made him comfortable with various offerings. Listening to him relate how he had come there, she spoke to him with disgust while her body quivered. “Who except you, a fool though knowledgeable of all scriptures, would harm yourself for a drop of spoiled liquor. I should be cursed. A sinful person, I cheat men with a false show of love and steal their minds and wealth. This would not have happened if you had developed attraction for Lord Kṛṣṇa. Tomorrow I should give up everything and worship Kṛṣṇa.” Deciding in this way, she served him that night by singing songs about the pastimes of Kṛṣṇa with Rādhā in the groves where they performed the *rāsa* dance.

Hearing her words, he criticized himself and thought, “I should also give up everything tomorrow and worship the Lord.” He could not sleep that night. His previous sprout of *prema* was awakened by hearing Cintāmaṇi’s song. Thinking of Kṛṣṇa, dear to Rādhā, as dearer than a million of his lives, in the morning he offered respects to Cintāmaṇi and, going on the road, approached the greatest Vaiṣṇava named Somagiri on the bank of the river. Informing him of his situation, he received from Somagiri the Gopāla-mantra, king of *mantras.* Having received the *mantra*, his attraction for Kṛṣṇa awakened, but though he longed to go Vṛndāvana and was agitated with tears and hairs standing on end, he remained at that place for some days to serve his *guru*. There he wrote a book describing the pastimes of Kṛṣṇa. Seeing the book, his *guru* named him Līlāśuka, the parrot who recites Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes. Because he was harassed by relatives in that place, he took *sannyāsa.* He then departed with great eagerness for Vṛndāvana with the permission of his *guru.*

While going along the path, first he prayed for an appearance of the Lord with his pastimes while thinking himself empty, having fallen in the waves of longing arising from currents of *prema* which had swelled up by the Lord’s sudden appearance within. Arriving at the area of Mathurā, swallowed by a whirlpool of greed appearing in the ocean of *anurāga* agitated by the sudden appearance of the Lord’s pastimes within, he prayed to see the Lord directly. Arriving at Mathurā he thought he had directly met the Lord during his appearance in his mind. Arriving at Vṛndāvana, he saw Kṛṣṇa directly and described him though Kṛṣṇa was beyond mind and words. Whatever he uttered at that time was recorded and preserved by the devotees in his association. He remained for some days in Vṛndāvana. Then he entered into pastimes with Kṛṣṇa. This is the common story well known to all and coming through the *guru-paramparā*.

Departing from his home for Vṛndāvana with the madness of *prema*, Bilvamaṅgala offers an opening verse in the form of *saṅkīrtana*, invoking auspiciousness for his *guru* and for his Lord who is also a form of his *guru.* This invocation is not meant to destroy obstacles to the desired goal as with other authors’ invocations, since he could not even allude to a purpose for the work since he spoke out of the madness of *prema.* He speaks in Sanskrit, a language common in South India and he speaks in verse because he was the best of poets. But it is the nature of the pure Vaiṣṇava to remember *guru* and the Lord while sleeping, eating or walking about. Therefore he begins the work invoking his *guru* and the Lord.

Somagiri, my *guru,* exists with all excellence (*jayati*). Who is he? Being most excellent, he is described as *cintāmaṇi,* because one can fulfill all desires simply by taking shelter of him. Or, *jayati* can mean “I offer my respects to him.” *Kāvya-prakāśa* says *jayaty-arthena namskāra ākṣipyate*: the word j*ayati* indicates offering respect. Thus *somagirir jayati* means “I offer respects to Somagiri.”

I offer respects to Bhagavān, the object of my worship. Who is Bhagavān? He is ornamented with peacock feathers on his head, or has head ornament with peacock feathers (*śikhi-piccha-mauliḥ*). This refers only to Kṛṣṇa, who wanders about Vṛndāvana. The present tense of *jayati* indicates that the pastimes of Kṛṣṇa are eternal. He is shown to be a *śikṣā-guru* by verses such as the following:

*naivopayanty apacitiṁ kavayas taveśa*  
*brahmāyuṣāpi kṛtam ṛddha-mudaḥ smarantaḥ*  
*yo ’ntar bahis tanu-bhṛtām aśubhaṁ vidhunvann*  
*ācārya-caittya-vapuṣā sva-gatiṁ vyanakti*

**O my Lord! Intelligent persons can not fully express their indebtedness to you, even if they were endowed with the lifetime of Brahmā, because on remembering what you have done form them, their joy increases even more: appearing externally as** ***guru*** **and internally as Paramātmā, you reveal the goal of** ***prema*** **to the devotees. SB 11.29.6**

*teṣāṁ satata-yuktānāṁ bhajatāṁ prīti-pūrvakam \|*  
*dadāmi buddhi-yogaṁ taṁ yena mām upayānti te \|\|*

**I appear within the hearts of those who constantly desire to be with me and worship me with great love. By this appearance in their heart, they attain my direct association. BG 10.10**

*ācāryaṁ māṁ vijānīyān nāvanmanyeta karhicit*  
*na martya-buddhyāsūyeta sarva-deva-mayo guruḥ*

**One should know the** ***ācārya*** **as myself and never disrespect him in any way. One should not envy him, thinking him an ordinary man, for he is the representative of all the** ***devatās.*** **SB 11.17.27**

*karṇākarṇi sakhī-janena vijane dūtī-stuti-prakriyā*  
*patyur vañcana-cāturī guṇanikā kuṇḍa-prayāṇ niśi \|*  
*vādhiryaṁ guru-vāci veṇu-virutāv utkarṇateti vratān*  
*kaiśoreṇa tavādya kṛṣṇa guruṇā gaurī-gaṇaḥ paṭhyate \|\|*

**O Kṛṣṇa, today your age of youth, in the role of** ***guru,*** **is teaching the golden** ***gopīs*** **the art of whispering in each other’s ears, the method of making verses of praise for messengers when alone, cleverness in cheating husbands, practice in sneaking to the forest at night, deafness to the words of the elders, and rapt hearing of the flute sound. BRS 2.1.333**

Thus, Kṛṣṇa is also my *guru* in giving realization of various aspects of his sweetness.

Who is Kṛṣṇa? He is my *śikṣā-guru.* This is also described in verses such as the following:

*premadaṁ ca me kāmadaṁ ca me*  
*vedanaṁ ca me vaibhavaṁ ca me \|*  
*jīvanaṁ ca me jīvitaṁ ca me*  
*daivataṁ ca me deva nāparam \|\|*

**You are my object of surrender. Nothing else is. You alone give me prema. You alone give conjugal prema to me. You alone give its teachings. You alone are my wealth. You alone are the cause of my life. You alone are my life.** ***Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta*** **104**

Bilvamaṅgala realized the various aspects of sweetness through Kṛṣṇa’s sudden appearance, described in following verses:

*tāsām āvirabhūc chauriḥ smayamāna-mukhāmbujaḥ*  
*pītāmbara-dharaḥ sragvī sākṣān manmatha-manmathaḥ*

**Then Lord Kṛṣṇa, a smile on his lotus face, appeared before the** ***gopīs.*** **Wearing a garland and a yellow garment, he directly appeared as one who can bewilder the mind of Cupid, who himself bewilders the minds of ordinary people. SB 10.32.2**

*yan martya-līlaupayikaṁ sva-yoga*  
*māyā-balaṁ darśayatā gṛhītam*  
*vismāpanaṁ svasya ca saubhagarddheḥ*  
*paraṁ padaṁ bhūṣaṇa-bhūṣaṇāṅgam*

**Kṛṣṇa possesses that form suitable for human pastimes to show the full capacity of his** ***yoga-māyā.*** **That form astonishes even the lord of Vaikuṇṭha. It is the pinnacle of auspicious qualities and enhances the beauty of his ornaments. SB 3.2.12**

*gopyas tapaḥ kim acaran yad amuṣya rūpaṁ*  
*lāvaṇya-sāram asamordhvam ananya-siddham*  
*dṛgbhiḥ pibanty anusavābhinavaṁ durāpam*  
*ekānta-dhāma yaśasaḥ śrīya aiśvarasya*

**What austerities must the** ***gopīs*** **have performed! With their eyes they always drink the nectar of Lord Kṛṣṇa’s form, which is the essence of loveliness and is not to be equaled or surpassed. That loveliness is the only abode of beauty, fame and opulence. It is self-perfect, ever fresh and extremely rare. SB 10.44.14**

He thus describes him with the peacock feather on his head. He then considers in his mind objects suitable for comparison to Kṛṣṇa’s limbs. Seeing that all things were most unsuitable for comparison, he has a revelation that all things are conquered just by the glow from Kṛṣṇa’s toe nail. With a vision of Rādhā’s heart being enchanted by Kṛṣṇa’s sweetness, he concludes the verse with words having a double meaning.

The feet of Kṛṣṇa, pink like lotuses, are buds of the desire tree, because they can fulfill all desires. The goddess of victory obtains happiness (*rasa*), which chooses its own object, arising from pastimes, just by the beauty at the tips the toe nails of his feet. (The feet conquer all other objects in beauty just by the luster of their toe nails.) It is said later *kamala-vipina-vīthī-garva-sarvaṁ kaṣābhyām*: his feet completely crush the pride of rows of lotus groves (verse 12). There are many other verses glorifying the luster of his toe nails such as:

*vadanendu-vinirjitaū śaśī*  
*daśadhṁ deva padaā prapadya te*  
*adhikaā śriyam aśnutetarṁā*  
*tava kṁruṇya-vijṇmbhitam kiyat*

**O playful Lord! The moon, conquered by your moon-like face, serves your feet in ten forms. Your feet, manifesting mercy, attain indescribable, ever-increasing beauty.** ***Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛata*** **96**

The other meaning of *jayaśrī* is “she who possesses glory *(śrī*) with excellence (*jaya*) in beauty, amorous pastimes, water pastimes, joking and gambling. Or*, jaya* can mean “she who defeats other groups of Vraja girls, as well as Arundhatī, Parvatī and others by her beauty, vows of chastity, good fortune and cleverness. Though *śrī* refers to Lakṣmī, it also refers to Rādhā, because Lakṣmī is her portion. Kṛṣṇa is the root of Nārāyaṇa:

*nārāyaṇas tvaṁ na hi sarva-dehinām*  
*ātmāsy adhīśākhila-loka-sākṣī \|*  
*nārāyaṇo ’ṅgaṁ nara-bhū-jalāyanāt*  
*tac cāpi satyaṁ na tavaiva māyā \|\|*

**Are you not the original Nārāyaṇa, O supreme controller, since you are the Soul of every embodied being and the eternal witness of all created realms? Indeed, Lord Nārāyaṇa is your expansion, and he is called Nārāyaṇa because he is the generating source of the primeval water of the universe. He is real, not a product of your illusory Māyā. SB 10.14.14**

*yasyaika-niśvasita-kālam athāvalambya*  
*jīvanti loma-vilajā jagad-aṇḍa-nāthāḥ \|*  
*viṣṇur mahān sa iha yasya kalā-viśeṣo*  
*govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi \|\|*

**I worship the Supreme Lord Govinda whose first expansion is Mahā-viṣṇu, who determines the appearance and disappearance of the rulers within the universe (even Kṣirodakaśāyī-viṣṇu) by his exhalation and inhalation of breath.** ***Brahma-saṁhitā*** **48**

Similarly, his beloved Rādhā is the root of Lakṣmī. Thus Jaya-śrī refers to Rādhā. She attains *rasa* which boldly chooses its object (*svayamvara*). At first her deep attraction remained with downcast face because of its nature of shyness. But with her eyes absorbed in the ocean of his beauty after seeing the splendor of his toe nails, Rādhā became enchanted. By that deep attraction (*līlayā*), by particular qualities sprouting forth because of her *bhāva,* she attained a *rasa* which was independent, which gave up all shyness concerning the rules of *dharma*. The present tense (*labhate*) is used to indicate that she had newer and newer experiences at every moment of his sweetness and of her own love.

According to the opinion of some, the feet mentioned refer to those of Somagiri. His toe nails attain a wealth of victory (*jaya-śrīḥ*) over the sixty-two obstacles\[2\]arising from the five types of suffering,\[3\]the senses and the six enemies starting with lust.\[4\]

Or the verse can indicate remembrance of his three *gurus*: the *vartmoddeṣa-guru* (Cintāmaṇi), the *mantra-guru* (Somagiri) and the *śikṣā-guru* (Kṛṣṇa). Some say that the word *cintāmaṇi* refers to the prostitute. He regards her as most excellent (*jayati*) since by her words alone his attraction for Kṛṣṇa was aroused.

### Text 2

*asti svas-taruṇī-karāgra-vigalat-kalpa-prasūnāplutaṁ*  
*vastu prastuta-veṇu-nāda-laharī-nirvāṇa-nirvyākulam \|*  
*srasta-srasta-niruddha-nīvi-vilasad-gopī-sahasrāvṛtaṁ*  
*hasta-nyasta-natāpavargam akhilodāraṁ kiśorākṛti \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**He is the supreme object, having the intrinsic form of a youth. He is motionless because of the bliss caused by the waves of music from his flute used for bewildering the** ***gopīs.*** **He is covered with desire tree flowers flowing from the quivering hands of heavenly damsels and is surrounded by thousands of brilliant** ***gopīs*** **who must hold up their skirts with their hands because their skirts repeatedly become loosened by their excitement. He gives** ***prema*** **to those surrendered to him and is the most munificent of all beings.**

**Комментарий:**

As he wanders along the path towards Vṛndāvana, he makes a statement which reveals the conclusion of *bhakti* through longing, according to the conduct of a *sādhaka*, but which also reveals pure *rasa* with the greed of a perfected devotee because of going to a visionary state at that time. Because of the presence of these two states, he utters one statement with two meanings. After revealing the meaning according to his inner state, I will briefly show the meaning according to his external state.

Even though investigation of such states does not take place during fainting in the state of *unmada* (madness), pure *prema* spontaneously by itself manifests non-contradictory *rasas* according to the conclusions of *bhakti.* It is the nature of *prema* that it does not touch *rasābhasa,* which is contrary to the conclusions of *bhakti,* during the states of *moha* or *unmada.*

The devotees following him will ask the leader, “O master! Why are you going there? What is there?” He will then describe that highest form, the supreme object to be worshipped by all persons, called the *āśraya* in the *Bhāgavatam,* which is the supreme shelter of all spiritual forms such as *prābhava* and *vaibhava avatāras*, *śaktyāveśāvatāras,* the shelter of Kṛṣṇa’s *vilāsa* forms\[5\]such as *bālya* and *paugaṇḍa* forms as well his *prakāśa* forms, the shelter of the unlimited Vaikuṇṭha arising from his *cit-śakti,* the shelter of the unlimited universes which are his display of power *(vaibhava*), arising from his *māyā-śakti,* and the shelter of the *jīva-śakti*. Because of entering internally at that time, he sees again a manifestation of Kṛṣṇa and then begins to utter some words, which form the description in this verse.

Here is the external meaning. There is an object (*vastu*) which always exists *(asti*) in Vṛndāvana (this phrase should be supplied). *Vastu* can be derived from the root *vas* (to dwell). Adding the suffix *tu* it becomes *vastu*. It thus indicates an object that dwells in Vṛndāvana in one form in all three phases of time, performing pastimes. The word *vastu* is in the neuter gender to indicate a general condition.

“Is this object the formless Brahman?” No, it is not. It has the form of a young boy (*kiśorākṛti*). He has an intrinsic form (*svarūpa*) which is at the peak of new youth (*kiśora*). His form or body is not different from himself. A body like that of a *jīva* is rejected by this phrase. These facts are also mentioned in the *Bhāgavatam*: *vedyaṁ vāstavam atra vastu*: the Lord is the real (*vāstavam*) object (*vastu*), who can be understood by those without selfish intentions. (SB 1.1.2) *Vinācyutād vastutarāṁ na vācyaṁ*: no object other than the Lord (*vasutarām)* can be described. (SB 10.42.43) *Nātaḥ paraṁ parama yad bhavataḥ svarūpam*: I do not see that you are different from your form. (SB 3.9.3)

“But all the Lord’s forms are youthful. Which form is this among all those forms?” He is full of the highest bliss (*nirvāṇa)* caused by the waves of notes, scales, and melodies coming from the sounds of his flute which he uses during the *rāsa* dance to attract the young women of Vraja. *Nirvāṇa* can also mean that his mind and senses become completely absorbed in the waves of sound. But he remains completely without bewilderment (*nirvyākulam*). *Nir* means “without” or can stand for *nirgatam*: freed from disturbance. This means that Kṛṣṇa is completely motionless because absorption of his mind and senses. The word *nirvāṇam* means happiness and liberation according to *Viśva-kośa.*

In the evening, he becomes covered with flowers from the desire tree, which flow from the quivering hands of the young women of heaven who are attracted by the sound of his flute and have lost control on seeing his sweetness. Because of Bilvamaṅgala’s lack of control during *prema*, he mentions *kalpa* instead of *kalpa-taru.* Or among friends, the one word can be understood to represent the whole phrase.

Because of hearing his flute, the girls’ skirts become repeated loose even in front of their elders and husbands. Out of fear of embarrassment, they tie up their skirts properly, but again they become loose. Then they hold them up with their hands. Some of them, unable to tolerate the time it takes to tie up their skirts, hold them up with their hands. Kṛṣṇa is surrounded by thousands of *gopīs* shining with youth, beauty, grace, and deep love, who hold their skirts up with their hands. This object (*vastu*) is Kṛṣṇa at the beginning of the *rāsa* dance described in *Bhāgavatam*. It does not describe a scene for meditation mentioned in the scriptures, but is specifically Kṛṣṇa during the *rāsa* dance, since later in the text there is no mention of others who surround Kṛṣṇa in this manner.

He is the cause of putting liberation (*apavarga*) in the hands of his devotees *(nata*)--destroying their gross and subtle bodies--by bestowing a spiritual body of bliss as one of his associates:

*martyo yadā tyakta-samasta-karmā*  
*niveditātmā vicikīrṣito me \|*  
*tadāmṛtatvaṁ pratipadyamāno*  
*mayātma-bhūyāya ca kalpate vai \|\|*

**When a person who gives up all** ***karmas*****, offers himself entirely to** ***guru,*** **eagerly desires to render service to me alone, he achieves freedom from death and, more important, is qualified to be an associate of mine in** ***prema.*** **SB 11.29.34**

Or *apavarga* can simply mean *prema-bhakti.* The phrase the means “He gives *prema* to his devotees.” *Apavarga* can mean *bhakti* as in the phrase *yathā-varṇa-vidhānam apavargaś cāpi bhavati: bhakti* is then achieved as indicated in the Vedas. (SB 5.19.19) This is how Śrīdhara Svāmī explains the word *apavarga* in that context.

He is more generous than all others, even the desire tree (*akhilodaram*). That is because he gives more than one desires.

*satyaṁ diśaty arthitam arthito nṛṇāṁ*  
*naivārthado yat punar arthitā yataḥ \|*  
*svayaṁ vidhatte bhajatām anicchatām*  
*icchāpidhānaṁ nija-pāda-pallavam \|\|*

**The Lord certainly gives desired objects to devotees who request them, but he does not give those objects in such a way that the devotee will ask again after finishing his enjoyment. In other words, he gives his lotus feet, which include all desirables, to those worshippers who do not even desire them. SB 5.19.27**

Or, another meaning is “he is supreme (*udaram*) because of all of his good qualities suitable for the worshipable leader.”

The internal meaning is as follows. This object exists from previously (*vastu asti*). The word *vastu* indicates that beauty, sweetness, grace and other good qualities dwell (*vasanti*) in him. Or *vastu* can stand for *vas* meaning “to clothe.” He completely covers the hearts of all beings from *ātmārāmas* to even insentient objects, and particularly women-- and especially of the women of Vraja--with sentiments of bewilderment and fainting caused by his sweetness, singing and the sound of his flute. What is that object? It has the form of a young boy. He may worry. “How can the chaste dependent *gopīs* come to me? How will the *rāsa* dance take place?” But he is without worry (*nirvyākulam)* because of the bliss generated from hearing the sound of the *gopīs’* belts and ankle bells. They have been attracted by the waves of sound from his flute.

He is the cause of liberation (*apavarga*) from the chains of shyness and restraining rules imposed by elders for the *gopīs* who have taken shelter of his feet. This has been easily produced (*hasta-nyasta*) by the sound of his flute. It is said:

*na pāraye ’haṁ niravadya-saṁyujāṁ*  
*sva-sādhu-kṛtyaṁ vibudhāyuṣāpi vaḥ \|*  
*yā mābhajan durjara-geha-śṛṅkhalāḥ*  
*saṁvṛścya tad vaḥ pratiyātu sādhunā \|\|*

**I am not able to repay my debt for your spotless service, even within a lifetime of Brahmā. Your connection with me is beyond reproach. You have worshiped me, cutting off all domestic chains, which are difficult to break. Therefore please let your own glorious deeds be your compensation. SB 10.32.22**

He is generous to all the *gopīs* (*akhilodaram*). He fulfills all their desires by performing pastimes exactly as they desire. Jayadeva has said *viśveṣām anurañjanena janayann anandam*: he generates bliss in the *gopīs* by his love. (*Gītā-govinda* 48)

Or, the phrase can mean “he who is the highest in excellence (*udāram*) because of having all qualities worthy of worship.” The rest of the interpretation is the same as the external one.

*ākṛṣya rādhāṁ vraja-subhruvāṁ gaṇād*  
*bhaṅgyā tayā gūḍha-vilāsa-lābhataḥ \|*  
*kuñje rasāsvāda-viśeṣa-labdhaye*  
*prārambhi rāso rasikendra-maulinā \|\|*

**Attracting Rādhā from among the group of beautiful women of Vraja by trickery, after enjoying intimate pastimes with her, the king of** ***rasa*** **then began the** ***rāsa*** **dance in the grove in order to attain a special taste of** ***rasa.\[6\]***

As I am the compiler of this work, I will speak about the meaning arising from the external condition after and will first relate in detail the internal meaning, which is my preferred meaning. (This has not been done in this verse, but will be done in the following verses where there are several meanings.)

Bilvamaṅgala developed a greed for Rādhā on hearing about Rādhā’s *anurāga* for Kṛṣṇa from the mouth of the prostitute. This produced his worship on the path of *rāgānuga.* On this path of *rāgānuga,* even the *sādhakas* who have not developed *bhāva* produce in their minds a desired spiritual body and perform service to the Lord in that body. That body appears spontaneously however in persons who have developed *bhāva* or *rati.* Bilvamaṅgala had developed *bhāv*a of the *madhura* type and then attained the level of *anurāga* (after *prema, sneha and rāga*). At that time his spiritual body was continually present.

*iṣṭe svārasikī rāgaḥ paramāviṣṭatā bhavet \|*  
*tan-mayī yā bhaved bhaktiḥ sātra rāgātmikoditā \|\|*

***Rāga*** **is defined as spontaneous, deep thirst for the object of love.** ***Bhakti*** **that is impelled exclusively by such a thirst is called** ***rāgātmika-bhakti.*** **BRS 1.2.272**

*virājantīm abhivyaktāṁ vraja-vāsī janādiṣu \|*  
*rāgātmikām anusṛtā yā sā rāgānugocyate \|\|*

***Rāgānuga-bhakti*** **is defined as that** ***bhakti*** **which follows after the** ***rāgātmika-bhakti*** **found distinctively in the inhabitants of Vraja. BRS 1.2.270**

*rāgātmikāika-niṣṭhā ye vraja-vāsi-janādayaḥ \|*  
*teṣāṁ bhāvāptaye lubdho bhaved atrādhikāravān \|\|*

**That person who is greedy for attaining a** ***bhāva*** **similar to that of the inhabitants of Vraja—who are fixed solely in** ***rāgātmika-bhakti*****—is qualified for** ***rāgānuga-bhakti.*** **BRS 1.2.291**

*tat-tad-bhāvādi-mādhurye śrute dhīr yad apekṣate \|*  
*nātra śāstraṁ na yuktiṁ ca tal-lobhotpatti-lakṣaṇaṁ \|\|*

**The appearance of that greed is indicated when the intelligence does not depend on rules of scripture and logic, after realizing to some degree the sweetness of the Vraja inhabitants’ love through the process of hearing from the scriptures. BRS 1.2.292**

*syād dṛḍheyaṁ ratiḥ premā prodyan snehaḥ kramād ayam \|*  
*syān mānaḥ praṇayo rāgo’nurāgo bhāva ity api \|\|*

**When** ***rati*** **becomes fixed it becomes** ***prema. Prema*** **develops into** ***sneha,māna, pranaya,rāga, anurāga*** **and** ***mahābhāva.***

*bījam ikṣuḥ sa ca rasaḥ sa guḍaḥ khaṇḍa eva saḥ \|*  
*sa śarkarā sitā sā ca sā yathā syāt sitopalā \|\|*

**This is similar to a seed which becomes sugar cane, sugar cane juice, molasses, dried molasses, unrefined sugar, refined sugar and rock candy.** ***Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi*** **14.59-60**

*Anurāga* is described as follows:

*sadānubhūtam api yaḥ kuryān nava-navaṁ priyam \|*  
*rāgo bhavan nava-navaḥ so’nurāga itīryate \|\|*

**When** ***rāga*** **becomes ever fresh and makes ones lover an ever-fresh experience though one has experienced the lover continually, it is called** ***anurāga*****.** ***Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi*** **4.146**

This will become clear later in the text.

### Text 3

*cāturyaika-nidāna-sīma-capalāpāṅga-cchaṭā-mantharaṁ*  
*lābvaṇyāmṛta-vīci-lolita-dṛśaṁ lakṣmī-kaṭākṣādṛtam \|*  
*kālindī-pulināṅgana-praṇayinaṁ kāmāvatārāṅkuraṁ*  
*bālaṁ nīlam amī vayaṁ madhurima-svārājyam ārādhnumaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**We serve the Lord, that youth with blackish complexion, who enjoys pastimes in bowers on the bank of the Yamunā River, who becomes reverent on attaining the glance of Rādhā, who has eyes that quiver with longing on seeing Rādhā’s beauty, who then stuns Rādhā by the array of his glances, which are the ultimate in fickleness and the main source of his cleverness, who manifests a sprout of** ***prema*** **for Rādhā, and who makes his sweetness easily available to her.**

**Комментарий:**

He recognizes himself as one *gopī* among secondary *sakhīs,* among the *sakhīs* situated by the side of Rādhā, the chief of all the *gopīs*, who is endowed with the auspicious *anurāga* of which he has previously heard and who is standing beside Kṛṣṇa. He then speaks this verse.

We (*amī vayam*), the followers, serve that youth (*bālam*) by actions such as waving a *cāmara* and giving betel. The word *bālam* should mean a youth, not a baby, since the previous verse already described him as *kiśorākṛti* and in later verses he is also describes as such in his pastimes. As well, in the *smṛti* and *alaṅkāra* scriptures, in considering the three ages of a person, it is well known that the word *bāla* refers to a youth. *Bālyam āṣoḍaśābdāntam*: *bālyam* means“till the end of the age of sixteen.” If one were to explain the meaning otherwise it would be impossible for Kṛṣṇa to express his sentiments of conjugal love.

He is blackish, the color of a blue sapphire (*nīlam*). This means he is the embodiment of *śṛṅgāra-rasa*, since *śyāma* is its specific color.\[7\]It is also said *śṛṅgāraḥ sakhi mūrtimān:* O friend, Kṛṣṇa is the embodiment of *śṛṅgāra-rasa*. (*Gītā-govinda* 1.48) He enjoys pastimes (*praṇayinam*) in rooms (*aṅgana)* made of four *madhavī* creepers on the bank of the Yamunā River. He becomes reverent on attaining the glance of Rādhā (*lakṣmī-katākṣādṛtam)*, who is standing with downcast face, though she has the most intense longing for him because of her great shyness and her obstinate mood (called *vāmya-bhāva*). Out of thirst, his eyes tremble with waves of sweet beauty for Rādhā among all those standing around.

Deciding to give up the other *gopīs* because of desire to have private pastimes with Rādhā, he shows his cleverness by indicating that they should both leave, while being unseen by others. He stuns her by the beauty of his glance which is endowed with the highest (*sīma*) fickleness and which is first among the primary sources *(nidāna*) of his cleverness. Then he becomes intent *(ādṛtam*) on her glancing. This is the science of intimations or hints. Just by his glance he says“Let this be understood.”

Another meaning can be given. According to *Brahma-saṁhitā*, Kṛṣṇa is adored by the glances of the women of Vraja in verses like *śriyaḥ kāntāḥ kāntaḥ parama-puruṣaḥ* and *lakṣmī-sahasra-śata-sambhrama-sevyamāna*. Still, Kṛṣṇa is absorbed Rādhā’s activities. He is stunned by the beauty of the glance of Rādhā who is most skilful (*cāturyaika*), the utmost limit of causes of skillfulness in order to have pastimes with him.

From Kṛṣṇa arises a sprout of manifestation (*avatāra*) of special *prema (kāma)* for Rādhā. *Kāma* here means *prema* as explained in the following verse:

*premaiva gopa-rāmānāṁ kāma ity agamat prathām*  
*ity uddhavādayo ’py etaṁ vāñchanti bhagavat-priyāḥ.*

**The pure love of the** ***gopīs*** **has become celebrated by the name** ***kāma*****. The dear devotees of the Lord, headed by Śrī Uddhava, desire to taste that love. BRS 1.2.285**

Bilvamaṅgala experiences Kṛṣṇa’s sweetness. He says that all types of sweetness (*madhurima*) are easily available (*svārājyam*) in Kṛṣṇa. That sweetness follows from his intimate friendship with Rādhā. This friendship is evident in later verses such as *rādhā-payodharotsaṅga*: he cannot leave Rādhā’s breast. (*Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta* 79) *ye vā śaiśava-cāpalya-vyatikarā rādhāvarodhonmukāḥ*: he is engaged in childish pranks such as obstructing Rādha. (*Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta* 109)

The external meaning is as follows. Not only does that attractive object (*vastu* of the previous verse) exist for Kṛṣṇa’s associates. We also (*amī vayam*) worship that object. We worship the youth (*bālam*) praised by Brahmā and Śukrācārya.

*jānanta eva jānantu kiṁ bahūktyā na me prabho*  
*manaso vapuṣo vāco vaibhavaṁ tava go-caraḥ*

**Brahmā said: There are people who say, “I know everything about Kṛṣṇa.” Let them think that way. As far as I am concerned, I do not wish to speak very much about this matter. O my Lord, let me say this much: As far as your opulences are concerned, they are all beyond the reach of my mind, body and words. SB 10.14.38**

*nāyaṁ sukhāpo bhagavān dehināṁ gopikā-sutaḥ*  
*jñānināṁ cātma-bhūtānāṁ yathā bhaktimatām iha*

**The Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, the son of mother Yaśodā, is accessible to devotees engaged in spontaneous loving service, but he is not as easily accessible to mental speculators, to those striving for self-realization by severe austerities and penances, or to those who consider the body the same as the self. SB 10.9.21**

There is astonishment hinted by the modulation of the voice. Because he remembers his previous material state, the masculine plural is used (*amī*).\[8\]Plural number is used in the verb to include others who have taken to worshipping Kṛṣṇa. He then describes in details his object of love by stating Kṛṣṇa’s wonderful qualities. His nature is to enjoy in houses on the bank of the Yamunā (*kālindī-pulināṅgana-praṇayinaṁ*). His nature is sweetness (*madhurima-svārajyam*). He displays cleverness in attracting the *gopīs* by his crooked requests, convincing speech, and tricks (*cāturyeka-nidāna*). This shows his cunning nature. He has a most flickering glance which stuns Rādhā. This shows his power to enchant. He becomes stunned (*mantharam*) by the array or luster of glances of the *gopīs.* This shows his nature of being controlled by *prema.*

Because of him, the eyes of Rādhā and others seeing him begin to quiver out of thirst, because of sweet waves in that ocean of beauty which have appeared by seeing Rādhā’s face. This shows his beauty. He is worshipped by the glances of Lakṣmī situated in the sky, who has become attracted by the sound of his flute. Thus he is gazed at with longing. This shows his ability to steal the minds of women.

He is the first tender shoot of a tree trunk for the appearance of *avatāras* and the origin of material lust, which is like the leaf coming from the sprout, and which are dull reflections of many forms of Pradyumna of the *catur-vyūha,* within unlimited universes, which are like the branches from the trunk. This means that Kṛṣṇa, the young boy of Vṛndāvana, is the original Cupid, the source of all spiritual and material Cupids. This is indicated by the fact that he is worshipped in the scriptures by the *kāma-bīja* and the *kāma-gāyatrī-mantra*. As well he is present even today in Vṛndāvana in the form of Madana (Cupid) Gopāla, endowed with a great quantity of power evident by intense symptoms of love, who is an ocean of nectar of the sweetest, natural beauty which attracts the hearts of unlimited people and bewilders billions of Cupids. The phrase *kāmāvatārāṅkuram* thus indicates that Kṛṣṇa is the source of all *avatāras* and the essence of all love.

*rāsa-līlā jayaty eṣā yayā saṁyujyate’niśam \|*  
*harer vidagdhatā-bheryā rādhā-saubhāgya-dundubhiḥ \|\|*

**All glories to the** ***rāsa-līlā,*** **by which the kettledrums of Kṛṣṇa’s cleverness are joined with the drums of Rādhā’s beauty.**

### Text 4

*barhottaṁsa-vilāsa-kuntala-bharaṁ mādhurya-magnānanaṁ*  
*pronmīlan-nava-yauvanaṁ pravilasad-veṇu-praṇādāmṛtam \|*  
*āpīna-stana-kuṭmalābhir abhito gopībhir ārādhitaṁ*  
*jyotiś cetasi naś cakāstu jagatām ekābhirāmādbhutam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**May that light illuminate our hearts! It has the form of a youth fully manifesting the last stage of** ***kaiśora*** **age. He has a mass of curly hair topped with a quivering peacock feather. His face is immersed in sweetness. He is the cause of nectar by means of the frolicking sounds of his flute. He is served on all sides by** ***gopīs*** **with full, bud-like breasts. He astonishes all by his intense love of one** ***gopī,*** **Rādhā, among all the** ***gopīs*** **dallying around him.**

**Комментарий:**

There are three stages in the Lord manifesting himself to others. In his first appearance he is manifested in the consciousness (*sphūrti*). Then there is a confused state with inability to distinguish his appearance in consciousness and his direct manifestation. Then he directly manifests his form (*sākṣāt-kāra*).By hearing topics related to conjugal relationships, there arises a state of longing (*lālasā*) in separation even before having met Kṛṣṇa for the first time directly (*pūrva-rāga*).\[9\]Bilvamaṅgala now prays in eight verses for a *sphūrti* of Kṛṣṇa, who sports in the *rāsa-līlā*, with a mentality colored by misery and despondency which arises in *gopīs* who have already met Kṛṣṇa directly, even it is only an internal *sphūrti* at this time.\[10\]This is expressed in *Gītā-govinda* 3.15 as well (though Kṛṣṇa is longing in separation in following verse):

*tāni sparśa-sukhāni te ca taralaḥ snigdhā dṛśor vibhramās*  
*tad-vaktrāmbuja-saurabhaṁ sa ca sudhā-syandi gira-vakrimā \|*  
*sa bimbādhara-mādhurīti viṣayāsaṅge ’pi cen mānasaṁ*  
*tasyām lagna-samādhi hanta viraha-vyādhiḥ kathaṁ vardhate \|\|*

**My skin thrills on experiencing the happiness of her pure touch. I see her trembling, restless, affectionate eyes. I smell the fragrance of her lotus mouth. I can hear the trickling ambrosia of her crooked words. I taste the nectar of her bimba-fruit lips. Though my senses have been satisfied, my mind has become absorbed in her. Why is my suffering from the disease of separation increasing at every moment?**

Then one verse confirms his presence. After that, thirty-three verses\[11\]pray for his direct manifestation with a display of the *gopīs’* lamentation out of long to see Kṛṣṇa after his disappearance from the *rāsa* arena. Five verses then express confusion between seeing internally (*sphūrti*) and externally (*sākṣāt-kāra*). Then seven verses again pray to see him directly. After having direct vision of him, twenty-eight verses then describe Kṛṣṇa as someone beyond words and conception. Seventeen verses then are answers of Bilvamaṅgala to statements made by Kṛṣṇa. This is the sequence of the verses.

First the poet speaks to *sakhīs* in the same condition as himself (herself), about an internal appearance of Kṛṣṇa performing pastimes with them, in order to increase their and Rādhā’s longing for Kṛṣṇa in order to accomplish all goals, with desire that Kṛṣṇa have solitary pastimes with Rādhā. This is expressed in the *Bhāgavatam:*

*bāhu-prasāra-parirambha-karālakoru*  
*nīvī-stanālabhana-narma-nakhāgra-pātaiḥ \|*  
*kṣvelyāvaloka-hasitair vraja-sundarīṇām*  
*uttambhayan rati-patiṁ ramayāṁ cakāra \|\|*

**Kṛṣṇa threw his arms around the** ***gopīs*** **and embraced them. (This expresses the goal.) He aroused Cupid in the beautiful young ladies of Vraja (this expresses the increase in longing) by touching their hands, hair, thighs, belts and breasts, by playfully scratching them with his fingernails, and also by joking with them, glancing at them and laughing with them. In this way the Lord enjoyed his pastimes. SB 10.29.46**

First he becomes joyful on realizing the impersonal mass of light from *gopīs’* beauty and ornaments, from Kṛṣṇa’s cloth and ornaments, and from Kṛṣṇa’s beauty. Out of greed, he becomes confused. May that light, which reveals itself and others, an object which is an elixir for the mind and eyes, illuminate our hearts!

He then describes a slight differentiation. That object possesses the last stage of *kaiśora* age (*nava-yauvanam*) fully manifesting itself (*pronmilāt*). It possesses a mass of locks of hair with a peacock feather that sways about (*vilāsa*) because of the soft breeze and his dance steps.

In Kṛṣṇa there is nectar arising from the outstanding sound (*pranāda*) of the flute which cavorts (*pravilāsat*) energetically by means of waves of notes and improvisations. It is called nectar *(amṛta* means sweet and deathless) because it is most sweet and brings to life inanimate objects and beings who have withered.

He is served (*ārādhitam*) on all sides with kisses and embraces by *gopīs* with full, bud-like breasts. Among all the *gopīs* who are wandering around (*jagatām*) in a crooked manner with desire to touch him, he is the intense lover (*abhirāma*) of one *gopī* (*ekā)*, Rādhā, and this causes astonishment (*adbhūtam*) for the *gopīs* as they see and remember it. This is indicated by the fact that he previously danced with her, putting his hand on her shoulder.

In the external meaning, Bilvamaṅgala lists Kṛṣṇa’s qualities. The meaning is clear. *Jagatām ekābhirāmādbhutam* can mean “He is the cause of astonishment and chief delight of the three worlds.”

### Text 5

*madhuratara-smitāmṛta-vimugdha-mukhāmburuhaṁ*  
*mada-śikhi-piñchita-manojña-kaca-pracayam \|*  
*viṣaya-viṣāmiṣa-grasana-gṛdhnuni cetasi me*  
*vipula-vilocanaṁ kim api dhāma cakāstu ciram \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**May that indescribable effulgence, having the sweetest lotus face, attractive with a sweet smile, having wide eyes and abundant, charming locks decorated with a proud peacock feather, forever illuminate my heart, greedy for possessing the desired object which is poison and bondage for the heart.**

**Комментарий:**

Again he speaks with great longing to the *gopīs* for a sweet appearance of the Lord within. As in the previous verse he says “Let the indescribable effulgence forever light up my heart.” He criticizes his heart because it is futile to crave through remembrance, since the heart merely suffers. He describes his heart. The word *viṣaya* means “that which completely binds up *(viśeṣeṇa sinoti*) the bee of the mind by the honey of Kṛṣṇa’s sweetness.” It is poison because it has the power to burn like poison. That effulgence is a pleasing object (*āmiṣam*), to be desired like nectar. My heart is greedy to quickly associate (*grasanam*) with that effulgence, a pleasing object, but which has the power to burn and which binds up my mind. It is said:

*pīḍābhir nava-kāla-kūṭa-kaṭutā-garvasya nirvāsano*  
*niḥsyandena mudāṁ sudhā-mādhurīm āhaṅkāra-saṅkocanaḥ \|*  
*premā sundari nanda-nandana-paro jāgarti yasyāntare*  
*jñāyante sphuṭam asya vakra-madhurās tenaiva vikrāntayaḥ \|\|*

**O beautiful friend! If the devotee of Kṛṣṇa develops** ***prema*** **in his heart, there will clearly appear a destruction of poisonous pride, a restriction of false ego by the experience of suffering, and sweet nectar by inundation of various joys—by which there will be a pervasion of cruelty and sweetness.** ***Vidagdha-mādhava*** **2.18**

That cause is explained. That effulgence has the sweetest lotus face, charming (*vimugdham*) with a sweet smile. It has wide eyes. The peacock feathers think with pride, “We are decorating Kṛṣṇa.” They become intoxicated with their good fortune. He has heaps of locks naturally attractive, decorated with peacock feathers intoxicated with their good fortune and with love sentiments arising on seeing Kṛṣṇa’s complexion which defeats the color of a rain cloud.\[12\]In the compound *mada-śikhi-piñchita* the word *yukta* after *mada* is missing and should be supplied for obtaining the correct meaning. Or *mada* can stand for *madātiśayātta* or *mada-rūpa*: peacock feather which are intoxication personified. The meaning is then “Kṛṣṇa has locks of hair with proud feathers coming from proud peacocks.”

The external meaning is the same except the phrase *viṣaya-viṣa* refers to Kṛṣṇa who is poison for women (*viṣaya*). The meaning is then “May that effulgence forever illuminate my heart, greedy for possessing the desired object which is poison for women.” If he mercifully makes an appearance, I will approach him. But that is very rare. Thus he feels miserable. Medinī says *āmiṣam* means flesh or a desirable object. The dictionary meaning of *āmiṣam* is also “a desired object.”

### Text 6

*mukulāyamāna-nayanāmbujaṁ vibho*  
*muralī-nināda-makaranda-nirbharam \|*  
*mukurāyamāṇa-mṛdu-gaṇḍa-maṇḍalaṁ*  
*mukha-paṅkajaṁ manasi me vijṛmbhatām \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**May his lotus face filled with the honey of the sound of his flute, with two tender cheeks acting like sapphire mirrors, with half-closed lotus eyes like flower buds, blossom in the lake of my mind.**

**Комментарий:**

In this verse, he speaks with longing (for a *sphūrti)*, with mind attached to Kṛṣṇa’s lotus face. May the lotus face of the Lord full of a wealth of sweetness and cleverness, blossom within the lake of my mind! What is that face? It is filled with the honey of the sound of his flute. That face has two tender cheeks which act like mirrors (*mukurāyamāna*) of shining sapphire. Another meaning is “The cheeks are also like two slightly blooming lotuses (*mukura*) located on another fully opened lotus (the face).” This is an example of astonishing comparison (*adbhūta upameya*). Or, the lotus eyes of the approaching g*opīs* which are half closed to prevent the *bhāva* from bursting forth are reflected on his cheeks, as if making friends with the lotus of his face. Or, the eyes of Rādhā, like wagtails,\[13\]become reflected on those cheeks.

The external meaning is clear. There is a gradual increase of longing in the last three verses. In verse 4 he desires the Lord to appear in his heart. In verse 5 he desires the Lord to remain there forever. In this verse, he desires the Lord to remain in his heart with his face. Further increase of longing should be understood in the following verses as well (till verse 11).

### Text 7

*kamanīya-kiśora-mugdha-mūrteḥ*  
*kala-veṇu-kvaṇitādṛtānanendoḥ \|*  
*mama vāci vijṛmbhatāṁ murārer*  
*madhurimnaḥ kaṇikāpi kāpi kāpi \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**May the smallest particle of the most beautiful Lord devoid of reproach, with pleasing, youthful, attractive form, with moon-like face served by the sound of his gentle flute, appear in my words!**

**Комментарий:**

“I please *priya-sakhīs* who are distraught because of not seeing Kṛṣṇa by this description, which gives pleasure through an internal manifestation of that ocean of sweetness.” Thinking this, he becomes stunned by the manifestation of such infinite rewards of his words and then speaks.

May a small particle *(kaṇikāḥ*) of the most beautiful person (*madhurimnaḥ*), the enemy of reproach, who is devoid of reproach (*murāreḥ*), manifest in my words! *Kaṇa* means particle, *kaṇi* means a small particle, and *kaṇikā* means a very small particle. May something at least (*kāpi kāpi*), something related to his *kaiśora* age, his beauty, his face with a flute in his mouth, manifest in my words! He reveals some of those particles. He has a beautiful, youthful, attractive (*mugdha*) form. He has a moonlike face served or praised (*āḍrta*) by melodious flute sounds.

Here is the external meaning. Let there at least be some appearance in my heart of Kṛṣṇa. He speaks after becoming morose. Feeling most wretched and humble, he does not request the form with many aspects of sweetness, but just his sweetness. Feeling even more disqualified, he requests not the ocean of sweetness, but just a very small particle of that sweetness, by which the whole universe will become inundated. Feeling even lower, he merely requests merely something (*kāpi kāpi*).

### Text 8

*mada-śikhaṇḍi-śikhaṇda-vibhūṣaṇaṁ*  
*madana-manthara-mugdha-mukhāmbujam \|*  
*vraja-vadhū-nayanāñjana-rañjitaṁ*  
*vijayatāṁ mama vāṅ-maya-jīvitam \|\|*

***All glories to the life of my words, the Lord ornamented with proud peacock feathers, with an attractive lotus face languid with rising passion, colored with the collyrium of the women of Vraja.***

**Praising the Lord’s sweetness in his mind, he then speaks joyfully because of a sudden urge to see the private pastimes of Kṛṣṇa with Rādhā. All glories to the Lord who gives life to my words, for when my mind is imbued with the descriptions of the Lord, the words and the subject of the words appear simultaneously. This indicates, “Let him go for those intimate pastimes now.” Or another meaning is “All glories to my words and the cause of the life of my words.” It is like the expressions “Ghee is life.” This means ghee is the cause of long life. How is he described? He is ornamented with a proud peacock feather. This is similar to the description of the peacock feather in verse 5. He has an attractive lotus face languid (*manthara*) because of actions of love increasing in his heart. Or, he has an attractive lotus face which stuns (*manthara*) Cupid (*madana*). He is marked with the collyrium of the women of Vraja because of kissing them.

*nayana-gala-kapolaṁ danta-vāso mukhāntaḥ- \|*  
*stana-yugala-lalāṭaṁ cumbana-sthānam āhuḥ \|\|*

**It is said that the places for kissing are the eyes, the throat, the cheeks, the lips, the interior of the mouth, the two breasts and the forehead.**

The external meaning is as follows. He speaks to his companions who are saying that the appearance of the Lord is rare. There is a grammatical description *kṛd-vihito bhāvo dravyavat prakāśate*: impersonal verbs with *krṭ* affixes produce their own subjects and objects. (*Mahā-bhāṣya* 3.1.67) In a similar way, he believes his words will produce the appearance of the Lord. In this verse he speaks joyful with the realization that his words describing the Lord’s sweetness are non-different from the Lord. It expresses the mood,“Why should I worry?”

**As Kṛṣṇa strolls through Vraja with his lotus-petal-like feet, marking the ground with the distinctive emblems of flag, thunderbolt, lotus and elephant goad, He relieves the distress the ground feels from the cows’ hooves. As he plays his renowned flute, his body moves with the grace of an elephant. Thus we** ***gopīs,*** **who become agitated by Cupid when Kṛṣṇa playfully glances at us, stand as still as trees, unaware that our hair and garments are slackening. SB 10.31.16-17**

They glorify the person who has embraces that are not weak *(amandam*) since those embraces destroy the *gopīs* positions of respect. But those embraces are intense (*amandam*) since they give pleasure to the women with full breasts.

*nadyas tadā tad upadhārya mukunda-gītam*  
*āvarta-lakṣita-manobhava-bhagna-vegāḥ \|*  
*āliṅgana-sthagitam ūrmi-bhujair murārer*  
*gṛhṇanti pāda-yugalaṁ kamalopahārāḥ \|\|*

**When the rivers hear the flute-song of Kṛṣṇa, their minds begin to desire him, and thus the flow of their currents is broken and their waters are agitated, moving around in whirlpools. Then with the arms of their waves the rivers embrace Murāri’s lotus feet and, holding on to them, present offerings of lotus flowers. SB 10.21.15**

He has a smile which makes the women completely disturbed (*unmada*) and gives pain (*ākula*) to those who see it. That smile is also active and intense *(amandam*), not giving pain to others, giving happiness. The foolish women glorify this smile in the following verse:

*vraja-janārti-han vīra yoṣitāṁ*  
*nija-jana-smaya-dhvaṁsana-smita \|*  
*bhaja sakhe bhavat-kiṅkarīḥ sma no*  
*jalaruhānanaṁ cāru darśaya \|\|*

**O you who destroy the suffering of Vraja’s people, O hero of all women, your smile shatters the false pride of your devotees. Please, dear friend, accept us as your maidservants and show us your beautiful lotus face. SB 10.31.6**

The root *mad* from which *unmada* is derived means to tremble. It belongs to the *ghaṭādi* class and does not lengthen the vowel. (Pāṇini 6.4.92)

Or this verse can express sarcasm. The *gopīs* glorify without steadiness *(adhīram*) your disappearance but I speak attractively about you. This is a quality of *divyonmāda.Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi,* speaking of the final state of *prema,* describes two conditions: *rūḍha* and *adhirūḍha. Adhirūḍha* has two types: *modana* and *mādana. Modana* becomes *mohana* in a condition of separation.

*etasya mohanākhyasya gatiṁ kām apy upeyuṣaḥ \|*  
*bhramābhā kāpi vaicitrī divyonmāda itīryate \|\|*  
*udghūrṇā-citra-jalpādyās tad-bhedā bahavo matāḥ \|\|*

**Whenthe ecstatic emotion of** ***mohana*** **progresses,itbecomes similarto bewilderment. This is called di*****vyonamada*** **(transcendental madness). Its varieties are** ***udghūrṇa*** **and** ***citra-jalpa*****.** ***Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi*** **14.190-191**

*Citra-jalpa* is described:

*preṣṭhasya suhṛdāloke gūḍha-roṣābhijṛmbhitaḥ \|*  
*bhūri-bhāva-mayo jalpaś citra-jalpaḥ sa ucyate \|\|*

**Conversation concerning the beloved in the companyof friends which contain deep anger and intense love are called** ***citra-jalpa*****.** ***Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi*** **14.195**

“In the company of friends” can also mean “while Kṛṣṇa or his friends are present.” *Citra-jalpa* has ten states; *Prajāpati, perihelia, vial, jalap, sañjalpa, abhijalpa, ajalpa, pratijalpa* and *sujalpa.* These manifest in the Song of the Bee in the Tenth Canto. The present verse is an example of *prajalpa*. Its characteristics ar as follows:

*asūyerṣyā-mada-yujā yo’vadhīraṇa-mudrayā \|*  
*priyasyākauśalodgāraḥ prajlapaḥ sa tu kīrtyate \|\|*

***Prajalpa*** **is speech that denigrates the tactlessness of one’s lover with expressions of disrespect. It is spoken in a mood of envy, jealousy and pride.** ***Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi*** **14.199**

A *gopī* speaks in this fashion in the following verse:

*madhupa kitava-bandho mā spṛśaṅghriṁ sapatnyāḥ*  
*kuca-vilulita-mālā-kuṅkuma-śmaśrubhir naḥ \|*  
*vahatu madhu-patis tan-māninīnāṁ prasādaṁ*  
*yadu-sadasi viḍambyaṁ yasya dūtas tvam īdṛk \|\|*

**O honeybee, O friend of a cheater, don’t touch my feet with your whiskers, which are smeared with the** ***kuṁkuma*** **that rubbed onto Kṛṣṇa’s garland when it was crushed by the breasts of a rival lover! Let Kṛṣṇa satisfy the women of Mathurā. One who sends a messenger like you will certainly be ridiculed in the Yadus’ assembly. SB 10.47.12**

The phrase *ārdra-jalpitam* refers to *ājalpa.* It is described as an outburst of disgust with the beloved, indicating that he gives happiness to others.

*vayam ṛtam iva jihma-vyāhṛtaṁ śraddadhānāḥ*  
*kulika-rutam ivājñāḥ kṛṣṇa-vadhvo hariṇyaḥ \|*  
*dadṛśur asakṛd etat tan-nakha-sparśa-tīvra*  
*smara-ruja upamantrin bhaṇyatām anya-vārtā \|\|*

**Faithfully taking his deceitful words as true, we became just like the black deer’s foolish wives, who trust the cruel hunter’s song. Thus we repeatedly felt the sharp pain of lust caused by the touch of his nails. O messenger, please talk about something besides Kṛṣṇa. SB 10.47.19**

When Rādhā says that the immature *gopīs* praise like this but she does not, and indicates her cleverness, it is called *parijalpa*. This is defined as speech which shows one’s own cleverness by revealing the deceit and merciless nature of the lover.

*sakṛd adhara-sudhāṁ svāṁ mohinīṁ pāyayitvā*  
*sumanasa iva sadyas tatyaje ’smān bhavādṛk \|*  
*paricarati kathaṁ tat-pāda-padmaṁ nu padmā*  
*hy api bata hṛta-cetā hy uttamaḥ-śloka-jalpaiḥ \|\|*

**After making us drink the enchanting nectar of His lips only once, Kṛṣṇa suddenly abandoned us, just as you might quickly abandon some flowers. How is it, then, that Goddess Padmā willingly serves his lotus feet? Alas! The answer must certainly be that her mind has been stolen away by his deceitful words. SB 10.47.13**

The words *adhīram* indicates *sañjalpa.* This is an outburst of showing ungratefulness of the lover, with signs of abusive and sarcasm.

*visṛja śirasi pādaṁ vedmy ahaṁ cātu-kārair*  
*anunaya-viduṣas te ’bhyetya dautyair mukundāt*  
*sva-kṛta iha viṣṛṣṭāpatya-paty-anya-lokā*  
*vyasṛjad akṛta-cetāḥ kiṁ nu sandheyam asmin*

**Keep your head off my feet! I know what you’re doing. You expertly learned diplomacy from Mukunda, and now you come as his messenger with flattering words. But he abandoned those who for his sake alone gave up their children, husbands and all other relations. He’s simply ungrateful. Why should I make up with him now? SB 10.47.16**

The phrase *amandam āliṅgitam* indicates *avajalpa. Avajalpa* is an outburst made in fear and envy, accusing the lover of lust and harshness.

*mṛgayur iva kapīndraṁ vivyadhe lubdha-dharmā*  
*striyam akṛta virūpāṁ strī-jitaḥ kāma-yānām \|*  
*balim api balim attvāveṣṭayad dhvāṅkṣa-vad yas*  
*tad alam asita-sakhyair dustyajas tat-kathārthaḥ \|\|*

**Like a hunter, he cruelly shot the king of the monkeys with arrows. Because he was conquered by a woman, he disfigured another woman who came to him with lusty desires. And even after consuming the gifts of Bali Mahārāja, he bound him up with ropes as if he were a crow. So let us give up all friendship with this dark-complexioned boy, even if we can’t give up talking about him. SB 10.47.17**

Āk*ulonmadasmitam* indicates *ujjalpa*. It is defined as abusing the lover because of his false words, out of pride and anger. It is found in the following verse:

*divi bhuvi ca rasāyāṁ kāḥ striyas tad-durāpāḥ*  
*kapaṭa-rucira-hāsa-bhrū-vijṛmbhasya yāḥ syuḥ \|*  
*caraṇa-raja upāste yasya bhūtir vayaṁ kā*  
*api ca kṛpaṇa-pakṣe hy uttamaḥ-śloka-śabdaḥ \|\|*

**In heaven, on earth or in the subterranean sphere, what women are unavailable to him? He simply arches his eyebrows and smiles with deceptive charm, and they all become his. The supreme goddess herself worships the dust of His feet, so what is our position in comparison? But at least those who are wretched can chant his name, Uttamaḥśloka. SB 10.47.15**

The internal meaning of Bilvamaṅgala is that this verse expresses anger because Kṛṣṇa has rejected Rādhā. The external meaning is that the *gopīs* know how to glorify Kṛṣṇa for his sweetness.

### Text 28

*astoka-smita-bharam āyatāyatākṣaṁ*  
*niḥśeṣa-stana-mṛditaṁ vrajāṅganābhiḥ \|*  
*niḥsīma-stavakita-nīla-kānti-dhāraṁ*  
*dṛśyāsaṁ tribhuvana-sundaraṁ mahas te \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**May I see your splendor, a lustrous blue creeper with unlimited flower clusters, with a profusion of smiles, with wide eyes, with limbs rubbed by the breasts of all the women of Vraja, a form most beautiful in the three worlds!**

**Комментарий:**

At that moment, not seeing Kṛṣṇa, thinking he has gone, being disrespected by her behavior, she suffers pain generated from the disappearance and speaks with longing. This is longing is described in four verses. This present verse is an example of *sujalpa*. It is defined as follows.

*yatrārjavāt sa-gāmbhīryaṁ sa-dainyaṁ saha-cāpalam \|*  
*sotkaṇṭhaṁ ca hariḥ pṛṣṭaḥ sa sujalpa iti smṛtaḥ \|\|*

***Sujalpa*** **is known as sincere questioning of the Lord with gravity, humility, agitation and longing.** ***Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi*****14.217**

The following is an example of *sujalpa*:

*api bata madhu-puryām ārya-putro ’dhunāste*  
*smarati sa pitṛ-gehān saumya bandhūṁś ca gopān*  
*kvacid api sa kathā naḥ kiṅkarīṇāṁ gṛṇīte*  
*bhujam aguru-sugandhaṁ mūrdhny adhāsyat kadā nu*

**O Uddhava! It is indeed regrettable that Kṛṣṇa resides in Mathurā. Does He remember his father’s household affairs and his friends, the cowherd boys? O great soul! Does he ever talk about us, his maidservants? When will he lay on our heads his aguru-scented hand? SB 10.47.21**

Successively the four lines of the *Bhāgavatam* illustrate each of the items gravity, humility, agitation and longing. Similarly in four verses these four states are represented in *Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta*. With longing to see Kṛṣṇa, first gravity manifests in the speech as in the first line of the *Bhāgavatam* verse with the words *api bata.*May I see your abundance of beauty (*mahas*). Just as it is possible for a person situated Mathurā to sometimes appear here (since it is so close), it is possible to see him directly here even if just his beautiful effulgence manifests here. This expresses gravity.

There are no limits in your beauty and other qualities (*niḥsīma*). And you are without limitation since you leave me and go elsewhere. In that beauty there is a dark blue creeper (*dhāra)* with flower clusters in the form of sandalwood, *kuṁkuma* and *yāvaka* because of association with other women. In that place there is also a profusion of smiles which are not insufficient (*astoka*), because of secret association with other women, while you cheat me. Because of that, your eyes are wide.

“Scolding me for having enjoyed with other women, what do you desire to see?” Thinking he would say this, she then speaks with humility. You have been rubbed by the breasts of all the women of Vraja, not just by one woman’s breasts. This means “You have also given me happiness.” The reason is for all this is that he is the cause of beauty in the three worlds (*tribhu*va*naṁ sundaram*).

The inner state is that Kṛṣṇa is endowed with glances and smiles for giving instructions to his beloved. The external meaning is clear.

*tava kathāmṛtaṁ tapta-jīvanaṁ*  
*kavibhir īḍitaṁ kalmaṣāpaham*  
*śravaṇa-maṅgalaṁ śrīmad ātataṁ*  
*bhuvi gṛṇanti ye bhūri-dā janāḥ*

**The nectar of your words and the descriptions of your activities are the life and soul of those suffering in this material world. These narrations, transmitted by learned sages, eradicate one’s sinful reactions and bestow good fortune upon whoever hears them. These narrations are broadcast all over the world and are filled with spiritual power. Certainly those who spread the message of Godhead are most munificent. SB 10.31.9**

Bilvamaṅgala depicts that mood in this verse.

Your exchanges with the passionate wives of the cowherds quiver (*luṭhanti*) in the pious women’s (*sukrṭām*) hearts which are overcome with love (*bhāve)*. This means “It cannot happen to me, since my heart is so disturbed.” However, not only the exchanges but he, the cause of such disturbance, will enter her heart, and she cannot tolerate this. This is similar to the expression “The lances enter the room.”\[24\]This means “The lances and their bearers enter the room.” The word *bhāva* indicates the hearts filled with *bhāva* (*bhāva-saroruham)* in the following *Bhāgavatam* verse:

*praviṣṭaḥ karṇa-randhreṇa svānāṁ bhāva-saroruham*  
*dhunoti śamalaṁ kṛṣṇaḥ salilasya yathā śarat*

**Kṛṣṇa, entering the lotus of the heart through the ears of the devotees, washes away all contamination until it is completely pure like the pools of water in the autumn seasons. SB 2.8.5**

Instead of *bhāvinībhiḥ* the word *bhāminībhiḥ* (by beautiful women) is also seen. This indicates pride (*mada*) in those women. “We wives enjoy illicitly in the forest as we please. How can he control us?” They are endowed with anger in love, saturated with pride. This indicates the appearance of *kila-kiñcita-bhāva*.

*garvābhilāṣa-rudita-smitāsūyā-bhaya-krudhām \|*  
*saṅkarī-karaṇaṁ harṣād ucyate kila-kiñcitam \|\|*

**The mixture of pride, affection, weeping, smiling, envy, fear and anger produced out of joy is called** ***kila-kincita-bhava. Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi*** **11.44**

How are those talks of Kane described? They are attractive with play on the meaning of words and attractive verses (*padārtha-bhaṅgī-valgūni*). Attractive verses are illustrated by verses from *Vilāsa-mañjarī*:

*sadātra cinumaḥ prasūnam ajane*  
*vayaṁ hi niratāḥ surābhi-bhajane \|*  
*na ko’pi kurute niṣedha-racanaṁ*  
*kim adya tanuṣe pragalbha-vacanam \|\|*

**We always come here to this secluded place to gather flowers for the worship of the demigods. No one forbids us. Why do you speak these arrogant words?** ***Stava-mālā*** **5.13**

*parijñātam adya prasūnālim etāṁ*  
*lunīṣe tvam evaṁ pravālaiḥ sametām \|*  
*dhṛtāsau mayā kāñcana-śreṇi-gauri*  
*praviṣṭāsi gehaṁ kathaṁ puṣpa-cauri \|\|*

**I know that you have picked many flowers and leaves today. O golden-complexioned flower-thief, now I have you in my grip. How will you be able to go home?** ***Stava-mālā*** **5.16**

Play on the meaning is illustrated in *Dāna-keli-kaumudī:*

*kṛṣṇa-kuṇḍalinaś caṇḍi kṛtaṁ ghaṭṭanayānayā \|*  
*phutkṛti-kriyayāpy asya bhavitāsi vimohitā \|\|88\|\|*

**Ferocious girl, what will you gain by trying to attack this coiling black snake? If it so much as hisses, you will fall unconscious.**

(The other meaning is: Ferocious girl, what will you gain by trying to attack Kṛṣṇa decorated with graceful earrings? His slightest whisper will make you bewildered with love for him.)

*dharṣaṇena kula-strīṇāṁ bhujaṅgeśaḥ kṣamaḥ katham \|*  
*yad etā daśanair eṣa daśan nāpnoti maṅgalam \|\|42\|\|:*

**How can the king of snakes (debauchee) succeed in attacking a mongoose? Even biting the animal with its teeth, the snake will not attain anything good.**

Those words produce *śṛṅgāra* and other *rasas (rasāni)* which are filled completely with nectar. Those words cause his and the *gopīs*’ wide eyes to tremble. Or while speaking, his eyes and the *gopīs’* eyes move about with hidden messages. Those talks continue incessantly, with mutual desire to conquer the opponent, because of the natural fickleness of the *kaiśora* age (*bālya*).

In the internal interpretation, the talks enter into his heart through the ears and cause bliss. The rest of the verse is the same. The external meaning is clear.

*adhīra-bimbādhara-vibhrameṇa*

Remembering his previous indications to her, Rādhā reaches a state of *unmāda*, and becomes completely overwhelmed with mental weakness from not seeing him, and begins to rebuke him for saying during his previous appearance “Why do I make your mind unsteady?” That is depicted in this verse.

You afflict my mind by beauty of your bimba-red lips unsteady with wordless indications. O cheater! This should be added to her words*. Hā* she utters in pain. *Hanta* she utters in lamentation. To express intensity, the syllables are repeated.

You cause suffering by that form which I see now (*anena*). “If that is so, then go to the bower.” It is impossible to explain because it is not really existing, though it has appeared there (*kena api*). What fear do you have, though you make a joke of killing respectable women? That is the meaning of the statement.

### Text 37

*yāvan na me nikhila-marma-dṛḍhābhighātaṁ*  
*niḥsandhi-bandhanam upaiti na ko’pi tāpaḥ \|*  
*tāvad vibho bhavatu tāvaka-vaktra-candra-*  
*candrātapa-dviguṇitā mama citta-dhārā \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**O most capable Lord! As long as indescribable** ***moha,*** **striking intensely at my heart and senses, to my very depths, does not occur, may the streams of my thoughts be doubly covered by the canopy of your moon-like face!**

**Комментарий:**

In this verse, Bilvamaṅgala depicts Rādhā’s words after being licked by the heat of the sun of separation and attaining the stage of *moha* (illusion), previous to the stage of deep illusion. The characteristic of *moha* is described. *Moho vicittatā proktaḥ*: *moha* refers to the state of unconsciousness. (*Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi* 15.40)

O Kṛṣṇa, capable of removing all suffering (*vibho*)! As long as indescribable (*ko ‘py*) *moha* (*tāpah*), violently striking my heart and senses (*nikhila-marma*), having tight bondage, being most intense (*niḥsandhi-bandhanam*), does not occur, that long may the streams of my consciousness be doubly covered by the canopy of your moon-like face. This means that by showing your moon-like face, you should remove my suffering. Because there are various functions of consciousness, the phrase “streams of my thoughts” (*citta-dhārāḥ*) is used in the plural. The verse also describes the *vyabhicārī-bhāva* called *vyādhi* (sickness).

In the internal meaning, the moon-like face, sweet with love, indicates appointments with Rādhā. The rest is the same. The external meaning describes him speaking after falling on the ground on the path. The meaning is clear.

### Text 38

*yāvan na me nara-daśā daśamī kuto’pi*  
*randhrād upaiti timirīkṛta-sarva-bhāvā \|*  
*lāvaṇya-keli-sadanaṁ tava tāvad eva*  
*lakṣyāsam utkvaṇita-veṇu-mukhendu-bimbam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**As long as the tenth state of man, destroying all the senses and mind, somehow does not arrive because of weakness, I desire to see the orb of your moon-like face with loud sounding flute, the pleasure room of all beauty.**

**Комментарий:**

This verse depicts the speech of Rādhā, her mind and senses covered by *moha,* calling for Kṛṣṇa in misery, fearing death has arrived. The learned describe that state as a state similar to death,\[25\]since death itself is inauspicious. By the description she gives, a state before death is appropriately meant. As long as the tenth state of man, death, does not somehow arrive because of weakness (*randhrāt*), I desire to see the orb of your moon-like face. This is her aspiration.

“Why do you express such a longing? Remaining for some time, you will see my face.” That state destroys the body and senses (*timirīkṛta-sarva-bhāvāḥ*) (therefore I will have no more suffering). “If death occurs, you will not see me. Then what will you do?” She then speaks with longing. That face is the pleasure house of all beauty, in which the flute loudly sounds. This indicates that death is unfortunate without seeing his sweet face. The nature of a heart overcome by *prema* is that one does not desire death because of fear of final separation. This is the meaning of the following when the inhabitants of Vṛndāvana were threatened by a forest fire:

*su-dustarān naḥ svān pāhi kālāgneḥ suhṛdaḥ prabho*  
*na śaknumas tvac-caraṇaṁ santyaktum akuto-bhayam*

**O Lord, we are your true friends and devotees. Please protect us from this insurmountable fire of death. We can never give up your lotus feet, which drive away all fear. SB 10.17.24**

In internal state Kṛṣṇa’s moon-like face, sweet with love, gives directions to Rādhā. The rest is the same. The external meaning is clear.

### Text 39

*ālola-locana-vilokita-keli-dhārā-*

While speaking, she then fainted. The *sakhīs* came and placed Kṛṣṇa’s chewed betel nut in her mouth, and said “He has come. Behold your beloved.” Awakening, with closed eyes because of exhaustion, she speaks. “Please tell me if this is true.” This state is described in the verse.

I hear the jingling of his jeweled anklets, as he comes dancing. I am aware of this. The cause of his coming is explained. He is an ocean of mercy (*karunāmbu-rāśeḥ*). That sound is made fresh by the notes of his flute, which are mixed (*pūra*) with answering sounds—the sounds of his feet, the palms, bells, and bracelets. The sound is made newer by the tips of his feet worshipped by playful streams of glances from trembling eyes. ### Text 40

*he deva he dayita he bhuvanaika-bandho*  
*he kṛṣṇa he capala he karuṇaika-sindho \|*  
*he nātha he ramaṇa he nayanābhirāma*  
*hā hā kadā nu bhavitāsi padaṁ dṛśor me \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**O playboy! O protector! O sole friend in the world! O attractor! O fickle one! O sole ocean of mercy! O master! O enjoyer! O delight of the eye! Ah! Ah! When will you be visible to me?**

**Комментарий:**

Getting up, she looks around and says, “I hear the sound of my friends’ anklet, but I do not see him. That cheater is inside the bower enjoying with someone.” Again she enters into the state of *unmāda*, and speaks with indignation (a*marṣa*) to him, seeing him in front of her with the marks of enjoyment from another woman. Then thinking that he has gone, impatience (*autsukya*) manifests out of repentance. Then there is a mixture of sentiments (*bhāva-sandhi*)*. Sarūpayor bhinnayor vā sandhiḥ syād bhāvayor mūrtiḥ*: when two versions of the same *bhāva* or two differing *bhāvas* mix it is called *bhāva-sandhi.* (BRS 2.4.235)

*Amarṣa* is defined as follows. *Adhikṣepāpamānādeḥ syād amarṣo ’sahiṣṇutā:* intolerance arising from contempt or insult or other causes is called *amarṣa*. (BRS 2.4.159) *Ausukya* is defined as follows. *Kālākṣamatvam autsukyam iṣṭekṣāpti-spṛhādibhiḥ:* inability to tolerate the passing of time, arising from desire to see or attain a desired object is called *autsukyam (*impatience). (BRS 2.4.151) When she takes shelter of two conflicting emotions is also called *bhāva-śavalyam*. This is defined as *śabalatvaṁ tu bhāvānāṁ saṁmardaḥ syāt parasparam: w*hen many *bhāvas* conflict with each other, the state is called *śābalya*. (BRS 2.4.244) In this case there is a mixture of *asūya* (fault-finding), *aurgrya* (ferocity), *avahitthā* (concealment) following *amarṣa*, and *mati* (consideration), *dainya* (feeling disqualified) and *cāpala* (insolence) following *autsukya.* This verse depicts Rādhā speaking with *bhāva-sandhi* and *bhāva-śāvalya* arising from *unmāda.*

Thinking that Kṛṣṇa has enjoyed with other women she becomes indignant (*amarṣa*). She then takes shelter of her natural quality of *dhīrādhira-madhyā,* and calls out with tears and crooked words. O enjoyer of other women (*he deva*)! This means “If that is so, then go to them!” The definition of *dhīrādhīra* is given. *Dhīrādhīra tu vakroktyā sa-bāṣpaṁ vadati priyaṁ*: *dhīrādhira* means that one speaks to the beloved with tears and crooked words. (*Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi* 5.39)

Thinking that he has gone because of her disrespect, she repents and speaks with impatience to see him. O protector (*he dayita*)! You are the protector of my life. How can you give me up? Please show yourself again!

Thinking he has returned to console her, fault-finding (*asūya*) arises. From that she takes shelter of *dhīra-madhyā\[26\]*and speaks with sarcasm and crooked words. O sole ocean of the world (*he bhuvanaika-sindho*)! What fault do you have? You belong not only to me, but to all the *gopīs.* Moreover, you are the friend of all the women in the world who are attracted to your flute. Go then and satisfy all*. Dhīrā tu vakti vakroktyā sotprāsaṁ sāgasaṁ priyam*: a *dhīra* woman speaks to her lover with crooked words, derision and fault-finding. *Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi* 5.34

Thinking he has gone again, she speaks with a manifestation of consideration (*mati)* following after impatience *(autsukya*). O Kṛṣṇa! OŚyāmasundara! Attractor of the heart! You have stolen my heart. What is the use of my pride? Show yourself even once.

She thinks he has again returned, consoling her by saying “Dearest! I was outside. I didn’t go anywhere. Please be satisfied.” She then takes shelter of the quality of *adhīra-madhyā* and speaks in anger. O fickle one (*hecapala*)! Lecher for the cowherd women! Stealer of others’ wives! Go! Go! *Adhīrā paruṣair vākyair nirasyed vallabhaṁ ruṣā:* the *adhīrā* woman drives away her lover with anger and harsh words. (*Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi* 5.37)

Thinking he has again left, she speaks pitifully, out of feeling disqualified *(dainya),* thinking “Ah! Being disrespected, he has gone and will not return.” O only ocean of mercy! Though I am an offender, show yourself since you are soft with mercy.

Thinking he has again returned, and is consoling her by saying “Dearest! Why do you wrongly torture me out of anger? Please be satisfied.” Taking shelter of the quality of *dhīra-pragalbhā* arising from concealment (*avahitthā*), she speaks indifferently. O master (*he nātha*)! You are the protector of us inhabitants of Vraja. What unintelligent woman would not speak to you? But I have accepted a vow of silence along with the *brāhmaṇa* women. You should excuse my offense. *Udāste surate dhīrā sāvahitthā ca sādarā:* the *dhīra-pragalbhā* heroine isuninterestedin enjoyingamorous pastimes with her loverand pretends to treat her lover with respect,while sheactually burns with jealousy.(*Ujjvala-nīla-maṇi* 5.53)

Thinking he has gone again, with a rise of insolence (*cāpalam*)\[27\]she thinks “He has been driven away constantly. He will not return.” In humility she speaks. “If he mercifully shows himself again, I will embrace him by the neck.” O enjoyer (*he rāmana*)! Enjoy me always. You are the enjoyer. Come now and enjoy me!

Thinking he has returned again, with a mood of eclipsed, incidental indignation (*amarṣa*), while the mind is overcome with strong, natural impatience (*autsukya*), she spreads her arms to embrace him, but does not attain him. She then speaks in great agitation after having seen him in a temporary external manifestation (*bāhya-sphūrti*). O delight for the eyes (*nāyanābhirāma*)! When will you be visible to my eyes? *Hā hā* indicates her great distress.

The internal meaning of Bilvamaṅgala is explained as follows. Thinking Kṛṣṇa is pacifying him in order to meet Rādhā, he shows indignation towards him. Thinking Kṛṣṇa has gone, he becomes impatient to bring him back. One should understand the other words suitably. Achieving the stage of *anurāga* such states arise even in the body of a *sādhaka*.\[28\]In the external meaning, the various calling of names indicate wretchedness, impatience and other emotions in the devotee.

### Text 41

*amūny adhanyāni dināntarāṇi*  
*hare tvad-ālokanam antareṇa \|*  
*anātha-bandho karuṇaika-sindho*  
*hā hanta hā hanta kathaṁ nayāmi \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Ah! Oh! How can I pass the days and nights which are inauspicious without seeing you? O stealer of my mind and senses! O friend of those without shelter! Of sole ocean of mercy!**

Disturbed by the flames of the fire of separation, thinking of a moment as many days, Rādhā speaks with agitation. That is described in this verse. O Lord *(hare*)! The length of these days and nights (*dināntarāṇi*) are a mere collection of moments. The predicate should be supplied. Or another meaning is “It is impossible to pass these days and nights which are like a million *kalpas*.” *Hā* indicates pain and distress. *Hanta* indicates despair. The words are repeated to indicate the intensity of the emotions. How can I pass my time without seeing you? Please tell me how. The reason is given. These days are inauspicious.

“If you are burning with lust, go to your husband.” This is what he advised in *Bhāgavatam:*

*mātaraḥ pitaraḥ putrā bhrātaraḥ patayaś ca vaḥ*  
*vicinvanti hy apaśyanto mā kṛḍhvaṁ bandhu-sādhvasam*

Not finding you at home, your mothers, fathers, sons, brothers and husbands are certainly searching for you. Don’t cause anxiety for your family members. SB 10.29.20

Imagining that Kṛṣṇa is saying this, she speaks in the manner of the *gopīs* in the *Bhāgavatam*:

*kurvanti hi tvayi ratiṁ kuśalāḥ sva ātman*  
*nitya-priye pati-sutādibhir ārti-daiḥ kim \|*  
*tan naḥ prasīda parameśvara mā sma chindyā*  
*āśāṁ dhṛtāṁ tvayi cirād aravinda-netra \|\|*

**Expert transcendentalists always direct their affection toward you because they recognize you as their true self and eternal beloved. What use do we have for these husbands, children and relatives of ours, who simply give us trouble? Therefore, O supreme controllers grant us your mercy. O lotus-eyed one, please do not cut down our long-cherished hope to have your association. SB 10.29.33**

O friend of the shelterless (*anātha-bandho*)! You are the friend of us cowherd women who have left our husbands. We have left them because they give suffering.

“But this is not proper.” Kṛṣṇa says in the *Bhāgavatam:*

*bhartuḥ śuśrūṣaṇaṁ strīṇāṁ paro dharmo hy amāyayā*  
*tad-bandhūnāṁ ca kalyāṇaḥ prajānāṁ cānupoṣaṇam*

**The highest religious duty for a woman is to sincerely serve her husband, behave well toward her husband’s family and take good care of her children. SB 10.29.24**

She replies:

*cittaṁ sukhena bhavatāpahṛtaṁ gṛheṣu*  
*yan nirviśaty uta karāv api gṛhya-kṛtye \|*  
*pādau padaṁ na calatas tava pāda-mūlād*  
*yāmaḥ kathaṁ vrajam atho karavāma kiṁ vā \|\|*

**Until today our minds were absorbed in household affairs, but you easily stole both our minds and our hands away from our housework. Now our feet won’t move one step from your lotus feet. How can we go back to Vraja? What would we do there? SB 10.29.34**

O stealer of your hearts and senses (*he hare*)! It is your fault!

“You lusty women are unsteady! How can I give up *dharma*?”

She answers with wretchedness:

*tan naḥ prasīda vṛjinārdana te ’nghri-mūlaṁ*  
*prāptā visṛjya vasatīs tvad-upāsanāśāḥ \|*  
*tvat-sundara-smita-nirīkṣaṇa-tīvra-kāma*  
*taptātmanāṁ puruṣa-bhūṣaṇa dehi dāsyam \|\|*

**Therefore, O vanquisher of all distress, please show us mercy. To approach your lotus feet we abandoned our families and homes, and we have no desire other than to serve you. On hearing Kṛṣṇa’s sweet, affection words, Bilvamaṅgala speaks, agitated with bliss. O performer of pastimes (*deva*)! May you remain with all excellence (*jaya*)! This is repeated three times because of great respect and bliss. O Lord whose name is auspicious and attractive (*divya)* for the three worlds!

Another meaning of these lines is as follows. O *deva-deva-deva*! *Deva* refers first to *devatās* worshipped by mankind; second, to your devotee associates, worshiped by the *devatās;* and third, to the Lord worshiped by the devotees. O Lord worshiped by the devotees who are worshiped by the *devatās!* Remain with all excellence (*jaya*)! It is said*anuvratā yatra surāsurārcitāḥ****:*** **in Vaikuṇṭha the inhabitants are fully dedicated to the Lord and are worshippable by the** ***devas, asuras*** **and devotees. (SB 2.9.10) O Lord auspicious for the three worlds! Remain excellent (*****jaya*****)! O Lord whose name is bliss (*****divyam*****) and non-different from your** ***svarūpa*****! Remain excellent (*****jaya*****)!\[35\]**

O Lord of pastimes (*deva*)! Remain with all excellence! O Lord of pastimes named Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-deva*)! Remain with all excellence! Of Lord whose manifested form is nectar for the ears, mind and eyes! Remain with all excellence!

### Text 109

*tubhyaṁ nirbhara-harṣa-varṣa-vivaśāveśa-sphuṭāvirbhavad-*  
*bhūyaś cāpala-bhūṣiteṣu sukṛtāṁ bhāveṣu nirbhāṣiṇe \|*  
*śrīmad-gokula-maṇḍanāya manasāṁ vācāṁ ca dūra-sphuran-*  
*mādhuryaika-mahārṇavāya mahase kasmaicid asmai namaḥ \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**I offer respects to the indescribable mass of bliss, the principal ocean of sweetness, revealing itself beyond words and mind, which manifests itself in love-laden hearts of persons with** ***prema*****, which manifests itself in hearts ornamented with profuse agitation caused by your appearance which is produced by longing to attain you, which manifest itself in hearts uncontrollable because of showers of joy. I offer respects to the ornament of Gokula.**

**Комментарий:**

Because of being mad with bliss through realizing Kṛṣṇa’s extreme sweetness, he desires to describe Kṛṣṇa but cannot do so. He simply offers respects, which embody the conclusion of his words. Quarrelling with Kṛṣṇa, who argues with him for fun, he speaks. I offer respects to the indescribable (*kasmaicit*) mass of bliss *(mahase*).

“Describe that sweetness. I desire to hear it.” I offer respects to the principle ocean of all sweetness, which manifests itself far beyond words.

“If it is beyond words, think of the sweetness, using your mind.” That sweetness is also far beyond the mind. That sweetness is not manifested by words or thoughts.

“Since the sweetness cannot be grasped by words or the mind, it cannot be experienced by anyone.” That sweetness has the quality of revealing itself (*nirbhāsine*) in the hearts which are overcome with *bhāva (bhāveṣu),* belonging to persons possessing *prema* especially for you (*sukṛtām*). What are those *bhāvas*? Those *bhāvas* are ornamented by many agitations which arise because of your temporarily appearances (*sphūrti*) which causes longing to attain you directly *(āveśa).* Those b*hāvas* are uncontrollable because of showers of intense joy.

“Does that mean that you are describing me as the impersonal Brahman, since you call me a mass of bliss?” No, you are the ornament (*maṇḍanāy*a) of Gokula, like a beautiful brilliant sapphire. Therefore I offer respects only to you.

### Text 110

*īśāna-deva-caraṇa-bharaṇena nīvī-*  
*dāmodara-sthira-yaśaḥ-stavakodbhavena \|*  
*līlā-śukena racitaṁ tava kṛṣṇa-deva*  
*karṇāmṛtaṁ vahatu kalpa-śatāntare’pi \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**O Lord Kṛṣṇa! May this work, nectar for your ears, ornamented with the feet of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, composed by Līlāśuka, who has produced this praise of the unfading fame of Kṛṣṇa who is bound up by Rādhā’s belt, flow for a hundred** ***kalpas*** **in the hearts of the devotees.**

**Комментарий:**

“O Līlāśuka! Because of your work, which is called nectar for my ears, I am coming close. Make a request. What else do you desire?”

O performer of pastimes! I am fully satisfied with directly seeing you. What should I request? Well, give this blessing then. He then speaks.

O Kṛṣṇa-deva! May this work which is nectar for your ears, composed by me, flood the hearts of those who relish devotion to you, for a hundred *kalpas.*

How should I describe myself? The feet of the Lord who engages in play (*deva*) and who is the Lord of all lords (*īśāna*) decorate my head and heart. *Īśāna* can also mean the life (*āna*) of Rādhā (*īśā*). Or it can mean Rādhā (*īśā*) proud of her face (*ānana*). The syllable ā is dropped from *ābhāraṇa* because of the exigencies of the meter.

*Nīvī-dāmodara* means “he who is bound at the waist by the belt of Rādhā who was disappointed with him during Kārtika month.” There is a verse from the *Bhaviṣya Purāṇa*:

*saṅketāvasare cyute praṇayataḥ saṁrabdhayā rādhayā*  
*prārabhya bhrū-kuṭiṁ hiraṇya-raśanā-dāmnā nibaddhodaram \|*  
*kārttikyāṁ jananī-kṛtotsava-vara-prastāvanā-pūrvakaṁ*  
*cāṭūni prathayantam ātta-pulakaṁ dhyāyema dāmodaram \|\|*

**When Kṛṣṇa broke his appointment, Rādhā grabbed him, and frowning, bound him around the waist with golden ropes. Kṛṣṇa told her that he could not come because he had attended his mother's autumn festival. Let us meditate on Dāmodara, with his hairs standing on end, as he speaks clever words.**

*Nīvī* can also mean “principle stock or treasure.” This indicates *prema*. Thus the sentence can mean “I have produced a bouquet of flowers with unfading fame for Dāmodara, who is bound around the waist by the treasure of *prema* or for Dāmodara, bound by the belt of Rādhā.” Some say that *īśāna* refers to Śiva and that *nīvī-dāmodara* means the names of Līlāśuka’s mother and father. The meaning would then be “I have produced a bouquet of flowers with unfading fame because of my parents Nīvī and Dāmodara.

### Text 111

*dhanyānāṁ sarasānulāpa-saraṇī-saurabhyam abhyasyatāṁ*  
*karṇānāṁ vivareṣu kām api sudhā-vṛṣṭiṁ duhānaṁ muhuḥ \|*  
*vanyānāṁ sudṛśāṁ mano-nayanayor magnasya devasya naḥ*  
*karṇānāṁ vacasāṁ vijṛmbhitam aho kṛṣṇasya karṇāmṛtam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**It is my good fortune that this manifestation of my words is nectar for the ears of Kṛṣṇa, performer of pastimes, who is completely absorbed with mind and eye in the women of Vraja during separation and meeting. And this work produces a shower of indescribable nectar in the ears of the** ***gopīs*** **and qualified devotees. May the fragrance of the waves of incessant conversations filled with** ***madhura-rasa*** **be repeatedly tasted!**

**Комментарий:**

“Ah! Because of its qualities, this nectar of the ear that you have created is also for my dear *gopīs* and for my expert devotees having *rasa.*” Thinking “May my words also serve this purpose!” and that his words would bring satisfaction to all those persons Bilvamaṅgala then speaks with bliss and wonder.

This manifestation of my words is nectar for ears of you who perform pastimes (*devasya*), the crest jewel of *rasikas* skilful at all types of play (*krṣṇasya*). This is my good fortune (*aho*).

“This is not really astonishing, since it is merely composed of discussions and outbursts caused by meeting and separation.” But even Kṛṣṇa’s eyes are absorbed in the beautiful *gopīs* during meeting, and his mind is absorbed in them during separation. His mind and senses are absorbed in them because of their prattling in separation and discussions in meeting. Those women are from Vṛndāvana (*vanyānam*), having skills desired even by Lakṣmī.

Moreover, the work is suitable not only for you who have affection for your devotee’s words. The work also produces (*duhānam*) a shower of nectar in the openings of the *gopīs*’ ears. That is astonishing! Because it shows graphically the states of separation and union, it is not only for them, but for your devotees also. It produces a shower of nectar in the holes of the ears of those who are fortunate (*dhanyānām*), your special devotees. That is astonishing.

“It is suitable for them (to hear once) because they hear charming language never before heard.” No, let the fragrance of the waves (*saraṇī*) of incessant conversations (*anulāpa*) accompanied by your *madhura-bhakti-rasa* be repeatedly tasted (*abhyasyatām*). I say this since you asked me to make a request.

### Text 112

*anugraha-dviguṇa-viśāla-locanair*  
*anusmaran mṛdu-muralī-ravāmṛtaiḥ \|*  
*yato yataḥ prasarati me vilocanaṁ*  
*tataḥ sphuratu tavaiva vaibhavam \|\|*

**Перевод:**

**Wherever my eyes wander, remembering constantly your sweetness, at that place may your wealth of pastimes and sweetness appear, along with the nectar of the gentle sound of your flute and the eyes of both of you, doubly widened to give mercy to me!**

**Комментарий:**

“O Līlāśuka! Truly, your words are shining with your deep, pure *prema*. I am purchased by such *anurāga.* I am controlled by you. But you have come to Vṛndāvana now. Experience for some days the happiness of seeing the *rāsa* dance in Śrī Vṛndāvana which can be relished even in your present body. Later, in the near future, you will enter into my pastimes.” In this way the Lord consoled Bilvamaṅgala in order to disappear at that time.

Seeing Kṛṣṇa along with Rādhā glancing at him with mercy and affection, disturbed by losing Kṛṣṇa’s presence, praying with humility for a method of conquering his misery, Bilvamaṅgala speaks. O Lord of pastimes! Wherever my eyes which constantly remember *(anusmaran*) your sweetness wander, there, your wealth of beauty, cleverness and pastimes (*vaibhavam*) along with the sweet sound of your gentle flute and the eyes of both of you, naturally wide, but doubly widened by giving mercy to me, should constantly appear!

*akṣṇor agre sadā tiṣṭha naya vā māṁ padāntikam \|*  
*iti dīnaḥ kathaṁ brūyāṁ netrāgre sphuratāt sadā \|\|*

**“Remain forever before my eyes, or lead me to your feet.” How can an unfortunate person like me say this, since I have never seen you with my eyes?**

*jayatāṁ suratau paṅgor mama manda-gater gatī \|*  
*mat-sarvasvaṁ padāmbhojau rādhā-madanamohanau \|\|*

**May the lotus feet of Rādhā and Madana-mohana who are engaged in conjugal affairs, those feet which are my very life, the goal for a person like me, lame and slow moving, remain excellent forever.**

*jayati madhura-rādhā-kṛṣṇa-līlā-rasodyan*  
*naṭana-vidhi-sudhābhiḥ sārtha-saṁjñām akārṣīt \|*  
*viṣaya-viṣa-visaṁjñāṁ yo rasajñāṁ naṭīṁ me*  
*sarasa-bhajana-lāsye sūtradhāra-svarūpaḥ \|\|*

**May the manifestation of** ***rasa*** **in sweet pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa remain forever excellent. Acting as the stage manager for charming dancing and worship, that** ***rasa*** **has made me a dancer knowledgeable of** ***rasa,*** **endowed with clear consciousness, by the sweetness in its various ways of dancing*****--*****though I was previously unconscious because of the poison of material enjoyment.**

*akṣuṇṇe pathi me’ndhasya skhalat-pāda-gater muhuḥ \|*  
*sva-kṛpā-yaṣṭi-dānena santaḥ santv avalambanam \|\|*  
*śrī-rūpa-caraṇābjāli-kṛṣṇa-dāsena varṇitā \|*  
*kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛtasyaiṣā ṭīkā sāraṅga-raṅgadā \|\|*

**May the devotees be my support by applying the club of their mercy, since I am blind on a new path, constantly stumbling! This commentary on** ***Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta*** **called “Bestower of Joy to the Swans” has been written by Kṛṣṇa-dāsa, a bee at the lotus feet of Śrī Rūpa.**
